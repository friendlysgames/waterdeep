# The Sundering: open-web research notes

Researched 2026-11-12. Scope: what the open web adds on how the Second Sundering (1482 to 1489 DR) and the six-novel Sundering series (2013 to 2014) resolved the 4e/Spellplague changes. The offline wiki dump, SCAG and Heroes of Faerun book text are covered by another agent.

## Reliability caveat (read first)

WebFetch returns answers from a small summarizing model, not raw page text. Quotes below were returned by it and are probably faithful, but the fetches behind the Wikipedia, ICv2, Den of Geek and Kemp quotes were done more than once and agree. Spot-check any quote before it goes into a published page. Where a fetch could not confirm a point, the note says "not found". Three sources could not be read: Fandom (blocked by design), bellofsouls (HTTP 403), Powell's (HTTP 403). The Candlekeep thread returned 503. Reviews of the novels were found only from blogs and reader sites, so plot summaries in section 3 are lower-confidence than the quotes in sections 1 and 4.

Source tiers used: **A** = named Wizards staff or the novels' authors speaking; **B** = reputable reviews, Wikipedia, press; **C** = forum posts (unverified).

## 1. What the Sundering set out to do

**Kemp (author of The Godborn), blog, 20 August 2012 (A).** https://paulskemp.net/blog/the-sundering/
- "It is not a reboot of the setting."
- "It's not a retcon."
- "Everything that happened, happened."
- Fragment: "The Sundering moves the setting forward" (the post goes on to say the movement circles back to what he believes are the roots of the Realms).
- Fragment: "the separation of the currently 'overlapping' worlds of Abeir and Toril"; and "an end to the Age of Upheaval".

**Liz Schuh (WotC Head of Publishing and Licensing), ICv2 interview, 2013 (A).** https://icv2.com/articles/comics/view/26417/exclusive-interview-d-d-the-sundering-part-1
- "The Sundering is all about those two worlds separating--coming apart--"
- "At the end of this story arc, Abeir and Toril will be separate again."
- "Markings that marked spell-plagued people and animals will fade and go away."
- She says the collision "changed the pantheon of the gods." The page does not say the Sundering restores the old pantheon, and Mystra, the Weave and 4e races are not mentioned on that page.

**Chris Perkins (WotC), Den of Geek, 17 November 2014 (A).** https://www.denofgeek.com/games/chris-perkins-interview-dungeons-dragons-the-sundering-and-shared-worlds/
- "embrace the past and not pass judgment or rewrite history."
- "Whether we like them or not, they are very much a part of the Forgotten Realms." (about the Time of Troubles and the Spellplague)
- "We didn't want to undo or overwrite something that might be a fan's favourite part of the setting."
- "rather let the fans tell us what they like about the Realms and focus on those elements going forward."

**Erin M. Evans (author of The Adversary), Den of Geek, 29 April 2015 (A).** https://www.denofgeek.com/books/erin-m-evans-interview-forgotten-realms-the-sundering-dd-2
- On reboot: "we would be cheating everyone out of what made the Forgotten Realms really great."
- "The Realms still feel like they've always existed."

**Ed Greenwood, Tribality interview, 13 July 2015 (A).** https://www.tribality.com/2015/07/13/ed-greenwood-creator-of-forgotten-realms-interview/
- Welcomes the Realms "getting back on its feet" as opposed to "magic going wild and gods dying".
- Calls post-1987 material the "new history" of the Realms.
- Greenwood is bound by NDAs on storylines, so he gives no mechanics of the reversal.

**Wikipedia, "The Sundering" (B).** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sundering
- Cites Curtis D. Carbonell (Dread Trident, 2019): "A new sundering was needed to return FR to its pre-Spellplague state." That is a scholar's view, not a WotC statement.
- Cites Greenwood (Gen Con 2012 archive page) describing the event as "war, gods, and plain folks trying to get by."
- First announced at Gen Con 2012 ahead of D&D Next.

**Wikipedia, "Forgotten Realms" (B), citing Shannon Appelcline, Designers & Dragons.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forgotten_Realms
- The Sundering "reversed many of the 4e changes to the Realms, but without rebooting the timeline."
- R. A. Salvatore: "the changes were being driven from a different direction" (authors were told, not consulted).

**Synthesis.** The people who made it describe it as neither a reboot nor a retcon (Kemp, Perkins, Evans). It is a bridge: an in-fiction event that ends the Abeir/Toril overlap and moves the calendar forward, so that 5e can start from a Realms that resembles pre-4e. The Carbonell and Appelcline readings ("return to pre-Spellplague state", "reversed many of the 4e changes") are interpretive statements by outsiders and should not be attributed to WotC.

| Spellplague markings on people and animals | Reversed (supported) | Schuh: "Markings ... will fade and go away." |

## 3. The six novels

Sources are reviews unless stated. Dates are review dates.

1. **The Companions** (R. A. Salvatore, Aug/Sep 2013). Review, thefoundingfields.com, 11 Sep 2013: centers on Cattie-brie, Bruenor and Regis, reborn into new lives; the reviewer says the book "never explains the Sundering" and the link is by inference. Cattie-brie becomes entangled with Shadovar sorceresses of the Shade Enclave. Contribution: sets up the new calendar (companions reborn decades later); little Sundering mechanics. https://thefoundingfields.com/2013/09/the-companions-shadowhawk
2. **The Godborn** (Paul S. Kemp, Oct 2013). Review, bookwraiths.com, 25 Nov 2013: Vasen, son of Erevis Cale, half-human half-shade, raised at the Abbey of the Rose by followers of Amaunator; the plot resolves "Whether the god Mask triumphs over Shar." Contribution: closes Kemp's Twilight War arc and moves Mask and Shar's divine conflict forward. The Wikipedia summary (B) adds that Riven becomes Mask; I could not verify that elsewhere. https://bookwraiths.com/2013/11/25/the-godborn-delivers
3. **The Adversary** (Erin M. Evans, Dec 2013). Review, thefoundingfields.com, 8 Dec 2013: a "major time skip, tied to the Sundering, moves the story forward nearly eight years"; Farideh ends up in a Shadovar internment camp "caught in the conflict between Asmodeus and Shar." Reviewer calls it the first book to show the event's larger scope. Contribution: the Chosen and the gods' wars. https://thefoundingfields.com/2013/12/the-adversary-by-erin-m-evans-book-review-shadowhawk/
4. **The Reaver** (Richard Lee Byers, Feb 2014). Publisher-side summary found via search: Anton Marivaldi, a reaver, captures a boy for a bounty and is "caught in the riptide caused by the sundering of worlds." Contribution: the experience of ordinary people at sea during the separation. I could not fetch a full review (redirect failure), so this is thin.
5. **The Sentinel** (Troy Denning, Apr 2014). Review by Kelly Jensen, 25 Mar 2014, sfcrowsnest.info: Kleef Kenric, Joelle, Malik and Arietta travel to Grumbar's temple in the Underchasm; setting described as "the separation of Abeir from Toril," during the Era of Upheaval; gods compete "often through their Chosen." Contribution: the Chosen and elemental powers at the point of separation. https://www.sfcrowsnest.info/?p=12987
6. **The Herald** (Ed Greenwood, Jun 2014). Publisher blurb (risingshadow.net) and Bookwraiths review, 13 Aug 2014: Mystra "has withdrawn from the world"; Shar may seek to replace the Weave with her Shadow Weave; Shadovar search Candlekeep; Elminster, Storm Silverhand and Amarune try to reinforce the Weave; "The choice of worlds lies in the balance." The reviewer calls the ending rushed. Contribution: the climax, with the Weave at stake. Wikipedia notes it is a prequel to the adventure Confrontation at Candlekeep. The final state of Mystra and the Weave is not stated in any source I could read. https://www.risingshadow.net/book/38851-the-herald and https://bookwraiths.com/2014/08/13/the-herald-book-6-of-the-sundering-by-ed-greenwood

Review consensus (B): the novels are meant to stand alone and can be read in any order (Bookwraiths on The Godborn), and several reviewers found the link to the Sundering thin or the ending hard to follow.

## 4. What Wizards said 5e canon is

- Perkins (Den of Geek, 17 Nov 2014, A): no wiping of 3e, 4e or older events; "embrace the past and not pass judgment or rewrite history", and past events including the Spellplague remain "part of the Forgotten Realms".
- Perkins, same article: fans decide which elements to emphasize ("focus on those elements going forward"). That is a stance of selective emphasis, not formal canon tiers.
- Kemp (A): "Everything that happened, happened."
- Evans (A, 2015): the authors rejected a reboot.
- Wikipedia (B): 5e has covered mostly the Sword Coast; Polygon's Cacciatore (cited) calls 5e details "limited in scope".
- ENWorld, EN World thread (C, 7 Aug 2014, Xodis): "Xodis also said the setting will not simply undo 4e changes but will move forward" (my paraphrase of the fetch summary; the poster cites unnamed Gen Con questions). Treat as unverified.
- Not found: a Wizards statement that ranks editions (for example "3e and 4e lore both count") or one about older material. Perkins's "every edition ... adds something new" line was returned by the fetch tool; it matches the same article but I did not see it in the page myself, so verify.

## 5. Heroes of Faerun (2025) vs the 2014 Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide

Open-web evidence is thin; the book text is the other agent's job.

- Official product pages (WPN, D&D Beyond, 11 Nov 2025) describe the book as a 192-page player compendium with "a primer on the 42 gods" and "An overview of the 10 Forgotten Realms regions". Neither page mentions the Sundering, the Spellplague or 4e. https://wpn.wizards.com/en/products/forgotten-realms-heroes-of-faerun and https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2032-everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-new
- **Deity count (gamingnexus, 8 Nov 2025, B).** https://www.gamingnexus.com/News/71977/In-DDs-new-Forgotten-Realms-Heroes-of-Faerun-sourcebook2c-the-number-of-major-deities-has-changed-for-the-third-time-in-10-years — reports "a pantheon of 40" major gods; 2014 PHB had 36, SCAG added 11. Heroes brings back Eilistraee, Lolth and Shaundakul and drops nine (Akadi, Grumbar, Gwaeron Windstrom, Hoar, Istishia, Jergal, Kossurth, Savras, Valkur). Of SCAG's gods, only Amaunator, Asmodeus and the Red Knight remain. The article does not mention the Sundering, Spellplague or 4e; its only explanation is a line from the book that the pantheon "has shifted over the ages."
- Forum (C): the D&D Beyond thread (Oct 2025) has Dr_Selastraga saying "a lot of the lore is the same as SCAG, the 2014 DMG and W:DH. Nothing has changed." Another poster says the book keeps Yarin Frostmantle ruling Damara in 1501 DR, which implies about 1501 DR as the setting date, and asks why Tymanther is no longer a country. EN World posters (22 Nov 2025) debate Halruaa and Dambrath. All unverified.
- Not found: an official statement about how Heroes of Faerun treats the Sundering, the Spellplague, the Weave or Abeir.

## 6. Where sources disagree

1. **Deity count.** gamingnexus (8 Nov 2025): "a pantheon of 40" major gods. WPN and D&D Beyond product pages (Nov 2025): "a primer on the 42 gods". Likely a counting difference (major vs all, or which entries count); resolve from the book.
2. **Timing.** Wikipedia: the Sundering started in 1484 DR "about a century after the Spellplague". Fan wiki results (C/B, found by search) give 1482 to 1487 DR, and your brief says 1482 to 1489 DR. The sources differ on both start and end dates.
3. **Reversal vs no rewrite.** Carbonell (via Wikipedia): "A new sundering was needed to return FR to its pre-Spellplague state." Kemp: "It is not a reboot of the setting." Perkins: "not pass judgment or rewrite history." These can be reconciled (undo effects, keep history) but a reader who quotes only the first will overstate the reset.
4. **Why it was made.** Roll20 and wiki-relaying forum posts (C) say Greenwood and Salvatore designed it to "erase" 4e changes. Kemp, Perkins and Evans (A) explicitly deny a retcon or reboot. Prefer the A sources.
5. **Mystra.** Greenwood, in a Q&A relayed on a fan page (C), says the Spellplague killed her. The Herald's blurb says she "has withdrawn from the world." Fan summaries (C) say she returns. No named official source confirms the final state.
6. **Is The Companions part of the Sundering?** One reviewer says it "never explains the Sundering" (thefoundingfields, B); reviews elsewhere call it the opening of the event. Treat it as a series tie-in whose plot barely touches the mechanics.
