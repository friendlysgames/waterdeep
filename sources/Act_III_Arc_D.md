# Act III: Fireball!

*3rd Level — Trollskull Alley, the Sea Ward & the North Ward*

---

<div class="fvtt advice">
    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/svg/book.svg">
    </figure>
    <article>
        <h4>The End of Peace</h4>
        <p>Act II ended with a mood: the characters had a home, a neighborhood, and people worth protecting. Lif poured drinks. The urchins knew their names. The guilds sent holiday greetings.</p>
        <p>On the morning of Ches 22nd, that changes in a fraction of a second.</p>
        <p>The fireball does not arrive as a campaign hook. It arrives as a catastrophe. It kills people the characters know. It detonates at their front door. It makes investigation not a plot obligation but a personal imperative — because the only way to protect what they have built is to understand what just tried to destroy it.</p>
        <p>Before running Act III, identify the NPC from Act II the players care most about — a neighbor, a guild contact, a regular at the tavern — and confirm that person is among the dead or grievously wounded in the blast. The investigation stops being optional the moment it becomes personal.</p>
    </article>
</div>

**Dramatic Question:** When a magical explosion detonates on their doorstep, killing their neighbors and a gnome spy carrying an ancient artifact, can the characters use forensic cunning, neighborhood contacts, and sheer determination to follow the evidence from the crater in Trollskull Alley to a noble estate under siege — and secure the *Stone of Golorr* before every faction in Waterdeep closes in around it?

**Inciting Incident:** The *fireball* detonates in Trollskull Alley at first light on Ches 22nd.

**Level Range:** The characters begin Act III at 3rd level and advance to 4th at the end of Arc E.

**Timeline:** The crime scene investigation unfolds over the morning of Ches 22nd. The nimblewright investigation takes one to three days depending on the route the characters choose. Urstul Floxin's strike team raids Gralhund Villa on the night of Ches 24th. The characters must reach the villa before the City Watch seals it permanently.

    <article>
        <h4>What Actually Happened</h4>
        <p>Dalakhar, a rock gnome spy working for the exiled Lord Dagult Neverember, had been attempting to reach Trollskull Manor to hand off the <em>Stone of Golorr</em> to the characters — adventurers who had already proven trustworthy by rescuing Renaer Neverember. He was being tracked simultaneously by Urstul Floxin's Zhentarim, the Xanathar Guild, the Cassalanters, and the Gralhund family.</p>
        <p>As Dalakhar came down Trollskull Alley toward the tavern's front door, Floxin and two Zhentarim sellswords moved to intercept him. The Gralhund nimblewright — a construct sold to House Gralhund by Jarlaxle Baenre's Sea Maidens Faire operation, armed with a <em>necklace of fireballs</em> by Lady Yalah Gralhund — was watching from a nearby rooftop. When it appeared Dalakhar might be taken before House Gralhund could claim the <em>Stone</em>, the nimblewright hurled a bead into the crowd below.</p>
        <p>The fireball killed Dalakhar, two of Floxin's agents, and nine bystanders. Floxin barely survived, burned and incapacitated. The nimblewright dropped from the roof, rifled through Dalakhar's pockets, took the <em>Stone of Golorr</em>, and fled east across the rooftops toward Gralhund Villa. Floxin, wounded and furious, identified the construct and followed it back to the estate. There, Lady Gralhund disarmed him at sword-point and confined him in one of the ground-floor rooms under guard.</p>
        <p>He did not stay confined. Within hours, Floxin killed his guards and escaped — without the Stone, which Gralhund refused to yield. He retreated to his Black Network safe house, assembled his full strike team, and began planning a return. That return is the Zhentarim raid in Arc E: Floxin arriving at the head of eight operatives on the night of Ches 24th, intending to take the villa by force.</p>
        <p>The adventure begins the moment the shockwave rattles every bottle behind the bar.</p>

</div>

# Arc D: The Nimblewright Investigation

*The Three Clue Trail from Trollskull Alley to Gralhund Villa*

---

<aside class="notable">
    <h4>Arc D at a Glance</h4>
    <p><strong>Structure:</strong> Web-of-Clues Investigation. Three independent forensic trails each lead to the same conclusion: a nimblewright belonging to House Gralhund fired the killing blast and took the <em>Stone of Golorr</em>. Any one trail is sufficient. No single failed check or missed NPC ends the investigation.</p>
    <p><strong>The Three Trails:</strong></p>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Trail A — The Crime Scene:</strong> Physical evidence, eyewitness testimony, and <em>Speak with Dead</em> identify the nimblewright and place it heading east toward the Sea Ward.</li>
        <li><strong>Trail B — The Temple of Gond:</strong> The construct's description leads to the House of Inspired Hands and its runaway creation, then to the Sea Maidens Faire as the source of all nimblewrights in the city.</li>
        <li><strong>Trail C — Renaer Neverember:</strong> The characters' friend confirms Dalakhar was his father's spy and that Urstul Floxin — a known Zhentarim operative — was seen entering Gralhund Villa after the blast.</li>
    </ul>
    <p><strong>Convergence:</strong> All three trails lead to one address: Gralhund Villa, Saerdoun Street, North Ward.</p>
</aside>

---

## The Explosion

The fireball detonates in the early morning of Ches 22nd, before the alley's foot traffic has fully gathered.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The sound hits before the light — a concussive boom that shakes the timbers of Trollskull Manor and sends every bottle behind the bar rattling. Then the windows go white. By the time you reach the door and wrench it open, the alley is a smear of black smoke, scattered cobblestones, and screaming. Bodies lie in the street. Eleven of them. One is very small, dressed in a burned cloak, and was clearly running toward your door.</p>
</div>

Let the players react freely. Characters who make a DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana) check recognize the residue as that of a *fireball* spell — or its equivalent, such as an incendiary bead.

The characters have a narrow window before the City Guard arrives and the scene is sealed. Every moment counts.

---

## Phase 1: The Crime Scene

The blast crater sits in the middle of Trollskull Alley, roughly thirty feet from the manor's front door. Broken cobblestones radiate outward in a scorch-darkened ring. Eleven bodies lie within or at the edges of the blast radius. Smoke still drifts from the blackened stones. Glass from every ground-floor window in a thirty-foot radius litters the street.

    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/skills/targeting/target-glowing-yellow.webp">

        <h4>The Investigation Window</h4>
        <p>The characters have approximately five minutes from the explosion before the first six City Guard soldiers appear and cordon off the alley. Twenty minutes after the blast, Barnibus Blastwind and Sergeant Saeth Cromley arrive with twenty constables and formally take charge. After that, approaching the bodies requires a DC 15 Dexterity (Stealth) check to remain unnoticed — or requesting access through official channels.</p>
        <p>The bodies are removed to the North Ward Watch Station by midmorning. A <em>Gentle Repose</em> spell is cast on each corpse. They remain available for <em>Speak with Dead</em> for the next five days.</p>

### The Dead

The blast killed eleven people:

- **One elderly female human** in a gray traveling cloak. No one recognizes her. She was taking her morning walk.
- **Two cloaked male humans** in leather armor, each with a sheathed longsword. One has a black, winged snake tattooed on his right forearm — the symbol of the Black Network.
- **Two female humans and one male half-elf** in plain clothes — servants of wealthy North Ward families, killed while running errands before the markets opened.
- **One male rock gnome** in a burned cloak, clutching a dagger in a scorched fist. His boots and the hem of his cloak are caked with dry gray silt — the distinctive residue of Waterdeep's sewer tunnels.
- **Four halflings** — two women who had been playing a flute and a fiddle, and two men who had been dancing to the music.

    <figure class="icon">

    <article>
        <h4>Making It Personal: The Twelfth Body</h4>
        <p>Before running this scene, add a twelfth victim: the NPC from Act II the players care most about. It might be one of the alley urchins, a neighbor who bought rounds at the tavern, a guild contact they grew fond of. Whoever it is, they are lying in the street. Do not narrate around it. Let it land.</p>
        <p>If no clear candidate exists, use one of the halfling musicians — name them, give them a specific detail (the fiddle player had a yellow ribbon tied around her bow) — and let that be enough. Grief does not require months of backstory. It requires one specific thing made vivid.</p>

### Searching the Bodies

A character who searches the bodies and succeeds on a DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check finds:

- One of the dead Zhentarim has a **winged snake tattoo** on his right forearm — the unmistakable brand of the Black Network.
- The gnome's boots are caked with **dried sewer silt**, suggesting he spent considerable time underground in the days before his death. He carries a **pouch containing five 100-gp gemstones**.

Barnibus Blastwind is not stupid, and Sergeant Cromley has been doing this a long time.</p>

### The Watch Arrives

Twenty minutes after the explosion, Sergeant **Saeth Cromley** escorts **Barnibus Blastwind** of the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors to the crime scene. Barnibus — a soft-spoken, meticulous dwarf mage in an ink-stained coat — quietly takes charge of the forensic work while Cromley directs twenty constables to knock on every door in the alley.

Barnibus reaches three conclusions he prefers to keep private:

- The gnome was running from armed pursuers — at least three of them. The third pursuer is not among the dead.
- The gnome and all three pursuers were moving toward the tavern at the end of the alley.
- None of them saw the blast coming. It originated from above and to the east.

Barnibus does not share these findings voluntarily. He has no interest in involving civilians in a Watch investigation. A character who presses him respectfully and succeeds on a DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion) check can nudge him into sharing his conclusions.

### The Eyewitnesses

Three people who were present in Trollskull Alley at the time of the blast have specific information to share. No ability check is needed to learn what they saw; they are shaken and willing to talk.

#### Fala Lefaliir

**Fala** (they/them), the wood elf herbalist who runs Corellon's Crown, was watering plants in their second-floor greenhouse when the blast blew out the windows.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"I was in the greenhouse when the windows just exploded inward. Glass everywhere. I'm lucky I wasn't killed. Through the smoke — I saw a cloaked man stoop over one of the bodies. The gnome, I think. He took something from the body. He was badly burned and he kept looking over his shoulder, like he was frightened. He was limping north toward the Bent Nail."</p>
</div>

Fala saw **Urstul Floxin**, wounded and retreating — the nimblewright had already taken the Stone; Floxin found the gnome empty-handed and limped away in furious confusion.

<aside class="notable">
    <h4>The Gralhund Back Story</h4>
    <p>The Gralhunds and Floxin's Zhentarim were not working together. Floxin's team was independently tracking Dalakhar at the same time the Gralhund nimblewright was watching from the rooftop. The fireball that killed the gnome killed most of Floxin's men in the same stroke. Floxin identified the nimblewright and followed it to the villa, coming to collect what he viewed as rightfully his. Lady Gralhund disagreed.</p>
    <p>Jarlaxle Baenre sold the nimblewright to the Gralhunds and is therefore aware of exactly what it witnessed and where it went — placing him ahead of every other faction, including Xanathar, who cannot confirm the Gralhund connection without the shipping records Jarlaxle holds.</p>
</aside>

#### Jezrynne Hornraven

A Waterdavian noblewoman born of wealth and little patience, Jezrynne had just departed the Tiger's Eye — Vincent Trench's detective office — when the blast hit.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"It was not a man. I want you to understand that. It was not a man. It was on the roof — right there, above the cooperage — and it was shaped like a man, but it moved wrong. Like a puppet on wires, only there were no wires. It had something in its hand. It threw something down into the street and that was the explosion. Then it just ran. Across the roof. East. Toward the Sea Ward."</p>
</div>

Jezrynne's description — a slight, articulated figure of metal and pale wood, moving with uncanny precision across the rooftops — matches no common creature. A character who succeeds on a DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana) check recognizes her description as consistent with a **nimblewright**, a rare Luskanite construct not previously known to operate in Waterdeep. If the characters attended the Day of Wonders parade in autumn, they may connect the description to the mechanical figures in the Temple of Gond's procession (no check required if they have lived in Waterdeep for a full year).

#### Martem Trec

Martem Trec is twelve years old. An hour ago he was watching Sarra and Duvall play fiddle and flute in the alley while their fathers danced. He knows all four of them by name. He has been sitting behind the rain barrel ever since and has not moved.

Finding Martem requires actively looking — he is not standing in the street with the other witnesses. A character who searches the alley or asks a neighbor about children will be pointed toward the rain barrel at the east end of the blast radius. Martem is crouched behind it with his arms around his knees, eyes dry now because he ran out of tears about twenty minutes ago.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He doesn't look up when you approach. When he does finally raise his head, his face is the face of someone who has seen something that should not be seen by anyone, let alone a child. He has ash on his cheek. His hands are shaking slightly. He says nothing for a moment. Then:</p>
    <p>"Duvall was standing right there." He points at the crater without looking at it. "He was trying to get Sarra to teach him the refrain. She kept saying he wasn't listening hard enough." He stops. "I hid. I heard them— I hid." He pulls something wet from the barrel beside him and holds it out without looking at it. "I found this in the water. After. Is it important? Do you need it?"</p>
</div>

The object is a *necklace of fireballs* with two beads remaining and a broken clasp, still dripping. As the nimblewright fled east across the rooftops it caught the chain on a chimney stack, tearing it free. The necklace slid off the roof's edge and fell into Martem's rain barrel.

Martem does not want to keep the necklace. He wants someone to take it away from him. A DC 8 Charisma (Persuasion) check — made with Advantage if any character shows genuine compassion rather than simply asking for the object — convinces him the characters are trustworthy enough to take it. On a failure, he pulls it back and holds it tighter, not because he wants it but because he is not ready to trust a stranger with something this dangerous.

He also saw the nimblewright. He will describe it if asked — quietly, precisely, the way children sometimes describe terrible things, without affect — as "a person made of metal, like a clock, that ran across the roof and dropped the bead that killed them and didn't look back at all, not even once."

The recovered *necklace of fireballs* has two beads remaining. Each bead can be detached and thrown as an action, detonating on impact in a 20-foot-radius sphere of fire. See the item's entry in the *Dungeon Master's Guide* (2024) for full rules. If the necklace itself takes fire damage, all remaining beads detonate simultaneously.

### Speaking with the Dead

The bodies of Dalakhar and the two Zhentarim sellswords are removed to the North Ward Watch Station by midmorning. Clerics from local temples cast *Gentle Repose* on each corpse, preserving them for up to five days.

Characters who wish to cast *Speak with Dead* can arrange access in one of three ways:

- **Faction Access:** A Renown of 1+ in the Harpers, Lords' Alliance, Order of the Gauntlet, or Force Grey lets a character petition their faction to arrange access and hire a cleric.
- **Direct Donation:** A donation of at least 25 gp to a local temple buys one casting, with the characters providing questions in advance.
- **Their Own Magic:** A character who knows *Speak with Dead* needs only access to the body, which requires a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check with Barnibus or Cromley.

#### What Dalakhar's Corpse Knows

*Speak with Dead* can extract up to five answers. The following can be provided if the right questions are asked:

- He stole an artifact called the *Stone of Golorr* from the lair of a beholder called **Xanathar**, deep in the dungeons beneath Waterdeep.
- He worked for the rightful Open Lord — by which he means Lord Dagult Neverember, not Laeral Silverhand.
- The *Stone of Golorr* is the key to finding a hidden hoard of gold dragons sealed somewhere in the city.
- He heard about adventurers who rescued Neverember's son and decided the *Stone* would be safe in their hands while he drew off his pursuers. He was running to their door when the fireball took him.
- He came to them because Renaer Neverember vouched for them. He trusted Renaer's judgment.

#### What the Dead Zhentarim Know

The two dead sellswords were named **Bashekk Ortallis** and **Wern Malkrave**. Each can answer up to five questions:

- They worked for **Urstul Floxin** and were operating in the North Ward.
- Their assignment was to intercept and take into custody a gnome named Dalakhar.
- According to Floxin, whatever Dalakhar was carrying would make them all "as rich as kings."
- They do not know where their billet was based or who arranged it.

### Consulting Renaer Neverember

Renaer is a regular at Trollskull Tavern and considers the characters friends. When he hears about the explosion — and he will, within hours — he comes to them directly.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"I know who he is. Dalakhar. He's one of my father's — one of my <em>estranged</em> father's — operatives. A spy. The man has followed me off and on for years, watching me on my father's behalf. I want nothing to do with any of it. But I spoke to some of Dalakhar's acquaintances once, when I was curious. Apparently he had been on some special mission to recover something called the <em>Stone of Golorr</em>. He thought the Zhentarim and the Xanathar Guild were about to catch him. When he heard what you had done for me — rescuing me from the Zhentarim — I think he decided you were trustworthy enough to hold it for him temporarily while he lost his pursuers. He was running to <em>you</em>."</p>
</div>

If the characters mention Fala's sighting of the burned man fleeing toward the Bent Nail, Renaer reaches out to his Harper contacts. Within a day, he returns with the following:

- The man Fala described matches **Urstul Floxin**, a known Black Network operative believed to be Manshoon's chief field agent in Waterdeep.
- A North Ward resident reported seeing Floxin, visibly burned and limping, enter **Gralhund Villa** on Saerdoun Street shortly after the explosion.
- Two Watch constables visited Lord Gralhund and were told politely that everything was fine. They had no grounds for a warrant.

## Phase 2: The Nimblewright Investigation

Once the characters have identified the construct responsible for the blast, their next task is to trace it to its origin and determine its current location. Three routes exist. Any one is sufficient.

### Route A: The Temple of Gond

The description Jezrynne provides — a slight humanoid construct of curved metal plates and pale wood, moving with uncanny precision — rings a bell for anyone who has seen the Day of Wonders parade. Those mechanical figures march in the procession of the **House of Inspired Hands**, the Temple of Gond on the corner of Seawatch Street and Shark Street in the Sea Ward.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The House of Inspired Hands looks less like a temple and more like a workshop that has been given a very tall ceiling and a pair of ceremonial doors. The toothed cog of Gond — four-spoked, polished brass — is mounted above the entrance and also appears, in miniature, on every doorknob and gutter spout. As you approach, a silhouette perches on the rooftop parapet: a humanoid shape, slight and still, arms extended. It releases something into the air — a small metal sparrow, wings beating in rapid, mechanical arcs. The bird loops twice and then banks directly toward you.</p>
</div>

The creature on the roof is **Nim**, a nimblewright created in Luskan and gifted to the temple by a visiting Lantanese artificer. Nim is not the guilty construct — but it built the one that is.

<div class="fvtt advice">
    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/skills/melee/maneuver-greatsword-yellow.webp">
    </figure>
    <article>
        <h4>Trap: The Mechanical Sparrow</h4>
        <p><strong>Trigger.</strong> A creature approaches within 60 feet of the temple entrance while Nim observes from the rooftop parapet.</p>
        <p><strong>Initiative.</strong> The sparrow acts on Initiative count 10.</p>
        <p><strong>Effect.</strong> Nim releases the sparrow, which flies at full speed (60 ft.) in a straight line toward the nearest creature. It makes one attack roll (+0 to hit) on that creature. <em>Hit:</em> 2 (1d3) piercing damage. <em>Miss or Hit:</em> The sparrow shatters on impact regardless of the result, and is destroyed.</p>
        <p><strong>Countermeasures.</strong> The sparrow can be swatted from the air with a successful melee attack roll against AC 14, or targeted with a ranged attack (AC 14, 1 hit point). A character who readies an action to intercept it destroys it before it reaches its target.</p>
        <p><strong>After the Sparrow.</strong> Nim vanishes through a secret rooftop hatch into the attic the moment the sparrow is released, hoping to avoid the consequences of its miscalculation.</p>
    </article>
</div>

#### Inside the Temple

The temple is open during daylight hours. Its main hall holds two dozen marble pedestals bearing prize-winning inventions: a 4-foot working clock tower that chimes every hour, a wooden flying machine with flapping wings, a miniature mechanical dragon turtle labeled *"Big Belchy — Sank in Deepwater Harbor on the Day of Wonders, 1363 DR,"* a "waking helmet" with tiny articulated arms that slap the wearer if they fall asleep, and a brass-plated model of a Lantanese submersible labeled *"The Scarlet Marpenoth — Launched 1489 DR."*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>A tall bronze dragonborn emerges from behind a curtained archway, wiping her hands on a cloth that was once white. Her scales are the warm amber-bronze of old coins, and her eyes — a clear amber yellow — take you in with a single assessing sweep. She wears the toothed cog of Gond as a collar clasp, and carries the faint smell of machine oil and soldering flux with the ease of a woman who considers it perfume. She has the compact, deliberate movements of someone who has spent decades navigating crowded workshops. Her gaze moves from face to face before settling, as if she has already decided something about each of you and is filing it away for later. "I am Valetta," she says. "I suspect this is about the figure on the roof."</p>
</div>

**Valetta** — the temple's senior cleric and unofficial liaison with the outside world — is patient, methodical, and protective of the temple's reputation. If the characters describe the figure they saw on the roof, she identifies it immediately as Nim and escorts them up a spiral staircase to the attic workshop. She finds the attic door locked with a mechanism she does not recognize — new since the last time she was up here, which tells her something about Nim's state of mind.

The lock can be picked with a successful DC 20 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) check, opened with a *Knock* spell, or unlocked by Nim itself if the characters persuade it through the door (DC 17 Charisma (Persuasion) check; Valetta's firm encouragement grants Advantage).

#### Nim's Attic

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The attic is packed floor to rafter with half-finished contraptions: copper tubing coiled like vines, gear-trains suspended on wire racks, a dozen projects in various stages of assembly. Some of it is recognizable — a music box here, a piston mechanism there. Most of it is not. In the corner, half-hidden behind a rack of delicate clockwork, something the size of a small child crouches and watches you with expressionless brass eyes.</p>
</div>

Nim understands Common but cannot speak. It communicates in **Common Sign Language** — the same gestural language used by the deaf and hard-of-hearing communities throughout Waterdeep and the Sword Coast. Valetta has learned enough to hold basic conversations with Nim; she can translate, though she misses nuance and occasionally asks Nim to repeat itself.

<aside class="notable">
    <h4>Players Who Speak Common Sign Language</h4>
    <p>Any character who selected Common Sign Language as one of their languages can communicate with Nim directly, without Valetta as intermediary. Nim, who spends most of its existence relying on a partial translator, will visibly respond to being spoken to fluently. It signs faster, more precisely, and with evident relief. It may share details with a fluent signer that it would not bother conveying through Valetta's approximate translation — the emotional texture of its loneliness, the pride it took in its creation, the guilt it has carried since the construct fled.</p>
    <p>Give the player a moment. Nim has been waiting a long time to have a real conversation.</p>
</aside>

Through patient communication, the characters can learn:

- Nim built a second nimblewright roughly three months ago to ease its loneliness. The creation panicked — the result of an incomplete behavioral lattice — and fled the temple grounds a month ago.
- Nim has been trying to find it ever since but cannot leave the temple grounds.
- Nim built a device to locate its creation. It never had the opportunity to use it.

As Valetta angrily orders acolytes to begin clearing Nim's tools from the attic, a character who searches the workshop finds a **1-foot-long copper contraption** with an umbrella-like metallic protrusion at one end. A *Detect Magic* spell reveals a strong aura of Divination magic. Nim allows the characters to take it.

<div class="fvtt advice">
    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/weapons/swords/sword-jeweled-red.webp">
    </figure>
    <article>
        <h4>Nimblewright Detector</h4>
        <p><em>Wondrous Item, Uncommon (requires attunement)</em></p>
        <p>A foot-long copper instrument, tarnished to a warm amber-green patina, with a telescoping handle wrapped in cracked leather and an umbrella-like array of delicate brass fins at one end. Each fin is engraved with a different arcane symbol. A small trigger lever is recessed into the grip, worn smooth from Nim's repeated testing. The whole device hums faintly at the threshold of hearing when held.</p>
        <p>While attuned to this device, you can use a Magic Action to activate it. While active, the brass fins begin to spin, click, and whir whenever the device comes within 500 feet of a nimblewright other than the one that built it. The spinning and clicking increase in speed and volume as the nearest nimblewright gets closer, reaching maximum intensity when a nimblewright is within 30 feet. The device makes no sound when inactive and can be pocketed without drawing attention.</p>
        <p>Activating or deactivating the device requires a Magic Action.</p>
    </article>
</div>

#### Reward

Valetta offers **500 gp** for proof of the escaped nimblewright's destruction, plus one invention of the characters' choosing: adjustable stilts, a backpack parachute (reaction to deploy while falling; prevents all falling damage; must be repacked in 10 minutes), a barking box (a metal cube that barks when it detects vibrations within 15 feet; activates for 8 hours per winding), or a matchless pipe.

---

#### Finding the Nimblewright

Armed with the detector, the characters conduct a ward-by-ward search of the city. When they enter the North Ward and walk toward Saerdoun Street, the device activates — clicking and spinning with increasing urgency as they close on **Gralhund Villa**.

Before that happens, the detector may lead them somewhere else entirely.

---

### The Dock Ward Distraction

When the characters sweep the Dock Ward with the nimblewright detector active, it begins to whir and spin well before they reach the North Ward. The signal grows stronger as they move toward the waterfront, then plateaus — coming from somewhere out over the harbor and from two points along the quays.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The Dock Ward hits you before you see it: the smell of brine and rendered tallow and the sour undertone of fish that has spent too long in the sun. The cobblestones here are perpetually damp, slick with spray and spillage from the morning's unloading. Stevedores haul crates up gangplanks, gulls wheel and shriek over the quay-heads, and the masts of a hundred ships make a bare forest against the gray sky. Your device is spinning. Not subtly — it is nearly vibrating out of your grip, and the clicking has become something close to a continuous tone. You are close to a nimblewright. Possibly more than one.</p>
</div>

The detector is reacting to **three ships** belonging to the Sea Maidens Faire, a carnival operation that arrived in Waterdeep from Luskan several weeks ago: the *Heartbreaker* and *Hellraiser*, two galleons tied at a rented pier on the south side of the Dock Ward, and the *Eyecatcher*, the operation's flagship, anchored some distance out in Deepwater Harbor. Each vessel carries at least one nimblewright.

---

#### The Pier

The Sea Maidens Faire has erected a modest carnival on the pier between the two docked galleons — painted stalls and bright-colored canvas awnings wedged between coils of rope and dock equipment. A strongman demonstrates feats of grip strength with iron rings. A fortune-teller in an elaborately embroidered coat reads cards from a velvet-draped table. Two acrobats in silver-sequined costumes work a low balance rope strung between two posts. The carnival smells of spiced sausages and sawdust and the particular oversweet tang of cheap perfume applied in quantity.

The staff are all Luskanite and answer questions about the nimblewrights with cheerful evasion: *Oh, those are just part of the show. Captain Zord likes mechanical things. They're not for sale. The Captain handles all inquiries personally.*

**Captain Zord's Office** is accessible — he keeps regular hours and is known to enjoy meeting interesting visitors. His real name is **Jarlaxle Baenre**. He does not advertise this.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>His office takes up a generous cabin at the stern of the <em>Heartbreaker</em>, decorated with the kind of deliberate excess that suggests wealth that doesn't need to prove itself but enjoys doing so anyway: deep blue rugs from Calimshan, a pair of matched silver candlesticks shaped like dancing women, a drinks cabinet with crystal decanters in three colors. The man seated behind the desk is dressed in carnival master's attire — a deep crimson coat, a hat that is several inches taller than strictly necessary, one eye covered by a jeweled patch that catches the light as he turns to greet you. His smile arrives before the rest of his expression catches up. He looks at each of you in turn with the particular attention of a person who finds people genuinely interesting rather than merely useful.</p>
    <p>"Welcome aboard. I was wondering when someone would come asking." He does not explain what he means by that. He gestures toward the chairs across the desk. "Sit. Tell me what brings you to the Sea Maidens Faire."</p>
</div>

If the characters ask Captain Zord about the nimblewright, he listens with evident interest, excuses himself for a few minutes, and returns with the information that the construct was purchased by House Gralhund of the North Ward. He provides the address with a smile that suggests he already knew the answer before he left the room.

Asking Zord directly is easy. The consequence is that they have now told Jarlaxle Baenre everything he needs to know about their investigation and their intentions.

#### The Ledger of Nimblewright Sales

Whether found through a search of the *Heartbreaker* (the partial version) or received directly from Jarlaxle (the complete version), the ledger is a significant intelligence find.

The **partial ledger** — recovered from the locked officer's cabin aboard the *Heartbreaker* — is a slim folio of waxed vellum pages, each ruled into columns. Most of the entries have been removed with a blade: entire rows excised cleanly, leaving nothing but bare column lines and the occasional fragment of ink at the margin. Nine rows total. Seven have been cut out entirely. Two remain.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The folio falls open to a pair of surviving rows amid the ghost-lines of seven others. The columns read: <em>Unit Reference — Crystal Identifier — Dispersal Date — Buyer — Address — Final Balance</em>. The first surviving entry: <em>NW-01 / CR-01 / Marpenoth 3 / B. Summertaen / Watchful Order, Castle Ward / 25,000 gp</em>. The second is more interesting. The buyer's name has been cut away — the blade went through the vellum so cleanly there's nothing left but a sliver of the column line — but the rest survived: <em>NW-03 / CR-03 / Marpenoth 12 / [excised] / Yellowspire, Castle Ward / 25,000 gp</em>. Seven other rows are simply gone.</p>
</div>

The partial ledger tells the characters that at least two nimblewrights were sold in Waterdeep in Marpenoth of last year: one to the Watchful Order of Magists, one to an unknown buyer associated with Yellowspire in the Castle Ward. It confirms that a network of these constructs exists in the city — but NW-02, the Gralhund entry, is one of the seven rows that has been cut out. The Gralhund connection cannot be confirmed from this document alone.

#### The *Eyecatcher*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>A quarter-mile out in the harbor, riding at anchor, the flagship makes the two docked ships look modest. Carved figureheads — a trio of laughing women in flowing stone dresses — decorate the prow. The flagship rides at anchor a quarter-mile out in Deepwater Harbor. No public gangplank connects it to the shore. Reaching it without a launch requires hiring passage from one of the harbor skiffs (2 sp, departing every hour from the harbor steps) or swimming (DC 12 Strength (Athletics); the water is cold and the current awkward). The crew will not allow unauthorized visitors aboard. Getting aboard requires a credible cover story (DC 16 Charisma (Deception)), an invitation from Captain Zord, or a clandestine approach at night (DC 14 Dexterity (Stealth) to reach the hull unseen; DC 16 to board without alerting the watch crew).

Below the *Eyecatcher*, connected to the ship's keel by a docking collar, is a Lantanese submersible called the **Scarlet Marpenoth**. The characters cannot see it from the surface. The partial inventory on the *Heartbreaker* also references a "submersible annex."

### Route B: Canvassing and Research

Characters who pursue the nimblewright lead through research or canvassing rather than going to the temple directly can still reach the same conclusion.

**Arcana Research (DC 13 Intelligence):** The construct is a nimblewright — a rare Luskanite creation based on ancient Calishite designs, not previously documented operating in Waterdeep. On a DC 17 success, the character also learns that **Bowgentra Summertaen**, Lady Master of the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors, is known to have recently acquired one of these constructs.

**Canvassing (DC 13 Charisma):** A character who spends an hour asking around the neighborhood learns of individuals who own nimblewrights. All owners, when questioned, reveal the same source: **Captain Zord** of the Sea Maidens Faire.

### Backtracking Dalakhar

Characters who want to understand why Dalakhar was in Trollskull Alley may trace his recent movements. A successful DC 15 Charisma (Investigation) check, made over a day's inquiry through inns and lodging houses in the Trades Ward, reveals he rented a room for one night at the **Inn of the Dripping Dagger** in the Trades Ward and then departed.

#### The Inn of the Dripping Dagger

The Inn of the Dripping Dagger sits on Snail Street in the Trades Ward — a blocky, unremarkable building squeezed between a tallow chandler's shop and a money-changer's office, its sign a painted dagger with three teardrop-shaped red drips that could be blood or could be rubies. It attracts traveling merchants, minor officials passing through the city on business, couriers, and the occasional person who wants a clean room without being asked too many questions.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The common room is low-ceilinged and smells of tallow candles and the day's soup, which appears to have been cabbage. Four round tables occupy the floor; a fifth has been pushed against the wall to make room for a travelling merchant's sample case. A barkeep — a stout middle-aged woman with ink-stained fingers, currently reading something behind the counter — looks up when you enter. The hearth is going. The light is adequate. It is, in every respect, a perfectly ordinary inn.</p>
</div>

The proprietor is **Myl Dunpier**, a practical woman who takes no interest in her guests' business and considerable interest in being paid on time. She remembers Dalakhar clearly: a rock gnome, traveled alone, paid in coin, asked for an upper-floor room with a window visible from the street. He left before breakfast on Ches 22nd, which struck her as odd given he had prepaid through the evening.

Getting access to Dalakhar's room requires one of the following:

- **Direct Request:** A DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) check, combined with a truthful explanation that the gnome is dead, softens Myl. She will allow access if the characters promise not to remove anything — and if she can accompany them.
- **Watch Authority:** A Renown of 1+ in any faction, or a Watch badge obtained during the crime scene, automatically gains entry.
- **Bribery:** 5 gp buys access without conversation. 1 gp does not.
- **Stealth:** A DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check while walking the upper hallway locates the room (it smells faintly of sewer — Dalakhar's boots). A DC 14 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) check on the door lock enters it without Myl's knowledge.

#### Dalakhar's Room

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The room is small and entirely unremarkable — a bed, a window, a writing table, a wash basin. Everything has been straightened with mechanical precision: the sheets tucked tight, the basin dried, the candle burned down to a stub and the wax drippings scraped away. Someone cleaned this room. </div>

The room has been scrubbed by Dalakhar before he left — a professional habit. A character who searches it and succeeds on a DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) check finds:

- At the base of the room's oil lamp, inside the reservoir housing, a thin residue of fine gray ash clings to the metal. A piece of correspondence was burned here. It cannot be reconstituted by nonmagical means. Dalakhar burned it himself before leaving — a professional precaution. Its contents are unknowable without magic.
- The underside of the writing table has a faint scratch pattern where something small was hidden. Whatever was there has been taken with him.
- The window latch shows faint tooling marks on the exterior side, as if someone tested whether it could be opened from outside. The marks are not recent — perhaps two days old.

On the pillow, however, nothing has been erased. Lying in plain sight, placed there deliberately, is a **disk of black stone** roughly the size of a large coin — flat, polished, and painted on one face with a single stylized eye: a ringed iris with a vertical slit pupil. It is Xanathar's sigil.

### Kalain's Letter

Four days after Dalakhar's death, a letter arrives at the Inn of the Dripping Dagger addressed to him. If the characters left their names with Myl Dunpier and asked her to contact them if anything arrived, she sends a street child to Trollskull Manor with it. Otherwise, it sits uncollected behind the bar until the characters follow up.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The letter is written on good paper in a cramped, slightly erratic hand:</p>
    <p><em>Dalakhar,</em></p>
    <p><em>I had to give considerable thought to your request. But you were always kind to me even when your demonic master was not. If you are still in need of my aid, you may claim whatever sanctuary I can offer.</em></p>
    <p><em>Kalain of the Nine Waters</em></p>
</div>

Before he was killed, Dalakhar had been casting about desperately for any safe harbor he could find. He even contacted Kalain — a former mistress of Lord Dagult Neverember's, now largely estranged from polite society — on the slim hope that her old affection for him might translate into shelter. Her reply arrived too late.

A character who succeeds on a DC 12 Intelligence (History) check, or who asks around the Sea Ward, can identify Kalain as a once-celebrated Waterdavian painter. Her address is findable with a day's inquiry. She lives alone in a dilapidated tower not far from the harbor.

---

#### Kalain

**Appearance:** A beauty marred by years of grief; deep lines of sadness etched into a face that was once painted by half the artists in the city. Long black hair streaked through with silver going to gray.

**Roleplaying:** Believes everyone she meets is secretly an assassin sent by Lord Dagult to finish what he started. Loses the thread of conversations mid-sentence and resurfaces talking about something entirely different. Rubs her cheek with increasing vigor as she grows distressed. Calls animated figures from her smaller, older, more peaceful paintings to fetch things or run small errands. Speaks of Neverwinter as if the city were a red-headed woman who seduced Lord Dagult from her arms. Occasionally confuses Neverwinter with Alethea Brandath, Neverember's late wife.

**Background:** Kalain was commissioned to paint Lord Dagult Neverember's portrait in 1475 DR. The commission became a torrid affair lasting over a year. Dagult's attention drifted to Neverwinter; he made promises he didn't keep; when she pressed him, he treated her cruelly. Kalain's paintings turned dark — monsters that, in her mind, all wore Dagult's face. He used his political influence to ruin her. She has been alone in her tower for years. The onset of madness was gradual and is now complete.

Her paintings are alive. The newer ones — the monsters — are dangerous. The older ones — peaceful landscapes, bowls of fruit, sleeping cats — produce harmless animated figures she uses as company and servants.

**What Kalain Knows:**

- Dalakhar was a spy working for Lord Neverember. She will say so openly, and also that she is glad he's dead (meaning Neverember — it takes a moment to clarify which dead man she is talking about).
- If told Dalakhar himself was killed, she immediately accuses the characters of killing him on Neverember's orders and rapidly escalates toward concluding they are here to kill her. A DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) check can talk her back, though she will remain watchful.
- During their final days together, Lord Neverember was obsessed with something he called a "Melairkyn ceremonial vault." He talked about it constantly. She cannot now remember why it mattered so much to him — the specifics feel burned away, like a word she cannot quite retrieve. (This is the Stone of Golorr's influence, still active in its current state. No one remembers the vault's location without the Stone's guidance.)
- Lord Victoro Cassalanter visited her a few weeks ago, asking about Neverember and the vault. She won't volunteer this without prompting; asked directly, she confirms it.

**Lord Dagult's Papers:** Kalain has a lockbox containing correspondence from her time with Neverember (DC 13 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) check to open). Among the papers:

- Love letters she exchanged with Dagult. A second drawer holds similar letters, torn to confetti. She periodically removes a letter from the first drawer, tears it up, and adds it to the second. This has been going on for years.
- A crossed-out task list that includes the entry *"move the dragon to the Melairkyn ceremonial vault."*
- Correspondence with Hammond Kraddoc of the Vintners', Distillers', and Brewers' Guild confirming that Kraddoc paid Lord Dagult large bribes to cover up a contaminated-liquor scandal in the Dock Ward.
- Notes on the Melairkyn dwarves, their cult of Dumathoin, and ceremonial vaults built to contain the god's secrets. A vault, according to an ancient source Neverember was citing, *"can be opened by standing before Dumathoin's doors and striking the scale of a dragon with a mithral hammer in the place where the sun's light should fall."*
- An unsigned letter written to Neverember four years ago: *"The last of the three Eyes has been secured."*

These papers are among the most valuable intelligence finds in the entire investigation. The note about the Eyes confirms that all three were in Neverember's hands four years ago — and that he deliberately separated them from the Stone.

## Choosing a Course of Action

By the time the investigation concludes, the characters know — through one, two, or all three routes — that Gralhund Villa is their destination. Their options:

**Let the Watch Handle It:** The characters can report what they know to any North Ward Watch station. Barnibus Blastwind and Sergeant Cromley will listen, corroborate the intelligence, obtain a warrant, and conduct the raid. When they arrive, they will find the aftermath: Lord Gralhund unconscious, Lady Gralhund in shock, Hrabbaz bloodied but unbowed. Urstul Floxin and the nimblewright are both gone. The *Stone of Golorr* is nowhere to be found. Cromley will visit the characters afterward as a courtesy and summarize these findings. The investigation hits a dead end.

**Go Themselves:** The characters take matters into their own hands.

<aside class="notable">
    <h4>Why Going Themselves Matters</h4>
    <p>The characters have no legal standing to raid a noble estate, and a direct assault risks arrest, fines, and a permanent stain on their reputation in the city. At the same time, the Watch raid yields nothing — the Stone will be gone before the warrant is processed. Characters who investigate thoroughly and act decisively will reach the villa while the situation is still fluid: preparation, timing, and the willingness to act without official sanction are exactly what this moment demands.</p>

### What the Factions Say

If the characters are uncertain and consult their faction contacts before acting, each organization has a distinct perspective:

**Mirt the Moneylender (Harpers):** Mirt listens to the full account, refills his own glass, and thinks for a moment. *"The Watch will take three days to process a warrant on a noble house. Whatever's in there will be gone in three hours. You know this."* He does not tell them to break in. He tells them the Harpers' interest in whatever Dalakhar was carrying is genuine, and that if they bring it to him before anyone else, there will be significant gratitude from people whose gratitude is worth having. *"I won't tell you what to do. I will tell you that the Watch's involvement ends this. One way or another."*

**Tashlyn Yafeera (Doom Raiders):** With Davil in Watch custody, Tashlyn is running the cell. She is direct. *"If Floxin is inside that house and Manshoon's prize is attached to whatever the gnome was carrying, we want to know before he does. Go in, get out, bring back whatever you find. We will handle the consequences. And if you can bring back proof that Floxin ordered the fireball, I will personally make sure Davil walks out of that Watch station."*

**Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance):** Jalester is the only contact who explicitly cautions against unauthorized entry. *"I cannot instruct you to break into a noble residence. That is not something the Alliance sanctions."* Pause. *"What I can tell you is that the Open Lord has a strong interest in whatever Dalakhar was carrying, and that a timely retrieval would be of significant value to the city. I can also tell you that certain Watch captains in the North Ward are sympathetic to private citizens who act in the city's best interest, and that I know which ones."* He offers to arrange a meeting with Watch Captain Saeth Cromley, who will agree to look the other way if the characters sign a formal statement agreeing to share all intelligence with the Watch and the Open Lord's office within 48 hours. This is the cleanest legal path to the villa. It is also the slowest.

**Vajra Safahr (Force Grey):** Vajra is direct and unhesitating. *"You are not Force Grey operatives, which means I cannot authorize you and I cannot protect you from the legal consequences if this goes wrong. What I can tell you is that this city has a half-million gold dragons missing from its treasury, that a beholder crime lord is involved, and that the Watch's warrant process will take longer than we have. You have information the Watch doesn't. You have mobility the Watch doesn't. If you go in tonight and you bring me whatever you find, I will speak personally to the Open Lord on your behalf."* She does not wait for an answer. *"I would go myself, but that is not how Waterdeep works. Not openly."*

**Jarlaxle Baenre / Captain Zord (Bregan D'Aerthe):** If any characters are D'Aerthe operatives and have already made contact at the Sea Maidens Faire, he will have reached out by now — a discreet note delivered by a carnival acrobat, suggesting they meet aboard the *Heartbreaker* at their earliest convenience. In person, he is straightforward about his position: he wants to know who ends up holding the Stone and would like a seat at that table. He does not offer to accompany them. He offers the complete Ledger of Nimblewright Sales (if they do not already have it), a description of the villa's layout based on the nimblewright's clairvoyance crystal recordings, and the information that Xanathar's gazers are watching the exterior. *"You'll want to know about those before you go in."* He asks them to remember this conversation when they decide what to do with the Stone. For D'Aerthe characters specifically, he frames it as an official assignment: *"This would be a very good time to earn your rank."*

### If They Don't Go: Where the Stone Ends Up

If the characters decline to act, the Stone of Golorr does not stay at Gralhund Villa waiting for them.

        <h4>The Stone's Fate Without PC Intervention</h4>
        <p>The raid timeline is fixed: Floxin's strike team arrives on the night of Ches 24th, regardless of what the characters do. Shortly after, Jarlaxle's team moves in from Artheyn Manor.</p>
        <p><strong>If the characters never go to the villa:</strong> Floxin's team raids the house on Ches 24th. In the chaos, Jarlaxle's drow enter through the balcony, use Orond as leverage, and Yalah surrenders the Stone. Bregan D'Aerthe departs before dawn. The Watch seals the villa the following morning. From this point forward, the Stone is in Jarlaxle's possession aboard the <em>Eyecatcher</em> — and the characters must plan an entirely different kind of operation to recover it.</p>
        <p><strong>If the Watch raids the villa (characters report but don't act):</strong> The Watch arrives with a warrant on Ches 26th. They find twenty-three bodies, Lord Gralhund in a shocked stupor, Lady Gralhund absent, Hrabbaz dead. No Stone, no nimblewright, no Floxin. Cromley visits the characters with this summary. The trail is cold. Jarlaxle arranges a meeting.</p>
        <p><strong>The silver lining:</strong> In either case, the campaign is not over. Jarlaxle holding the Stone is a different story — one in which the characters must reckon with him directly. But the leverage they would have had by arriving first is gone. They arrive at the negotiating table empty-handed, and Jarlaxle knows it.</p>
