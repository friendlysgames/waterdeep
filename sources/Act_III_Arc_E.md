## Overview

Gralhund Villa sits on Saerdoun Street in an upper-class residential neighborhood in the North Ward. It is enclosed by a 12-foot-high stone wall and consists of a two-story brick mansion, a detached coach house, a guard barracks, and a well-tended yard with large shade trees. All ceilings inside the mansion are 20 feet high.

### General Facts

- The streets around the villa carry foot traffic and coaches at all hours, with heavier activity during the day.
- The enclosure walls require a successful DC 15 Strength (Athletics) check to scale without climbing gear or magic.
- The nimblewright has already left the villa by the time the characters arrive. The detector will not react inside the walls.

## Scouting the Villa

Characters who take time to observe the villa before entering will be rewarded for their patience. A character who spends at least an hour watching the exterior can learn the following without a check:

- The front gates are iron and locked.
- Two guards stand post inside the gates during the day, visible through the bars.
- The coach house has a side door accessible from the east lane.
- The enclosure wall can be climbed — it is tall but has no glass or spikes.
- There is a balcony on the second floor accessible from the yard.

A character who spends two hours watching, or who succeeds on a DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check during any period of observation, additionally learns:

- The guards change shift at noon and again at dusk.
- The side door of the coach house is often unlatched during the day.
- There are mastiffs on the grounds, though they are not visible during daylight hours.
- A groundskeeper in a broad hat appears at dusk and begins a slow circuit of the yard.

Characters who specifically attempt to gather intelligence about the interior — by talking to tradespeople who make deliveries, bribing a servant, or consulting guild blueprints — can learn the general floor plan. The Carpenters', Roofers', and Plasterers' Guild and the Cellarers' and Plumbers' Guild are most likely to have construction records. Obtaining records requires a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check and either a plausible cover story or a donation of at least 10 gp. Records show the first floor layout accurately but do not include the upper floor in detail.

## Observation Teams

Three factions are watching Gralhund Villa from the outside before the characters arrive.

**The Zhentarim Watchers** are two Toughs stationed on the rooftop of the building directly across Saerdoun Street. They have been watching since Floxin retreated here after the fireball. They will send a flying snake to Floxin the moment they detect the characters entering the compound (DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) to spot them). Floxin will assemble his strike team and arrive within 10+2d6 minutes of being alerted.

**Jarlaxle's Team** is billeted in **Artheyn Manor**, the vacant townhouse immediately north of the villa. The Artheyn family patriarch has relocated to the Moonshae Isles; drop cloths cover most of the furniture. Fel'Rekt Lafeen, two additional Drow Gunslingers, and six Drow occupy a ground-floor watching post facing the villa's gate and an upper-floor observation room with sightlines to the balcony. A letter carelessly left on the ground-floor side table is signed "Jarlaxle Baenre" and instructs Fel'Rekt to watch the villa and seize any opportunity to take possession of the *Stone of Golorr*, with updates transmitted twice daily to Fenerus Stormcastle via message drop.

**Xanathar's Gazers** — two gazers — are concealed in shadowed alcoves across from the villa. They do not engage in combat. They track anyone who leaves the premises and will follow the party if they depart with the Stone.

## Day Encounter Areas

### G1. Locked Gates (Day)

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Wrought iron gates, each panel worked into an ornate House Gralhund crest — a crowned serpent on a field of stars — stand between stone pillars at the head of a flagstone path. Beyond them, a graveled drive curves through a yard of old-growth trees toward the face of a two-story brick mansion. A second path branches east toward a low stone outbuilding. The gates are locked. Through the bars you can see everything clearly — and so, presumably, can anyone on the other side.</p>
</div>

An *Arcane Lock* spell has been cast on the gates. Forcing them open requires a successful DC 25 Strength (Athletics) check; picking the lock requires a successful DC 20 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) check; a *Knock* spell or similar magic also opens them. The spell does not impede members of the Gralhund family, their staff, their guards, or the nimblewright.

Two Gralhund Guards stand post just inside. They challenge anyone who approaches and cannot be fast-talked without a DC 15 Charisma (Deception or Persuasion) check — and even success here only buys an escort to the main door, not unimpeded access.

<div class="fvtt narrative">

<div class="fvtt narrative">

### G9. Parlor (Day)

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>This room is furnished for the comfort of those waiting on House Gralhund's pleasure: dainty armchairs upholstered in green velvet, a chaise longue near the window, a tall wine cabinet with a lead crystal decanter of red wine and four matching glasses set out on its shelf as if company is expected momentarily. Framed oil portraits of long-dead Gralhunds hang on three walls, their subjects rendered with studied dignity.</p>
</div>

A maid dusts the portraits and tidies the room in the morning. Visitors who are escorted to the house are typically asked to wait here until Lord or Lady Gralhund is available.

**Meeting the Gralhunds:** Characters who gain authorized access to the villa — whether through deception, a legitimate pretext, or faction credentials — will be kept waiting in this room for up to ten minutes before either Lord Orond or Lady Yalah appears. Which of them comes depends on the stated purpose of the visit; Yalah handles anything political or financial, Orond handles anything related to the household's physical operations or legal matters.

Both Gralhunds are skilled at this kind of meeting. They will not volunteer information. They will not be rude. They will be surgically unhelpful.

**If Orond Receives Them:**

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>A broad-shouldered man in a well-cut coat enters with the careful confidence of someone who has decided in advance not to be surprised by anything today. He pours himself a glass of wine without offering one, settles into the armchair across from you, and regards you with the patient attention of a man who considers your visit an inconvenience he intends to be done with quickly. "Lord Orond Gralhund," he says, as if you might not have known. "What can I do for you?"</p>
</div>

Orond listens to whatever the characters present, asks one or two clarifying questions that reveal nothing, and then tells them — pleasantly, immovably — that he cannot help them. He does not acknowledge the explosion, Dalakhar, the nimblewright, or Urstul Floxin by name unless directly confronted with evidence he cannot plausibly deny. If presented with such evidence, he grows visibly uncomfortable but maintains his position: he has no knowledge of any of it, he deeply regrets the tragedy on Trollskull Alley, and if the Watch requires a statement he is of course available at their convenience. A DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check reveals that he is frightened, not contemptuous — something has gone wrong in this house and he is desperately hoping these particular visitors will leave before they notice.

**If Yalah Receives Them:**

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Lady Yalah Gralhund is shorter than the portraits suggest, and more watchful. She comes in without announcement, takes the chair nearest the door, and sits with the upright precision of someone who was trained from childhood to occupy space with intention. She is wearing riding clothes. She looks at each of you in turn — not aggressively, simply with the thorough attention of a woman who finds people easier to deal with once she has taken their measure. "You wanted to speak with someone. Here I am." A beat. "Tea?"</p>
</div>

Yalah is more dangerous than Orond in this setting. She is not frightened; she is calculating. She listens to everything the characters say and gives them exactly as much as she judges they already know, framed to make it seem like cooperation. If they mention the nimblewright, she expresses polite bewilderment — her household bought an automaton from the Sea Maidens Faire carnival; it performed functions in the house; she has not seen it recently; how distressing. If they mention the fireball, she offers her sincere condolences for the tragedy and notes that Trollskull Alley is some distance from the villa. If they produce the necklace bead or describe the construct's rooftop position, her expression does not change, but a character who succeeds on a DC 18 Wisdom (Insight) check sees the precise moment she decides they know enough to be a problem.

She will not incriminate herself. She will not panic. She will, if pressed hard enough, ask Hrabbaz to show the characters out — firmly, but without violence unless they resist.

**What Either Will Confirm Without Pressure:**

- House Gralhund recently purchased an automaton servant from the Sea Maidens Faire.
- The automaton has not been in the household for several days. Its absence is unusual.
- They are not aware of any connection between their household and the Trollskull Alley explosion.

**What Requires a Check to Extract (DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation)):**

- The automaton was equipped for security purposes, including defensive capabilities. Orond will admit this; Yalah will not.
- The automaton was last dispatched on a specific errand on the morning of Ches 22nd. Neither will say what the errand was.

## The Zhentarim Raid Timeline

Each step notes where **Floxin** is — track his position carefully, as it determines the shape of the encounter when the characters arrive.

**Step 1 — Entry (G6, G5):** Floxin and eight operatives scale the rear wall and pick the pantry door. Inside, they surprise the head butler and cook. The cook bolts for the kitchen screaming; both servants die within seconds. *Floxin is at the rear of the column, directing the entry.*

**Step 2 — The Laundry (G7, back stairs):** The head maid hears the screaming and investigates. She is killed on the back stair. Her screams alert guards in G8; one breaks for the barracks. Three guards move into G7. *Floxin pushes forward through G6 toward G8's south entrance.*

**Step 3 — The Hall Rush (G8, G9):** With guards drawn into G7, Floxin rushes his team through the unguarded south end of the great hall. Two guards patrolling G9 walk out and are killed. Lord Orond, reading late in G12, hears the noise, opens the library door, locks eyes with Floxin in the hall below, and runs for the stairs. *Floxin is in G8, coordinating the push.*

## The Jarlaxle Intervention

Fel'Rekt Lafeen's team will make their move regardless of whether the characters are present. The only question is when.

**Default Timing (No PC Intervention):** Once the surviving Gralhund guards have killed the last Zhentarim operatives and are reduced to four or fewer, the drow act. Two Drow Gunslingers open fire through the G13 windows with sniper crossbows. Simultaneously, Fel'Rekt and his team drop from the Artheyn roofline onto the G17 balcony and force the glass doors.

Lady Yalah flees to G18 with her children. Fel'Rekt duels Hrabbaz — a brutal fight that Hrabbaz loses. The drow drag Orond from G15b into G16. Through the locked children's door, Fel'Rekt offers a simple trade: the Stone for Orond's life. Yalah, with her children listening on the other side of the door, eventually surrenders it. The drow retreat across the rooftops as the City Watch comes through the front gate.

**With PC Intervention:** The situation is volatile and the drow respond to new variables.

- If the characters help the guards destroy the Zhentarim, Fel'Rekt moves up his timeline — the drow may enter while the characters are still in the villa.
- If the characters side with the Zhents, the surviving guards lock G16 more effectively, and Fel'Rekt may wait to take the Stone from whoever wins.
- If the characters secure the Stone and attempt to leave, Jarlaxle's team follows them. Xanathar's Gazers follow as well. The characters step into a street where multiple parties are deciding whether to take the artifact from them.
- If the characters befriend Yalah and she surrenders the Stone voluntarily — whether because of the family threat or genuine negotiation — Hrabbaz allows it. This is her call and he trusts it.

This earns D'Aerthe Renown as per the faction appendix.</p>
        <p>If D'Aerthe characters instead secure the Stone themselves and attempt to leave with it, Fel'Rekt's team escorts them out discreetly rather than confronting them. Jarlaxle will want to discuss terms later — but he will be smiling when he does.</p>
        <p><strong>If the Characters Seem Overwhelmed:</strong> At any point during the villa encounter — whether fighting Zhentarim, Hurv, or a combination — if D'Aerthe characters are Bloodied or clearly losing ground, Fel'Rekt will order one or two Drow Gunslingers to intervene from range. They will pick off priority targets from the shadows without drawing attention to themselves, then melt back into the darkness before anyone can confirm where the shots came from. Fel'Rekt does not want his potential asset dead on a Gralhund carpet.</p>
    </article>
</div>

        <p>Gralhund Villa is not a puzzle with one solution. It is a volatile, multi-faction scenario where the characters' choices determine the outcome. ## Aftermath

### Escaping the Villa

However the characters secure the Stone — theft, negotiation, combat, or lucky timing — they face a final challenge: getting out before the City Watch seals the block.

Neighbors who heard the fighting will have sent runners. A Watch response team (a Mage, six Warrior Veterans, and a Guard Captain) arrives 1d6+4 minutes after the first serious noise. If the characters are inside the villa when the Watch arrives, they face questioning and will be detained unless they can establish a credible reason for being there (DC 14 Charisma (Deception or Persuasion); Lords' Alliance characters with Jalester's temporary papers pass automatically).

If they are already on the street, they may blend into the gathering crowd with a DC 13 Dexterity (Stealth) check for the group.

### If They Are Being Pursued

If the characters are being chased — by Fel'Rekt's drow, surviving Zhentarim operatives, or Floxin himself — use the **chase rules** below rather than reducing the escape to a single roll.

<div class="fvtt advice">
    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/skills/targeting/target-glowing-yellow.webp">
    </figure>
    <article>
        <h4>Chase Rules Summary</h4>
        <p><strong>Beginning the Chase.</strong> Determine the starting distance between the characters (quarry) and their pursuers. All participants who are not already in Initiative order roll Initiative. Each participant can take one action and move on their turn.</p>
        <p><strong>Dashing.</strong> Participants are strongly motivated to Dash every round. A participant can Dash a number of times equal to 3 + their Constitution modifier (minimum once) without penalty. Each additional Dash beyond this limit requires a DC 10 Constitution saving throw at the end of that turn; on a failure, the creature gains 1 Exhaustion level. A participant with 5 Exhaustion levels drops out of the chase. Exhaustion gained in a chase is removed by a Short or Long Rest.</p>
        <p><strong>Spells and Attacks.</strong> Participants can make attacks and cast spells against targets within range. Chase participants cannot normally make Opportunity Attacks against each other (all are assumed to be moving in the same direction), but they can still be targeted by Opportunity Attacks from non-participants — for example, from Zhentarim bystanders who recognize Floxin's quarry.</p>
        <p><strong>Ending the Chase.</strong> A chase ends when one side stops, when each quarry escapes, or when the pursuers close to catching distance. At Initiative count 0 each round, after all participants have taken their turns, the quarry makes a Dexterity (Stealth) check. If the quarry is never out of the lead pursuer's sight, the check fails automatically. Otherwise compare the total to the Passive Perception scores of all pursuers. If the total exceeds the highest Passive Perception score, the quarry escapes. If not, the chase continues for another round.</p>
        <p><strong>Escape Factors.</strong> The quarry has Advantage on the Stealth check when there are many things to hide behind or the area is very crowded; Disadvantage when there are few things to hide behind or the area is uncrowded.</p>
    </article>
</div>

**The Pursuit Route** runs across the North Ward's rooftops: uneven tiles and lead flashing, chimneys and weather vanes, gaps between buildings, and the ever-present risk of a section of old roof giving way underfoot. This is the likely escape route if the characters are fleeing from an upper floor — the balcony (G17) opens directly onto the roofline, and any pursuer worth the title will follow.

**Rooftop Chase Complications:** Roll 1d20 at the end of each participant's turn. Consult the table below. If a complication occurs, it affects the **next participant** in the Initiative order, not the one who rolled.

| d20 | Complication |
|-----|-------------|

You can jump over if your Strength is 10 or higher (each foot cleared costs 1 foot of movement), and you must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check or fall Prone on the far rooftop. Alternatively, a 10-foot-long rope line stretches between the two rooftops and can be used to cross. |
| 2 | The next rooftop is 10 feet higher than the one you're on. Make a DC 10 Strength (Athletics) check. On a failure, the height change counts as 10 feet of difficult terrain. |

### The Stone of Golorr

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>It is smaller than you expected. An ovoid of polished black stone, cool and slightly heavy, with three concave sockets spaced evenly around its equator — each roughly the size and shape of a large eye. The sockets are empty. Whatever was set into them is gone. When you hold it and clear your mind, you feel something stir behind the smooth surface: not warmth, not light, but the persistent, patient impression of an intelligence — vast and abolethic and very old — waiting to be asked the right question. It has nothing to say to you yet. Its three eyes are blind.</p>
</div>

<div class="fvtt advice">
    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/weapons/swords/sword-jeweled-red.webp">
    </figure>
    <article>
        <h4>The Stone of Golorr</h4>
        <p><em>Wondrous Item, Legendary (requires attunement)</em></p>
        <p>An ovoid of polished black stone, cool to the touch and faintly heavier than it looks, with three concave sockets spaced evenly around its equator — each shaped to receive a large gem. The sockets are currently empty. The Stone is the transformed remnant of an aboleth named Golorr, a creature of vast memory that was polymorphed into artifact form centuries ago by a Netherese arcanist. It retains Golorr's memories, but cannot fully communicate them while its three Eyes are absent.</p>
        <p><strong>Incomplete State.</strong> Without all three Eye-gems set into its sockets, the Stone cannot be fully attuned. A creature that attempts to attune to the Stone in this state receives only fragmented impressions: a sense of deep stone, the smell of old gold, rushing water, and a persistent image of three empty sockets — <em>wanting</em>. Attunement still completes, but the Stone's properties are limited until restored.</p>
        <p><strong>Telepathic Contact.</strong> Once attuned, the Stone can communicate telepathically with its attuned holder. While incomplete, its communications are fragmented: single images, emotional impressions, half-formed words. It will never lie to its attuned holder, but it may be unable to complete a thought.</p>
        <p><strong>Vault of Dragons (Eyes Required).</strong> Once all three Eye-gems are restored and set into the sockets, the attuned holder can ask the Stone to reveal the location of the Vault of Dragons. The Stone will provide the location clearly and completely, along with the three keys required to open the vault and a description of the guardian creatures bound inside. This is a one-time revelation; the Stone will repeat the information on request but cannot provide further detail beyond what Golorr's memory contains.</p>
        <p><strong>Memory of Waterdeep.</strong> Golorr's memories extend back centuries. Once attuned (even incompletely), the holder can ask the Stone questions about the history of Waterdeep — significant events, past inhabitants of specific locations, historical secrets. The DM determines what Golorr would and would not know. Questions that require the Stone to speculate about the present or future receive no response.</p>
        <p><strong>Abolethic Taint.</strong> The Stone is a creature, not merely an object. Extended attunement carries a risk: at the end of each Long Rest while attuned, the holder must succeed on a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw or develop a minor personality trait aligned with Golorr's nature (patient, territorial, fixated on secrets, subtly contemptuous of creatures who forget things). These traits fade within a week of ending attunement. If a creature fails this saving throw three times while attuned to the same Stone, the trait becomes permanent until removed by a <em>Remove Curse</em> spell or similar magic.</p>
        <p><strong>Sentience.</strong> The Stone of Golorr is a Sentient item. Intelligence 18, Wisdom 15, Charisma 10. It can perceive its surroundings through senses equivalent to Blindsight 30 ft. It has no alignment and no goals of its own except to be restored and to have its knowledge valued. It does not pursue conflict with its attuned holder, but if asked to assist in something directly contrary to the preservation of Waterdeep's great secrets, it may go silent for 1d4 days in protest.</p>
    </article>
</div>

Surviving the villa with the information they need — even without the Stone itself — is sufficient for advancement.

## What Comes Next

The Stone of Golorr is in the characters' hands — or almost in their hands — and the Grand Game has found them. Four factions now know that a new party of players has entered the competition:

- **The Cassalanters** know the Gralhund connection has been compromised. Lady Ammalia will quietly distance herself from the Gralhunds and accelerate her own timeline.

The Stone needs three Eyes. Each Eye is held by a faction. Act IV is a heist campaign.
