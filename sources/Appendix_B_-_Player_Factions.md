# Appendix B — Player Factions

The factions of Waterdeep are not abstractions. They are communities of real people with histories, rivalries, and ambitions — organizations that have survived for decades or centuries because they know how to make themselves useful to capable individuals. When a faction recruiter approaches the party, they are making a calculated offer: resources, purpose, and belonging in exchange for loyalty and service.

Characters are not required to join a faction. Those who do will find their relationships with Waterdeep's power structures deepened, their access to allies and information expanded, and their obligations to something larger than themselves made very real.

---

<aside class="notable">
<h4>The Grand Game and the Factions</h4>
<p>Once the player characters have joined a faction, that faction becomes a lens through which the larger conflict of the Grand Game is filtered. Factions push for information, offer resources conditional on missions, and bring their own agendas to bear on the Vault of Dragons and the Stone of Golorr. The alliances formed here will shape how the campaign's central conflict resolves — and what happens to half a million gold dragons.</p>
</aside>

## Renown

Renown measures a character's standing, trust, and reputation within a particular faction. It is tracked separately for each faction — a character might be a trusted operative of the Harpers while being entirely unknown to the Order of the Gauntlet.

### Gaining Renown

Characters gain renown by completing missions assigned by their faction and by taking actions that directly advance their faction's interests in the Grand Game. Specific mission rewards are listed in each faction's mission table. Additional renown from Grand Game actions is described in each faction's **Earning Renown** section.

The DM may award bonus renown — typically +1 or +2 — for exceptional individual acts not covered by either category: a clever improvisation that saves a mission, roleplay that deepens a key relationship, or a sacrifice made in the faction's name. Hugely significant acts, such as delivering a decisive advantage in the Grand Game's endgame, may award +3 or +4 at once.

### Losing Renown

Ordinary disagreements with faction members do not reduce renown. However, serious offenses — betraying a mission, harming faction members, working against the faction's interests, or compromising its operations — result in renown loss at the DM's discretion. A character's Renown Score can never fall below 0.

For the **Lords' Alliance** specifically: refusing or failing to complete an assigned mission without good cause can result in formal suspension (no missions until reinstated) or dismissal (loss of membership and all renown in the faction).

## The Harpers

*"We work from the shadows so that others may stand in the light."*

The Harpers are a loose confederation of bards, spies, sages, and do-gooders who have worked for centuries to preserve freedom and prevent any single entity from seizing dominion over Faerûn. They oppose tyranny, protect the vulnerable, and hoard information the way other organizations hoard gold. Their symbol — a silver harp cradled within a crescent moon — is known across the continent, though most Harper operatives are careful never to display it openly.

In Waterdeep, the Harpers maintain strong ties to the city's intellectual and artistic communities. Several of their senior members are also Masked Lords, identities they guard with exceptional care. Their primary concern in the current moment is the threat posed by Manshoon's clone and the consolidation of the Black Network's ruthless wing under his control. The Harpers are also quietly alert to the Stone of Golorr — they believe it contains intelligence of strategic importance, and they want it.

### Joining the Harpers

The Harpers recruit good-aligned characters who show promise as spies, investigators, and field agents. Potential recruits receive a message delivered by *paper bird*:

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Renaer tells us you are a good bet. He bought you tickets to the opera tonight at the Lightsinger Theater in the Sea Ward. If you are interested, meet Mirt at intermission. Private Box C. Formal attire is required for admittance."</p>
</div>

Enclosed are tickets for the entire party to *The Fall of Tiamat*, an opera sung in Giant describing the Dragon Queen's defeat at the Well of Dragons. The invitation is deliberate — the Harpers want to see how the party handles a setting that requires social grace.

**Contact: Mirt the Moneylender.** Mirt is the party's primary Harper contact. He presents himself as a bluff, jovial sea merchant grown fat on good wine, but is in fact one of the most politically dangerous men in Waterdeep — a senior Harper, a Masked Lord, and a close confidant of Open Lord Laeral Silverhand. He is warm, calculating, and never forgets anything. His manor in the Sea Ward is theoretically available for visits; in practice, he is almost never home.

**Secondary Contact: Remallia "Remi" Haventree.** A precise, guarded elf widow who manages Harper intelligence operations in the North Ward. She lost her husband to political violence and responded by becoming very good at preventing more of it. Harder to read than Mirt, but ultimately more reliable.

### First Meeting

### The Invitation

The invitation arrives by *paper bird*: a note folded into the shape of a sparrow that taps on a window of Trollskull Manor and waits. When unfolded, it reads in a flowing, unhurried hand:

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Renaer tells us you are a good bet. He bought you tickets to the opera tonight at the Lightsinger Theater in the Castle Ward. If you are interested, meet Mirt at intermission. Private Box C. Formal attire is required for admittance."</p>
</div>

Enclosed are tickets for the entire party to *The Fall of Tiamat*, an opera sung in Giant describing the Dragon Queen's defeat at the Well of Dragons. The language requirement ensures that most of the audience cannot understand the libretto — the Harpers appreciate the metaphor.

Formal wear can be hired from a tailor on Delzorin Street if the party is not prepared; Mirt's note anticipates this, and the tailor has been told to expect them.

### Arriving at Lightsinger Theater

The theater stands on a well-kept street in the Castle Ward, its exterior lit by *continual flame* lamps that paint the stonework amber. A pair of doormen in livery check tickets at the entrance with the unhurried precision of people who have turned away difficult guests before.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The interior of the Lightsinger is everything the exterior promises. Tiers of dark lacquered galleries rise from the pit to the ceiling, hung with velvet curtains that muffle sound to a soft, concentrated hush. Driftglobes drift in slow rotation above the pit orchestra, casting a warm, unsteady light across the audience below — finely dressed merchants, minor nobles, and a scattering of people who are clearly very good at looking as though they belong here.</p>
    <p>The smell is beeswax candles and expensive perfume and something faintly floral from the arrangements on every landing. A program printed on cream card stock has been pressed into your hand by an attendant who did not introduce himself. The orchestra is already warming up below: a low, rolling thunder of brass and strings searching for their key.</p>
    <p>Private boxes line the upper gallery. Box C is at stage left, its curtain half-drawn. From inside, the stage is visible at a slight angle — not the best view in the house, but private enough for a conversation no one is meant to overhear.</p>
</div>

### Meeting Mirt

The man in Box C is large in every dimension. He sprawls in the best chair with the confidence of someone who owns the chair, the box, and probably a share in the building.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The man who rises to meet you fills most of the box. He is broad-shouldered and barrel-chested, with a grey beard trimmed without particular conviction and small, sharp eyes set in a face that has been lived in thoroughly. He wears good clothes — expensive fabric, you can tell by the weight of it — but with the casual disregard of someone who has been rich long enough to stop thinking about it. There is a wine stain on one sleeve that has been dabbed at and abandoned.</p>
    <p>He smiles when he sees you, and it is a real smile, the kind that reaches the eyes. "Good. You came dressed." He gestures to the decanter already breathing on the sideboard and the glasses arranged beside it. "Sit down. The first act has its moments, but the real conversation happens at intermission."</p>
</div>

During the first act, Mirt keeps conversation light and purposeful — Renaer spoke well of the party; the business at the Yawning Portal and the warehouse was handled with commendable directness; capable people are rarer than they should be. He is watching, throughout, to see how they move, how they hold their glasses, who speaks first and who listens.

At intermission, when the curtain drops and the audience noise swells in the galleries below, he sets down his glass and becomes direct.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The Harpers." He says it as if testing the weight of the word. "Perhaps you've heard of us. Probably a story or two, half of them exaggerated, and at least one about me personally and almost certainly wrong." He refills his own glass. "We are not a guild. Not a government. We are people who care about the city — about all the cities — and who have decided to do something about it rather than wait for someone better qualified to show up."</p>
    <p>"We work quietly. We protect the vulnerable. We resist anyone — anyone at all — who tries to seize enough power to stop being answerable to anyone else. That is the whole of the philosophy. The practical work is more varied."</p>
    <p>He looks at you steadily across the rim of his glass.</p>
    <p>"Waterdeep has a problem at present. Actually several. The relevant one, for this conversation, involves people operating entirely outside the law and entirely without accountability, and the ordinary mechanisms of justice cannot reach them. What we need are people who can. People who are good at finding things out and acting on what they find, rather than waiting for permission."</p>
    <p>He pauses.</p>
    <p>"Renaer thinks you are that kind of people. He is usually right about these things. I want to find out if he is right this time."</p>
</div>

Mirt will answer reasonable questions about the Harpers plainly: their Waterdeep operation is relatively small but well-connected; they work with information more than force; membership is discreet; rank and trust are earned not declared. He does not mention the Stone of Golorr, Manshoon, or the gang war's deeper origins. He is assessing, not briefing.

Characters who accept membership receive a silver pin — a small harp cradled within a crescent moon — pressed into their hand from his coat pocket, as if it was already there waiting.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"My manor is in the Sea Ward if you ever need to find me." He says it with a certain warmth. "I should warn you that I am almost never home." A beat, then almost gently: "You'll hear from us soon. The city doesn't give us much time to rest."</p>
    <p>He settles back into his chair as the house lights dim for the second act. He watches the stage with the patient expression of a man who is not, in fact, watching the stage at all.</p>
</div>

### Earning Renown

In addition to assigned missions, Harper characters can earn renown through the following actions:

- **Feed intelligence to Mirt (+1).** Report credible, actionable information about a Grand Game faction's current position, assets, or plans. One award per faction per act, not repeatable with the same information.
- **Expose a Manshoon agent (+1).** Identify and confirm the identity of a Zhentarim double-agent operating within the Harpers or another allied organization. Reporting a name Mirt can act on is sufficient; the Harpers handle what follows.
- **Protect a civilian from faction crossfire (+1).** Directly intervene to prevent a non-combatant from being harmed as collateral damage during Grand Game operations. Applies once per significant incident.
- **Recover or deliver the Stone of Golorr to the Harpers (+3).** The Harpers believe the Stone contains Abolethic Sovereignty intelligence of strategic value. Surrendering it to Mirt or Remallia is the single highest-value act a Harper operative can perform short of ending the Grand Game.
- **Identify the Cassalanters as diabolists and report it (+2).** The Harpers are aware that the Cassalanters are Asmodeus cultists and will move to warn and protect any PC with connections to them. A character who independently uncovers this and reports it to Mirt demonstrates exactly the kind of field intelligence work the Harpers prize.
- **Refuse to act against civilians on faction orders (+1).** The Harpers value moral clarity alongside operational effectiveness. A character who declines a mission that would require harming innocents — and explains why — earns quiet respect, not punishment.

## The Lords' Alliance

*"Civilization is the most fragile thing we have built. We will not let it fall."*

The Lords' Alliance is a coalition of rulers and political operatives from Waterdeep and the allied cities of the North — Neverwinter, Baldur's Gate, Silverymoon, and others — united by the conviction that civilization must be actively defended. In Waterdeep, the Alliance functions as a pro-city intelligence and security apparatus, funding field agents who monitor external and internal threats to the city's stability.

The Alliance's position on the Grand Game is unambiguous: the 500,000 stolen dragons are a destabilizing factor that belongs to the city. They want the gold recovered and returned. They are also deeply alarmed by Manshoon's activities, which represent an attempt to seize control of Waterdeep's institutions from within.

The Lords' Alliance is the most demanding faction in terms of professional conduct. Refusing missions has consequences. Members are expected to behave like agents, not adventurers.

### Joining the Lords' Alliance

The Alliance recruits characters who are Waterdeep residents and who place the security of the city ahead of personal gain. Membership is offered by Jalester Silvermane, who spends much of his time at the Yawning Portal and other adventurers' haunts.

**Contact: Jalester Silvermane.** A no-nonsense human field agent who reports directly to Open Lord Laeral Silverhand. Professional, precise, and direct. He does not enjoy pleasantries and takes the work seriously. Characters who treat missions as optional will find his patience exhausted quickly.

### First Meeting

### The Approach

The Lords' Alliance does not send paper birds. Jalester Silvermane finds the party himself — at the Yawning Portal, where he spends much of his time, or at Trollskull Manor if the party rarely ventures out. He introduces himself without ceremony and gets to it.

### Back at the Portal

By now the party knows this room. The windlass with its illegible tally, Durnan's expression of comprehensive unimpression behind the bar, the well dropping into the dark. After the events of Act I, the Yawning Portal is familiar ground — the place where the campaign properly began, and where certain reputations were made.

A middle-aged man in a plain dark doublet sits alone at a corner table with a tankard he has not touched. He watches the door. When the party enters, he nods almost imperceptibly, as if confirming something.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He stands as you approach — a lean man, thinning dark hair, the composed stillness of someone who has either spent a long time in professional service or a long time grieving, and has let them become the same thing. His clothes are unremarkable by design. There is a City Watch badge on his belt, worn casually enough that you might miss it.</p>
    <p>"Jalester Silvermane." His handshake is firm and brief. He does not smile, but he is not cold — he is simply economical. "I work for the Lords' Alliance, which means I work for Waterdeep. Sit down."</p>
    <p>He waits until you have settled. Then: "I've been watching you since the warehouse. You handled a difficult situation with competence and without causing a diplomatic incident. That's rarer than it should be."</p>
</div>

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The Alliance needs capable people in the city right now. People who aren't Watch, aren't Guard, and can therefore operate in situations where neither can or should appear. In exchange for that, you get resources, backing, and the genuine gratitude of the people who keep this city standing." He holds your gaze. "What you do not get is the option to pick and choose your assignments, or to disappear when things become complicated."</p>
    <p>A pause. He seems to be giving you room to respond — though he is also clearly listening to how you fill that room, and filing it away.</p>
    <p>"Waterdeep is a city worth protecting. That is the whole argument. I'm not going to make it more complicated than that."</p>
</div>

Jalester answers questions about the Alliance's structure directly: it reports to Open Lord Laeral Silverhand; he is her field agent; the party would operate under his direction. He will not discuss active intelligence at this stage. If they accept, there is no ceremony.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Good." He straightens his doublet and stands. "You will receive your first assignment shortly. We do not let grass grow."</p>
    <p>At the door he stops without turning. "One more thing. Assignments from us are not optional. If that changes your answer, now is the time to say so."</p>
    <p>He waits three seconds. Then he leaves.</p>
</div>

### Earning Renown

In addition to assigned missions, Lords' Alliance characters can earn renown through the following actions:

- **Report Grand Game activity to Jalester (+1).** Brief Jalester on any confirmed faction operation, location, or agent. One award per significant piece of intelligence; vague tips do not qualify.
- **Bring the Grand Game to Laeral's attention (+2).** Formally brief Jalester with enough evidence for him to escalate to the Open Lord. This triggers Laeral's direct involvement and is one of the most significant acts an Alliance operative can perform.
- **Assist the City Watch in a crisis (+1).** Aid the Watch in any situation that exceeds their capacity — monsters, organized assault, mind-controlled citizens. Applies once per incident, not per enemy defeated.
- **Expose or neutralize a Manshoon operative (+2).** Identify, capture, or eliminate a named agent of Manshoon's Zhentarim cell. Delivering a live prisoner to Jalester for questioning earns the full reward; confirmed elimination earns +1.
- **Recover the stolen dragons and arrange their return to the city (+4).** The Alliance's primary objective in the Grand Game. This is the single highest-value act available to Alliance operatives and will elevate any character directly to the attention of the Open Lord.
- **Prevent an act of large-scale violence in Waterdeep (+1).** Stopping a gang war flashpoint, preventing an assassination of a public figure, or averting a mass-casualty event in the city all qualify. One award per significant incident.

## The Emerald Enclave

*"The city is built on nature. We remember that. It would be good if everyone did."*

The Emerald Enclave is an organization of druids, rangers, and survivalists dedicated to maintaining the balance between civilization and the wild. Their Waterdeep presence is smaller than the other factions — the city proper is not their natural territory — but they monitor anything that might upset the natural order of the region. A beholder crime lord building an empire in the city's sewers is exactly the kind of aberrant corruption that draws their attention.

The Enclave has no stake in Neverember's gold. They have no position on the Grand Game and will make no demands of their members regarding the Vault or its contents. This makes them a uniquely clean patron in the campaign — their help is freely given to proven loyalists, and they want nothing extraordinary in return.

### Joining the Emerald Enclave

The Enclave seeks out characters who are naturalists, healers, or who have demonstrated concern for the natural world. Recruitment is delivered by *animal messenger* — a white cat arrives with an invitation:

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Interested in joining the Emerald Enclave? Come meet us at Phaulkonmere in the Southern Ward."</p>
</div>

**Contact: Melannor Fellbranch.** The groundskeeper of Phaulkonmere, a compound in the Southern Ward. Friendly but humorless, Melannor is a half-elf druid who communicates primarily through *animal messenger* spells — cats and pigeons are his preferred couriers.

**Contact: Jeryth Phaulkon.** The lady of the estate, a noblewoman who has become a demigod and Chosen of Mielikki. She manifests as a disembodied voice heard throughout the villa's gardens. She cannot be harmed in this state, has access to the full druid spell list, and will cast spells for Enclave members whose renown equals or exceeds the spell's level.

### First Meeting

### The Invitation

A white cat appears on the sill of Trollskull Manor one morning. It sits with the self-possessed patience of its kind, then opens its mouth and speaks — not in a cat's voice, but in a calm, pleasant male baritone:

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Interested in joining the Emerald Enclave? Come meet us at Phaulkonmere in the Southern Ward."</p>
</div>

Then it jumps down, wanders around the corner, and is not seen again.

### Arriving at Phaulkonmere

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Phaulkonmere sits behind an iron gate in the Southern Ward, worked with reliefs of leaves and birds, standing open in the morning air. Beyond it is a garden of conspicuous wildness — not neglected, but deliberately allowed to grow in the directions nature would choose rather than those a gardener would impose. Stone paths run through it, worn smooth with long use. Old oak trees spread their branches overhead, and there are birds in them — actual birds, apparently unconcerned with being watched.</p>
    <p>The manor house at the garden's end is large and old and shows it. It has accumulated wings and rooms without any obvious architectural plan over what must be many generations, and looks comfortable rather than grand. The whole compound smells of turned earth and something green and quietly alive.</p>
</div>

### Meeting Melannor Fellbranch

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The half-elf at the gate is of middling height and indeterminate age, wearing practical clothes that suggest he has never once cared what they looked like. His hands are callused and there is dark soil under his nails. He has a pleasant, reserved face — friendly without performing friendliness — and he does not seem surprised to see you.</p>
    <p>"Melannor Fellbranch. I keep the grounds." He holds the gate and nods you through. "Walk with me for a moment. The garden is worth the time."</p>
</div>

He leads them along the main path without hurry, pointing out things as they pass — a patch of herbs being coaxed back from frost, a moss-colonised wall left that way by design, a nest he has been monitoring in the old oak since early spring. He is watching how they move through the space.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The Enclave isn't what most people think," he says, pausing at a low stone bench near the garden's center. "People hear the word 'nature' and they picture trees and rivers. Pleasant things. That is not quite it." He crouches briefly to examine something in the soil, then stands. "Balance is a better word. What we watch for are disruptions — things that shouldn't be where they are, pulling at what keeps the world from unraveling. Sometimes that is in a forest. Sometimes it is in a city. Sometimes it is in a sewer beneath a city, if the sewer happens to contain a beholder who has been expanding his territory for twenty years."</p>
    <p>He looks at you with calm directness. "We offer honest work and the knowledge that it matters. We also offer this garden, as a place to come to when the rest of Waterdeep becomes too much. Lady Phaulkon keeps it safe." He glances toward the heart of the estate. "You should hear her."</p>
</div>

### Meeting Jeryth Phaulkon

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The voice comes from nowhere in particular — warm, present, completely unhurried — as if someone is speaking from just behind a tree that is not there.</p>
    <p>"Welcome to Phaulkonmere. I am sorry I cannot greet you more visibly. It has been some time since I could manage that."</p>
    <p>The garden seems, in some indefinable way, to be paying attention.</p>
    <p>"I am Jeryth Phaulkon. This was my family's estate. In some sense it still is, though I occupy it differently now. Mielikki asked something of me several years ago, and I said yes, and here we are." There is no bitterness in the voice; it is simply a report. "I watch over the garden. I watch over the city when I can. And I watch over those who do honest work in the world."</p>
    <p>Something like attention settles briefly and distinctly on each of you in turn.</p>
    <p>"If you join us, this place is yours as a refuge. No questions asked, no favors owed for it — that is not how we work. What we ask instead is that you pay attention to what is being disrupted, and that you act on what you see. That is the whole of it."</p>
</div>

Each character who accepts feels, at that moment, something restored — a bruise unknotting, a small weight lifting — as Jeryth bestows a *charm of restoration* without ceremony or announcement.

## The Order of the Gauntlet

*"Evil does not rest. Neither do we."*

The Order of the Gauntlet is a fellowship of paladins, clerics, and devout martial warriors who pursue and destroy evil actively, without waiting for it to come to them. In Waterdeep, the Order functions as a quasi-religious law-enforcement body — more aggressive and less subtle than the City Watch, and considerably less interested in political nuance. They are exactly the kind of allies you want when facing devil-worshippers or a mind-flayer-backed crime lord.

The Order has a particular interest in the Cassalanters (active Asmodeus cultists conducting devil-bargain rituals) and in Xanathar's Guild (whose use of intellect devourers and enslaved creatures they consider an abomination). Characters whose investigations brush against either of these threats will find the Order increasingly engaged.

### Joining the Order of the Gauntlet

The Order looks for recruits who seek to fight evil in all its forms. Adventurers who worship Helm, Torm, or Tyr are especially sought after. Recruitment is handled personally by Savra Belabranta, who visits the characters' residence. Accepted recruits are invited to the Halls of Justice, the temple of Tyr in the Castle Ward, where they are sworn in with the recitation of an oath and the donning of a silver gauntlet — the order's symbol.

**Contact: Savra Belabranta.** A knight of the Order from a Waterdavian noble family known for raising griffons for the Griffon Cavalry. Savra is atoning for a darker past — she was once a member of the Howling Hatred elemental cult — and has channeled that shame into an iron commitment to justice. She communicates mission briefings personally.

### The Approach

Savra Belabranta comes to the party directly. She arrives at Trollskull Manor, knocks, and waits. She is dressed in the travelling clothes of a Waterdavian of means — a dark riding coat, good boots, a silver gauntlet pin at her collar — and holds herself with the deliberate uprightness of someone who has spent years making herself stand up straight.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The woman at your door is in her late twenties and looks older in the way that people who have been through something and come out the other side look older. Dark hair worn back. A strong jaw. The patient, measured manner of someone who has learned to think before she speaks, and found the habit worth keeping.</p>
    <p>"Savra Belabranta." Her handshake is firm. "Knight of the Order of the Gauntlet. I would appreciate a few minutes of your time, if you can spare them."</p>
</div>

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The Order of the Gauntlet exists to hunt evil. Not to observe it from a safe distance and file reports. Not to wait for it to commit a crime the Watch can prosecute. To find it, pursue it, and destroy it." She speaks without drama or heat — this is simply what she believes and what she does. "We serve the Triad — Helm, Torm, Tyr — but membership is not restricted to the devout.     <p>"Waterdeep has things in it right now that the Watch cannot touch. A criminal empire backed by a beholder. We operate past those limits."</p>
    <p>"I will not pretend the work costs nothing. It does. But we offer purpose, genuine support, and the knowledge that when you stand in the dark and hold a line, there are people standing with you."</p>
</div>

She will answer questions plainly. If asked why she herself joined, she pauses before answering honestly: she is making amends for things she did before, and this is the work she found that made that meaningful. She does not elaborate unless pressed.

### The Halls of Justice

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The Halls of Justice are stone and high-ceilinged, built to make the individual feel appropriately small against the weight of law. A priest stands as witness but says nothing; the ceremony is between the recruit and the Order.</p>

To protect those who cannot protect themselves. To stand when standing is required. The priest seals it with a brief invocation of Tyr. Then it is done.</p>
    <p>Savra clasps your arm in the manner of soldiers. When she speaks, she means it without irony: "Well done. The city is a little safer for it."</p>
    <p>She turns toward the door. "You will hear from me shortly. The work does not wait."</p>
</div>

## The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders)

*"The best thing about being part of the Black Network? The Network is always there when you need it."*

The Black Network is a mercenary organization-turned-criminal conspiracy that operates throughout Faerûn, running legitimate businesses as cover for smuggling, information brokering, and coercion. In Waterdeep, the faction available to player characters is the **Doom Raiders** — a cell of experienced operators who arrived in the city several years ago and built a network of commercial fronts they genuinely believe in.

The Doom Raiders are professional criminals who prefer contracts to confrontations, and who sincerely believe the Black Network works best when it operates like a legitimate enterprise. They are not good, but they are not sadistic, and they can be trusted to honor their agreements.

They are, however, facing an existential threat from within.

        <p>The Doom Raiders and Manshoon's faction are at active internal war. A character who joins the Doom Raiders is joining one half of a fractured organization and will inevitably be pulled into the conflict between them. Information shared with the Doom Raiders will not reach Manshoon's agents — the two cells actively despise each other. However, characters may be caught in the crossfire as Manshoon moves to eliminate Davil's cell entirely. This is a feature, not a complication to be managed away. Let the split create real pressure.</p>

### Joining the Doom Raiders

The Doom Raiders contact morally flexible or pragmatic characters via flying snake, with a parchment reading:

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Want to be part of something big? Speak to Davil Starsong at the Yawning Portal."</p>
</div>

**Primary Contact: Davil Starsong.** A sun elf retired adventurer who leads the Doom Raiders' Waterdeep operation. Charming, direct, and genuinely interested in ending the street violence Manshoon's cell has caused. He is arrested by the City Watch after the characters' second mission and held for questioning; subsequent missions arrive from his colleague Tashlyn Yafeera.

**Secondary Contact: Tashlyn Yafeera.** Takes over mission briefings after Davil's arrest, communicating via flying snake. She has sharper intelligence on Manshoon's faction than Davil did, and her assignments reflect it.

### Meeting Davil Starsong

He is already at his table. By the look of it, he has been here for a while.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The sun elf at the center table is lean and unhurried, with amber skin, close-cropped gold hair shot through with early silver, and the easy, comfortable presence of a man who is genuinely at ease wherever he happens to be sitting. He wears good dark clothes that announce nothing about him and a signet ring on his right hand that he turns occasionally as he speaks. The bottle on the table is open and there are glasses already waiting.</p>
    <p>He stands when you arrive, extends a hand, and introduces himself by first name only. Then he pours without asking and sits back down.</p>
    <p>"Appreciate you coming. I know the note wasn't exactly an engraved invitation." He smiles — a real one. "We find that the people we want to talk to respond better to a flying snake than to formal correspondence. Keeps out the timid."</p>
</div>

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"I'm going to be straightforward with you, because I think you'll appreciate it more than a sales pitch." He settles back, fingers loosely around his glass. "I'm Zhentarim. The Black Network. I know what that means to some people, and I'm not going to waste your time arguing the reputation. What I'll tell you instead is what we actually are in this city."</p>
    <p>"Me and my colleagues arrived in Waterdeep about four years ago. We took over the local Black Network operation and we've spent those four years turning it into something that functions like a legitimate business — because we believe the Network is most effective that way, and because it is, frankly, a better way to live. We provide loans. Security. Information, which in this city is the most valuable currency there is. People come to us when other options have failed, and we help them, and they remember it."</p>
    <p>He pauses and turns the signet ring once.</p>
    <p>"There is a complication. Another faction of the Black Network has come to Waterdeep — not ours. Brutal people, working for someone we thought was long dead. "We need capable, discreet people who can operate in spaces the Watch cannot. In return: resources, information, protection, and the knowledge that the people you're working for are invested in Waterdeep's stability, not its exploitation."</p>
    <p>"I'm not going to tell you we're good. I'm going to tell you we're useful, and that we honor our commitments." He lifts his glass slightly. "In my experience, that's rarer."</p>

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Think it over if you need to." He refills your glass with the easy manner of a man who does this reflexively. "But not for too long. The city doesn't have the patience for it right now, and honestly neither do I. You'll hear from us within a day or two — assuming you're in."</p>
    <p>He raises his glass.</p>
    <p>"To capable people, and whatever they decide to do with that."</p>

## Bregan D'aerthe

*"Everything is a transaction. The only question is whether you know what you're paying."*

Bregan D'aerthe is a mercenary company of drow exiles and surface operatives, led by the brilliant and infuriating Jarlaxle Baenre. They have been outmaneuvering Underdark politics for generations and operate across Faerûn wherever opportunity presents itself. Jarlaxle has come to Waterdeep with three disguised warships — the *Sea Maidens Faire* carnival fleet — and an expression of profound, delighted interest in everything that is happening.

Bregan D'aerthe does not brawl in the streets. It acquires information, sells it selectively, positions operatives in every major faction, and acts decisively only when the odds are already arranged in its favor. Jarlaxle's agents are embedded in or adjacent to every significant organization in Waterdeep. He has almost certainly been reading over everyone's shoulder since the day he arrived.

<div class="fvtt advice">
    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/sundries/books/book-red-exclamation.webp">

    <article>
        <h4>Warning: Bregan D'aerthe Is Not Safe</h4>
        <p>Joining Bregan D'aerthe means working for Jarlaxle Baenre, who is simultaneously one of the most useful allies and one of the most dangerous individuals in the campaign. He genuinely values capable people and will treat his agents well — until their interests diverge from his, at which point he will maneuver them into serving his ends anyway, with a smile. Characters who join this faction should understand they are playing a game where the other player is better at it than they are. That is not a reason not to play. It is a reason to pay attention.</p>
    </article>
</div>

### Joining Bregan D'aerthe

Bregan D'aerthe gives serious recruitment consideration only to drow characters, though Jarlaxle will work with non-drow who prove sufficiently capable and interesting.

When one or more characters are drow, Jarlaxle has three lieutenants — **Fel'rekt Lafeen**, **Krebbyg Masq'il'yr**, and **Soluun Xibrindas** — shadow the party at a careful distance for several days. Characters with a passive Perception of 18 or higher catch glimpses of the surveillance; a DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check confirms the watchers have a particular interest in drow party members.

If the party reports the drow to the City Watch, Jarlaxle ends contact for now. If the characters attempt to confront the spies, they melt away and leave a black eye patch as a calling card. The next day, Jarlaxle himself appears at the party's headquarters disguised as a haberdasher named **J.B. Nevercott** — and makes his offer directly to any drow present.

**Contact: Jarlaxle Baenre.** Operating in Waterdeep as the flamboyant Illuskan carnival master Zardoz Zord. He is always performing, but the performance is genuine — a man who chose delight as a survival strategy and discovered that it works. He gives compliments that are precise and earned. He remembers everything you said three conversations ago. He is never threatened by capable people; he collects them. The tell players should learn to watch for: when Jarlaxle stops smiling, the situation has become genuinely serious.

### First Meeting

### The Surveillance

Before the first meeting, there is a period of watching. Drow characters notice, over several days, glimpses of figures who should not be there: a silhouette in a doorway that was empty a moment before, a reflection in a window that does not match the street. Characters with a passive Wisdom (Perception) of 18 or higher catch these glimpses clearly enough to identify them as drow. A DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check confirms the watchers are specifically interested in drow party members.

The surveillance ends when the party either reports it to the Watch — in which case the watchers vanish and no contact follows — or moves to confront the drow spies directly. If confronted, they do not engage. They melt into the city and leave a single black eye patch on the cobblestones where one of them stood.

### The Morning Visit

The next morning, someone knocks at Trollskull Manor's door.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The man on the step is dressed for a profession that does not quite fit the street: a deep burgundy coat with brass buttons, a cravat arranged with unnecessary care, a broad hat that is slightly too large for him worn at an angle that suggests deliberate choice rather than poor fit. He carries a merchant's case of the kind used for travelling samples of fine goods. He has a pleasant, open face and the relaxed posture of someone who has been welcomed at a great many doors.</p>
    <p>"Good morning." He produces a small card and presents it with practiced ease. "J.B. Nevercott, Sea Ward, hats and accessories of quality. I heard the new proprietors of Trollskull Manor were people of taste, and I wanted to introduce myself before you had occasion to go elsewhere."</p>
    <p>He has opinions about hat brims. He is very willing to share them.</p>
</div>

He maintains the fiction of being a haberdasher for as long as it amuses him — which, under the *hat of disguise* that makes all of this possible, means Jarlaxle Baenre is watching the party with the focused, delighted attention of someone who has found something genuinely interesting.

The performance continues until a drow character steps aside from the group, at which point J.B. Nevercott glances around, lowers his voice by an octave, and sets the hat down.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The eye patch." He says it simply. "A calling card. We leave them for people we think are worth knowing." His posture changes — not dramatically, but enough. The haberdasher is still there, but something else is visible underneath it now, something considerably more precise. "You handled the surveillance well. Most people don't notice it at all. You noticed it and did something about it. That is exactly the quality we look for."</p>
    <p>He meets your eyes directly for the first time.</p>
    <p>"My name is not J.B. Nevercott. I represent an organization called Bregan D'aerthe — a mercenary company with operations across Faerûn and, as of some months ago, Waterdeep. We are very good at what we do. We have an interest in this city, resources that most factions here would trade considerably to access, and a particular interest in capable drow who are already making themselves notable in Waterdeep's current — interesting — political climate."</p>
    <p>He lets that sit a moment.</p>
    <p>"I want to make you an offer. Not a full briefing — that comes later, if you are interested. A first assignment. Small enough that declining costs you nothing. Simple enough that accepting costs you very little." He produces a card — not the haberdasher's card, but plain black with a ship embossed in silver on one side. "Consider it an introduction."</p>
    <p>The smile returns. It is the same smile as before, but now you can see what is behind it.</p>
    <p>"I'll be in touch shortly regardless. I find you interesting enough to keep an eye on either way."</p>
</div>

He replaces his hat, closes the case, and takes his leave with the unhurried manner of a man who has many other appointments and is enjoying all of them. He does not confirm his real identity to anyone who asks. He remains J.B. Nevercott until circumstances make the fiction untenable — which, with Jarlaxle, may take quite some time.

### Earning Renown

In addition to assigned missions, Bregan D'aerthe characters can earn renown through the following actions:

- **Deliver intelligence that expands Jarlaxle's picture of the Grand Game (+1).** Jarlaxle's initial reports are meticulous but acknowledge gaps. Any confirmed new information about a faction's Golorr artifact, lair location, or internal politics that he does not yet possess earns renown. One award per distinct intelligence item; Jarlaxle will tell the character, with characteristic courtesy, if the information is already known.
- **Acquire a Golorr artifact and deliver it to Jarlaxle (+2).** Jarlaxle's goal is control of the Stone — either the artifact itself or its three Eyes. Delivering any Golorr artifact to him is the most direct service D'aerthe operatives can perform. Characters who negotiate terms before delivery may also extract additional personal benefits.
- **Protect Nar'l Xibrindas's cover within Xanathar's Guild (+1).** Jarlaxle's spy inside Xanathar's council is his most valuable single asset in Waterdeep. Any action that deflects suspicion from Nar'l, discredits a threat to his position, or removes someone who has come close to identifying him earns D'aerthe renown — whether or not Jarlaxle explicitly requested it.
- **Successfully use the Sea Maidens Faire as cover or a conduit for an operation (+1).** The carnival ships are D'aerthe's logistical backbone in Waterdeep. Using them as a meeting point, exit route, smuggling channel, or false identity source — and doing so without compromising the cover — demonstrates exactly the kind of operational thinking Jarlaxle rewards.
- **Impress Jarlaxle with unexpected creativity or competence (+1, DM's discretion).** Jarlaxle awards renown for quality, not just results. A plan that is genuinely elegant, an improvisation that should not have worked but did, a social maneuver that leaves everyone at the table better off than before — these earn D'aerthe respect in a way that blunt execution does not. The DM should apply this sparingly and only when the bar is genuinely cleared.
- **Retrieve the nimblewright shipping records from the Sea Maidens Faire without tipping off Jarlaxle's crew (+2).** This requires a successful heist on D'aerthe's own ships — which Jarlaxle will discover and appreciate as a demonstration of capability. Characters who pull it off earn renown regardless of whether they then join the faction; if they are already members, the renown represents Jarlaxle's genuine delight at being robbed by his own people.

## Force Grey (The Gray Hands)

*"Waterdeep has enemies it cannot acknowledge. We handle those."*

Force Grey is Waterdeep's official cadre of elite adventuring agents, organized and commanded by Vajra Safahr, the Blackstaff — the city's most powerful individual wizard and its designated guardian against arcane threats. Force Grey is not a public organization. Its operations are deniable, its membership discreet, and its mandate broad: handle threats the City Watch and City Guard cannot or should not touch.

Characters who join the Gray Hands — the entry tier of Force Grey — are not yet full Force Grey operatives. Vajra is testing them. What they earn in the meantime is something more immediately valuable: the personal patronage of the Blackstaff.

<div class="fvtt advice">
    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/sundries/books/book-open-turquoise.webp">
    </figure>
    <article>
        <h4>The Blackstaff's Patronage</h4>
        <p>Vajra Safahr's backing is not something that can be purchased or networked into. It is earned. Characters who prove themselves to the Blackstaff gain access to Blackstaff Tower's considerable resources, legal cover for certain otherwise-illegal activities, and the implicit threat that anyone who moves against them is picking a fight with the most powerful wizard in Waterdeep. This is extraordinary leverage — and it comes with extraordinary expectations.</p>
    </article>
</div>

### Joining Force Grey

Vajra reaches out directly via *sending* spell to one party member shortly after the events of Chapter 1:

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"I am Vajra Safahr, the Blackstaff. Come to Blackstaff Tower in the Castle Ward at once. Bring your friends."</p>
</div>

If the party declines, Vajra contacts a different member a day later. If refused again, she waits until the characters gain a level before trying once more. She is persistent but not petty.

**Contact: Vajra Safahr, the Blackstaff.** The youngest person to hold the Blackstaff title, and among the most formidable wizards in Waterdeep. She communicates primarily via *sending* spells and is not given to small talk. A genuine ally to characters who prove themselves, and a genuinely intimidating authority figure to those who cross her.

### First Meeting

### The Sending

The *sending* spell arrives without warning, the way such things do: a voice in a character's head, clear and precise, spending its twenty-five words with economical intent.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"I am Vajra Safahr, the Blackstaff. Come to Blackstaff Tower in the Castle Ward at once. Bring your friends."</p>
</div>

If the character declines or does not respond, the spell reaches a different party member the following day. If the second invitation is also refused, Vajra waits until the party reaches their next level before attempting again. She does not take the refusal personally. She notes it.

### Arriving at Blackstaff Tower

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Blackstaff Tower stands near Swords Street in the Castle Ward, and it repays attention. It is taller than its base should allow — the upper floors extending into a geometry that does not hold up to prolonged examination, the stone at the top a shade slightly different from the stone below, as if the building has always been here and also as if it arrived recently. Both of these things feel equally true.</p>
    <p>The door responds to a spoken name. You do not have to speak yours. It opens anyway, which means you were expected.</p>
    <p>Inside, the entry hall is austere and functional: maps pinned to boards, bookshelves crowded past capacity, a standing desk covered in correspondence that is clearly losing the battle with the correspondence. The air smells of parchment and magical discharge — the faint, clean ozone of sustained spellwork. It feels like the office of someone who has too much to do and not quite enough time, but who has decided not to let that stop her.</p>
</div>

### Meeting Vajra Safahr

She is at the standing desk. She does not move to a sitting room. This is how Vajra treats people she has not yet decided about.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>She is younger than the title suggests — a young woman with dark, unruly hair above sharp green eyes and a long coat of blackish-purple covered in elven-inked designs that seem to shift slightly when you are not looking directly at them. She holds the Blackstaff: an irregular length of dark wood that hums at a frequency just below hearing. She drums her fingers on it while she looks at you.</p>
    <p>Her eyes are narrow. You have the strong impression she is making a judgment, and making it quickly.</p>
    <p>"Thank you for coming." Not warm, but not cold — a professional acknowledgment. She does not offer refreshment. She does not move away from the desk. "I'll keep this brief, because you probably have questions and I have a city to watch."</p>
</div>

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Renaer told me about the warehouse. He told me you handled a difficult situation with competence and without causing a diplomatic incident, which puts you ahead of several people I have worked with for years." She looks at each of you in turn. "I lead Force Grey. We are not the City Watch and we are not the Lords' Alliance. We handle situations that neither can or should be seen handling — magical threats, high-profile targets with political protection, situations where official involvement makes things worse rather than better."</p>
    <p>"I am not recruiting you for Force Grey. That requires a longer track record than one successful rescue. What I'm offering is the Gray Hands — the entry tier of our operation. You demonstrate your worth, you receive more access. You don't, we part cleanly." A pause. "What you receive in the meantime is my attention. In this city, that is worth considerably more than it sounds."</p>
    <p>She turns back to the desk for a moment, then looks up again. Her eyes have not gotten any wider.</p>
    <p>"Waterdeep has a problem with magical coercion right now. People in positions of influence are acting in ways they would not otherwise act. The Watch does not have the tools for this. I do, but I need field operatives I can trust to act on what they find." A shorter pause. "Are you interested?"</p>
</div>

She answers questions plainly and without embellishment. She does not discuss Undermountain, the Stone of Golorr, or the Grand Game's broader dimensions — she is not yet fully aware of them. She knows about the mind-control operations. She has suspicions about their connection to the gang war that she has not yet confirmed.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Good." She nods once and makes a brief note. The door opens, apparently of its own accord. "You will receive an assignment within the next few days. I communicate by <em>sending</em> — you will recognise my voice." She glances up one final time. "Try to get some sleep before then. The work does not wait for people to be rested."</p>
    <p>Something that might, under the right circumstances, be humour crosses her face for just a moment.</p>
    <p>"I speak from experience."</p>
</div>

### Earning Renown

In addition to assigned missions, Force Grey characters can earn renown through the following actions:

- **Report a significant arcane threat to Vajra before it becomes a crisis (+1).** Intellect devourers in city officials, a necromancer operating in the City of the Dead, an illegal gate or summoning circle discovered during Grand Game operations — any magical threat the Watch cannot handle and that Vajra does not yet know about. One award per confirmed new threat.
- **Bring the Grand Game fully to Vajra's attention (+2).** A formal briefing with sufficient evidence for Vajra to escalate to Laeral Silverhand. This is one of the most consequential acts a Gray Hand can perform; it transforms the political landscape of the campaign's final act.
- **Purge an intellect devourer infestation or free a mind-controlled Waterdavian (+1).** Directly countering Xanathar's mind-control operations is Force Grey's most urgent priority in the Grand Game. One award per significant incident — a single implanted citizen counts as one incident; discovering and purging an entire network of implanted citizens counts as two.
- **Protect Blackstaff Tower or its staff from a direct threat (+2).** Any coordinated attack on Blackstaff Tower — by Manshoon, Xanathar, or another Grand Game faction — is taken personally by Vajra. Characters who defend the tower or prevent such an attack earn significant standing.
- **Assist Vajra with arcane research connected to the Stone of Golorr or the Vault (+1).** Vajra has a professional interest in abolethic artifacts and Undermountain's upper access points. Characters who contribute to her research — delivering a Golorr artifact for examination, exploring a relevant tunnel network, translating a relevant text — earn renown even when Vajra has not formally assigned the task.
- **Keep Laeral Silverhand's involvement in the Grand Game clean (+1).** Force Grey's value to the Open Lord lies partly in its deniability. Characters who handle a problem without creating a political incident that must be managed earn Vajra's appreciation in a way that flashy violence does not. One award per arc for an operation resolved with notable discretion.
