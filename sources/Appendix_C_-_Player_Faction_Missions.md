# Appendix C — Player Faction Missions

Faction missions are delivered to characters upon gaining each level from 2nd through 5th. They are designed as compact, self-contained scenarios — each taking roughly a third of a session — woven into the daily life of Waterdeep rather than set apart from it. They are not dungeon crawls. They are urban stories with social, investigative, and occasionally violent cores, each one introducing an NPC or location that the campaign will return to, each one pressing gently but firmly on a question of character.

The missions are presented here as full adventure chapters with scene structure, read-aloud text, NPC detail, encounter parameters, and narrative guidance. Each arc follows the same architecture: an opening situation that establishes stakes, a central challenge that requires more than one kind of engagement, and a resolution that leaves something behind — a relationship, a complication, a thread the players can pull or leave alone.

        <h4>Design Note: Missions as the City's Heartbeat</h4>
        <p>The faction missions accomplish something that the Grand Game's heist structure cannot: they make Waterdeep feel inhabited. Every mission introduces a named street, a named person, a situation that has nothing to do with half a million stolen dragons and everything to do with what kind of city this is and what kinds of people live in it. Run them for texture as much as for content. The dungsweepers' names, the bookseller's cat, the retired adventurer who has become something terrible — these are the details players remember long after they have forgotten which Eye belonged to which faction.</p>

# Act IIa — The Harper Arcs

*The Harpers work from the shadows. Their missions should feel like that: oblique briefings, information that arrives by trained bird, encounters with people and creatures who are not what they seem. The Harper arcs introduce the party to the city's intelligence layer — the layer beneath commerce and politics where information is the only currency that matters.*

## Arc H-1 — The Talking Mare
### *2nd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

Mirt asks the party to locate a talking draft horse named Maxeene who pulls hire-drays through the city streets, and to extract whatever intelligence she has accumulated about Zhentarim operations. What sounds like an easy errand — find a horse, ask it questions — proves more nuanced: Maxeene is cautious, has standards, and has already been noticed by a Xanathar Guild operative who suspects she is more than she appears. The party must earn her trust before her situation becomes dangerous.

**Dramatic Question:** *Can the party convince a talking horse that they are worth trusting — before someone else decides she is worth eliminating?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

Mirt delivers this mission either at the Lightsinger Theater or via paper bird to Trollskull Manor, depending on whether the party has attended the opera yet.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"One of the drays working the city is pulled by a talking mare named Maxeene. She has a sharper ear than most people give credit, and she has spent the last several months carrying passengers who did not realize what they were sharing in earshot. Locate her. Find out if she's learned anything about Zhent operatives — names, locations, intentions. She won't speak to a stranger, so you'll need to convince her you're worth talking to. Be discreet. She's been doing this work for two years without incident, and I'd like that to continue."</p>
    <p>He pauses, as if considering whether to add something, then does: "She has opinions about most people. Don't take it personally."</p>
</div>

He has no further information about Maxeene's current route — she works the hire-dray circuit between the Dock, Trades, and Castle Wards and is not assigned to a fixed schedule. Finding her is the first part of the mission.

---

### Scene 2 — Finding Maxeene

The party has two options: legwork, or asking the right people.

**Legwork (Investigation).** Asking stable-masters, dray dispatchers, and street vendors along the hire-dray routes in the Trades and Dock Wards. A DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation) check, representing roughly three hours of inquiries, locates Maxeene working the Trades Ward. On a failure, the party can try again after another two hours, or shift strategy.

**Asking the Right People.** The party's Trollskull Alley neighbors are a useful shortcut. **Tally Fellbranch** of the Bent Nail saw a hire-dray with no visible driver pulled up outside the Silvermoon Institute three days ago — he mentioned it because he thought the dray's owner was napping inside. **Fala Lefaliir** at Corellon's Crown regularly uses hire-drays for herb deliveries and knows the Trades Ward circuit better than most. Either neighbor can reduce the Investigation DC to 10 or direct the party toward Maxeene's general area without a check, if the party thinks to ask.

    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/skills/targeting/target-glowing-yellow.webp">
    </figure>
    <article>
        <h4>DM Tip: The Search as World-Building</h4>
        <p>The search for Maxeene is an opportunity to establish the texture of the Trades Ward at ground level — the guild offices, the narrow streets smelling of fish and spices, the constant traffic of carts and pedestrians that makes Waterdeep feel genuinely busy. Drop a detail or two: a dungsweepers' crew breaking for lunch at a corner stall, a Watch patrol moving at a deliberate pace past a market stall that goes very still as they pass. These details cost nothing and do the work of making the city feel real before the larger dramas begin.</p>
    </article>
</div>

### Scene 3 — The Dray on Salabar Street

When the party locates Maxeene, she is working the middle of the Trades Ward, her empty hire-dray parked at a stand near the intersection of Salabar and Immar Streets while she waits for her next fare. She is a large grey roan with a white blaze, well-groomed, wearing a dray harness of excellent quality. To all visible evidence, she is a horse waiting for a passenger.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The grey roan at the dray stand is the only horse on the street without a handler nearby. She is standing in the harness with the calm, patient weight of a very large animal that has decided it is going to stay exactly where it is for as long as necessary. Her tail flicks at flies with a precision that seems slightly too deliberate. She watches you approach with a dark, liquid eye and does not react — but she is absolutely aware of you.</p>
</div>

**Maxeene's Caution.** Maxeene will not speak to strangers in a public street. If the party approaches her openly and addresses her as a talking horse, she remains horse-like — ears forward, no reaction. She has been maintaining this fiction for two years and is very good at it. A DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check notices the micro-expressions of a creature that is very actively deciding not to respond, rather than simply being an animal.

**Establishing Privacy.** Maxeene can be guided to a quieter location — an alley off Salabar Street, or a stable yard the party knows — with a successful DC 11 Animal Handling check or by simply offering to hire the dray and directing it somewhere quiet. Once away from the public street, she will acknowledge the party, though she is not immediately forthcoming.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>In the relative quiet of the side alley, the horse turns her head and looks at you with an expression that is, unmistakably, assessment.</p>
    <p>"You smell like someone sent you," she says. Her voice is unhurried, slightly dry, with the accent of someone who has spent a lot of time listening to the Castle Ward upper classes. "The question is whether I care who."</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 4 — Earning Maxeene's Trust

Maxeene will not share intelligence with someone who cannot demonstrate Harper affiliation and basic competence. She has been used as an asset for two years and has developed clear opinions about who is and is not worth her risk.

**Demonstrating Affiliation.** Showing Mirt's silver harp-and-crescent pin is sufficient. Knowledge of the Harper safe house location on Delzorin Street also works. Without either, the party must succeed on a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check — but the manner of the approach matters as much as the roll. Treating Maxeene as an animal, talking down to her, or expressing surprise that she speaks will impose Disadvantage. Treating her as a professional contact earns Advantage.

**Maxeene's Personality.** She is matter-of-fact and slightly indignant at the general failure of people to recognize her capabilities. She dislikes being called "girl," having her mane patted by strangers, or being spoken to in the slow, loud cadence some people adopt with animals and foreigners. She uses complete sentences and does not explain herself unless asked a direct question. She will answer direct questions directly.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Two days ago," she says, without preamble. "A sun elf and a half-orc. They hired me at the corner of Salabar and Coin Alley and rode to the Yawning Portal. The elf talked. The half-orc mostly watched the street." A pause. "People assume I'm not listening. I always am."</p>
    <p>"He said they'd had difficulty finding good informants. That the Watch was too visible and the Guild too unreliable. He wanted people who could move through the city without attention. He mentioned the Xanathar Guild specifically — wanted to know where their hideouts were. The half-orc said she knew someone who knew someone. He said he'd need three names by the end of the tenday."</p>
    <p>She looks at the party with a flat, evaluating gaze. "I don't know who he was. The elf was well-dressed and smelled expensive. The half-orc had a tattoo of a winged snake on the back of her neck."</p>
</div>

Maxeene's description matches Davil Starsong and Yagra Stonefist precisely. A DC 12 Intelligence check allows a character who has already met them to make the connection immediately.

---

### Scene 5 — The Complication: She Is Being Watched

Unknown to Maxeene — and to the party, unless they notice — a Xanathar Guild **Spy** named Vosk has been watching Maxeene for the past three days. Vosk is a thin, unremarkable Calishite man in the clothes of a middle-class merchant, and he has been positioned at a coffeehouse window across from the dray stand, making notes. He noticed that Maxeene sometimes refuses passengers and behaves in ways a horse should not, and he has flagged her as an anomaly worth investigating. He is not yet certain what she is. He is close.

**Detecting Vosk.** A character with a passive Perception of 14 or higher notices him as the party speaks with Maxeene — a man at a coffeehouse window who has not looked away from the alley for an unusual length of time. A DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check made actively also detects him.

**What Vosk Does.** If the party does not notice him, he notes their faces and their interaction with the horse. Within 48 hours, a report reaches Xanathar Guild contacts that "a talking horse pulling hire-drays in the Trades Ward appears to be a Harper intelligence asset, associated with [party description]." This does not create an immediate crisis, but it means that in approximately one tenday, Maxeene will find her route disrupted — either a Guild agent attempts to hire her and keeps asking specific questions, or she simply stops appearing at her usual stands. Mirt will note, if the party reports back, that Maxeene has gone quiet, and he will be displeased if the party cannot account for how that happened.

**If the Party Notices Vosk.** They have options: follow him, confront him, or warn Maxeene and let her determine her own response. Vosk will flee if directly approached; he is an observer, not a fighter, and will use the crowded Trades Ward streets to disappear if pursued. A successful DC 14 Dexterity (Stealth) approach catches him before he can react, making interrogation possible. He knows only that he was sent to watch "the horse that doesn't act like one." He reports to a Xanathar Guild lieutenant named Keln Trigos at the Terasse Estate on Elsambul's Lane — an address that will become relevant in the Grand Game investigation.

**Maxeene's Response.** If the party warns her, she is still and silent for a moment. Then: "I see. Well. I'll need to reroute." She gives the party a look that is not gratitude exactly — it is closer to the acknowledgment of a debt noted — and adds: "Tell Mirt I'll find a new circuit. And tell him to be more careful with who he sends."

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Harper character gains **+1 Renown**. The intelligence about Davil Starsong and Yagra Stonefist is one of three clue paths connecting the Doom Raiders to the Grand Game's wider network.

If the party successfully protected Maxeene from Vosk, she becomes an occasional minor intelligence source — she can be asked, once per act, what she has overheard from a specific type of passenger (merchants, Watch officers, Dock Ward residents). This requires finding her on her new route, which Mirt can facilitate.

If the party failed to notice Vosk, make a note. Maxeene's disappearance from her usual route should appear as a background detail some sessions later — the Trades Ward dray stand where she used to wait is empty. It is a consequence, not a punishment, and it is recoverable. But the players should eventually learn what happened and why.

## Arc H-2 — The Gazer in the Bookshop
### *3rd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

A gazer has taken up residence in a bookseller's three-story shop in the Trades Ward and has been hunting the bookseller's cat through the stacks. The bookseller, Uza Solizeph, has a reputation for dramatic storytelling that has cost her credibility with the City Watch — but this time she is not exaggerating. The party must enter the locked shop, navigate a hazardous environment where stray eye rays will destroy irreplaceable inventory, and deal with the gazer without burning the place down.

**Dramatic Question:** *Can the party clear the shop without destroying what makes it worth saving?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Uza Solizeph is an old woman who sells books out of a narrow three-story building on Sorn Street in the Trades Ward. She claims to have trapped a monster in her shop and fears for the welfare of her books and her cat. The City Watch isn't likely to lend a hand given Uza's propensity for tall tales, but the Harpers owe her a favor. You'll find her at Felzoun's Folly, a tavern on the corner of Sorn and Salabar. Make haste — she was very specific about the cat."</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — Felzoun's Folly

A small, comfortable tavern at the corner of Sorn and Salabar Streets, frequented by the Trades Ward's small merchant class — book dealers, clerks, guild scribes. It smells of ink, warm bread, and a fire that has been burning in the same grate for approximately forty years.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The woman near the back of the tavern is wrapped in a shawl despite the warmth of the room, clutching a cup of tea with both hands in the manner of someone anchoring themselves to something solid. She is perhaps seventy, with the fine-boned face of a Mulan merchant and hair gone entirely white, and she has been crying. Not theatrically — the quiet, exhausted crying of someone who has been at it for a while and is past the dramatic phase.</p>
    <p>She looks up when the party approaches, and her expression goes through hope and relief in rapid sequence before settling on urgent. "The Harpers sent you. Good. Is Fillipa — do you know about Fillipa?"</p>
</div>

**Uza Solizeph** (LG female human Mulan **Commoner**). An elderly book dealer who has been selling rare and used texts from her Sorn Street shop for thirty years. She has an extensive network of connections among Waterdeep's intellectual and clerical communities — she knows scholars, church archivists, and scribes throughout the city. She is not, as Mirt suggested, given to dramatic exaggeration: she is given to detailed, precise, and comprehensive description of dramatic events, which other people experience as exaggeration.

**What She Describes.** Three days ago, she arrived at her shop to find the front door open, which she had not left open. Inside, she discovered a small floating creature — "spherical, about the size of my head, with one large central eye and smaller eyes on stalks" — that immediately turned several of its smaller eyes on her and chased her out. The creature is now inside the shop. Fillipa, her cat, was also inside when the gazer entered and has been unable to escape. Uza has been sleeping at Felzoun's Folly ever since, coming by twice a day to leave food near the door and listen for sounds.

She hands the party two keys — front door and back — and her only request is that they bring Fillipa out alive.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The books matter," she says. "Don't misunderstand me. But the books can be replaced." A pause. "Most of them. Some of them cannot. And Fillipa cannot."</p>
</div>

If the party asks why the Harpers owe her a favor, she shakes her head. "That's not relevant. What's relevant is the cat."

---

### Scene 3 — The Bookshop

**Exterior.** A narrow three-story building on Sorn Street, between a glassblower's workshop and a cartographer's office. The signboard reads *Solizeph's Rare Books & Manuscripts* in faded gold letters. The front windows are shuttered from the inside. The street-level door is locked.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The bookshop's ground floor has been rearranged by something that does not care about shelf organization. Books lie open on the floor at intervals, as if something has been batting them down from the shelves and examining them with its eyes before losing interest. Two display cases of illuminated manuscripts have been knocked over; one is cracked, its contents scattered. The smell of something faintly ozone-sharp is in the air — like lightning, or the discharge of magical force. It has been here for days.</p>
    <p>From the upper floors, you hear a cat.</p>
    <p>Not a cat making a single sound. A cat in sustained, focused, escalating, eloquent communication with the situation it has found itself in — a running feline commentary on being trapped in a building with a floating eye monster, delivered through the ceiling with impressive persistence.</p>
</div>

**The Gazer** (**Gazer**, 2024 *Monster Manual*) is on the third floor, hunting Fillipa. It has been at this for three days and has not caught her. It will not stop. The gazer is hungry, cornered, and increasingly frustrated, which makes it somewhat more erratic than a rested one.

**Fillipa** is a black-and-white cat of approximately eleven years who has demonstrated, over the past three days, a tactical intelligence that has kept her ahead of a creature with magical eye rays. She has been using the bookshelves as terrain — jumping from shelf to shelf, knocking books into the gazer's path, and retreating to the attic rafters when cornered. She is currently in the third-floor attic. She is unhurt, highly agitated, and will not accept comfort from anyone until the gazer is dead.

---

### The Three Floors

**Ground Floor (Shop).** Bookshelves, display cases, a reading counter, a small lockbox behind the counter containing 15 gp and a spare set of shop keys. The gazer passed through here on the way up; there is minor damage. The staircase to the second floor is intact.

**Second Floor (Living Quarters).** Uza's apartments: a bedroom, a small sitting room, a kitchen. There are more books here — personal collection, not for sale. The gazer has been through here and has destroyed a small hand-mirror and a ceramic pot containing dried herbs, apparently by accident. The stairs to the third floor are narrow.

**Third Floor (Attic Storage and Workshop).** The highest floor is half storage — rolled maps, uncatalogued acquisitions in paper bundles — and half workspace, with a long table covered in bookbinding tools. The ceiling is low. The gazer is here.

---

### The Gazer Encounter

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The attic is low-ceilinged and dimly lit, the only light coming through a single shuttered window and the gaps between old roof planks. Hovering near the far end of the space, casting slow looks at the rafters above it, is a sphere of mottled flesh about the size of a cantaloupe, covered in eyes. Its central eye — large, yellowish, rimmed with thin veins — swings toward you as you emerge through the stair hatch. Four smaller eyes on stalks angle downward. Several eye-rays begin to charge simultaneously.</p>
    <p>From somewhere in the rafters overhead: a single, extraordinarily judgmental cat sound.</p>
</div>

<div class="fvtt advice">
    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/skills/melee/maneuver-greatsword-yellow.webp">
    </figure>
    <article>
        <h4>Gazer Combat: Protecting the Inventory</h4>
        <p>The <strong>Gazer</strong> (2024 <em>Monster Manual</em>) fires randomly targeted eye rays — it does not distinguish between party members and bookshelves. On any attack that misses a character, roll 1d6: on a 1–2, an eye ray strikes a shelf or bundle of books. First miss: books worth 15 gp are destroyed. Second miss: a shelf collapses into the stairwell, creating Difficult Terrain on the stairs (costs double movement to navigate). Third miss: a bundle of rare manuscripts catches a brief arcane flame — it will not spread, but they are ruined. Uza will notice all three categories of damage; she will note them without placing blame but the party will know.</p>
        <p>The attic space is a 30-by-20-foot room with 8-foot ceilings. Fillipa is in the rafters, 10 feet up, and will yowl at anyone who moves directly under her regardless of alignment or intention.</p>
        <p>The gazer has no interest in retreating. It has been trapped in this building for three days and is operating on frustrated instinct. It fights to the death.</p>
    </article>
</div>

**After the Gazer Falls.** Fillipa descends from the rafters approximately thirty seconds after the gazer stops moving. She will allow herself to be picked up by the first character who crouches down and holds out a hand without looking directly at her. She purrs with the frequency and volume of a small engine and does not leave willingly.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Harper character gains **+1 Renown**. The party delivers Fillipa to Uza at Felzoun's Folly. Uza's relief is quiet and complete; she holds the cat and says nothing for a moment.

Then she gives the party a used spellbook. "From a wizard who owed me more than he could pay. Four first-level spells and three second-level. The binding is coming loose but the text is sound." The specific spells are the DM's choice from the standard wizard spell list; *detect magic*, *identify*, *misty step*, and *invisibility* are appropriate for this stage of the campaign.

**The Skeemo Thread.** If the party asks Uza whether she knows anything about recent unusual purchases or inquiries in her area, she thinks for a moment. "A gnome came in last month. Skeemo Weirdbottle — runs a concoctions shop in this ward. He wanted books on the history of notable families and their scandals. Old materials. I sold him three volumes." She pauses. "He paid very well and asked about no one in particular, which made me wonder who he was interested in specifically."

This is not a required clue delivery — if the party does not ask, Uza does not volunteer it. But it plants Skeemo's name weeks before he becomes directly relevant, and a party that notes it will feel prescient when he resurfaces.

## Arc H-3 — The Doppelganger Assessment
### *4th Level — Renown +2*

---

### Overview

Harper operative Mattrim "Threestrings" Mereg has befriended a gang of doppelgangers and wants the organization to recruit them. The party is tasked with assessing each one's trustworthiness. What appears to be a procedural HR exercise rapidly becomes something more human: Mattrim genuinely cares about these people, some of them are genuinely worth caring about, and the party must decide what trust actually means when extended to a creature that can wear anyone's face.

**Dramatic Question:** *What does it mean to trust someone who is, by nature, unknowable? And can the party tell a trustworthy shapeshifter from an untrustworthy one — before one of them has already sold what it learned?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"One of our members, Mattrim Mereg — you may know him as Threestrings, the bard who busks at the Yawning Portal — has allied himself with a gang of doppelgangers and believes the Harpers should recruit them. We need an unbiased opinion. Track down and speak with each of the five. Gauge their trustworthiness. Mattrim will introduce you to their leader. We need to know: are any of them worth bringing in?"</p>
    <p>Mirt sets down his glass. "I should tell you — and this is not idle chatter — that one of the five has, in my considered opinion, already sold information about the party's Harper affiliation to someone with an interest in that information. I don't know which one. That is part of what you are there to find out."</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — Meeting Threestrings

Mattrim Mereg is at his usual spot near the Yawning Portal's main entrance, playing the three-stringed lute with more skill than the instrument's condition suggests is possible. He has lanky brown hair, gaudy clothes of purple and green, and the kind of goofy lopsided smile that people deploy to make you underestimate them. It works more often than it should.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Oh, good," he says, tucking the lute under one arm with evident relief. "Mirt sent you. Yes. Good. Look — I want to be clear about something before we go further. These are my friends. I know what that sounds like. But I've spent three months with them and I know them better than most people know anyone, and I think you'll find that Bonnie especially is — well. See for yourselves." He pauses. "Don't be rude to them. They'll know if you are, and they'll stop being honest."</p>
</div>

Mattrim will arrange for each doppelganger to meet the party at a specific table in the Yawning Portal, one per day over the following week, all appearing in human form. He trusts the party to handle the interviews but will be present at each one, nursing a drink and playing very quiet songs that are more perceptive than they sound.

---

### Scene 3 — The Five Interviews

Each interview takes place at the Yawning Portal over a separate evening. The party has access to whatever social tools they possess, plus whatever they can observe across the length of a shared table conversation.

**The Key Mechanic.** Each doppelganger's true disposition is concealed beneath a social surface. A DC 16 Wisdom (Insight) check made during the interview reveals something specific about their trustworthiness — not just a general sense, but a specific tell that informs the party's recommendation. The check can be assisted by specific conversational tactics: asking about a topic the doppelganger is invested in (something they care about reveals authentic response), or pressing on a claimed experience (untrustworthy ones have shallower backstories when questioned specifically).

**Bonnie** appears as a tiefling woman with violet skin and the exhausted competence of someone who has been navigating the Yawning Portal's social ecosystem for too long. She is direct, careful, and genuinely curious about the party. She asks as many questions as she answers. DC 16 Wisdom (Insight) reveals: she is telling the truth about essentially everything, including the things she declines to answer. She is trustworthy. She is also, quietly, frightened — she knows one of her gang has sold information and doesn't know who, and she cannot admit this without implicating herself. She will not name the traitor but will confirm, if the party presses with skill, that "there's been some trouble I haven't fully resolved."

**The Traitor.** The doppelganger who has sold information is whichever one the DM chooses or rolls for — except Bonnie. The tell: when pressed on a specific detail of their claimed background, they produce a story that is smooth and detailed in a way that suggests it was prepared, rather than remembered. Authentic memory produces hesitations, corrections, and specific irrelevant details. Prepared lies produce fluency. DC 16 Wisdom (Insight) reveals this specifically.

**The Hostile One.** A tall, angular human figure who introduces himself as Kael and immediately makes clear he considers this meeting pointless. He leaves after twenty minutes regardless of how the conversation goes. His Insight reveal: he is not untrustworthy so much as uninterested — he does not want Harper membership and is only here because Bonnie asked him to come. He is not the traitor.

**The Uncertain One.** Presents as a middle-aged Tethyrian woman named Syla. She answers every question with another question, not evasively but genuinely — she is using the interview as much as the party is. Her Insight reveal: she does not know if she wants to be trustworthy. She is not lying; she is genuinely undecided about who she wants to be. Not the traitor.

**The Self-Interested One.** Charming, personable, forthcoming about everything except the things that actually matter. His Insight reveal: he is already considering how Harper affiliation could be monetized. Not actively dangerous, but not trustworthy in the way the Harpers need.

---

### Scene 4 — The Complication: What Mattrim Knows

After the party completes all five interviews, Mattrim pulls them aside. He has been thinking. He has come to an uncomfortable conclusion.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He sets the lute down carefully and looks at the table for a moment. "I think one of them told someone about you. About your affiliation." He says it quietly, without drama. "I don't know which one. I've been going through the conversations and I can't — I keep coming up wrong." He looks up. "Whatever you recommend to Mirt, I'll abide by. But I need to tell you something else."</p>
    <p>"Bonnie knows. She's known for about a week. She hasn't told me because she thinks she can fix it herself. She can't." A pause. "If you're going to recommend against all of them, I won't fight it. I just wanted you to know that she tried."</p>
</div>

This puts the party's assessment in a different light. Bonnie is trustworthy, and she has been trying to protect her gang while knowing it is compromised. What the party recommends to Mirt — and whether they tell him about Bonnie's knowledge — is a character-defining moment in miniature.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Harper character gains **+2 Renown**. Every contributing party member receives 50 gp from Mirt as an assessment fee.

**Mirt's Response.** Mirt reads the party's recommendation and says nothing for a moment. Then: "Bonnie. Yes. I thought it might be Bonnie." He does not explain what he thought. He informs the party that he will reach out to Bonnie directly and that the other four will not be recruited. He does not ask what to do about the traitor. He has his own methods.

**The Downstream Effect.** The traitor's sale of information means that somewhere — at a Manshoon cell briefing, or in a Xanathar Guild report — there is now a document stating that the party are Harper operatives. This will not surface immediately. It will surface at the worst possible moment in Act III or IV, when someone already hostile to the party turns out to know more about them than they should.

## Arc H-4 — The Spy at Haventree's Party
### *5th Level — Renown +2*

---

### Overview

Remallia Haventree is hosting a dinner party at her North Ward villa, and a drow spy has infiltrated the guest list. The party must attend in appropriate attire, navigate the social demands of Waterdeep's upper-middle nobility, and identify the spy before the evening ends. The spy is Jarlaxle Baenre, operating under a flawless disguise, who has been attending Remallia's parties for three months and is using them to quietly identify Harper operatives. The encounter is a social puzzle with a DC of 24 and no combat whatsoever — the most dangerous kind.

**Dramatic Question:** *Can the party identify a drow master of disguise in a room full of people who are all, in their own way, performing?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Lady Remallia Haventree is hosting a party at House Ulbrinter, her villa on Delzorin Street between Vhezoar and Brondar's Way in the North Ward. We have reason to suspect a drow spy has infiltrated the guest list. Attend. Identify them. Dress sharply — Remallia's guests are the kind of people who judge you in the first three seconds, and she prefers her operatives to pass that judgment."</p>
    <p>He adds, after a pause, "Remallia knows you're coming. She doesn't know why. From her perspective, you are guests she invited as a favor to me. Behave accordingly."</p>
</div>

Formal wear is available for hire at a tailor on Delzorin Street; Mirt has left word that the party is expected.

---

### Scene 2 — House Ulbrinter

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>House Ulbrinter sits behind a low stone wall on Delzorin Street, its facade lit by pale driftglobes set in iron sconces. The entry hall is hung with tapestries depicting the history of an old Waterdhavian trading family, and a steward in silver-grey livery takes your names without expression and leads you through to the reception salon, where the evening's company has already assembled.</p>
    <p>The room is not large, but it is very carefully arranged. Tall windows overlook a private garden. A fire is going in the grate — more for atmosphere than warmth. The twelve guests are distributed across the room in that specific configuration that wealthy people use to signal their relationships to each other: who stands near whom, who is in conversation and who is performing availability. The smell is expensive flowers and more expensive wine.</p>
    <p>Remallia Haventree stands near the window — a precise, composed elven woman in grey and silver, hair swept back, watching the room with the patient attention of someone who knows exactly where all the pieces are. Her gaze finds you the moment you enter, confirms something, and moves on.</p>
</div>

**Remallia Haventree** (Harper operative; see Appendix A). She is warm in public and gives nothing away. She covers for the party's social rough edges with the practiced ease of someone who has been covering for operatives for a long time. If the party makes a significant social error (failing a DC 12 Charisma check for basic social navigation), she is at their elbow within thirty seconds with a smoothing remark that defuses the moment.

---

### Scene 3 — The Guests

The twelve guests are: three merchants (two of them known to the party through Waterdeep commercial dealings), a retired naval officer, an ambassador's aide from Neverwinter with a heavy accent, a House Ulbrinter cousin visiting from Silverymoon, a playwright, a young noble who has been recently widowed and is attempting to disguise this badly, and **Erystian Demarne** — a charming young actor from Luskan who seems to know everyone in the room.

**Erystian Demarne** is Jarlaxle in disguise. He has the appearance of a sandy-haired Illuskan northman in his late twenties: open-faced, enthusiastic, with a quick laugh and theatrical name-dropping. He speaks with an accent. He knows the history of every prominent Luskan theater company and several anecdotes about performances that, if checked, turn out to be real performances that took place in Luskan. He is, on all surface evidence, exactly who he claims to be.

**Detecting the Disguise.** A DC 24 Wisdom (Insight) check reveals the tells: Erystian Demarne is comfortable in every social context he encounters in a way that a young actor from Luskan should not be — as if he has been comfortable in social contexts far stranger than this, for far longer than any northman should have been. He orders wine in a way that reveals familiarity with a vintage only available at Luskan's finest establishments — and knows its provenance, unprompted. His hands, briefly visible when he adjusts his cuffs, have the small calluses of a hand-crossbow user along the thumb and index finger of the right hand, not the palm calluses of a theater worker.

The DC can be reduced by active investigation rather than passive observation. Characters who hold a sustained conversation with Erystian can reduce the DC by 2 per specific probing question — asking about his childhood neighborhood in Luskan (he has one, perfectly detailed), his training (also detailed), whether he knows a specific actor who worked at the Seven Masks Theater in Waterdeep (he knows, without hesitation, that the theater exists and who owns it). Each answer is perfect. That is, itself, suspicious to a careful observer — DC 22 after three questions, DC 20 after six.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Erystian Demarne has a quality that is difficult to name precisely. He is delightful. He is attentive. He remembers the name of your character who mentioned something in passing three sentences ago and uses it naturally in the next exchange, the way someone does when they were actually listening. He asks questions that are interesting without being intrusive. He makes everyone around him feel like the most interesting person in the room.</p>
    <p>He is enjoying himself enormously.</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 4 — Exposing Erystian Demarne

If the party successfully identifies "Erystian," they face a choice: how to expose him, to whom, and what to expect when they do.

Confronting him directly in company will cause a scene. Pulling him aside quietly for a private conversation is possible — he accepts the invitation with a smile.

In private, confronted with the specific tells the party has identified, he holds the persona for exactly as long as it continues to be interesting. Then:

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He considers you for a moment with the same open-faced expression. Then, very slowly, the smile shifts — still warm, but showing something else underneath it now. Something considerably more precise.</p>
    <p>"The calluses," he says. "I should have worn gloves." He straightens his cuffs, adjusting them back into place. "Well. You are better than I expected, which is a genuine pleasure. Most people who try this spend the evening watching my eyes and checking for dark skin."</p>
    <p>He looks at you with an attention that is no longer social performance. "I think Lady Haventree is going to hear about this tomorrow, and she will be very satisfied with herself. I think you should let her. The next time she and I meet, she'll be a little more confident than she should be, and that," he adds, with the air of a man filing something away, "will be useful."</p>
    <p>He inclines his head, adjusts his hat — which is, now that you look at it, slightly more theatrical than the rest of his outfit — and adds: "You've been excellent. Genuinely. I'll see you again."</p>
    <p>He walks back into the reception room, thanks Remallia for a lovely evening in perfect Illuskan-accented Common, and departs.</p>
</div>

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Harper character gains **+2 Renown**. Every party member who attended receives 200 gp from Remallia after the event — delivered the following morning by paper bird with a note that says simply: *Well done.*

**Mirt's Response.** If the party reports Jarlaxle's identity, Mirt is very quiet for a moment. "Jarlaxle Baenre. He's been attending her parties for three months." Another silence. "He's been mapping our Waterdeep network. He knows who we are and where we meet." A long pause. "He let you identify him. He could have left without anyone being the wiser." He looks at the party with an expression that is equal parts impressed and unnerved. "Which means he wanted us to know he knows."

This is the first moment in the campaign where Jarlaxle reveals himself as an actor who operates at a level above the other players. Characters who have also encountered him through Bregan D'aerthe have the particular pleasure of recognizing that everything they know about him is a fragment of a much larger picture.

# Act IIb — The Lords' Alliance Arcs

*The Alliance operates in daylight. Its missions are professional, assigned with clear expectations, and judged against those expectations. The Lords' Alliance arcs should escalate in moral weight: from straightforward public service, to targeted elimination, to political interference, to a final mission that puts the party directly between their faction's orders and their own conscience.*

---

## Arc LA-1 — Protecting the Dungsweepers
### *2nd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

Jalester assigns the party to protect a team of Dungsweepers' Guild workers through a tenday of street-cleaning shifts in the Trades Ward. The work is tedious, the company unglamorous, and for eight days nothing happens except the slow accumulation of knowledge about the city at ground level. On the ninth day, a carrion crawler erupts from the sewers. The mission's real drama, however, is not the monster — it is what eight days of escort duty teaches the party about the ordinary people who keep Waterdeep functioning.

**Dramatic Question:** *Eight days of nothing, one day of genuine danger. What does it mean to show up, day after day, for work that seems beneath you?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"A gang war is causing unrest throughout the city. We have offered protection to members of the Dungsweepers' Guild, and you have been assigned to protect a group of them. Meet them at the Muleskull Tavern, on Ship Street in the Dock Ward, at six bells and guard them while they work. Do this every day for a tenday."</p>
    <p>He says it with the same tone he would use to assign any other mission. He does not comment on the nature of the work. He does not say it will be boring, because he considers that irrelevant.</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — The Muleskull Tavern at Six Bells

A working dockworkers' bar that opens before dawn for the people who start work before the city wakes. At six bells in the morning it smells of strong tea and yesterday's spilled beer and the cold salt air coming off the Dock Ward waterfront.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The four dungsweepers assembled near the door have the specific quality of people who are about to start a job that nobody wants to do and have made their peace with it years ago. They are carrying brooms, rakes, and a wheeled cart with a large covered bin. Their foreman is a stout dwarf woman of approximately sixty, with a grey braid and the expression of someone who has lived entirely on her own terms and sees no reason to change that now.</p>
    <p>She looks the party up and down with the assessment of someone who has been assigned assistants before and found them consistently disappointing. "You're the escort," she says. It's not a question.</p>
</div>

**Berna** (dwarf **Commoner**). Twenty years with the Dungsweepers' Guild, seventeen of them doing the Trades Ward circuit. She is not rude; she is economical. She will not make small talk. She expects the party to do their job — which she defines as staying close, looking capable, and not getting underfoot. She will soften over the tenday as she determines that the party is, in her assessment, not useless.

---

### The Eight Days

Run the first tenday as a montage, interrupted by specific moments the party experiences. The DM should describe two or three incidents from the list below, spaced across the days, to give texture to the routine.

**Day Two.** Berna's crew is working near the Market when a City Watch officer stops them for a routine check. The officer treats them with undisguised contempt — the dungsweepers are beneath notice, and their escort barely registers. Berna's jaw tightens but she says nothing. After the Watch officer leaves, she goes back to work without comment.

**Day Four.** While working through a Market alley, one of the sweepers — a young Calishite man named Herath — finds a coin purse containing 12 gp, apparently dropped by someone in a hurry. He immediately turns it in to the nearest Watch post without discussion, then returns to work. He catches the party watching and shrugs. "It's not mine," he says. This is the entirety of his moral framework on the subject.

**Day Six.** Over lunch at a corner stall, Berna talks, unprompted, about why the gang war is more dangerous for her workers than for most people. "We go into alleys that nobody else goes into. We move slow. We carry equipment, not weapons. If something happens, the Watch isn't going to respond quickly for us." She is not complaining. She is briefing.

**Day Seven.** A drunk man in the early morning tries to start an argument with one of the sweepers over whose guild takes priority in a specific alley. This is an actual guild jurisdictional dispute that has been unresolved for six years. Berna defuses it with four sentences, all of them technically accurate, and keeps moving. She is very good at her job.

---

### Scene 3 — Day Nine: The Carrion Crawler

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>It's highsun on the ninth day, the Trades Ward beginning to fill up with lunchtime foot traffic, when the alley to the left erupts. The sound arrives before the creature — a wet, chittering scrape of too many legs on cobblestone — and then a carrion crawler the size of a cart horse lunges out of a sewer grate at street level, its centipede body whipping around as it hauls itself clear of the opening.</p>
    <p>Two City Watch guards sprint around the corner behind it, both of them clearly losing the pursuit. One is limping. The crawler rears back on its hindmost legs, tasting the air with its eyestalks, and fixes on the nearest person — which is Berna, who drops her broom with the calm efficiency of someone who has been doing this job for twenty years and has not survived it by panicking.</p>
    <p>"Well," she says. "There we are."</p>
</div>

**The Carrion Crawler** (2024 *Monster Manual*). Stagger Saving Throw DC 13 (Constitution); on a failed save the target is Incapacitated until the start of its next turn. The creature came up through the sewer system — likely disturbed by Xanathar Guild activity in the tunnels below — and is disoriented and aggressive. It is in a 20-foot-wide Trades Ward alley with market stalls on both sides.

The two **City Watch Guards** will assist but are already depleted (each at approximately half hit points from the pursuit). Berna and her three sweepers take cover behind the cart. Herath has found a broom handle and is considering his options with the expression of someone who knows the answer is "no" but wants to be sure.

**After the Combat.** Berna picks up her broom. She looks at the downed crawler, then at the party. "Same time tomorrow," she says. She means it.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Lords' Alliance character gains **+1 Renown**. Berna's name and trust are a minor but genuine asset. She knows the Trades Ward's alleys better than almost anyone, and she has seen things — packages moved at odd hours, people who don't belong — that she will share with the party if asked directly. She will not do it for free; she will do it if asked as one professional to another.

A small additional detail: Herath, on the last day, presses a folded note into the party's hands as they part ways. It gives the location of a sewer access point in the Trades Ward that his crew uses — a back-route into the sewer system that bypasses the Watch checkpoint on Coin Alley. He offers it with a shrug. "In case you ever need to move fast."

## Arc LA-2 — Finding Harko
### *3rd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

Jalester assigns the party to locate and eliminate Harko Swornhold, an exiled adventurer who has returned to Waterdeep illegally and is being used by the Xanathar Guild to incite gang violence. The investigation leads through the Dock Ward's night life, concluding in an alley confrontation — but the real drama comes from Harko's coded note, which points toward a Xanathar Guild operation that is larger than this single mission, and from the quiet moral weight of an extrajudicial order from a legitimate authority.

**Dramatic Question:** *The Alliance wants this done quietly. "Quietly" means without report, without warrant, and without a body the Watch can trace back. Is that something the party will do?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Harko Swornhold, an evil adventurer who was exiled three years ago for attempting to bribe a city magistrate, has returned to Waterdeep illegally. We think the Xanathar Guild is using him to incite violence — recruiting kenku for street disruption, keeping the gang war burning. He was last seen in the Dock Ward. Find him and quietly put him to the sword."</p>
    <p>He meets your eyes. "Quietly."</p>
</div>

If the party asks why they can't simply arrest Harko: "He has no outstanding warrants in Waterdeep that can be proved to the magisters' standard. The original bribery incident was handled by exile rather than prosecution. His return is a crime, but a minor one — the penalty is re-exile, not execution, and re-exile means he's back in three months." He pauses. "The damage he does in three months will exceed this conversation."

He does not pressure the party further. He is telling them the Alliance's reasoning. What they do with it is their decision.

---

### Scene 2 — The Investigation

Three DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation) checks, each representing approximately 8 hours of canvassing the Dock Ward. Other characters can assist, granting Advantage. Three failures before three successes means Harko has moved; the party must wait 24 hours and start again.

**Sources.** The Dock Ward is not cooperative, but it is not monolithic. A fencewoman on Net Street has seen kenku gathering near the Muleskull Tavern after dark. A retired harbor pilot at the Muleskull itself has heard raised voices — "a big human, northern accent" — in the alley behind the tavern on multiple nights. A Night Soil Guild worker (Berna's counterpart in the Dock Ward, if the party made that connection) has seen a man matching Harko's description twice near the Dock Street warehouses, coming and going after midnight.

The investigation should be rolled out as actual conversations, not just dice results. Dock Ward residents are not hostile to the party, but they are not eager to involve themselves in faction business, and some of them know — or suspect — that Harko's backers include people with longer memories than the Watch.

---

### Scene 3 — The Alley Behind the Muleskull

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The alley behind the Muleskull Tavern is narrow enough to force single file. At the far end, three large crates provide partial cover against the wall. The light here is what spills from the tavern's rear windows — orange and uneven, turning everything slightly amber.</p>
    <p>Harko Swornhold is here. He's a broad-shouldered human in a scarred leather coat that has seen more than one fight, speaking in low tones to two kenku who are exchanging something between them in quick, practiced silence. He clocks you the moment you enter the alley. His hand goes to his blade without hesitation and without visible surprise. He has been doing things like this long enough that being cornered in an alley at night is not, by itself, alarming.</p>
    <p>"Alliance," he says. Not a question. "Jalester's still sending children, I see."</p>
</div>

**Harko Swornhold** (**Bandit Captain**, 2024 *Monster Manual*). A former mid-level adventurer, fifty years old, still capable of genuine violence but not exceptional at it. He does not panic and he does not negotiate: he has been offered exile twice and considers that bridge thoroughly burned. He fights to win and will not surrender.

**His Two Kenku Companions** (**Kenku**, 2024 *Monster Manual*). They fight alongside Harko but will flee if he is reduced to 0 hit points or clearly losing. They carry nothing of intelligence value.

**Harko's Coat.** In the inside left pocket is a folded note written in Xanathar Guild cipher. The cipher is not sophisticated — it was designed for use by people with limited literacy — and can be decoded with a DC 12 Intelligence check or with a day's work by someone with cryptographic training. The decoded contents identify two upcoming "disruption targets" — a specific intersection in the Trades Ward and a named warehouse in the Dock Ward — and reference a Guild contact by the letter K, meeting "at the eastern staircase" at a time that is two days away. K is Keln Trigos, based at the Terasse Estate on Elsambul's Lane.

---

### Scene 4 — The Complication: A Body and a Witness

Harko falls in the alley. The kenku flee. The party is alone with a dead man in the Dock Ward at night.

Or almost alone.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The sound of rapid footsteps from the alley entrance. A young woman — early twenties, aproned, carrying a tray she clearly just set down somewhere in a hurry — rounds the corner and stops dead at the sight of what has happened. She looks at Harko, then at the party, then at Harko again. She is a serving girl from the Muleskull. She has a face that has very abruptly processed a great deal of information and is still working on what to do with it.</p>
    <p>"I was just going to the back door," she says.</p>
</div>

The serving girl is **Lira**, a Calishite woman who works evenings at the Muleskull. She is not a Xanathar Guild agent or a Zhentarim plant. She is a person who just witnessed a killing. What she does next depends entirely on how the party handles the next sixty seconds.

**If the party is immediately honest:** She is frightened, but responds to directness. She knows Harko — he has been drinking at the Muleskull for two weeks, always with the kenku, always talking quietly, never starting trouble inside. She is not going to go to the Watch tonight. She will decide what to do tomorrow. "He wasn't a good person," she says quietly. "But that's not the same as this being right." She leaves.

**If the party lies or threatens:** She says nothing and leaves. She goes to the Watch the following morning. A Watch report enters the system noting a body in the alley behind the Muleskull and a description of the party. Jalester receives the report, sighs, and says: "I said quietly." He does not formally discipline the party — there is no official mission record — but he notes the competence gap.

**What "Quietly" Actually Means.** This scene is the mission's real test. Jalester's order to do this "quietly" was not just about avoiding a Watch investigation — it was a test of whether the party can operate at the level the Alliance needs them to. Lira's presence is not a trap; it is the reality of working in a city full of people. The party that handles Lira with honesty and care is the party the Alliance wants. The party that doesn't will still complete the mission — but Jalester will notice.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Lords' Alliance character gains **+1 Renown**. Harko's coded note is a Grand Game intelligence bonus — the two disruption targets and the reference to "K" point toward the Terasse Estate, a Xanathar Guild outpost that becomes relevant in the Grand Game investigation.

## Arc LA-3 — Scuttling the Thayan Deal
### *4th Level — Renown +2*

---

### Overview

Jalester assigns the party to prevent Manshoon's Zhentarim from recruiting a Red Wizard of Thay named Esloon Bezant. The mission offers three routes: deception, confrontation, and bribery. Its real interest lies in Esloon himself — a man who fled real persecution, runs a marginally-criminal protection operation, and is being courted by people who will eventually have him killed. The party can choose to give him something to think about beyond the immediate transaction.

**Dramatic Question:** *How do you stop a deal without making an enemy of the man who was about to close it?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The Zhents are courting a Red Wizard of Thay named Esloon Bezant — Manshoon's people, not the Doom Raiders. He fled Thay some years back and has been running a small protection gang in the Dock Ward ever since. We think they want him for his arcane capability. A Thayan mage allied with Manshoon's cell is not a problem we want to develop." He hands over a description. "Scuttle the deal. However you like, as long as it doesn't trace back to us. Fast."</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — The Rusted Anchor

Esloon Bezant can usually be found in the back corner of a Dock Ward tavern called the Rusted Anchor on Dock Street — a low-ceilinged place that smells of salt, cheap oil, and the particular brand of quiet that exists in places where people are conducting business they would prefer not to be observed.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Esloon Bezant does not look like a Red Wizard. He looks like a careful man in his forties who has been somewhere difficult and has been thinking about it ever since. Compact, precise, with a shaved head that shows the faded remnants of the tattoos of someone who started the Red Wizard initiation and stopped partway through. He has a cup of something warm in front of him and he is watching the door in the way of someone who always watches the door.</p>
    <p>He looks at the party as they enter, makes some assessment, and does not react. "You're not who I was expecting," he says. "Sit down."</p>
</div>

**Esloon Bezant** (LE male Thayan human **Mage**, 2024 *Monster Manual*). A careful, intelligent man who has spent five years building something functional from nothing in a city that had no reason to welcome him. He is not good — his protection operation involves coercion and occasional violence — but he is not sadistic, and he has a clear-eyed sense of exactly where he sits in Waterdeep's power structure. He knows the Zhentarim offer is not entirely sincere. He is considering it anyway because his current situation is precarious and the alternative is worse.

His five **Thugs** are present in the tavern, distributed at other tables, not concealed. He has made no effort to hide them. He is not expecting a fight; he is establishing that a fight would be expensive.

---

### The Three Paths

**Deception.** The party manufactures evidence that Manshoon's people intend to eliminate Esloon once his usefulness is exhausted — a forged letter "intercepted" from a Zhentarim courier suggesting that his recruitment is a pretext to acquire his spellbook before "resolving" him. This requires 25 gp in bribes to establish the letter's apparent provenance and a DC 16 Charisma (Deception) check. On a success, Esloon's expression goes still and cold. He reads the letter twice. He puts it in his coat. "I'll consider this," he says. He will — and his consideration will result in a politely venomous message to the Zhentarim cell that ends the negotiation permanently.

**Direct Confrontation.** Telling Esloon plainly that the Lords' Alliance is aware of his recruitment and will make his life in Waterdeep considerably more complicated if he accepts. This requires either demonstrating real leverage (Jalester's authority, knowledge of Esloon's specific illegal operations) or winning a DC 15 Charisma (Intimidation) check. On a success, he looks at the party for a long moment. "Inconvenient," he says. On a failure, he invites the party to leave.

**Bribery.** Offering Esloon 500 gp to decline the deal. He looks at the money, looks at the party, and says: "That's not a lot, for what you're asking." But he considers it, and if the party can add something else — assurance of Watch non-interference with his current operation for six months, or a letter of introduction to a neutral party who might offer him a more legitimate arrangement — he will accept.

---

### Scene 3 — The Complication: What Esloon Actually Wants

After the initial negotiation, however the party has approached it, Esloon leans back and says something unexpected.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"I want to ask you something. Not about the deal." He looks at the table for a moment. "I fled Thay because they were going to kill me. Not because I had done something wrong by their standards — I had done something right, which amounted to the same thing." He meets the party's eyes. "I have been in this city for five years. I have built something here. Not legitimate, I know. But real."</p>
    <p>"The Zhentarim offered me protection. Real protection, not the Alliance's kind where you exist at their convenience. I am considering it because it is the only serious offer I have received."</p>
    <p>He looks at them directly. "Is there another offer? Not from you personally. From whoever decides these things."</p>
</div>

Esloon is asking whether there is a path in Waterdeep that does not end with him being used and discarded by whoever is currently convenient. There may or may not be — but the party can choose to bring this question back to Jalester. Jalester's response, if the party does: a long pause, then: "He's not wrong that we're asking him for a favor without offering much. I'll look into whether there's something we can formalize." This is not a commitment. But it is Jalester acknowledging a point.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Lords' Alliance character gains **+2 Renown**. Esloon's spellbook (if obtained through combat) is a significant asset. If Esloon is left neutral rather than hostile, he becomes an occasional minor intelligence source about Dock Ward criminal activity — not allied, but aware that the party dealt with him honestly, which counts for something in his world.

## Arc LA-4 — The Rooftop Assassin
### *5th Level — Renown +2*

---

### Overview

A rooftop assassin has been picking off targets in the North Ward. Jalester wants him found and delivered to the Watch alive. The hunt leads back to Trollskull Alley itself — and to a man named Ziraj the Hunter, a Doom Raiders operative conducting unsanctioned kills on personal motivation. The mission places Doom Raiders characters in direct conflict with their faction's interests and forces all characters to decide what "alive" means when the person being arrested is, arguably, on their side.

**Dramatic Question:** *The mission says alive. The person you find is complicated. What do you actually do?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"We have reports of an assassin prowling the rooftops near the North Ward, picking off targets with a hand crossbow. Three dead in the last tenday — pattern suggests Xanathar Guild members, but the Watch can't confirm it. My sources say he goes to ground somewhere near Trollskull Alley." He pauses. "Find him. Alert the Watch to his whereabouts. Aid in his arrest if you can."</p>
    <p>Another pause. "Don't kill him. Doing that could escalate the violence further — there are people in the Guild who would treat a dead Zhentarim operative as license for retaliation. Alive, he might be turned. Alive, the Watch can process him. Alive, the violence stays where it is." He looks at the party steadily. "Alive."</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — The Hunt

Three DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation) checks, each representing 8 hours. On three successes, the party locates Ziraj. On three failures before three successes, he has moved; wait 24 hours.

**Sources.** Trollskull Alley's residents are the key. **Fala Lefaliir** at Corellon's Crown noticed the greenhouse rooftop access has been used — the lock has been opened and re-locked from the outside, twice, and there's a faint smell of crossbow oil near the door. **Vincent Trench** at the Tiger's Eye, the private investigator, has been watching the same roofline for his own reasons — someone hired him to monitor activity on Trollskull Alley's upper floors — but declines to say who hired him. **Rishaal the Page-Turner** at the Book Wyrm noticed a very large, very quiet individual buy a candle, three strips of dried meat, and a thin blanket "for cold nights" at a market stall near the alley about a week ago. None of these are conclusions on their own. Together, they point clearly at Corellon's Crown.

---

### Scene 3 — Corellon's Crown Greenhouse

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The greenhouse on the third floor of Corellon's Crown is a glass-and-iron structure built as an extension of the upper story, filled with Fala's herbs, flowering trees, and medicinal plants in clay pots. At this hour it is lit only by the slow cycling of the driftglobes Fala sets at night — soft blue-white, drifting among the leaves like tethered moons.</p>
    <p>Between two large flowering trees near the eastern wall, a figure sits cross-legged on the floor. He is enormous — a half-orc of unusual even by half-orc standards, broad across the shoulders, with a face that has been in a number of fights and remembers all of them. A hand crossbow lies disassembled on a cloth in front of him: bolt casing, string, the individual components of the mechanism, laid out with the careful precision of someone who does this for maintenance rather than repair. He has not heard you enter.</p>
    <p>Then he tilts his head, very slightly, still looking at the crossbow components, and says: "I wondered when you'd come."</p>
</div>

**Ziraj the Hunter** (**Assassin**, *Dragon Heist* appendix B; treat as **Veteran** with assassin modifications for the 2024 rules). He is enormous and quiet in the way that very large, very capable people sometimes are — compression rather than absence. He is not afraid of the party. He is not going to fight the party. He lowers the crossbow the moment they enter, sets it on the cloth, and waits.

He is a Doom Raider. His kills have been Xanathar Guild members who murdered a woman he was paid to protect six months ago. He tracked each of them for weeks before acting. He did not tell Davil because Davil would have told him to stop.

He will go with the Watch without resistance. He believes, with some justification, that the Doom Raiders will extract him from custody.

**What He Offers.** He has watched enough Xanathar Guild movements from the rooftops of the North Ward to know the location of one Guild outpost in the Dock Ward — the Terasse Estate's side entrance on Copper Alley, used for weapons transfers. He will trade this for one thing: a message delivered to Yagra. The message is two words: *"He's sorry."* He will not explain what the message means or why he cannot send it himself.

---

### Scene 4 — The Complication: What Happens When the Watch Arrives

When the City Watch arrives at Corellon's Crown — either because the party alerted them, or because the sound of the confrontation attracted attention — Fala Lefaliir is also awake, drawn by the noise.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Fala appears at the greenhouse door in a robe, lamp in hand, taking in the situation with the calm of someone who has learned not to react to things before understanding them. They look at Ziraj, then at the party, then at Ziraj's disassembled crossbow on the cloth.</p>
    <p>"He has been sleeping in my greenhouse for a week," Fala says quietly, to no one in particular. "He fixed the leak in the east gutter. I was going to ask him to leave, and I didn't, and I am not sure now whether that was right." They look at the party. "Is he going somewhere difficult?"</p>
</div>

Fala will not obstruct the Watch. But they will remember this, and if the party ever needs Fala's testimony about a Trollskull Alley incident, this moment will be in Fala's mind — the night they watched someone taken away from their greenhouse, and the party that made it happen.

**Doom Raiders Characters.** The mission requires Doom Raiders characters to hand a colleague to the Watch. This is exactly the kind of conflict the Alexandrian Remix is designed to produce. They can follow Jalester's orders, find a creative way to comply with the letter while violating the spirit, or refuse. All three options have consequences. All three options are valid.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Lords' Alliance character gains **+2 Renown**. Each character who aids in Ziraj's capture receives 50 gp from Jalester. The Terasse Estate intelligence, if Ziraj's trade was accepted, is a Grand Game bonus clue. The message to Yagra, if delivered, earns a moment of genuine emotion from her — she says nothing, but the party will notice.

# Act IIc — The Emerald Enclave Arcs

*The Enclave's missions are the quietest in the campaign — the most geographically wide-ranging, the most attuned to the non-human world, and the most deliberately unhurried. They take place outside the Grand Game's immediate pressure. They are about the city's relationship to the natural world — and about what happens to that relationship when the natural world pushes back.*

---

## Arc EE-1 — The Undercliff Scarecrows
### *2nd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

Three animated scarecrows are terrorizing farms east of Waterdeep. The City Guard is occupied with the gang war and considers animated agricultural fixtures beneath their threshold. The party must locate and destroy all three in the Undercliff's open terrain — but the real challenge is tactical, not martial: three independent threats spread across a mile of farmland, operating at unpredictable intervals, with no fixed location.

**Dramatic Question:** *How do you hunt something that doesn't stay still?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Outlying farms are being terrorized by scarecrows come to life. It has slaughtered livestock, chased horses, and knocked one farmer unconscious. No people killed yet, but the City Guard is dragging its heels — they have a gang war to manage and three animated scarecrows are below their threshold." A brief pause. "Something must be done."</p>
</div>

Melannor delivers this by *animal messenger* — a pigeon that lands on the windowsill of Trollskull Manor and speaks the briefing in his voice before flying away. He has not specified a deadline. The Enclave trusts the party to understand urgency.

---

### Scene 2 — The Undercliff

The Undercliff is the farming terraces east of Waterdeep's walls, accessible through Trollgate. In spring it is green and actively worked: root vegetable plots, sheep runs, small grain fields, orchards coming into blossom. It smells of earth, manure, and the cold clean air off the harbor below.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The Undercliff at late afternoon has the specific quality of a place that has recently been frightened. The birds are thin on the ground. The farms you pass show signs of activity — tools put down mid-task, a garden gate left open — but no one is working. A sheep is standing alone in a field with its back to a stone wall, facing outward, which is not how sheep usually stand. Near the farthest east field, you can see the post where a scarecrow used to be: the crossbar is still tied with rope, but the straw and clothes are gone.</p>
</div>

**The Three Scarecrows.** Each **Scarecrow** (2024 *Monster Manual*) wears a distinct appearance and operates in a different section of the Undercliff:

- *The Sackcloth One* (northern fields): moves mostly at dusk, has been attacking the livestock rather than people.
- *The Pumpkin Head* (central orchards): operates at night, prefers chasing anything that runs — horses, dogs, people moving quickly.
- *The Blanket* (southern terraces near the Trollgate road): the most recently animated; has been seen in daylight twice and appears to be establishing a territory near the road.

---

### Scene 3 — The Farmer

A halfling named Gerrick runs the northernmost farm that has been attacked. He is fifty years old, extremely angry, and has decided to deal with the situation himself.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Gerrick Goodbarrel is standing alone in the middle of his north field with a pitchfork, a lantern, and the expression of a halfling who has run out of patience with animated straw.</p>
    <p>"The Guard said they'd send someone in a fortnight," he says, without turning around. He has heard the party approaching. "A fortnight. Three of my chickens and my best laying goose. And Marigold — that's my cart horse — she hasn't been right since Tuesday." He turns around. "Are you the Guard?"</p>
    <p>"No? Good. The Guard hasn't done anything useful in forty years."</p>
</div>

Gerrick has information: he has been watching the patterns. The sackcloth one comes from the north when the wind is off the harbor. The pumpkin head moves when there are lights in the orchard after dark — he thinks it's attracted to light, or to movement near light. The blanket one he's only seen once, near the road, moving south.

He also knows, because he has farmed this land for thirty years, that the Undercliff has a specific topography: a dry streambed runs east-west through the middle terraces, which creates a natural funnel that anything moving from north to south has to cross. "If you could set something in that streambed," he says, not quite asking.

**The Tactical Problem.** The streambed suggestion is useful. The party can use it to create a position that intercepts at least one scarecrow naturally, but they still have to locate and deal with the other two. A DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) check identifies tracks left by each scarecrow's movement pattern, allowing the party to predict the next likely appearance point for each one. Setting a fire (scarecrows are flammable — fire damage suppresses their Damage Immunity) is permitted but risks spreading to the fields in dry conditions.

---

### Scene 4 — The Encounters

Each scarecrow encounter takes place in terrain that plays to the creature's habits. Run these as separate combat encounters on separate nights, or as a single intensive effort if the party chooses to force all three in one day by moving aggressively through the terrain.

The Pumpkin Head pursues any fleeing character. The Sackcloth One uses its reach to attack livestock before targeting party members — it will make for the party's horses or mounts if they have them. The Blanket has taken up a position near a road milestone and will attack anyone who approaches it; it cannot be snuck up on (its supernatural senses) but can be drawn into the open by a light source.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Emerald Enclave character gains **+1 Renown**. Gerrick, if the party saved him from attempting to pitchfork a scarecrow, owes them a favor — his family has farmed the Undercliff for four generations and knows the ground beneath the terraces better than most. He knows an old drainage tunnel, sealed years ago, that connects to the cistern system under the Castle Ward. This is not immediately useful. It will be later.

## Arc EE-2 — The Necromancer's Harvest
### *3rd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

The party assists Sir Ambrose Everdawn, a champion of Kelemvor, in a ten-night patrol of the City of the Dead's northern section. Someone is stealing bones and animating them as skeletons; the necromancer is never found. The mission is atmospheric above all else — ten nights in the city's vast, driftglobe-lit cemetery, watching the dead move and not finding the person making them move. The value is in what happens in the spaces between the encounters.

**Dramatic Question:** *What does sustained vigilance look like when the threat remains invisible?*

---

### Scene 1 — Meeting Sir Ambrose

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Sir Ambrose Everdawn is waiting at the southern gate of the City of the Dead at sunset, and he has clearly been there for a while. He is a grizzled Tethyrian man of approximately sixty, built like a retired blacksmith — broad, heavy, still clearly dangerous — wearing plain travelling clothes and a sword that has the look of long use rather than ceremony. His only symbol of office is a small silver gauntlet pin at his collar, which is Kelemvor's, not Tyr's. He has the patient quality of someone who has spent a lot of nights in graveyards and does not find it strange.</p>
    <p>He looks the party over with the assessment of someone who has been assigned assistants before and has not always been pleasantly surprised. "You intend to help," he says. It's not a question, but it isn't certain either.</p>
    <p>A DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check, or simply demonstrating competence and sincerity, converts his assessment from skeptical to collegial. Without it, he assigns the patrol with specific instructions and expects results, not conversation.</p>
</div>

He explains the situation plainly: bones have been going missing from three mausoleums in the northern section. He patrols the south half after dark. He is asking the party to cover the north. They have permission from the City Guard to be present. He will meet them at the central gate path at dawn to compare notes.

---

### Scene 2 — Ten Nights in the City of the Dead

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The City of the Dead at night fills with driftglobes. They come from the living city at dusk — hundreds of them, from every ward and street — and spend their hours of darkness drifting among the headstones and mausoleums in slow, purposeless paths, like thoughts looking for something to settle on. No one knows why they come here. No one has been able to stop them. The accepted position is that they do not hurt anything and they provide excellent visibility for the cemetery's nighttime guardians, so the question of why has been tabled indefinitely.</p>
</div>

Each night, there is a 10% cumulative chance of encountering six **Skeletons** in the northern section. The encounters themselves are not dramatic — skeletons are not clever — but the pattern is disturbing: each time they appear, they are moving toward a specific mausoleum, then returning from it. The mausoleum is the Brandath family crypt.

The necromancer is never found. The bones stop moving after the tenth night.

**What Sir Ambrose Shares** (over the ten nights, if the party invests in the relationship). He has been patrolling this cemetery for fifteen years. He knew something was wrong in the northern section three months before the bones started moving — there was a quality to the air near the Brandath crypts that he recognized from two other times in his career: the presence of something old and wrong, contained but restless. He does not know what is in the Brandath crypts. He knows something is there that should not be.

This is a Grand Game seed. The Vault of Dragons is beneath the Brandath crypts. Sir Ambrose has been feeling its presence for months without knowing what it is.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Emerald Enclave character gains **+1 Renown**. Each party member who patrolled for all ten nights receives 100 gp from the Enclave. Sir Ambrose, at the final dawn meeting, says: "The Brandath crypts. Whatever is happening there — it wasn't the bones. The bones were a symptom." He looks north across the cemetery. "Be careful near that mausoleum."

## Arc EE-3 — The Doppelganger Problem
### *4th Level — Renown +2*

---

### Overview

Melannor asks the party to convince Bonnie's doppelgangers to leave Waterdeep. The Enclave's concern is stability, not trustworthiness. The mission places the party in an uncomfortable position: Bonnie may be someone they have come to know through the Harper missions, and she has done nothing wrong. The Enclave's request is not unjust — shapeshifters operating freely in a city under faction pressure are a genuine threat to civic balance — but it is also not kind.

**Dramatic Question:** *The right thing for the city may not be the right thing for the person. How do you hold both of those at once?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Doppelgangers threaten the balance of power in Waterdeep. They can become anyone, learn anything, and no one they impersonate knows until it is too late. We have reports that a gang of them has taken up residence in the Yawning Portal, using the constant traffic of strangers to blend in. The Enclave wants them gone." A pause. "We are not asking you to destroy them. We are asking you to convince them to leave."</p>
</div>

If the party has completed the Harper mission H-3 and knows Bonnie, this is the moment to let that complicate the briefing. Melannor does not know about the party's Harper relationship with the doppelgangers. He knows only what the Enclave knows: there are five shapeshifters using the Yawning Portal as a base, and their presence is a destabilizing factor.

---

### Scene 2 — Finding Bonnie

Bonnie is at her usual station at the Yawning Portal, working the evening shift in her tiefling guise. She sees the party the moment they enter and there is, in her expression, a flicker of something — not surprise, because doppelgangers do not surprise easily, but recognition of a new complication.

She signals the party to a quiet table. If the party has met her before through Harper channels, she speaks first: "You're here about the Enclave. Someone told them we're here." She does not seem alarmed. She seems resigned.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"I know what you're going to ask," she says. "And I know why the Enclave wants it. It's reasonable." She looks at the table for a moment. "Waterdeep is the most interesting city I have lived in. I have lived in eleven cities. I have been here for eight months and I have not had to be anyone I didn't choose to be." She looks up. "That is not a small thing."</p>
    <p>"How long do I have?"</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 3 — The Negotiation

Convincing Bonnie requires a DC 15 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion) check, but the DC drops to 12 if the party acknowledges the genuine cost of what they are asking and engages honestly with it. She is not unreasonable; she is weighing options.

The complication: one of her gang — the same untrustworthy one identified in H-3, if that mission was completed — is present in the tavern and works actively to undermine the negotiation. He does not want to leave Waterdeep. He has arrangements here that he is not going to disclose. He interposes in the conversation to redirect it, to raise new objections, to suggest that the party cannot be trusted.

**Identifying the Saboteur.** A DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check reveals that his objections are self-interested rather than principled — he is not arguing for staying; he is arguing against leaving, which are not the same thing. If the party calls this out directly, the dynamic shifts: Bonnie looks at him for a long moment, and then looks at the party, and says: "He's the one who sold your names."

This is the moment where the mission becomes about something bigger than the Enclave's request. The traitor has been found — not through the Harper investigation but through a different faction's mission colliding with it. What happens next is determined by what the party does with the information.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Emerald Enclave character gains **+2 Renown**. If Bonnie agrees to leave, she gives the party a small piece of information as a parting courtesy: the name of the person who bought the intelligence her traitor sold. This name connects to a Manshoon cell operative and becomes directly relevant in Act III.

## Arc EE-4 — The Grells in the Dock Ward

### Overview

Two grells have established themselves in an abandoned warehouse near the Dock Ward waterfront and have been snatching citizens. The party must hunt them down, deal with a dark and treacherous combat environment, and rescue at least one living victim before the mission is complete.

**Dramatic Question:** *Two monsters, one dark warehouse, one victim you might save if you move quickly enough. Which problem do you address first?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The Xanathar Guild is releasing monsters to distract the City Watch while its members operate elsewhere. The authorities have been having trouble with flying aberrations in the Dock Ward — something has been snatching citizens from the waterfront at night. An old woman disappeared three nights ago. Unless we intervene, she won't be the last."</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — The Investigation

Finding the grells requires a DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation) check (representing approximately an hour of information-gathering) followed by a DC 18 Wisdom (Survival) check to track them to their nest. Both checks represent different types of work: the Investigation check involves talking to Dock Ward residents and a Harbor Master's assistant; the Survival check involves reading signs in the physical environment — overturned crates near the water, a smell that does not belong, the absence of the usual cats that prowl the dock warehouses.

The nest is in a warehouse at the southern end of the Dock Ward's waterfront, two blocks from the harbor. Its loading doors on the upper story have been left open.

<div class="fvtt narrative">

The things hanging from the rafters are not shapes you recognize at first — you process "round," then "large," then "tentacles," then the cold-water realization of what you are looking at. Two of them. One has noticed you. The other is focused on something in the far corner of the floor, something small and not moving very much, wrapped loosely in a web of material that might be organic, might be fibrous, might be something from a world that is not this one.</p>

**Two Grells** (2024 *Monster Manual*). They attack with tentacles (DC 13 Constitution or Incapacitated until end of next turn) and beaks. Both are present; one was distracted by the victim and will turn its attention to the party on the second round.

**Mirsa** (the victim). An elderly Tethyrian woman, a seamstress who works the docks market. She is unconscious and cocooned in grell secretion — immobile but uninjured. Freeing her requires an Action and a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check; the secretion is tough but not adhesive. She cannot be freed safely while a grell is within 5 feet of her.

**The Tactical Problem.** Both grells in the open space. One victim in the far corner. Ranged attacks require dealing with the 30-foot distance to the rafters (the grells can fly; they will attempt to maintain altitude and attack from above). Getting to the victim requires crossing the floor under the second grell's attention. There is no clean sequence — the party must choose.

Mirsa, when she recovers, is coherent and specific. She was grabbed from the waterfront near a specific pier — Pier 17, on the south quay. # Act IId — The Order of the Gauntlet Arcs

*The Order's missions are the most morally direct in the campaign — and the most morally complicated. Breaking up a fight is simple. Investigating a masked thief reveals a more important target. Hunting wererats raises questions about proportionality. And walking into a Field Ward square to fight spined devils opens a door the Order cannot officially walk through.*

---

## Arc OG-1 — Breaking Up the Brawl
### *2nd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

The party must prevent a gang fight from escalating in the Field Ward. Four thugs, paid by Manshoon's Zhentarim, have cornered a Xanathar Guild dealer. The mission is a straightforward use-of-force problem — but the Field Ward is Waterdeep's most lawless district, the thugs are not fools, and the gallery of locals watching the confrontation has its own dynamics.

**Dramatic Question:** *How much force is proportionate? And who decides?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

Savra delivers this in person, standing at the door of Trollskull Manor with the morning sun behind her.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The Manshoonian Zhentarim have been paying gang leaders in the Field Ward to attack suspected Xanathar Guild members. Fights are breaking out daily. The City Guard barely sets foot in the Field Ward. We need someone to stop one of these confrontations before it starts — and send a message that the Order is paying attention." She pauses. "Go there. Find a fight. Stop it."</p>
    <p>"Preferably without killing anyone. Hurt, I can explain. Dead complicated things."</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — The Field Ward at Noon

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The Field Ward smells of cookfire smoke and damp earth and the specific quality of an area that has been built by people who needed to build something quickly rather than well. The streets are unpaved. The buildings lean on each other for structural support. The City Watch, if present at all, are three wards away.</p>
    <p>You hear the trouble before you see it: raised voices from a market square two streets over, the specific pitch that a crowd uses when it has decided to watch rather than leave.</p>
</div>

The square contains: four **Thugs** (hired by a Manshoon intermediary who has already left); a Xanathar Guild dealer named Pell (**Bandit** stat block) and his two companions; approximately fifteen locals watching from varying distances; and a large dog that belongs to none of the parties and is barking.

**The Crowd.** The crowd is entertainment-seeking rather than hostile. They will not intervene but they will respond to the dynamics of the confrontation — cheering or booing depending on how it goes, which creates pressure. A dramatic display of force earns immediate deference; a hesitant approach invites the thugs to play to the gallery.

**Resolution Options:**

Three DC 12 Charisma (Intimidation) checks before three failures disperse the thugs. Each failure emboldens them; by the third failure, they have decided the party is not a real threat and the Xanathar dealer is in immediate danger. Combat at that point involves four thugs and counting.

Physical confrontation: four **Thugs** against the party, ideally non-lethally. Knocking them unconscious rather than killing them is achievable and earns Savra's specific approval.

Lateral approaches work. Pell, if the party can signal him privately (a DC 12 Dexterity check to catch his eye and gesture without the thugs noticing), will follow a lead for exiting the confrontation without either side losing face publicly — he does not want to fight today and will accept a graceful out.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Order of the Gauntlet character gains **+1 Renown**. Pell, the Xanathar Guild dealer, owes the party a genuine favor. He will not say so directly, but the party can find him at the Rusted Anchor in the Dock Ward. He knows which Field Ward gang leaders have been paid by the Zhentarim and which haven't — intelligence that is useful for anyone tracking Manshoon's cell activities.

## Arc OG-2 — The Black Viper Investigation
### *3rd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

Savra sends the party to interview the publisher of the Waterdeep Wazoo about the Black Viper's return. The publisher's information is fabricated. The real Black Viper is someone else entirely. The mission's true value is in what the party notices while pulling the thread: the Cassalanters, who have nothing to do with the Black Viper, have already heard that someone is asking about them.

**Dramatic Question:** *You're investigating a lie. What does the lie reveal about the person who invented it?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"A notorious thief called the Black Viper, long thought dead, has apparently returned to Waterdeep. She has robbed at least a dozen noble estates. No one knows her identity because she wears a mask, but The Waterdeep Wazoo reports she's a noble. Find out what else the publisher knows. Report back."</p>
    <p>She adds, with mild distaste: "The Wazoo. Yes. I know."</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — The Waterdeep Wazoo

The Wazoo's offices occupy a narrow second-floor room at the corner of Immar and Stallion Streets in the North Ward. The smell of fresh ink and cold coffee. Broadsheet pages drying on strings overhead.

**Gaxly Rudderbust** (N male Illuskan human **Commoner**). A professionally untidy man of forty, with the specific dishevelment of someone who considers themselves a journalist. He is protective of his sources in the way that people are protective of things they know are not quite right but are too committed to abandon. A DC 12 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion) check, or 50 gp, loosens his tongue.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The Black Viper," he says, with the relish of someone who has been waiting to say this. "My source — and I want to be clear, I protect my sources — my source tells me she is the secret twin sister of Lady Ammalia Cassalanter. A twin kept hidden from society since birth because of a disfigurement. The mask isn't vanity. It's concealment."</p>
    <p>He spreads his hands. "I know how it sounds. But the Cassalanters have always been — there's something there. Everyone knows it."</p>
</div>

**The Investigation.** A DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation) check, representing a day's work interviewing servants, checking birth records at the Hall of Records, or speaking with social-register contacts, confirms with certainty that no such person exists. The Cassalanters have two children. There was no twin. Gaxly's source invented this story.

The party can choose to pursue Gaxly's source further. Gaxly will not give a name — he physically cannot, he says, having promised confidentiality — but he can confirm that the source approached him, not the other way around, and offered the story "as a public service." The source was well-dressed and Tethyrian. This description matches Seffia Naelryke, a Cassalanter operative — who planted the story deliberately, to watch who came asking.

---

### Scene 3 — The Complication: They're Already Watching

Within 48 hours of the party's investigation, a small detail shifts. Nothing dramatic. A hire-coach passes Trollskull Alley twice in one day at a slow roll — not stopping, not threatening, but noting. A party member with a passive Perception of 14 or higher notices a small, flying creature — barely visible, shaped wrong for a bat — hovering near Trollskull Manor's upper windows late at night. It is gone by the time anyone looks directly at it.

The Cassalanters have been monitoring who asks questions about them since well before this campaign began. Their imp network — the invisible watchers embedded throughout the city's noble estates — flagged the party's inquiry within hours. This does not create an immediate threat. It creates a note, in a ledger, in a villa in the Sea Ward, that these people have started asking about the Cassalanters.

**Reporting to Savra.** Savra receives the party's report — there is no evil twin, the story is fabricated — and is quiet for a moment. Then: "Who benefits from a false story that points investigators at the Cassalanters?" She is not asking rhetorically; she genuinely wants the party's analysis. This is the Order of the Gauntlet's first formal attention toward the Cassalanters' inner workings.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Order of the Gauntlet character gains **+1 Renown** for reporting what Gaxly said. The Cassalanter thread is now officially on the Order's radar, and the party has become persons of interest to the Cassalanters — which means something will happen, eventually, when the Grand Game's pressure increases.

</div>

What I gave Manshoon's people was partially real and partially fabricated. I built in a kill switch: three of the safe house locations I gave them were false, and two of the informants are people who volunteered to be identified as compromised so that Manshoon's people would trust me." He holds your gaze. "I was a double agent. Poorly managed, I admit. But I was working both sides."</p>

</div>

Whether the version he is telling is complete and accurate is a different question. A DC 14 check reveals that he is genuinely afraid of Manshoon's cell, not just of the party.

The city's great spring festival moves through the Castle Ward toward the Market. The streets are lined three people deep. The crowd noise is enormous.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The Twin Parades are two processions that converge at the Market: one moving north from the harbor, one east from the Castle Ward, meeting in a grand convergence that the city has performed every spring for two hundred years. The crowd on Bazaar Street is dense enough to make individual movement difficult and easy at once — difficult to move quickly, easy to be unseen.</p>
    <p>The Sea Maidens Faire floats are in the procession from the harbor: carnival performers, a mechanical beholder hovering twenty feet over the crowd that makes everyone near it slightly nervous, and — near the end of the Sea Maidens section — a nimblewright performing tricks on a raised platform, its clockwork joints moving with inhuman precision through a series of acrobatic feats while the crowd applauds. It wears a small hat. The hat falls off during the flip. The crowd laughs. The nimblewright pauses for half a beat, then continues. It does not retrieve the hat.</p>
</div>

**Maester Roderick Bartlethorpe** (**Noble**, 2024 *Monster Manual*). A compact, self-satisfied man of fifty in good wool standing exactly where Nevercott said he would be, watching the parade with the expression of someone who considers attendance at public events a civic obligation rather than a pleasure.

**Acquiring the Handkerchief.** DC 12 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) for a pickpocket attempt; DC 12 Charisma (Deception or Persuasion) for a social approach (manufacturing a spilled drink, a stumble, a moment of introduced conversation). A failed pickpocket makes Roderick aware that someone attempted something; he looks around with offended dignity but has no specific description.

---

### Scene 3 — Net Street and Dock Street

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The crate at the corner of Net and Dock Streets has a small carved sun on its left panel, as advertised. The sun was carved carefully, with a tool, not scratched. The crate's interior, visible through a gap, contains a folded blanket, a small lantern, and a book.</p>
    <p>The girl inside is approximately eleven, with bright orange skin and small horns barely visible beneath her hair. She takes the handkerchief without apparent surprise, holds it for a moment without opening it, and then looks up at the party. "He said you'd come today." She tucks the handkerchief into the book. "Thank you."</p>
</div>

She will not say who "he" is or what the handkerchief means. She thanks the party again, in the tone of someone completing a transaction, and goes back to reading.

**The Handkerchief.** The bergamot scent is layered over a secondary chemical scent — DC 18 Wisdom (Perception) or *detect magic* (the handkerchief radiates faint transmutation) reveals it. The handkerchief is a communication device: the bergamot scent contains a message written in a scent-coded language used exclusively by Bregan D'aerthe operatives. Mira — the girl — is a D'aerthe courier. The message arranges a meeting between two D'aerthe agents using a location system embedded in the scent itself.

The party can know as much of this as their investigation allows. They cannot know all of it — the language itself is not available to them. But a party that investigates will know that J.B. Nevercott is running a courier operation through a child living in a crate in the Dock Ward, and that this operation is more sophisticated than anyone assigned a pickpocketing job would assume.

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Bregan D'aerthe character gains **+1 Renown**. The nimblewright in the parade is a detail the party carries forward: a nimblewright with a small hat that fell off during a trick. This will become relevant in Act III when the nimblewright investigation begins and the party is asked to describe the construct involved.

## Arc BD-2 — The Wazoo Drop
### *3rd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

Jarlaxle has written an exposé on devil worship among unnamed Waterdavian noble families and wants it delivered anonymously to the Waterdeep Wazoo. The delivery requires a simple office break-in. The content of the document, read carefully, reveals things the party may have begun to suspect about the Cassalanters — and was designed to do exactly that to exactly this audience.

**Dramatic Question:** *You are delivering a document that is, as far as you can tell, factually accurate. Does the source change what you should do with it?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>J.B. Nevercott produces a sealed leather document case and sets it on the table with the care of someone setting down something valuable. "This mission is so easy a gang of street urchins could pull it off. I want you to deliver this exposé to the publisher of The Waterdeep Wazoo — Gaxly Rudderbust, corner of Immar and Stallion Streets in the North Ward — without his knowing who wrote it or where it came from. His office will be unoccupied at lunch or after closing. Leave it on his desk."</p>
    <p>He stands, adjusting his hat. "Do not read it," he adds. He knows they will read it.</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — The Document

The document seal is wax over a standard paper closure — not a formal binding, not a magical seal. Any character who decides to read it can do so in approximately twenty minutes.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The document is dense with detail. It describes devil worship among unnamed Waterdavian noble families — "a cult devoted to the Archdevil Asmodeus, operating in multiple safe locations throughout the city." It mentions rituals. It mentions a shrine somewhere near Aveen Street. It mentions a converted windmill in the Southern Ward. It mentions a ceremony attended by forty or more participants from the city's social upper class. It attributes specific ritual acts to "one leading family whose charitable works are well known and whose name is withheld pending further verification."</p>
    <p>The details are specific enough to be credible and vague enough to be legally deniable. They are also, in their geography and methodology, entirely consistent with what the party may know or suspect about the Cassalanter network.</p>
</div>

The document was written by Jarlaxle. Its purpose is to create public pressure on the Cassalanter cult in a way that doesn't require anyone to name them — the Wazoo's readers will speculate, and the speculation will create scrutiny that the Cassalanters will have to manage. It also, in Jarlaxle's calculation, causes the party to think about what they know about the Cassalanters and what they should do with it.

---

### Scene 3 — The Break-In

The Wazoo offices are on the second floor of a building on Immar Street. During the lunch hour (noon to two bells) and after closing (seven bells onward), Gaxly is absent. A locked door (DC 10 Dexterity with thieves' tools, or *knock*) and two DC 15 Dexterity (Stealth) checks allow access and departure without being seen by the building's other tenants.

Gaxly's desk is exactly as described: covered in papers, notes, printer's proofs, and a cup of cold coffee. Placing the document where it will be found is trivial. The party can also look around — there are notes on the desk from recent stories, including one about the Black Viper investigation that shows Gaxly's source as "anonymous, Tethyrian, well-dressed," which matches Seffia Naelryke's description from the Order of the Gauntlet investigation.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Bregan D'aerthe character gains **+1 Renown**. The exposé will be published within a tenday. Its publication causes a minor social stir and, more importantly, causes the Cassalanters' imp network to flag the Wazoo as a threat — which means Gaxly Rudderbust begins experiencing small, inexplicable difficulties in his professional life shortly thereafter.

Jarlaxle, if the party mentions they read the document: "I assumed you would. Did you find it informative?" He does not ask what they plan to do with the information. He knows what they will do — he designed the situation so that doing something is the obvious response.

## Arc BD-3 — Guarding Ott Steeltoes
### *4th Level — Renown +2*

---

### Overview

An Xanathar Guild prisoner appears in Trollskull Manor's basement, bound by magical restraints, with no explanation of how he arrived. The party must guard him for three nights while Jarlaxle "makes arrangements." Each night brings escalating Xanathar Guild response teams. The mission is a three-night siege of the party's home, using Trollskull Manor itself as the defensive terrain, with Lif as an involuntary ally.

**Dramatic Question:** *How do you defend something you care about against something that doesn't care about the cost?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Discovery

No briefing precedes this mission. The party discovers the situation themselves.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The basement of Trollskull Manor smells of stone and old wine and something else — recent occupancy. In the middle of the floor, secured by bands of iron that seem to grow from his wrists and ankles rather than merely encircle them, is a shield dwarf of approximately fifty years. He is wearing a leather skullcap stitched with fake beholder eyestalks, which are slightly comical, and an expression that blends indignity with the resolute dignity of someone who has decided that indignity is the situation and he will navigate it.</p>
    <p>He looks up at the party. "I knew someone would come eventually." A pause. "You're not Xanathar's people." Another pause, during which he reassesses. "Are you with the man in the hat? Because the man in the hat said someone would come."</p>
</div>

A flying snake arrives an hour later with a note in Jarlaxle's hand: *He is a guest. Three nights. Do not let Xanathar's people retrieve him. I will be in touch.* No signature. The snake departs.

**Ott Steeltoes** (**Shield Dwarf Scout**, *Dragon Heist* appendix B). A low-level Xanathar Guild operative, unremarkable in most respects, who has been caught in the middle of something larger than him. He knows nothing about the Grand Game. He does not know where Xanathar's Eye is kept. He is useful to Jarlaxle for reasons Ott cannot explain, which suggests the usefulness is about his value as bait rather than his value as an informant.

He is oddly cheerful. He asks for food. He has opinions about Waterdeep's taverns. He is genuinely grateful to the party for not immediately threatening him.

---

### The Three Nights

**Night One: The Bugbears.** Six **Bugbears** arrive at Trollskull Manor through the front and back doors simultaneously, having decided that waiting is not their skill set. They are not subtle.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Lif's first warning is the candles — every candle in the taproom goes out at once, snuffed by invisible hands, and then you hear the front door. Not the knob turning. The door itself, on the hinges.</p>
</div>

Lif, the ghost barkeep, is an involuntary but functional ally in this fight. He cannot attack, but he can slam shutters, lock and unlock doors, cause things to fall from shelves onto attackers, and — if the party has been good to the tavern — position furniture to slow the bugbears' advance. Each round, the DM can have Lif perform one of these actions as a free effect without requiring a character action.

**Night Two: The Possessed Dungsweepers.** Four **Commoners** with **Intellect Devourers** in their skulls arrive during the evening business hours, dressed for the tavern. They order drinks. They sit. They watch. A DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check reveals the wrong quality of their attention — focused, scanning, never distracted. A DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check notices that their pupils are the wrong size for the room's light level.

They leave if they confirm Ott is not accessible. They attack if they locate him.

**Night Three: The Beholder Zombie.** Midnight. The sewer grate at the end of Trollskull Alley opens and does not close again.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Lif wakes you — if you are sleeping — by the simple method of making every glass in the building ring simultaneously at exactly the same pitch. The sound dies immediately. In the silence after it: something moving through the alley outside. Something large. Something that floats.</p>
</div>

The **Beholder Zombie** (2024 *Monster Manual*) uses its Central Eye (Disintegration, DC 15 Dexterity) and random eyestalk rays to assault the building and whoever is in it. It is not intelligent. It has a task — retrieve or eliminate Ott Steeltoes — and it will perform that task until it cannot.

After the zombie falls, Ott disappears before dawn. The iron bands are gone with him. No explanation arrives.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Bregan D'aerthe character gains **+2 Renown**. Three nights of siege are the experience reward. The tavern has taken some damage from the beholder zombie's rays — one wall has a scorch mark, one window frame is cracked. Lif has done something he has never done before: he polished the bar while the party slept between the second and third nights. It is the cleanest it has been since the place was first built.

## Arc BD-4 — The Compromised Spy
### *5th Level — Renown +2*

---

### Overview

Jarlaxle sends the party to eliminate Nar'l Xibrindas, his own agent inside Xanathar's Guild. Nar'l has been compromised; his continued presence is a liability. The party infiltrates Xanathar's lair via Jarlaxle's route, finds Nar'l — and Nar'l opens the conversation with an offer to negotiate. He knows about the mission. He has prepared. The choice of what to do in the next few minutes will define the party's relationship with Jarlaxle for the rest of the campaign.

**Dramatic Question:** *Jarlaxle sent you to kill someone who wants to talk. He is a better strategic thinker than you. Does that mean you should do what he says?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The message arrives by flying snake, on black paper in silver ink. J.B. Nevercott's handwriting is careful and precise:</p>
    <p>"We have a spy deep within Xanathar's organization, but I fear he has been compromised. It breaks my heart to do this, but I am sending you to eliminate him. Make it quick and painless, and for Lolth's sake, be discreet. I will give you a route."</p>
    <p>A separate folded sheet contains a map of underground passages leading to Area X1 of Xanathar's Lair — drawn in detail that suggests firsthand knowledge.</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — The Route

The route is legitimate. It passes through a sewer system below the Dock Ward, through a passage marked with red eyes at the junctions (Nar'l's old guildsign, still partially intact), and surfaces in Area X1 — the secret entrance — without passing through any of the lair's guarded corridors.

The passage requires three DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) checks to follow the guildsign without losing the path; on a failure, the party backtracks and adds thirty minutes to the journey. The sewer passage itself contains one encounter: two **Giant Rats** (Xanathar's Guild keeps the passages monitored) that can be avoided with a DC 12 Dexterity (Stealth) check or dispatched quickly.

---

### Scene 3 — Area X35: Nar'l's Office

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Area X35 is a small office off one of Xanathar's administrative corridors — a desk, a stool, shelves of ledgers, a single lamp that burns low. The drow mage who occupies it is seated at the desk when you enter. He does not react to your appearance with surprise. He is approximately sixty by drow standards, thin-faced, with the composed expression of someone who has been expecting visitors and has had time to decide what to say to them.</p>
    <p>He sets down his stylus. "I know why you're here," he says. "And I know who sent you. I would like, before we reach the inevitable, to offer you a transaction."</p>
    <p>He gestures at the door. "Close it. The grell is on its patrol cycle. We have perhaps twelve minutes."</p>
</div>

**Nar'l Xibrindas** (**Drow Mage**, *Dragon Heist* appendix B). He is not going to fight. He knows he cannot win and he is not a man who engages in gestures. He is offering to trade: the location of Xanathar's Eye (Area X19, in Sylgar's fishbowl) and the specific bypass through Area X18 that allows access without triggering the Panopticus network, in exchange for his life and extraction from the lair.

He also tells them something Jarlaxle did not share: the reason Jarlaxle considers him compromised is that the grell bodyguard Xanathar assigned him has made it impossible to send or receive D'aerthe communications unobserved. Nar'l is not actively working against D'aerthe. He has simply been isolated. Jarlaxle decided it was simpler to eliminate him than to extract him. "He made a business calculation," Nar'l says, without apparent bitterness. "He was not wrong, from a business perspective."

**Is He Telling the Truth?** DC 18 Wisdom (Insight): yes, substantially. He is presenting the facts accurately, though his account is necessarily self-serving. DC 14: he is telling most of the truth and does not appear to be manufacturing his account. DC 10: he is not panicking, which is either evidence of deception or evidence of genuine composure under pressure.

**The Twelve Minutes.** The grell will return. The party must make their decision and act before it does. Staying past the grell's return means a combat in Xanathar's Lair with a creature that Nar'l knows the patrol schedule of — not impossible, but complicated.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Bregan D'aerthe character gains **+2 Renown**. Each party member receives a gold statuette of Jarlaxle (worth 250 gp) delivered the following morning with a note: *"Well done. As always, a pleasure working with professionals."*

**The note says nothing specific about what was done.** Jarlaxle will find out. He always finds out. But what he does with the information depends on what the party did — and Jarlaxle's response to being outmaneuvered by his own operatives is, characteristically, appreciative.

*Force Grey's missions are the most institutionally serious in the campaign — they carry the weight of Waterdeep's official magical defense. Vajra is testing the party rigorously, in sequence: can they handle an eccentric asset, an unknown quantity, a corruption that requires more than surveillance, and finally a direct assault on the city's most dangerous internal threat?*

## Arc FG-1 — Consulting Hlam
### *2nd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

Vajra sends the party up Mount Waterdeep to consult a reclusive monk named Hlam about threats to the city. The ascent is the first challenge. Hlam himself is the second. What he says is brief and oblique. The mission's value is in earning his trust enough to hear the specific thing he will only say to people he deems worthy.

**Dramatic Question:** *How do you convince someone who has no needs and no wants to tell you what they know?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Seek out Hlam, a monk who lives in a cave on the side of Mount Waterdeep. Ask him what he's heard about threats to the city." A pause. "Try not to annoy him. He dislikes visitors on principle and tends to express that dislike through silence. Do not overstay your welcome. He will make it clear when the meeting is over."</p>
    <p>She adds, with the air of someone providing context that is borderline relevant: "Hlam and I have a complicated relationship. Don't mention my name unless he asks."</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — The Climb

The path up Mount Waterdeep is unmarked but worn. The mountain rises steeply behind the city's western wards; the lower slopes are maintained city park; the upper slopes are bare rock and wind.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The city falls away below you as you climb — first the rooftops of the Sea Ward, then the harbor's curve, then the open water beyond. At this height the wind has opinions. The path narrows to a ledge in two places and disappears entirely in one, requiring a short scramble across bare stone.</p>
</div>

**The Constitution Saving Throw.** DC 12 Constitution to arrive without Exhaustion. On a failure, the character arrives with 1 level of Exhaustion (−2 to all d20 rolls in 2024). The climb can be made easier by taking extra time (Advantage on the save) or by a character with a climbing speed or Expertise in Athletics assisting (automatic success for that character, Advantage for those they assist).

**The Cave.** A narrow fissure in the mountain's western face, barely visible unless you know where to look. Inside: bare stone, a bedroll, a clay cup, nothing else. The view from the cave mouth extends across the harbor to the horizon.

---

### Scene 3 — Hlam

He is seated in meditation facing the harbor when the party arrives. He opens his eyes when he hears them on the path — he heard them much earlier.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The monk who turns to look at you is old in a way that makes it difficult to determine how old. His face is more lines than flesh, his eyes a dark, specific grey, and he has the quality of a person who has shed everything unnecessary and retained only the parts that function. He is wearing grey clothes that match the rock around him.</p>
    <p>He looks at the party with the assessment of someone who has looked at many people and found most of them either amusing or unremarkable. He appears to be in the process of deciding which category applies to you.</p>
    <p>After a silence that becomes deliberate, he says: "You want to know what I know. Everyone does."</p>
</div>

**Earning His Cooperation.** DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion), but the approach matters more than the roll. Hlam responds to directness and sincerity; he will give Disadvantage to characters who appeal to authority, urgency, or flattery. A character who simply states the truth — "We work for the Blackstaff. She needs to know what threats are moving against the city." — earns straight roll or Advantage.

If the check succeeds: *"Evil's twin hides its face for now. Expect that to change before winter's end."* He will not elaborate if asked directly — the elaboration requires another successful check, DC 15. On a second success, he adds: "An archmage thought dead has returned. He is rebuilding something. The shape of it is familiar to anyone who remembers what Waterdeep was like before it was rebuilt after the spellplague."

He is referring to Manshoon. He knows what is happening. He is choosing how much to say with characteristic precision.

---

### Scene 4 — The Complication: What He Offers Unprompted

As the party prepares to leave — he indicates the meeting's end by returning to facing the harbor — he says one more thing. Not in response to a question.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"The Blackstaff carries more than she knows," he says. "Not the staff — she knows what the staff is. The city. She does not know what is buried beneath it, and the buried thing is waking." He closes his eyes. "Tell her that from me. She will understand the half of it that matters."</p>
</div>

This is the Stone of Golorr's influence registering on Hlam's senses — the abolethic artifact buried in the Vault below the Brandath Crypts, which has been radiating faint psychic resonance for weeks. He does not know what it is. He knows something is there. Vajra, when the party delivers this message, goes very still.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Gray Hand character gains **+1 Renown**. Hlam's prophecy about Manshoon is the first explicit reference to the Zhentarim splinter's scope. The additional message about the "buried thing waking" is one of three clue paths toward the Vault in Act IV.

## Arc FG-2 — The Dragon in the Harbor
### *3rd Level — Renown +1*

---

### Overview

A young bronze dragon named Zelifarn has taken up residence in Deepwater Harbor. Vajra provides potions of water breathing. ### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">

### Scene 2 — The Descent

The harbor bottom at depth is a different world. Cold, dark at the edges, lit in the center by the diffracted harbor light. The silt is undisturbed except for the tracks of the bottom-feeding creatures. The old Elvish wreck rises from the sediment forty feet down — two hundred years of barnacles and weed, its timber softened but its frame still standing.

<div class="fvtt narrative">

</div>

**Zelifarn** (**Young Bronze Dragon**, 2024 *Monster Manual*). Curious, gregarious, and profoundly acquisitive in the specific way of young dragons who haven't yet worked out the distinction between wanting everything and being entitled to it. He will spend the entire encounter attempting to acquire something from the party — not through threats, through the relentless application of enthusiasm and logic. He wants shiny things, interesting things, things that have a story. A character who gives him something they genuinely value earns his warmest regard.

**Assessing His Intentions.** DC 13 Wisdom (Insight) confirms he is harmless and genuinely curious about the harbor. A failure leaves his motives uncertain — the party can report accurately that he "didn't attack anyone," but can't confirm he won't.

### Scene 3 — The Eyecatcher

After a few minutes of negotiation over belt buckles and interesting items, Zelifarn mentions something casually.

<div class="fvtt narrative">

It comes out sometimes, at night, and goes back."</p>

</div>

In Waterdeep's harbor. For how long?" The question is rhetorical. Zelifarn is now an occasional source of harbor intelligence — he notices everything that moves below the surface and will share it in exchange for something interesting.

## Arc FG-3 — The Trouble with Meloon
### *4th Level — Renown +2*

---

### Overview

Meloon Wardragon is acting strangely. Vajra wants the party to observe him for a tenday and report back. What the party discovers is that Meloon has been compromised by an intellect devourer — a creature from Nihiloor's network, using him as a Force Grey observation post. The tenday of observation is the mission's body; what the party chooses to do about what they find is its heart.

**Dramatic Question:** *Surveillance became something else. What do you do with that?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"A member of Force Grey has been acting strangely of late. Meloon Wardragon. His happy-go-lucky demeanor has soured. He's been at the Yawning Portal more than usual." A pause. "Observe him for a tenday. Don't approach him. Don't interact unless you must. Report back what you see."</p>
    <p>She does not explain why she doesn't observe him herself. The party may assume she has reasons; she does.</p>
</div>

---

### Scene 2 — Observing Meloon

Meloon keeps a room at the Yawning Portal and spends his days there — at the bar, at a table near the well, occasionally watching the Portal's mouth with an attention that used to be casual and is now something else.

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Meloon Wardragon used to take up a lot of room in a room — not physically, though he is large, but in personality. Durnan has mentioned once that Meloon used to make everyone at his table laugh within five minutes of sitting down. Now he sits at his table, and people don't quite sit with him, and he doesn't quite notice.</p>
    <p>He has *Azuredge* strapped to his side at all times. He used to carry it with the ease of someone who has had the same weapon for twenty years. Now he carries it like it is arguing with him.</p>
</div>

**Each Morning.** A character who has positioned themselves to observe Meloon's room at dawn — the Yawning Portal offers several sightlines from the gallery level — witnesses the morning ritual: Meloon sits on the edge of his bed, the axe across his knees, and stares at it. His lips move. No sound. This continues for several minutes. Then he straps the axe on and leaves.

A DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check made during this observation reveals: the axe and the man are in genuine conflict. The axe is pressing for something. Meloon is refusing. And Meloon's refusal has the quality not of willpower but of someone who is struggling to articulate why they should refuse — as if the refusal itself is being suppressed.

**Approaching Meloon.** If the party approaches him in conversation: he is cordial, warms slightly to adventurers, asks questions about their work with genuine-seeming interest. A DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check detects the tell: his responses lag slightly — a beat too long between your words and his, as if something is deciding how to respond. He does not produce this from his own character; something is using him as a transmission point.

**Confirming the Possession.** *Detect evil and good* reveals an aberrant presence. *Detect magic* reveals that Meloon himself is a magical source. *See invisibility* reveals nothing — the intellect devourer is not ethereal, it is physically inside his skull.

---

### Scene 3 — The Complication: What Azuredge Is Trying to Do

A character who succeeds on a DC 18 Wisdom (Insight) check during the morning ritual, or who succeeds on a DC 14 check and then asks *Azuredge* (the axe is sentient) a direct question using its name — learns something specific. The axe cannot speak. But it responds to direct address through the force of its personality: the character who addresses it directly feels, with certainty, the following three things in sequence:

*There is something wrong with him.* (Urgency.)
*I cannot reach him.* (Frustration.)
*You can.* (Hope.)

This transforms the surveillance mission into something the party was not assigned: can they do something about what they found?

**Removing the Intellect Devourer.** Requires a *wish* spell — beyond 4th-level capability. But the party can report this to Vajra with enough specificity that she can act immediately. Alternatively, a character who is a 4th-level spellcaster can attempt to cause the intellect devourer to manifest by disrupting Meloon's cognition through targeted magical stress — this is not written in any manual; it is creative problem-solving that has a chance of working (DC 18 Intelligence (Arcana), failure deals 4d6 psychic damage to Meloon, success causes the devourer to reveal itself and attempt to flee, at which point it can be targeted directly).

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Gray Hand character gains **+2 Renown**. If the intellect devourer is removed, Vajra provides the *wand of secrets* with something in her expression that is close to relief — she had suspected but could not confirm. Meloon, restored, is disoriented and then grateful with the specific intensity of someone who has been not-themselves for weeks. He will not forget this.

## Arc FG-4 — Destroy the Intellect Factory
### *5th Level — Renown +2*

---

### Overview

Vajra sends the party into Xanathar's Lair to destroy the Spawning Pool — the source of Nihiloor's intellect devourer operation. This is the Gray Hands' graduation mission. It is extremely dangerous, requires careful infiltration of a hostile lair, and ends with a direct confrontation with one of the campaign's most dangerous non-villain NPCs. Vajra said *raise dead* in the briefing. She meant it.

**Dramatic Question:** *You are 5th level, inside a beholder's lair, trying to destroy a mind flayer's breeding operation. How do you survive this?*

---

### Scene 1 — The Briefing

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>"Xanathar is using intellect devourers to take control of Waterdavians in key positions throughout the city. We must deal with this problem at once. Infiltrate Xanathar's lair and destroy whatever is responsible for creating these creatures." She pauses. "I will cover the cost of any raise dead spells that become necessary."</p>
    <p>She says it without inflection. The party should understand what it means.</p>
</div>

She hands them a potion of resistance (psychic) each, and one additional *potion of water breathing* in case their entry route requires it.

---

### Scene 2 — Getting In

Multiple routes exist; the party's previous missions have opened or closed various options.

**Jarlaxle's Route** (if the party completed BD-4 and has D'aerthe connections): the underground passage to Area X1. Three DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) checks to follow the guildsign.

**The Sewer Hideout Route** (if the party has the Terasse Estate intelligence from LA-2 or OG-1): through the Orb Confectioners' cellar (passphrase: "eight small eyes / around an ocular cake"), into the Xanathar Sewer Hideout, through Area B8 which has been cleared of its staircase — the party must find another way through.

**The Gathering Point Route** (if the party has a Guild passphrase from any investigation): through one of the Terasse Estate gathering points, escorted blind to Area X1 as if attending the gladiatorial fights.

---

### Scene 3 — Navigating the Lair

The lair is active. The adversary roster is in place. The party is moving through a hostile environment with a specific objective: Area X26, the Spawning Pool, where Nihiloor manages intellect devourer production.

**The Route.** X1 → X2 (Watched Hall; beholder zombie + gas spores; the zombie reacts to movement, gas spores react to magic) → corridor through X22 → X24 (Extraction Chamber; Nihiloor, 50% chance; one drow prisoner) → X25 (Food for Thought) → X26 (Spawning Pool).

Random encounters at each public area: 1 in 10 chance, 1d4 roll: 1 = Flutterfoot Zipswiggle (goblin; skittish, can be bribed with food), 2 = Ahmaergo (duergar; inspection; DC 15 Deception to pass as authorized), 3 = Nar'l Xibrindas + grell bodyguard, 4 = Ott Steeltoes.

---

### Scene 4 — Nihiloor

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Area X24 is a circular chamber that smells of ozone and something organic and wrong. The ceiling is high — thirty feet — with iron fixtures from which things can be suspended. Currently nothing is suspended. The drow prisoner in the corner is unconscious. And across the room, standing at a stone workbench with its back to you, is a creature that should not exist in Waterdeep, in the waking world, in the proximity of living human minds.</p>
    <p>Nihiloor turns around.</p>
</div>

**Nihiloor** (**Mind Flayer**, 2024 *Monster Manual*). It does not want to fight. It wants to leave. Its first priority is escape, not victory. It uses *Mind Blast* (60-foot Cone, Intelligence DC 15, 4d8+4 psychic + Stunned) if directly blocked, *Dominate Monster* if cornered, and moves purposefully toward the exit from the first round. It will sacrifice its intellect devourer companion without hesitation.

### Scene 5 — The Spawning Pool

Area X26 contains a circular pool of brine eight feet across and four feet deep, its surface slightly luminescent. In the brine, visible through the glow, are three developing intellect devourers — brain-shaped things moving slowly in the fluid, not yet fully formed.

**Destroying the Pool.** Any of the following works: *bless water* (four vials emptied into the pool causes an immediate violent reaction — the devourers die, the brine destabilizes, and the pool becomes inert within a minute); physically destroying the pool's containing structure (Strength DC 18 to break the stone basin, which is also loud); or sustained acid or fire damage (2d6 acid or fire damage per round for three rounds destroys the contents and renders the pool unusable).

The violence of the brine's reaction to *holy water* is accompanied by a sound like a distant scream, audible throughout the lair. The party has approximately three minutes before this attracts Xanathar's closest enforcers.

**Escape.** The way they came in, or through Area X21 if the party knows the emergency exit route. Moving quickly through the lair at this point — with alerts triggered and enforcers mobilizing — is the final challenge.

---

### Aftermath and Rewards

Each Gray Hand character gains **+2 Renown** and is elevated to **Force Grey** status. Every participating character receives a *potion of resistance* (psychic). Vajra covers all *raise dead* costs. She receives the party's report with the focused attention of someone who has been waiting for this — and then says, very quietly: "Well done." She does not elaborate. She does not need to.

Meloon Wardragon, now restored, buys the party a round at the Yawning Portal and tells them three stories about previous Force Grey operations that are so harrowing they have become funny with distance. Durnan, behind the bar, says nothing. But he charges the party nothing for the evening.

<aside class="notable">
<h4>The Missions and the Grand Game</h4>
<p>The faction missions of Act II are not sidequests. They are the campaign's connective tissue — the network of relationships, intelligence, and consequence that gives the Grand Game its weight. Maxeene's information seeds the Doom Raiders investigation. Sir Ambrose's intuition about the Brandath Crypts seeds the Vault revelation. Zelifarn's submarine observation seeds the Sea Maidens Faire heist. Gysheer's confession seeds the Cassalanter endgame. The missions do not exist to supplement the Grand Game; they exist to make the party feel that they are discovering it rather than being shown it.</p>
<p>Run them in the spirit of that intention. Let the players pull threads they notice. Let some threads go unpulled. Not every piece of intelligence leads somewhere immediately, and the ones that lead somewhere three acts later are the ones that feel most like a living world.</p>
</aside>
