'''Oghma''' ({{Pronounce|OGG|mah|refs=<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" />}} {{Pronounce|OG|muh|refs=<ref name="SoFS-p59" />|alt=yes}}) was the [[neutral]] or [[unaligned]] [[greater deity|greater]] [[god]] of inspiration, invention, and knowledge and the patron of [[bards]] in the [[Faerûnian pantheon]].<ref name="DD1e-29" /><ref name="Dr54-p8" /><ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="FRA-p29" /><ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /><ref name="SoFS-p59" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /><ref name="FRPG-136,152" /><ref name="PHB5e-59" /><ref name="SCAG-21,35" /> The '''Lord of Knowledge''' was the leader of the [[Deities of Knowledge and Invention]]<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> and as such was the most powerful god of knowledge in [[Faerûn]].<ref name="F&A-131-133" /> The '''Binder of What is Known''', or simply '''the Binder''',<ref name="SCAG-21,35" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="DD1e-29" /> was also a member of the [[Celtic pantheon]] as a neutral or [[neutral good]] [[intermediate deity]] of speech and writing, as well as patron of the arts and everything beautiful and creative in all genders.<ref name="DD1e-29" /><ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /> Oghma's domain was nothing less than the whole of knowledge and thought, whether they were written down, spoken aloud, or even still remained in the mind,<ref name="FRPG-136,152" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /> for the idea was the purest and highest incarnation of knowledge.<ref name="SCAG-21,35" />

Oghma was also known as '''Curna''', goddess of wisdom, in the [[Shining Lands]], where she was a part of the faith of the [[Adama]],<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref>{{Cite book/Shining South|9,116}}</ref> and to the [[shalarin]] he was '''Ri'dea'goma'''.<ref name="SoFS-p56" />

Knowledge is the greatest tool of the mortal mind, outweighing anything made by mortal hands. ==Description==
Although he was seldom sighted<ref name="F&A-131-133" /> and was depicted in many different forms,<ref name="RtR-47" /> Oghma's [[avatar]] most often appeared as a handsome, dark-skinned man in bright, resplendent, and stylish clothes<ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /> and ornate armor more appropriate to a fantastic stage play than anywhere else.<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> Typically, his outfit included a [[tabard]] with projecting, ornamented shoulder plates; a [[cummerbund]] with an exquisitely beautiful scene painted on its [[medallion]]-shaped [[buckle]]; and open-meshwork [[boots]] with long pointed toes that curled around and were tipped with dangling crystal teardrops that nearly touched the toes.<ref name="F&A-131-133" />

On very rare occasions, when battle was expected, Oghma appeared as an enormous, elderly but brawny man entirely cloaked by his long, flowing hair and beard, which was either white or black streaked with white.<ref name="DD1e-29" /><ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" />

==Personality==
While Oghma was outwardly carefree<ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /> and cheerful in manner, often with a quiet humor and a ready smile, the Binder was possessed of great wisdom and could be solemn and righteous when needed.<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /> He was legendary for his geniality and all beings apart from the most evil and hateful appreciated his fine singing voice and fabled musical talents.<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> He possessed profound powers of persuasion, using his good looks, peerless charm, and rhetorical prowess to sway even his most fierce opposition toward his point of view, and he did so at any opportunity.<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /> However, his critics—like fellow deities Lathander and Lliira, who were often radically opposed to conservatism—saw such oratory and charm as manipulative and narrow-minded.<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> 

But it was Oghma's burden to choose which ideas and thoughts would spread and which would fade away with their creator, so that all others would not be in jeopardy. Resting heavily in his heart were the experiences of millennia and hence in this duty he was serious, firmly protective, and deeply cautious.<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /> He favored a doctrine of ideological conservatism and sticking strictly to the status quo, rather than risking a rogue idea disrupting the delicate balance of knowledge he had been preserving since history began.<ref name="FaP-52-54" />

Yet after the Spellplague, something changed in Oghma. He was still the judge of ideas and innovations, choosing which would be remembered and forgotten, but the Lord of Knowledge could not stand the suppression of any original thought, regardless of whether the consequences were good or bad. He thrived on ideas, especially new ones, and the sharing of knowledge in all its forms.<ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /><ref name="FRPG-136,152" />

If the Wise God had a weakness, it might be his tendency to overthink things. He often concocted convoluted schemes in his head and implemented those rather than simply act directly.<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" />

==Powers==
Oghma had all the abilities of a great [[bard]] and [[wizard]] and more, as well as, of course, a god.<ref name="DD1e-29" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /> In particular, every non-divine being who heard Oghma's avatar speak could be [[charm monster|''charmed'']] by him; while they could resist the magic, there was no immunity and it continued as long as he kept talking. He himself was invulnerable to all spells and powers that charmed, altered emotions, or harmed or hampered one's intellect, wisdom, or sanity.<ref name="F&A-131-133" />

Oghma was known to use all [[school of magic|schools of arcane magic]] and was especially potent in spells of [[abjuration]] and [[divination]].<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /> He possessed four [[Psionics#Nature of Psionics|psionic power]]s (three devotions, one science) with all attack and defense modes.<ref name="DD1e-29" />  He also had ''[[ESP]]'', allowing him to read the minds of all creatures up to {{SI|180|yard|long=yes}} away.<ref name="F&A-131-133" />

Oghma could sense any idea or type of creative expression anywhere in the world, up to 160 days before it occurred and retain the feeling for as long afterward.<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> The Lord of Knowledge could answer any question put to him, though it might take even him a little while to research for the trickier topics.<ref name="DD1e-29" />  

Oghma also knew (or could see) the secret name or [[true name]] of every non-divine creature, especially on worlds where the Celtic pantheon held sway. With this name, Oghma could control any creature or cause them to die.<ref name="DD1e-29" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /> Though this was usually not applicable on Toril,<ref name="CotR-13" /> [[truename magic]] was clearly associated with the Oghmanyte faith.<ref name="CC-Tn" /><ref group="note">The 1{{st}}-edition ''Deities & Demigods'' introduces 'secret names' as part of its Celtic mythology. The 1{{st}}-edition ''Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting'': ''Cyclopedia of the Realms'' refers to these as 'true names' and says there is a "lack" of them in the ''Forgotten Realms'', except in the spell ''[[truename]]'', suggesting Oghma lacks the ability. However, true names would be introduced to the setting in later sources, so Oghma might retain the knowledge of them on Toril.</ref> This was one reason he was known as the Binder,<ref name="OHG-p70" /> but some accounts alleged the origin of the title<ref name="DD1e-29" /> laid in his power to force [[demon]]s, [[devil]]s, and other [[fiend]]s into a special prison he had created or of his choosing, where they would remain trapped until he chose to let them out for some reason.<ref name="DD1e-29" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /> 

===Manifestations===
When Oghma did manifest, it was commonly as a blue-green radiance with an accompaniment of musical [[Wikipedia:chord (music)|chords]]. These were distinctive, being described as crawling or rising and almost menacing in their pitch.<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="DDaD">{{Cite web enhancement/Deity Do's and Don'ts}}</ref> The chords never changed between manifestations and were proprietary to the Patron of Bards—any musician who dared to try play them was instantly met with a manifestation warning them to desist. Those who continued were also encircled by a ring of blue flame that could burn them, inflicting serious injuries and even death if they had angered the god enough. In any case, those who beheld the blue-green radiance had a strong feeling that were being watched. Oghma was known to speak through it in a voice that was aged, refined, and echoing and made laconic statements such as "Well said," "Tis well done," "Desist from thy course, or perish," or "What ye seek is to be found in..."<ref name="F&A-131-133" />

An alternative manifestation was as a white light of blinding intensity. It could display a pair of eyes of blazing flames;<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="DDaD" /> project colored beams of force to apply magical effects; or extrude arms and hands capable of carrying items, wielding weapons, and indicating things of importance.<ref name="F&A-131-133" />

To aid his followers or show his approval or disapproval of their actions, the Binder could send [[golem]]s of various makes; [[metallic dragon]]s, particularly [[electrum dragon]]s;<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="DDaD" /> [[feystag]]s, [[translator]]s, [[Watcher (beholderkin)|watchers]] (a type of [[beholderkin]]); [[watchghost]]s,<ref name="F&A-131-133" /> and [[lillendi]].<ref name="DDaD" />

==Possessions==
Oghma usually carried a [[Oghma's yarting|special yarting]] made of white [[snowwood]].<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /> This instrument could play any tone or song flawlessly, sound like several instruments at once, and perfectly mimic any noise. Any music Oghma played from it could influence the emotions of the listener, filling them with enrapturing joy, deep despair, or uncontrollable fury. The yarting seemed to possess other magical powers that might change each time. Oghma sometimes lent it to his followers when they were carrying out some important mission for him, in which case its powers could differ from those Oghma commanded.<ref name="F&A-131-133" />

In battle, Oghma wielded a magical [[longsword]] known as ''[[Mortal Strike]]''.<ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /> He wore ''+4 [[chainmail]]''.<ref name="DD1e-29" />

==Tactics==
Oghma preferred to use his spells in combat, but was incredibly capable as a warrior. When he had need of a weapon, he could summon his longsword into his hand, and was highly specialized in its use.<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /> His battle-specialized avatar flew through the air hurling [[spell]]s.<ref name="F&A-131-133" />

In addition, he was exceptional at the sport of wrestling,<ref name="DD1e-29" /><ref name="FRA-p29" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /> being the best among the Celtic pantheon at least<ref name="DD1e-29" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /> and their chosen champion in their battles with [[giant]]s. He would often call out foes for a wrestling match, with the loser being forced to leave the field of battle. However, his strength in wrestling was always the sum of his own strength and that of his opponent.<ref name="DD1e-29" />

==Divine Realm==
Oghma's [[divine realm]] was known as the [[House of Knowledge (plane)|House of Knowledge]] which, contrary to its name, was actually a wilderness of old oaks and clear pools, a lonely woodland across which could be heard the echoing music of harps.<ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /> The plane was dedicated not just to preserving written text in dusty scrolls, but to living, oral tales, the blessings of speech, the understanding of deep familiarity with others, and all other forms of knowledge. Oghma maintained the three magical springs scattered amidst the woods, the question-answering Spring of Knowledge, language-granting Spring of Poetry, and performance-enhancing Pool of Music, each of which changed locations at his leisure,<ref name="OHG-p70" /><ref>{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|158}}</ref> but he spent only about half of his time in his realm.<ref name="OHG-p70" />

Within the [[Great Wheel cosmology]], it was found in the [[Outlands]], also called Concordant Opposition, a [[plane]] of complete neutrality.<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="FRA-p29" /><ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /> There, it lay in the land of [[Tir na Og]].<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="OHG-p70" /> But in the [[World Tree cosmology]], the House of Knowledge was considered a plane in its own right<ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|257,258}}</ref> and in the [[World Axis cosmology]], it was a [[Astral dominion|dominion]] in the [[Astral Sea]].<ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /> 

==Activities==
Oghma much preferred quiet conversations with travelers and sages to combat.<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> He loved collecting and maintaining knowledge, spending half his time traveling to learn new things. He particularly sought to learn secrets,<ref name="OHG-p70" /> and was also interested in the classification and definition of things.<ref name="CC-Tn" /> He valued all knowledge, as well as all innovation, inspiration, and discovery, and truth no matter what form it took, and he accepted all who pursued such things.<ref name="NWCS">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|22,57}}</ref>

However, it was his role to decide what place each idea and invention had to have in the world.<ref name="NWCS" /> Oghma passed judgement on each and every new idea and innovation, determining if it should be released to the world or if it should remain with the one who formed it.<ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /> Thus, he was widely viewed as the source of creativity and inspiration.<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> He was quite protective of this duty<ref name="FRPG-136,152" /> and of the body of knowledge that was in the world.<ref name="FaP-52-54" />

Oghma loved music and the bardic arts,<ref name="OHG-p70" /> and so enjoyed hearing stories, poetry, and songs that he regularly adopted human form and wandered the world in search of the most talented composers and performers. When a song was sung for the first time, there was a one-in-a-hundred chance that Oghma would be in the audience. Otherwise, when a new song or story was then spread by others, there was a one-in-twenty chance the Patron of Bards would soon hear it and gift its creator with [[gold]], often via the local lord strangely choosing to bestow it.<ref name="DD1e-29" /> His avatars would perform the greatest ballads in all Faerun for those willing to stop and listen.<ref name="FaP-52-54" />

==Relationships==
[[File:Gods of Knowledge and Invention.jpg|thumb|left|250px|''The Deities of Knowledge and Invention: Gond and Oghma debate some new invention, while Milil strums his lyre and Deneir gets on with his reading.'']]
Oghma was the head of a small pantheon called the [[Deities of Knowledge and Invention]], including himself, [[Milil]], the god of music; [[Deneir]], the god of writing and images; and [[Gond]], the god of crafts.<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> These three gods were allies of Oghma, but were secondary to him, acting as his servants and agents in the preservation and propagation of knowledge in a slightly patronizing arrangement.<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="SCAG-21,35" /><ref name="Dr54-p55">{{Cite dragon/54/Down-to-earth divinity|52,55}}</ref><ref name="CotR-p17">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Set/Cyclopedia of the Realms|11,17}}</ref><ref name="RtR-53">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|53,55}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|102}}</ref> Milil and Deneir were both Oghma's intermediaries, who brought new knowledge to him and delivered it to beings he favored.<ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /> Deneir was called the Scribe of Oghma<ref name="RtR-53" /><ref name="SCAG-21,35" /> and considered his right hand, while Milil was the left hand, also called the One True Hand. No insult was intended to Deneir by this title, it merely represented a belief in the tendency for artistic talent to be linked with left-handedness, and the idea that the highest art arose from the acceptance of truth.<ref name="SCAG-21,35" /> In the 15{{th}} century [[DR]], Milil became Oghma's [[exarch]]<ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /> while Deneir had written himself into the [[Metatext]] in an attempt to stabilize the failing [[Weave]], seemingly becoming one with it as a result.<ref name="TGK">{{Cite book/The Ghost King}}</ref>

The third hand, however, was Gond.<ref name="CotR-p17" /> Gond's role was to put ideas in physical form<ref name="F&A-131-133" /> as crafts, devices, and new inventions. While they enjoyed amiable relations however, these were somewhat strained by some of Gond's inventions, especially the [[firearm]]s developed in [[Lantan]] in the 1360s DR.<ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="RtR-p50">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|50}}</ref><ref name="FRCS-p242">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|242}}</ref><ref name="FaP-p26">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|26}}</ref> Gond pushed for the speedy proliferation of all new devices across Faerûn,<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-p242" /> some of which seemed ill-considered,<ref name="RtR-p50" /> and though Oghma did admire Gond's creativity and even his enthusiasm, he disapproved of how he placed innovation above introspection and always pushed his technology too far.<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> Sometimes Gond acted so independently of Oghma that many forgot he was actually supposed to serve him.<ref name="FRCS-p242" /><ref name="FaP-p26" /> 

Oghma had no superior himself,<ref name="F&A-131-133" /> being the most powerful deity of knowledge in the Realms, second only to [[Mystra]] ''if'' one counted magical ability as a form of knowledge.<ref name="RtR-47" /> He was allied with the first [[Mystra]] and [[Mystra (Midnight)|second Mystra]], the goddesses of magic, and her own suboordinate [[Azuth]], the patron of mages.<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref>{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|34,96,128}}</ref><ref name="FaP-p38">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|38,50}}</ref> Oghma and his subordinate gods regularly worked with the first Mystra, likely in areas of magical knowledge.<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="Dr54-p55" /> The two apparently had a secret agreement regarding the sanctity of [[Candlekeep]].<ref>{{Cite web/Wyrms of the North/Miirym, "The Sentinel Wyrm"}}</ref>

Other allies of Oghma included [[Lathander]], god of creativity, and  [[Lliira]], goddess of joy.<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref>{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|34,96,128}}</ref><ref name="FaP-p38">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|38,50}}</ref>  Oghma and his gods were brought into contact with Lathander in the area of creativity. Oghma was one of the few deities (the others being [[Chauntea]] and Lliira) who were aware of Lathander's efforts to attempt again what had led to the [[Dawn Cataclysm]], though ''circa'' [[1372 DR]] they kept their own counsel in order to focus on the direct threat posed by Bane and other evil deities.<ref name="FaP-p38" /> Although Lliira served [[Sune]], goddess of love, she was linked most closely with Milil, and thus was counted as an ally of the Deities of Knowledge and Invention, so much so that sages habitually mistook her for a servant of Oghma.<ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref>{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|96}}</ref><ref group="note">Oddly, the 2{{nd}}-edition ''Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting'': ''Running the Realms'', page 54, states "Lliira serves Oghma alongside Deneir, and is also an ally of Milil." but it's unclear why Lliira should serve Oghma. This likely arises from a misreading of the text on page 17 in 1{{st}}-edition ''Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting'': ''Cyclopedia of the Realms''. ''Faiths & Avatars'' returns Lliira's allegiance to Sune, lists Oghma as only an ally, and gives the above in-universe reason for the discrepancy. Nevertheless, her portfolio of dance could be counted as a form of creative expression much like Milil's music and Deneir's art and literature, placing this under Oghma's purview.</ref>

Outside the Faerûnian pantheon, Oghma established around [[1370 DR]] a mutual accord and tentative alliance with [[Thoth]], his counterpart in the [[Mulhorandi pantheon]]. They even had a friendly contest going to claim followers in [[Unther]].<ref>{{Cite book/Powers & Pantheons|129,130}}</ref> Oghma was also allies with [[Dugmaren Brightmantle]], dwarven god of scholarship and invention; [[Labelas Enoreth]], the elven god of history; and [[Cyrrollalee]], the halfling goddess of crafts.<ref>{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|57,117,169}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|128,148}}</ref> Further afield, Oghma was good friends with [[Bragi]] of the [[Norse pantheon]]; they respected one another and collaborated on creating some of the best music known in the [[upper planes]].<ref>{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|142}}</ref>

===Enemies===
Oghma's most common foes were [[Mask]], patron of thieves; [[Cyric]], the god of lies; [[Talos]], the god of destruction; and [[Bane]], the god of tyranny,<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /> and the feeling was mutual.<ref>{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|36,51,109,155}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|238}}</ref><ref name="FaP-p20">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|15,20,46,69}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|50}}</ref> He saw these gods as the greatest threats to his balance of knowledge,<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> as they all tried to lock away knowledge for their own end, corrupt it, or destroy it altogether.<ref name="FRCS-247" /> Oghma had also opposed the former goddess of lies, [[Leira]], and their conflicts were legend. Over the centuries, every time Leira, goddess of lies, sent revolutionary and dangerous ideas to her faithful, Oghma restricted them to remain among her small cult, thus preventing them doing harm to wider society.<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> In the planes, the [[Lords of the Nine]] and [[Druaga]] hated Oghma for his habit of trapping fiends, but he did not fear them.<ref name="OHG-p70" />

===Others===
Oghma's [[proxy]] was the bard [[Fial Cairbre]], who was believed to be his son.<ref name="OHG-p70" />

A minor servant of Oghma was the [[brass dragon]] [[Ileuthra]]. According to legend, the [[Dragon aging|great wyrm]] adored the game of chess and became so great at it that no mortal players could provide enough of a challenge. Finally, Ileuthra challenged Oghma himself to a match of three games—and handily defeated the god each time. Ileuthra then politely thanked Oghma and offered to then play a game of the god's choice. Oghma would raise Ileuthra to his servitor status and the two would continue to play chess, Ileuthra continuing to win.<ref name="D-p40">{{Cite book/Draconomicon|40}}</ref><ref group="note">It's not specified what game Oghma challenged Ileuthra to. It may be that serving Oghma is the game, or possibly the price for losing.</ref>

==Worshipers==
{{Main|Church of Oghma}}
{{Quote|Whatever I may say, you know already. Thus, I will speak our knowledge to those without it.|A basic prayer to Oghma.<ref name="BG3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

The Lord of Knowledge had the distinction of being revered by beings of every race, every stratum of society, and every philosophy<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> and moral alignment.<ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="SoFS-p59" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="CotR-18">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Set/Cyclopedia of the Realms|18}}</ref>  Unlike most neutral gods, his [[cleric]]s could be of any alignment<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> including neutral.<ref name="DD1e-29" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /> All folk who valued, preserved, shared, sought, created, or uncovered knowledge and learning were perforce followers of Oghma<ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="FRPG-136,152" /><ref name="SCAG-21,35" /> and anyone looking for information, especially if it was lost or concealed, would say a prayer to the Binder and beseech him for a blessing.<ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /> Those who worshiped Oghma included artists, artisans, [[bards]], cartographers, clerks, inventors, librarians, [[loremaster]]s, sages, scholars, scribes, smiths, and [[wizards]].<ref name="CotR-18" /><ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="FRPG-136,152" /><ref name="SCAG-21,35" /><ref name="Dr54-p55" /> [[Archivist]]s and [[truenamer]]s might pray to him as well.<ref>{{Cite web/Class Chronicles: Archivists and Dread Necromancers}}</ref><ref name="CC-Tn">{{Cite web/Class Chronicles: Truenamers}}</ref> Oghma was also among the deities of knowledge favored by the [[Fraternity of Order]] in the planes.<ref>{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|47}}</ref>

The worship of Oghma was favored in the [[Dalelands]], the [[Western Heartlands]], the [[Shaar]], and in [[the North]], especially in the [[Silver Marches]] and [[Silverymoon]],<ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|23}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|12,14,16,25}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web/Player's Guide to Faerûn Errata}}</ref> and it was a major faith in [[Waterdeep]].<ref>{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|13,43}}</ref> In the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]], he could be followed by the aquatic races of [[Serôs]], including [[shalarin]],<ref name="SoFS-p56">{{Cite book/Sea of Fallen Stars|56,181}}</ref><ref name="SoFS-p59" /> [[dolphin]]s, and [[whale]]s (who had some of the greatest bards).<ref>{{Cite book/Sea of Fallen Stars|42,43}}</ref><ref name="SoFS-p59" />

===Symbol===
[[File:Oghma Symbol-5e.jpeg|thumb|right|''The holy symbol of Oghma.'']]
Oghma's [[holy symbol]] was a blank [[scroll]], plain and partially unrolled.<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref name="FRA-p29" /><ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FRCS-247" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="PHB5e-59" /><ref name="SCAG-21,35" /> In the Celtic pantheon, Oghma's symbol was a Celtic-style chalice.<ref name="OHG-p70" />

==History==
Oghmanytes believed that, soon after the creation of the world by [[Selûne]] and [[Shar]] and the birth of Chauntea, a traveller from a faraway realm arrived. He found a land in chaos, filled with vague, ill-defined concepts wanting to be given form, so he bestowed a name on each one, giving it a definition forevermore. These names held such power that the concepts were bound to physical forms within the material world and order was brought to the chaos. Some held that this was the origin of his title as the Binder of What is Known and so did he take his place as one of the oldest and greatest of the gods of Toril.<ref name="FaP-52-54" />

Whether this was true or not,<ref name="FaP-52-54" /> Oghma was an interloper deity of the Celtic pantheon<ref name="CotR-13" /> who had links to many different planes.<ref name="FRCS-247" /> But finding himself being pushed out power in his own pantheon, he looked elsewhere, to [[Toril]], as many other deities did.<ref>{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|38}}</ref> He gained power in Toril's [[crystal sphere]] around the time of the rise of [[Netheril]],<ref name="P&P-p2">{{Cite book/Powers & Pantheons|2}}</ref> when a wave of planar immigrants came from [[Earth|another world]]<ref name="F&A-p105">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|105}}</ref> and settled in [[Faerûn]].<ref name="P&P-p2" /> One of these groups of people came from [[Wikipedia:Ireland|a rugged land]] with a culture tied to nature, the sea, and the warrior history of its [[Daghdha|dozen-king greater god]], bringing with them their own style of society and faiths,<ref name="P&P-p2" /> and their gods, of whom Oghma was but one.<ref name="F&A-p105" />

Myths regarding Oghma were as old as human history, if not far older. As well as the one above about being responsible for all concepts, Oghma was also said to have given written language to the world. Scholars and theologians debated their veracity, but none disputed he was an ancient deity whose faith predated recorded history.<ref name="FaP-52-54" />

Shortly after ascending to divinity, Oghma fathered many [[Demigod|demi-power]] children.<ref name="SL|2363">{{Cite digital book/Spinner of Lies|2363}}</ref> A few of the children decided to specialize in a single subject and learn everything they could about it. At some point [[Cyric]] lured twenty-two of the children away from Oghma<ref name="SL|2377">{{Cite digital book/Spinner of Lies|2377}}</ref> and transformed them into paintings known as the [[Whispering Children]].<ref>{{Cite digital book/Spinner of Lies}}</ref>

===Time of Troubles===
When the gods were cast down to walk the Realms in mortal form during the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, the fate and deeds of Oghma, were, perhaps surprisingly, a matter of mystery.{{speculation}} Early on, in [[Kythorn]], one old man in [[Arabel]] fervent in his belief, insisted that Oghma had adopted the form of his [[cat]], [[Pretti]]. Supposedly the cat even talked, speaking to the man on things of great import. No one in Arabel believed him, and they were distracted by [[Tymora]]'s much more public arrival anyway.<ref>{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|27|2}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/The Forgotten Realms Atlas|128}}</ref>

More importantly, Grand Patriarch [[Cullen Kordamant]], head of the whole Oghmanyte church and widely recognized as the Voice of Oghma, vanished without a trace from his home in [[Procampur]],<ref name="FRA-p29" /><ref name="RtR-47" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /><ref name="PoF-p53">{{Cite book/Power of Faerûn|53}}</ref><ref name="Dun198">{{Cite dungeon/198/Backdrop: Suzail|71}}</ref> as did much of his immediate clergy.<ref name="RtR-47" /> The disappearance was a subject of much mystery and speculation. Unfortunately, answers from Oghma and other gods were confusing and contradictory, providing no clear solution.<ref name="FRA-p29" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /> Though some thought that Kordamant simply died during the chaos, others claimed that it was actually Oghma who died, and the Grand Patriarch ascended to replace him.<ref name="FRA-p29" /> Some admitted the possibility that the Patriarch might be on another [[plane]] or had ascended to semi-divinity.<ref name="F&A-131-133" />

With the Oghmyte church left without an ultimate head, the mystery caused a growing split into rival factions, disagreeing along hierarchical and theological lines.<ref name="FRA-p29" /><ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="Dun198" /><ref name="EGPEFR-p159">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|159}}</ref> In Procampur, the [[Orthodox Church of Oghma]] stated that Kordamant had ascended to semi-divinity and was currently serving Oghma and, until he was proven dead or the god appointed a replacement or commanded otherwise, Kordamant was still the only true Patriarch and they would not recognize any replacement. On the other hand, the [[Oghmanyte Church in Exile|Oghmanyte Church]] in [[Sembia]] and the [[Pursuers of Pure Knowledge]] in [[Mintar]] claimed that a new Patriarch had in fact already been appointed by Oghma himself, and naturally put forward their own competing candidates. <ref name="F&A-131-133" /><ref name="FaP-52-54" /><ref name="PoF-p53" /><ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /><ref name="Dun198" /><ref name="EGPEFR-p159" /> This remained unresolved more than a century later.<ref name="FRCG-64,75,80" /><ref name="Dun198" />

Later, there arose a belief or heresy, depending on the faction, suggesting that Kordamant's fate had been concealed by Oghma as a test of faith for his priests, to determine whether they would hold to the quest of discovering and recording knowledge, or if they would fall prey to power struggles and reinterpretations of scripture.<ref name="EGPEFR-p159" /> The cat's involvement and opinions are unknown.<ref group="note">Many deities possessed mortal worshipers as their avatars during the Time of Troubles, so it's likely Oghma chose Kordamant for his.</ref>

In the {{yearlinkName|1368}}, Cyric created the ''[[Cyrinishad]]'', a book of great power that gave a false account of his life and twisted the reader to his faith. To counter it, Oghma collaborated with Mask, Mystra, and [[Fzoul Chembryl]] to create another book, an accurate biography titled ''[[The True Life of Cyric]]''.<ref name="FaP-p20" /><ref name="HLB-p103">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|56,103,138}}</ref><ref name="RoZK-p24">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|24}}</ref> Both Cyric and Oghma enlisted the Zhentish scribe [[Rinda]] to write their respective books. Oghma granted Rinda an ''[[amulet of godblinding]]'' to shield her from all detection, allowing her to escape with the ''Cyrinishad'' in her care.<ref name="RoZK-p24" /><ref name="HLB-p103" /> The books were switched and ''The True Life'' read by Fzoul, revealing Cyric's treachery to the Zhents. This thwarted the schemes of the Prince of Lies, at least for the time being, but only after much of [[Zhentil Keep]] was destroyed and Mask inadvertently read the ''Cyrinishad'' and lost his power.<ref name="FaP-p20" /><ref name="RoZK-p24" />

==Appendix==
===Background===
As depicted in the 1{{st}}-edition [[Deities & Demigods 1st edition|''Deities & Demigods'']], page 29,<ref name="CotR-13" /><ref>{{Cite dragon/54/Down-to-earth divinity|53}}</ref> Oghma is based on the [[Wikipedia:Ogma|Ogma, or Oghma]], an Irish god of language, learning, and eloquence, and on the related [[Wikipedia:Ogmios|Ogmios]], a Gallic god of eloquence, both echoes of a lost Celtic deity. In particular, the 'Binder' title arises from a depiction of Ogmios with chains of gold and amber linking his mouth to followers' ears, representing the power of persuasion and eloquence to bind and control the listener.
