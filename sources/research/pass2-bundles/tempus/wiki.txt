'''Tempus''' ({{Pronounce|TEM|pus|refs=<ref name="FRCS3e-p252" /><ref name="FPp71" />}}), was the [[greater deity|greater]] [[god]] of war in the [[Faerûnian pantheon]]. The dogma of the '''Lord of Battles''' was primarily concerned with honorable combat, forbidding cowardice and encouraging the use of force of arms to settle disputes.<ref name="FPp71" /> To the '''Foehammer''', war was a force of nature to be respected, for it had the power to remake civilizations.<ref name="FRCG-p80-81" />

{{Quote|In the name of Tempus, Lord of Battles, you will die honorably in righteous fire.|A [[dragonborn]] breathing flames at their foes.<ref name="MTPT">{{Cite MTG|CLB|165|{{MTGc|Breath Weapon||CLB|#=165}}|[[Adam Vehige]]}}</ref>}}
==Description==
Tempus appeared as a {{SI|12|ft|long=yes}} tall human in battered and bloodied [[plate mail]], his face hidden by a giant war helm, though his gaze remained a palpable force. His arms and legs remained bare (although he did wear gauntlets) and were crisscrossed by wounds oozing with his smoking, [[wine]]-red blood, yet he remained unaffected by these injuries.<ref name="F&A-p158" />

==Personality==
Tempus was a being of chaos, distributing his favor randomly. Such was the nature of war that he might side with one army one day and turn against them the next.<ref name="F&A-p158" /> He was not heartless, but exuberant, said to live only for excess, whether it be love for food, drink, hunting, or his greatest passion, battle.<ref name="F&A-p158" /><ref name="OHG-171">{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|171}}</ref>

Despite his fickleness and what others might think of him because of it, Tempus was also not treacherous. Indeed, he was honorable in battle, and would hate to betray a confidence. The chaotic god simply answered to his own warrior code, carving out his own path and letting none stand in his way. Rather than switching sides, it would be more accurate to say that the Lord of Battle's chaotic nature was on all sides equally,<ref name="OHG-171" /><ref name="F&A-p158" /> so long as each was committed to victory.<ref name="FRCG-p77">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|77}}</ref>

==Realm==
Tempus's realm of [[Warrior's Rest]] was anything but restful. Based in [[Limbo]], the realm was even more violent than the plane of [[Asgard]], defined by its endlessly raging battles. Like in Asgard, any that "died" would simply lie on the ground until their wounds healed, at which point they would fight for the one who defeated them.<ref name="OHG-171" /> Permanent death was reserved as a punishment for those who showed cowardice on the battlefield, generally in the form of Tempus withholding regeneration immediately before a quick and final death.<ref name="OHG-171" /><ref name="PGtF-p164">{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|164}}</ref>

==Relationships==
===Allies===
Tempus was quiet and solitary towards the other gods of the Faerunian pantheon. This was partially because he pursued no alliances or dalliances for any length of time himself<ref name="F&A-p158" /> and partially because the others feared he'd turn on them at any given moment.<ref name="OHG-171" /> The Foehammer did have a friendly relationship with Gond for his war machines<ref name="FRCS3e-p242">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|242}}</ref> a casual friendship with [[Nobanion]],<ref name="FRCS3e-p252" /> and had a lover in [[Beshaba]].<ref name="FRCG-p65">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|65}}</ref>

It was Tempus who sponsored the ascension of the [[Red Knight]], he who encouraged her worship, and only he who knew her true name. She looked to him as a father figure<ref name="PP-44">{{Cite book/Powers & Pantheons|44}}</ref> and their relationship was akin to that of a fond and protective father with a brilliant daughter that worked hard and successfully in the family business, namely war.<ref name="F&A-p158" /> The two spent a great amount of time together, mostly looking into the battles of Faerun past and present, but they also simply hunted or shared stories in his feasting hall.<ref name="PP-44" />

Tempus was the only ally of Uthgar, deity of the [[Uthgardt]] [[barbarian]]s of the [[Sword Coast North]] and had sponsored his divinity as well, having admired his fighting spirit. It was under Tempus's counsel that he didn't rashly declare war against various other gods both good and evil; he would have not only have ended up fighting several of the [[Gods of Fury]] but also [[Helm]] and [[The Triad]], which would have prompted a great deal of concerted retaliation.<ref name="PP-66-67">{{Cite book/Powers & Pantheons|66-67}}</ref><ref name="FAP-p85">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|85}}</ref> The Red Knight, and through her Tempus, had also tried to convince Valkur to take greater interest in naval conflicts. While he was more focused on protecting sailors,<ref name="FAP-p110">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|110}}</ref> he did eventually move to Warriors Rest, although his realm of Safe Harbor was a placid expanse of ocean between the raging battles of land and sea.<ref name="PGtF-p164" /> He enjoyed casual friendships with both Uthgar and Valkur.<ref name="FRCS3e-p252" />

===Enemies===
Strangely for a god of war, Tempus had very few actual enemies,<ref name="F&A-p158" /> although he was known to be derisively referred to as "the Butcher" by followers of [[Eilistraee]].<ref name="SF-p66">{{Cite book/Silverfall/Paperback 2005/Qilué|66}}</ref> [[Sune]] considered him a foe for his destruction of beautiful things, but besides her very nature making it hard for others to stay mad at her for long, he considered her flighty and irrelevant, not worth getting into active conflict with or even harboring a mutual dislike.<ref name="FRCS3e-p251">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|251}}</ref> One might think that of any deity Tempus would take issue with his diametric opposite [[Eldath]], the goddess of peace, yet the two did not consider each other enemies. Though he considered her weak and naïve, he respected her deep conviction and so generally ignored her and punished followers who abused her priests. Perhaps the Lord of Battle felt that war had little meaning or definition without the existence of peace to contrast it.<ref name="FRCS3e-p252" /><ref name="FPp71" /><ref name="FA-p57">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|57}}</ref>

The one being that deemed Tempus an enemy that he actually acknowledged was [[Garagos]]. Once the primary war god of [[West Faerûn]], the young [[demigod]] Tempus managed to defeat him, purportedly winning a century long battle by using his own berserker fury against him, and taking the position of the god of war as the spoils from their duel. The Blood-Reaver's loss to Tempus had only shortened his fuse, and his new incarnation possessed none of his former craftiness or mercy. While Garagos still harbored deep resentment towards Tempus unless his destructive abilities could someday surpass his tactical weakness, he would suffer if they someday met on the battlefield.<ref name="PP-44">{{Cite book/Powers & Pantheons|44}}</ref><ref name="FAP-p95">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|95}}</ref>

The main mystery behind this ancient feud was why Tempus, who lacked any other enemy he cared about, continued to tolerate Garagos's existence.<ref name="FPp71" /> He allowed Garagos to dwell in Warrior's Rest despite him being hostile to everyone else there<ref name="PGtF-p164" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p258">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|258}}</ref> and some said that he had uplifted the pragmatic and strategic Red Knight so that she could to act as his natural counterbalance.<ref name="PP-44" /> Some sages speculated that Tempus kept him around to act as bait, preferring to let the Bloodreaver lure out potential challengers. Others, meanwhile, suggested that there was one aspect of war that even Tempus found personally distasteful, the rabid rage of a mindless, murderous frenzy, and that he deliberately left it behind with Garagos when he became the god of war.<ref name="FPp71" />

==Worshipers==
{{Quote|Mighty Foehammer, lend me your strength as I wage your righteous war.|A basic prayer to Tempus.<ref name="BG3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}[[File:Tempus symbol.jpg|thumb|left|Holy symbol of Tempus.]]
[[File:Tempussymbol.png|thumb|245x245px|Another holy symbol of Tempus.]]
[[Faerûn]] was a violent land, and thus from sheer number of worshipers Tempus was one of the mightiest deities in the Realms. Nearly everyone who drew a sword or nocked an arrow had fought alongside a cleric of the Foehammer, and just as many had fought against one.<ref name="FPp71" />

Temples to the Lord of Battle looked more like military fortresses than the archetypal temple. They featured barracks, mess halls, armories, and training grounds.<ref name="FPp71" />

Due to its tendency to have followers and priests on both sides of any engagement, the church of Tempus had no central authority that might support one side or the other exclusively. Within a given temple or order, however, there was a strict hierarchy and chain of command.<ref name="FPp71" />

[[Specialty priest]]s of Tempus possessed an exceptional instinct for weapons, able to judge their craftsmanship and sense any potential magical properties simply by handling them.<ref name="FRA-2ed-p32">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|32}}</ref>

Among the [[Northlander]]s of the [[Moonshae Isles]], Tempus was worshiped as a god of not only war, but of storms and plunder.<ref name="FRCS2e-p110">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|110}}</ref> 

===Orders===
;Order of the Broken Blade: The [[Order of the Broken Blade]] honored those warriors and clergy who could no longer fight at their best. They continued to serve Tempus by working at shrines and temples.<ref name="FPp71" />
;Order of the Steel Fang: The [[Order of the Steel Fang]] were an elite group that took on the most dangerous missions. Their leaders were battle-tested members of the clergy.<ref name="FPp71" />

===Hierarchy===
All clergymen of Tempus were known as "Hammers". Each Hammer received their own ceremonial [[armor]], depending on their rank. Hammers were broken down further into ranks:<ref name="GHotR-p170-171">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|170-171}}</ref>
* Acolyte: The lowest in the hierarchy, they wore [[leather armor|leather jackets]] and [[baldric]]s.
* Stalwart: Priests, who wore [[chainmail]].
* Hardhar: Warrior-priests, who wore [[breastplate]]s and [[bracer]]s.
* Arahar: Battle-chaplains, who wore [[splint mail]].
* Rauthat: Swordmasters, who wore [[plate mail]] with shoulder spikes.
* Direhar: Guardian priests, who wore [[Plate mail|full plate]].
* Warlyon: High priests, who wore gilded magic plate mail that enabled flight.

===Dogma===
Tempus' orders to all combatants were simple and direct:
# Be fearless
# Never turn away from a fight.
# Obey the rules of war.<ref name="FRPG">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Player's Guide|153}}</ref>

===Rituals===
The words "Tempus thanks you" were used by the deity's faithful in conjunction with the response "and I thank Tempus" to indicate the completion of a deed that would please Tempus.<ref name="CaN-p166">{{Cite book/Cormyr: A Novel/Paperback 1998|166}}</ref>

==History==
{{Quote|We worship Tempos, not Tempus. Your people have even gone so far as to change His name and deny us even that small link. You feel names and titles have no value, outlander? To us, a name defines us as a people, and carries with it honor and respect—yet you would dare to make light of it, even when a god's name is concerned? You insult us.|King [[Wylfdene]]<ref name="iwdh">{{Cite game/Icewind Dale: Heart of Winter}}</ref>}}<br/>
Tempus was originally one of many potential war gods who emerged from the primordial clashes between [[Selûne]] and [[Shar]]. These gods fought constantly with each other, the victors absorbing the essence and power of the defeated. This continued until Tempus stood as the sole god of war in the [[Faerûnian pantheon]], having defeated and absorbed all of his competitors (with the notable exception of [[Garagos]], whom he defeated but spared).<ref name="FPp71" /> The barbarians of [[Icewind Dale]] claimed that Tempus' original name was "Tempos".<ref>{{Cite game/Icewind Dale}}</ref>

===The Time of Troubles===
In the [[Time of Troubles]] of the [[1358 DR|Year of Shadows, 1358 DR]], Tempus' [[avatar]] appeared in a ruined castle in [[Battledale]], just over five miles southwest of [[Essembra]]. Immediately following the [[Godswar]], [[Eldan Ambrose]], an [[Amn]]ian [[cleric]] of Tempus, saw Tempus during a battle in [[Swords Creek]]. After the fighting ended, Ambrose followed his god's trail back to Battledale, and found the castle (which originally belonged to [[Belarus]], a long-dead Tempuran). In the ruins of the great hall, Ambrose had a vision confirming the site as sacred to the Foehammer. Ambrose and his allies rebuilt the castle, establishing the [[Abbey of the Sword]].<ref name="FPp152">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|152}}</ref>
