'''Mask''' ({{Pronounce|MASK|audio=Mask.ogg|refs=<ref>{{Cite book/FRCS|246}}</ref><ref name="FP-p45-47" />}}) was the [[intermediate deity|intermediate]] [[god]] of shadows, thievery, and formerly intrigue in the [[Faerûnian pantheon]], a loner god most often associated with those of ill-repute. The '''Lord of Shadows'''  was a [[chaotic neutral]] or [[neutral evil]] deity symbolized with a velvet black mask tainted with red.<ref name="F&A-p109-112" />

==Description==
[[Avatar]]s of Mask within his temples usually appeared as slightly built human males wearing soft gray leather armor, a black cloak, and a black mask tinged with red (believed by scholars to be his true or chosen form). He might also manifest as a soft-spoken human female with pale, glowing skin, dark, floor-length hair, and starry white lights for eyes. This form always walked barefoot and was constantly shrouded in a shadow-like gloom. In public however, Mask's form was entirely inconsistent, appearing as members of all races, genders, ages, builds, and capabilities. No matter his form however, his movements made no sound and left no trail.<ref name="F&A-p109-112" />

===Manifestations===
One of Mask's abilities was taking on a shape of any humanoids or any mask. Some of the masks he shapeshifted into were patches of gauze cut in rectangle shape and lacking eye-holes. In this form of a piece of harmless-looking cloth, Mask could move about without detection.<ref name="TEV" />

==Personality==
Known for his constant scheming, cool head, and oft-reserved mocking comment, Mask lost a significant portion of his power, the intrigue portfolio, to [[Cyric]]. This, of course, led to two things: an enduring hatred of Cyric, and the Lord of Shadows training himself to be more direct than he was in his prior, elaborate plots.<ref name="FRCS3e-p246" />

He was a manipulator and valued subtle schemes, so artful and light that no one noticed the god's touch. Mask enjoyed eavesdropping, and he could whisper in perfect imitation of a voice he had recently heard. He used this mimicry to purr falsehoods into mortals' ears or talk with voices of recently dead to create an illusion of them still being in the land of the living.<ref name="TEV">{{Cite web/theedverse/2022-12-19 - Mask Facts}}</ref>

==Possessions==
The Shadowlord wielded a pair of magical, twin [[longsword]]s known as ''[[Stealthwhisper]]'' and ''[[Shadowblade]]''.<ref>{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|45, 46, 47}}</ref>

==Divine Realm==
Mask kept a divine realm called [[Shadow Keep]] on [[Niflheim]], the second layer of [[Hades]] in the [[Great Wheel cosmology]] and the [[Plane of Shadow]] in the [[World Tree cosmology]]. Visitors might glimpse the Keep, but very few ever reached it, as it was constantly shrouded in a heavy fog that led them astray.<ref name="F&A-p109-112" /><ref name="FRA-26">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|26}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Planes of Conflict/Liber Malevolentiae|47-48}}</ref>

==Relationships==
Simply put, Mask was a loner and unlike other deities he enjoyed his own company greatly.<ref name="FRCS3e-p246" /><ref name="TEV" /> However, before this, he had frequent alliances with [[Bane]]. If nothing else, their sizable hatred of Cyric gave them common ground in addition to their history of working together. Mask was also at direct odds with [[Waukeen]], the goddess of merchants and honest trade. All guardians of light, knowledge, and duty were opposed to him. This included [[Selûne]], whose light tended to reveal his own faithful whilst they worked.<ref name="FRCS3e-p246" />

===Chosen===
* [[Avner|Avner of Hartsvale]] (briefly a Chosen of [[Kelemvor]], during the [[Time of Troubles]])
* [[Erevis Cale]]<ref name="Twilight Falling">{{Cite book/Twilight Falling}}</ref>
* [[Drasek Riven]]<ref name="Twilight Falling" />
* [[Kesson Rel]] (First Chosen)

He was allied with the [[drow]] god of thievery [[Vhaeraun]].<ref name="DD-36">{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|36}}</ref> The relationship itself was businesslike.<ref name="FaP-114">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|114}}</ref> However, Mask planned to compensate what he lost to Cyric with Vhaeraun's divinity,<ref name="LoD-29">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|29}}</ref> while Vhaeraun used the similarity between their symbols to recruit [[half-elves]] and [[human]]s into his [[church of Vhaeraun|church]].<ref name="DD-38">{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|38}}</ref> The two were often confused with one another among non-drow on the surface.<ref name="DD-37">{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|37}}</ref> They also shared one very similar title, Mask's "Lord of Shadows"<ref name="FaP-45">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|45}}</ref> and Vhaeraun's "Lord of Shadow".<ref name="DDG-93">{{Cite book/Drizzt Do'Urden's Guide to the Underdark|93}}</ref>

==Worshipers==
[[File:Mask symbol.jpg|thumb|right|The symbol of Mask.]]
The [[church of Mask]] stated that wealth rightfully belonged to those who could acquire it. Honesty was for fools but apparent honesty was a very valuable thing, and subtlety was everything.<ref name="F&P-p110">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|110}}</ref>

The Clergy of Mask were called Maskarrans, with elite [[specialty priest]]s referred to as demarchesses ({{pronounce|deh|mar|KESS|es}}) if female, and demarchs ({{pronounce|deh|MARKS}}) if male.<ref name="F&P-p110" /> Maskarrans addressed each other as "Brother/Sister Shadow", no matter their rank. Clergy that completed an especially dangerous heist or complex act of manipulation were often admitted to the [[Circle of the Gray Ribbon]].<ref name="F&P-p110" />

Shrines of gods radiated that part of their philosophical orientation they shared the most. For example, a shrine of a chaotic evil deity, who was more chaotic than evil, radiated chaos. Mask's shrines during the 14{{th}} century DR radiated chaos,<ref name="CoR-99">{{Cite book/Champions of Ruin|99}}</ref> despite him being a neutral evil deity during that time.<ref name="FaP-45" />

Adventuring clerics devoted to Mask had typically worn standard armored vestments, indistinguishable from those of other faiths. In contrast, specialty priests adopted the guise of common street-goers, often resembling [[thieves]] in both attire and demeanor. Their everyday clothing helped them blend into urban crowds, avoiding unwanted attention.<ref name="FRA-26" />

However, during ceremonies, their appearance became far more elaborate. They had donned brightly colored motley tunics and trousers, featuring puffed sleeves and ornate cuffs. Over this vibrant ensemble, they had worn a gray hooded cloak, which could be drawn tightly to conceal the colors beneath. Beneath the hood, all priests—whether standard or specialty—had worn black cloth masks.<ref name="FRA-26" />

The material of these masks had varied depending on the region. In places where thievery was tolerated and Mask’s worship accepted, the masks had been made of sheer fabric. In contrast, throughout much of the North, they had been crafted from thick wool to obscure the wearer’s identity. A common saying had circulated: "The degree of law in a town could be read on the face of a cleric of Mask."<ref name="FRA-26" />

Mask's priesthood had operated independently across cities and regions. This decentralization had served to protect temples and guilds from collective retaliation—ensuring that actions taken against one enclave did not endanger others. In regions dominated by a single thieves' guild, temples or shrines dedicated to Mask had often been linked to the guild hall via hidden underground tunnels. In larger cities with rival guilds, Mask's temple had typically been located in a subterranean space, recognized by all factions as neutral ground.<ref name="FRA-26" />

Specialty priests of Mask had operated more like thieves than traditional clergy. They had picked locks, moved silently, and navigated the underworld with ease. Fluent in thieves’ cant, they had spoken in riddles and coded slang, understood only by those who lived in the shadows. Unlike other priests, they had no power over the undead — they neither turn nor commanded them. Their strength lay in secrecy, deception, and survival, not divine confrontation.<ref name="FRA-26" />

===Dogma===
Mask's faithful were led to believe that the god saw everything that happens in the dark and to trust it, as one would be made an easy target by walking into plain light. The abilities to deceive, as truth was a foolish virtue and manipulation was better than brute force, and to be dextrous were also appreciated. Whilst the faith advocated for accumulating riches, it also said to only steal what was necessary. They also believed that owning something simply required being in possession of it.<ref name="FRA-26" />

==History==
===Godsbane===
During the [[Time of Troubles]] of [[1358 DR]], Mask took the shape of a powerful blade called [[Godsbane]].<ref name="F&A-p109-112" /> He eventually came to be wielded by the then-mortal Cyric; he acquired the [[sword]] by stealing it from a [[halfling]] named [[Atherton Cooper|Sneakabout]],<ref name="FRE3-p10">{{Cite book/Waterdeep (adventure)|8,10}}</ref><ref name="Wd-c3">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|52-52|3}}</ref> who in turn had killed the former wielder of the sword.<ref name="Wd-c14">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|257-263|14}}</ref>

In the years following the Time of Troubles, Mask released the powerful hound [[Kezef]] to try and kill Cyric, but the hound turned instead on the Lord of Shadows, not stopping the chase until it had bitten off one of the god's legs. Mask, seemingly too weak to heal, acquired the powerful weapon [[Houndsbane]] to defend himself; the weapon was a gift from the deity of magic, Mystra.<ref>{{Cite book/Crucible|?}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Champions of Ruin|146-147}}</ref>

===Death===
In the {{YearlinkName|1374}}, Mask relinquished his divinity and his life over to [[Shar]] to repay a millennia-old debt to the goddess, who was revealed to be his "mother" as well as the person he served as a herald. Just before his death, Shar revealed Mask's true name as '''Lessinor''', though its origin (either as a mortal name or a birth name) was not explained. A portion of Mask's divinity—the portion stolen by [[Kesson Rel]]—was absorbed in equal portions by [[Erevis Cale]], [[Drasek Riven]], and [[Rivalen Tanthul|Prince Rivalen]] of the [[Thultanthar|Shade enclave]]. Erevis then sacrificed himself to [[Mephistopheles]], the [[archdevil]] Lord of [[Cania]], in payment for releasing the half of the soul of [[Magadon]] that he ate; Mephistopheles in turn absorbed the portion of Mask's divinity held by Cale.<ref name="Shadowrealm" />

===Rebirth===
There were several hints that Mask could return, either in person or via a successor who took his place in the pantheon, namely:{{Speculation}}
* When Mask surrendered to Shar, his last thoughts were about the secret he kept from her.<ref name="Shadowrealm">{{Cite book/Shadowrealm}}</ref>
* Mask apparently arranged that Cale's son would be born in safety after the [[Spellplague]] of [[1385 DR]].<ref name="Shadowrealm" />
* Drasek Riven, himself ascended to a [[demigod]] of shadows, prophesied to Magadon of the return of Mask.<ref name="Shadowrealm" />

This was proven to be true when the Shadowlord's plans to thwart the Cycle of Night perpetuated by Shar on countless worlds resulted in Mask's divinity (both the Shar-consumed and Kesson Rel portions) being gathered back into a single being—Drasek Riven, Mask's former Second of Five—resulting in the aforementioned cycle being thwarted, along with the stripping of divinities from both Mephistopheles and Rivalen Tanthul.<ref name="The Godborn">{{Cite book/The Godborn}}</ref>
