'''Cyric''' ({{Pronounce|SEER|ik|refs=<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p240">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|240}}</ref><ref name="F&P-p20-22" />}}), known as '''Sirhivatizangpo''' by his [[Gugari]] worshipers<ref name="TH-p57"/> and as '''N'asr''' by the [[Bedine]],<ref name="HL-p106">{{Cite book/Hall of Heroes|106}}</ref><ref name="RoF-p106">{{Cite book/Races of Faerûn|106}}</ref> was the monomaniacal [[Faerûnian pantheon|Faerunian]] [[god]] of lies, trickery,<ref name="SCAG-p27"/> and strife,<ref name="SCAG-p27"/> having previously held dominion over tyranny, murder, lies, death, intrigue, and illusion.<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /><ref name="F&P-p20-22" /> It was the unholy radiance of the '''Dark Sun''' that drew power-hungry mortals to their inexorable deaths like moths to a flame,<ref name="F&P-p20-22" /> and it was the '''Prince of Lies''' who murdered [[Mystra]] and caused the [[Spellplague]], throwing the cosmos into turmoil in an act that cost him much of his following.<ref name="FRCG-p45,282"/> 

{{Quote|If I can destroy the love of gods, then I can certainly fill the lives of Faerûn's mortals with strife and discord.|Cyric, during his trial.<ref name="Cru-p371">{{Cite book/Crucible|371}}</ref>}}
==Description==
[[File:CyricAsMortal.JPG|thumb|left|184px|''Cyric as a mortal.'']]
As a mortal man, Cyric had a lean athletic build. His dark brown hair outlined a face of sharp angles, highlighted by crow's-feet eyes,<ref name="HoH-p14">{{Cite book/Hall of Heroes|14}}</ref> a pointed chin, and hawkish nose.<ref name="Waterdeep-p24">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|24}}</ref>

On first becoming a god, Cyric's [[avatar]] took on a similar appearance, but with ghastly pale skin and intense, dark eyes.<ref name="F&P-p20-22" /><ref name="F&A-p51-52" /><ref name="PoL-p65">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|65}}</ref> He exhibited human mannerisms, and was often prone to wriggling and squirming when he became anxious or impatient,<ref name="PoL-p25">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|25}}</ref><ref name="PoL-p195">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|195}}</ref><ref name="PoL-p315">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|315}}</ref> But after a decade of godhood, Cyric's avatar began to more reflect the horrific nature of his character. His face went so gaunt it resembled a skull and the skin wrapped around his tightly wound cords of muscle turned red.<ref name="PoL-p65">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|65}}</ref> Cyric's eyes appeared as small flames within his skull, his teeth turned crooked and yellowed,<ref name="PoL-p186">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|186}}</ref> and his fingers were stripped of flesh until only bone remained.<ref name="PoL-p104">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|104}}</ref> With some effort, Cyric could hide this appearance with use of [[illusion]] magic when making appearances in the Realms.<ref name="PoL-p156">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|156}}</ref>

Following his descent into madness, Cyric's skeletal visage<ref name="Cru-p225">{{Cite book/Crucible|225}}</ref> and bony hands were scarred and scorched by the flaming souls of his [[Burning Men|wretched faithful]]. Unlike before, these wounds could not be hidden by any form of magical means.<ref name="PoL-p374">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|374}}</ref> Cyric's fractured psyche caused him to speak in a thousand voices at once.<ref name="Cru-p225">{{Cite book/Crucible|225}}</ref> What remained of his heart appeared as a mass of [[poison]]ous brown sludge interwoven with white cords, which were in fact remnants of the two gods he had slain.<ref name="Cru-p146-149" /> 

==Personality==
{{Quote|If you lavish godhood on the pretenders that chained you here, then you're right. I'm no god. I'm very much more than that.|Cyric, to [[Kezef]] the Chaos Hound.<ref name="PoL-p139">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|139}}</ref>}}
In life, Cyric was a petty and selfish man that believed everyone was truly alone in life. Placing trust in others was folly that would only end in suffering or, in all likelihood, death.<ref name="HoH-p14" /> While he occasionally acted in the interest of others<ref name="Shadowdale-p33-35" /> and once famously rallied the [[Dalesfolk]] in a [[Second battle of Shadowdale|terrific battle]],<ref name="Shadowdale-p289-291" /> Cyric was unfortunately overcome by the worst aspects of his nature.<ref name="Shadowdale-p138-151" /><ref name="Tantras-p54-64" />

Cyric's selfishness, inability to cope with intense feelings,<ref name="Shadowdale-p255" /> and unwillingness to rely upon others for support,<ref name="HoH-p14" /> led Cyric to isolate and withdraw himself from people that actually cared for him.<ref name="Shadowdale-p164">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|164}}</ref> As a result, Cyric suffered and languished in a state of inner turmoil. His earnest but malnourished desire to do right by others remained at odds with his sense of self-preservation and a yearning to prove himself by appearing powerful.<ref name="Shadowdale-p269">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|269}}</ref>

[[File:Cyric Godsbane.png|thumb|right|250px|Cyric wielding ''[[Godsbane]]'' above his head in a moment of "triumph" within the [[Bone Castle]].]]
Cyric's experiences as an adventurer accentuated the darker aspects of his persona, namely his perverse fascination with killing,<ref name="Shadowdale-p249">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|249}}</ref> the ease with which he spoke half-truths, his inclination to incite fear in others, the urge for bloodlust he harbored, and the pleasure he felt when exerting dominance over the weak.<ref name="Tantras-p54-64" /> The worst aspects of Cyric's humanity were only magnified when he ascended to divinity. As a god Cyric was entirely egocentric,<ref name="FRCS3e-p240" /> and took immense joy from manipulating mortals into ruining or ending their own lives.<ref name="FRCS3e-p240" /><ref name="F&P-p20-22" /> For a time Cyric came to believe himself superior to all other beings in the [[multiverse]].<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /><ref name="Cru-p1-2" /> 

As the youngest god of the Faerûnian pantheon,<ref name="FRA-p17" /><ref name="FRCS2e.RTR-p46" /> Cyric's self-absorption led to him often acting out like a child. He was irrationally impatient and became restless<ref name="PoL-p95">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|95}}</ref> and moody<ref name="PoL-p68">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|68}}</ref> when things didn't go his way fast enough for his liking.<ref name="PoL-p197-198" /> He formed petty, nasty, and sexist nicknames for the other gods and he insisted that courtiers in his divine court only refer to them as such.<ref name="PoL-p28">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|28}}</ref> In truth, Cyric was terrified of the other powers, and was even put ill at ease by some [[demigod|demipowers]],<ref name="BGII.ToB" /> to the point that he became crippled with paranoia.<ref name="PoL-p176" /><ref name="FRPG-p151">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Player's Guide|151}}</ref>

During his descent into madness, Cyric's unpredictability and erratic behavior became much worse.<ref name="PoL-p197-198">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|197-198}}</ref> He was overtaken with a myriad of delusions and countless voices in his mind<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /> that some speculated were the remnants of the gods he had slain.<ref name="RZK.CB-p113-114" /> He would fantasize horrors that could be inflicted upon those that wronged him, conflate those fallacies with half-truths and false memories, then accept the results as new reality that comprised glorious victories over his enemies.<ref name="PoL-p69">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|69}}</ref> Cyric's cracked and chaotic psyche could barely hold back his myriad of conflicting selves.<ref name="PoL-p375">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|375}}</ref><ref name="PoL-p65">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|65}}</ref> Cyric abandoned any precept of a polytheistic pantheon, and came to believe himself the only true god of the Realms,<ref name="Cru-p30-40">{{Cite book/Crucible|30-40}}</ref> and demanded that all his followers appease and worship him as such.<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /><ref name="EFR-p133">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|133}}</ref>

==Abilities==
For a time Cyric was among the most powerful deities among the Faerûnian pantheon, having taken on at least partial portfolios of five different deities.<ref name="FRA-p17" /> He drew much of his own power from the constant in-fighting between the various sects and cells of [[Church of Cyric|his church]].<ref name="PoF-p53">{{Cite book/Power of Faerûn|53}}</ref>

Cyric's avatar was entirely immune to the effects of illusion, [[charm]], [[fear]]-inducing, or other emotion-altering magic, and was always ignored by [[undead]] beings. He could [[feeblemind|inflict madness]] upon others with his mere touch. He often appeared as an avatar that resembled his mortal body, but could choose any form he wished,<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /> at times preferring those of a terrible spider,<ref name="Cru-p193-195">{{Cite book/Crucible|193-195}}</ref> or the visage of a skull.<ref name="Sent-p193-197" /> Cyric's divine powers were amplified while on [[Pandemonium]], the plane of pure chaos.<ref name="PoL-p134">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|134}}</ref> For a brief time, Cyric was stripped of any and all magical powers by Mystra, when she refused to grant him access to [[the Weave]].<ref name="PoL-p64-67" />

When he appeared as a manifestation, Cyric preferred to take on the appearance of a [[wraith]] that haunted the dreams of mortals or that of a poisonous cloud of smoke. When he couldn't be bothered to appear himself, Cyric merely created nightmares of undead in the psyche of his victims.<ref name="F&A-p51-52" />

==Possessions==
As an adventurer, Cyric kept an array of weapons and handy magical items. He kept on his person several [[dagger]]s, a [[handaxe]], both [[long sword|long]] and [[short sword]]s, and a [[longbow]] with a [[quiver]] of [[arrow]]s.<ref name="HoH-p14" /><ref name="Shadowdale-p40">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|40}}</ref> At times he wore a suit of [[plate mail]],<ref name="HoH-p14" /> and at others shrouded himself within a dark ''[[cloak of displacement]]''.<ref name="Shadowdale-p40" /> 

[[File:Cyric Symbol-5e.png|thumb|right|200px|''Cyric's unholy symbol on a ring.'']]
Cyric's throne room within his [[Bone Castle|divine realm]] was a monument to his victories. It housed twisted trophies that besmirched other gods and degraded their followers, including a painting of a [[Deneir]]an faithful, the pigment of which was made of children's blood; the executioner's axe of a crazed king that venerated [[Tyr]]; and the nail of a worshiper of [[Sune]] used to blind himself after receiving a vision of the goddess of beauty.<ref name="PoL-p73">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|73}}</ref> He drank from a [[ruby]]-studded [[silver]] chalice crafted to resemble a cloven heart. It was said to be filled with the tears of jilted lovers and crestfallen idealists,<ref name="FRCS3e-p240" /><ref name="PoL-p107-108" /> and even held those shed by two lovelorn gods.<ref name="Cru-p352-374" />

===Weaponry===
In his final months as a mortal,<ref name="Waterdeep-p240-244" /> and first decade as a god, Cyric wielded the [[Sentient item|sentient]] shortsword ''[[Godsbane]]''. He was completely enamored with the sword,<ref name="PoL-p69">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|69}}</ref> and revered her as his most prized possession and dearest companion.<ref name="PoL-p143">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|143}}</ref> Cyric nourished her with the blood of the powerful and the innocent.<ref name="Waterdeep-p240-244" /> ''Godsbane'' was in fact the transformed sentience of [[Mask]], the God of Illusions.<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /> While in Cyric's possession, Mask cooed into Cyric's ear, fulfilling the role of his lover and enabler of his worst delusions and most twisted schemes.<ref name="PoL-p176-178">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|176-178}}</ref>

Following the revelation of Mask's treachery, Cyric acquired a new blade, the ''+5 longsword'' called ''[[Razor's Edge]]''.<ref name="FRCS3e-p234">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|234}}</ref><ref name="F&P-p22">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|22}}</ref>

==Divine Realms==
Following his ascension, Cyric took over the former [[divine realm]] of [[Myrkul]], the [[Bone Castle]], in the [[Gray Waste]] (also known as Hades in the [[Great Wheel cosmology|Great Wheel cosmological model]]).<ref name="FRCS2e.RTR-p46" /> When he lost dominion over the dead to Kelemvor, Cyric relocated to Pandemonium and fashioned himself a warped and changeable realm he named the [[Supreme Throne|Castle of the Supreme Throne]], known as the Shattered Castle by others.<ref name="F&A-p51-52"/><ref name="FRA-p17"/><ref name="F&A-p84"/><ref>{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|124}}</ref> Within the [[World Tree cosmology]], Cyric took the [[batrachi]] realm of the Supreme Throne from [[Limbo]] and fashioned his own plane from it. The Supreme Throne survived the transition as the cosmos reshaped into a [[World Axis cosmology|newly accepted model]], though it became a prison for the Mad God.<ref name="GHotR-p5">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|5}}</ref><ref name="GHotR-p159"/>

==Activities==
{{Quote|Didn't you hear [[Ao]]? There was no crime. Leira died because I willed it. Any of you could be next. That's my place in [[the Balance]]: To weed out the weak from this pathetic pantheon.|Cyric, to his fellow greater powers.<ref name="PoL-p63" />}}
As a god Cyric begrudgingly fulfilled his divine duties to a point, but mainly focused on his plots to strike out against those he hated.<ref name="PoL-p176-178" /> He carried this neglect so far as to disrupt the Balance of the divine powers, and sow discord among the other members of the pantheon.<ref name="Cru-p30-40" />

==Relationships==
{{Quote|I have little desire to see yet another godling who might be a possible opponent. If you even have any power worth noting.|Cyric, to [[Abdel Adrian]] in [[1369 DR]].<ref name="BGII.ToB">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate II: Throne of Bhaal}}</ref>[[File:Cyric - ToB.mp3]]}}

===With Mortals===
While operating in the [[Naug-orls|thieves' guild]] of Zhentil Keep, Cyric was instructed by a ruthless mentor named [[Marek]]. Cyric maintained an acquaintance with the vicious thief [[Quicksal]], though they could hardly be called friends.<ref name="Shadowdale-p122-123">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|122-123}}</ref> Cyric did find true camaraderie with his fellow adventurer [[Mystra (Midnight)|Midnight]],<ref name="Shadowdale-p204-205">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|204-205}}</ref> and the pair formed a close bond in short order.<ref name="Shadowdale-p158-159" /> Midnight tended to Cyric<ref name="Shadowdale-p189">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|189}}</ref> when he was gravely wounded,<ref name="Shadowdale-p183-186">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|183-186}}</ref> and the thief was genuinely grateful.<ref name="Shadowdale-p204-205" /> That bond did not last long however, as Midnight's burgeoning romance with their fellow adventurer [[Kelemvor]] incited fierce jealousy in Cyric.<ref name="Shadowdale-p161-162">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|138-151}}</ref> After Mystra spurned Cyric's romantic interests, he grew increasingly impatient and then hateful towards her,<ref name="Tantras-p54-64" /> a dynamic that continued following their ascensions to godhood.<ref name="F&P-p20-22" />

===Divine Foes===
Cyric looked down upon<ref name="PoL-p28" /> and even hated most of the other deities of [[Toril]], and held particular loathing for Mystra, Kelemvor, and [[Bane]],<ref name="F&P-p20-22" /> whose death he claimed credit for and whose divine portfolio he briefly took over.<ref name="FRCS3e-p238">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|238}}</ref> Cyric's hatred for Mystra and Kelemvor originated during their time together as mortal companions, and intensified after his ascension. Cyric lashed out at everyone and everything the goddess Midnight held dear,<ref name="HL-p8" /> and scoured the multiverse to enact revenge Kelemvor's mortal soul<ref name="PoL-p70" /><ref name="PoL-p244-246" /> His loathing for Kelemvor intensified after the hero arose as a god himself, and was given power over a portion of Cyric's domain.<ref name="RZK.CB-p30" />

Cyric drew the ire of [[Oghma]] when he manipulated the god's dominion over the spread of knowledge in an attempt to convert all the Realms to the Cyricist faith.<ref name="PoL-p176">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|176}}</ref>

Over time, Cyric became paranoid of the Realms' greater powers, believing they all conspired together to strip him of his divinity,<ref name="PoL-p176" /> and was ultimately left alone with no allies.<ref name="PoL-p64-67">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|64-67}}</ref> Cyric's fear led to making many enemies among the pantheon: [[Azuth]], Tyr, [[Torm]], Deneir, Leira, [[Iyachtu Xvim]], Bane, and many others.<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /><ref name="F&P-p20-22" /> 

===Divine Allies===
During his time as Lord of the Dead, Cyric was dutifully served by his seneschal [[Jergal]],<ref name="PoL-p107">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|107}}</ref> the god whose former portfolios Cyric later claimed.<ref name="F&A-p37">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|37}}</ref> Jergal attempted to advise Cyric, guide him during his dealings with other deities—especially the [[Circle of Twelve Powers]]—and help properly govern his divine domain.<ref name="PoL-p176-178" /> The former god of the dead was wholly unsuccessful in his efforts.<ref name="Cru-p1-2" /><ref name="PoL-p318-321" />

Cyric maintained a partnership with Mask for a time, but believed him a weakling and coward.<ref name="PoL-p72">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|72}}</ref> When Mask revealed himself to be Cyric's sword ''Godsbane'',<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /> the two became hated enemies.<ref name="F&P-p20-22" /> He later forged a similar alliance with Shar<ref name="GHotR-p156" />—the divine mother of Mask—<ref name="Shadowrealm-p330-333">{{Cite book/Shadowrealm|330-333}}</ref>but was manipulated by the Lady of Night into carrying out plots that benefited her, and burdening the blame afterwards.<ref name="GHotR-p159" /><ref name="CM-p113-114" /> Cyric lashed out at Shar in revenge, but by that time was too ineffectual to harm her or her following.<ref name="Sent-p193-197" />

In his desperate struggle to maintain power, Cyric resorted to cavorting with at least two of the [[Seven Lost Gods]], [[Kezef]] the Chaos Hound and [[Dendar]] the Night Serpent. These dangerous otherworldly beings were feared by even the greater powers of the Realms.<ref name="PoL-p138-144">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|138-144}}</ref><ref name="PoL-p84">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|84}}</ref> 

==Worshipers==
Even before godhood Cyric attracted a zealous contingent of Zhent warriors called the [[Company of the Scorpions]] that devoted themselves to his cause. They followed Cyric during his quest to retrieve the ''[[Tablets of Fate]]'' and cast aside their loyalties to their former employers and home realms. But while Cyric did spare them from inadvertent death,<ref name="Waterdeep-p28-31">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|28-31}}</ref> he only traveled with them so long as they proved useful,<ref name="Waterdeep-p170-182" /> and completely abandoned them afterwards.<ref name="Waterdeep-p186-190" /><ref name="Waterdeep-p240-244" />

===Church===
[[File:Cyric symbol.jpg|thumb|right|300px|''Unholy symbol of Cyric ''circa'' the mid–14{{th}} century DR.'']]
{{Main|Church of Cyric}}
The Cyricist church was hated across Toril with good reason: Cyric's church was pledged to spread strife and work murder everywhere in order to make folk believe in and fear the Dark Sun. It supported cruel rulers and indulged fostered fear and intrigue while avoiding the spread of outright war. The church was often beset by internal feuds and backstabbing for some time before eventually waning.<ref name="F&P-p20-22" /><ref name="LoD-p7-8">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|7-8}}</ref> Many outside the church viewed it as a twisted den of madness, trickery, and death. Cyricist clergy condemned this outlook, preferring to see their religion as one of enlightenment. It purportedly revealed that all societal bonds of friendship, family, and love were nothing but ties that always withered away, and were therefore useless, weak, and pitiful.<ref name="SCAG-p27"/>

Cyric's [[clerics]] prayed for spells at night, after moonrise, and often trained as [[rogues]] or [[assassins]].<ref name="F&P-p20-22" /> His [[specialty priest]]s were known as [[strifeleader]]s.<ref name="F&P-p204">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|204}}</ref> They celebrated few holy days and did not acknowledge the date of his ascension to divinity, because it coincide with that of Midnight. Whenever a temple acquired something, or someone, important enough to be sacrificed, its high priest declared a Day of the Dark Sun to signify the holiness of the event. Eclipses were considered holy occasions, and were accompanied by feasts, fervent prayers, and bloody sacrifices.<ref name="F&P-p20-22" />

In addition to the worshipers Cyric claimed during his ascension, he also garnered those of the [[Church of Leira|Leiran]] faith after slaying the Lady of Deception.<ref name="GHotR-p144" /> Cyric's faith spread across some unlikely groups, including the insular Bedine people of [[Anauroch]],<ref name="Anauroch-p70">{{Cite book/Anauroch|70}}</ref> [[Tethyr]]ian cultists of Ao that remained unaware of their true divine patron,<ref name="F&A-p30" /> some of the more malevolent [[tiefling]]s of the Realms,<ref name="RoF-p127">{{Cite book/Races of Faerûn|127}}</ref> and renegade [[githyanki]] that forewent worship of their [[lich]]-queen.<ref name=Poly159.KLQ-p29">{{Cite polyhedron/159/Incursion: Knights of the Lich-Queen|29}}</ref>

===Center of Worship===
In the early years of Cyric's divinity, [[Zhentil Keep]]<ref name="RZK.CB-p51">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|51}}</ref> and the surrounding lands of the [[Moonsea]] were considered the center of his worship in the Realms.<ref name="M.RG-p11-12">{{Cite book/The Moonsea/Reference Guide|11-12}}</ref><ref name="MotM-p124">{{Cite book/Mysteries of the Moonsea|124}}</ref> During this time the [[Zhentarim]] served as an extension of the Cyricist church.<ref name="CotH-p118">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|118}}</ref><ref name="LoD-p96-97">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|96-97}}</ref> Following the decline of Cyric's influence, his religion endured throughout [[Amn]] and the [[Lands of Intrigue]].<ref name="LoI.A-p5">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|5}}</ref><ref name="FRCG-p92">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|92}}</ref> The tenets of Cyricism fit in well with their culture of ambition, lack of reliance on others, and "buyer beware"–type contracts.<ref name="SCAG-p27">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|27}}</ref> 

===Deceased===
Cyric's immortal servants were known as the 'denizens'<ref name="HL-p57">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|57}}</ref> or 'shades', souls that dwelled within the Bone Castle and the surrounding [[City of the Dead (Hades)|City of Strife]]. He also held dominion over [[the False]]—souls of the dead that lied about the god they worshiped—and the [[Faithless]], whose tortured forms composed [[Wall of the Faithless|the Wall]].<ref name="PoL-p77">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|77}}</ref> Cyric considered his denizens to be servile minions whose only purpose was to appropriately fear their god and cater to his needs.<ref name="PoL-p106-108">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|106-108}}</ref> Cyric had their immortal forms changed to grotesque and powerful monstrosities that better suited his liking.<ref name="PoL-p180">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|180}}</ref> He offered protection from his terrible plots in exchange for their eternal service, but only upheld his end of the bargain when convenient.<ref name="PoL-p107-108">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|107-108}}</ref>

The mortal Cyric was born a bastard to two of Zhentil Keep's most unfortunate souls sometime around the early 1330s [[DR]]. His mother was an unsuccessful and destitute [[bard]] that lived in the poorest part of Zhentil Keep, and his father was a low-ranking [[Zhentilar]] officer that refused to acknowledge Cyric as his son. Cyric's mother resorted to [[prostitution]] and depended on the good will of others to keep her son alive, which she managed until the day she was slain by Cyric's father. The illegitimate Cyric was sold into slavery by his father, as recompense for the inconvenience of his existence, and shipped off to the southern nation of [[Sembia]].<ref name="HoH-p14" /><ref name="PoL-p118-121">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|119-121}}</ref>

The infant Cyric was purchased by a childless merchant-class couple as an heir to pass on their wine-making business. [[Astolpho]] the vintner and his wife raised Cyric in the lap of luxury for years, catering to the young lad's every want and desire. At the age of ten, ''circa'' the {{YearlinkName|1340}}, Cyric uncovered the truth of his birth and dramatically ran away from his family's estate. While he was promptly returned home by local guardsmen, Cyric boisterously asserted to all that his true home was Zhentil Keep and word of his origins spread rapidly among the Sembian elite. Over the course of the next two years, Cyric's parents became pariahs among their peers and their businesses came to ruin. The young man's growing mistrust of his parents turned to derision and outright hatred of them. At the mere age of 12 years old, Cyric murdered his parents in their sleep and left Sembia, heading north for Zhentil Keep.<ref name="HoH-p14" /><ref name="PoL-p118-121" />

Believing the death of his parents would grant him peace, Cyric set out to make a life of his own. But the reality of surviving in the Realms quickly set in with Cyric. Within a [[tenday]] he found himself near-starving and stricken with fever at the border of the [[Dalelands]]. In a stroke of twisted fate, the dying child was captured by Zhent slavers and taken north to Zhentil Keep in the Moonsea.<ref name="HoH-p14" /> He eventually escaped from bondage, lived on the streets for a time, and eventually joined the local thieves' guild.<ref name=Cru-p368">{{Cite book/Crucible|368}}</ref>

===The Adventurer===
''Circa'' the {{YearlinkName|1350}},<ref name="PoL-p270">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|270}}</ref><ref group="note">While an exact date is not given, Cyric remembers the event taking place around 15 or 20 years before [[1368 DR]].</ref> Cyric attempted to steal the ''[[Ring of Winter (artifact)|Ring of Winter]]'' from a powerful tribe of [[frost giant]]s led by [[Thrym (frost giant)|Thrym]], but was unsuccessful and became trapped in their cave. The opportunity to flee only came when a band of adventurers, including the mercenary [[Kelemvor Lyonsbane]], attacked the frost giants' lair while also trying to find the ''Ring of Winter''. Kelemvor's entire party, save himself, was slain and only he and Cyric managed to escape.<ref name="Shadowdale-p30-31">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|30-31}}</ref> Once back in [[Arabel]] the two eventually joined [[Adon]], a young cleric of Sune, and they took work from the local city watch to uncover a traitor.<ref name="Shadowdale-p33-35">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|33-35}}</ref>

During the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, Cyric went to make offerings to an avatar of [[Tymora]] when she appeared in Arabel. Cyric was doubtful about the gods' arrival in the mortal realms and postulated that a divine being would have no use for his [[currency|gold]].<ref name="Shadowdale-p38">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|38}}</ref> Some time later, Cyric, Kelemvor, and Adon met [[Caitlan Moonsong]],<ref name="HL-p7" /> a mysterious young girl. Caitlan beseeched the trio to help her on a quest to free the now-mortal Mystra, who had been imprisoned in [[Castle Kilgrave]] by the avatar of the god Bane.<ref name="Shadowdale-p46-47">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|46-47}}</ref> They were soon joined by Midnight,<ref name="Shadowdale-p54-64">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|54-64}}</ref> a charming adventuring [[magic-user]] to whom Cyric felt an immediate connection.<ref name="Shadowdale-p158-159">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|158-159}}</ref> 

[[File:Adventurers Shadowdale.png|thumb|left|300px|''Kelemvor, Midnight, Adon, and Cyric standing before Castle Kilgrave.'']]
The group freed Mystra from the clutches of Bane on [[Midsummer]], and accompanied her to the [[Celestial Stairway]], where she confronted still-divine [[Helm]], the God of Guardians. Despite Mystra's insistence that she deliver to Ao the message that Bane and Myrkul had stolen the ''Tablets of Fate'', Helm would not let her re-enter the planes. The avatars of the two gods fought a brief but terrible battle, one that excited Cyric's bloodlust like never before, and Helm slew Mystra on the steps of the Stairway.<ref name="Shadowdale-p138-151">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|138-151}}</ref>

After passing through [[Tilverton]],<ref name="Shadowdale-p197-203">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|197-203}}</ref> the group journeyed eastward to seek out [[Elminster]] in [[Shadowdale]].<ref name="Shadowdale-p225-227">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|225-227}}</ref> While passing through the [[Spiderhaunt Wood]] they encountered the [[Company of Dawn]] adventuring band,<ref name="Shadowdale-p231-233">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|231-233}}</ref> and Cyric uncharacteristically became fast friends with a young, naïve thief named [[Brion]]. Their connection was tragically cut short however, when [[giant spider]]s attacked the two adventuring groups and Brion was violently killed before Cyric's eyes.<ref name="Shadowdale-p244-247">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|244-247}}</ref> Cyric was clearly traumatized by the experience, but did not speak of it to others.<ref name="Shadowdale-p255">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|255}}</ref>

===The Hero===
{{Quote|You wish glory? You wish to lay down your worthless lives? Alright. But will you seek it at the cost of their lives? Leave this place and who will protect them?...Stand with me and you stand with Shadowdale!|Cyric to the Dalesfolk during the [[Second battle of Shadowdale]], [[1358 DR]].<ref name="Shadowdale-p289-291" />}}
The troupe arrived at Shadowdale on the eve of a Zhentilar invasion. Seeing the Dalesman confronted with overwhelming odds, Cyric's old mentor Marek suggested they leave together and defect to the Zhents' cause. Marek claimed they were both born for a life of murder and theft. Cyric refused, insisting that he would fight for his honor among his new friends. Marek forsook his friendship with the younger thief and walked away from his pupil. Cyric stabbed Marek in the back and justified his murder by claiming his former teacher was a Zhent spy.<ref name="Shadowdale-p271-275">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|271-275}}</ref>

[[File:Ashaba Bridge.png|thumb|right|300px|''Riders crossing the bridge over the Ashaba.'']]
Cyric and the rest of the adventuring party joined in the defense of Shadowdale as the Zhents marched down from the north. He demonstrated great bravery when he confronted a group of Dalesmen abandoning their duties, and convinced them to help the town's refugees leave for safety by means of the [[Ashaba River]].<ref name="Shadowdale-p289-291">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|289-291}}</ref> During the [[Second battle of Shadowdale|battle]], Cyric led the ambush against the Zhentilar cavalry as the mounted soldiers crossed the [[Ashaba bridge]], and even scored a direct bow shot against the Zhent commander and [[Banite]] priest [[Fzoul Chembryl]].<ref name="Shadowdale-p314-318">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|314-318}}</ref> He was celebrated as a champion of the town by many of the local Dalesfolk.<ref name="Tantras-p6">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|6}}</ref>

Unfortunately, Cyric had little time to bask in praise as Midnight and Adon were accused of killing Elminster in a magical explosion in the [[Twisted Tower of Ashaba|Twisted Tower]] during the battle.<ref name="Tantras-p10">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|10}}</ref> The pair were brought before Lord [[Mourngrym Amcathra]] for judgement and, after a brief trial, were sentenced to death the following day. Cyric had to suppress his instincts to immediately murder Lord Mourngrym,<ref name="Tantras-p28-30">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|28-30}}</ref> but rather set out that night to free his companions from captivity. Overcome by indifference and bloodlust, Cyric silently slaughtered many unsuspecting guardsmen as he 'rescued' Midnight and the near-catatonic Adon.<ref name="HL-p7">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|7}}</ref><ref name="Tantras-p49-50">{{Cite book/Tantra/MMP (2003)s|49-50}}</ref> In the dead of night, the trio set out east down the Ashaba ''en route'' to Tantras, where one of the ''Tablets of Fate'' was rumored to lay in wait.<ref name="Tantras-p42-47">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|42-47}}</ref>

===The Traitor===
During their travel downriver, Cyric's cynicism and dark nature took over. He tormented the troubled Adon and tried to convince Midnight to abandon any hope of reuniting with Kelemvor. By this time, Cyric saw Midnight and the arcane power she demonstrated in the Twisted Tower as his means to acquire the ''Tablets of Fate'' for himself.<ref name="Tantras-p54-64">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|54-64}}</ref> After some time the trio came to [[Blackfeather Bridge (bridge)|Blackfeather Bridge]], atop which stood Kelemvor and a group of Dalesman charged with their apprehension. Cyric panicked, capsized their skiff and plummeted into the waters of the Ashaba.<ref name="Tantras-p105-107">{{Cite book/Tantra/MMP (2003)s|105-107}}</ref> He was recovered by the Dalesmen after a short time and managed to lie and deceive his way into being taken captive rather than immediately killed for his grievous crimes in Shadowdale.<ref name="Tantras-p129-132">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|129-132}}</ref>

Cyric's time as a prisoner was short-lived, as the Dalesmen were soon stopped by a superiorly outfitted band of Zhentilar soldiers, the Company of the Scorpions. A fight broke out between the two groups and the Zhent company emerged victorious. Cyric expertly maneuvered the subsequent conversation to his advantage and secured himself a spot within the company by killing the last remaining Dalesman in a twisted game.<ref name="Tantras-p134-147">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|134-147}}</ref> He traveled with the Zhents across the eastern Dalelands for some time,<ref name="HL-p7" /> learned more about their history and hierarchy<ref name="Tantras-p189-196">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|189-196}}</ref> and eventually took over the Company of the Scorpions for his own.<ref name="Tantras-p203-204">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|203-204}}</ref>

In a short time, Cyric and his company were granted audience with Bane in Scardale. In an ironic twist, Bane's avatar remained in the body of Fzoul Chembryl, the Zhent priest Cyric had wounded during the Battle of Shadowdale.<ref name="Tantras-p220-225">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|220-225}}</ref> Cyric offered nearly all he knew about his former companions and their quest for the ''Tablets of Fate'' to the Dark Lord, but held back offering freely Midnight's true name. Bane tasked Cyric and the assassin [[Durrock]] with killing Kelemvor in Tantras, and apprehending Midnight,<ref name="Tantras-p220-225">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|220-225}}</ref> but unfortunately they proved unsuccessful.<ref name="Tantras-p267-271">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|267-271}}</ref> The Company of the Scorpions and their leader were designated as Bane's personal guard,<ref name="Tantras-p292-294">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|292-294}}</ref> protecting him as he received the benefits of a [[Myrkulyte]] ritual that cost the lives of [[Bhaal]]'s assassins across [[Faerûn]].<ref name="Tantras-p299-301">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|299-301}}</ref>

[[File:Bane v Torm.png|thumb|right|300px|''The battle of the gods Bane and Torm.'']]
Bane took a new avatar in the form of a towering [[obsidian]] colossus and marched across the [[Dragon Reach]] to Tantras with a fleet of Zhent ships—among them ''[[The Argent]]'' that passaged Cyric and his company—following closely behind.<ref name="Tantras-p308-309">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|308-309}}</ref> The colossal Bane was met in battle by the equally mighty avatar of Torm, and the two gods battled until each destroyed the other's mortal form.<ref name="Tantras-p326-329">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|326-329}}</ref> Sensing his moment to once again gain the upper hand, Cyric ordered his ship away from the dueling gods and avoided the destructive magic Midnight and recently returned Elminster delivered unto Bane's army. The ''Argent'' then sailed west in pursuit of Midnight, who had reunited with Kelemvor and Adon.<ref name="Tantras-p337-338">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|337-338}}</ref> Despite what was written in a [[Cyrinishad|certain book]] that detailed Cyric's life,<ref name="PoL-p283-297" /> he had no hand in the god's death but merely survived it by means of treachery.<ref name="Tantras-p337-338" />

Cyric and the Company of the Scorpions continued in pursuit of Kelemvor, Midnight, and Adon over the following [[tenday]]s,<ref name="Waterdeep-p12">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|12}}</ref> determined to retrieve the tablet they'd recovered from Tantras.<ref name="Waterdeep-p28-31" /> During that time, the company slaughtered the [[halfling]] village of [[Black Oaks]]<ref name="Waterdeep-p43">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|43}}</ref><ref name="W.Adv-p8">{{Cite book/Waterdeep (adventure)|8}}</ref> and Cyric obtained ''Godsbane'', an intelligent blade inhabited by the god Mask<ref name="F&A-p51-52">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|51-52}}</ref> that guided him to give in to his murderous instincts.<ref name="Waterdeep-p95-97">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|95-97}}</ref>

===The Godslayer===
[[File:Cyric, Sneakabout, and Godsbane.jpg|thumb|right|300px|''Cyric and Sneakabout battle to the death for Godsbane in Yellow Snake Pass.'']]
Having established themselves in the [[Haunted Halls]] of Cormyr, Cyric's company abandoned all ties to the Zhentarim and dedicated their lives to his quest for the ''Tablets of Fate''. During this time, horrific murders appeared across the Heartlands, with all signs pointing to the avatar of Bhaal, the Lord of Murder. Unable to stay in one locale for too long,<ref name="Waterdeep-p24-37">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|24-37}}</ref> they continued tracking Midnight and the others until they found refuge in the fortress of [[High Horn (hold)|High Horn]].<ref name="Waterdeep-p64">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|64}}</ref> When the adventurers left under Cormyrean escort<ref name="Waterdeep-p102">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|102}}</ref> west through [[Yellow Snake Pass]], Cyric saw his moment of opportunity to act.<ref name="Waterdeep-p109-110">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|109-110}}</ref> As Cyric as snuck upon their camp, Kelemvor took notice and confronted him, with their former fellow companions, the Cormyrean soldiers, and Cyric's own company looking on. Despite Midnight's protests—and lingering hope Cyric would rejoin their group—Cyric and Kelemvor fought a vicious battle. When Kelemvor took the upper hand and held Cyric at the mercy of his blade, Cyric took advantage of Mystra's sympathy, got out from Kelemvor's grasp, and murdered their traveling companion [[Sneakabout]], before fleeing into the night.<ref name="Waterdeep-p114-123">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|114-123}}</ref>

[[File:Cyric_killing_Bhaal.png|thumb|left|300px|''Cyric raising ''Godsbane'' to slay the avatar of Bhaal...'']]
Distraught after witnessing Cyric's true cruelty, Midnight went off to confront him alone,<ref name="Waterdeep-p146-147">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|146-147}}</ref> but was kidnapped by Bhaal in his most recently claimed avatar.<ref name="Waterdeep-p153">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|153}}</ref> The god of murder confided that only she held the power that could best Helm—despite Mystra's previous failure—and recover the second tablet from the Realm of the Dead.<ref name="Waterdeep-p158-159">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|158-159}}</ref> Putting their past aside for a moment, Kelemvor, Adon, and Cyric agreed to work together to rescue Midnight from Bhaal and rode off to [[Boareskyr Bridge]]. Fighting alongside each other once again, the former companions wore Bhaal down and Cyric delivered a final killing blow through Bhaal's chest.<ref name="HL-p7" /><ref name="FRA-p11">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|11}}</ref><ref name="FRCS3e-p228">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|228}}</ref> A great blast of white energy engulfed the bridge along with everyone atop it.<ref name="Waterdeep-p170-182">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|170-182}}</ref>

[[File:Bhaal's death.png|thumb|right|300px|''...and the resulting explosion that enveloped Boareskyr Bridge.'']]
Cyric awoke to hear his former companions discussing what should be done with him. Ultimately they decided to leave him to his fate and continue on to [[Dragonspear Castle]]. Most importantly to Cyric, he retained possession of ''Godsbane''.<ref name="Waterdeep-p186-190">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|186-190}}</ref> Cyric stalked after the trio as they stole into the ruined castle, struck out at them as they descended into its basement, and—while aiming for Kelemvor—killed Adon with a single shot from his bow. He was turned away by Mystra's magic,<ref name="Waterdeep-p207-212">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|207-212}}</ref> and forced to flee west from the castle towards [[Daggerford]].<ref name="Waterdeep-p240-244">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|240-244}}</ref>

Cyric bided his time and tracked his companions to the city of [[Waterdeep]] in mid–[[Marpenoth]]. He infiltrated the city's [[Waterdeep/Sewers|sewer system]], and shadowed the avatar of Myrkul to the entrance beneath [[Blackstaff Tower]], where the God of the Dead deposited one of the tablets.<ref name="Waterdeep-p290-292">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|290-292}}</ref> He ascended the tower and found Elminster, Midnight, Kelemvor, and a resurrected Adon on the roof, all in possession of both ''Tablets of Fate''.<ref name="Waterdeep-p310">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|310}}</ref> As Myrkul's army of the dead descended upon Waterdeep, Cyric gravely wounded Midnight, slew Kelemvor, and took the ''tablets''<ref name="HL-p7" /><ref name="Waterdeep-p314-321">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|314-321}}</ref> to the Celestial Stairway atop [[Mount Waterdeep]]. There Cyric presented himself before Lord Ao, and pled his case to the overgod. He was granted godhood, but could only use it to serve both Ao and his newfound faithful throughout the Realms. Cyric would never again feel any satisfaction or joy, and would forever have to contend with Midnight, the restored incarnation of Mystra and new Goddess of Magic.<ref name="HL-p8">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|8}}</ref><ref name="Waterdeep-p328-335">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|328-335}}</ref>

===The God===
{{Quote|Cyric was a mistake, an exception that will not be permitted a second time.|Ao's [[solar]], to [[Amelyssan]] the Blackhearted.<ref name="BGII.ToB-chp21">{{Cite book/Baldur's Gate II: Throne of Bhaal||chp=21}}</ref>}}
[[File:Cyric novel cover.jpg|thumb|right|250px|''Cyric as a god''.]]
On [[Marpenoth 15]] in 1358 DR, Cyric ascended to godhood,<ref name="F&A-p30">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|30}}</ref> taking on the portfolios of Strife, Murder, and Death,<ref name="Waterdeep-p328-335" /> and took on nearly all the worshipers of Bane, Bhaal, and Myrkul.<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /><ref name="FRA-p17" /><ref name="FRCS2e.RTR-p46">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|46}}</ref>

Some months after his ascension,<ref name="GHotR-p144">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|144}}</ref> Cyric conspired with Mask to kill the goddess [[Leira]].<ref name="PoL-p63">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|63}}</ref> Cyric took over her portfolios of Deception<ref name="PoL-p59">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|59}}</ref> and Illusion,<ref name="F&A-p93">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|93}}</ref> and along with Mask, successfully withheld this information from the rest of the pantheon.<ref name="PoL-p177">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|177}}</ref>

Over the following decade, Cyric ordered his faithful to scour every corner of the Realms to uncover the soul of Kelemvor Lyonsbane.<ref name="PoL-p70">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|70}}</ref> Unbeknownst to Cyric, Kelemvor's eternal being was being hidden away by Mask.<ref name="PoL-p241">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|241}}</ref> At the same time, Cyric directed his clergy to scribe the ''[[Cyrinishad]]'', a tome that would magically convert anyone that read it or heard it read aloud to Cyric's faith.<ref name="HL-p138">{{Cite book/Hall of Heroes|138}}</ref><ref name="RZK.CB-p24" /><ref name="PoL-p41-48">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|41-48}}</ref> 

On [[Marpenoth 30]] of {{YearlinkName|1361}},<ref name="RZK.CB-p21" /><ref group="note">Page 21 of ''[[Forgotten Realms Campaign Set|Forgotten Realms Campaign Set: Running the Realms]]'' states this occurred in [[1360 DR]].</ref> Cyric directed his patriarch in Zhentil Keep to carry out an inquisition against the lingering faithful of Bane in Zhentil Keep, in a series of killings known as the [[Banedeath]]. Banites in the city were forced to convert to Cyricism to explain to the new God of Strife in person as to why they should be exempt of his worship.<ref name="F&A-p46">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|46}}</ref><ref name="GHotR-p146">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|146}}</ref>

[[File:Cyric Rinda.png|thumb|left|250px|''Cyric commissioning the ''Cyrinishad'' from the scribe Rinda.'']]
===The Cyrinishad===
{{Quote|It all depends on me, you see. Nothing is certain until I have beheld it and set it in place, until I have placed myself above it or below, before it or after...I am the One, the All, the Face Behind the Mask. I am the Everything.|Cyric, on the [[Night of Despair]], [[Nightal 28]], [[1368 DR]].<ref name="Cru-p1-2">{{Cite book/Crucible|1-2}}</ref>}}
In the {{YearlinkName|1368}}, Cyric attempted to create the ''Cyrinishad'' himself by means of his magic,<ref name="PoL-p41-48" /> but was cut off from the Weave by Mystra.<ref name="PoL-p64-67" /> Shortly thereafter Cyric was informed that the spirit of Kelemvor could be found somewhere within the City of Strife, its exact resting place obscured by magic.<ref name="PoL-p74-75">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|74-75}}</ref> He immediately ordered the city's denizens to scour the city.<ref name="PoL-p77" />  With the search underway, Cyric personally commissioned the Zhent scribe [[Rinda]], daughter of [[Bevis]] the Illuminator, to pen the ''Cyrinishad'' herself.<ref name="HL-p103">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|103}}</ref><ref name="PoL-p97-99">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|97-99}}</ref> He rapidly became frustrated with the fruitless search for Kelemvor, however, and ordered Jergal to completely annihilate one denizen within his realm each hour until it was uncovered.<ref name="PoL-p107-108" /> 

At the same time Rinda worked with Fzoul Chembryl<ref name="PoL-p201">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|201}}</ref> and the gods Mask and Oghma to craft a second book, ''[[The True Life of Cyric]]''. Used in place of the ''Cyrinishad'', ''The True Life'' would demonstrate to Cyric's faithful just how deranged and treacherous he actually was.<ref name="HL-p103" /><ref name="RZK.CB-p24">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|24}}</ref><ref name="PoL-p118-121" />

[[File:Inquisitor.png|thumb|right|250px|''Cyric's Inquisitor carries out the Second Banedeath.'']]
Cyric focused all his attention on the Mortal Realms and ignored his denizens, leading to growing unrest in the City of Strife.<ref name="PoL-p176-178" /> He then initiated an inquisition against the heretical Bane-worshipers in cities across the Moonsea.<ref name="PoL-p179">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|179}}</ref> Armed with powerful [[Gond]]ar suits of armor,<ref name="PoL-p74-75" /> Cyric's inquisitors began in Zhentil Keep,<ref name="RZK.CB-p24" /><ref name="PoL-p200-201">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|200-201}}</ref><ref name="PoL-p205">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|205}}</ref> and continued the slaughter throughout Darkhold, [[Mulmaster]], [[Teshwave]], [[Yûlash]], and the [[Citadel of the Raven]].<ref name="RZK.CB-p30">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|30}}</ref><ref name="PoL-p224-225">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|224-225}}</ref> This slaughter later came to be known as the [[Second Banedeath]].<ref name="GHotR-p148">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|148}}</ref>

After his success with the inquisition, Cyric manipulated the god Oghma into sharing the rites to a bloody [[Gargauth]]an ritual that could grant insight to the whereabouts of Kelemvor's soul.<ref name="PoL-p244-246">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|244-246}}</ref> He presented the rites before his patriarch [[Xeno Mirrormane]] in dramatic fashion and forced his faithful to prepare for its performance.<ref name="PoL-p276-278">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|276-278}}</ref> Cyric then raised an army of [[dragon]]s and frost giants from [[Thar]] to the north and directed them to march south in an invasion of Zhentil Keep,<ref name="PoL-p275-274">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|272-274}}</ref> with the intent of saving the city and emerging as its savior.<ref name="RZK.CB-p24" /> Finally, Cyric retrieved the final draft of the ''Cyrinishad'' from Rinda, and revealed that he was aware of her treachery and took the book for himself. He compelled Fzoul Chembryl to read from the tome and then murder the young scribe. Unbeknownst to Cyric, Mask had taken Fzoul's mortal form, faked Rinda's death, and together they were poised share ''The True Life of Cyric'' with all the god's faithful. However one complication remained:<ref name="PoL-p283-297" /> Mask's reading of the ''Cyrinishad'' lessened his divine power to that of a demigod, transferred to Cyric his dominion over intrigue,<ref name="F&A-p109" /> and left him believing Cyric was the one true god of the Realms.<ref name="F&A-p109">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|109}}</ref> The ''Cyrinishad'' worked on deities just as it would with mortals.<ref name="PoL-p283-297">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|283-297}}</ref>

Mystra garnered support from the Circle to stop Cyric's abuse of dark magic, turned his former inquisitors against him, and spurred full rebellion in the City of Strife.<ref name="RZK.CB-p24" /><ref name="PoL-p302">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|302}}</ref> Fzoul Chembryl completed the reading of ''The True Life of Cyric'' in Zhentil Keep,<ref name="HL-p103" /><ref name="PoL-p310-314">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|310-314}}</ref> inciting riots in the city<ref name="Cru-p1-2" /> just as Cyric's monstrous armies [[Fall of Zhentil Keep|broke though its northern walls]]<ref name="PoL-p316-318">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|316-318}}</ref> and ran rampant through its streets.<ref name="GHotR-p149">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|149}}</ref> Cyric returned to witness the denizens storming the Bone Castle.<ref name="PoL-p318-321">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|318-321}}</ref> While Cyric was weakened and his faith waned across the Realms, Mask revealed himself as ''Godsbane'' and professed his 'revelation' of Cyric's singular divinity.<ref name="Cru-p1-2" /> In response, Cyric broke his beloved weapon in two and greatly weakened Mask.<ref name="FRCS3e-p264">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|264}}</ref> Kelemvor arrived to lead the denizens to the throne room of the Bone Castle,<ref name="Cru-p1-2" /> ran Cyric through with the broken blade half of ''Godsbane'', and threw Cyric's diminished body to the Burning Men.<ref name="PoL-p347-357">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|347-357}}</ref> The City of Strife's denizens elevated Kelemvor as the new God of the Dead,<ref name="PoL-p366-370">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|366-370}}</ref> transferring a major part of Cyric's divine power to his most hated foe.<ref name="FRCS3e-p264" /><ref name="F&A-p85">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|85}}</ref>

===The Crucible===
{{Quote|As for Cyric, now he sits alone in his Shattered Keep, lost in delusions of grandeur and absolute power, leaving his church of Faerûn to grow ever more fragmented and weak... Cyric was the first to read the ''Cyrinishad''; his own lies drove him mad.|Excerpt from ''The True Life of Cyric''.<ref name="Cru-p109">{{Cite book/Crucible|109}}</ref><ref name="Cru-p249">{{Cite book/Crucible|249}}</ref>}}
[[File:Cyric the mad god.jpg|thumb|right|300px|''Cyric the Mad God.'']]
After his defeat, Cyric reappeared in the plane of Pandemonium where he constructed his new realm,<ref name="PoL-p374-376">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|374-376}}</ref> the [[Supreme Throne]].<ref name="F&A-p51-52" /> Having read from the ''Cyrinishad'' himself Cyric was driven entirely mad.<ref name="RZK.CB-p113-114" /> He believed himself superior to the entirety of the pantheon,<ref name="PoL-p374-376" /> with power that rivaled that of the overgod Ao.<ref name="Cru-p30-40" /> In truth he was left with greatly diminished divine power, and only held onto the portfolios of Intrigue, Murder, and Strife.<ref name="Cru-p26-29">{{Cite book/Crucible|26-29}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|1369}}, Cyric briefly appeared within the [[Pocket Plane]] that manifested from the divine realm of Bhaal. Cyric spoke with one of the last remaining [[Bhaalspawn]], [[Abdel Adrian]], to assess how much of a threat he posed by ascending as the God of Murder. Cyric appeared to be aware of the madness he held, but remained civil and did not stray from Ao's decree that he not interfere with the [[Bhaalspawn crisis]].<ref name="BGII.ToB" />

Over the next couple years Cyric abandoned his divine responsibilities and peace and serenity spread across much of the Realms. By the {{YearlinkName|1371}}, this became apparent to even the gods and [[Tempus]]' ire grew. The god of war was eventually convinced by Mask<ref name="Cru-p26-29" /> to formally accuse Cyric of neglecting his faithful and disrupting [[the Balance]]. Cyric was brought before the Circle of Twelve and Tyr officially charged him with being innocent by reason of insanity.<ref name="Cru-p30-40" /><ref name="GHotR-p151">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|151}}</ref> On the eve of the trial, Cyric commanded his loyal follower [[Malik el Sami]] to recover the lost ''Cyrinishad'' from [[Candlekeep]], so that he might read it before the other gods at the trial and inflict upon them the same delusion it had on Mask.<ref name="PoL-p283-297" /><ref name="Cru-p47-51">{{Cite book/Crucible|47-51}}</ref>

During the initial stages of Cyric's trial, the former God of the Dead turned attention to his accusers. He alleged that Mystra did not allow [[good|morally upstanding]] individuals to die by means of magic and that Kelemvor offered such great rewards to the virtuous after death that they no longer feared their own mortality.<ref name="Cru-p100-102">{{Cite book/Crucible|100-102}}</ref> Tyr decreed that the charges of incompetence and disrupting the Balance against Cyric would be linked to those put forth against Mystra and Kelemvor.<ref name="Cru-p140-141">{{Cite book/Crucible|140-141}}</ref>

In the city of [[Elversult]], Adon, now Mystra's new patriarch, was performing last rites when he became became possessed by a force appearing to be linked to Cyric.<ref name="Cru-p165-166">{{Cite book/Crucible|165-166}}</ref> In truth, it was Mask forcing Adon<ref name="Cru-p235-236">{{Cite book/Crucible|235-236}}</ref> to view Mystra as Cyric did, and the mortal priest was driven mad in the process.<ref name="Cru-p188-189">{{Cite book/Crucible|188-189}}</ref> Mask carried out this plot in an attempt to take back his portfolio from Cyric,<ref name="Cru-p215">{{Cite book/Crucible|215}}</ref> and compel Mystra to withhold magic from Cyric's followers.<ref name="Cru-p195">{{Cite book/Crucible|195}}</ref> Meanwhile, Malik el Sami continued on towards Zhentil Keep, determined to cure Cyric of his madness, by once again replacing the ''Cyrinishad'' with ''The True Life''.<ref name="Cru-p254">{{Cite book/Crucible|254}}</ref>

To prevent Mystra from interfering with Malik's quest, Cyric manipulated Mystra into attacking Mask, and she was temporarily interred within Helm's prison.<ref name="Cru-p291">{{Cite book/Crucible|291}}</ref> After Mystra was released, she visited Kelemvor in his newly formed [[Crystal Spire]] in the City of the Dead. Mystra came to witness the dramatic change that Kelemvor had undergone: Cyric's machinations had forced the new God of the Dead—who remained as her longtime lover—to disregard the morality of his subjects. While wholly dedicated to his new divine duties, Kelemvor became cold, indifferent, and passionless. He was no longer the man or god Mystra fell in love with.<ref name="Cru-p312-315">{{Cite book/Crucible|312-315}}</ref>

At the trial, each of the accused gods presented their cases before the Circle of Twelve. Kelemvor demonstrated his newfound commitment to fair and neutral judgement of the dead, and Mystra offered a similar revelation, that she would no longer unjustly wield access to the Weave to suit her own desires. When time came for Cyric to offer his defense—by having Malik read from the ''Cyrinishad''—some members of the Circle intervened and chaos broke out. Malik was allowed by Tyr to present his god's defense, and dutifully acted in Cyric's best interests by reading from ''The True Life''. The process was excruciating for Cyric but by the end of Malik's recitation, the Mad God had recovered his senses and offered his final defense: even when in the throes of madness he was the only one worthy to be the God of Strife. Through all his plotting and scheming Cyric had finally managed to strip Kelemvor and Midnight of their passions and destroy the love they shared. The same love that incited feelings of jealousy and rejection in Cyric,<ref name="Shadowdale-p161-162" /><ref name="Tantras-p54-64" /> and led to him break the vows of friendship in his quest for ultimate power,<ref name="Waterdeep-p28-31" /><ref name="HL-p7" /><ref name="Waterdeep-p314-321" /> was reduced to twin teardrops that swirled around his [[Cyric#Possessions|silver chalice]]. Cyric was found guilty and allowed to remain as the God of Strife, with Mystra herself casting the final vote in his favor.<ref name="GHotR-p151">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|151}}</ref><ref name="Cru-p352-374">{{Cite book/Crucible|352-374}}</ref>

Freed from his madness, Cyric and Malik pursued a plot to chronicle their time together and unite the fractured Cyricist faith.<ref name="Cru-p377-378">{{Cite book/Crucible|377-378}}</ref> This new tome that would document and glorify Cyric's feats would be entitled: ''[[An Honest and True Account of the Trial of Cyric the Mad, or How Our Dark Lord Saved Faerûn (Again)]]''. Cyric demanded that this book be published before a sect of [[Waterdhavian]] [[Oghmanyte]]s published their own accounting of the events.<ref name="DrM245.H&T-p57-64">{{Cite dragon/245/Honest and True|57-64}}</ref>

As Cyric's insanity led to his decline in power, Bane's immortal son Iyachtu Xvim was able to take back some of his father's lost portfolios from the Mad God, including Hatred and Tyranny.<ref name="F&A-p82">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|82}}</ref>

===The Prisoner===
{{Quote|The white queen is troubled by can't say why. The black queen hates the white and gives the assassin a black cloak. The assassin steals up on the white queen. She can't see him gliding through the shadows. The sword screams. The white queen falls. Her city falls.|[[Yaphyll]]'s prophecy about Mystra, Shar, and Cyric.<ref name="Undead-p7">{{Cite book/Undead|7}}</ref>}}
In the {{YearlinkName|1373}}, a new and more capable champion of Cyric named [[Zasian Menz]] arose to carry out his will throughout the Realms. Zasian managed to open a pathway to [[Celestia]] from the [[City of Brass]],<ref name="GP-p301-308">{{Cite book/The Gossamer Plain/MMP|301-308}}</ref> a plot that claimed twelve years of the priest's life.<ref name="'FS-p29">{{Cite book/The Fractured Sky|29}}</ref> In the years that followed, Cyric forged an alliance with the goddess [[Shar]], working together to weaken Mystra's hold over the Weave and lessen her influence in the Realms.<ref name="GHotR-p156">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|156}}</ref>

Over a decade later, in the {{YearlinkName|1384}}, Cyric unleashed his wrath on Helm by orchestrating his death at the hands of Tyr.<ref name="DrM381.CoT-p91">{{Cite dragon/381/Channel Divinity: Champions of Torm|91}}</ref>

The [[1385 DR|following year]], Cyric's priest Zasian freed the Sharran 'shadow mystic' [[Kashada]] from imprisonment in Celestia.<ref name="FS-p64-67">{{Cite book/The Fractured Sky|64-67}}</ref> Kashada was in fact an aspect of the goddess Shar, the Goddess of Night.<ref name="FS-p311">{{Cite book/The Fractured Sky|311}}</ref> Shar had manipulated Cyric's Zasian to help her supplant the Weave with her own [[Shadow Weave]].<ref name="FRCG-p50" /> Cyric himself accompanied Shar to the [[Hall of Petitions]],<ref name="FS-p288">{{Cite book/The Fractured Sky|288}}</ref> and Shar distracted the god Azuth long enough for Cyric to recover his arcane staff. Cyric used Azuth's ''[[Old Staff]]'' to finally murder Mystra, and exacted revenge upon his former companion and one-time friend.<ref name="FS-p278-282">{{Cite book/The Fractured Sky|278-282}}</ref> Mystra's death wrecked the bonds that held together the Weave, and brought about the Spellplague,<ref name="FRCG-p50">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|50}}</ref><ref name="SCAG-p17">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|17}}</ref> a calamitous event that was felt across the cosmos.<ref name="FRCG-p60">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|60}}</ref><ref name="GHotR-p159" /> Once again, Cyric broke the heart of someone dearest to Mystra, her former lover and longtime confidant Elminster.<ref name="DunM181-p39">{{Cite dungeon/181/Campaign Workshop: Hero Battle: Elminster|39}}</ref>

In retaliation for his murder of Mystra, [[Lathander]], Tyr, and Sune trapped Cyric in the Supreme Throne for 1000 years.<ref name="GHotR-p159">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|159}}</ref><ref name="CM-p113-114">{{Cite book/The Crystal Mountain/MMP|113-114}}</ref> It was believed by some that Cyric foresaw his own imprisonment and formulated a plot to forge a key that would grant his escape.<ref name="SL-chp24">{{Cite digital book/Spinner of Lies||chp=24}}</ref> This did not come to pass however, and Cyric's power continually waned over the next century. The worship of Bane flourished in its place,<ref name="EFR-p144">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|144}}</ref> as the faith of the Black Lord became the state religion in the realm of [[Thay]].<ref name="Undead-p198-206">{{Cite book/Undead|198-206}}</ref><ref name="FRCG-p181">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|181}}</ref>

Cyric would not appear in the mortal Realms for over a century, and even then only as a mere manifestation. In the {{YearlinkName|1486}}, during the world-shaping [[Second Sundering]], Cyric ordered his long-faithful servant and newly designated chosen Malik to foil Shar's faithful from securing the ''[[Eye of Gruumsh (relic)|Eye of Gruumsh]]'' to initiate the [[Cycle of Night]] on Toril. While Malik and his companions—chosen of Helm, Sune, and [[Siamorphe]]—ventured through the [[Chondalwood]], Cyric manifested before Malik as the visage of a skull in a dead tree. The imprisoned god ordered Malik to slay his fellows at their exact moment of triumph.<ref name="Sent-p193-197">{{Cite book/The Sentinel/Hardcover|193-197}}</ref>

==Rumors & Legends==
A small group of scholars of the Realms theorized that Ao possessed advanced knowledge of Cyric's ascension to godhood.<ref name="RZK.CB-p21">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|21}}</ref>

The play entitled ''[[The Curious Tale of Wisteria Vale (play)|The Curious Tale of Wisteria Vale]]'' supposed there was a very old crystal touched by Cyric that could turn others to commit [[evil]] deeds.<ref name="CM.CTWV-p148">{{Cite book/Candlekeep Mysteries/The Curious Tale of Wisteria Vale|148}}</ref>

It was speculated that the divine Cyric finally acquired the ''Ring of Winter'' he originally sought after in Thar as a mortal.<ref name="HL-p20">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|20}}</ref>

Though there was no proof, rumors circulated among the gods that Cyric killed [[Hoar]],<ref name="FRCS2e.RTR-p58">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|58}}</ref> claimed a portion of [[Waukeen]]'s divine power following her disappearance in the Time of Troubles,<ref name="FRA-p114">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|114}}</ref> and played a part the slaying of Helm by Tyr decades later.<ref name="GHotR-p159" /> The allegation regarding Waukeen's divinity was false,<ref name="F&A-p178">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|178}}</ref> whereas Cyric's involvement in Helms's death was confirmed as truth.<ref name="DrM381.CoT-p91" />

After Cyric's imprisonment, it was alleged that there were seven black [[iron]] keys that could be used to free him from the shackles of the Supreme Throne. Such keys fetched prices upwards of 50,000 [[gp]].<ref name="FRCG-p45,282" />

According to the doctrine of the [[Tormtar]] church, the God of Duty would sacrifice himself in a ultimate battle between the gods, one that would save the Realms from ever again falling victim to Cyric's influence.<ref name="F&A-p163">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|163}}</ref>
