'''Lathander''' ({{Pronounce|lah|THÆN|der|audio=Lathander.ogg|refs=<ref name="FRCS-244" /><ref name="FP-37-38" />}}) was a [[Faerûnian pantheon|Faerûnian]] [[greater deity|greater]] [[god]] with a vast portfolio including birth, renewal, spring and youth, as well as athletics, self-perfection, vitality and creativity. '''The Morninglord''' was symbolized by the rising sun, but rather than being the god of the sun itself, he was the god of dawn, which represented the potential of a new day. A god of hope and beginnings, Lathander's name was invoked at the start of new endeavors, whether sealing a new deal, or setting out on a new journey.<ref name="CotR-11" /><ref name="RtR-46-47" /><ref name="FA-90-92" /><ref name="FRCS-234-235" /><ref name="FP-37-38" /><ref name="PHB5e-p294" /><ref name="SCAG5e-p32" /><ref name="SCAG p35">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|35}}</ref>

Some saw the '''Commander of Creativity''' as the [[neutral good]] [[aspect]] of [[Amaunator]]<ref name="DFR">{{Cite dragon/Domains in Eberron and the Forgotten Realms|37}}</ref> but the two were considered separate deities again after the [[Second Sundering]].<ref name="SCAG p32">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|32}}</ref>

==Description==
During his numerous visits to the [[Great Mother's Garden]], Lathander appeared as a giant of a man, to match the size of his lover, [[Chauntea]]. His visage radiated bright shine of the morning sun and his hair burned with flaming orange-red fire. Lathander appeared as a handsome young slender muscled [[human]] man. His preferred clothes were a set of colorful [[robe]]s of reds, pinks, and yellows. A red and [[gold]]en [[sash]] embraced the god's waist. He wore golden [[sandal]]s and left scorch marks everywhere his feet touched the ground. Lathander often floated and liked to materialize in a glorious spectacle of dawn sun beams. His radiant face darkened akin to solar eclipse when the god was angered or upset.<ref name="tymore5-6">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|5-7|1}}</ref>

==Personality==
Despite being among the oldest of [[Faerun]]'s gods, Lathander nonetheless retained the cheery hopefulness of youth.<ref name="FP-37-38" /> His was an eternal optimist,<ref name="FA-90-92" /> with a constant willingness to focus on hopes for the future rather than wallow in the failures of the present.<ref name="FP-37-38" /> He was a doggedly determined god<ref name="FA-90-92" /> who encouraged proactive altruism and constant reevaluation of the old ways.<ref name="FP-37-38" /> Exuberant and friendly,<ref name="FA-90-92" /><ref name="FP-37-38" /> his interests laid in vibrant life (regarding both birth and nature),<ref name="FA-90-92" /> and conversely urged the destruction of the corrupted mockeries of life that he saw the [[undead]] as.<ref name="FP-37-38" />

Though liked for his many positive qualities, Lathander also had a reputation for displaying the flaws common to the young, such as zealotry, vanity, and excess.<ref name="FA-90-92" /> Though enthusiastically altruistic and only slightly vain, it was said by his critics that his aggressive do-gooder mentality often prevented him from taking more sensible courses of action. His headstrong conceit could blind him to the consequences of his actions as in his idealistic crusades he simply attacked directly and hoped for the best, ignoring the ramifications.<ref name="FP-37-38" />

==Relationships==
Lathander and Chauntea had been romantically connected for centuries and saw each other as kindred spirits.<ref name="tymore9">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|5-6|1}}</ref> His allies included [[Gond]], [[Tymora]], [[Tyr]], [[Torm]], [[Ilmater]], [[Sune]], [[Selûne]], [[Oghma]], [[Kelemvor]], and [[Mielikki]]. His foes were [[Cyric]], [[Talos]], and [[Shar]]. [[Helm]] held a great deal of resentment for Lathander, as the latter indirectly caused the death of Helm's lover, [[Murdane]].<ref name="FaP-38" />

Prior to Chauntea, Lathander's paramour was [[Tyche]], an ancient goddess of fortune who later split into Tymora and [[Beshaba]]. Tyche and Lathander once observed the birth of a new universe for an entire year while lounging on a [[Kindori|space whale]]'s back. The couple watched as a [[crystal sphere]] engorged and then split apart, new stars lit inside them. The Morninglord held that memory dear.<ref name="tymore5-6" />

==Worshipers==
[[File:Lathander symbol.jpg|thumb|left|The holy symbol of Lathander.]][[File:Lathander Symbol-5e.png |thumb|right|The holy symbol on a flag.]]
{{Main|Church of Lathander}}
{{Quote|I shall let all who dwell in dark feel your holy dawn, Morninglord. Hear my prayer.|A basic prayer to Lathander.<ref name="BG3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

Novices in the Lathanderian faith were called the Awakened, while [[clerics]] were known as [[Dawnbringer]]s. The full priests took a new name in his service when they were ready to signify that Lathander personally recognized and accepted them. This new name could either be used instead of their old name or simply used only when addressing other Dawnbringers and when in solitary prayer. Titles used by Dawnbringers (in ascending order) were: Dawngreeter, Dawnlord, High Dawnlord, Dawnmaster, Morninglord, High Morninglord, Mornmaster, High Mornmaster, and Sunrise Lord.<ref name="FA-91">{{Cite book/Faiths and Avatars|91}}</ref> [[Specialty priest]]s were also known as [[Morninglord of Lathander|morninglord]]s.<ref name="FA-92" /> All followers were required to be of neutral to good alignment.<ref name="FA-90-92" />

All of Lathander's clergy respected art, liberty, nature, and culture; promoted betterment of oneself; and strove to bring hope to their followers and others. Many of these followers worked in various creative arts. They were intolerant of evil, especially [[undead]] and inaction that caused evil to prosper.<ref name="FaP-38" /> Most ceremonies of Lathander were held at dawn and actions and contracts agreed to at sunrise were said to be blessed by him. Funerals, among his followers, were held at dusk, and followed by a wake that lasted until dawn.<ref name="FA-90-92" />

==History==
Lathander was the central figure in the [[Dawn Cataclysm]], an ill-fated attempt by the Morninglord to reshape the Faerûnian pantheon in his own image. Several deities were killed in the events that followed, including the goddess [[Murdane]], a death for which [[Helm]] never forgave Lathander. The Morninglord believed that his efforts went so wrong because of the interference of agents of [[Shar]], and secretly began working on a second attempt at reforming the pantheon.<ref name="FaP-38">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|38}}</ref>

The biggest loss for Lathander, however was [[Tyche]], his lover at the time. She refused to partake in Lathander's grab for power and left him to wander the Realms, leaving him a kiss of misfortune as a parting gift. It was during that time she came across a beautiful [[rose]] high in the mountains of Toril. The flower was so perfect, the goddess thought it to be an apology from her misguided consort. In reality, the flower was a ''[[seed of Moander]]''. Tyche tucked the rose in her hair, from whence the flower corrupted and rotted her from the inside. To save her, [[Selûne]] was forced to split the goddess of luck, birthing [[Tymora]] and [[Beshaba]].<ref name="TL-p91-92">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|91-92}}</ref> Neither of two women were to Lathander's liking and he missed his lost lover for centuries to come.<ref name="TL-p7">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|7}}</ref>

In [[916 DR]],<ref name="GHotR-p109">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|109}}</ref> Lathander, enraged with the unchanging, tyrannical, undead-centric philosophy of the [[Cult of the Dragon]], sent an avatar to Cormanthyr to personally do battle with [[Sammaster]] during a [[Harper]] raid. He destroyed Sammaster but was wounded in the battle.<ref name="VGttD-p231">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the Dalelands|231}}</ref>

In [[1345 DR]], Lathander's worshipers, along with those of [[Selûne]], [[Shar]], and [[Tempus]] were involved in religious unrest, culminating in the [[Night of Temple Fires]].<ref name="CoS.W-p11">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|11}}</ref>

In [[1358 DR]], Lathander met the [[Heroes of Waterdeep]] after they restored one of his sacred statues in [[Myth Drannor]]. He requested that they rid his [[Temple of Lathander (Myth Drannor)|temple]] there of the [[Dark God]] and offered them help in their quest. During the event in which the adventurers recovered the [[Morning Light]], Lathander bestowed to them the magic sword ''[[Dhauzimmer]]''.<ref name="EotB3">{{Cite game/Eye of the Beholder III: Assault on Myth Drannor}}</ref>

Lathander, however, returned during the events of [[Second Sundering]] in [[1486 DR]], thanks to the effort of his [[Chosen]] and prophet [[Stedd Whitehorn]].<ref name="TR">{{Cite book/The Reaver}}</ref> However, by the year [[1491 DR]], Amaunator and Lathander were once again worshiped as separate beings.<ref>{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|13,21,24,32}}</ref> It is unknown if they were actually separate beings by this time or if Lathander was simply granting spells to the followers of Amaunator.{{Speculation}}
