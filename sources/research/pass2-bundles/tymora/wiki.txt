'''Tymora''' ({{Pronounce|ty|MOR|ah|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-p15"/><ref name="FP3e-p77">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|77}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|253}}</ref>}}) was the [[god]]dess of good luck in the [[Faerûnian pantheon]] and the second incarnation of the goddess of [[luck]] after her predecessor [[Tyche]] was split into her and [[Beshaba]], goddess of bad luck.<ref name="F&A-p44"/><ref name="SCAG-p40"/> In the 14{{th}} century [[DR]], Tymora held the [[portfolio]]s of good fortune, skill, victory, and [[adventurer]]s.<ref name="FRCS-235"/> Commonly known as '''Lady Luck''', Tymora shone upon those who took risks and blessed those who dealt harshly with the followers of Beshaba.<ref name="MiBG.CG-p49">{{Cite book/Murder in Baldur's Gate/Campaign Guide|49}}</ref>

==Description==
Before the [[Time of Troubles]], Tymora used to manifest in her [[avatar]] form, as a crafty-faced, boyish, barefoot tomboy with brunette hair.<ref name="F&A-166"/><ref name="FRCS2e-p52"/><ref name="DR365-p24"/><ref name="FPp79"/><ref name="DD-p167">{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|167}}</ref> In [[1368 DR]], she still appeared as a slender short-haired woman, but dressed in a short white [[satin]] gold-trimmed [[tunic]] and high brown [[leather]] [[boot]]s. Her head was adorned with a delicate silver coronet that glistened in the light.<ref name="ty-p80">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|80}}</ref> However, after surviving a foiled plot to steal her powers and merge with her sister, she changed her form into a tall, thin, almost boyish, yet graceful maiden with a regal face, blue-black eyes and long, flowing, unbound platinum blond or silver hair adorned with a modest gold crown.<ref name="F&A-166"/><ref name="FaP-78"/><ref name="Pol71"/><ref name="FPp79"/><ref name="FRCS2e-p52"/><ref name="HoF-p126" />

By [[1489 DR]], some [[tiefling]] followers of Tymora claimed to have received visions of her in their own image, describing her as the "dark, devilish lady of fortune". The tieflings argued that these visions meant the heart and soul meant more to the gods than appearance and bloodline.<ref name="SCAG-p119">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|119}}</ref>

[[Halfling]]s of the realms portrayed Lady Luck as a female halfling rogue.<ref name="FRCS2e-p62">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|62}}</ref>

When Tymora appeared, which was rare, she liked to take a form of a fit female, with an impish nose and long flowing white hair with a capricious lock that periodically danced on her forehead. She usually materialized barefoot and wore a blue robe with silver trim. She liked to appear as a member of whatever race the observer belonged to, which meant that each person could simultaneously see her as a member of their own race.<ref name="DD&D-p15"/><ref name="FPp79"/><ref name="FPp78"/><ref name="F&P3e-p138-139"/>

==Personality==
[[File:Brandobaris and tymora.png|thumb|''Tymora's [[halfling]] aspect and her ally [[Brandobaris]], The Irrepressible Scamp.'']]
Tymora was a cheerful and curious goddess, who inherited [[Tyche]]'s grace and kindness, when the latter was split apart. She did not have any malice or vengeance in her, since both of these emotions were inherited by [[Beshaba]]. She remained jovial even in the darkest times, preferring mischief and ironic justice to open violence.<ref name="FRCS-235"/><ref name="F&A-166"/><ref name="Dra388"/> Tymora was jaunty and high-spirited, but not rude or haughty. In battle she sang, whooped, or emitted beast-calls with gusto.<ref name="Pol71"/> Tymora was well-known for her antics over the centuries. She was much more adventurous and less serious than any of her fellow [[Deity|deities]].<ref name="TNTS">{{Cite web/The Night Tymora Sneezed}}</ref><ref name="Dra388"/><ref name="VGttSC,p37-38">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the Sword Coast|37-38}}</ref>

Just like Tyche, Tymora used to romance many deities, and just like her, she would cut-short their relationships, whenever she was bored of them or her attention was drawn to something or someone else. Moreover, scholars from the [[Outer Planes]] had a theory, that Tymora might have been Tyche from the beginning. Their theory suggested that since no divine corpse was found in the [[Astral Plane]], Tyche didn't die, as the Faerûnians believed. According to this theory, Tyche learned to manifest as the two twin goddesses instead of dying. However, it was not known if Tyche was the dominant personality in each of the two goddesses, or if she had faded away and was just an unconscious source of power for them.<ref name="OHG">{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|127}}</ref>

Unlike her sister Beshaba, Tymora was generous with sharing her godly powers. She empowered magic items, granted powers of luck to adventurers, created [[power key]]s for her worshipers who embarked on planar adventures, and lent her power to allied gods and their clerics.<ref name="FD&D-p27">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|27}}</ref> This meant Tymora had less divine power reserved while Beshaba hoarded her divinity.<ref name="ty-148">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|148}}</ref>

Despite her daring personality, Tymora did not neglect caution. She refused to intervene in the domains of other deities, as evidenced by her actions in [[Zhentil Keep]], where she made a conscious decision not to extend influence beyond her [[Tymora's Holy Hall of Good Fortune and Casino|shrine]], sensing the presence of [[Bane]]'s son, [[Iyachtu Xvim]], and being aware of [[Cyric]]'s hold on the city.<ref name="RoZK2e.CB-p87">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|87}}</ref>

Lady Luck was very fond of her followers, secretly aiding them in times of extreme danger<ref name="tRoUCGtU-p2">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Campaign Guide to Undermountain|105}}</ref> or continuing to support them even after they stopped worshiping her, fondly remembering their exploits.<ref name="VGtW-229">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|229}}</ref> Nevertheless, creatures faithful to Tymora were looked upon with disfavor if they continually tried to rely directly on the goddess, as this was seen as a lack of trust in her luck.<ref name="F&A-p168">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|168}}</ref>

==Powers==
When Tymora manifested as an avatar her voice could carry for more than a 100 miles. Any game based on chance would be won by ridiculously improbable odds if played within a hundred yards of her avatar. She was capable of sensing acts of good fortune as soon as they happened. Beings of her faith would also receive small [[magic resistance]].<ref name="F&A-167"/> She could also change between bird and human forms at will. The shift required roughly 6 seconds.<ref name="Pol71"/><ref name="FPp79"/>

Tymora was immune to all [[illusion]]s, [[charm]] [[spell]]s, priest spells from [[numbers sphere]]s, [[thought sphere]]s, [[chaos sphere]]s, [[law sphere]]s, and [[time sphere]]s. Any [[wild magic]] [[wizard]] spells cast near Tymora would twist and aid her or her allies.<ref name="F&A-167"/> If Tymora was unaffected by a magical spell, the spell was turned back on the caster with full effect. Any form of spells or powers that were used to control her will or dominate her mind were instantly nullified.<ref name="F&A-167"/><ref name="Pol71">{{Cite polyhedron/71/The Everwinking Eye: Beshaba, Tymora, and Xvim|20–21}}</ref>

Tymora would [[Chosen|choose]] among those who lived by her principles and infuse them with her extraordinary luck. Some, however, would remain unaware that they were [[chosen]], mistaking her blessing for simple good fortune.<ref name="HoF-p126" />

==Possessions==
Tymora used to wield ''+5 silver [[luck blade]] [[longsword]]'' named ''[[Silver Tear]]'', that would appear as a silver tear from her eye and then be reshaped in midair into a weapon whenever the goddess desired.<ref name="F&A-166"/><ref name="FPp79">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|79}}</ref><ref name="FPp78">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|78}}</ref> It was not, however, her favored weapon; that honor went to the coins that she used as ''+5 [[distance]] [[speed]] [[shuriken]]s''.<ref name="FPp79"/>

===Associated Items===
* ''[[Stone of Tymora]]'': An artifact sacred to the goddess of good fortune. When the stone was in close proximity to the creature of its own choosing, it would bind itself to their soul and allow them to perform feats of extraordinary luck. However, when it altered the luck of its wielder, somewhere another soul would experience an equal amount of bad luck.<ref name="TS-SOT">{{Cite book/The Stowaway/Paperback|284-285|30}}</ref>
* ''[[Tymora's cup]]'' was a ceremonial offering to the goddess of luck, located in [[Lady Luck Tavern]]. It was always prepared for her and filled to the brim, should she want to stop by for a drink. There were times when the goblet would suddenly and silently get enveloped in flames, and the wine inside would disappear. It was believed that during those times, Tymora herself drank with patrons of the tavern.<ref name="VGttSC,p38">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the Sword Coast|38}}</ref><ref name="SotSC-17-18">{{Cite book/Scourge of the Sword Coast|17-18}}</ref><ref name="N.GSF-p19-20">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/Daggerford|19-20}}</ref>
* ''[[Tear of Selûne]]'': An artifact created when Selûne shed a single crystalline tear after the death of Tyche, the goddess of luck. Some of the cosmic energy released when Tyche broke into Tymora and Beshaba was absorbed by the Tear as it fell from the heavens. Anyone who tried using its destructive potential had a small cumulative chance per use of splitting into two undead creatures of pure good and pure evil, each claiming to be the original and locked in eternal conflict. Some believe that the artifact would return to the heavens if Tymora and Beshaba merged to once more become Tyche.<ref name="Volo-ATM-p118-119">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to All Things Magical|118-119}}</ref>

==Divine Realm==
Tymora resided in the [[Gates of the Moon]] after the events of the [[Spellplague]], allied to, but independent of, that realm's twin mistresses. Her realm was the Great Wheel, seven [[earthmote]]s connected by lofty bridges, where games of chance, tests of luck, and gambling abounded. She named it after her former gambling quarter on the plane of [[Brightwater]].<ref name="FP3e-p77"/><ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|63}}</ref><ref name="PGtF-147">{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|147}}</ref>

In [[Brightwater]], Tymora resided within the grand casino of the [[Hall of Chance]], filled with petitioners playing every game of chance known in the Realms and beyond. The streets that led to the casino were paved in glistening illusory gold and had gaudy statues of all shapes along them.<ref name="ty-79">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|79}}</ref> Adjacent to the Hall of Chance laid lush but chaotic garden frequented by the goddess. The garden appeared to have been planted by randomly tossing various seeds and letting it all grow. Among the trees in the garden grew [[birch]] trees, illuminated by [[firefly|fireflies]] after dusk.<ref name="ty-82">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|82}}</ref>

==Relationships==
Sages have claimed that Tymora had fostered many brief trysts with good-aligned male deities, always ending them amicably when her attentions were drawn elsewhere. She was known to count [[Azuth]], [[Baravar Cloakshadow]], [[Brandobaris]], [[Dugmaren Brightmantle]], [[Erevan Ilesere]], [[Finder Wyvernspur]], [[Garl Glittergold]], [[Haela Brightaxe]], [[Hanali Celanil]], [[Lathander]], [[Lliira]], [[Marthammor Duin]], [[Selûne]], [[Shaundakul]], [[Sune]] and [[Vergadain]] as her allies.<ref name="F&A-166"/><ref name="F&A-p45"/><ref>{{Cite book/Planes of Chaos/The Book of Chaos|39}}</ref>

Tymora had a very close friendship with Selûne, who was not only her oldest friend in this life, but also in the one before, when Lady Luck was still goddess Tyche. Moreover, Selûne was the reason, why Tymora was "born", since she was the one that split Tyche into Tymora and Beshaba. She also invited Tymora to live with her at the [[Gates of the Moon]], when Tyr broke her heart.<ref name="Dra388"/><ref name="F&A-p44">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|44}}</ref><ref name="SCAG-p40">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|40}}</ref><ref name="F&A-p45">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|45}}</ref><ref name="F&A-p4">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|4}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p159">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|159}}</ref>

She also had a good relationship with Brandobaris, acting as his accomplice when he played tricks on others. Their friendly attitude to each other, was likely the reason for his good luck.<ref name="FRCS2e-p62"/> Some halflings even speculated that Lady Luck and the Master of Stealth could have been romantically linked.<ref name="DD-p203">{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|203}}</ref> Brandobaris, Erevan Ilesere, Garl Glittergold and Tymora, formed an informal group of mischief, which sometimes worked on the nerves of more serious gods.<ref name="F&P3e-p138-139">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|138-139}}</ref><ref name="DD-p166-168">{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|166-168}}</ref>

Beshaba was her twin sister's fiercest foe since the day they were "born" from Tyche's husk. They were opposites of each other and fought constantly, but Beshaba wasn't Lady Luck's only enemy. Tymora also held enmity towards [[Bane]] and [[Moander]].<ref name="Shadowdale-p280-281">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|280-281|13}}</ref><ref name="Tantras-p20">{{Cite book/Tantras/MMP (2003)|20}}</ref> Though she held no malice towards them, she occasionally played tricks and pranks on the more staid deities, such as [[Helm]] and [[Tyr]].<ref name="FaP-78"/><ref name="F&A-p45"/>

Finder Wyvernspur was one of Tymora's closest and loyal allies. It's was not surprising, since she sponsored his rise to godhood, and was his old patron deity, that presumably helped him permanently slay Moander. He was jokingly referred to as the god of reckless fools, which Tymora may have appreciated.<ref name="TML">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|90}}</ref><ref name="DD&D-p5, 6">{{Cite web enhancement/Deity Do's and Don'ts|5, 6}}</ref>

In the aftermath of the Dawn Cataclysm, Shaundakul was rumored to have shared a fleeting romance with Tymora while rejecting the overtures of her sister, Beshaba. This rejection only deepened the existing animosity between the two sisters.<ref name="F&A-p144">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|143}}</ref>

==Worshipers==
{{Main|Church of Tymora}}
{{Quote|But a glancing touch of your grace would befit me well, Lady Luck.|A basic prayer to Tymora.<ref name="bg3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

Commonly consisting of adventurers and others who relied on a mixture of luck and skill to achieve their goals, the Tymoran clergy encouraged folk to pursue their dreams.<ref name="HoF-p126" /> Members of Tymoran faith believed that one should be bold and trust in one's own luck. Priests of Tymora were taught to think of themselves as their own masters and to be brave enough to accept both good and bad fortune, as a trust in their goddess and in themselves. They were also duty-bound to aid the daring by providing healing and even some minor magic items.<ref name="F&A-166-167"/><ref name="N.GSF-p25">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/The Wilderness|25}}</ref><ref name=EotS-p51/><ref name="Pol71"/><ref name="N.GSF-p51">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/Cities & Civilization|51}}</ref><ref name="VGtC">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Cormyr|176}}</ref><ref name="SLW">{{Cite book/Dungeons & Dragons Essentials Kit/Storm Lord's Wrath}}</ref><ref name="AB-p20">{{Cite book/Azure Bonds|20|2}}</ref>

Shrines and temples of Tymora were as widespread as the needs of adventurers to be healed, which made the temples wealthy.<ref name="FRCS3e-p226">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|226}}</ref><ref name="FRA-p108">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|108}}</ref> These places of worship often differed significantly from each other in powers, manners, and titles though, with little overall authority or hierarchy. They were independent from each other, and each temple reflected the tastes of its high priestess or priest. The temples provided [[potion]]s, [[scroll]]s, or other little things like [[glowstone]]s, often as rewards to those who served Tymora and her tenets well. However, despite their differences, most of the Tymoran temples shared some traditions. The common item worn by all clergy of Lady Luck was the [[disk of Tymora]], usually carried on a small chain and always prepared for the greeting rituals. Blue and silver colors were commonly seen on clerical dresses. The Church of Tymora didn't discriminate, and saw all races and sexes as equal in the eyes of Tymora. However, in practice, most exalted ranks of the priesthood consisted of [[human]] women.<ref name="Pol71" /><ref name="poly81-p8">{{Cite polyhedron/81/The Everwinking Eye: As Cold As Bare Fingers|8}}</ref><ref name="RoV">{{Cite book/Realms of Valor/Patronage|133-135}}</ref><ref name="AB-p18">{{Cite book/Azure Bonds|18|2}}</ref>

Tymora was the most famous deity around the country of [[Cormyr]], after she protected their capital, Suzail, during the Time of Troubles. Her efforts prompted a resurgence of her faith throughout the nation. The priests of Tymora didn't charge visitors for service, however most people provided offerings, believing that it would increase their luck. Thanks to those donations, the Tymoran temples around Cormyr were extremely wealthy.<ref name="D198"/><ref name="AB-p20">{{Cite book/Azure Bonds|20|2}}</ref><ref name="C-p21">{{Cite book/Cormyr|21}}</ref>

Nobles and merchants were constantly seeking the blessing of Tymora, and no significant venture was undertaken without the prayers of the Luckbringers.<ref name="D198"/>
[[File:Luckbringer of Tymora - Randy Gallegos.jpg|thumb|left|''A luckbringer of Tymora.'']]
Tymora, being the patron of gamblers and game players, favored skillful and daring risk-takers and watched over those who ventured to improve their lives by taking risks.<ref name="HoF-p126" />

She was one of "The travelers' gods", alongside [[Tempus]] and [[Waukeen]], revered deities worshipped by the wanderers of [[The Vast]].<ref name="IRB,TLS-p53">{{Cite book/Inside Ravens Bluff, the Living City|53}}</ref>

Tymora was worshipped by many pirates and privateers, who were often found praying in one of her many temples that were based in friendly ports. Her worship was common in the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]], where she was the patron deity of the [[Cormyrean Freesails]].<ref name="PotFS-p17">{{Cite book/Pirates of the Fallen Stars|17}}</ref>

The faith of Tymora was popular among [[Halfling]]s, in particular halfling adventurers.<ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|33}}</ref> They believed that, since Tymora often manifested as a halfling to them, she was actually a halfling deity herself and had conned the 'big folk' into worshiping her as well.<ref name="DD-p161-162">{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|161-162}}</ref> Some even considered her to be one of [[Yondalla's Children]], partially due to her close work with [[Yondalla]] in protecting halflings.<ref name="DD-p179">{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|179}}</ref> She was the most prominent deity in the halfing kingdom of [[Luiren]].<ref name="tSS-p45">{{Cite book/The Shining South (1993)|45}}</ref> Halflings called Tymora either “Tymora” or “Shalamora” or referred to her as Lady Luck or Our Smiling Lady.<ref name="TEV-Halfling-names"/><ref name="poly139-p15">{{Cite polyhedron/139/City Stories: The Hall of Luck|15}}</ref>

===Symbol===
[[File:Tymora symbol.jpg|thumb|right|250px|''Tymora's symbol.'']]
Throughout her existence, Tymora had several [[Holy symbol|symbols]] related to her. That included: featureless disk of silver,<ref name="FRCS1e-p15"/><ref name="Pol71"/><ref name="FRCS2e-p62"/> triangle of silver shooting stars falling in the night sky,<ref name="Pol71"/> and floating, randomly and slowly turning, sphere of everbright silver. However, Tymora's most well-known and latest holy symbol was a face-up silver coin with an image of her face surrounded by shamrocks.<ref name="FRCS1e-p15"/><ref name="FRCS-235"/><ref name="PHB5e-p62-63,294"/><ref name="FP3e-p77"/><ref name="F&A-166-167"/><ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|33}}</ref>

In [[Amn]], due to [[pearl]]s being deeply associated with luck, clerics of Tymora used coins and black pearls as symbols of their goddess. However, this practice was regarded as nearly heretical by Tymoran clerics from other regions.<ref name="LoI-p102">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue (boxed set)|102}}</ref>

===Prayers===
Offerings made to the goddess of luck were often accompanied by the prayer, "A [[copper]] to the Lady returns tenfold in [[gold]]," which a priest would answer with "Dare much, and the Lady keep you."<ref name="MM">{{Cite book/Midnight's Mask/MMP|64|4}}</ref>

==History==
===Creation===
During times known as the [[Dawn Cataclysm]], the former deity of luck, [[Tyche]], controlled both good and bad luck. While traveling around the world, she came upon a beautiful rose, which she attempted to pluck from the earth. Unable to do so, Tyche cursed it with bad luck, whereupon its stem broke and it fell to the ground. She placed the rose in her hair, oblivious to a fact that it was in fact a trap from [[Moander]], deity of rot and decay. His evil essence worked its corruption into Tyche’s ear, after which started to drain her lifeforce, wither her form within and corrupt her in body and soul. When she finally returned to the [[Upper planes]], the oblivious Tyche came upon her friends: [[Selûne]], goddess of the moon, [[Lathander]], god of birth and renewal, and [[Azuth]], god of [[arcane magic]]; who were waiting to speak to her. However, instead of beautiful goddess, the three deities witnessed the disgusting creature that had once been Tyche, rotting from the inside. They saw the corruption destroying her, and unable to help her in any other way, Selûne casted bolt of light as the last effort to purify her. However, Tyche instead split apart, creating Tymora, the goddess of good luck, and then [[Beshaba]], the goddess of misfortune.<ref name="Dra388"/><ref name="FP3e-p77"/><ref name="F&A-p44">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|44}}</ref><ref name="SCAG-p40">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|40}}</ref><ref name="FPp79"/><ref name="FPp78"/><ref name="F&A-p45">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|45}}</ref><ref name="F&A-p4">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|4}}</ref><ref name="DD&D-p3, 4">{{Cite web enhancement/Deity Do's and Don'ts|3, 4}}</ref><ref name="FRCS.CotR-p10">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Set/Cyclopedia of the Realms|10}}</ref><ref name="TDL-p44">{{Cite book/The Dalelands|44}}</ref><ref name="FRA-p106">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|106}}</ref>

===The Time of Troubles===
In [[1358 DR]] during the [[Time of Troubles]], Tymora's [[avatar]] manifested in the [[Cormyr]]ian city of [[Suzail]] and then traveled to the [[Lady's House]], a temple devoted to her worship in [[Arabel]].<ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|1}}</ref><ref name="poly139-p14">{{Cite polyhedron/139/City Stories: The Hall of Luck|14}}</ref><ref name="Shadowdale-p26-27">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|26-27}}</ref><ref name="VGtC-p56">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Cormyr|56}}</ref> She protected that city during the crisis.<ref name="Tour-45">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|45}}</ref> It was thought that her presence in that city spared Cormyr from the chaos which affected most of the rest of [[Faerûn]] at the time.<ref name="D198">{{Cite dungeon/198/Backdrop: Suzail|71}}</ref>

===Recent History===
In [[1384 DR]], shortly before the catastrophic events of the [[Spellplague]], Tymora was involved in an unfortunate misunderstanding when the goddess of love [[Sune]], encouraged her and [[Tyr]], god of law and justice, to pursue a romantic relationship to restore balance to the [[Celestial Planes]], after [[Siamorphe]] left the [[House of the Triad]] to make her realm in Brightwater. Tyr was led to believe (some say due to the machinations of the god of deception, [[Cyric]]), that [[Helm]], god of guardians, who was chaperoning the couple, was seeing Tymora behind his back, and completely stole her heart. This led to Tyr challenging Helm to a duel, which the latter accepted because he felt that it was the honorable act to accept the challenge. As a result of a duel, Helm was apparently slain, leading to more chaos in the Celestial realms. So upset was Tymora at this turn of events, that she abandoned her realm in Brightwater, and was accepted by [[Selûne]] to live in the [[Gates of the Moon]].<ref name="TGHotR-p159">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|159}}</ref> In [[1368 DR]], [[Iyachtu Xvim]] attempted to deceive Lathander into rejoining Tymora and Beshaba back into Tyche, however his plans were foiled.<ref name="TGHotR-p148">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|148}}</ref>

===Background===
In [[Ed Greenwood]]'s original campaign, Tymora was called "Tyche". Her name was changed to Tymora by [[Jeff Grubb]] because he was afraid that calling her Tyche might offend Wiccans.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://grubbstreet.blogspot.com/2010/03/realms-and-remembrance.html |title=Realms and Rememberance |author=[[Jeff Grubb]] |date=March 25, 2010 |accessdate=14 November 2010}}</ref>
