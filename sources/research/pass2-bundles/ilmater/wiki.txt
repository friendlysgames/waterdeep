'''Ilmater''' ({{Pronounce|ihl|MAY|ter|audio=Ilmater.ogg|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/><ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/>}}), known to the [[Iulutiun]] people of the [[Great Glacier]] as '''Ayuruk''' and to the [[Angulutiun]] people as '''Itishikopak''',<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="tGC-p39">{{Cite book/The Great Glacier|39, 48}}</ref> was the [[Faerûnian pantheon|Faerûnian]] [[intermediate deity|intermediate]] [[deity]] of endurance, martyrdom, perseverance, and suffering. Known variously as the '''Crying God''', the '''Rack-Broken Lord''', and the '''One Who Endures''', Ilmater was the patron of the suffering, oppressed, and persecuted. He encouraged them to endure and encouraged others to help them by taking their burdens or places. Ilmater was a willing sufferer, bearing the pain of others to spare them from it, and it was said that if he had his way he would do so for all the suffering in the world.<ref name="SCAG5e-p21,30"/><ref name="FRCS1e-p11">{{Cite book/Cyclopedia of the Realms|11, 18}}</ref><ref name="FRA-p21">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|21}}</ref><ref name="FRCS2e-p51">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|51, card}}</ref><ref name="FaA-p75">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|75–76}}</ref><ref name="FRCS3e-p242">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|234, 235, 242–243}}</ref><ref name="FaP-p30">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|30–32}}</ref><ref name="FRCG-p80">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|62, 80}}</ref> 

{{Quote|Lord Ilmater, I beseech you look upon me and behold my sin. Let it stand before your infinite patience and balk in its presence - for while I am weak, you stand mighty. Let me be absolved before your weeping majesty, that I may cleanly bear the burden of others in your name. My knees may stagger, my bones may break, my will may fracture - but my faith stands strong on your shoulders, Lord, if you will let it.|''The [[Prayers of Repent]]'', a book of prayers asking for Ilmater's forgiveness.<ref name="BG3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

==Description==
In [[avatar]] form, Ilmater appeared as a short man with a burly build wearing only a [[breechcloth]], with a plain but kind and comforting face, a balding head, and a hairy body. But this body bore the marks of mutilation and torture on the rack, covered with open cuts, closed scars, burns, and a range of other wounds. Ilmater's joints were broken, his limbs were ravaged, and his hands were smashed yet still functional. Moving caused him a great deal of pain as he dragged himself around. No matter how much his avatar healed, it still showed these terrible wounds.<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/><ref name="FRCS2e-p51"/><ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/>

==Personality==
Ilmater was the incarnation of compassion, and the eternal foe of suffering,<ref name="FRPG-p154">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Player's Guide|154}}</ref> a generous, good-spirited, and even-tempered deity, with a kind and gentle nature.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/> He sought to offer aid and relief and words of comfort to those in pain, who were oppressed, or otherwise in great need.<ref name="FRCS2e-p51"/><ref name="FaA-p75"/> He encouraged them to endure their pain, suffering, and abuse, for good things came to those who waited.<ref name="FRCS2e-p51"/> He encouraged his faithful to halt and relieve the suffering of others, and to take it on themselves if they must. Ilmater was himself a willing sufferer, taking one's place to bear their burden, seeking to endure any pain if it would lessen the pain of another.<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/><ref name="FRCS2e-p51"/><ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/> 

Ilmater did not anger quickly, but when he did, he was fearsome in his wrath. He was greatly angered by extreme cruelty and atrocities and by those who inflicted such suffering. He was particularly enraged by those who harmed or abused children and other young creatures. At such times, he set aside his doctrine of endurance and nonviolence and unleashed his full force to put an end to cruelty.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/> Although his ravaged appearance or his righteous rage could scare children, Ilmater took great care to reassure them and protect them. He treasured all children and young creatures in general.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/>

Ilmater was the most forgiving of beings; it was thought he could even forgive [[Loviatar]], Maiden of Pain, if she repented of her cruel deeds.<ref name="CoV-p11">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|11}}</ref> Though he knew full well the atrocious and horrific acts that wicked creatures could do, he remained forever hopeful that evil beings could one day be turned to good or destroyed, and so he stayed firm in his goals.<ref name="CoV-p14">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|14}}</ref>

He had a good if rather rustic sense of humor, and liked to hear funny stories.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/>

==Powers==
When manifested as an avatar, Ilmater could cast a wide variety of magic [[spell]]s, and was strongest with healing magic and beneficial [[necromancy]]. Only rarely did he cast a spell that injured another, except in anger and retribution for a cruel or sadistic deed performed by the target, or to protect another from harm.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/>

He fought unarmed and was a supreme martial artist. However, he usually chose not to defend himself, instead simply taking and absorbing the damage, and his avatar was extremely hard to destroy. As with his spells, Ilmater only attacked in retribution or protection, or to deflect missiles or destroy items. He also used nonlethal methods to disable his powerful foes.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/> His unarmed strike counted as a ''+5 [[keen]] [[Alignment|lawful]] [[vorpal]]'' weapon.<ref name="FaP-p30"/>

Alternatively, he could possess any [[good]] and innocent creature that was being tortured, provided that the torture contravened local laws. This creature glowed white, all pain was relieved, and any wounds were regenerated immediately. Torture or restraining devices were immediately destroyed, releasing the sufferer, alert and healthy. In the case of extreme torture or associated murder, Ilmater granted the possessed being the ability to cast destructive spells, such as ''[[flame strike]]'', ''[[lightning bolt]]'' or ''[[chain lightning]]'', ''[[meteor swarm]]'', ''[[sink]]'', or ''[[imprisonment]]'' for a short time in retribution.<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/><ref name="FaA-p75"/>

When not appearing via his avatar or through possession, Ilmater manifested as an unseen presence that made a howling or whimpering sound yet felt watchful. In this form, he could speak, cast spells, or move objects around by ''[[telekinesis]]''.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="F&P-WE">{{Cite web enhancement/Deity Do's and Don'ts}}</ref>

To act in his place, Ilmater could send [[deva]]s, [[einheriar]] spirits of slain martyrs, [[hollyphant]]s, [[incarnate of courage|incarnates of courage]], [[planetar]]s, [[solar]]s, and some beings that remained unidentified. To indicate or suggest his presence or awareness, to show his favor, and to give his faithful encouragement, Ilmater could create [[daisy|daisies]] and white [[rose]]s, or send white [[donkey]]s, white [[dove]]s, [[field mouse|field mice]], or [[sparrow]]s. The simple appearance of the colors gray and red served as a minor manifestation of Ilmater's favor.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="F&P-WE"/>

Finally, Ilmater could sense any kind of suffering the moment it took place, anywhere in the world. He also knew whenever a person sacrificed themselves for another being. He could also instantly create any [[magical item]] that could heal or reduce suffering or that was [[good]] or [[Alignment|lawful]] in nature.<ref name="FaP-p30"/>

==Realm==
Ilmater made his home in the divine realm of [[Martyrdom]], a mountain of reward and peace for suffering in life, where none could feel pain or weariness.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="PGTF-p159"/><ref name="FRCG-p80"/> Under the [[Great Wheel cosmology]], this was located on the layer of [[Shurrock]] in the [[Bytopia|Twin Paradises of Bytopia]].<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/><ref name="FRA-p21"/><ref name="FRCS2e-p51"/><ref name="FaA-p75"/> Under the [[World Tree cosmology]], however, Martyrdom was found in the [[House of the Triad]], on a lesser mountain of [[Celestia]], alongside the mountain realms of Ilmater's fellow gods of the Triad, Tyr and Torm.<ref name="PGTF-p159"/>

However, following the breaking of the Triad in 1384 DR, Ilmater moved his domain out of the House of the Triad. At Sune's invitation, he relocated it to [[Brightwater]] on the World Tree.<ref name="tGHotR-p159"/> By 1479 DR, Ilmater had reestablished Martyrdom in [[Celestia]], now under the [[World Axis cosmology]].<ref name="FRCG-p80"/>

==Relationships==
[[File:The Triad.jpg|thumb|250px|left|''The deities of the Triad, with Ilmater leaning on Tyr and Torm leading the way.'']]
The ranks of Ilmater's closest allies included [[Tyr]] the Maimed God, god of justice (who was his superior prior to the [[Spellplague]]); and [[Torm]] the True, god of loyalty and duty. Collectively, these three deities formed the [[Triad]], an alliance of lawful good deities that was stronger as a united force than individually. In fact, Ilmater could often be found traveling with Tyr, assisting him and teaching him to live without his sight and to rely upon his feelings instead.<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/><ref name="FRCS2e-p51"/><ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/><ref name="FRPG-p154"/> Ilmater showed Tyr that true justice required a judge to know how much punishment was sufficient, whether it was too harsh or whether mercy was better.<ref name="CoV-p11"/> Ilmater also had an excellent relationship with Torm.<ref name="FaA-p75"/>

Another of Ilmater's allies was [[Lathander]] the Morninglord, god of renewal and vitality.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/> He was also allied with [[Ibrandul]], the Lord of the Dry Depths, the freedom-loving god of caves and darkness.<ref name="FaA-p75"/>

Ilmater stood against those gods and goddesses who enjoyed causing destruction and spreading pain and suffering for others. In particular, considering their contrasting portfolios, the Crying God and his followers were a bane to [[Loviatar]], the Maiden of Pain, and her worshipers.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/> Loviatar in turn hated Ilmater with a passion, as he protected her victims from her torments.<ref name="FaP-p100">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|100}}</ref> Ilmater also opposed [[Talona]], Mistress of Disease.<ref name="FaP-p30"/> Among his other foes were [[Bane]], [[Bhaal]], [[Garagos]], [[Malar]], [[Myrkul]], [[Shar]], and [[Talos]].<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/>

==Worshipers==
:{{Main|Church of Ilmater}}
{{Quote|Until I can bear a fraction of your burden, Sufferer, I shall.|A basic prayer to Ilmater.<ref name="BG3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}
Those who were oppressed, sick, lame, or poor were likely to be worshipers of Ilmater,<ref name="FaP-p30"/> and people who had been injured or were otherwise suffering would often call upon him for aid.<ref name="FRCS2e-p51"/> His veneration was popular among the poor in big cities, and with serfs and slaves,<ref name="FaP-p30"/> as well as merchants, thieves, and a few guards.<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/> Although people of any [[alignment]] could worship Ilmater,<ref name="FaA-p75"/> he was largely followed by folk of a lawful and good bent, especially among his own clergy.<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/><ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/>

Ilmater's clergy numbered [[cleric]]s, [[paladin]]s, and [[monk]]s.<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/> They were organized into the [[Church of Ilmater]], with many affiliated knightly and monastic orders.<ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/>

Across [[Faerûn]], the hardy people of the harsh and war-torn land of [[Damara]] particularly venerated Ilmater, as well as his champion, [[St. Sollars]].<ref name="FRCS1e-p37">{{Cite book/Cyclopedia of the Realms|37}}</ref><ref name="FRCS2e-p117">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|117}}</ref> In [[Calimshan]], Ilmater was one of the most frequently worshiped gods, especially among the lower classes and slaves.<ref name="EotSS-p55">{{Cite book/Empires of the Shining Sea|55}}</ref>

Attending clerics of Ilmater could be recognized in battle by the coarse furred shirts they wore. It was considered a great wrong to harm these priests, as they helped ease suffering. Even [[orc]]s and [[goblin]]s held them in esteem for ministering to their fallen as well as to [[human]]s.<ref name="SCAG-p30">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|30}}</ref>

===Symbol===
[[File:Ilmater Symbol-5e.png|thumb|right|250px|''A holy symbol of Ilmater.'']]
Ilmater's holy symbol was originally a blood-stained rack, such as used to torture a victim by stretching. This remained in use until the mid-14th century [[DR]]. However, by [[1356 DR]], a new symbol had entered common usage: a pair of white hands crossed and bound at the wrist with a blood-red cord. After the [[Godswar]] of [[1358 DR]], this was used almost exclusively. This newer and less gruesome symbol increased his popularity across [[Faerûn]].<ref name="FRCS1e-p11"/><ref name="FRA-p21"/><ref name="FRCS2e-p51"/><ref name="FaA-p75"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/>

==History==
{{quote
|Today is the first day in what's left of your life... so live it well.
|A cynical phrase with a typical Ilmatari addition.<ref name="FaA-p76">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|76}}</ref>
}}
Ilmater was an older god<ref name="FRCS3e-p242"/><ref name="FaP-p30"/> and an obscure deity in the 3{{rd}} century before [[Dalereckoning]], but he came to prominence during [[Tyr]]'s [[Procession of Justice]] in the [[Vilhon Reach]]. In {{Yearlink|-247}}, Tyr had led a force of [[Celestial archon|archon]]s out of a ''[[gate]]'' to pacify the remnants of the fallen empire of [[Jhaamdath]], which had fallen into anarchy, lawlessness, and banditry. Tyr's deeds and sacrifices caught the attention of Ilmater, who allied himself with the Just God in the [[-243 DR|Year of the Rack, -243 DR]]. The Procession came to an end in {{Yearlink|-238}}<ref name="FaP-p80">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|80}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p94">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|94}}</ref> but Tyr remained on [[Abeir-Toril]], and the two deities continued to work together.<ref name="FaP-p80"/>

Many years after the Procession, [[Torm]] joined them as Tyr's war leader. Together, Ilmater, Torm, and Tyr formed the [[Triad]], a long-lasting alliance of [[good]] and [[Alignment|lawful]] deities.<ref name="FaP-p80"/>

An ancient and unsupported legend told that the human [[Khala]] of [[Imaskar]] had devoted themselves to the god [[Bane]] the Black Lord and perpetrated numerous atrocities. For their deeds, the gods of law inflicted curses upon them. Among them, Ilmater accused the Khala of tormenting their victims and tearing heart and soul from them. Thus he cursed them with an unnatural hunger, to devour the hearts of their prey so they might live. Out of all the gods' curses was born the [[peryton]], a vicious, stag-headed, eagle-bodied beast. The female peryton needed to devour the heart of a humanoid to become fertile—some considered it ironic that Ilmater's curse drove the peryton to further murder and savagery.<ref name="EE-tGGLoT-p27">{{Cite book/Elminster's Ecologies/The Great Gray Land of Thar|27–28}}</ref>

According to legend, an [[avatar]] of Ilmater once died somewhere south of the [[Inner Sea]] some time in the 4{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="VGtATM-p109">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to All Things Magical|109–110}}</ref><ref group="note">Specifically, this is "over a millennia" before the present time of around [[1369 DR]].</ref> In fact, an ancient religious text from the island of [[Ixinos]] written in the 300s DR titled ''[[Divine Rapture of Ilmater]]'' told of the appearance of the Crying God and how he wrestled with "the cur of [[Gehenna]]," a corrupted minion of [[Bhaal]], Lord of Murder. Enduring all its clawing and biting, Ilmater carried the beast into the sea and there died with it. Only his iron helm was found on the beach later.<ref name="BG3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref> The ''[[Iron Helm of Heroes]]'' became a minor relic of Ilmatari faith.<ref name="VGtATM-p109" />

The Triad was broken in the [[1384 DR|Year of Three Streams Blooded, 1384 DR]], when Tyr was manipulated into slaying [[Helm]], god of guardians. Ilmater chose to leave the [[House of the Triad]] and, at the invitation of [[Sune]], goddess of beauty, he relocated his divine realm to the plane of [[Brightwater]].<ref name="tGHotR-p159">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|159}}</ref> By 1479 DR, however, Ilmater had returned to Torm's side and reestablished his realm in [[Celestia]].<ref name="FRCG-p80"/>

During the [[Second Sundering]] of the late 15th century [[DR]], a [[Chosen]] of Ilmater began a passive slave revolt in [[Calimshan]] against their [[genie]] masters. During this time, he disappeared mysteriously. Though the overlords were cast down, it was only by bloody means. Many humans still longed for the return of Ilmater's Chosen so he could complete the country's transition to a better non-violent society.<ref name="SCAG-p9">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|9}}</ref>

==Appendix==
===Background===
Although they share a similar name, and his followers are known as "Ilmatari", Ilmater bears no apparent resemblance to [[Wikipedia:Ilmatar|Ilmatar]], a female air spirit of [[Wikipedia:Finnish mythology|Finnish mythology]]. There is also no resemblance or known connection to Ilmatar, the lawful good mother goddess of D&D's Finnish mythology presented in [[Deities & Demigods 1st edition|''Deities & Demigods'' (1st edition)]], ''[[Legends & Lore]]'', and ''[[On Hallowed Ground]]''. This is despite other goddesses of D&D's Finnish mythology—[[Kiputytto]], [[Mielikki]], and [[Loviatar]]—making actual transitions into the ''[[Forgotten Realms]]'' setting.

Ilmater is much closer in nature to [http://scrollsoflankhmar.com/rpgguide:issek Issek of the Jug], a god created by [[Wikipedia:Fritz Leiber|Fritz Leiber]] for his [[Wikipedia:Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser|''Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser'']] series and similarly presented in the early D&D sources ''Deities & Demigods'' and ''Legends & Lore''. Issek is also a god of peace and perseverance worshiped by the poor and disfigured, whose symbol is a man on a broken rack. [[Ed Greenwood]] originally created Ilmater for his own D&D game and described him as similar to Issek of the Jug in his "[[Down-to-earth divinity]]" article in [[Dragon magazine 54|''Dragon'' magazine #54]].
