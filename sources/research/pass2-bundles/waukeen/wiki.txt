'''Waukeen''' ({{Pronounce|wau|KEEN|refs=<ref name="CotR-16" /><ref name="FA-176" />}} {{Pronounce|wah|KEEN|refs=<ref name="FRCS3e-255" /><ref name="FaP-87" />|alt=yes}}) was a [[lesser deity]] of wealth and trade in the [[Faerûnian pantheon]]. '''The Merchant's Friend''' was venerated and appeased by not only merchants, from lowly peddlers to the wealthy owners of trading companies, but investors, accountants, entrepreneurs, caravan guides, warehouse owners, philanthropists, deal-makers, moneylenders, and other such individuals.<ref name="FA-176" /><ref name="FaP-88">{{Cite book/Faiths & Pantheons|88}}</ref> '''Liberty's Maiden''' was a goddess of commerce and the accumulation of wealth through free and fair trade, as well as the beneficial use of wealth to improve civilization. At the same time '''The Golden Lady''' was the goddess of illicit trade<ref name="FA-177">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|177}}</ref> and the patron of many smugglers, fences, black marketeers, and "businessmen" on the shady side of commerce.<ref name="FA-178">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|178}}</ref>

==Description==
[[File:Waukeensymbol.png|thumb|left|182px|''The holy symbol of the Merchant's Friend.'']]
Waukeen was portrayed as a slender, young woman with long, lustrous golden hair; always splendidly dressed. Some depictions included a pair of golden lions resting at her feet.<ref name="CotR-16" /> Her avatar was 10 ft (3 m) tall<ref name="FA-177" /> and typically had eyes that appeared as solid [[gold]]. She wore a [[gown]] woven from strips of precious metals polished to a gleaming finish and sprinkled with all manner of [[gem]]stones. Her [[sash]] was of spun gold and her cloak was a lattice of gold coins. On her feet were boots of laced pearls with gold soles.<ref name="FA-177" /><ref name="FaP-89">{{Cite book/Faiths & Pantheons|89}}</ref>

On occasions where subtlety was more appropriate, the Golden Lady sometimes manifested as a sprouting of daffodils to give a sign of favor, hope, or inspiration. Often she would provide money in unexpected places, like a coin, face-up, to indicate the correct path at an intersection, or nuggets of gold, [[citrine]]s, [[pyrite]] (also known as fool's gold&mdash;perhaps as a sign of disfavor). Slightly less subtle was a pair of golden eyes observing from a curtain of impenetrable darkness; or a large stack of gold coins that writhed like a snake, flying or wrapping itself around something or someone to which she wanted to draw attention before it shattered and scattered, often causing a scramble to collect them. All such tokens from the goddess were highly valued by Waukeenar as "divine essence of the goddess". When more interaction or aid was needed she could send creatures such as golden-colored [[cat]]s, [[lock lurker]]s, golden [[lion]]s, palomino [[horse]]s, [[eagle]]s (preferably golden ones), or extraplanar creatures such as [[ferrumach]]s and [[plumach]]s from the [[Outlands]].<ref name="FA-177" />

==Personality==
Waukeen was an active and enthusiastic deity who enjoyed the ebb and flow of goods via commerce and the rapid pace of business in a thriving marketplace full of spirited bargaining<ref name="FaP-87" />&mdash;so much so that rumors of her presence at particularly intense negotiations involving one of her worshipers became legendary.<ref name="FA-176" /> Her side always got the best bargain, but the other party usually walked away also feeling satisfied about the terms of the deal.<ref name="FA-176" /> Waukeen was driven to accomplish a goal and move on to the next one and, rather than being frustrated when the usual business practices failed to work out a solution, she was willing to try alternate methods.<ref name="FA-176" /> This manifested itself in her church sometimes allowing the ends to justify the means.<ref name="FaP-88" /> Her stubbornness and dogged persistence occasionally got her in predicaments and likely led to her imprisonment during the [[Time of Troubles]].<ref name="FA-176" />

==Powers==
Although her avatar was not seen during the [[Godswar]],<ref name="FaP-87" /> sightings before that time reported that she cast spells as both a [[wizard]] and a [[cleric]], choosing spells from any [[Schools of Magic|school]] or [[sphere]].<ref name="FA-177" /> Her weapon of choice was a pummeling stream of gold coins that emitted from either hand (the coins did not disappear, so they could also be given as a boon). The coin cloud acted like a ''+5 [[impact]] [[Bane (property)|evil outsider bane]] [[nunchaku]]''.<ref name="FaP-89" /> In addition, she could either spit molten gold up to 6 ft (1.8 m) or call down a ''[[flame strike]]'' that resembled a conflagration wrapped in a rotating helix of gold coins (which did disappear afterward) on any creature within 200 ft (61 m). Rather than fly she chose to walk on air, levitating and appearing to take strides as she moved.<ref name="FA-177" />

After her return from imprisonment in the [[Abyss]], she was known to have added [[rogue]] skills to her avatar while still being a formidable cleric and wizard.<ref name="FaP-88" /> She could extend her senses seven miles (11.3 km) or perceive anything within seven miles of her temples, holy sites, worshipers, or objects, or anywhere her name or one of her titles had been spoken within the previous hour. Her awareness extended to include any business deal, contract, exchange, sale, theft, or destruction of a merchant's property that impacted at least five hundred people in scope.<ref name="FaP-89" />

==Realm==
[[File:Harlot's Coin.jpg|thumb|179px|''Graz'zt admiring Waukeen's coin.'']]
According to the [[Great Wheel cosmology]] model, Waukeen's home was on the [[neutral]] [[plane]] of [[Concordant Opposition]], also known as the Outlands, in a shared realm called the [[Marketplace Eternal]]&mdash;a huge bazaar that covered many square miles/kilometers. The Marketplace Eternal was divided into four quarters and Waukeen's quarter was called the House of Barter.<ref group="note">For interested readers, the other three quarters were the ''Scales of Wealth'' governed by Shinare of Kyrnn, Zilchus of [[Oerth]]'s ''Seat of Luxury'', and the ''House of Resolve'' belonging to Sera of Aebrynis. See [[For Duty & Deity]], page 57, and [[On Hallowed Ground]], page 164.</ref> Somewhere in the midst of her quarter Waukeen had an opulent golden palace that contained a portal to Lliira's realm in Brightwater on the plane of [[Arborea]].<ref name="FDaD-57" /><ref name="OHG-181">{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|181}}</ref> Shortly after Waukeen's return to power, the [[World Tree cosmology]] model came into favor and [[Brightwater]] was described as its own plane with the Marketplace Eternal as the center of the populated region, and Waukeen its sole owner. Surrounding the Golden Lady's demesne were four quarters, divided between [[Lliira]], [[Tymora]], [[Sune]], and [[Sharess]].<ref name="FRCS3e-258">{{Cite book/FRCS|258}}</ref><ref name="PGtF-146-147">{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|146&ndash;147}}</ref>

After the [[Spellplague]], Brightwater was no more and the Marketplace Eternal moved to the plane of [[Eternal Sun]] where it sprawled around [[Amaunator]]'s [[Palace of the Four Suns]].<ref name="FRCG-63">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|63}}</ref>

==Relationships==
Waukeen's closest ally was [[Lliira]], who was the trustee of Waukeen's portfolio while she made her ill-fated attempt to get back to her realm in the [[Outlands]] during the [[Time of Troubles]].<ref name="FA-177" /> However, when Lliira started answering the prayers of Waukeen's followers during her long incarceration, many of the Waukeenar assumed their goddess was dead and switched allegiances<ref name="FRCS2eRtR-56" /> and rededicated their temples to Lliira.<ref name="FRCS2eRtR-54">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (Revised)/Running the Realms|54}}</ref> After Waukeen returned to power, she was jealous and upset that so many of her faithful did not return to her service, and this slightly soured the friendship between the two goddesses.<ref name="FaP-100">{{Cite book/Faiths & Pantheons|100}}</ref>

The Golden Lady was one of the first to support [[Gond]] and his inventions over the doubts of most other deities.<ref name="FA-176" /> [[Shaundakul]] was also an ally of the Merchant's Friend as his portfolio touched hers in areas such as long-range traders, and caravans.<ref name="FaP-88" /><ref name="FA-142-143">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|142&ndash;143}}</ref> Waukeen had no real enemies among the [[Faerûnian pantheon|pantheon]] except [[Mask]], the Master of All Thieves, because larceny is bad for business. She naturally had an unending hatred and thirst for revenge against the [[demon lord]] [[Graz'zt]] who broke a deal, took advantage of her weakened state, and held her captive during the [[Avatar Crisis]].<ref name="FaP-88" /><ref name="FA-177" /><ref name="PGtF-50">{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|50}}</ref>

==The Church==
{{main|Church of Waukeen}}
{{quote|''To the Merchant's Friend, the sound of clinking coins is as a hymn, the murmur of trade a prayer!''|[[Alanna]] of [[Neverwinter]]<ref name="SOZ">{{Cite game/Neverwinter Nights 2: Storm of Zehir}}</ref>}}<br/>
Collectively, Waukeen's worshipers were known as Waukeenar.<ref name="FA-178" /> Ornate temples to the glory of the Golden Lady were usually found in centers of commerce from [[Tashalar]] in the south, up through [[Amn]], all around the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]] ([[Chessenta]], [[Cormyr]], [[Sembia]], [[the Vast]], [[Impiltur]]) and over to [[Thesk]] on the [[Golden Way]].<ref name="FRCS3e-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|23}}</ref> Her clerics, called [[Goldeye|Goldeyes]], and other worshipers traveled the trade routes aiding merchants and caravans or worked in the opulent temples as hosts for wealthy donors, moneylenders, [[currency]] exchangers, venture capitalists, warehousers, fences (covertly, of course) and, most importantly, security guards.<ref name="FaP-88" /><ref name="FA-178" />

===Harlot's Coin Heresy===
This heretical schism of Waukeen's faith taught that Waukeen had gradually sold off pieces of her divinity and virtue to Graz'zt in order to finance her church. They believed that it was for failing to pay her debts to him that Graz'zt imprisoned her during the Time of Troubles. These heretics taught that Waukeen earned her freedom only because she finally paid her debt to the demon, resulting in Graz'zt owning the majority of Waukeen's godhood. The heretics therefore believed that all who worshiped Waukeen were really worshiping Graz'zt instead, and they were content with that arrangement.<ref name="D355-p71" />

==History==
[[File:WaukeenSymbolOld.jpg|thumb|right|200px|''A more iconic holy symbol of Waukeen.'']]
Waukeen was a relatively late addition to the [[Faerûnian pantheon]], joining sometime after [[Tyr]]<ref name="CotR-16" /> who first appeared in {{Yearlink|-247}}.<ref name="GHotR-52">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|52}}</ref><ref name="FaP-80">{{Cite book/Faiths & Pantheons|80}}</ref> Her popularity grew with the expansion of trade and the thriving merchant class across the Realms<ref name="FRCS2eRtR-56" /><ref name="FA-176" /> where the combination of generosity, risk taking, and shrewd business sense made her church enviably wealthy and widely admired.<ref name="FaP-87" />

Then came the [[Time of Troubles]], when she nearly lost her admirers, her church, her divinity, her freedom, and her life. 

When [[Lord Ao]] cast nearly all the Faerûnian deities into mortal avatar form and scattered them across the surface of [[Toril]], Waukeen was among them, and like the majority of the fallen gods, wanted most to return home (and the sooner she could reclaim her realm and marshal her resources, the quicker she could influence unfolding events in her favor). As luck would have it, the first being she met was the avatar of [[Lliira]] and the two goddesses decided to stick together while they worked out a plan of action.<ref name="FA-176" /><ref name="FDaD-3">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|3}}</ref>

The Golden Lady first advocated a straightforward approach: to climb the [[Celestial Stairway]] from its landing in [[Shadowdale]] and bribe [[Helm]] with his heart's desire to allow the two goddesses back into the [[Outer planes]]. Not known for her ambition, [[Lliira]] went along with the brash young deity's plan. They traveled to Shadowdale and began climbing the Staircase when Helm appeared and threatened them. Alas, nothing that Waukeen or the Lady of Joy offered Helm could entice the God of Guardians to shirk his duty, so they retreated to the [[Cormanthor]] forest rather than face his wrath.<ref name="FA-177" /><ref name="FDaD-3" />

Being intimately familiar with illicit trade as well as legitimate commerce, Waukeen decided to smuggle herself back to the [[Outlands]] by way of the [[Astral plane]] and a circuitous route through the [[Lower Planes]] by making deals, calling in favors, and bribery. By doing so, she hoped to avoid detection by Ao's servants and sneak back into her realm.<ref name="FA-177" /><ref name="FDaD-4">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|4}}</ref>

Through a twisted chain of contacts<ref group="note">It is unknown if spellcasters in the service of Waukeen or Lliira assisted in contacting a being outside of [[Realmspace]]. If any did, they have never admitted helping or even seeing the Golden Lady.</ref> she managed to call in a favor from [[Celestian]], the God of Wanderers from the sphere of [[Oerth]].<ref name="OHG-166">{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|166}}</ref> He agreed to transport her to the Astral plane as payment for the debt, but would not go so far as to include Lliira in the bargain, for fear of the added risk. When it came time to complete the deal however, he found that he could not budge her off her [[Prime Material Plane]]&mdash;Lord Ao had bound the Faerûnian gods to Toril with an unbreakable bond.<ref name="FA-177" /><ref name="FDaD-4" />

Out of options, Waukeen gambled that it was her divinity that was tied to the plane and not the vessel carrying it, so Lliira graciously agreed to hold Waukeen's power and portfolio in stewardship until Waukeen could reclaim it. Once the transferal of her divinity to Lliira was complete, Waukeen became an ordinary human woman, but still armed with an extraordinary body of knowledge and formidable spellcasting powers. Celestian was then able to shift her into the Astral plane for the next leg of her journey.<ref name="FA-177" /><ref name="FDaD-4" />

[[File:WaukeenAndGraz'zt.jpg|thumb|230px|right|''Waukeen as a "guest" of Graz'zt.'']]
Once free of Toril, Waukeen contacted some minions of an extremely powerful [[Abyssal lord]] named [[Graz'zt]] and negotiated<ref name="FDaD-4" /> (or had previously arranged for<ref name="FA-177" />) safe passage through the [[Abyss]] to the Outlands. In return, Graz'zt was to receive the location of a number of secret treasure hoards on Toril and across the [[planes]],<ref name="FA-177" /> and even more useful information about the financial dealings of Graz'zt's rival demon lords&mdash;information that could give him an advantage in the eternal [[Blood War]] between the demons and the [[devil]]s.<ref name="FDaD-4" /> The Merchant's Friend was escorted to the Dark Prince's vast realm [[Azzagrat]], which spanned the 45th, 46th, and 47th layers of the Abyss, to the sprawling city of [[Zelatar]], and finally to the sixty-six [[ivory]] towers<ref name="BoVD-131">{{Cite book/Book of Vile Darkness|131}}</ref> of the [[Argent Palace]]; Graz'zt's seat of power.<ref name="FA-177" /><ref name="FDaD-4" />

Graz'zt of course never planned on honoring the deal struck with Waukeen. Once she arrived in the heart of his realm he had no trouble detaining the mortal form of the Golden Lady and told her she would be his "guest" while they renegotiated the deal previously agreed upon, knowing full well she would not alter a closed contract. This manufactured impasse gave the Dark Prince time to work on his master plan: to find a way for his daughter [[Thraxxia]] to possess, control, or impersonate Waukeen well enough to fool the gods and elevate Thraxxia to be the new goddess of wealth.<ref name="FDaD-33">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|33}}</ref>

[[File:CaravanInTheAbyss.jpg|thumb|250px|left|''Caravans used to shuttle Waukeen about were often decoys.'']]
Until his plan came to fruition, Graz'zt had to keep Waukeen alive and under his control. He knew it was only a matter of time before her worshipers discovered her whereabouts and sent out rescue parties, so he began moving her back and forth between the Argent Palace and the city of [[Samora]] where his trusted servant, the [[succubus]] [[Maretta]], Lady of the Counting-House, lived.<ref name="FA-177" /><ref name="FDaD-34">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|34}}</ref> The truth was that most of the heavily guarded and slow-moving caravans were decoys designed to lure potential rescuers out into the open and be destroyed.<ref name="FDaD-33" /> But on one occasion Waukeen managed to bribe a guard and escape into the [[Viper Forest]] called [[Zrintor]], only to be recaptured later by a search party that could not be bribed into assisting her; they had witnessed the fate of the traitorous demon at the hands of Graz'zt and their fear outweighed their greed.<ref name="FA-177" /><ref name="FDaD-4" />

Luck was apparently on the side of the demon lord because no one, not even Lliira, knew what happened to Waukeen after she disappeared into the Abyss. All attempts at divination failed to reveal anything clear or useful. When the [[Godswar]] ended in the autumn of [[1358 DR]],<ref name="FDaD-3" /> all other faiths resumed getting upper-level spells from their deity, but not the Waukeenar.<ref name="FA-177" /> They continued to be limited to first- and second-level spells<ref name="FRA-36">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|36}}</ref> and received no inkling of guidance from the Golden Lady.<ref name="FRCS2eRtR-56" /> By [[1365 DR]], the [[church of Waukeen]] was falling apart and Lliira decided to intervene to save what was left of it or there would have been nothing for the Merchant's Friend to return to. She sent her prophet to all of Waukeen's temples and announced that she would act as regent for Waukeen's portfolio and answer prayers for spells. Many of the distraught Waukeenar interpreted this to mean that their goddess was truly dead.<ref name="FRCS2eRtR-56" /><ref name="FA-177" />

[[File:Waukeen bar.png|thumb|''A tapestry with Waukeen's image.'']]
It was twelve long years after the Godswar before the fevered dreams of [[Halanna Jashire]], a promising young priestess of Waukeen stationed in the [[Goldspires]] temple in [[Athkatla]], the capital of [[Amn]],<ref name="FDaD-6">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|6}}</ref> gave hope to the anguished members of the Golden Lady's church that their missing deity was alive and being held by the Abyssal lord Graz'zt.<ref name="FDaD-13">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|13}}</ref> A daring band of adventurers set out climbing the [[Celestial Staircase]], made it to the Abyss, and heroically rescued Waukeen from numerous minions of Graz'zt, returning her via the Staircase to her realm in the Outlands.<ref name="FaP-87" /><ref name="FDaD-56">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|56}}</ref>

When she finally strode down a lane between the tents and stalls of the sprawling open-air market that was her House of Barter, the cacophony of commerce fell silent as they slowly realized who walked among them. In front of her golden palace, Waukeen was joyfully met by her seneschal, a [[gynosphinx]] named [[Keelira]], who proclaimed the return of the Goddess of Wealth in a voice like thunder as the crowd erupted in ecstatic celebration. Keelira was sent to fetch Lliira, and soon the Mistress of the Revels returned Waukeen's mantle of divinity. And there was much rejoicing.<ref name="FDaD-57">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|57}}</ref>

On the [[Nightal 24|24th of Nightal]], [[1370 DR]], the Golden Lady revealed to her church and all of Faerûn that she had returned from her long absence, and that date became yet another festival celebrated by the worshipers of Waukeen.<ref name="FDaD-58">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|58}}</ref>

After the exuberance of the celebrations died down, there began a period of recovery for the church of Waukeen. During her long absence, referred to as the Interdeium of Waukeen, the church suffered an alarming amount of attrition and had to be built back up. Not one to avoid hard work, Waukeen set about revitalizing her faithful and combating the wild rumors and speculation that her disappearance and unusual return had generated, including the belief that she was dead.<ref name="FaP-87" /> One of the most damaging allegations was known as the Harlot's Coin Heresy inculcated by the [[League of Six-Fingered Gentlemen]], a group that had insinuated itself into the church hierarchy. Their doctrine was that Waukeen had traded or sold much of her divine power to Graz'zt and that she was now just a figurehead.<ref name="D355-p71">{{Cite dragon/355/Volo's Guide: Demon Cults of the Realms|71}}</ref>
