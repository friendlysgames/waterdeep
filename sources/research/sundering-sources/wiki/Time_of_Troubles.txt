The '''Time of Troubles''' was a cataclysmic time period in the chronology of [[Faerûn]]. Taking place during the {{YearlinkName|1358}},<ref name="FRCS-p271">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|271}}</ref> the Time of Troubles was a period during which the [[deities]] of [[Faerûn]] were forced to walk the earth in their mortal [[avatar]] forms. Several major deities died during the Time of Troubles (see [[#Deaths, Ascensions, and Resurrections|Deaths, Ascensions, and Resurrections]]) and a handful of mortals rose to divinity.<ref name="FRCS-p264">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|264}}</ref>

==Etymology==
Other names that this cataclysmic period was known by included '''the Arrival''',<ref name="FRCS-p271" /> the '''Avatar Crisis''', the '''Godswar''',<ref name="FRCS-p271" /><ref name="P&P-p3">{{Cite book/Powers & Pantheons|3}}</ref><ref name="F&A-p23">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|23}}</ref> the '''Time of the Avatars''', the '''Fall of the Gods''', and the '''Intervention of Ao'''.<ref name="P&P-p3" />

==Summary==
The Time of Troubles was precipitated by an attempt by the gods [[Bane]] and [[Myrkul]] to steal the [[Tablets of Fate]] from the overdeity [[Ao]]. Angry at the gods for their habitual pursuit of power and negligence toward their mortal faithful,<ref name="FRCS-p264"/> Lord Ao relegated every god (except for the guardian god [[Helm]], who was selected to protect the gates to the heavens<ref name="SD-p1">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|1-8|0}}</ref>) to walk among their followers on Toril.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/> This included the gods of [[pantheon]]s far removed from Faerûn, such as the [[Kara-Turan pantheon]] and [[Maztican pantheon]].<ref name="PBF">{{Cite web/theedverse/2018-10-13 - Time of Troubles - Pantheons Beyond Faerûn}}</ref>

The immediate effects of this edict were threefold. First, [[divine magic]] (spells granted to [[cleric]]s by their [[deity|patron deities]]) ceased to function altogether<ref name="Siege">{{Cite book/Siege of Darkness}}</ref> unless the cleric was within {{SI|1|mile|k|long=yes}} of their deity's avatar. Second, [[Magic|arcane magic]] (a force channeled from the [[Weave]] by [[wizard]]s and [[sorcerer]]s) ceased to be regulated by its steward, [[Mystra]], and became dangerously unpredictable.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/> Third, the characteristically immortal and aloof deities were now vulnerable (though still devastatingly powerful) and dwelling among the civilizations of Faerûn.<ref name="FRCS-p265">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|265}}</ref>

==History==
* Ao cast the gods down in the early evening of [[Kythorn 15]].<ref name="TFRA-p128" />
* Helm destroyed Mystra for attempting to ascend the Celestial Stairway on [[Midsummer]].<ref name="TFRA-p128" />
* Bane and the Celestial Stairway were destroyed on [[Flamerule 18]].<ref name="TFRA-p128" />
* Myrkul brought Bane back to Toril and he possessed [[Fzoul Chembryl]] on [[Flamerule 25]].<ref name="TFRA-p128" />
* Bane and Torm destroyed each other on [[Eleasis 13]].<ref name="TFRA-p128" />
* Cyric slew Bhaal on [[Eleint 26]].<ref name="TFRA-p128" />
* Myrkul was slain by Midnight on [[Marpenoth 15]].<ref name="TFRA-p129" />
* Midnight and Cyric ascended to godhood on Marpenoth 15.<ref name="TFRA-p129" />

==Deaths, Ascensions, and Resurrections==
===Deaths===
The Time of Troubles was a time of significant turnover among the gods of the [[Faerûnian pantheon]].<ref name="FRCS-p265"/> Several deities were "destroyed" (see below) during this period. The following are deities who were killed or incapacitated during the Time of Troubles:
* [[Midsummer]]: [[Mystra]], greater goddess of [[magic]] and among the most powerful of the gods, was destroyed<ref name="FRCS-p264"/> when she attempted to bypass Helm at the [[Celestial Stairway]] and he slew her.<ref name="Sd-p141">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|141-151|7}}</ref><ref name="TFRA-p128">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Atlas|128}}</ref>
* [[Eleasis 13]]: [[Bane]], greater god of tyranny, strife, and hatred, a member of the [[Dead Three]], was slain in a climactic battle with the [[demigod]] [[Torm]] in battle outside of [[Tantras]].<ref name="FRCS-p264"/><ref name="TFRA-p128" />
* Eleasis 13: [[Torm]], demigod and patron deity of paladins, was annihilated by Bane with his dying breath. He was later resurrected by Ao because he died fulfilling the obligations of his portfolio.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/><ref name="TFRA-p128" />
* [[Eleint 16]]: [[Bhaal]], intermediate god of murder, was slain by the young mortal [[Cyric]] with the sword ''[[Godsbane]]''.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/><ref name="TFRA-p128" /><ref name="F&A-p45">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|45}}</ref> What remained of his essence was absorbed by the [[Winding Water]].<ref name="F&A-p45" /> However, Bhaal had foreseen his own death, and had populated Faerûn with his progeny, the ''[[Bhaalspawn]]'', in a bid to resurrect himself.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/>
* [[Tiamat]]'s three-headed incarnation was slain by [[Gilgeam]]. Her essence was divided among three [[dragon]]s, the [[Red dragon|red]] [[Tchazzar]], the [[Blue dragon|blue]] [[Gestaniius]], and the [[Green dragon|green]] [[Skuthosiin]]. Tchazzar consumed the other two and was subsumed by Tiamat. The Dragon Queen then destroyed Gilgeam late in the Godswar.<ref name="F&Pp108">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|108}}</ref>
* [[Gilgeam]], demigod and king of [[Unther]], was slain by Tiamat.<ref name="FRCS-p265"/>
* [[Herne]], a corrupted avatar of the [[Master of the Hunt]] worshiped in the [[High Forest]], was hunted and killed by Malar who then subsumed his worship.<ref name="F&A-p105">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|105}}</ref>
* [[Ibrandul]], a lesser god of caverns, was destroyed by [[Shar]], who then masqueraded as the dead deity.<ref name="FaithsNAvatarsp72">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|72}}</ref>
* [[Moander]], demigod of decay, was slain by [[Finder Wyvernspur]].<ref name="F&Pp94">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|94}}</ref>
* [[Ramman]], Untheric lesser god of war and storms, was slain by [[Hoar]]<ref name="FRCS-p264"/><ref name="AHoU">{{Cite web/Books/The Alabaster Staff: A History of Unther}}</ref> by having one of his thunderous lightning attacks repelled back at him. But before Hoar could steal his portfolio, Ramman transferred it to [[Anhur]] of the [[Mulhorandi pantheon]].<ref name="AHoU" />
* [[Waukeen]], lesser goddess of wealth, attempted to reclaim her divinity during the Time of Troubles by leaving her portfolio in the care of [[Lliira]] and dealing with the demon prince [[Graz'zt]] but was captured by him and imprisoned in the [[Argent Palace]] in the [[Abyss]] for more than a decade.<ref name="F&Pp87">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|87}}</ref>
*The dragon goddess of magic, [[Kereska]], entirely subsumed the worship and portfolio of [[Kalzareinad]], a demigod of dragon magic. Kalzareinad died shortly thereafter.<ref name="CotD">{{Cite book/Cult of the Dragon|120}}</ref>
* [[Marpenoth 15]]: [[Myrkul]], greater god of the dead and another of the Dead Three, was killed in a duel with [[Midnight]], a mortal woman wielding the powers of Mystra, in the skies over the city of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="Waterdeep">{{Cite book/Waterdeep (1989)|?}}</ref><ref name="TFRA-p129">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Atlas|129}}</ref>

===Ascensions===
{{quote|Gods have ascended through far stranger means – a circle drawn on a globe, a walk in a swamp, the destruction of a [[Finder's Stone|magical stone]]. Why not with a [[Crown of Karsus|symbol]] of such innate authority?|[[Gale]] of [[Waterdeep]]<ref name="bg3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}<br/>
Furthermore, a selection of mortals were chosen by Lord Ao to ascend to the heavens to fill the void left by those deities who died:
* [[Cyric]], a petty, sadistic mercenary, slew Bhaal with the sword ''[[Godsbane]]'' (actually the god [[Mask]] in disguise). After the end of the Time of Troubles, he was granted control of nearly all the portfolios of the Dead Three by Ao, making him briefly the most powerful of the gods.<ref name="FRCS-p240">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|240}}</ref><ref name="FRCS-p264"/>
* [[Kelemvor]], a sullen adventurer and companion of Cyric and Midnight, was killed by Cyric with ''Godsbane'', but his soul was hidden by Mask<ref name="FRCS-p264"/> and unintentionally gained the portfolio of death by defeating Cyric with the help of ''Godsbane'' in the {{yearlinkName|1368}}, ten years after the Time of Troubles.<ref name="PrinceofLies">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|?}}</ref> He then strove to change the horrifying image of death promoted by his predecessors.<ref name="FRCS-p243">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|243}}</ref>
* After her death, the goddess Mystra entrusted her essence with the young mage [[Midnight]], who ascended after the Time of Troubles as the new Mystra.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/>
* The exceptional adventurer [[Finder Wyvernspur]] absorbed and adapted Moander's portfolio, making it the cycle of life.<ref name="F&Pp93">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|93}}</ref>

Finally, Lord Ao lifted the barrier that prevented the [[Mulhorand]]i god-kings from reuniting with their divine selves on the [[Outer Planes]]. The physical incarnations of the Mulhorandi gods departed Faerûn and left governance of the empire to mortal rulers under their guidance.<ref name="P&P-p96">{{Cite book/Powers & Pantheons|96}}</ref>

===Resurrections===
However, death is anything but permanent in the Realms, and many of these deities did in some way circumvent their own destruction:
* After Bane's death, his portfolio was divided among the newly anointed god Cyric and Bane's own half-demonic son [[Iyachtu Xvim]]. Upon the death of one of the greatest forces of evil in existence, all of Faerûn breathed a sigh of relief; however, in [[1372 DR]], Bane was resurrected, destroying Iyachtu Xvim and reestablishing his church.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/>
* Bhaal had already foreseen his own demise and had populated the world with scores of his own progeny in previous years, all as part of a grand scheme for his own reincarnation.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/>
* Myrkul infused the sinister ''[[Crown of Horns]]'' with the remnants of his essence, and teleported it away. While its location was unknown, the semi-sentient [[artifact]] presumably was fomenting a plan for Myrkul's resurrection.<ref name="MF-p171">{{Cite book/Magic of Faerûn|171}}</ref> He also left behind a curse called the [[Akachi|Spirit-eater]], actually the half-destroyed soul of one of his [[Chosen]].<ref>{{Cite game/Neverwinter Nights 2: Mask of the Betrayer}}</ref>
* Though Ao had decreed that none of the gods fallen during the Time of Troubles should be reinstated, a complex convergence of factors regarding Torm's death led the overgod to make a single exception in his case, resurrecting him and elevating him to the status of lesser god.<ref name="Waterdeep" />
* After a decade of isolation from her followers, Waukeen was freed from her prison in [[1371 DR]] by a band of adventurers, and resumed her place in the heavens.<ref name="F&Pp87"/>

==Lasting effects==
* [[Wild magic|Wild]] and [[Dead-magic zone]]s, areas where magic behaved erratically or ceased to function, were lasting effects of the Time of Troubles.<ref name="CaN-p287">{{Cite book/Cormyr: A Novel/Paperback 1998|287}}</ref> Some referred to them as "The Legacy of Helm."<ref name="FRA-p21">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|21}}</ref>
* Public opinion on the [[Church of Helm]] significantly worsened in [[the North]], owing to the havoc wreaked there. Direct worship of the faith decreased, while persecution of its followers increased.<ref name="FRA-p21" /><ref>{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|69}}</ref>
* Mortals became aware of Ao's existence, leading to the development of many short-lived cults, with the longest-lasting being the [[Cult of Ao]] in Waterdeep and their temple [[Cynosure (building)|Cynosure]].<ref>{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|30}}</ref><ref name="CoSW-p96">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|96}}</ref>
* The stricture that all [[dwarven]] priests must be the same gender as their god was relaxed.<ref>{{Cite book/Demihuman Deities|42}}</ref>
* With the Mulhorandi pantheon reunited with their divine essence on the Outer Planes, their mortal incarnations or "lesser avatars" lost their innate divinity, leaving [[Horustep III]] as the first truly mortal [[pharaoh]] of Mulhorand.<ref name="P&P-p96" />
* Because of the destruction of the Tablets of Fate, the planar barrier that separated the worlds of [[Abeir]] and Toril weakened, and both worlds began to exchange small, almost imperceptible parts of their lands between them.<ref name="FRCG-p50-51">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|50-51}}</ref>
* Some denizens of the Outer Planes theorized that the gods of Realmspace became interested in becoming multi-spheric because of the heavy-handed methods used by Ao during the Time of Troubles.<ref name="OHG-p168">{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|168}}</ref>
* The amount of accessible [[incarnum]] increased dramatically throughout the Realms.<ref name="CC.I">{{Cite web/Class Chronicles: Incarnum}}</ref>
* The Winding Water became poisoned by the absorbed essence of Bhaal, turning jet-black and foul-smelling. The taint spanned from [[Boareskyr Bridge]] to [[Trollclaw Ford]].<ref name="F&A-p45" />
