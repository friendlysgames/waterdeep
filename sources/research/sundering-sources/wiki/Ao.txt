Lord '''Ao''' ({{Pronounce|AY|oh|refs=<ref name="F&A-p30" />}}) was the [[Overgod]] of the worlds of [[Abeir]]-[[Toril]] and the [[Realms]]ian embodiment of [[the Balance]], exhibiting no extremes. As Overgod, all [[Deity|deities]] and [[primordial]]s of Abeir and Toril,<ref name="F&P-p4"/><ref name="ADDc-20">{{Cite comic/Dark of the Moon|6,9}}</ref><ref name="Youtube">{{Cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eAoq-vwWHHg|title=What is the Sundering? (Part 1)|accessmonthday=September 7th|accessyear=2017|author=[[Ed Greenwood]],  [[Erin M. Evans]], [[Paul S. Kemp]], [[R.A. Salvatore]], [[Richard Lee Byers]], [[Troy Denning]], [[James Wyatt]] |date=August 21th, 2012}}</ref><ref name="WD40">{{Cite book/Waterdeep (adventure)|40-41}}</ref>—including those active on other [[Crystal sphere|spheres]] and [[Cosmology|planes]], such as [[Lolth]] and [[Tyr]]—were subject to him. To be more precise, only aspects of gods directly connected with Abeir-Toril fell under his authority.<ref name="F&P-p4"/><ref name="F&A-p14-16">{{Cite book/Faiths and Avatars|14-16}}</ref>

Without the '''Hidden One'''<nowiki>'s</nowiki> involvement in the [[Time of Troubles]], he would likely have remained unknown to the mortals of [[Faerûn]].<ref name="F&P-p4"/> 

{{Quote
|Know ye that there is one god above all the gods of Faerun, a being known as Ao, the One Who Is Hidden. Ao it was, so we are told, who created the gods, to bring order out of chaos, so that our world could exist, each beast in its place and each plant in its niche, all in harmony. Standard talk, I know, but evidently true.<ref name="WD13">{{Cite book/Waterdeep (adventure)|13}}</ref>

==Description==
Ao only took physical form once, during the Time of Troubles. In that instance, he took the form of a cryptic and incredibly imposing being, towering over {{SI|12|ft|long=yes}} tall. Otherwise however, the Overpower's appearance reflected his nature as a being of balance. He was  ageless (neither young nor old), and had a beard and hair of purest white with an absolutely average visage, even and symmetrical, neither pleasant nor unpleasant. He wore a black robe dotted by millions of stars and moons, arranged in a not-quite-perceptible pattern but which had a beautiful and harmonious feel.<ref name="F&A-p30"/><ref name="WD40" /> Otherwise, he was envisioned as a very literal beard in the sky, with only a face and hands.<ref name="ADDc-20"/>

==Personality==
{{fq
|text= Ao would see that which sets us all apart from the beasts: the magnificence of deed and achievement, love and friendship and aiding one another-that which soars above mere survival. And so, of course, ye come into the picture.
|source= Elminster<ref name="WD13" />
| width  = 300px
}}

Ao held himself aloof from most events as an eternal, rarely-interfering observer.<ref name="F&A-p23" /> He didn't care what the deities or primordials were doing as long as they upheld their individual [[portfolio]]s and did not completely ignore their worshipers,<ref name="F&P-p4"/> and did not care to be challenged on his verdicts.<ref name="WD40" /><ref name="SCAG-40" /> When a deity did something that wasn't accounted for by their portfolio, Ao would punish that deity, and such punishments were usually harsh.<ref name="OHG-p168"/>  He did not see every divine role as beneficial to its bearer, and indeed might even grant godhood as a form of penalty.<ref name="WD40" /> He cared even less about the day-to-day goings-on of mortals<ref name="F&A-p30"/> and some theorized that he didn't want to be known by them.<ref name="F&P-p4"/>

Despite his deliberate indifference, Ao was not emotionless. Even he was known to smile upon exceptional oration, and could be impressed by a great act of self-sacrifice,<ref name="FaP-74">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|74}}</ref> being known to bring back deities when they died in service to their ethos.<ref name="FA-p162">{{cite book/Faiths and Avatars|162-163}}</ref> In contrast, he was angered by petty behavior and pointless demands for self-destructive displays from mortals for their own aggrandizement.<ref name="Youtube" /><ref name="WD40" /><ref name="WD2">{{Cite book/Waterdeep (adventure)|2}}</ref>

It was unclear if Ao was omniscient, with everything that occurred being according to his grand designs, if his plans merely followed the general trajectory he aimed for, or if he could be outright mistaken, if his plans could do more harm than good and the Overgod would be compelled to change his mind.<ref name="Youtube" />

==Powers==
Theoretically, Ao could do anything he wanted.<ref name="F&A-p30"/> He was often credited as being the creator of the cosmos,<ref name="FRCG4e-74"/> and once separated [[Abeir-Toril]] into their specific worlds.<ref name="Youtube"/> He existed beyond concepts such as [[alignment]]<ref name="FRCG4e-74"/> and [[divine rank]],<ref name="F&A-p30"/> and was more powerful than all the gods and primordials of Abeir-Toril combined.<ref name="F&A-p30"/><ref name="OHG-p168"/><ref name="F&A-p15">{{Cite book/Faiths and Avatars|15}}</ref> He could damage deities permanently,<ref name="SCAG-40"/> demote them at will, and had the power to create new gods from sentient beings (if they accepted the responsibilities of godhood).<ref name="F&A-p30"/>

In addition, unlike the gods under him, Ao did not need the worship of mortals, nor did he desire it,<ref name="F&A-p30"/> whereas those "normal" gods who did not receive the worship of mortals could die from lack of it.<ref name="F&A-p14">{{Cite book/Faiths and Avatars|14}}</ref> Ao initiated this after the Time of Troubles in order to enforce his will that the gods act as guardians of [[the Balance]], rather than kings of mortals.<ref name="OHG-p168"/><ref name="Youtube"/>

===Weaknesses===
Ao's powers were also confined to Realmspace, and he could not control or influence anything outside it.<ref name="OHG-p160"/> For gods that had divine bonds to dimensions or crystal spheres beyond Realmspace, he could only sever their connections to his [[crystal sphere|sphere]].<ref name="F&A-p15"/> Ao was not necessarily invulnerable or undefeatable within Realmspace either; other overpowers potentially existed within the world of Toril, though it was unclear if these were merely other versions of him, and if they were not, what relations they had with each other.<ref name="FRCS2e-p61">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|61}}</ref>

Though Ao was considered to be more powerful than all the other gods in Toril, able to confine them all to avatar-like forms,<ref name="F&A-p15" /> it was not impossible for his will to be opposed. The restrictions he placed during the Avatar Crisis (whether or not he could place harsher ones) applied only to the incarnations of divinity rather than the individuals that held them, allowing [[Waukeen]] to escape with the help of [[Celestian]], a non-Realmsian god of interplanar travel (which Ao may or may not have known).<ref name="FDND-p4">{{Cite book/For Duty & Deity|4}}</ref> Even more pressingly, by some sources, [[Mystra]] was an overgoddess in waiting, and were it in her nature to desire such power, she may have had the capacity to claim such a position.<ref name="EdGreenwood">{{Cite web/Candlekeep forum

Given that overdeities became stronger when more deities were under their dominion, the outright decrease of gods would not be helpful to him. His punishment during the Time of Troubles might have played a part in the Torilian gods seeking to expand into the [[Outer Plane]]s,<ref name="OHG-p160">{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|160}}</ref><ref name="OHG-p168"/> and it was not uncommon (if incredibly risky) for gods that suffered under despotic overgods to simply abandon their area of control all together.<ref name="OHG-p168" />

==Activities==
{{fq
|text= Keepers of the Balance, you have summoned me needlessly.... Cyric is Lord of Murder, so he should strive to blot out even the lives of gods. [[Mask]] is Lord of Intrigue, so he should strive to conceal such deeds. It is your responsibility to stand against Cyric—just as it is his to destroy you if you fail. Such is the way of the Balance. 
|source= Lord Ao speaking to the [[Circle of Greater Powers]]<ref name="PoL-p62-63">{{Cite book/Prince of Lies|62-63}}</ref>
| width  = 300px
}}

Despite their great might, overpowers acted less like grand puppeteers and more as supervisors.<ref name="OHG-p38">{{Cite book/On Hallowed Ground|38}}</ref> Ao existed both to ensure the deities within his purview abided by the rules of the cosmos<ref name="OHG-p168"/> and to protect the Realms from influences beyond its cosmos. He had to report such developments to a "[[luminous being]]" that existed beyond the normal [[cosmology]].<ref name="F&A-p30"/><ref name="Wat-p331">{{Cite book/Waterdeep|345}}</ref>

Another of his functions was to decide which interloper deities were allowed in the sphere of Abeir-Toril and which mortals could be raised to godhood. If Ao didn't allow it, a being could not ascend to divinity, and an interloper deity could not enter the sphere or affect it in any way, regardless of how powerful it was.<ref name="F&A-p4">{{Cite book/Faiths and Avatars|4}}</ref><ref name="F&A-p15.16">{{Cite book/Faiths and Avatars|15-16}}</ref>  He was also able to decide which dead gods—gods that had lost their connection with the sphere of Abeir-Toril—were to be revived or returned to the sphere, and allow such development if he deemed it necessary.<ref name="F&A-p30">{{Cite book/Faiths and Avatars|30}}</ref>

Ao established rules concerning the management of the divine.<ref>{{cite web|url=|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20020625093618/http://www.geocities.com:80/TimesSquare/Castle/2566/eb-pantheons.htm|archivedate=2002-06-25|author=[[Eric L. Boyd]]|title=Eric Boyd on Pantheons of the Realms|accessdate=2010-09-27}}</ref> For instance:
* No two gods in the same pantheon could have identical portfolios.
* When two gods clashed, one of three results occurred:
** One god faded from the Realms.
** Both gods merged.
** One (or both) god(s) altered their portfolio(s) sufficiently that both could remain in or join the [[Faerûnian pantheon]].

These rules were more problematic than they were worth, because they encouraged the gods to battle among themselves for supremacy.<ref name="F&P-p4"/> During the [[Second Sundering]], Ao discarded such rules, reassigned portfolios, and created more flexible rules.<ref name="Youtube"/><ref name="SCAG-19">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|19-20}}</ref>

==Relationships==
{{fq
|text= Do not thank me, evil [[Cyric]]. Being given the role of God of Strife and Death is no gift. It is eternal punishment. At first, you will enjoy it, for it is the only thing you are suited to. But you will find it wearisome in the end. The freedom you seek lies not with godly power. You serve me now... and your worshipers.
|source= Ao to Cyric.<ref name="Youtube"/>
| width  = 300px
}}

Despite having power over all the gods of Toril and having to report the world's developments to a "[[luminous being]]",<ref name="F&A-p30"/><ref name="Wat-p331" /> Ao did not truly serve anyone, nor was he truly served.<ref name="FRCG4e-74" />

Torm's martyrdom in slaying [[Bane]] was such that even Ao was impressed.<ref name="FaP-74" /> Ao punished Tyr with blindness partially for allowing discord among Toril's gods.<ref name="FaP-79">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|79}}</ref>

==Worshipers==
{{Main|Cult of Ao}}
The cult of Ao was led by "ministers" instead of [[clerics]], as these cultists never received any [[spell]]s from the Overgod. The cult was more philosophical than religious in nature.<ref name="F&A-p30"/>

Among the known cults of Ao were a [[Cynosure (building)|cult in Waterdeep]], and another in [[Zazesspur]], [[Tethyr]]. This cult was remarkable for the fact that its ministers could cast [[Divine magic|divine]] spells, but in the end it was revealed that those individuals received their divine powers from [[Cyric]] and not from Ao.<ref name="F&A-p30"/>

The gods of the established faiths of Faerûn informed their [[priest]]s about the fact that Ao did not interact with mortals. Because of this, members of other faiths neither feared nor spoke out against the cults of Ao.<ref name="F&A-p30"/>

==History==
[[File:Minister of Ao.png|thumb|''A minister of Ao in a ceremonial garb of the church.'']]
===Creation of the World===
There were no exact accounts on how he created the universe, but nonetheless he was credited for the deed.<ref name="FRCG4e-74"/> In some traditions, Ao just created the sphere that covered [[Realmspace]], and the goddesses [[Selûne]] and [[Shar]], and they later went to create all the worlds and stars, and other heavenly bodies.<ref name="FRCS-p260">{{Cite book/FRCS|260}}</ref><ref name="F&A-p141">{{Cite book/Faiths and Avatars|141}}</ref> In other accounts, it was Ao who directly created all that exists, not only Realmspace, but also the worlds and heavenly bodies, and even the [[Astral Sea]], using the raw energy of the [[phlogiston]].<ref name="FRCG4e-42">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|42}}</ref> Ao himself claimed to be the creator of the gods, though not of the Realms themselves.<ref name="FRA">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|37}}</ref>

===The Tearfall===
During the [[Days of Thunder]], when the [[batrachi]] were losing their war against the [[titan]]s, they performed a powerful summoning [[ritual]] and released several primordials from their ancient prisons. The gods quickly moved against their ancient foes, and those battles caused the worldwide catastrophes that destroyed the batrachi civilization. A primordial known as "[[Asgorath]] the World-Shaper", determined to destroy the world if she couldn't control it, threw an ice moon at the planet, creating the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]]. This event was known as the [[Tearfall]].<ref name="FRCG4e-31"/> 

Before the world was completely destroyed, Lord Ao intervened and sundered the original world of [[Abeir-Toril]] into two twin worlds, Abeir and Toril, giving the former to the primordials and the latter (the original world) to the gods, ending the conflict.<ref name="FRCG4e-31">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|31}}</ref> The [[Tablets of Fate]] were created by Ao after the Tearfall as a way to maintain the new worlds of Abeir and Toril apart, and to ensure a balance between the forces of Law and Chaos, as well as between the gods and the primordials.<ref name="Youtube"/>

===The Mulhorandi and Untheric pantheons===
When the [[Imaskar]]i captured the ancestors of the [[Mulan]] peoples from another [[Earth|world]] and brought them to Toril, Ao contacted the ancient being [[Ptah]], and invited the [[Mulhorandi pantheon|Mulhorandi]] and [[Untheric pantheon|Untheric]] gods to manifest in Toril. With help and permission from Ao, these deities were able to follow their worshipers and end their servitude.<ref name="FRCS-p185">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|185}}</ref><ref name="GHotR-p31">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|31}}</ref>

===The Time of Troubles===

{{fq
|text= I created gods to serve the Balance, not to twist it to your own ends. But this point was lost on you. You saw the tablets as a set of rules by which to play juvenile games of prestige and pomp. Then when the rules became inconvenient, you stole them.
|source= Ao regarding his stolen tablets.<ref name="Youtube"/>
| width  = 300px
}}

In [[1358 DR]],<ref name="GHoTR-p142">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|142, 144}}</ref> the gods [[Bane]] and [[Myrkul]] stole the Tablets of Fate from Ao and hid them in Faerûn,<ref name="F&A-p23">{{Cite book/Faiths and Avatars|23}}</ref> wrongly suspecting that some of the Overgod's power was derived from these tablets.<ref name="Shad-p86">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|68-70}}</ref> When Ao discovered the Tablets of Fate were missing he summoned all the deities and asked for those guilty to hand them over. When no one stood forward to admit to stealing the Tablets of Fate, Ao cast down all the gods from the heavens, taking their divine power in the process. He stated the reason for this was primarily that he was displeased with the gods treating the world as if it was for their own amusement, rather than the purpose they were created, though the theft of the Tablets were part of it. Ao tasked [[Helm]] with guarding the [[Celestial Stairway]]s that would lead the deities back into their divine realms.<ref>{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|4-5,8|0}}</ref>

[[Luna]], the [[avatar]] of [[Selûne]], who'd already been dwelling in [[Waterdeep]] as a mortal before the other gods were cast down and had missed these events, believed Ao responsible for the gods' turmoil. She informed her friend [[Kyriani]] of the One Who Is Hidden, but no more than that. Somehow, [[Khelben Arunsun]] knew more about Ao and he explained to Kyriani that Ao had brought order out of chaos so that the world could exist and had assigned the gods their parts in the balance of nature, which were inscribed upon the Tablets of Fate. However, known to few mortals, the gods had come to neglect their duties and fallen out of favor with Ao, leading to dissension and chaos.<ref name="ADDc-20"/> Later, Luna sighted the Celestial Stairway rising from [[Mount Waterdeep]] and showed it to her friends, declaring it meant someone had or was about to cross between the mortal realm and the [[planes]] and the realms of the gods. She wondered if Lord Ao had put it there as part of a plan.<ref name="ADDc-23">{{Cite comic/Total Eclipse|15-16}}</ref>

After valiant heroes (among them the mortals [[Cyric]], [[Kelemvor]], and [[Mystra (Midnight)|Midnight]]) recovered the Tablets of Fate and returned them to Ao, the Overgod himself destroyed the Tablets of Fate, grinding them into powder. Though the Tablets had been returned, by then, as Ao had seen it, the gods had proven they were indifferent to their actual duties. This act however, while meant to teach them a lesson,<ref name="Wat-p331"/><ref name="Youtube"/> unraveled the laws of Realmspace, beginning the chaotic [[Era of Upheaval]].<ref name="Youtube"/>

Many cults of Ao arose after the Time of Troubles, but disappeared as quickly in the following years.<ref name="F&A-p30"/> By [[1372 DR]], even written records about Ao had disappeared,<ref name="F&P-p4"/> and by [[1479 DR]] all the cults of Ao had vanished from Faerûn altogether.<ref name="FRCG4e-74"/>

===The Second Sundering===
{{fq
|text= Great stories remain to be told in this era but they are not the tales of gods and god-like beings. They are the tales of mortal heroes, taking a stand to preserve the world they love.
|source= The word of Ao by the end of the [[Era of Upheaval]].<ref name="Youtube" />
| width  = 300px
}}

It was not known how long Ao expected the warring of the gods to last, but nonetheless in [[1482 DR]], he began the Second Sundering, as a way to restore the worlds of Toril and Abeir after the ravages of the Spellplague.<ref name="Youtube"/><ref name="Kemp">{{cite web
|url = https://web.archive.org/web/20140327123608/http://paulskemp.com/blog/the-sundering/
|title = The Sundering
|author = [[Paul S. Kemp]]
|date = 2012-08-20
|accessdate = 2016-12-13
}}</ref> During the Second Sundering, Ao recreated and rewrote the Tablets of Fate, inscribing the names and purposes of the gods and primordials he chose to serve in a new, inclusive divine reality.<ref name="Youtube"/>

==Rumors & Legends==
Some believed that the ''[[Shadow of Ao]]'', a powerful [[artifact]] that had the power to split a world in two, was related to the Tearfall. It was believed to be located somewhere in [[Laerakond]].<ref name="FRCG-p210">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|210}}</ref>
