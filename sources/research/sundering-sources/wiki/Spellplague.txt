The '''Spellplague''', called the '''Blue Breath of Change''' by the inhabitants of [[Abeir]],<ref name="FRCG-p200">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|200}}</ref> was a disaster that struck [[Realmspace]] and even the [[planes]] themselves on the date of [[Tarsakh 29]] in the {{YearlinkName|1385}}, and was caused by [[Mystra (Midnight)|Mystra]]'s assassination at the hands of [[Cyric]] and [[Shar]].<ref name="FRCG-p50">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|50}}</ref><ref name="Ud-p64">{{Cite book/Undead|64}}</ref> It continued for a decade, leading to the [[Wailing Years]], during which [[arcane magic]] ceased to function and the planet of [[Toril]] was transformed.<ref name="TWY">{{Cite dragon/362/Countdown to the Realms: Spellplague: The Wailing Years}}</ref>

{{Quote|In the infamous, calamitous year of [[1385 DR]], a conspiracy between the goddess of darkness, Shar, and the god of trickery, Cyric, sought to end Mystra's control over the Weave and influence over the realms by cravenly assassinating her. But instead of merely breaking the goddess of magic's dominance, her death threw the Weave into utter chaos and collapse. Magic spells faltered, or failed entirely. Countless spellcasters were killed or driven insane. A vast storm of blue flame arose from the [[Mhair Jungles]] and swept the continent, churning and reshaping the land, and leaving whole regions devastated.|''[[Magical Histories]], Volume 2: The Spellplague''<ref name="BGIII">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

==Omens==
{{Quote|''"The white queen is troubled but can't say why. The black queen hates the white and gives the assassin a black coat. The assassin steals upon the white queen. She can't see him gliding through the shadows. The sword screams. The white queen falls. Her city falls. Stones fall in the cavern to crush the soothsayer. The tree burns and thrashes in agony. Branches break. Branches twist and grow together..."''|A vision of the Spellplague from the [[diviner]] [[Yaphyll]]<ref name="Ud-p7">{{Cite book/Undead|7}}</ref>}}

In her vision, the diviner Yaphyll referred to Mystra as the white queen and to Shar, goddess of the night, as the black queen. Cyric, god of murder, was the assassin;<ref name="Ud-p75">{{Cite book/Undead|75}}</ref> [[Savras]], the god of divination, was the soothsayer,{{speculation}}, and [[Dweomerheart|the city]], cavern and [[World Tree cosmology|tree]] symbolized the ordered structures of magic crumbling into chaos.<ref name="Ud-p75"/>

==Origins==
The most popular and widely accepted theory about the origins of the Spellplague was that this calamity was caused by the death of Mystra, the [[god]]dess of [[Magic]]. For eons, magic had been bound within Mystra's [[Weave]], the universal structure of arcane forces, and her death tore the Weave apart and unleashed the [[raw magic]] contained within it, creating the cataclysm that was later known as the Spellplague.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/><ref name="NV">{{Cite game/Neverwinter}}</ref>

Other scholars, however, didn't consider this to be a logical explanation. Historically, two earlier incarnations of Mystra had died before only to be reborn, and the Weave maintained its hold over the power of magic for the duration of her deaths. In these scholars' views, the truth would never be fully known to mortals, and perhaps not even to the [[god]]s.<ref name="NV"/><ref name="PoS-p79">{{Cite book/Plague of Spells|79}}</ref>

Around [[1396 DR]], the sentient [[construct]] [[Cynosure (construct)|Cynosure]] theorized that the escape of an entity named the [[Carnis|Traitor]] from its confinement in the dungeons of [[Stardeep]], around the same time Mystra was murdered, may have also influenced the Spellplague's virulent onslaught.<ref name="PoS-p79"/>

Around [[1479 DR]], scholars and sages who studied the [[Neverwinter/Chasm|Chasm]] of [[Neverwinter]], such as [[Rhazzad]], believed the [[aboleth]]s of the [[Abolethic Sovereignty]] may have been the actual creators of the Spellplague, although there were no definite proof.<ref name="NV"/>

==The Plague==
Mystra's death caused the Weave to collapse and, without the Weave, the [[Shadow Weave]] was unable to be maintained and collapsed as well. The breakdown of the Weave was felt by all [[wizard]]s across [[Faerûn]].<ref name="Ud-p64">{{Cite book/Undead|64}}</ref><ref name="FRCG-p50"/> The corrupted madness of Cyric defiled what arcane forces remained from the dissolution of the Weave, resulting in a new magical source of defiling arcane energy in the form of blue flames that destroyed Dweomerheart and continued to spread across the cosmos.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/>

Anything that came into contact with the Spellplague risked being destroyed or warped by its energies. Creatures and individuals risked becoming "[[plaguechanged]]", which entailed massive physical mutations and often a complete loss of sanity, and sometimes bestowed the creature or individual with terrible powers.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/><ref name="NCS-p95">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|95}}</ref> Those who came into contact with weaker versions of the Spellplague were sometimes lucky enough to escape with only a [[spellscar]], which could also bestow potent magical abilities.<ref name="FRCG-p5">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|5}}</ref><ref name="FRCG-p46">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|46}}</ref> Physical places could also be horribly warped by the Spellplague, becoming a [[changeland]].<ref name="FRCG-p46" /><ref name="YotAO">{{Cite dragon/362/Countdown to the Realms: Year of the Ageless One}}</ref>

==The Storm of Blue Fire==
[[File:Spellplague FRCG.jpg|thumb|360px|''A [[Thunderstorm|rider]] flees from the blue flames of the Spellplague.'']]
The Spellplague manifested in Toril as a storm of blue flames in the [[Mhair Jungles]], west of [[Halruaa]], and quickly grew to a massive size.<ref name="TWY" /> Halruaan wizards, having divined the death of Mystra and the destruction of the Weave, were able to use the energy of the blue fire to save most of their kingdom by shifting it into another world, Abeir.<ref name=SCAG>{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|10}}</ref> However, the remaining Halruaan lands in Toril were laid to waste due to the heavy [[wild magic]] activity in the area, in such a catastrophic way that the explosion was felt as far as [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="FRCG-p123">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|123}}</ref> [[Sespech]], the [[Golden Plains]], and the [[Nagalands]] were also affected, and those lands were transformed into an unpredictable and surreal place, dubbed the [[Plaguewrought Lands]].<ref name="FRCG-p50"/> 

The blue flames also infected [[portal]]s and planar [[gate]]s, spreading even further across Toril.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/> Almost every part of Faerûn was affected by the Spellplague and certain areas were eliminated entirely, while others were created anew. Thousands of [[spellcaster]]s were either destroyed or went insane due to the collapse of the Weave after Mystra's death.<ref name="TWY" /> In [[Cormyr]], a third of the [[War Wizards]] were either killed or driven mad. Those who survived lost their ability to use arcane magic.<ref name="TWY" />

In [[1386 DR]],<ref name="TWY" /> the Spellplague traveled across time and space, and reached the world of Abeir through the planar gates, and large parts of Abeir and Toril switched places (small, almost imperceptible parts had been switching places since the [[Time of Troubles]] but this event was markedly more noticeable).<ref name="FRCG-p50-51">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|50-51}}</ref> In Abeir, the Spellplague first appeared in [[Gontal]], the southwestern region of the continent of [[Laerakond]], where it made contact with ancient [[Glaur]], the fortress of the [[Primordial]] [[Nehushta]].<ref name="HotECp-33">{{Cite book/Heroes of the Elemental Chaos|33}}</ref><ref name="Dra375">{{Cite dragon/375/Gontal: Dominions of Nehu|78}}</ref> The fortress detonated when the "Blue Breath of Change" ran through it, in an explosion as potent as the [[Tearfall]], rocking the entire planet of Abeir and creating the [[Glaur Barrens]] where the old fortress once stood.<ref name="Dra375"/> Laerakond was torn apart in a powerful earthquake, and a portion of the nation of [[Skelkor]], where the capital of the [[dragonborn]] nation of [[Tymanchebar]] was located, was torn asunder and disappeared amid the blue fire.<ref name="FRCG-p212">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|212}}</ref>

That region, where the city-citadel of [[Djerad Thymar]], was located, crashed on [[Unther]], still enveloped in the blue flames. Many believed the Untherites and the Mulhorandi occupation forces were obliterated in the explosion that leveled and reshaped the whole region except for the [[Black Ash Plain]],<ref name="FRCG186">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|186}}</ref> but actually Unther was instead transported to the world of Abeir <ref name="SCAG2">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|13-14}}</ref> to the lands of [[Shyr]], the equivalent continent to Faerûn in Abeir.<ref name="TDYK-54">{{Cite book/The Devil You Know|54}}</ref>

The rest of Laerakond was sent to the [[Trackless Sea]], where it was believed it subsumed the lands of [[Maztica]].<ref name="FRCG-p87-200"/> Actually, Maztica was transported to Abeir,<ref name="D+11">{{Cite dragon+/11/Lore You Should Know: Tabaxi vs. tabaxi}}</ref> and Laerakond, which became known as 'Returned Abeir', remained on Toril, in a different location in the [[Great Sea]].<ref name="FRCG-p87-200">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|87, 200, 201}}</ref>

==Effects==
===Effects on magic===
* Despite efforts, with the structure of magic out of balance many spells failed<ref name="Ud-p69">{{Cite book/Undead|69}}</ref> or produced unreliable results.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/>
* Some spell effects became much more difficult to produce such that they could only be achieved with elaborate [[ritual]]s. Meanwhile, some other spell effects became so trivial to produce that spellcasters could manifest them at will.<ref name="D&D.N">{{Cite game/Neverwinter}}</ref>
* The [[Wards of Silverymoon]] were shattered.<ref name="FRCG-p53">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|53}}</ref>

===Effects on the Gods===
* [[Savras]] died as a result of the dissolution of Mystra's home plane, Dweomerheart.<ref name="TWY" />
* [[Azuth]] was thrown from Dweomerheart to the [[Nine Hells]], where [[Asmodeus]] stole his divine essence, fused with Azuth, and became a god.<ref name="FRCG-p73">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|73}}</ref><ref name="AotT-398-399">{{Cite book/Ashes of the Tyrant/Hardcover|398-399}}</ref>
* Cyric was tried by a conclave formed of [[Lathander]], [[Sune]], and [[Tyr]]. They found him guilty of Mystra's murder and sentenced him to imprisonment in the [[Supreme Throne]].<ref name="FRCG-p74">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|74}}</ref>
* Lathander transformed back into [[Amaunator]].<ref name="FRCG-p72">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|72}}</ref>
* [[Malar]] was weakened and was said to have been slain by his own beasts.<ref name="RoS-ch2-3">{{Cite digital book/The Rose of Sarifal/Kindle||2-3}}</ref>
* [[Nobanion]] tried to save a pride of lions in [[Gulthandor]] from oncoming blue fire. He came in contact with the blue fire, and it mutated him into a dark and bestial lion with a mane of blue flame.<ref>{{Cite book/The Reaver (novel)|206–8}}</ref>
* [[Talos]] was believed to be one of several gods who was severely weakened after the events of the Spellplague,<ref name="RoS-ch3">{{Cite digital book/The Rose of Sarifal/Kindle||3}}</ref> and in an event known as the [[Stormstar Requiem]], he was said to have revealed himself to be the orcish god Gruumsh, which led to his expulsion from the ranks of the Gods of Fury.<ref name="Drag367">{{Cite dragon/367/Hall of the Frostmaiden|61}}</ref>

===Effects on geography===
* The Spellplague affected the landscape, making the ground rumble and heave up and down like the surface of the sea.<ref name="Ud-p64">{{Cite book/Undead|64}}</ref> Curtains of blue flame swept the landscape, reshaping the land by cutting crevasses or lifting and sculpting the plain into hills and ridges.<ref name="Ud-p72">{{Cite book/Undead|72}}</ref> Shards of earth wrenched themselves free and became [[earthmote]]s.<ref name="FRCG-p52">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|52}}</ref> Such areas changed by the Spellplague came to be known as "changelands".<ref name="FRCG-p46" /><ref name="YotAO" />
* Rifts opened in the [[Underdark]] beneath the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]], causing the level of water to drop, cutting many ports off from their livelihood and creating the [[Underchasm]].<ref name="FRCG-p50"/>
* Southeast [[Chessenta]] was obliterated by the creation of the Underchasm.<ref name="FRCG100">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|100}}</ref> Chessenta survived and prospered - though its beloved ruler [[Tchazzar]] disappeared without a trace.<ref name="The Captive Flame">{{Cite book/The Captive Flame|100-102}}</ref>
* A portion of [[Shyr]] violently changed places with large sections of [[Chondath]] and western Chessenta. Displaced [[genasi]] quickly set about creating a kingdom of their own, [[Akanûl]].<ref name="TWY" />
* [[Mulhorand]] was completely destroyed, and the [[Mulhorandi pantheon]] disappeared. The land was later settled by [[Deep Imaskari]] and became the empire of [[High Imaskar]].<ref name="FRCG138">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|138}}</ref>
* [[Chult]] became a large island when most of the [[Chultan Peninsula]] was cut off from the mainland.<ref name="FRCGMap" /> Much of [[Samarach]] was drowned, its survivors spellscarred, and the [[yuan-ti]] realm of Serpentes fell.<ref name="FRCG102-103">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|102-103}}</ref> To save the city of [[Mezro]] from destruction, [[Ubtao]]'s [[bara|barae]] created a [[demiplane]] and transported their city and all its citizens there. They left behind artificial ruins occupied by [[undead]] to discourage people to find what really happened to Mezro.<ref name=ToA-p74>{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|74}}</ref> According to the bara [[Alisanda Rayburton]] and the [[naga]] [[Saja N'baza]], Mezro would not return to Toril as for as long [[Ras Nsi]] was alive.<ref name=ToA-p74/><ref name=ToA-p83>{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|83}}</ref> Likewise, the Chultan jungle became home to massive monsters from Abeir.<ref name="FRCG102-103"/>
* The peninsula known as [[Var the Golden]] became a region of the [[Great Sea]] called [[Var the Drowned]].<ref name="FRCGMap" />
* The [[Misty Vale (forest)|Misty Vale]] was destroyed by changes in climate, though the wild elves that occupied it survived. Known as [[Elfharrow]], it was fiercely defended from all intrusions.<ref name="FRCG122-123">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|122-123}}</ref>
* The [[Shaar]] became a wasteland called the Shaar Desolation.<ref name="FRCGMap" />
* The region surrounding the [[Great Rift]] became the Underchasm.<ref name="FRCGMap" /> Bits of what used to be the Underdark began to float above the Underchasm as earthmotes.<ref name="FRCG-p52"/>
* The [[halfling]] realm of [[Luiren]] was submerged underwater, becoming the [[Gulf of Luiren]] in the [[Great Sea]].<ref name="FRCGMap">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|Fold-out Map}}</ref> In the century since that great disaster, the waters receded, and stories told by travelers from the south told of halfling communities that survived as island redoubts.<ref>{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|11}}</ref>
* [[Evermeet]] was pushed into the plane of [[Faerie]], leaving behind a pale shadow of what the island of the elves used to be, though the isle's residents could move back and forth from Faerie to Faerûn at will.<ref name="FRCG132-133">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|132-133}}</ref>
* The island of [[Nimbral]] suddenly and mysteriously disappeared.<ref name="FRCG163">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|163}}</ref>
* Parts of [[Conyberry]] were exchanged with lands from Abeir. The Abeirans survivors were soon accepted by the citizens of Conyberry, and Conyberry became a prosperous village, at least for a time.<ref name="NCS-189">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|189}}</ref>
* The sudden shifting of continental bodies sent tsunamis all along the island and coastal regions of Faerûn, devastating them all. [[Lantan]] was hit harder than most.<ref name="FRCG162">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|162}}</ref> Before the waves reached the island, all the ''[[smokepowder]]'' and magical reagents exploded at the same time, causing great chaos. And as the waves reached the coastline, most of Lantan was engulfed by the blue fire and send to Abeir.<ref name="SCAG71">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|71}}</ref> What remained of Lantan on Toril was entirely flooded, killing the remaining Lantanese and cleaning away their advanced technology.<ref name="FRCG162">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|162}}</ref> 

===Effects on the planes===
* The [[World Tree]] was destroyed.<ref name="TCM">{{Cite book/The Crystal Mountain|14}}</ref>
* Most of the [[Outer Planes]] were either destroyed or merged with others, creating the [[Astral dominion]]s. Entirely new Astral dominions also arose because of the Spellplague.<ref name="FRCG-p62">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|62}}</ref>
* Dweomerheart itself was dissolved.<ref name="TWY" />
* The Spellplague pulled back the [[Feywild]] into Toril's proximity, reopening the paths between the two planes again.<ref name="FRCG-p68">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|68}}</ref>
* With his newfound godly power, Asmodeus took advantage of the planar instability to hurl the [[Abyss]] to the [[Elemental Chaos]] in an attempt to end the [[Blood War]].<ref name="FRCG-p50"/><ref name="FRCG-p73">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|73}}</ref> Asmodeus failed, however, as throwing the Abyss into the Chaos did not stop the Blood War, although it was stalled by a hundred years.<ref name="FitB-94">{{Cite book/Fire in the Blood/Hardcover 2014|94}}</ref>

===Other effects===
* Waves of azure fires appeared everywhere, killing whatever they touched.<ref name="Ud-p65">{{Cite book/Undead|65}}</ref> Its energies then reanimated some of those it killed, which arose as hungry [[undead]] known as [[plaguechanged ghoul]]s.<ref name="FRCG-p267">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|267}}</ref>
* Humans that were somehow tied to the [[Shadowfell]] were affected by the Spellplague and transformed into a "new race", the [[shadar-kai (Netherese)|shadar-kai]].<ref name="DR391-p14">{{Cite dragon/391/A Legacy in Shadow|14}}</ref> 
* The illusions that cloaked the settlements on [[Selûne (moon)|Selûne]] failed, revealing them to those on Toril.<ref name="TWY" />
* In Neverwinter, although the city was not as hard hit by the Spellplague like other places, its effects were also felt.<ref name="NCS-138">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|138}}</ref> Some buildings were affected by the aberrant magic, such as [[Castle Never]],<ref name="NCS-152">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|152}}</ref> the [[Cloaktower]] (that was sent to the Far Realm<ref name="D&D.N" />), and the [[Fallen Tower]],<ref name="NCS-157">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|157}}</ref> Chunks of earth near and in the Neverwinter's [[Docks District (Neverwinter)|Docks District]] became earthmotes,<ref name="NCS-147">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|147}}</ref> including the land on which the [[Moonstone Mask]] was built.<ref name="NCS-142">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|142}}</ref> Also, a [[Cauldron of Blue Fire|small plagueland]] was formed in the Underdark below Neverwinter.<ref name="NCS-190">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|90}}</ref>
* In [[Waterdeep]], the [[Walking Statues of Waterdeep]] (most resided in the [[Ethereal Plane]]) appeared in the city and began to rampage. The [[Blackstaff]] at the time was unable to control them or banish them back to the Ethereal Plane.<ref>{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|182-184}}</ref>
* The [[Tower of Twilight]], hit by the azure flames, was expelled from the normal timeline and vanished from reality. It did not return to Faerûn until after the [[Ruining|eruption]] of [[Mount Hotenow]], in [[1451 DR]].<ref name="NCS-175">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|175}}</ref>
* In [[Leilon]], the monsters that were forced to guard the [[High Tower of Thalivar]] and its secrets were released from their bonds to terrorize the city. The magic inside was redirected outward into the town, paralyzing anyone who gazed at it where they stood.<ref name="NWCS-10">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|10}}</ref>

==Aftermath==
The Spellplague led to political upheaval across the land that resulted in many attempted coups, invasions, and uprisings, especially against governments and nations that had relied heavily on arcane magic for defense or controlling their population.<ref name="YotAO" />

By [[1395 DR]], the majority of the effects of the Spellplague had come to an end and most arcane magic had returned to a semblance of normality, with a handful of major changes to the way spells are cast.<ref name="TWY" /> The Empire of [[Netheril]] came across the [[Lost Vale]] and displaced the [[saurials]] there into the Underdark.<ref name="FRCG112">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|112}}</ref>

The number of people gifted with [[psionics]] increased exponentially on Toril after the Spellplague. Some scholars believed this was a reaction against the Far Realm-defiling energy of the Spellplague.<ref name="PsP-p110">{{Cite book/Psionic Power|110}}</ref>

By 1479 DR, areas of [[Toril]] still affected by the Spellplague were referred to as [[Plaguelands]].<ref name="FRCG-p54">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|54}}</ref> In Neverwinter, victims of the Spellplague were expelled from the city to live in [[Helm's Hold]],<ref name="NCS-p165">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|165}}</ref> where those with severe mental or physical afflictions were sequestered in a terrible [[Sanatorium]].<ref name="NCS-p169">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|169-170}}</ref>

By [[1480 DR]], the Spellplague had ended with the event known as [[Mystra's Return]]. The Weave was resurrected with the goddess and magic became what it was before the Spellplague, though some areas with lingering effects from it remained.<ref name="EE-4836">{{Cite digital book/Elminster Enraged/Kindle|4836}}</ref>

In [[1482 DR]] Lord Ao began the Second [[Second Sundering|Sundering]], as a way to restore the worlds of Toril and Abeir of the ravages of the Spellplague.<ref name="Kemp">{{cite web
