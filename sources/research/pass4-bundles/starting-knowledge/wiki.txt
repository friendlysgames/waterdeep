'''Dalakhar''' was a [[thief]] and spy in [[Waterdeep]] working for Lord [[Dagult Neverember]] in the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="WDH5e-p11">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|11}}</ref>

==History==
In [[1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} Dalakhar was spying upon [[Renaer Neverember]], the estranged son of his master.<ref name="WDH5e-p49">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|49}}</ref> At one point, however, Lord Dagult ordered many of his agents, including Dalakhar, to obtain the ''[[stone of Golorr]]'' from the lair of the [[Xanathar]]. The rock gnome managed to steal the stone, ruining a meeting between Xanathar and the [[Zhentarim]].<ref name="WDH5e-p5">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|5}}</ref>

Later, Dalakhar was targeted by agents of the Zhentarim, the [[Xanathar's Thieves' Guild]], [[Bregan D'aerthe]], and others, making it impossible for him to get out of Waterdeep as planned.<ref name="WDH5e-p43-44" /> For the time being, Dalakhar decided to entrust the stone to some adventurers who had saved Renaer.<ref name="WDH5e-p46" /> However, Dalakhar was ambushed by some Zhentarim led by [[Urstul Floxin]] and hit by a ''[[fireball]]'' launched by the [[nimblewright]] of [[Gralhund|House Gralhund]], killing the rock gnome along with ten more people.<ref name="WDH5e-p43-44">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|43-44}}</ref>

==Personality==
Dalakhar was very loyal to his master, believing Dagult to be the rightful [[Open Lord]] of Waterdeep.<ref name="WDH5e-p46">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|46}}</ref>

The '''''Stone of Golorr''''' was an [[artifact]] that contained the consciousness and memories of an [[aboleth]] named [[Golorr]].<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

==Description==
The artifact was a small and glossy greenish-gray stone that could fit in the palm of a [[humanoid]]'s hand.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

==Powers==
The power of the ''Stone of Golorr'' could be called upon to access the aboleth's memory in a similar way as the ''[[legend lore]]'' spell. This power could be used up to three times, until the ''stone'' recharged at the next dawn.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

However, knowledge given by the aboleth was fleeting. Once a creature broke the attunement to the ''Stone of Golorr'', there was a chance that any knowledge acquired via the stone, and even possession of the stone itself, was forgotten by the user. This magically induced amnesia could sometimes be removed by a ''[[remove curse]]'' spell, but more powerful spells such as ''[[greater restoration]]'' had a better chance of success.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

As the trapped creature inside, the ''Stone of Golorr'' hungered for knowledge and did not like to stay attuned to the same individual for too long. It had an alien personality and saw itself as a [[god]]. If its user ignored the ''stone's'' demands, it could attempt to seize control of the user.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

==History==
The ''Stone of Golorr'' was in fact an aboleth that was transformed into an object. It could be freed if the stone was destroyed via an ''[[antipathy/sympathy]]'' spell cast with the appropriate parameters. If Golorr were freed in this way, it could go berserk and attack any nearby creatures.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

In the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]], the ''Stone of Golorr'' was used to store the knowledge of the location of the [[Vault of Dragons]], which contained a treasure that had been embezzled by [[Dagult Neverember]] shortly before he was ousted as the [[Open Lord]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="WDH5e-p192" /> At some point the stone fell into the hands of [[Xanathar]]. Then in [[1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} Lord Dagult ordered many of his agents to obtain the stone from Xanathar's lair, with one [[Dalakhar]] ultimately being the one to do so while the crime lord was in the middle of a meeting with the [[Zhentarim]].<ref name="WDH5e-p5">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|5}}</ref>

'''Nar'l Xibrindas''' ({{Pronounce|NAR|ul|_|zeh|BRIN|das|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was the [[wizard]] advisor of the [[Xanathar]] in [[Waterdeep]] during the early 1490s [[DR]].<ref name="WDH5e-p211" />
 
==Activities==
Usually Nar'l was in the audience chamber inside the Xanathar's lair.<ref name="WDH5e-p107">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|107}}</ref>

==History==
The family's house was destroyed and only Nar'l and his elder brother [[Soluun Xibrindas|Soluun]] survived. They decided to join [[Bregan D'aerthe]].

In [[1491 DR]], Nar'l was assigned a mission to infiltrate the [[Xanathar's Thieves' Guild]] and get closer to the [[beholder]] crime lord. Nar'l was so successful that he became the only advisor of the Xanathar.<ref name="WDH5e-p211" />

In [[1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} however, Nar'l was worried about the growing paranoia of the beholder, who'd started to suspect something about the drow.<ref name="WDH5e-p211" /> [[Jarlaxle]] too believed that Nar'l was double-crossing Bregan D'aerthe and was willingly working with the beholder.<ref name="WDH5e-p64">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|64}}</ref>

==Personality==
Nar'l was a nervous individual. He always chose his personal safety over that of his allies. He had with him a secret poison to blind the Xanathar.<ref name="WDH5e-p211" /> 

==Relationship==
Nar'l had always at his side a [[grell]] bodyguard assigned by the Xanathar; the grell had secret orders to dispose of the drow if it saw signs of disloyalty.<ref name="WDH5e-p211" />

'''Rogues''' were versatile masters of stealth, nimble combat, and various skillful tricks. Where other [[adventurer]]s had the power to defeat their enemies, rogues used their wits and their natural resourcefulness to exploit their foes' vulnerabilities as well as to lead their allies safely through dangerous environments, disarming [[trap]]s and opening [[lock (item)|locks]] along the way.<ref name="PHB5e-p94">{{Cite book/Player's Handbook 5th edition|94}}</ref>

{{quote|Thieves? Ah, such an ugly word&hellip; think of them instead as kings-in-training. Ye seem upset, even disputatious. Well, then, look upon them as the most honest sort of merchant.|Excerpt from ''[[Shards and Swords]]''<ref name="elm-p62">{{Cite book/Elminster: The Making of a Mage/MMP|62|4}}</ref>}}

==Culture==
Common to most cities in [[Faerûn]], rogues were an incredibly diverse group, including burglars, assassins, and con artists as well as more lawfully employed persons, like [[locksmith]]s, investigators, exterminators, or treasure hunters.<ref name="PHB5e-p94-95">{{Cite book/Player's Handbook 5th edition|94-95}}</ref> To a large extent, the criminal sort of rogue was considered more typical and both [[thieves' guild|thieves']] and [[assassins' guild]]s were found widely throughout the world, some of which were incredibly powerful, such as the [[Shadow Thieves]].<ref name="FRCS3e-p26">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|26}}</ref> Regardless of their specific occupation, rogues shared a common affinity for cunning and daring feats of skill, as well as for getting into things others would rather be left unfound or unopened. {{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

==History==
Renaer was born to Dagult Neverember, who served as the Open Lord of Waterdeep. Renaer's mother died in his youth. Renaer began managing his family's extensive property holdings while his father spent time at the palace.<ref name="Blackstaff Tower">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower}}</ref>

In [[Nightal]] of [[1479 DR]], Renaer was confronted by [[Laraelra Harsard]] about suspicious happenings at a property owned by the [[Neverember]] family.  When he entered the house with Laraelra and her bodyguard [[Meloon Wardragon]] to investigate, they were kicked out by [[Khondar Naomal]], who was using the house with the permission of Dagult Neverember.  Renaer and friends returned at night and found the [[Blackstaff]]'s Heir [[Vajra Safahr]], who had been imprisoned and tortured by Khondar beneath the house in a plot to seize control of the city. The group rescued Vajra and they fled with his companions through a ''[[portal]]'' in [[Neverember Manor]] to [[Varadras]], disused estate outside Waterdeep. Khondar publicly accused Renaer of murdering Vajra.<ref name="Blackstaff Tower" />	

The still semi-lucid Vajra recovered somewhat at the estate and the group used another ''portal'' to travel to Waterdeep where Vajra regained her senses. As Laraelra, Meloon, and [[Osco Salibuck]] tried to head Khondar off, Renaer met with his father, the Open Lord, and threatened to reveal Dagult's involvement in Khondar's actions unless Dagult rescinded the charges against Renaer and his friends and allowed Renaer additional autonomy in the Neverember household, including restoring Neverember Manor to its original name [[Brandarthall]]. Renaer and Dagult returned to [[Blackstaff Tower]] in time to witness Khondar be killed by spectral hounds of the [[Black Hunt]].<ref name="Blackstaff Tower" />

With Vajra's approval, a tenday later Renaer founded a group similar to the [[Moonstars]] to serve as heroes of the city. The first members of this group included Renaer, Vajra, Laraelra, Meloon, and [[Eiruk Weskur]].<ref name="BST-p305">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|305}}</ref>

In [[1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} Renaer was involved in the search of the lost gold hidden by his father. ==Geography==
This criminal and slaver network was one of the most powerful on the Sword Coast, but often unintentionally acted as a buffer against other evil organizations. They operated out of [[Skullport]], but were otherwise active primarily in Waterdeep.<ref name="FRCS-p281"/>

The Xanathar Guild had many connections in [[Luskan]], [[Baldur's Gate]], and the surrounding [[Underdark]], mostly the Underdark north and west of Waterdeep.<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p5">{{Cite dungeon/206/The Xanathar: Crime Lord of Waterdeep|5}}</ref>

==Aims & Goals==
The Xanathar Guild worked to gain financial and political power through its portfolio of spies, mercenaries, slavers, and thieves.<ref name="C&D-p139">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|139}}</ref> They hoped to rule both Skullport and Waterdeep by installing Xanathar spies as Masked Lords in the City of Splendors.<ref name="C&D-p142">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|142}}</ref>

==Organization==
The Xanathar Guild had a loose method of organization due to the instability of its leader, and members inside regularly assassinated one another as desperate attempts to vie for the beholder's favor.<ref name="WDH-p220">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|220}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p17"/> During the reign of [[Xanathar (Zushaxx)|Zushaxx]] in {{Yearlink|1492}}, members could be killed at any time at the whim of the beholder.<ref name="WDH-p100">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|100}}</ref>

===Under Xanathar (original)===
During the original Xanathar's reign, the Guild was often populated by freelance thieves or mercenaries that were hired on a case-by-case basis.<ref name="CoS-WWiW-p88">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|88}}</ref> Xanathar's most senior agents were the Four Councilors, who were each assigned a domain to supervise: mercenaries, bookkeeping, thievery, and blackmail.<ref name="WatN-57"/>

'''Bregan D'aerthe''' ({{Pronounce|BRAY|gan|_|DAIRTH|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was an elite [[drow]] mercenary and later mercantile group founded and led by [[Jarlaxle]], primarily based in [[Menzoberranzan]] in the [[Underdark]].<ref name="H-p?" /> 

==Activities==
Bregan D'aerthe was primarily a mercenary and mercantile band. Members, both individuals and the band as a whole, were available for hire to the highest bidder.<ref name="H-p?" /><ref name="Extinct-p16">{{Cite book/Extinction/Paperback|16}}</ref> Bregan D'aerthe was involved in most conflicts in Menzoberranzan in one way or another.<ref name="RGLoD-p55" /> The band expanded to trade goods from the surface to Menzoberranzan and the Underdark.<ref name="TPK-p?" /><ref name="G-p?">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|?}}</ref> For example, they traded [[Netherese]] artifacts to Menzoberranzan via Luskan.<ref name="NotH-p309">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|309}}</ref>

Bregan D'aerthe was also an information broker, willing to provide information for both sides of a conflict—for the right price. Its contacts reached even as far as the [[svirfneblin]] city of [[Blingdenstone]].<ref name="H-p?" /><ref name="M.tH-pp63-64" /><ref name="Exile-p?">{{Cite book/Exile/MMP (1990)|?}}</ref>

Bregan D'aerthe had a reputation that normally allowed its agents to skip lines. Its members were given "credit" — often they didn't need to pay because of the threat of retaliation from Bregan D'aerthe.<ref name="Annih-pp86-87">{{Cite book/Annihilation/Paperback|86-87}}</ref>

==Base of Operations==
Bregan D'aerthe's main headquarters were hidden in the [[Clawrift]] in Menzoberranzan.<ref name="RGLoD-p122">{{Cite book/A Reader's Guide to R. A. Salvatore's the Legend of Drizzt|122}}</ref> However, they had a number of smaller headquarters and hide-outs in many locations throughout Menzoberranan and Ched Nasad.<ref name="M.tH-pp63-64" />

The band established a presence on the surface in order to further the band's influence as well as trade for Menzoberranzan. In [[1366 DR]], it briefly had a surface base in [[Calimport]].<ref name="SotS-p?" /> 

By [[1377 DR]], Bregan D'aerthe had developed a significant surface presence in [[Luskan]] and used [[Ship Kurth]] as its outward face.<ref name="TPK-p?" /> By [[1483 DR]], the drow agent [[Beniago]], disguised as a [[human]], was [[High Captain]] [[Kurth]].<ref name="TC-p341">{{Cite book/The Companions/Hardcover|341|26}}</ref> Bregan D'aerthe headquarters in Luskan were underground in [[Illusk]]. There was a path that connected the base to [[Closeguard Isle]] and [[Ship Kurth]]. Jarlaxle had a private room in this base.<ref name="NotH-p101">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|101}}</ref>

In {{Yearlink|1492}},{{WDH-time}} Urstul Floxin was the leader of the Zhents headquartered in the villa of the [[Gralhund]] family. From there, he directed the search for the ''[[Stone of Golorr]]'', which was in possession of the [[gnome]] [[Dalakhar]].<ref name="WDH5e-p44">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|44}}</ref>

However, not fully trusting Urstul, [[Yalah Gralhund]] also sent one of her personal servants, a [[nimblewright]] with a ''[[necklace of fireballs]]'', to search for the [[rock gnome]]. Floxin and two of his minions managed to ambush Dalakhar but then a ''[[fireball]]'' launched by the nimblewright killed the rock gnome and ten more people, including the two Zhent minions. Despite some injuries, Urstul survived and finally took the ''Stone''.<ref name="WDH5e-p43-44">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|43-44}}</ref>

Then, finally in possession of the ''Stone'', Urstul went to question Yalah about the nimblewright. However, she took advantage of his injures and took the ''Stone'',<ref name="WDH5e-p50">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|50}}</ref> triggering the events of the confrontation later known as the [[Gralhund Villa Bloodbath]].<ref name="WDH5e-p56">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|56}}</ref>

Urstul was confined in the villa's parlor but managed to kill the guards and escaped.<ref name="WDH5e-p53">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|53}}</ref>

Next, he tried to find Lord [[Orond Gralhund]], who was hidden in one of the guest rooms, with a plan to capture and trade him to get back the ''Stone''. There Urstul was possibly confronted and defeated by some adventurers.<ref name="WDH5e-p54">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|54}}</ref>

If he survived, Urstul retreated to [[Kolat Tower]] with his master [[Manshoon]].<ref name="WDH5e-p159">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|159}}</ref>

==Personality==
Urstul was truly loyal to Manshoon, even after being punished.<ref name="WDH5e-p159" /> He was a charming and dangerous individual.<ref name="WDH5e-p216" /> 
 
==Relationship==
Urstul received his orders directly from a [[simulacrum]] of Manshoon. He had a collection of black [[flying snake]]s that he used as couriers.<ref name="WDH5e-p216" />

House '''Gralhund''' ({{Pronounce|GRAWL|hund|refs=<ref name="CS-WW2e-p15">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|15}}</ref>}}) was a [[Nobility of Waterdeep|noble house]] of [[Waterdeep]] since the [[1260 DR|Year of the Broken Blade, 1260 DR]]. Their primary business interests included contracting [[hiresword]]s and manufacturing [[weapon]]s.<ref name="NHoW-3" /><ref name="W&N-p47">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|47}}</ref>

==Organization==
The Gralhund family were of [[Tethyrian]] descent<ref name="NHoW-3" /> but included a few [[tieflings]] who claimed they were also of the blood of [[Gargauth]].<ref name="NHoW-8">{{Cite web enhancement/Noble Houses of Waterdeep|9}}</ref> The tieflings were usually sent to a Gralhund estate in [[Yartar]].<ref name="WDH5e-p53">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|53}}</ref>

==Activities==
In the [[1372 DR|Year of Wild Magic, 1372 DR]], the Gralhunds were competing fiercely with [[House Amcathra]] in [[blade]]-making, producing higher-quality swords and selling them at a lower price. Amcathra's reputation as the best in blades was in jeopardy because the Gralhunds were using a new alloy of [[steel]] with traces of [[mithral]] they bartered from [[Thaglar's Foundry]], run by [[Clan Xundorn]]<ref name="CoSW-61">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|61}}</ref> down in [[Skullport]].<ref name="D172-15">{{Cite dragon/172/Seeing the Sights in Skullport|15}}</ref>

In the [[1492 DR|Year of Three Ships Sailing, 1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} the heads of the Gralhund family decided to ally with the [[Zhentarim]] in the search for the lost gold of [[Dagult Neverember]] under the promise of getting a fair share of the treasure. The Zhents, led by [[Urstul Floxin]], used Gralhund Villa as their headquarters while seeking out the [[Stone of Golorr]].<ref name="WDH5e-p44">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|44}}</ref>

However, not fully trusting Urstul, Yalah also sent one of her personal servants, a [[nimblewright]] with a ''[[necklace of fireballs]]'', to search for the [[gnome]] [[Dalakhar]]. The successive blast and the death of Dalakhar ruined the alliance between the [[Zhent]]s and the Gralhunds.<ref name="WDH5e-p44" />

When Urstul finally took possession of the Stone, he demanded explanations from Yalah about the nimblewright and her profiting from his injuries, and took the Stone.<ref name="WDH5e-p50">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|50}}</ref> This triggered a fight between Gralhund guards and Zhentarim agents that cost many lives and was remembered as the [[Gralhund Villa Bloodbath]].<ref name="WDH5e-p50-57">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|50-57}}</ref>

==Tactics==
[[Yalah Gralhund]] planned to use the intelligence resources of the Black Network in order to unveil some of the [[Masked Lord|Masked Lords of Waterdeep]] and blackmail or bribe her way into membership.<ref name="WDH5e-p220">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|220}}</ref>

==Base of Operations==
The Gralhund family kept a villa in Waterdeep's [[North Ward of Waterdeep|North Ward]] between [[Saerdoun Street]] and [[Horn Street]] off of [[Pony Way]].<ref name="WDH5e-p48">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|48}}</ref><ref name="WDH-map">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist/Map}}</ref> The villa grounds were enclosed by a {{SIAdj|12|foot|long=yes|adj=high}} stone wall with a main gate onto Horn Street. The entire compound was roughly wedge-shaped and was approximately {{SI|23000|sq ft|long=yes}} in total area. The main building was two stories tall and was approximately {{SI|13000|sq ft|long=yes}} total. A {{SIAdj|1580|sq ft|long=yes}}, single-story guard barracks adjoined the manor house, and there was a separate, single-story, {{SIAdj|2240|sq ft|long=yes}} stable that had an entrance onto Saerdoun Street.<ref name="WDH5e-p51-52">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|51-52}}</ref>

=== Masked Lords ===
The Lords of Waterdeep were only seen in public when sitting in the Lord's Court or when appearing every four years at [[Shieldmeet]] Council when the Lord's Rule is reaffirmed.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The identities of the Lords, other than the Open Lord, were not supposed to be known to the public, although they were known to each other.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The intent of this was to ensure that the Lords were free of coercion and could be drawn from any social strata within the city.<ref name="CoS.CG-p67" /> While the masked Lords were considered to be equal to the Open Lord in authority, some executive authority was reserved for the Open Lord, including the vetting and selection of new Lords,<ref name="CS.W-p52" /> the naming of the next Open Lord,<ref>{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|31}}</ref> and determining the needed number of Lords.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" />

'''''Idle Champions of the Forgotten Realms''''' is a free-to-play [[Wikipedia:Incremental game|idle game]] developed by [[Wikipedia:Codename Entertainment|Codename Entertainment]]. It was released for early access on September 7, 2017.

==Description==
The core gameplay of ''Idle Champions'' revolves around the player selecting "champions" (such as [[Bruenor Battlehammer|Bruenor]], [[Minsc]], and [[Celeste]], among others) to defeat waves of increasingly difficult monsters, thereby accumulating gold. This gold is used to upgrade the player's champions, or unlock new ones. Each individual champion has its own set of unique abilities that empower either themselves or other champions. The player needs to place these champions in a "formation" that facilitates the overall strength of the party to ensure progress can be made against higher-level waves of monsters. As players complete objectives, or reset their current adventure, they accumulate "Favor" with a specific deity, which increases the amount of gold they find in future sessions. Furthermore, players can collect character-specific equipment that permanently boost their champions further, either as random drops, or through purchasing "chests" filled with random items. Equipment comes in four quality tiers, where "Epic" is the highest. Furthermore, equipment can be further boosted by being made "Shiny" (a rare chance at permanent improvement) or "Golden" (an even bigger improvement but generally limited to real-world currency purchases). Finally, "Epic" equipment can be forged into "Legendary" quality, which does not empower the item itself, but rather provides an additional positive effect for the party.

The game can be automated to a degree using the "Modron Automation" system which allows the game to handle all necessary input to repeatedly play itself, in four separate instances, for a period of up to 7 days.

The game is segmented into several "campaigns", each with its own unique Favor, ensuring Favor progress does not carry over between campaigns. Each campaign has a unique formation pattern, forcing players to rethink how to arrange their champions. Some of the campaigns are permanent, while others are event-based and only available for a short time. Event campaigns reward unique champions that are only available to unlock during their respective events. Permanent campaigns are usually adaptations of a tabletop adventure, such as ''[[Tomb of Annihilation]]''. All the campaigns are further divided into "adventures", which are narrative frames for the core gameplay, for example recounting how the champions help rescue a farmer's daughter who has been kidnapped by [[werewolf|werewolves]]. Most adventures also have additional challenge modes, unlocked after meeting various criteria. These challenge modes present the player with new obstacles to overcome, for example through limiting the champions available to choose from. The "Patron" system allows for these challenges to be replayed with even more limitations imposed on the player. For example, selecting the [[Strahd von Zarovich|Strahd]] patron means players are restricted to only using champions with a high intelligence score.

The "end game" of ''Idle Champions'' is the "Trials of Mount Tiamat", a cooperative campaign where five players undertake missions to defeat [[Tiamat]]. Over the course of a week, players must succeed in daily missions to help reach their goal. Success yields "Scales of Tiamat", which is a currency used to forge "Legendary" equipment.
