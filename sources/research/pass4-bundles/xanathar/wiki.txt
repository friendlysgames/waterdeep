'''Xanathar''' was a [[beholder]] of the mid–14{{th}} century [[DR]] and the founding crime lord of the [[Xanathar Thieves' Guild]] in [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="W&tN-p57"/><ref name="HoH-p104-105"/><ref name="VLB-p122">{{Cite book/Villains' Lorebook|122}}</ref>

==Description==
Xanathar resembled a typical beholder, having a large spherical body some 4.5 feet (1.4 meters) in diameter, with ten eyestalks and a large central eye, above a wide fang-filled mouth. His chitinous hide was colored a dark blue-gray and shaded to a dull yellow-orange around his mouth and on his underside. He normally floated 3 feet (0.9 meters) above the ground.<ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

==Personality==
Xanathar greatly enjoyed being a crime lord just under the noses of Waterdeep's Lords and laughed at their claims to have stamped out organized crime.<ref name="HoH-p104-105" /> He was confident in his power and his inconspicuousness to the Lords.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /> His main goal was simply to maintain his position.<ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

Xanathar loved luxury and pleasures, and very much enjoyed well-prepared foods (provided by Steeltoe), scented oils, and spiced tobaccos and herbs from the south. His appetite was huge, and included people. When not engaged in crime, he relaxed in a great glass tank of scented water, where he planned his crimes.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

Xanathar was devious.<ref name="CoS-WWiW-p90" />

==Relations==
Xanathar was served by his "four councilors". They met with him regularly and in turn dealt with others in the organizations. No one else knew of Xanathar's existence, let alone that he was a beholder.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" /><ref name="VLB-p122" />

[[Shindia Darkeyes]], the [[half-drow]] [[thief]], was Xanathar's lieutenant and personal favorite. She oversaw blackmail and extortion. She helped Xanathar kill Uthh, but could do nothing to save it from the Eye. Afterward, she served the Eye grudgingly while plotting its downfall.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" /><ref name="VLB-p122" /><ref name="DDGttU-p47">{{Cite book/Drizzt Do'Urden's Guide to the Underdark|47}}</ref><ref name="CoS-WWiW-p89">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|89}}</ref><ref name="CaD-p142">{{Cite book/Cloak and Dagger|142}}</ref>

[[Slan Thurbel]] was Xanathar's mercenary leader and hitman, but Xanathar insulted him continuously and, in Slan's words, did not "respect his talents". Glad Xanathar was dead, Slan much preferred the Eye in charge.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" /><ref name="VLB-p122" /><ref name="CoS-WWiW-p90" /><ref name="CaD-p143">{{Cite book/Cloak and Dagger|143}}</ref>

[[Slink Monteskor]] was Xanathar's bookkeeper and information gatherer, with a web of spies and snitches in the poorer parts of Waterdeep.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" /><ref name="VLB-p122" />

[[Ott Steeltoes]] was a dwarven pirate who provided Xanathar's delicacies.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" /><ref name="VLB-p122" />

Xanathar also owned two ''charmed'' [[intellect devourer]]s and a winglet of six [[gargoyle]]s for the defense of his lair.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

Anyone who managed to find and invade Xanathar's lair was interrogated for information, briefly kept as a pet, and finally eaten by the beholder.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" /><ref name="VLB-p122" />

==Equipment==
Although Xanathar shared his kind's distrust of magical items, he did have specially made an ''[[ring of proof against detection and location|eyestalk ring of proof against detection and location]]'', which he wore on his fifth eyestalk. He wore rings on all his other eyestalks, but these were non-magical jewelry, valued around 350 [[gp]] each.<ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

==Residence==
Xanathar laired in a hidden chamber behind a secret door in the sewers below Waterdeep, accessed by a series of trapped passages.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" /><ref name="VLB-p122" /> The traps were mostly nonlethal, comprising falling cages and nets or pits, in order to catch trespassers.<ref name="HoH-p104-105" /> None but Xanathar and his Councilors knew of the lair's existence and the safe route in, and the few who stumbled into it soon met their ends there. The lair was opulently furnished, and held several treasure vaults and libraries.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

==Activities==
Through the Xanathar's Thieves Guild, Xanathar controlled most of the crime in Waterdeep.<ref name="CoS-WWiW-p90" /> Rather than operate as a true "thieves' guild", and thus risk the attention of the [[Lords of Waterdeep]], he instead worked entirely through freelance thieves via third parties, so that no-one could reveal his whereabouts, identity, or even existence. Xanathar was the major supporter and advocate of the hundreds of small criminal enterprises in Waterdeep. He was amused by the Lords' claims that no thieves' guild existed in Waterdeep.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

Xanathar collected much information about the surface world, also via third parties, and used this to make his plans.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /> It was rumored he knew the most about Waterdeep's sewers and their connections to major buildings and to [[Undermountain]].<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" /> The guild operated heavily in the sewers and Undermountain.<ref name="RoU.CG-p105" /> His libraries closely tracked the powers and vulnerabilities of Waterdhavian mages.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /> However, all information in these libraries was written by Slink in a cipher.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

However, Xanathar's best defense was secrecy, and he would go to great efforts to maintain it. Operatives who wondered who was in charge was targeted for death by Slan's ritual killers. He also engaged in misdirection, with defenses against magical detection on his lair and having his organization function not at all like a real thieves' guild. He was sure the Lords would tear apart the city's sewers to locate his lair, if they but knew of his existence.<ref name="W&tN-p57" /><ref name="VLB-p122" /> His other reason for staying anonymous was so his former masters in the Zhentarim could not find him.<ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

By [[1358 DR]], Xanathar had not left his lair in years, and his lieutenants fulfilled all his needs.<ref name="HoH-p104-105" />

==History==
Xanathar originally came from the [[Anaurian Hive]].<ref name="SP-16">{{Cite book/Skullport|16}}</ref>

Xanathar was originally a follower of the eye tyrant [[Xantriph]], who was an ally of [[Manshoon]] of [[Zhentil Keep]]. Xanathar was sent by the [[Zhentarim]] to Waterdeep to plot the downfall of the [[Lords of Waterdeep]] and establish a ruler friendly to the [[Zhent]]s. He arrived hidden in cargo in a trading caravan from Zhentil Keep. However, after lurking around the docks and conspiring for a couple of months, Xanathar was inspired by Waterdeep's free market atmosphere and sensed a power vacuum in the city's underworld. He soon went independent, and vanished from his masters' view. Acting independently, he established a network of thieves who worked for him and located a hideout below the city. He brought many of the disparate freelance criminal operations under his covert leadership.<ref name="HoH-p104-105"/><ref name="VLB-p122"/><ref name="SP-23">{{Cite book/Skullport|23}}</ref>

By the 1350s [[DR]], Xanathar led a great but "unofficial" thieves' guild and only his lieutenants, the "four councilors"—[[Slan Thurbel]], [[Slink Monteskor]], [[Ott Steeltoes]], and [[Shindia Darkeyes]]—knew his true nature and aspirations. It was well established by [[1357 DR]].<ref name="W&tN-p57"/><ref name="HoH-p104-105"/><ref name="VLB-p122"/>

However, beginning around [[1354 DR]], another beholder had set its sights on the Xanathar's Thieves' Guild. Already the leader of [[Agents of the Eye|a slaving ring]] based in Skullport, the beholder known as [[Kirukeskai|the Eye]] desired to increase its power and influence by taking over the thieving organization. Over ten years, the Eye studied the Xanathar's Thieves' Guild and questioned ''[[charm]]ed'' people in the know, and found subtle similarities in how their two groups behaved, things only a beholder would recognize. It thus learned the Xanathar was a fellow eye tyrant from a different hive, and plotted to eliminate a rival and seize more power in one swoop. Xanathar remained completely unaware as, over a few years, the Eye's investigations revealed the location of its lair.<ref name="CaD-p139">{{Cite book/Cloak and Dagger|139-140}}</ref> The Eye kept its own nature secret from Xanathar.<ref name="RoU.CG-p105">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Campaign Guide to Undermountain|105-106,127}}</ref>

Xanathar once accidentally petrified [[Ilserv]], the [[illithid]] emissary of [[Ch'Chitl]].<ref name="DDGttU-p45">{{Cite book/Drizzt Do'Urden's Guide to the Underdark|45}}</ref>

In the mid-1360s [[DR]], Xanathar attempted to organize the disparate thieves of Waterdeep into a new guild. However, the effort was thwarted and Xanathar was defeated by bold [[adventuring companies]] commanded by Lord [[Piergeiron]] of the Lords of Waterdeep, and was the latest such effort by [[1367 DR]].<ref name="FRCS2E-p18">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|18,106}}</ref>

Xanathar's ruin came at last at some unknown time in the late 1360s DR, when the Eye, via its agents, carefully manipulated another Skullport beholder, [[Uthh]], and tricked it into attacking Xanathar in the sewers under Waterdeep. Although Xanathar defeated Uthh, it was left weakened and, taking advantage, the Eye arrived and easily killed Xanathar. Afterward, the Eye replaced it, telling Xanathar's former minions that "the Xanathar" was but a title. Persuaded by ''charm'' spells and by ''death rays'' at dissenters, most agreed to stay and serve it. The Eye retained most of the original Xanathar's policies and practices; the transition was so smooth that those outside the four councilors remained unaware of the change. "The Xanathar" had taken full control of the Xanathar's Thieves' Guild and gained great power and influence by [[1368 DR]].<ref name="VLB-p122"/><ref name="CoS-CGttC-p110">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|110}}</ref><ref name="CoS-WWiW-p90">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|90}}</ref><ref name="CaD-p139"/><ref name="SP-81">{{Cite book/Skullport|81}}</ref>

In truth however the original Xanathar was killed in [[1358]] by [[Heroes of Waterdeep|an adventuring band]] under orders of the Lords of Waterdeep and secretly aided by another beholder that assumed the identity of the Xanathar.<ref name="Dun206">{{Cite dungeon/206/The Xanathar: Crime Lord of Waterdeep}}</ref><ref name="eotb">{{Cite game/Eye of the Beholder}}</ref>

'''Xanathar's Thieves' Guild''', or simply the '''Xanathar Guild''', was a criminal and slaving organization in [[Waterdeep]] and [[Skullport]], run by a series of [[beholder|beholders]].<ref name="WatN-57">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|57}}</ref><ref name="FRCS-p281">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|281}}</ref><ref name="EtU-18">{{Cite book/Expedition to Undermountain|12}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p17">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|17}}</ref>

{{quote|I don’t know if the Xanathar really exists, but as long as I’m working in Waterdeep, I make sure he gets his cut. Why risk the alternative?|Termerin, cat burglar<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p1">{{Cite dungeon/206/The Xanathar: Crime Lord of Waterdeep|1}}</ref>}}

==Geography==
This criminal and slaver network was one of the most powerful on the Sword Coast, but often unintentionally acted as a buffer against other evil organizations. They operated out of [[Skullport]], but were otherwise active primarily in Waterdeep.<ref name="FRCS-p281"/>

The Xanathar Guild had many connections in [[Luskan]], [[Baldur's Gate]], and the surrounding [[Underdark]], mostly the Underdark north and west of Waterdeep.<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p5">{{Cite dungeon/206/The Xanathar: Crime Lord of Waterdeep|5}}</ref>

==Aims & Goals==
The Xanathar Guild worked to gain financial and political power through its portfolio of spies, mercenaries, slavers, and thieves.<ref name="C&D-p139">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|139}}</ref> They hoped to rule both Skullport and Waterdeep by installing Xanathar spies as Masked Lords in the City of Splendors.<ref name="C&D-p142">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|142}}</ref>

==Organization==
The Xanathar Guild had a loose method of organization due to the instability of its leader, and members inside regularly assassinated one another as desperate attempts to vie for the beholder's favor.<ref name="WDH-p220">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|220}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p17"/> During the reign of [[Xanathar (Zushaxx)|Zushaxx]] in {{Yearlink|1492}}, members could be killed at any time at the whim of the beholder.<ref name="WDH-p100">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|100}}</ref>

===Under Xanathar (original)===
During the original Xanathar's reign, the Guild was often populated by freelance thieves or mercenaries that were hired on a case-by-case basis.<ref name="CoS-WWiW-p88">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|88}}</ref> Xanathar's most senior agents were the Four Councilors, who were each assigned a domain to supervise: mercenaries, bookkeeping, thievery, and blackmail.<ref name="WatN-57"/>

===Under the Eye===
During the rulership of the Eye, eleven Masters were elected to serve eleven domains: assassination, blackmail, bookkeeping, enforcement, extortion, information gathering, magical defenses, mercenaries, slavery, smuggling, and thievery. Owing to the expanse of some of these domains, some Masters served one domain together, while others served several at once.<ref name="C&D-p140">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|140}}</ref>

Three of these Masters oversaw the Eye's slaving operations, and were referred to as the Eyes of the Hand. From lowest to highest, their ranks were Third of the Hand, Second of the Hand, and the Command of the Hand. Their underlings were referred to as simply "Hands," and operated as "snatch bands," groups who kidnapped people in order to sell them into slavery.<ref name="C&D-p140"/><ref name="TRoU-TEatH-p5">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain II/The Eye and the Hand|5}}</ref>

The Eye also appointed the [[Eye of Xanathar|Eleven Eyes of Xanathar]] to serve as personal servants, spies, and assassins, drawn from the recommendations of the Masters. The Masters resented the Eyes' prestige, but felt they were untouchable.<ref name="EEoX">{{Cite web

===Under Zushaxx===
During the reign of Zushaxx, four lieutenants,<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p2">{{Cite dungeon/206/The Xanathar: Crime Lord of Waterdeep|2}}</ref> later expanded to six, managed the Guild's operations.<ref name="WDH-p100">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|100}}</ref> Xanathar also had personal advisors, though by {{Yearlink|1492}} this number had dwindled to one.<ref name="WDH-p211">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|211}}</ref>{{Waterdeep-time}}

Xanathar had also established the following ranks from lowest to highest: Eyestalker, Agent of the Eye, Eye Ray, Guild Boss, and Hand of the Eye.<ref name="WDH-p14">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|14}}</ref>

==Membership==
Members of the guild were trained in stealth, discretion, and tactics with [[fighter]]s, [[rogue]]s, [[sorcerer]]s, [[wizard]]s, and [[expert]]s, which comprised most of its numbers. An ever-growing organization, it utilized multiple mouthpieces to protect and conceal the identity of the [[Xanathar]]. To join, a member had to prove their ruthlessness, obedience, and loyalty by committing a serious crime—killing or selling a friend or family member into slavery—as well as paying 300 [[gp]]. The typical agent earned dues of 60 gp a month.<ref name="EtU-18"/>

Support generally came in these ways:<ref name="WDH-p17"/>
* Any member given an assignment would receive a bodyguard or assistant, such as a [[bugbear]], [[wererat]], [[kenku]], or [[gazer]], with secret instructions to spy on and assassinate the agent in case of failure.<ref name="WDH-p17" />
* The guild provided safe refuges such as cellars in the sewers.<ref name="WDH-p17"/>

Among the low-ranking guild members, no one really knew who [[Xanathar]] was. Few had an inkling he was a beholder, and fewer still had spoken to him.<ref name="WDH-p17"/>

[[File:Surveillance.png|thumb|220x220px|''Five dwarves that served as a surveillance system for the guild.'']]
Some of the more unusual members of the guild in the late 1400s DR were known as the panopticus, a group of five [[shield dwarf|shield dwarves]] that had been modified by the [[mind flayer]] [[Nihiloor]] to operate and serve as a surveillance system for the [[Zushaxx|Xanathar]].<ref name="WDH-p106">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|106}}</ref>

The Xanathar Guild was also known to employ a number of [[goblin]]s and [[bugbear]]s, some of whom were infected with [[intellect devourer]]s, presumably by Nihiloor.<ref name="WDotMM-p13">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dungeon of the Mad Mage|13}}</ref>

==History==
The Xanathar Guild was originally established by the beholder [[Xanathar (original)|Xanathar]], who had been employed by the [[Zhentarim]] to establish a cell in Skullport. Sensing an opportunity for more, Xanathar cut off the Zhentarim and established its own criminal organization.<ref name="VL-p122">{{Cite book/Villains' Lorebook|122}}</ref><ref name="Sk-p23">{{Cite book/Skullport|23}}</ref>

[[File:Xanathar's Thieves' Guild original.jpg|alt=Cover art of ''Waterdeep and the North,'' showing the original Xanathar Guild.|left|thumb|200px|From left to right: [[Slan Thurbel]], [[Slink Monteskor]], [[Ott Steeltoes]], [[Xanathar (original)|Xanathar]], and [[Shindia Darkeyes]].]]

By {{Yearlink|1357}}, the Xanathar Guild was thriving as a thieves' guild, though its founder's identity remained cloaked in mystery. Rather than operate as a traditional guild, the Xanathar Guild conducted operations mostly through freelance agents, which had the benefit of agents knowing as little about Xanathar as possible.<ref name="WatN-57"/> Xanathar was also concerned with mages living within Waterdeep, and its libraries carefully catalogued their abilities and weaknesses.<ref name="WatN-58">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|58}}</ref> Below Xanathar, the Guild was run by the "Four Councilors": [[Slan Thurbel]], [[Slink Monteskor]], [[Shindia Darkeyes]], and [[Ott Steeltoes]].<ref name="WatN-57"/>

The Xanathar Guild had not yet become slavers, as slaving operations in Skullport were primarily controlled by the fearsome [[Agents of the Eye]]. Unbeknownst to Xanathar, this syndicate was commanded by another beholder, [[Kirukeskai]], known as "the Eye." Through years of careful spywork, the Eye determined that the mysterious Xanathar was a fellow beholder, and began plotting to take its place.<ref name="C&D-p139"/>

The Eye eventually discovered a pawn in [[Uthh]], a third beholder in Skullport, and manipulated it into attacking Xanathar in the sewers beneath Waterdeep. Assisted by Shindia, Xanathar was able to defeat Uthh, but was weakened severely.<ref name="CoS-WWiW-p89">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|89}}</ref> Taking advantage of this, the Eye swept in and slew Xanathar.<ref name="C&D-p139"/> As revenge for the fallen Xanathar, Shindia began to plot against the Eye.<ref name="CoS:W-p89">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|89}}</ref>

The Eye proceeded to take over the Xanathar Guild operations and renamed itself "the Xanathar," starting the Guild's tradition of using the name as a title. After killing the few original employees who didn't fall in line, the Eye successfully merged the Xanathar Guild and the Agents of the Eye, all without alerting its underlings that a regime change had taken place.<ref name="C&D-p140">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|140}}</ref> By {{Yearlink|1372}}, the Agents of the Eye had become interchangeable with the Xanathar Guild, though the former name was more often used in Skullport, and the latter used in Waterdeep.<ref name="CoS:W-p64">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|64}}</ref>

[[File:Xanathar 3e.JPG|alt=Illustration of the Eye.|thumb|The Eye surveys its domain.]]

Under the reign of the Eye, the Xanathar Guild expanded its operations immensely. The Guild became one of the most prominent slaving organizations in Skullport, as well as successfully eliminated Seirtych Xantaun, a rival beholder crimelord. The Eye also elected eleven Masters to serve as supervisors of Guild members, four of which were the original Xanathar's Councilors.<ref name="C&D-p140"/> These Masters included [[Ahmaergo]], [[Avaereene]], [[Colstan Rhuul]], [[Shindia Darkeyes]], [[Slink Monteskor]], [[Randulaith]], [[Ott Steeltoes]], [[Slan Thurbel]].<ref name="C&D-p141-143">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|141-143}}</ref>

By {{Yearlink|1372}}, the Eye had nearly dominated petty theft in Waterdeep,<ref name="CoS:W-p64"/> and had forced another beholder rival, [[Misker]], to flee deep into the Undermountain.<ref name="CoS:W-p65">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|65}}</ref> It had also appointed the [[Eye of Xanathar|Eleven Eyes of Xanathar]] to serve as personal servants, drawn from the recommendations of the Masters.<ref name="EEoX">{{Cite web
| url            = http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/prc/20061107b
| title          = Eleven Eyes of Xanathar
| accessdate     = 2018-06-10
| date           = 2006-11-07
| publisher      = Wizard of the Coast
| archiveurl     = https://web.archive.org/web/20161031215531/http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/prc/20061107b
| archivedate    = 2016-10-31
}}</ref>

Sources differed on the death of the Eye. One claimed it was eventually killed by one of its own Masters, Avaereene;<ref name="Dungeon200-B:S-p9">{{Cite dungeon/200/Backdrop: Skullport|9}}</ref> another claimed it was killed by a group of adventurers;<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p1"/> another claimed it lived still, and had taken on the name [[the Blind]].<ref name="Dungeon200-B:S-p9"/>

Regardless, the Eye was replaced, and other beholders continued to take on the title of the Xanathar. Two of these beholders facilitated their own retirement rather than risk being overthrown or killed; one was [[Xanathar (Xandulzrithral)|Xandulzrithral]], who retired peacefully, and another was [[Xanathar (Izulktur)|Izulktur]], who replaced itself with [[Xanathar (Zushaxx)|Zushaxx]].<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p1-2"/>

As the new Xanathar, Zushaxx ran the Guild ambitiously, and amassed as much information as possible to increase the Guild's power.<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p2"/> It was at least the fifteenth Xanathar by this point, as it kept the embalmed bodies of four other beholders within its lair, along with nine urns containing the ashes of others.<ref name="WDH-p112">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|112}}</ref> Zushaxx also appointed four lieutenants to serve it: [[Kal'dir]], [[Quid]], [[Sial Sapphire]], and [[Draak]].<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p2"/>

Through its spies, Zushaxx managed to acquire the ''[[Stone of Golorr]],'' a magical artifact considered the key to discovering treasure embezzled by Waterdeep's former [[Lords of Waterdeep#Open Lord|Open Lord]], [[Dagult Neverember]]. Though it had also been able to locate the vault within which the treasure was kept, it had been forced out by the vault's draconic guardian. Zushaxx had opted to keep the Stone within its lair, trusting it would be kept safe.<ref name="WDH-p220"/>

By {{Yearlink|1491}}, Zushaxx had taken [[Nar'l Xibrindas]], a [[Bregan D'aerthe]] spy, as its only advisor, choosing to eliminate all others.<ref name="WDH-p211"/>

[[File:Xanathar Lair.jpg|alt=Illustration of Xanathar in its lair.|left|thumb|300px|Xanathar battles adventurers in its lair.]]

By {{Yearlink|1492}}, Zushaxx had fully taken over Skullport, forcing the businesses within to pay it for protection.<ref name="WDotMM-p304">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dungeon of the Mad Mage|304}}</ref> It had also eliminated another rival, the beholder [[Nerozar]], who had challenged it for lordship of Skullport.<ref name="WDotMM-p52">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dungeon of the Mad Mage|52}}</ref> Additionally, it had established a new system of rankings within the Xanathar Guild.<ref name="WDH-p14">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|14}}</ref> Tattoos of the Xanathar Guild's symbol had also become popular among members.<ref name="WDH-p21">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|21}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p106"/><ref name="WDH-p25">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|25}}</ref>

By this time, Zushaxx had also appointed [[Thorvin Twinbeard]], a Harper informant, [[Nihiloor]], a mind flayer, and [[Noska Ur'gray]] to important positions. It had also come to own [[Sylgar]], a goldfish.<ref name="WDH-p100"/> It had become insanely devoted to the goldfish, and had even changed Ott Steeltoes' role from Master of Smuggling and Thieving<ref name="C&D-p143">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|143}}</ref> to Sylgar's keeper.<ref name="WDH-p100"/> However, Zushaxx was unaware that the fish had died and been replaced in secret several times.<ref name="WDH-p220">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|220}}</ref>

In {{Yearlink|1492}}, the Xanathar Guild and the Zhentarim had been in negotiations to merge into a single criminal guild.<ref name="WDH-p99"/> During these negotiations, the ''Stone of Golorr'' disappeared from its hiding place. Believing the Zhentarim to be the culprits, Zushaxx began a bloody street war.<ref name="WDH-p5"/>

A '''beholder''', sometimes called a '''sphere of many eyes''' or an '''eye tyrant''', was a large [[aberration]] normally found in the [[Underdark]]. These large, orb-shaped beings had ten eyestalks and one central eye, each containing powerful [[magic]]. Powerful and intelligent, beholders were among the greatest threats to the world.<ref name="MM3e-p24">{{Cite book/Monster Manual 3rd edition|24-25}}</ref>

{{Quote|Think ye weave cunning schemes and elaborate intrigues with fallback plans and positions? Beholders change, refine, discard, and spin anew scores of such plans all the time. To the average beholder, [[human]] intrigues are the fumbling of babies.|[[Elminster]]'s notes in ''[[Volo's Guide to Monsters (in-universe)|Volo's Guide to Monsters]]''.<ref name="VGtM-p6">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Monsters|6}}</ref>}}

==Description==
Beholders were immediately identifiable, being essentially a floating head with one single, [[cyclops]]-like eye surrounded by ten smaller eye stalks. Other than this, the main feature of a beholder's anatomy was its massive, gaping maw. Because of these features, beholders were occasionally known as "spheres of many eyes" or "eye tyrants,"<ref name="MM3e-p24" /> although the latter also referred to a specific type of beholder.<ref name="MM4e-p32">{{Cite book/Monster Manual 4th edition|32}}</ref>

Beholders of the Realms tended to be slightly larger than beholders found on other worlds, growing up to six feet in diameter where on other worlds they would average five feet wide. The majority of beholders living on Faerûn had skin colored in cool colors—purples and blues—on the top of their bodies that graduated into earth tones further down. Said skin had a pebbly texture. Most of Toril's beholders had nostrils and jointed, articulated eyestalks.<ref>{{Cite book/I, Tyrant|66}}</ref>

Because their entire body was covered in eyes, beholders had the capacity to see in all directions at once, making it nearly impossible to ambush them while also giving them an unusually high degree of perceptive ability. Although beholders lacked the capacity to see color, they had the ability to perceive even in the most darkened environment, under conditions in which a [[human]] or similar creature would be rendered completely blind.<ref name="MM3e-p24" />

Beholders were also capable of flight, in spite of their lack of wings or similar physical features, simply hovering above ground effortlessly. The effects of this flight resembled those of the [[wizard]] [[spell]] ''[[levitate|levitation]]''.<ref name="MM3e-p24" />

=== LINKED WIKI PAGE (first 5000 chars): Marking the years

{{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

'''Xanathar's Thieves' Guild''', or simply the '''Xanathar Guild''', was a criminal and slaving organization in [[Waterdeep]] and [[Skullport]], run by a series of [[beholder|beholders]].<ref name="WatN-57">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|57}}</ref><ref name="FRCS-p281">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|281}}</ref><ref name="EtU-18">{{Cite book/Expedition to Undermountain|12}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p17">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|17}}</ref>

{{quote|I don’t know if the Xanathar really exists, but as long as I’m working in Waterdeep, I make sure he gets his cut. Why risk the alternative?|Termerin, cat burglar<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p1">{{Cite dungeon/206/The Xanathar: Crime Lord of Waterdeep|1}}</ref>}}

==Geography==
This criminal and slaver network was one of the most powerful on the Sword Coast, but often unintentionally acted as a buffer against other evil organizations. They operated out of [[Skullport]], but were otherwise active primarily in Waterdeep.<ref name="FRCS-p281"/>

The Xanathar Guild had many connections in [[Luskan]], [[Baldur's Gate]], and the surrounding [[Underdark]], mostly the Underdark north and west of Waterdeep.<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p5">{{Cite dungeon/206/The Xanathar: Crime Lord of Waterdeep|5}}</ref>

==Aims & Goals==
The Xanathar Guild worked to gain financial and political power through its portfolio of spies, mercenaries, slavers, and thieves.<ref name="C&D-p139">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|139}}</ref> They hoped to rule both Skullport and Waterdeep by installing Xanathar spies as Masked Lords in the City of Splendors.<ref name="C&D-p142">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|142}}</ref>

==Organization==
The Xanathar Guild had a loose method of organization due to the instability of its leader, and members inside regularly assassinated one another as desperate attempts to vie for the beholder's favor.<ref name="WDH-p220">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|220}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p17"/> During the reign of [[Xanathar (Zushaxx)|Zushaxx]] in {{Yearlink|1492}}, members could be killed at any time at the whim of the beholder.<ref name="WDH-p100">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|100}}</ref>

===Under Xanathar (original)===
During the original Xanathar's reign, the Guild was often populated by freelance thieves or mercenaries that were hired on a case-by-case basis.<ref name="CoS-WWiW-p88">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|88}}</ref> Xanathar's most senior agents were the Four Councilors, who were each assigned a domain to supervise: mercenaries, bookkeeping, thievery, and blackmail.<ref name="WatN-57"/>

==Features==
===Architecture===
Generally speaking, Undermountain's natural chambers boast high ceilings of at least {{SI|16|ft}} and smooth floors made of gray [[granite]]. Many halls, rooms, and galleries were created by carving out the natural rock and were generally decorated in a way that made them look uniform and mundane. That said, exceptions existed.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Campaign Guide to Undermountain|16}}</ref>

A corridor in Undermountain was usually {{SI|10|ft}} wide and high. Stairs typically had no railings.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

===Magical Features===
Undermountain had lots of [[magical]] peculiarities that were created and utilized by Halaster. Undermountain's proprietor made it impossible to cast magic that teleported people into or out of Undermountain. Various forms of divination magic would also not function, though some magic objects and spells like wizard eye worked to some degree. This made it challenging if not impossible for would-be treasure hunters to gather information about what the dungeon had in store or what Halaster was doing. Summoning spells also had limitations; their functionality was limited to moving monsters that were already present in Undermountain, or trapping monsters from outside. However, magic items that summoned creatures worked, at least as a general rule.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

Destroying any wards or magical effects that managed these limitations was a task that was as good as impossible. A high understanding of magic was required to understand the many-layered incantations, particular knowledge to determine how to dissolve these effects, the skill to actually dispel these high level magics, combat prowess to protect oneself from monsters that were certainly attracted to people working to dissolve the layered magic, the ability to do this work at a speed that exceeded the speed at which the magic was restored, a lot of time, and the willingness to undertake this task despite the thankless and likely fruitless nature.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

Another effect of the many-layered magic of the Mad Mage was that most of the surfaces and features of the [[dungeon]] radiated some degree of magic, including the floor, ceilings, and walls. This made spells like ''[[detect magic]]'' largely useless because either the radiated magic was so strong that it was impossible to distinguish specific information or because the radiated magic masked the magical properties of other things.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

The complex magics of Undermountain were intertwined with Halaster himself. In the event of the Mad Mage's death or removal from Undermountain, its enchantments and spells would go wild. Countless ''[[gate]]s'' and ''[[portal]]s'' would become destabilized and available for many powerful monsters to utilize. '''Manshoon''' ({{Pronounce|man|SHOON|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-DM-p25-26" /><ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was the founder of the [[Zhentarim]],<ref name="FRCS-p282">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|282}}</ref> also known as the Black Network, until he was "slain" by [[Fzoul Chembryl]], his ally, and Lord [[Orgauth]] in [[1370 DR]]. However, it transpired that Manshoon had created a dozen or more [[clone]]s that the murderous duo then found themselves facing. Only three of these clones were known to have survived into the late 1370s [[DR]] and once more headed the evil organization known as the Zhentarim.<ref name="FRCS-p283">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|283}}</ref>

==Personality==
Completely fitting the archetype of the sly-tongued, sinister, and decadent master spellcaster, Manshoon ruthlessly crushed revolts within his own ranks of the Black Network, easily slaughtered his rivals and weeded the weak out the ranks of the young and ambitious by sending upstarts on nigh-impossible suicide missions. Hated and mistrusted by Fzoul, Manshoon manipulated him much as he did all others—remaining arrogant, untouchable in his power, and faintly amused by it all.<ref name="FRCS-p283" />

==Relations==
He had contacts with many creatures, mainly [[beholder]]s, such as [[Xarlandralath]] and [[Xualahuu]], some of whom held much respect and loyalty to him. For example, Xualahuu was deeply loyal to him and tried to advance the Black Network's plots.<ref name="A-p52">{{Cite book/Anauroch|52}}</ref>

'''Zhentil Keep''' ({{Pronounce|z|HENT|ill|_|keep|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-CR-p93">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Set/Cyclopedia of the Realms|93}}</ref>}}), also called '''Thargate Keep''',<ref name="RoZK.CB-p7" /> was once the most powerful city in the [[Moonsea]] region as well as the main base of operation for the [[Zhentarim]].<ref name="MotM p.123">{{Cite book/Mysteries of the Moonsea|123}}</ref> Along with the [[Citadel of the Raven]], Zhentil Keep was destroyed by the [[Shadovar]] in [[1383 DR]] during the [[Shadowbane War]].<ref>{{Cite dungeon/170/Monument of the Ancients|65}}</ref><ref name="FRCS4-p282">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|282}}</ref>

==History==
{{main|History of Zhentil Keep}}

===Early History===
Zhentil Keep began as a small trading camp located on the north side of the [[Moonsea]] around [[640 DR]].<ref>{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|93}}</ref>

In [[747 DR]], a [[Selgaunt|Chancelgauntian]] ship owner named [[Orlephar Flostren]], began hiring workers and mercenaries to construct fortifications and patrol the surrounding areas. By the next spring, a group of 12 Sembian merchants purchased the hold and began a major project of expansion. It was during this period that a large keep was raised in the northwestern quarter. <ref name="RoZK-p8">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|8}}</ref>

Following its completion, a merchant by the name of [[Elephstron]] quickly moved into the keep, titling himself "Lord of the Keep." In response to the objections of the other Sembian merchants, Elephstron proposed each be named a "Lord of the City," with the ability to collect taxes, enact laws, and establish militias for carrying out orders on their behalf. The eleven merchants agreed and the hold experienced a period of rapid growth as an increasing number of merchants moved in to buy and rent land.<ref name="RoZK-p8" />

Despite the measures taken by the Lords, monster attacks on caravans and nearby trade routes became more common and infighting between the merchants was growing increasingly dangerous. With the stability of the hold at stake, Elephstron sought out a [[wizard]] to aid him in combating these threats. In [[751 DR]], Elphestron met a wizard by the name of [[Zhentar]] in the city of [[Westgate]].<ref name="GHoTR-p101" /> Zhentar accepted Elephstron's request on the condition that the twelve Lords create a formal governing council and give him a seat with equal status in this new council. The twelve lords agreed to his terms and Zhentar relocated to the Hold as a Lord of the City.<ref name="RoZK-p8" />

'''Kirukeskai''', also called '''the Eye''', was an [[elder orb]] who was the first [[beholder]] to hold '''The Xanathar''' as a title instead of a name.<ref name="C&D-140" /> It was a crime lord and the leader of the [[Xanathar's Thieves' Guild]],<ref name="C&D-140" /> also called the Xanathar's Guild.<ref name="FRCS-281"/>

==Description==
Kirukeskai was an elder orb whose body had grown<ref name="CoSW-65" /> to the limit of a beholder and it was therefore very big for its race.<ref name="MM35e-26">{{Cite book/Monster Manual 3.5|26}}</ref> It was of very advanced age for its species and was much wrinkled.<ref name="CoSW-65" />

==Personality==
Kirukeskai was exceptionally paranoid,<ref name="CoSW-65" /> even for a beholder.<ref name="LoM-40">{{Cite book/Lords of Madness: The Book of Aberrations|40}}</ref> While beholders generally did not leave their homes and generally worked through intermediaries,<ref name="LoD-117">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|117}}</ref> Kirukeskai exclusively worked through intermediaries and never went out of its fortified homes in [[Skullport]] and the southern half of the third level of [[Undermountain]], which it managed because these homes were linked via a ''[[portal]]''.<ref name="CoSW-65"/> In fact, only the Masters of the [[Xanathar's Thieves' Guild|Xanathar's Guild]] knew that it was a beholder<ref name="FRCS-281">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|281}}</ref> or that it was not the first one to rule their criminal organization.<ref name="C&D-141">{{Cite book/Cloak and Dagger|141}}</ref> Another group that knew of the former fact were the eleven [[eye of Xanathar|eyes of Xanathar]], though it was not clear whether they knew of the latter fact.<ref name="EEoX"/>

Beholders in general were megalomaniac creatures<ref name="LoD-117"/> and Kirukeskai was no exception.<ref name="C&D-140">{{Cite book/Cloak and Dagger|140}}</ref> It viewed all creatures on the surface as slaves for its taking and it viewed [[humanoid]]s who tried to become closer to beholders by becoming [[ocular adept]]s as arrogant pawns.<ref name="CoSW-65" />

Beholders in general believed themselves to be intelligent.<ref name="LoD-117"/> The Eye was very intelligent even by beholder standards and a competent businessman.<ref name="C&D-140"/>

Kirukeskai was a cruel parent who ''charmed'' its offspring into subservience until they matured. Then he turned them into [[death tyrant]]s.<ref name="C&D-140"/>

The Eye felt the deteriorating effects of its great age, and by [[1370 DR]] it had begun trying magical means of staving of the ageing process. It drank ''[[potion of longevity|potions of longevity]]''

'''Piergeiron Paladinson''' ({{Pronounce|peer|GEER|on|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-DM-p29" />}}) was the [[Lords of Waterdeep|Open Lord of Waterdeep]] throughout most of the 1300s [[DR]] and leader of the [[Lords' Alliance]].<ref name="SF-p9">{{Cite book/The Savage Frontier|9}}</ref> He lived in [[Piergeiron's Palace]] in [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="CoS.W-p53">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|53}}</ref> 

Usually, the identities of the Lords of Waterdeep were concealed from the people, but Piergeiron was one of only four whose identities were known as of his tenure. He followed in the footsteps of his predecessors, Open Lords [[Ahghairon]], [[Baeron]], and [[Lhestyn]].<ref name="CoS.CG-p67">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|67}}</ref>

==Description==
Piergeiron was a tall, muscular, and strikingly handsome man with a firm jaw, and dark eyes.<ref name="CoS.W-p53" /><ref name="HoH-p92">{{Cite book/Hall of Heroes|92}}</ref> His hair was curly slightly greying at the temples, adding to the man's regal aura.<ref name="HoH-p92" />

==Personality==
Piergeiron did not speak very often, understanding the tact of silence, and when he did, he spoke very slowly weighing his every word. This led to some of those around him nicknaming him "the Thickskull," although never in his presence.<ref name="CoS.W-p53" />

He was aware of the nickname, sometimes even laughing about it. Piergeiron used this misconception of his mental capabilities to his advantage. Having his political enemies underestimate him led to often made slip-ups in the negotiations, giving the Open Lord an upper hand.<ref name="HoH-p93">{{Cite book/Hall of Heroes|93}}</ref>

Piergeiron did not fear battle, he was quite capable of defending himself thanks to years of service prior to his life as a ruler. He preferred diplomacy to battle, but when he was pressed into a physical confrontation he fought with the knowledge he was blessed by his god.<ref name="HoH-p92" />

==Activities==
[[File:LordsOfWaterdeep.png|thumb|right|250px|Piergeiron the Open Lord of [[Waterdeep]].]]

Every year on [[Ahghairon's Day]], Lord Piergeiron continued the tradition of stopping by a number of inns, taverns, and other establishments in order to offer his good will and best wishes to the Waterdhavian people.<ref name="CoS.W-p15">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|15}}</ref>

==Relationships==
Piergerion's father [[Athar]], "the Shining Knight", was famed for slaying the great [[Red dragon|red wyrm]] [[Kistarianth|Kistarianth the Red]].<ref name="CoS.W-p53" /> 

Piergeiron's first wife, [[Shaleen]], to whom he was married in his youth, died.  Piergeiron's second marriage was to [[Maethiira]], but she died as well in the {{YearlinkName|1344}}, leaving him with one daughter, [[Aleena Paladinstar]], who left Waterdeep to explore the [[Cosmology|planes]].<ref name="CoS.W-p53" /><ref name="CoS.W-p24">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|24}}</ref>

The '''Heroes of Waterdeep''' were an adventuring group famed for killing the first [[beholder]] to assume the name of [[Xanathar (title)|Xanathar]].<ref name="eotb">{{Cite game/Eye of the Beholder}}</ref>

==History==
===Initial assignment and rise to fame===
The group were appointed a mission of great urgency by the [[Lords of Waterdeep]]. Their task was to head down to the [[Waterdeep/Sewers|sewers of Waterdeep]] to investigate an unknown evil. They accepted the task and were given the [[commission and letter of Marque]], an official document describing their task.<ref name="eotb"/>

The adventurers achieved many feats, and were said to have aided a [[dwarf|dwarven]] clan: they rescued Prince [[Keirgar]] from the [[drow]], as well as curing King [[Teirgoh]] from a seemingly endless coma. After adventuring down twelve floors and acquiring the legendary ''[[Wand of Silvias]]'', they defeated Xanathar. The group destroyed the body of the evil beholder, ripped out his eye-stalk as proof of his death, and victoriously returned to the surface. They immediately reported to the lords.<ref name="eotb"/>

When the party entered, the [[Open Lord]], [[Piergeiron the Paladinson]], mocked them, stating that they had probably only slain a sewer [[rat]]. To his surprise, and to the joy of the Lords of Waterdeep, the group placed a beholder's eye-stalk—that of Xanathar—on his desk. After their heroic deed, the adventuring party were bestowed the honorary name of the "Heroes of Waterdeep".<ref name="eotb"/>

===Aftermath and second assignment===
After becoming a famed group, the heroes donated the historic artifacts they had found to the scholars of the city, who used them to help research Waterdeep's history. Later, their trusted friend [[Khelben Arunsun]] sent them a letter. He congratulated them in their great success over Xanathar, and informed them that he had sent healers to return them to full health. Khelben also informed them that another threat had arisen – the cult of [[Darkmoon]].<ref name="eotb2">{{Cite game/Eye of the Beholder II: The Legend of Darkmoon}}</ref> 

The Heroes of Waterdeep infiltrated the evil temple of Darkmoon, achieving great feats in the process. After slaying the evil cultists, they faced Darkmoon's leader, who turned out to be a [[polymorph|polymorphed]] [[red dragon]], known as [[Dran Draggore]].<ref name="eotb2"/>
