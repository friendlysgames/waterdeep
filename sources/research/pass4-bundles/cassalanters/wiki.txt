The '''Cassalanter''' ({{Pronounce|CASS|ah|lan|ter|refs=<ref name="CS-WW2e-p12">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|12}}</ref>}}, {{Pronounce|KAS|ah|lan|ter|alt=yes|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) [[Nobility of Waterdeep|noble house]] of [[Waterdeep]] was among one of the richest families within the city,<ref name="TW-23">{{Cite book/Tangled Webs/MMP (1998)|23}}</ref> having made their fortune in banking, money-lending and rumor-mongering for the highest payer.<ref name="WatN-p46">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|46}}</ref>

==Base of Operations==
[[File:Sea Ward-Assumbar-Cassalanter-Dezlentyr-Eirontalar Villa.png|thumb|right|270px|''Map showing the location of the Cassalanter Villa (labeled <code>$48</code>) ''circa'' 1372 DR.'']]
The family had a [[Cassalanter Villa|villa]] in the [[Waterdeep/Sea Ward|Sea Ward]], on the southwest corner of [[Delzorin Street]] and [[Diamond Street]].<ref name="CSW3e-p92,95">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|92,95}}</ref>

The Cassalanter family had an ancient burial crypt in the [[Dungeon Level]] of [[Undermountain]] with several of the family ancestors interred there. A Shrine of [[Myrkul]] was the part of the family crypt.<ref name="Dec" />

==History==
House Cassalanter was ennobled in the {{YearlinkName|1248}} when the [[Lords of Waterdeep]] recognized several merchant houses and granted them noble titles and seals.<ref name="CoS:W-p59">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|59}}</ref>

In [[1368 DR]], Cassalanters loaned two ten-million [[Golden lion (coin)|golden lions]] with a huge interest rate. The first loan was to [[Widow Silvermane]] who ran a [[lottery]] in the [[Waterdeep/North Ward|North Ward]] of Waterdeep, and the second one went to the [[Field of Triumph]] Race Track in the [[Waterdeep/Sea Ward|Sea Ward]]. The astronomical loans were caused by goddess [[Tymora|Tymora']]s luck going out of control all over the Realms. Every single gambler picked the winning number and winning [[horse]]s, costing Widow Silvermane and the Field of Triumph disastrous losses.<ref name="ty">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|101-104}}</ref>

In [[1370 DR]], a [[skeleton]] dressed in Cassalanter family garb was seen attacking several Waterdhavians. These incidents attracted the attention of the city's [[Lords of Waterdeep]]. The [[undead]] came from the family's old crypt in [[Undermountain]], and a lone hero was sent to investigate the restless Cassalanter ancestors. The hero successfully destroyed the undead and the [[ghoul]] [[necromancer]] behind the infestation, [[Ghoulmaster]] and the noble family offered a hefty reward for the deed.<ref name="Dec">{{Cite game/Descent to Undermountain}}</ref>

In the late 15{{th}} century DR, the Cassalanters faced imminent bankruptcy. In order to prevent it, in {{Yearlink|1489}}{{Waterdeep-time}} Ammalia and Victoro struck a deal with the [[archdevil]] [[Asmodeus]], trading the souls of their children in exchange for instant financial comeback. Their eldest son, Osvaldo, was taken immediately and transformed into a [[chain devil]], while the younger twins Elzerina and Terenzio were scheduled to be taken on the occasion of their 9{{th}} birthday, one [[tenday]] after [[Founders' Day]] of {{Yearlink|1492}}.<ref name="WDH5e-p115-116">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|115-116}}</ref>

Sometime before 1492 DR, one of the Ladies of House Cassalanter caused quite a stir by wearing a gown colored with a certain variety of loud maroon [[dye]] to one of her grand parties that took place in the [[Waterdeep/Sea Ward|Sea Ward]] of Waterdeep.<ref name="bg3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>

'''Victoro Cassalanter''' ({{Pronounce|vic|TOR|oh|_|KAS|ah|lan|ter|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was the lord of the [[Cassalanter]] family of [[Waterdeep]] in the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="WDH5e-p218" />
 
==Activities==
Victoro was schooled in the [[divine]] arts. He was the leader of a Cult of [[Asmodeus]] that involved certain nobles in Waterdeep.<ref name="WDH5e-p55">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|55}}</ref> Victoro was a priest of Asmodeus.<ref name="WDH5e-p218" />

On the surface, Victoro posed as a successful banker, philanthropist, and follower of [[Lathander]] but this was only a façade he needed for his businesses.<ref name="WDH5e-p218" />

==History==
In [[1489 DR]],<ref name="WDH5e-p116" />{{WDH-time}} Victoro and his wife [[Ammalia Cassalanter]] signed a contract with Asmodeus, in which they agreed to give up the souls of their children in exchange for power, good health, and long life. Their eldest son, [[Osvaldo Cassalanter|Osvaldo]], was taken immediately and transformed into a [[chain devil]]. [[Terenzio Cassalanter|Terenzio]] and [[Elzerina Cassalnter|Elzerina]], their youngest twins, were also scheduled to be taken by the [[archdevil]] when they turned nine years old in [[1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} to be transformed into [[lemure]]s. The only way to save the children was pay to the [[Nine Hells]] a tremendous amount of coin and the souls of numerous people in sacrifice.<ref name="WDH5e-p193-194">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|193-194}}</ref><ref name="WDH5e-p130">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|130}}</ref>

In 1492 DR, Victoro and his wife planned to recover the lost [[gold]] of [[Dagult Neverember]] and use it to buy back the souls of their children.<ref name="WDH5e-p6">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|6}}</ref> Victoro focused on searching for the gold while Ammalia was busy with other preparations for the ceremony to save the souls of their children.<ref name="WDH5e-p115">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|115}}</ref>

If Ammalia and Victoro presented the gold and ninety-nine souls to a [[devil]] servant of Asmodeus in time, then the souls of Terenzio and Elzerina would be safe.<ref name="WDH5e-p130" />

If the Cassalanters managed to obtain the ''[[Stone of Golorr]]'', five cultists protected the Stone inside the Cassalanter mausoleum in the [[City of the Dead (Waterdeep)|City of the Dead]]. However, two of the cultists, under the secret orders of Victoro Cassalanter, killed the other cultists and left one for dead in order to better hide their trails.<ref name="WDH5e-p115">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|115}}</ref>

Later, Victoro sent his [[doppelganger]] valet [[Willifort Crowelle]] to recover the Stone and return with it to the Cassalanter villa.<ref name="WDH5e-p60-62">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|60-62}}</ref>

==Personality==
Victoro was always polite and slow to anger but deep inside he was a self-interested individual. He cared only for gold and power and for that he sold the souls of his children to the [[Nine Hells]].<ref name="WDH5e-p218" />

The walled compound contained four separate buildings and several gardens. Each building stood between three and four stories tall.<ref name="CoS.CG-p41"/>

The '''Sea Ward of Waterdeep''' was the wealthiest ward in [[Waterdeep]] and located in the northwest of the city.<ref name="VGtW-p51" /><ref name="CoS.W-p91" />

==Geography==
The ward was delimited by the [[Trollwall]] on the north and west, by [[Julthoon Street]] to the south, and by [[Shield Street]] to the east, extending to the [[High Road (Waterdeep)|High Road]] north of [[Vondil Street]].<ref name="CS-AG2e-p15">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|52}}</ref>

The ward contained the [[Field of Triumph]], [[Heroes' Garden]] and the [[Sea's Edge Beach]].<ref name="CoS.W-p91" /> It also contained some of the city's largest temples.<ref name="CS-CG2e-p9">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|9}}</ref>

==History==
The Sea Ward was officially included as one of the official wards of Waterdeep in the {{YearlinkName|1276}}, together with the [[Waterdeep/North Ward|North Ward]].<ref name="GHR3e-p128" /><ref name="CS-CG2e-p31" />

'''Delzorin Street''' was a street in [[Waterdeep]] that ran east to west in the [[North Ward of Waterdeep|North Ward]]. The street ended on a left turn at [[Whaelgond Way]].<ref name="WDH-p166">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|166}}</ref>

==Notable Locations==
* [[Ulbrinter Villa]], the estate of the noble [[Ulbrinter]] family, was located here, between [[Vhezoar Street]] and [[Brondar's Way]].<ref name="WDH-p16">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|16}}</ref> Being the street's most remarkable building, the mansion was of beautiful architecture, with high-rising towers and conical turret-tops.<ref name="dm">{{Cite book/Death Masks}}</ref>
* The [[Massalan]] and [[Lanngolyn]] families also owned villas on Delzorin Street.<ref name="CoS.CG-p44">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|44}}</ref><ref name="CoSW-p94">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|94}}</ref>
* [[Yellowcrest Manor]], once the home of the [[Yellowcrest]] family, stood on Delzorin Street. After most of the Yellowcrests were horrifically murdered, the [[Falgarst]] family assumed ownership of the manor.<ref name="CM.BotR-p103">{{Cite book/Candlekeep Mysteries/Sarah of Yellowcrest Manor|103}}</ref>
* [[Nandar's Ride]], a small road leading up to the villa of the [[Nandar]] family.<ref name="Ask Ed">{{Cite web/Candlekeep forum/Questions for Ed Greenwood (2014)/Waterdeep alleys}}</ref>

* One of the rowhouses between Delzorin Street and Trollskull Alley was a [[Harper]] safehouse, and contained a [[magic]]al entryway to [[Harpers' Hold]].<ref name="Dr211">{{Cite dragon/211/"I Sing a Song by the Deep-Water Bay"}}</ref>

==Entrance==
===Yawning Portal===
The dungeon of the Undermountain was accessible via a winch over the pit in the common room of the [[Yawning Portal]], with a fee of 1 [[gp]] per person. It took roughly a minute to lower or raise the [[rope]]-and-[[pulley]] mechanism that powered the machine.<ref name="WDMM-p6"/> The well descended for {{SI|140|ft|long=yes}} to a floor of rough-hewn rock that was covered by a thin later of sand and the skeletal remains of a number of different creatures, some of which were [[humanoid]] in form.<ref name="RU.CGU-p18">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Campaign Guide to Undermountain|18}}</ref>

Sixty battered and cracked shields hung on the walls of the entrance chamber. They featured the heraldry of many [[Waterdhavian]] familial crests, some of which were recognizable as belong to the [[Nobility of Waterdeep|noble families]] of the 14{{th}} and 15{{th}} centuries [[DR]].<ref name="RU.CGU-p18"/> These ancient shields were so fragile that they broke when touched. ;The Grim Statue:
A sewer entrance that was under nobody's control led to the Dungeon Level. People had to come with their own ways to get to the ground and to deal with an electrical discharge similar to ''[[lightning bolt]]'' that happened every eight minutes. One way to deal with the latter was to cast ''dispel magic'' on either the ''lightning bolt'' or the statue. Doing the former dissipated the bolt, doing the latter cancelled the statue's next release. The door automatically closed at latest after a dozen days, something Halaster made sure happened. The statue itself hosted a secret entry into the Arcane Chamber, the second level, in the form of stairs that spiraled below.<ref name="TRUCG-9, 32-33">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Campaign Guide to Undermountain|9, 32-33}}</ref> The statue was located in the south-south-west of the Entry Hall.<ref name="TRULM1">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Level 1 map}}</ref>

==Features==
===Architecture===
Generally speaking, Undermountain's natural chambers boast high ceilings of at least {{SI|16|ft}} and smooth floors made of gray [[granite]]. Many halls, rooms, and galleries were created by carving out the natural rock and were generally decorated in a way that made them look uniform and mundane. That said, exceptions existed.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Campaign Guide to Undermountain|16}}</ref>

A corridor in Undermountain was usually {{SI|10|ft}} wide and high. Stairs typically had no railings.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

===Magical Features===
Undermountain had lots of [[magical]] peculiarities that were created and utilized by Halaster. Undermountain's proprietor made it impossible to cast magic that teleported people into or out of Undermountain. Various forms of divination magic would also not function, though some magic objects and spells like wizard eye worked to some degree. This made it challenging if not impossible for would-be treasure hunters to gather information about what the dungeon had in store or what Halaster was doing. Summoning spells also had limitations; their functionality was limited to moving monsters that were already present in Undermountain, or trapping monsters from outside. However, magic items that summoned creatures worked, at least as a general rule.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

Destroying any wards or magical effects that managed these limitations was a task that was as good as impossible. A high understanding of magic was required to understand the many-layered incantations, particular knowledge to determine how to dissolve these effects, the skill to actually dispel these high level magics, combat prowess to protect oneself from monsters that were certainly attracted to people working to dissolve the layered magic, the ability to do this work at a speed that exceeded the speed at which the magic was restored, a lot of time, and the willingness to undertake this task despite the thankless and likely fruitless nature.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

Another effect of the many-layered magic of the Mad Mage was that most of the surfaces and features of the [[dungeon]] radiated some degree of magic, including the floor, ceilings, and walls. This made spells like ''[[detect magic]]'' largely useless because either the radiated magic was so strong that it was impossible to distinguish specific information or because the radiated magic masked the magical properties of other things.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

The complex magics of Undermountain were intertwined with Halaster himself. In the event of the Mad Mage's death or removal from Undermountain, its enchantments and spells would go wild. Countless ''[[gate]]s'' and ''[[portal]]s'' would become destabilized and available for many powerful monsters to utilize. The '''North Ward''' was the northeastern sector of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="WatN-p24" /><ref name="CoS-p92" /> It was famous for being the quietest ward of the city, consisting mostly of residential areas.<ref name="VGtW-p81" />

==Geography==
The North Ward was delimited by the walls of the [[Waterdeep/City of the Dead|City of the Dead]] to the southeast and the [[Trollwall]] to the east and north. Its southern border was at [[Andamaar's Street]] and [[Trader's Way]] up to [[Shield Street]], which marked the western border until [[Vondil Street]]. North of that, the western border was defined by the [[High Road]] the rest of the way to the Trollwall.<ref name="CoS.AG-12">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|12}}</ref>

The architectural style of the North Ward was dominated by rowhouses, which filled the gaps between the villas of lesser [[Nobility of Waterdeep|noble houses]]. The ward also counted with a variety of taverns and shops.<ref name="VGtW-p81" /><ref name="WDH5e-p176">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|176}}</ref>

===Geographical Features===
The most prominent feature of the North Ward was the [[Cliffwatch]], a section of the Waterdeep plateau that was so steep and high that it provided a natural defense, so the area was marked by an interruption of the Trollwall between the [[Endcliff Tower]] and the [[Watchway Tower]] in the City of the Dead. A walkway traversed the entire Cliffwatch, along which some of the best inns and taverns of Waterdeep were located, taking advantage of the beautiful landscape to the east.<ref name="WDH5e-p176" /><ref name="CS-CG2e-p43">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|43}}</ref>

This stadium stood over five stories tall.<ref name="CoS.W-p92">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|92}}</ref> Its main entrance was known as the Lion Gate, due to it being carved to give visitors the impression that they were walking into the maw of a gigantic [[lion]].<ref name="CoS.AGthC-16">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|16}}</ref><ref name="VGtW-p51">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|51}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p174">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|174}}</ref>

==Activities==
The Field of Triumph was the venue for many public spectacles from late spring until late autumn, weather permitting. The [[Open Lord]] of Waterdeep used the Field as a public forum to give proclamations to the city, but most of the time it was host to exciting events such as [[horse]] races, shows of [[magic]] and [[illusion]], martial displays of formation and skill, gladiatorial combats, and exhibitions of strange and wondrous creatures and monsters from all across Faerûn and beyond. Attendance often numbered in the thousands.<ref name="CoS.CG-41" /><ref name="CoS.AGthC-17">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|17}}</ref>
===Holidays===
During [[Waukeentide]], this coliseum was one of the buildings that the [[Guilds of Waterdeep]] traditionally held the gala festival and dance of [[Guildsmeet]] in.<ref name=CoS.AGttC-p29">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|29}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p185">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|185}}</ref> On [[Mirtul 9]], the final event of the holiday [[Plowing and Running]] would be held here.<ref name="WDH-p185" /> And on [[Founders' Day]], the stadium would be host to illusory shows of Waterdeep's history and martial exhibitions by the [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]] and other warriors of note.<ref name=CoS.AGttC-p28">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|28}}</ref>

Once every four years, during [[Shieldmeet]], the [[Lords of Waterdeep]] would gather on the Field of Triumph to be reaffirmed as leaders of the city. They appeared in person, seated on sixteen thrones, wearing covered helms and [[robe]]s that disguised their identities.<ref>{{Cite book/Elfsong|207,297}}</ref>

==History==
Ghoulmaster was a sinister intelligent [[ghoul]] who worshiped Lolth, the Queen of Spiders, in the mid–14{{th}} century DR. Ghoulmaster discovered an old Shrine of [[Myrkul]] within the ancient crypt of the [[Cassalanter]] family on the [[Dungeon Level]] of [[Undermountain]]. '''Founders' Day''' was an annual holiday celebrating the founding of the city of [[Waterdeep]]. It was held on [[Flamerule 1]].<ref name="WDH5e-p186" /><ref name="CS-AGC2e-p28" />

==Activities==
Several locations in the city commemorated the holiday. In the [[Field of Triumph]], the history of the city was retold through [[illusion|illusory]] displays. The stadium was also the site of martial exhibitions by the [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]].<ref name="WDH5e-p186" /><ref name="CS-AGC2e-p28" />

It was also common for festhalls throughout the city to hold costume contests. Participants dressed up as historical figures such as [[Raurlor]] and [[Khelben Arunsun|Khelben the Elder]] and the best impersonators were awarded prizes.<ref name="WDH5e-p186" /><ref name="CS-AGC2e-p28" />

A particularly spectacular practice was the veiling of [[Castle Waterdeep]] with an illusion that made it appear like the ancient fortress of [[Nimoar]]. The illusion required the combined efforts of several [[wizard]]s and was typically in place between midday and sunset. It was considered an extraordinary display of magical art.<ref name="WDH5e-p186" />

==History==
In the mid‒14{{th}} century [[DR]], the practice of covering multiple locations of the city in illusions was deemed distracting and frivolous and was abolished for several decades.<ref name="CS-AGC2e-p28" /> However, by the late 15{{th}} century DR, at least the custom of masking Castle Waterdeep had been reinstated.<ref name="WDH5e-p186" />

On Founders' Day in the {{YearlinkName|1492}},{{Waterdeep-time}} [[Ammalia Cassalanter]] planned to [[poison]] multiple people during a feast in order to offer their souls as sacrifice to [[Asmodeus]] in an attempt to save the souls of her younger children, while at the same time her husband [[Victoro Cassalanter]] planned to steal the hoard of [[Dragon (coin)|dragon coins]] from the [[Vault of Dragons]] as the monetary counterpart of the deal.<ref name="WDH5e-p115">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|115}}</ref>

=== LINKED WIKI PAGE (first 5000 chars): Marking the years

{{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

=== Masked Lords ===
The Lords of Waterdeep were only seen in public when sitting in the Lord's Court or when appearing every four years at [[Shieldmeet]] Council when the Lord's Rule is reaffirmed.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The identities of the Lords, other than the Open Lord, were not supposed to be known to the public, although they were known to each other.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The intent of this was to ensure that the Lords were free of coercion and could be drawn from any social strata within the city.<ref name="CoS.CG-p67" /> While the masked Lords were considered to be equal to the Open Lord in authority, some executive authority was reserved for the Open Lord, including the vetting and selection of new Lords,<ref name="CS.W-p52" /> the naming of the next Open Lord,<ref>{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|31}}</ref> and determining the needed number of Lords.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" />

'''''Tymora's Luck''''', by [[Kate Novak]] and [[Jeff Grubb]], is the third and final novel in [[The Lost Gods]] trilogy.

{{Quote|Before the [[Dawn Cataclysm]], [[Moander|Moander the Darkbringer]] corrupted [[Tyche|Tyche, Goddess of Luck]]. In a desperate attempt to preserve Tyche's goodness the gods clove her in twain, creating two daughter goddesses: [[Tymora|Tymora, Lady Luck]]; and [[Beshaba|Beshaba, Lady Doom]]. In the eons since then, the two sisters have existed in total enmity.''

''Now a great power has hatched a mad scheme to re-create the goddess Tyche by reuniting Tymora and Beshaba, regardless of the potentially calamitous consequences.''

''In a decision fraught with godly intrigue, [[Joel the Rebel Bard|Joel, The Rebel Bard]], priest of [[Finder]], is chosen to uncover whoever is behind the abduction of the sister goddesses. Aided by his old allies, [[Holly Harrowslough]] and [[Jasmine|Jas]], and his new friend, the [[kender]] [[Emilo Haversack]], Joel must find a way to prevent the merger of Tymora and Beshaba before disaster overtakes the luckless Realms.''}}

==Summary==
[[Lathander]], upset when [[Chauntea]] rebuffs his advances, is approached by [[Iyachtu Xvim]] in the guise of [https://dragonlance.fandom.com/wiki/Sirrion Sirrion of the Flowing Flame], who offers to combine [[Tymora]] and [[Beshaba]] back into [[Tyche]]. In truth, Xvim plans to absorb both goddesses. They begin draining power from both goddesses. 

In [[Sigil]], [[Joel]] and [[Jasmine|Jas]] meet [[Emilo Haversack]], a [[Kender]] who arrived in the city after jumping into an unstable vortex. The deity [[Bahamut|Fizban]] granted him ability to remain imperceptible unless he speaks or is introduced. 

Jas is attacked by the church of Xvim, so she agrees to go to Finder’s domain to remove the dark stalker she believes was forced on her. They arrive in [[Fermata]], where Finder realizes that Jas is not actually a dark stalker, she just has the ability to shapeshift, which is controlled by her emotions. 

Jas agrees to go to [[Brightwater]] to ask for Tymora’s help, despite her grudge from being granted her wings by Tymora long ago. While pretending to remove the dark stalker, Tymora falls unconscious. 

Good and bad luck fluctuate wildly across the Realms. 

Because Tymora said “Beshaba” before falling, Joel, Emilo and Jas go to the [[Abyss]], where they learn Beshaba is missing too. They then go to [[Gehenna]], meeting up with [[Holly Harrowslough]] and Walindra, who has devoted herself to worshiping Beshaba and raised an army to reclaim her from Xvim’s fortress. Walidra dies, but they rescue Beshaba and are taking her to the Spire on the Outlands when Holly lets Lathander abduct her. Xvim reveals himself and tries to absorb the goddesses, but is stopped when Holly refuses to obey Lathander’s command to combine the goddesses against their will, and the fusion is used to reform the [[Finder's Stone]]. The goddesses escape, and Beshaba curses Lathander with bad luck. Lathander asks Tymora for good luck to counter the curse, but she replies that having Holly as a paladin is already more good luck than he deserves.
