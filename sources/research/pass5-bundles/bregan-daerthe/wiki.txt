'''Bregan D'aerthe''' ({{Pronounce|BRAY|gan|_|DAIRTH|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was an elite [[drow]] mercenary and later mercantile group founded and led by [[Jarlaxle]], primarily based in [[Menzoberranzan]] in the [[Underdark]].<ref name="H-p?" /> 

==Activities==
Bregan D'aerthe was primarily a mercenary and mercantile band. Members, both individuals and the band as a whole, were available for hire to the highest bidder.<ref name="H-p?" /><ref name="Extinct-p16">{{Cite book/Extinction/Paperback|16}}</ref> Bregan D'aerthe was involved in most conflicts in Menzoberranzan in one way or another.<ref name="RGLoD-p55" /> The band expanded to trade goods from the surface to Menzoberranzan and the Underdark.<ref name="TPK-p?" /><ref name="G-p?">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|?}}</ref> For example, they traded [[Netherese]] artifacts to Menzoberranzan via Luskan.<ref name="NotH-p309">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|309}}</ref>

Bregan D'aerthe was also an information broker, willing to provide information for both sides of a conflict—for the right price. Its contacts reached even as far as the [[svirfneblin]] city of [[Blingdenstone]].<ref name="H-p?" /><ref name="M.tH-pp63-64" /><ref name="Exile-p?">{{Cite book/Exile/MMP (1990)|?}}</ref>

Bregan D'aerthe had a reputation that normally allowed its agents to skip lines. Its members were given "credit" — often they didn't need to pay because of the threat of retaliation from Bregan D'aerthe.<ref name="Annih-pp86-87">{{Cite book/Annihilation/Paperback|86-87}}</ref>

==Base of Operations==
Bregan D'aerthe's main headquarters were hidden in the [[Clawrift]] in Menzoberranzan.<ref name="RGLoD-p122">{{Cite book/A Reader's Guide to R. A. Salvatore's the Legend of Drizzt|122}}</ref> However, they had a number of smaller headquarters and hide-outs in many locations throughout Menzoberranan and Ched Nasad.<ref name="M.tH-pp63-64" />

The band established a presence on the surface in order to further the band's influence as well as trade for Menzoberranzan. In [[1366 DR]], it briefly had a surface base in [[Calimport]].<ref name="SotS-p?" /> 

By [[1377 DR]], Bregan D'aerthe had developed a significant surface presence in [[Luskan]] and used [[Ship Kurth]] as its outward face.<ref name="TPK-p?" /> By [[1483 DR]], the drow agent [[Beniago]], disguised as a [[human]], was [[High Captain]] [[Kurth]].<ref name="TC-p341">{{Cite book/The Companions/Hardcover|341|26}}</ref> Bregan D'aerthe headquarters in Luskan were underground in [[Illusk]]. There was a path that connected the base to [[Closeguard Isle]] and [[Ship Kurth]]. Jarlaxle had a private room in this base.<ref name="NotH-p101">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|101}}</ref>
[[File:Bregan_d'aerthe-5e.jpg|thumb|''Bregan D'aerthe in the [[Dock Ward of Waterdeep|Dock Ward]] of [[Waterdeep]].'']]

==Possessions==
Members of Bregan D'aerthe carried an insignia of the band that was widely recognized throughout the Underdark, including in [[Sschindylryn]].<ref name="Annih-p79">{{Cite book/Annihilation/Paperback|79}}</ref> Unlike a house insignia, the Bregan D'aerthe insignia did not always allow its bearer to levitate, however<ref name="Insurr-p77">{{Cite book/Insurrection/Paperback|77}}</ref> (although in [[1484 DR]], [[Braelin Janquay]] was able to use his insignia to do so<ref name="NotH-p113">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|113}}</ref>).

Valas Hune had an upside-down cameo trinket of deep green jade that allowed him to send messages to his band when he touched it. Whether this trinket was the same as his insignia was unclear.<ref name="Annih-p273">{{Cite book/Annihilation/Paperback|273}}</ref>

In the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]], the organization had acquired the [[Lantan]]ese [[submarine]] ''[[Scarlet Marpenoth]]'', which was employed as a secret base for Jarlaxle.<ref name="WDH5e-pp132,141-145">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|132,141-145}}</ref>

==Relationships==
Bregan D'aerthe enjoyed relative freedom from the Ruling Council, mostly because of its close relationship with [[House Baenre]] (into which Jarlaxle had in fact been born). The relationship began with Matron [[Yvonnel Baenre]] but continued with her successors, [[Triel Baenre|Triel]] and later [[Quenthel Baenre]].<ref name="M.CoI-pp64-67" /> Jarlaxle also had a mutually beneficial relationship with his powerful brother, [[Gromph Baenre]], [[Archmage of Menzoberranzan]], who often used Jarlaxle to undermine their sisters' power.<ref name="NotH-p231">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|231}}</ref><ref name="VotID-p328>{{Cite book/Vengeance of the Iron Dwarf/Hardcover|328}}</ref><ref name="VotID-p307-308">{{Cite book/Vengeance of the Iron Dwarf/Hardcover|307-308}}</ref>

In the late fifteenth century DR, Bregan D'aerthe became concerned about the expansion of the [[Jaezred Chaulssin]], viewing them as religious zealots and fearing that the group would draw away potential Bregan D'aerthe recruits.<ref name="M.CoI-p67">{{Cite book/Menzoberranzan: City of Intrigue|67}}</ref><ref name="M.CoI-p65">{{Cite book/Menzoberranzan: City of Intrigue|65}}</ref> 

Among the houses of Menzoberranzan, Bregan D'aerthe was concerned about the fast growth of [[House Hunzrin]], led by Matron [[Shakti Hunzrin]]. Although Jarlaxle was able to have a firm grip on most mercantile operations in the city, he failed to be able to reign in the growth of the Hunzrin's mercantile empire.<ref name="M.CoI-p67" />

==History==
[[File:Jarlaxle.jpg|thumb|250px|''Jarlaxle, the leader of Bregan D'aerthe.'']]
===Founding===
Bregan D'aerthe was founded by the houseless rogue Jarlaxle, choosing to separate from the matriarchal drow society. It was built of mainly rogue drow males from destroyed houses of Menzoberranzan and [[Ched Nasad]]. It was widely employed by the ruling houses of Menzoberranzan, especially by [[House Baenre]], which had the resources to hire the group regularly, particularly given that Jarlaxle himself was a Baenre.<ref name="RGLoD-p55-56">{{Cite book/A Reader's Guide to R. A. Salvatore's the Legend of Drizzt|55-56}}</ref>

The group soon had an important role on major events at Menzoberranzan; for instance, it was hired by [[House Do'Urden]] to assist on the attack on the fifth house, [[House Hun'ett]], and it played a significant role in the demise of [[House Do'Urden]].<ref name="H-p?" />

===10{{th}} century DR===
Jarlaxle begins the organization slowly having [[Arathis Hune]] as his first Lieutenant.

===11{{th}} century DR=== 
In [[1018 DR]] Jarlaxle and [[Arathis Hune]] took part on a raid of [[House Simfray]] and were paid to acquire [[Zaknafein]] Simfray as a weaponsmaster for [[House Do'Urden]] and patron for [[Malice Do'Urden]].

From time to time [[Zaknafein Do'Urden]] was later used in other Bregan D'aerthe operations.<ref>{{Cite book/Timeless}}</ref>
Through mercenary opportunities in house to house infighting - the opportunity to acquire houseless rogues for the organization results in doubled membership by [[1118 DR]]. The rise in membership and demonstrated effectiveness of the band afforded it more respect and fear amongst matron mothers of the city. In order to protect the organization during this delicate time Jarlaxle chose to stay in Menzoberranzan and send Arathis Hune on jobs in Ched Nessad when required. For the first time ever the band has a castoff priestess as a part of its resources.<ref name="B-p?">{{Cite book/Boundless|?}}</ref>

===12{{th}} Century DR===
In [[1118 DR]] Year of the False Bargain, Jarlaxle celebrated 15 decades of Bregan D'aerthe but particularly the previous 100 years of growth. Conflicts and jealousy ensued as did attention to one of the affiliates of the band [[Zaknafein Do'Urden]]. In order to keep internal tensions down Jarlaxle enlisted a young [[Kimmuriel Oblodra]] a position in the band as a second lieutenant.  Conspiracy within the organization and from without by [[House Barrison Del'Armgo]] tests Jarlaxle's leadership and results in the death of [[Arathis Hune]] who had performed vital services of the organization.<ref name="B-p?" />

===14{{th}} century DR===
In [[1358 DR]], Bregan D'aerthe played a role in the drow attack on [[Mithral Hall]]. After the battle was over, the [[illithid]] advisor to Matron [[Yvonnel Baenre]], [[Methil El-Viddenvelp]], was believed dead. However, in truth he had been badly injured but was rescued by Bregan D'aerthe<ref name="DDGttU-p65">{{Cite book/Drizzt Do'Urden's Guide to the Underdark|65}}</ref> and brought back to the tunnels outside of the city, where he remained for more than a century, advising the band and serving as an object of study for [[Kimmuriel Oblodra]], a [[psion]] and one of the band's leaders.<ref name="NotH-p27">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|27}}</ref>

Circa [[1360 DR]], [[House Xorlarrin]] hired Bregan D'aerthe to help protect the [[Lustrum]] mines.<ref name="DDGttU-p67-68">{{Cite book/Drizzt Do'Urden's Guide to the Underdark|67-68}}</ref>

In [[1366 DR]], Bregan D'aerthe briefly expanded to a surface base in [[Calimport]], in part due to the influence of [[Crenshinibon]] on Jarlaxle. The [[human]] [[Artemis Entreri]] helped the group make contacts in Calimport.<ref name="SotS-p?" /> That endeavor did not last long, but Bregan D'aerthe soon began looking for contacts in the northern city of [[Luskan]]. Their first contact was [[Morik the Rogue]].<ref name="SotW">{{Cite book/The Spine of the World}}</ref> By [[1377 DR]], Bregan D'aerthe had developed a significant surface presence in [[Luskan]] and used [[Ship Kurth]] as its outward face.<ref name="TPK-p?" />

During Jarlaxle and Artemis Entreri's exploits in the [[Bloodstone Lands]] (around [[1368 DR]]), Kimmuriel increasingly took more command of Bregan D'aerthe while its main leader was gone.<ref name="TPK-p?" /> When Jarlaxle and Entreri departed that area, Bregan D'aerthe took [[Calihye]], who had been Entreri's lover. What happened to her after that was unknown.<ref name="NotH-p243">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|243}}</ref>

In [[1369 DR]], Bregan D'aerthe acted in [[Ust Natha]] in connection with [[House Jae'llat]] and [[Deirex]] the [[lich]].<ref name="BGII.SoA">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate II: Shadows of Amn}}</ref>

During the [[Siege of Menzoberranzan]] ([[1372]]-[[1373 DR]]), Bregan D'aerthe provided intelligence on the siege for city leaders. Jarlaxle, however, was not in the city at the time.<ref name="Annih-p76">{{Cite book/Annihilation/Paperback|76}}</ref> Meanwhile, Bregan D'aerthe agent [[Valas Hune]] was investigating the [[Silence of Lolth]], having been hired by [[Matron Mother]] [[Triel Baenre]] to escort [[Quenthel Baenre]]'s expedition through the Underdark.<ref name="Annih-p?">{{Cite book/Annihilation}}</ref>

In [[1376 DR|1376]]-[[1377 DR]] Bregan D'aerthe played a role in the fall of the Host Tower of the Arcane in Luskan and the subsequent grapple for dominance between Captain [[Deudermont]], who was supported by [[Waterdhavian]] nobles and the [[High Captains (Luskan)|High Captains]]. They secured trade relations with Luskan during this incident, aligning with [[Ship Kurth]]. Jarlaxle later used that connection to make a deal with [[Marchion]] [[Elastul Raurym]], opening trade with the city of [[Mirabar]].<ref name="TPK-p?" />

===15{{th}} century DR===
[[File:Bregan D'Aerthe Spy.jpg|thumb|200px|''A 15{{th}} century Bregan D'aerthe spy in [[Neverwinter]].'']]
In [[1466 DR]], Bregan D'aerthe, at the bequest of Jarlaxle, assaulted [[Draygo Quick]]'s compound in [[Gloomwrought]] in the [[Shadowfell]] to free his prisoners: [[Drizzt Do'Urden]] and the petrified [[Dahlia Sin'felle]] and [[Artemis Entreri]].<ref name="TLT-p?">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold}}</ref> In [[1469 DR]], Draygo Quick was continuing to work with Bregan D'aerthe; [[Kimmuriel Oblodra]] had agreed to share information he gathered from working with the [[illithids]] in return for forgiveness for the assault on Quick's home.<ref name="TC-pp146-147">{{Cite book/The Companions/Hardcover|146-147|12}}</ref>

Bregan D'aerthe had made strong headway in Luskan. By [[1483 DR]], the drow agent [[Beniago]], disguised as a [[human]], was [[High Captain]] [[Kurth]].<ref name="TC-p341">{{Cite book/The Companions/Hardcover|341|26}}</ref> Bregan D'aerthe also had its eyes set on the city of [[Neverwinter]].<ref name="M.CoI-pp64-67">{{Cite book/Menzoberranzan: City of Intrigue|64-67}}</ref> It retained a number of agents in the city in order to maintain an understanding of the forces at play and pounce on an opportunity for profit, should it arise.<ref name="NCS-pp44-45,121">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|44-45,121}}</ref>

In [[1484 DR]], Bregan D'aerthe was hired by Matron Mother Quenthel Baenre to clean up [[House Do'Urden]] in Menzoberranzan, as well as to make up the garrison for that house. For a brief time, two-thirds of the members of Bregan D'aerthe were serving in the garrison, until Jarlaxle began swapping them out and sending them back to Luskan. Jarlaxle was assigned the title of Captain of the Guard, although he bribed [[Gromph Baenre]] with [[psionic]]s lessons from Kimmuriel to get out of his duties and escape the city.<ref name="NotH">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter}}</ref><ref name="RotK">{{Cite book/Rise of the King}}</ref>

==Members==
[[File:Drowgunslinger.png|thumb|326x326px|''A member of Bregan D'aerthe.'']]
Most of the band's members were males from destroyed houses, primarily fighters, thieves, and mages, with very few members having [[cleric]]al powers.<ref name="M.tH-pp63-64" /> Although the band had some non-drow members (particularly [[hobgoblin]]s and [[bugbear]]s), exceptions were made on occasion but only drow members could achieve the highest ranks.<ref name="M.CoI-pp64-67" /><ref name="WDH5e-p15" />

===Leadership===
After the debacle with Crenshinibon in [[1366 DR]], Jarlaxle left the organization in the capable hands of his trusted lieutenant [[Kimmuriel Oblodra]] and adventured on the surface with [[Artemis Entreri]]. Kimmuriel longed for a time when he could return to his lieutenant status and let Jarlaxle take over again, allowing him to return to his personal [[psionics|psionic]] studies.<ref name="SotS-p?" />

Some time after his adventures in the [[Bloodstone Lands]], Jarlaxle returned and resumed his throne as leader of Bregan D'aerthe, still working in the shadows of society to establish trade and profits from the surface world, particularly in Luskan.<ref name="TPK-p?" /> Kimmuriel was still his second and was frequently the public face of dealings with the mercenary band while Jarlaxle ran off adventuring on the surface with [[Athrogate]] and others. Kimmuriel was, however, given the power to overrule Jarlaxle if he felt his master's involvement in matters of the surface world threatened the organisation as a whole. As time went on (by [[1463 DR]], Kimmuriel became widely understood as the band's leader,<ref name="CC-p277">{{Cite book/Charon's Claw/Hardcover|277}}</ref> and indeed Jarlaxle undertook missions for Kimmuriel and paid him for the services of Bregan D'aerthe when he wished to use the band for personal missions.<ref name="G-p221">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|221|15}}</ref> However, by [[1484 DR]], with Kimmuriel away at an illithids' hive, Jarlaxle was once again seen as an equal co-leader with Kimmuriel.<ref name="NotH-p16">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|16}}</ref>

After the expansion of Bregan D'aerthe, it was broken up into a number of cells led by lieutenants of equal standing, each of whom was given jurisdiction over an area of interest for the group. The cells were, for the most part, independent, although they still gave allegiance to Jarlaxle and Kimmuriel. For example, [[Beniago]] was the lieutenant largely responsible for Luskan.<ref name="M.CoI-p67">{{Cite book/Menzoberranzan: City of Intrigue|67}}</ref>

'''Jarlaxle''' ({{Pronounce|jar|LÆX|ul|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was a [[drow]] mercenary and leader of the [[Bregan D'aerthe]] mercenary company. He was one of a small number of male drow who actually had significant power in a [[Lolth]]-worshiping city,<ref name="NotH-p189">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|189}}</ref> although he increasingly spent little time in the city itself as he adventured on the [[Faerûn|surface]].<ref name="SotS"/>

==Description==
Slender, handsome, and tightly muscled, Jarlaxle was the dandy of [[Menzoberranzan]].<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55">{{Cite book/A Reader's Guide to R. A. Salvatore's the Legend of Drizzt|?}}</ref>

==Personality==
{{Fq
| text = Who is this maestro, this puppet master, pulling the strings of so many [[marionette]]s?<br/>Who is this maestro, turning the wheels of [[Faerûn]], playing the music of fate to the ears of all who would listen?
| source = [[Drizzt Do'Urden]]<ref name="mae" />
}}
More than anything, Jarlaxle was an opportunist. His wealth was considerable, but official station in drow society meant nothing to him. He observed that the best way for a male to survive and prosper in the matriarchal drow society was to be outside the system and to be too valuable to be eliminated—and he was audacious enough to take his life in that direction. Always calm and graceful, Jarlaxle enjoyed flaunting his independence and was proud of the fact that the noble houses came to him for aid. In the city of Menzoberranzan, intrigue and double-dealing were a way of life, and no one played them better than Jarlaxle did.<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55"/>

As a male outsider who had no qualms dealing with the "lesser races", Jarlaxle had much information few others beyond Lolth's priestesses were likely to have access to. He was one of the most powerful males, if not individuals, in the city and the surrounding Underdark.<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55"/> He benefited from the chaos of Menzoberranzan and was always able to align himself with the winning side in any conflict. In his earlier years, he thrived on the internal politics of Menzoberranzan, but they had always disturbed him. In his later years, he preferred to see adventure and profit in the World Above.<ref name="RotK-p265">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|265}}</ref> Once he escaped the "wretched hole in the ground," he did not want to return.<ref name="NotH-p232">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|232}}</ref> He found that he felt most alive when he was with friends and when he was taking risks.<ref name="TLT-p291">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold/Hardcover|291}}</ref> He had an "insatiable curiosity and a desire to know—everything."<ref name="RotK-p136">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|136}}</ref> This curiosity made him antsy when he remained stuck in one place too long and drove his quest for personal exploration.<ref name="RotK-p72">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|72}}</ref>

He had a very charismatic personality, always willing to talk his way out of situations rather than resort to force of arms. Moreover, talking was what Jarlaxle did best. His words easily worked past the handicap of his race's evil reputation, able to turn potential enemies into allies. He loved irony, sarcasm, and wit. He was extremely intelligent and a brilliant tactician who was always prepared for any imaginable or unimaginable circumstances.<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55"/> Jarlaxle's lieutenant [[Kimmuriel Oblodra]] revealed a deep understanding of Jarlaxle's personality, recognizing that his gambling and sometimes foolish-seeming attitude was a very good façade, underneath which lay a frighteningly intelligent mind.<ref name="TGK-p??">{{Cite book/The Ghost King/Hardcover|??}}</ref>

Jarlaxle preferred to avoid killing someone as long as that person could be manipulated to serve his interests, no matter how small his or her role might be. In fact, Jarlaxle didn't like to kill somebody unless he had to, an unusual trait in a drow—and likely the reason he did so in the first place. Several times, he played the hero just for the sake of it.<ref name="BotR-EJ">{{Cite book/The Best of the Realms/Empty Joys|?}}</ref>

Jarlaxle was also flirtatious. He had no qualms about flirting with (and sleeping with) dragons.<ref name="RotK-p289-290">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|289-290}}</ref>

==History==
===Early Life===
Jarlaxle was born to [[Yvonnel Baenre]], the [[matron mother]] of [[House Baenre]], the first house of Menzoberranzan. Due to the status of his birth order, Jarlaxle was to be sacrificed to [[Lolth]]. However, the sacrificial blade of Matron Baenre could not pierce the infant. Her second-oldest son, [[Doquaio Baenre]], was ordered to assist, but as he grabbed the infant, all of the kinetic energy of the attempted sacrifice was reflected on to him through the [[psionic]] power of the matron mother of [[House Oblodra]]. Doquaio was killed instead.<ref name="RotP-p290-291">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|290-291|19}}</ref><ref name="RotP-p296-297">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|296-297|20}}</ref> The failure of the sacrifice was kept secret, but Matron Baenre feared Jarlaxle from that time forward and believed that her fate was tied with his, while Jarlaxle was viewed as favored by the Spider Queen. Indeed, Lolth did favor him as an agent of chaos and granted him the memory of his near-sacrifice. However, he rejected her and she withdrew her favor, although the matron mothers of Menzoberranzan never learned of that fact.<ref name="RotP-p77">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|77|5}}</ref> Kimmuriel Oblodra was one of only three living drow who knew the truth of the story;<ref name="RotP-p77"/> the event and involvement of Matron Oblodra was apparently passed down through the years among the members of House Oblodra.<ref name="RotP-p290-291"/>

Jarlaxle attended [[Melee-Magthere]], the warriors' academy. He was recognized for his mastery of strategy and tactics and his strength was in the non-physical aspects of fighting, although he was a skilled swordsman as well. However, he favored a stealthy dagger over hand-to-hand combat.<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55"/>

In the {{YearlinkName|1018}},<ref name="Timeless-p?">{{Cite book/Timeless|?}}</ref> Jarlaxle got to know [[Zaknafein Do'Urden]], later the father of [[Drizzt Do'Urden]]. They became close friends in time, but he eventually betrayed Zaknafein in undisclosed circumstances and still felt guilty over it many years later.<ref name="RotP-p348">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|348|24}}</ref>

===Bregan D'aerthe===
Jarlaxle later left his house and his name (but not his connections) behind and founded [[Bregan D'aerthe]], a mercenary band of rogue males that grew to be very powerful. It could be said that the band forged its own underworld empire in Menzoberranzan. Bregan D'aerthe was present among the troops of Baenre during the fall of [[House Do'Urden]].<ref name="H">{{Cite book/Homeland/MMP (1990)|?}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|1357}}, Jarlaxle worked with [[Artemis Entreri]] during the early phases of Baenre's attempted conquest of [[Mithral Hall]]. Entreri had requested a fight against Drizzt Do'Urden—a fair one—and offered his services in return. However, a fair fight was not, at the time, able to be given.<ref name="TL">{{Cite book/The Legacy/Paperback (1993)|?}}</ref> Entreri "joined" Bregan D'aerthe and was led to Menzoberranzan, but there he felt trapped and fled the Underdark (with Jarlaxle's implicit aid) with Drizzt and Catti-brie.<ref name="SN">{{Cite book/Starless Night/Paperback (1994)|?}}</ref>

Jarlaxle arranged another fight between Entreri and Drizzt Do'Urden in the {{yearlinkName|1364}}. During the fight, he acquired [[Crenshinibon]]. At this time, Jarlaxle approached Entreri with the hopes of extending his empire to the surface, starting with [[Calimport]]. In the {{YearlinkName|1366}}, they created a faction out of the [[House Basadoni|Basadoni guild]] and placed Entreri as its [[pasha]]. Jarlaxle increasingly fell under the influence of Crenshinibon until his two lieutenants, Kimmuriel and [[Rai-guy Bondalek]], enacted a plot to overthrow him. He was only rescued from his own follies by Entreri, who claimed to need Jarlaxle alive to protect him from the two drow lieutenants. Jarlaxle made an agreement with Kimmuriel for co-leadership of Bregan D'aerthe while Jarlaxle went traveling around the surface.<ref name="SotS"/>

By [[1490 DR]], Artemis Entreri was considered Jarlaxle’s second-in-command and Kimmuriel remained as joint leader alongside Jarlaxle.<ref>{{Cite book/Starlight Enclave|56|3}}</ref>

===Bloodstone Lands===
After leaving Calimport, Jarlaxle and Entreri eventually wound up working in the [[Bloodstone Lands]] (namely [[Damara]] and [[Vaasa]]) in the {{YearlinkName|1368}}. He and Entreri started working for [[Ilnezhara]] and [[Tazmikella]], the [[copper dragon]] sisters in [[Heliogabalus]], who wanted them to investigate the appearance of [[Zhengyi]]an constructs in [[Vaasa]]. They joined a party of adventurers, battled the [[dracolich]] [[Urshula]], and invaded a replica fortress of [[Castle Perilous]]. Jarlaxle disappeared during the fight, drawing Entreri's mistrust, but Jarlaxle appeared and claimed the castle for "King Artemis I of the [[Ostraland|Kingdom of D'aerthe]]".<ref name="PotWK">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/Hardcover (2005)|?}}</ref><ref name="RotP-p?">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|?}}</ref> In Damara, Jarlaxle obtained ''[[Idalia's flute]]'' and gave it to Entreri in an apparent attempt to help him resolve his emotional problems.<ref name="TLT-p249" /> Instead, the flute brought up all sorts of memories, leading Artemis back to Calimshan to search for (and murder) his father. He refused to travel with Jarlaxle further after that point. However, [[Athrogate]] remained by Jarlaxle's side.<ref name="PotWK"/><ref name="RotP-p348"/>

===Later Travels===
{{Fq
| text = I am Jarlaxle. I have been everywhere.
| source = Jarlaxle in [[1484 DR]]<ref name="RotK-p177-178">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|177-178}}</ref>
}}
During his travels with Artemis, Jarlaxle stayed in regular contact with his lieutenant Kimmuriel, in whose capable hands he left Bregan D'aerthe. Although he did not at the time take an active role in leading his mercenaries, he still requested the resources of the group when it suited his needs. The option was open for Jarlaxle to retake control of the mercenaries, as Kimmuriel wished to be done with his duties leading the group so that he could spend more time with matters of the mind.<ref name="PotWK" /><ref name="RotP-p348"/>

At some point after they split ways, Jarlaxle was forced, against his will, to betray Artemis in [[Baldur's Gate]] to the [[Netherese]], who were seeking to recover the ''[[Charon's Claw]]'' sword. To refuse to aid the Netherese would have resulted in war between the Empire of [[Netheril]] and Menzoberranzan and possibly in Jarlaxle's own execution. Jarlaxle trapped Entreri in one of his extra-dimensional pockets, originally planning to rescue his human friend immediately after handing him over. However, Kimmuriel Oblodra and House Baenre had worked to lower Jarlaxle's magical defenses so that Kimmuriel could enter his mind and change his memories about the betrayal. He instead came to believe that Entreri had betrayed him. By the time Jarlaxle had found out the truth, Entreri was beyond Jarlaxle's reach, and Matron Mother [[Quenthel Baenre]] had forbidden Jarlaxle from involving himself in the affair further. Jarlaxle and Athrogate went on numerous adventures together over the next several decades but always did Jarlaxle regret what had happened to his old friend.<ref name="TLT-p116-117">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold/Hardcover|116-117}}</ref>

Over [[1376]] to [[1377 DR]], Jarlaxle and Bregan D'aerthe manipulated events in [[Luskan]] that resulted in the destruction of the [[Host Tower of the Arcane]], the death of [[Deudermont]], and Luskan becoming a free port. Jarlaxle allowed [[Regis]] and Drizzt to live and also took a [[scrimshaw]] piece from Regis that depicted Drizzt and [[Guenhwyvar]], rather than taking the valuable ''[[ruby pendant]]''.<ref name="TPK">{{Cite book/The Pirate King|?}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|1384}}, Jarlaxle's dreams were invaded by [[Yharaskrik]], an [[illithid]] killed years before, who had joined up with [[Hephaestus]], the [[red dragon]] Jarlaxle had helped kill, and the [[lich]] kings captured in [[Crenshinibon]], creating the [[Ghost King]]. He and [[Athrogate]] both joined up with Drizzt Do'Urden, Bruenor Battlehammer, and [[Thibbledorf Pwent]] on an adventure to [[Spirit Soaring]], where they hoped [[Cadderly Bonaduce]] could help them fend off the coming of the Ghost King. They succeeded in that task, albeit not in the manner they had expected.<ref name="TGK">{{Cite book/The Ghost King/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

After Catti-brie wandered off, Drizzt and Bruenor asked Jarlaxle to try to find her. He searched "half the Realms" to no avail.<ref name="RotK-p193">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|193}}</ref>

===Gauntlgrym===
In the {{YearlinkName|1451}}, Jarlaxle and Athrogate joined [[Dahlia Sin'felle]] in an expedition to [[Gauntlgrym]], where they were tricked into releasing the fire [[primordial]] that destroyed the city of [[Neverwinter]]. In the {{YearlinkName|1462}}, Jarlaxle, Athrogate, and Dahlia once again joined together and recruited Drizzt Do'Urden and Bruenor Battlehammer in an attempt to right this wrong. They were successful, but in the process Bruenor was killed and Jarlaxle and Athrogate were overtaken by fire. Drizzt (falsely) presumed the latter two dead.<ref name="G">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

However, in reality, Jarlaxle had not died in Gauntlgrym. Some time later, he discovered that Drizzt, Dahlia, and their companions had been captured by the [[Netherese]] lord [[Draygo Quick]] and were being held in his home. With the help of Bregan D'aerthe and his brother [[Gromph Baenre]], he assaulted the castle to free Drizzt and company. Artemis Entreri had also been traveling with Drizzt at the time and Jarlaxle ensured that his petrified form was returned to flesh, although it was clear that Artemis did not forgive Jarlaxle for his betrayal.<ref name="TLTMMPp-364">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold/MMP|364}}</ref> Drizzt disappeared in [[Icewind Dale]] not long afterwards, and Jarlaxle, despite many expensive attempts, was unable to locate him.<ref name="TLT">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold/Hardcover|?}}</ref><ref name="TC">{{Cite book/The Companions/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

===In Luskan===
When the reincarnated [[Regis]] passed through Luskan in the {{YearlinkName|1484}}, he stayed at [[One-Eyed Jax]], an inn and tavern on the northern bank that was named for and owned by Jarlaxle (through [[Ship Kurth]]).<ref name="TC-p339">{{Cite book/The Companions/Hardcover|339}}</ref> The barkeep, [[Serena]], was "spoken for" by Jarlaxle, who was well-known by the tavern's patrons.<ref name="TC-p335">{{Cite book/The Companions/Hardcover|335}}</ref> Jarlaxle had a conversation with Regis, and while the drow didn't recognize the [[halfling]], he was suspicious enough to send a Bregan D'aerthe agent to keep an eye on him in [[Icewind Dale]].<ref name="TC-p341">{{Cite book/The Companions/Hardcover|341}}</ref> That agent, [[Braelin Janquay]], discovered Regis's true identity, saving him and Catti-brie from some thugs from [[Ship Rethnor]] and presumably passing word of Regis's true identity to Jarlaxle.<ref name="TC-p356">{{Cite book/The Companions/Hardcover|356}}</ref>

===Return to Menzoberranzan===
In the {{YearlinkName|1484}}, Jarlaxle was summoned back to Menzoberranzan by First [[Matron Mother]] [[Quenthel Baenre]] in order to serve as Captain of the Guard for the restored [[House Do'Urden]] (and to keep him away from Drizzt Do'Urden).<ref name="NotH">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|?}}</ref> However, he bribed Gromph Baenre by arranging [[psionics]] lessons for him from Kimmuriel Oblodra in exchange for Gromph allowing Jarlaxle to leave the city. Jarlaxle subsequently learned about the return of Drizzt and the reborn [[Companions of the Hall]] and traveled to [[Damara]] to recruit the dragon sisters Tazmikella and Ilnezhara, as well as [[Afafrenfere]] and [[Ambergris (person)|Ambergris]], to aid him in his interference in the [[Silver Marches]] against Quenthel's plans.<ref name="RotK">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

===Host Tower of The Arcane===
In 1487 DR, Jarlaxle aided Gromph Baenre and the Companions of the Hall in the reconstruction of the Host Tower of the Arcane. His control over Luskan aided in transportation of goods and workers to the site.<ref name="AM">{{Cite book/Archmage/Hardcover (2015)|?}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Hero/Hardcover|?}}</ref><ref name="mae">{{Cite book/Maestro/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

===Return of Zaknafein===
Zaknafein's return to his son Drizzt in the {{YearlinkName|1488}}, was a shock and a welcome surprise to Jarlaxle. He was happy to have one of his oldest friends returned, but was unsure how this has occurred. Jarlaxle reunited with father and son in [[Longsaddle]].<ref name="Timeless>{{Cite book/Timeless|?}}</ref>

Some time later, drow forces led by Matron [[Zhindia Melarn]] and an army of demons continued Zhindia's quest to capture and kill Zaknafein and Drizzt.<ref name="B-p220-222">{{Cite book/Boundless|220-222}}</ref>

Having reassurance from two of Lolth's handmaidens that her quest would prove she was the most faithful priestess in the demon goddess' eyes, Matron Zhindia committed House Melarn to the defense of Gauntlgrym as the demon army bore down upon it. Jarlaxle remained in Gauntlgrym and helped the dwarves and halflings within defend the complex.<ref>{{Cite book/Relentless|?}}</ref>

===Discovery of Aevendrow===
In the {{YearlinkName|1490}}, Jarlaxle found in the far north region of [[Faerûn]] the hidden city [[Callidae]], which was home to the [[aevendrow]], a race of drow, who hadn't followed in the steps of the Spider Queen, but retained their original virtues, and their society was opposite to that of the [[udadrow]] society of Menzoberranzan. In Callidae, the company met the aevendrow and became close with a few of them: [[Galathae]], a paladin; [[Azzudonna]] and [[Vessi]], warriors; and [[Ilina]], a priestess. From here on, their troubles and adventures began. They included an army of [[Slaad|slaadi]], [[frost giant]]s, cante, n'divi, and wyrms eating heat crystals that might lead to the destruction of the whole city.<ref name="SlE">{{Cite book/Starlight Enclave}}</ref>

When Jarlaxle left [[Callidae]] for the first time, he agreed to have his memory wiped by a long ceremony performed by thirty priests.<ref>{{Cite book/Glacier's Edge/Paperback 2022|133|17}}</ref> However, he had secretly marked a code under his foot as a clue to himself.<ref>{{Cite book/Glacier's Edge/Paperback 2022|132|17}}</ref> When he returned to [[Longsaddle]], it became clear that any [[arcane]] and [[psionic]] attempts to restore his memory would not work. Only an intense intrusion performed by a hive mind could restore Jarlaxle's lost memories. While the [[illithid]] intrusion was considered to be deeply traumatic, Jarlaxle came out unharmed.<ref>{{Cite book/Glacier's Edge/Paperback 2022|183|23}}</ref> Instead, the mind flayers who took part in the ritual were affected by the mind of Jarlaxle and they started to communicate with more passion and a sense of humor.<ref>{{Cite book/Glacier's Edge/Paperback 2022|184|23}}</ref>

Jarlaxle could re-visit and leave Callidae without needing to partake in any further rituals or magical ''[[geas]]''. He maintained the secrecy of the location and existence of Callidae. Jarlaxle considered Callidae the realization of his dreams for the drow and further confirmation that Lolth had corrupted the Udadrow.<ref>{{Cite book/Glacier's Edge/Paperback 2022|2|0}}</ref>

===Menzoberranzan Civil War===
In Menzoberranzan, there was speculation about which side Bregan D'aerthe and Jarlaxle would choose in the war, with rumors in the [[Menzoberranzan/Braeryn|Stenchstreets]] that he would join the heretics' side.<ref>{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|18|2}}</ref> Meanwhile, Jarlaxle traveled with Drizzt, Kimmuriel, [[Dab'nay Tr'arach]], and members of Bregan D'aerthe to Menzoberranzan, where they ambushed drow ambushers and took prisoners.<ref>{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|99|12}}</ref> When in Menzoberranzan, during an ambush at the [[Oozing Myconid]], [[Azleah]], a Bregan D'aerthe agent, attempted and failed to assassinate Jarlaxle during the skirmish.<ref>{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|106|12}}</ref> Jarlaxle later traveled with Dab'nay, Kimmuriel, and Drizzt to rescue the captured [[Braelin Janquay]] from [[House Barrison Del'Armgo|House Del'Armgo]].<ref>{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|203|23}}</ref> 

In the resulting fight at House Del'Armgo, Jarlaxle lost his friend and co-leader Kimmuriel, who was trapped and destroyed by his own ''[[kinetic barrier]]''.<ref>{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|208|23}}</ref> However, Jarlaxle gained the scepter of House Del'Armgo after [[Mez'Barris Armgo|Matron Mez'Barris]]'s death.<ref>{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|222|25}}</ref> and important hostages along with saving Braelin.<ref>{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|223|25}}</ref>

Jarlaxle negotiated ending of the siege of [[House Baenre]] with his sister [[Sos'Umptu Baenre|Sos'Umptu]]. She agreed that those who wanted to leave Menzoberranzan could but would never be allowed to return.<ref name=":0">{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|254|28}}</ref> Those who remained at House Baenre were given mercy if they served her and [[Lolth]] once more.<ref name=":0" /> Jarlaxle left Menzoberrazan with approximately 2,000 refugees freed from Lolth.<ref>{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|258|28}}</ref> After the civil war, Jarlaxle became the singular leader of Bregan D'aerthe though he could contact Kimmuriel's spirit on rare occasions with the risk of it being their last communication.<ref>{{Cite book/Lolth's Warrior|261|Epilogue}}</ref>

==Skills==
Jarlaxle was extremely skilled in the two long blades fighting style commonly used by the drow, though when mismatched with less skilled opponents he was known to use the less effective but visually more showy swashbuckling style to distract them. His mastery of this style was enough to impress even Artemis Entreri, who generally found the style ineffective.<ref name="SotS"/>

When forced into combat, Jarlaxle could throw a seemingly unlimited supply of magical daggers conferred to him by his bracers; only one of every three were real, the rest were illusions.<ref name="PotWK-p10">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|10|0}}</ref> Of course, Jarlaxle also possessed the typical drow weapon: a [[hand crossbow]] adorned with bolts dipped in [[sleeping poison]].<ref name="RotP-p109">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|109|7}}</ref>

Jarlaxle was talented with languages. He spoke [[Common]] perfectly fluently,<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55"/> as well as his native [[Drow language|Drow]], [[Abyssal language|Abyssal]], [[Goblin language|Goblin]],<ref name="PotWK-p86">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|86|5}}</ref> [[Gnome language|Svirfneblin]], and [[Halfling language|Halfling]] tongues.<ref name="PotWK-p89">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|89|5}}</ref>

==Possessions==
{{quote|''Luskan. Minsc hears it is ruled by a drow with a very stupid hat. But, so it goes with many rulers, no?''|[[Minsc]] of [[Rashemen]]<ref name="bg3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}<br/>
Jarlaxle was very fond of magical items and was renowned for having a seemingly unlimited supply of magical tricks. He was rarely seen with the same item more than once or twice. The following list contains merely some of his confirmed items.<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55"/>

;Clothing
* A wide-brimmed purple hat with a huge white ''[[feather of diatryma summoning]]'',<ref name="WDH5e-p206" /> which could be used to summon one of the [[Underdark]] [[diatryma]] birds; the feather always grew back after being used for the summoning.<ref name="SotS-p282">{{Cite book/Servant of the Shard|282}}</ref><ref name="G-p237">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|237}}</ref> The hat itself was a ''[[hat of disguise]]'', which allowed him to use magic to disguise himself.<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55"/><ref name="WDH5e-p206" />
* One of his jeweled earrings could extend its size to that of a small grappling hook, used as such when tied to a rope.<ref name="PotWK-p262"/>
* A ''[[knave's eye patch]]''<ref name="WDH5e-p206" /> that prevented magical and [[psionic]] intrusion or, if shifted to the other eye, enhanced his vision. It also enabled Jarlaxle to see through doors.<ref name="PotWK-p43">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|43|2}}</ref><ref name="RGLoD-p54-55"/> It became less effective after the [[Spellplague]].<ref name="TGK"/>
* A ''[[brooch of shielding]]'' clasped at his cloak's front side.<ref name="PotWK-p10" />
* A pair of boots that could be noisy or silenced at will.<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55"/> These were common among drow nobles.<ref name="CC-p1">{{Cite book/Charon's Claw/Hardcover|1|0}}</ref>
* A house emblem of House Baenre, which allowed him to [[levitate]].<ref name="G-p337">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|337}}</ref>
* A [[Rings of arbitration|''ring of arbitration'']] that the drow retrieved after the [[half-orc]] [[Olgerkhan]] dropped it.<ref name="PotWK-p357">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|357|22}}</ref>
* A ring that created illusionary copies of the bearer.<ref name="G-p336">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|336}}</ref>
* A ring of ''[[teleport]]''.<ref name="G-p342">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|342}}</ref>
* A belt that was a snake that would uncoil into a snake-rope for climbing when needed, extending to whatever length was required.<ref name="PotWK-p101">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|101|5}}</ref>
* A fine cord that could elongate to at least 120 ft, tucked under the silken band of his hat.<ref name="PotWK-p262">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|262|16}}</ref><ref name="G-p271">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|271}}</ref>
* An earring that he could tug on to escape quickly. He used this only as a last resort.<ref name="NotH-p106">{{Cite book/Night of the Hunter/Hardcover|106}}</ref>
* A ''[[cloak of invisibility]]''.<ref name="WDH5e-p206" />
* A ''[[ring of truth telling]]''.<ref name="WDH5e-p206" />

;Weapons
* A ''[[bracer of flying daggers]]''<ref name="WDH5e-p206" /> on each wrist that could produce a nearly infinite supply of throwing daggers.<ref name="PotWK-p10" /> One of his ''bracers'' was [[gem]]-studded and known as the ''[[Blades Injurious]]''.<ref name="WoW">{{Cite game/Warriors of Waterdeep}}</ref>
* A variety of slender [[wand]]s that were capable of releasing anything from a ''[[lightning bolt]]''<ref name="G-p297">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|297}}</ref> to a greenish, gluey semi-liquid blob of goo.<ref name="SotS-p343">{{Cite book/Servant of the Shard|343}}</ref> One such wand could create a solid stone wall to cut off the pursuit of enemies.<ref name="PotWK-p18">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|18|0}}</ref> Another wand created illusions. In true Jarlaxle fashion, he spent much time practicing with it until he could produce an illusionary fireball so realistic that creatures within the illusion burned as if from a real ''[[fireball]]'', as long as they didn't know it was an illusion. He was known to fire a blast at his own feet, killing any surrounding enemies, and leaving himself unharmed.<ref name="PotWK-p83">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|83|5}}</ref> His favorite wand was the [[Rod of viscid globs|''wand of viscous globs'']].<ref name="G-p237">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|237}}</ref> He also carried a ''[[wand of web]]''.<ref name="WDH5e-p206" />
* Hand-held [[crossbow]] with darts dipped in drow [[sleeping poison]].<ref name="RotP-p109" />
* At least two enchanted [[rapier]]s: one with a very potent standard enchantment<ref name="WDH5e-p206" /> and one with a unique enchantment known as the ''[[rapier of derobement]]''.<ref name="WoW" />
* Strongly enchanted ''[[leather armor]]''.<ref name="WDH5e-p206" />
* He carried [[Khazid'hea]] temporary prior the events in [[Starlight Enclave|The Starlight Enclave]] and gave it to [[Zaknafein Do'Urden|Zaknafein]] as a present.
* A [[pistol]] (and plenty of ''[[smokepowder]]'') as of the late 15{{th}} century DR.<ref name="WoW" />

;Other
* Several extra-dimensional devices, including a ''[[bag of holding]]'', a ''[[bottomless pouch]]'', a ''[[foldable chest]]'',<ref name="WoW" /> a button on his waistcoat that turned into a bag designed to shield the magical emanations of its contents,<ref name="PotWK-p25">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|25|Part 1}}</ref>, a belt pouch of extra-holding which contained fine [[Cormyrean brandy]],<ref name="PotWK-p100">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|100|5}}</ref> and a ''[[portable hole]]''<ref name="WDH5e-p206" /> that he kept stored in the underside of his wide-brimmed hat.<ref name="PotWK-p101-102">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|101-102|5}}</ref> The portable hole could be used to create a temporary hole in whatever surface it touches. A similar (possibly the same) item created a hole that led to an extra-dimensional room large enough to hold several humanoids.<ref name="TLT-p286">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold/Hardcover|286}}</ref> He also carried a ring that opened a limited number of extra-dimensional [[portal]]s.<ref name="SotS-p343">{{Cite book/Servant of the Shard|343}}</ref>
* A black cloth in the shape of a small [[bat]] that turned into a living creature which could serve as a scout.<ref name="SotS-p344">{{Cite book/Servant of the Shard|344}}</ref>
* A palm-sized ornament in the form of a silvery rearing dragon, with wings and jaws spread wide. This was a gift from the dragon [[Ilnezhara]]. It was, in truth, a charm to secure the entrance of a room.<ref name="PotWK-p42-43">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|42-43|2}}</ref> It was a trap bringing forth the various breath forms of the deadly [[chromatic dragon]]s based on a previously set color.<ref name="RotP-p63">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|63|4}}</ref>
* A tablecloth that could magically provide a feast.<ref name="PotWK-p91">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|91|5}}</ref>
* A pair of ''obsidian steed'' [[Figurines of wondrous power|figurines of wondrous power]], fashioned after one that he recovered from the dead body of [[Mariabronne|Mariabronne the Rover]]. They summoned [[Nightmare (creature)|nightmares]]; he gave one to Artemis and kept the other for himself.<ref>{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|295|18}}</ref><ref name="RotK-p178">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|178}}</ref>
* A whistle that could only be heard by members of Bregan D'aerthe.<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55" />
* Two jewel [[skull gem]]s, artifacts obtained from relics of the lich [[Zhengyi]]. One had the power to raise humans from the dead<ref>{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|343-344|21}}</ref> and the other to employ the help of the powerful [[dracolich]] [[Urshula]].<ref name="PotWK-p354">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|354|22}}</ref> Jarlaxle later gave them to his lieutenant [[Kimmuriel Oblodra]] for study.<ref name="RotP-p236">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|236|15}}</ref>
* A small silver cone to create an area of amplified sensibilities enabling him to overhear the conversation of his subjects from a long distance.<ref name="RotP-p57">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|57|3}}</ref>
* A small orb which, when crushed, could throw him through the multiverse, but to which [[plane]] of existence Jarlaxle could not predict. This was his last route of escape.<ref name="RotP-p67">{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|67|4}}</ref>

This allowed him to not only form the fortress out of nowhere, but to carry a small army in his pocket.<ref name="LT-ch23">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold/MMP||23}}</ref>
* A [[viol]] which, when active, hovered in the air and played music on its own.<ref name="AM" />

==Relationships==
===Kimmuriel Oblodra===
Jarlaxle rescued [[Kimmuriel Oblodra]] from the fall of his house and made him into a lieutenant of Bregan D'aerthe.<ref name="SotS">{{Cite book/Servant of the Shard}}</ref> He and Kimmuriel shared a mutually beneficial relationship, and Jarlaxle passed on the bulk of the leadership of his band to Kimmuriel in [[1366 DR]] when he ran off with Artemis Entreri.<ref name="SotS" /> Originally, the arrangement was intended to be temporary, but Jarlaxle increasingly left the running of his band to Kimmuriel, who by [[1463 DR]] was widely understood as the primary leader.<ref name="CC-p277">{{Cite book/Charon's Claw/Hardcover|277|16}}</ref> Jarlaxle continued to work for Bregan D'aerthe, but he had his own interests and often found himself paying for the band's services.<ref name="TLT-p299-300">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold/Hardcover|299-300}}</ref>

===Catti-brie===
From their meeting in the Underdark, Jarlaxle showed an attraction to and fondness of [[Catti-brie]].<ref name="SN"/> He chose to aid Catti-brie after she was afflicted by the [[Spellplague]], and he even continued to search for her at Drizzt Do'Urden's request.<ref name="TGK"/>

===Artemis Entreri===
The relationship between Jarlaxle and [[Artemis Entreri]] was often strained, but the two traveled together for many years and seemed to have develop some form of friendship—perhaps more so from Jarlaxle's perspective.<ref name="TLT-p286-287">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold/Hardcover|286-287}}</ref> Jarlaxle and Artemis met when [[Vierna Do'Urden]] employed Artemis to help find, capture, and fight against Drizzt Do'Urden.<ref name="SN" /> In Calimport, Artemis rescued Jarlaxle—claiming he needed the drow for protection—and escaped the wrath of Rai-Guy and Kimmuriel during the Bregan D'aerthe takeover.<ref name="SotS" />

Jarlaxle's treatment of Artemis during their adventures was somewhat bewildering. Jarlaxle's intentions in giving Artemis ''Idalia's flute'' were to free him from his emotional burdens to help him enjoy life.<ref name="TLT-p249">{{Cite book/The Last Threshold/Hardcover|249}}</ref> However, Artemis saw it as blatant manipulation of his psyche and in some ways a betrayal of their friendship. Idalia's flute was one of the main reasons Artemis left Jarlaxle.<ref name="PotWK-p356">{{Cite book/Promise of the Witch-King/MMP (2006)|356,358,364|22}}</ref>

Jarlaxle later betrayed Entreri to the Netherese, for which Entreri never forgave him.<ref name="CC">{{Cite book/Charon's Claw/Hardcover|?}}</ref> Jarlaxle, for his part, had intended to rescue Entreri but was tricked by his fellow drow into forgetting the whole event. Jarlaxle greatly regretted the way events had turned out and it haunted him for many decades.<ref name="TLT-p116-117" />

===Drizzt Do'Urden===
[[Drizzt Do'Urden]] originally viewed Jarlaxle (rightfully) with a healthy dose of distrust. He was never quite sure of the mercenary's motivations. However, Jarlaxle claimed to have been a friend of Drizzt's father, and Drizzt came to believe it. Jarlaxle saved Drizzt's life a number of times, and after Drizzt's wife left [[Toril]], Drizzt tasked Jarlaxle with finding her again. (He tried but was not successful.) By [[1462 DR]], the two considered each other friends.<ref name="G-p250">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|250}}</ref>

Jarlaxle was particularly interested in Drizzt because, in Jarlaxle's words, "He was the one who escaped" the heritage and fate of the drow. In Menzoberranzan, Drizzt was a symbol of male rebellion and the lack of acceptance of males' positions among females.<ref name="G-p302">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|302}}</ref> Jarlaxle, who always felt there was something wrong with Menzoberranzan and drow society, truly admired him,<ref name="RGLoD-p54-55" /> though he feared that Drizzt was forgetting his principles.<ref name="G-p302">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|302}}</ref> Many years after Catti-brie's death, however, Jarlaxle worried that Drizzt was forgetting himself and crossing the line of honor he had always held himself to.<ref name="G-p247-248">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|247-248}}</ref>

===Athrogate===
[[Athrogate]] joined with Jarlaxle in [[Damara]] in [[1368 DR]]<ref>{{Cite book/Road of the Patriarch/MMP|23-27|23}}</ref> and traveled with him for many, many years. Jarlaxle came to consider Athrogate his closest friend, and vice-versa.<ref name="G-p246">{{Cite book/Gauntlgrym/Hardcover|246}}</ref> At Jarlaxle's request, Athrogate traveled to [[Nesme]] in [[1484 DR]] to join up with the reunited [[Companions of the Hall]].<ref name="RotK-p185">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|185}}</ref>

===Tazmikella and Ilnezhara===
Jarlaxle had a "flirtatious" relationship with the two dragon sisters of [[Heliogabalus]], [[Tazmikella]] and [[Ilnezhara]]. He flirted with them; [[Ilnezhara]] in particular found him very charming.<ref name="RotK-p289">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|289}}</ref> He informed the [[Yarin Frostmantle|king]] about their true identity as dragons then infiltrated their shops as a street waif. He claimed to "know" they wouldn't kill him and to "hope" they would reward him for his interference.<ref name="RotK-p289-290">{{Cite book/Rise of the King/Hardcover|289-290}}</ref>

The '''Northdark''' was an underground domain of the [[Underdark]] north and east of the city of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="UD-p126"/> 

==Geography==
The area was home to some of the most renowned subterranean realms of [[Faerûn]] like the [[svirfneblin]] ruins of [[Blingdenstone]], the [[duergar]] deepkingdom of [[Gracklstugh]] and the [[drow]] cities of [[Menzoberranzan]] and [[Ched Nasad]]. Numerous crumbled ancient realms were also found in the Northdark, such as [[Ammarindar (kingdom)|Ammarindar]], [[Delzoun]] and [[Netheril]], in the form of [[Skullport]]. The Northdark bordered on the [[Buried Realms]] in the east, meeting it underneath the ruins of [[Ascore]]. Deep within the [[Spine of the World]] many [[orc]] strongholds, [[troll]] caves, and [[giant]] delvings were buried.<ref name="UD-p126"/>

==Lower Northdark==
The lowest minor domain of the Northdark domain in the [[Lowerdark]] realm of the Underdark.<ref name="UD-p126"/> Located between 12<ref name="UD-p164">{{Cite book/Underdark|126}}</ref> and 18 mi (19.31 to 28.97 km)<ref name="UD-p134">{{Cite book/Underdark|134}}</ref> below the region known as the [[Northwest Faerûn]], the Lower Northdark had the [[Buried Realms]] to the east and [[Netherese Caverns]] to the south.<ref name="UD-p126"/>

'''Luskan''', also known as the '''City of Sails''', was a port city at the mouth of the [[River Mirar]] on the [[Sword Coast North]].<ref name="VoloN-p111" /> Luskan, despite the presence of the [[Ten Towns]], was considered by many to be civilization's farthest reach.

Although once a bustling city, in the days prior to the [[Spellplague]], the city would suffer greatly at the hands of [[Arklem Greeth]]. The resulting disaster would allow the Spellplague to nearly annihilate the city, with it having only a fraction of its former populace.{{fact}}

{{quote|''Tucked like a gold filling (or some would say a pus-filled abscess) at the mouth of the River Mirar, Luskan is a city of merchant princesses, swarthy bandit princes, and the most diverse, cutthroat, and fractured cabal of pirates on Toril. Torn into factions, these piratical groups are known as "Ships."''|Excerpt from ''[[Corsairs of Luskan]]''<ref name="bg3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

==Geography==
As of [[1370 DR]], the city was split into northern and southern halves by the River Mirar, and these were connected by the bridges [[Dalath's Span]], [[Harbor Cross]], and the [[Upstream Span]].<ref name="VoloN-p113">{{cite book/Volo's Guide to the North|113}}</ref> Other than the [[Host Tower of the Arcane]], the city was dominated by two- and three-story buildings.<ref name="RGLoD-142-143" />

The city and its five inhabited islands sat in the mouth of the [[River Mirar]], on the [[Mirarmouth Bay]].<ref name="grotto">{{Cite archive/Greenwood's Grotto/2024-05-10/Mirar Bay}}</ref> Three of these were inhabited: [[Cutlass Island]] (upon which lay the Host Tower of the Arcane, and which was connected to both sides of the river by bridges), [[Closeguard Island]] (which housed [[Ship Kurth]] and was connected to the southern bank by a bridge), and [[Blood Island]] (which housed Luskan's army garrison).<ref name="RGLoD-142-143" /><ref name="VoloN-p118-119">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the North|118-119}}</ref> The remaining uninhabited islands were [[Fang Island]] (named for its treacherous rocks) and [[Harbor Arm Island]] (which sheltered the city's harbor).<ref name="VoloN-p118" />

[[File:Luskan.png|thumb|''Luskan at night.'']]

==History==
The story of House Baenre's ascension to the premier rank in the city was not known to any living drow; the only person who might have known the true history of the house is [[Yvonnel Baenre]], the former [[Matron Mother]] who took power in {{Yearlink|-7}}.<ref name="M.CoI-p35">{{Cite book/Menzoberranzan: City of Intrigue|35}}</ref> Even with the death of Yvonnel Baenre during the failed invasion of [[Mithral Hall]] in [[1358 DR]], the first house's power could not be usurped; [[Triel Baenre]], the first and most powerful daughter of Yvonnel Baenre, took her mother's place as supreme ruler.<ref name="DotDp?">{{Cite book/Daughter of the Drow/2003|?}}</ref>

Soon after the death of Yvonnel, House Baenre was at the weakest it had been for centuries.<ref name="Condemn-p47">{{Cite book/Condemnation/Paperback|47|3}}</ref> During the [[Silence of Lolth]] in [[1372 DR]] and [[1373 DR]], particularly during the slave revolt and then siege of Menzoberranzan by the [[duergar]], Triel struggled to secure her hold over both her house and the city. She sought allies along the other houses, particularly [[House Xorlarrin]],<ref name="Condemn-p288">{{Cite book/Condemnation/Paperback|288|16}}</ref> and cultivated a close ally relationship with [[House Mizzrym]].

During the [[Siege of Menzoberranzan]] in [[1372]]-[[1373 DR]], there were only seven wizards of House Baenre: [[Gromph Baenre]], [[Nauzhror Baenre]], [[Julani Baenre]], [[Grendan Baenre]],  [[Noori Baenre]], [[Zoran Baenre]], and [[Prath Baenre]]. However, Julani was killed towards the end of the siege when his [[lightning bolt]] cast through a [[scrying]] gem on [[Nimor Imphraezl]] reflected back on him.<ref name="Extinct-p241">{{Cite book/Extinction/Paperback|241-251|24}}</ref>

Triel was not able to hold on to power forever. In [[1383 DR]], [[Quenthel Baenre]], Triel's younger sister, became matron mother.<ref name="M.CoI-p37">{{Cite book/Menzoberranzan: City of Intrigue|37}}</ref>

In [[1490 DR]], after the civil war in Menzoberranzan that began with the heresy of Quenthel and [[Yvonnel Baenre II]], a deal was brokered between Sos'Umptu Baenre and Jarlaxle to let all heretics leave the city, while Sos'Umptu would take over as the new matron mother of House Baenre.<ref>

The '''Dock Ward of Waterdeep''' was situated on the ''Great Harbor'' of [[Waterdeep]]. It contained the docks, shipbuilding yards, and warehouses for the sea trade. Compared to the rest of the city it was exceptionally dirty, smelly, and particularly dangerous. In the words of [[Elminster]] himself, the Dock Ward was a "riotous, nigh-perpetual brawl that covers entire acres, interrupted only by small buildings, intermittent trade businesses, an errant dog or two, and a few brave watchguards, who manage to keep the chaos from spreading beyond the docks; the whole lot wallowing in the stench of rotting fish."

==History==
''Circa'' [[1368 DR]], an enterprising [[halfling]] [[Joshuan Havabuck]] was involved in numerous schemes and business in the Dock Ward. His main profitable business was the [[lottery]] where the folk of Waterdeep placed bets on the total daily tonnage that entered the harbor. The numbers were reported by the dockmaster and the winnings were distributed accordingly. One ticket was sold for a single [[Golden lion (coin)|gold lion]] and the take, on average, was ten thousand coins. Amazingly, in 1368 DR, not ten people, picked the correct number, not even thirty. Every single gambler guessed it correctly. This was due the goddess of luck - [[Tymora]]'s power running amok in [[Faerûn]]. Joshuan Havabuck resorted to loaning ten million gold lions with steep interest from [[Mirt the Moneylender]] and pay the winnings out.<ref name="ty">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|101-104}}</ref>

After the [[Spellplague]], the northern section of Waterdeep harbor became dilapidated with most of the harbor choked with sunken ship hulls. This section became known as [[Mistshore]].<ref>{{Cite book/Mistshore|?}}</ref>

{{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

The '''''Scarlet Marpenoth''''' was a [[submarine]] secretly owned by [[Jarlaxle Baenre]].<ref name="WDH5e-p141-145" /> It was named for the month of [[Marpenoth]].

==Description==
The ''Scarlet Marpenoth'' was a red vehicle shaped like a [[manta ray]].<ref name="WDH5e-p46">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|46}}</ref>

The submarine had a top speed of 2 mph (3.2 km/h) and required a minimum crew of two in order to be properly piloted and kept. Its interior was always kept dark, so that its crew had to rely on [[darkvision]]. The ship was maintained by a crew of [[rock gnome]] engineers and run by a [[drow]] crew under Jarlaxle's command.<ref name="WDH5e-p141-145" />

The ship's air supply circulated between all its chambers through a complex ventilation system powered by quasi-magical machinery. Its airtight doors were kept always locked.<ref name="WDH5e-p141-145" />

The ''Scarlet Marpenoth's'' engine was a quasi-magical device that controlled the ship's depth, as well as the steering fins and rudder, and powered its propulsion system. It could be piloted from the ship's control room.<ref name="WDH5e-p141-145" />

The ship had a defense system that allowed its outer hull to become electrified for a short period of time.<ref name="WDH5e-p141-145" />

==History==
According to a commemorative plaque in the [[House of Inspired Hands]], the submersible was launched in [[1489 DR]].<ref name="WDH5e-p46" /> The ship was designed and built in the island-nation of [[Lantan]].<ref name="WDH5e-p132">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|132}}</ref>

In [[1491 DR]], one year before the arrival of the [[Sea Maidens Faire]] in [[Waterdeep]], the submarine was acquired by Jarlaxle and was kept secret even to the crews of the Faire's sailing ships.<ref name="WDH5e-p132" />{{Waterdeep-time}}

'''Deudermont''' was a sea captain and commander of the famous ''[[Sea Sprite]]'',<ref name="THG" /> a large pirate-hunting vessel commissioned to him by the [[Lords of Waterdeep]],<ref name="PtD-c1">{{Cite book/Passage to Dawn/Hardcover August 1996|?|1}}</ref> in the mid-to-late 14{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="THG" /> He was briefly governor of the city of [[Luskan]] during its rebellion.<ref name="FRCG4e-148" /> He was also a friend of [[Drizzt]] and the [[Companions of the Hall]].<ref name="THG" /><ref name="PtD" />

==Description==
Deudermont was a tall refined man. By his look, one would expect to find him amongst the lords and ladies during his shore time, but he cared about his crew. He typically spent his time keeping an eye on them, and making sure they stayed out of trouble.<ref name="THG-c4">{{Cite book/The Halfling's Gem/Paperback (1990)|?|4}}</ref>

==Personality==
Deudermont was almost unshakeably loyal to his crew, and expected that loyalty in return. When Drizzt arrived to sail with the ''Sea Sprite'', Deudermont was initially skeptical of the [[drow]]'s motives, but Drizzt quickly proved himself and found a place among the ship's complement.<ref name="THG">{{Cite book/The Halfling's Gem/Paperback (1990)|?}}</ref>

==Relationships==
His trusted right-hand man was the wizard [[Robillard]], who was second-in-command on the ''Sea Sprite'' and a capable magic user.<ref name="PtD" />

==History==
[[File:Deudermont.png|thumb|''A closeup of Deudermont's face.'']]
[[Drizzt Do'Urden]] and [[Wulfgar]] first met Deudermont in [[Waterdeep]] in the {{YearlinkName|1356}}, when they were trying to secure passage to [[Calimshan]] when looking for [[Regis]]. During their voyage, they were attacked by the pirate [[Pinochet]], who was sent by Pasha [[Pook]]. After defeating Pinochet, Deudermont obtained his oath that he would never seek vengeance against him. In the midst of the fighting, Drizzt accidentally dropped ''[[Agatha's mask]]'', exposing his [[drow]] form. However, Deudermont did not treat him with the same prejudice others had shown him.<ref name="THG" />

Drizzt and [[Catti-brie]] later spent several years as part of Deudermont's crew. In the {{YearlinkName|1364}}, while being escorted by Drizzt and Catti-Brie, he was attacked by a [[doppelganger]] that attempted to take his place as the captain of his ship. Fortunately, [[Guenhwyvar]] was able to identify the doppelganger, resulting in its capture and Deudermont's rescue. When the doppelganger attempted to escape, Deudermont grabbed ''[[Taulmaril]]'' from Cattie-brie and killed it. He then assumed that the doppelganger was sent by Pinochet, unaware that it in fact served [[Errtu]].<ref name="PtD">{{Cite book/Passage to Dawn/Hardcover August 1996|?}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|1365}}, Wulfgar was falsely accused of attempting to assassinate the good captain, alongside [[Morik]] the Rogue, though the attempt on his life was actually conducted by [[Tee-a-nicknick]] and [[Creeps Sharky]], who were later executed in [[Luskan]]. Deudermont survived the attack, despite being badly [[poison]]ed and seeming on the brink of death for a few days. Trusting in Wulfgar's innocence, despite Robillard's objections, he vouched for their release, which soured his relationship with the high magistrates.<ref>{{Cite book/The Spine

'''Mirabar''' ({{Pronounce|meer|uh|BAR|refs=<ref name="AL-RoK-p3">{{Cite organized play/AL/Relics of Khundrukar|3}}</ref>}}) was a mining city of great wealth located on the [[Sword Coast]]. As of [[1368 DR]] it was the richest city north of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="VoloN-p151-152">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the North|151-152}}</ref><ref name="TN-C&C-p25">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/Cities & Civilization|25}}</ref> In [[1370 DR]], the city was occupied by 1600<ref name="tTO-p55a">{{Cite book/The Thousand Orcs/MMP|55}} This number is reduced by four hundred, see p. 289.</ref> [[shield dwarves]] who lived underground near to their workshops, which produced metal goods for export in the region and beyond. The humans above cooperated with the dwarves to handle the mining, move the ore to market, and defend the city against magical threats. Mirabar was ruled by a hereditary [[marchion]], but the true power was in an elected<ref name="tTO-p54">{{Cite book/The Thousand Orcs/MMP|54}}</ref> assembly called the [[Council of Sparkling Stones]],<ref name="tTO-p31">{{Cite book/The Thousand Orcs/MMP|31}}</ref> a mixed dwarven and human group that met once a year to determine production quotas<ref name="tTO-p55">{{Cite book/The Thousand Orcs/MMP|55}}</ref> and whether or not to threaten current clients with reduced output. It was led by the dwarven [[cleric]] of [[Dumathoin]], [[Agrathan Hardhammer]]. Mirabar also had an elected sceptrana,<ref name="tTO-p54" /> [[Shoudra Stargleam]] in 1370 DR.<ref name="tTO-p53">{{Cite book/The Thousand Orcs/MMP|53}}</ref>

==Description==
Mirabar stood on a knoll on the north banks of the [[River Mirar]].<ref name="VoloN-p151-152" /> On the surface, Mirabar was populated with squat stone buildings and a few stone towers. '''Fel'rekt Lafeen''' ({{Pronounce|FEL|rekt|_|lah|FEEN|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was one of the top agents of the [[Bregan D'aerthe]] in [[Waterdeep]] during the early 1490s [[DR]].<ref name="WDH5e-p15">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|15}}</ref>

==Activities==
Fel'rekt, together with [[Soluun Xibrindas]] and [[Krebbyg Masq'il'yr]], was tasked by [[Jarlaxle]] with doing dirty deeds in the city.<ref name="WDH5e-p15" /> Fel'rekt and Krebbyg preferred to act during the night.<ref name="WDH5e-p34">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|34}}</ref> Fel'rekt resided at the ''[[Scarlet Marpenoth]]'' ship, where he cleaned his firearm.<ref name="WDH5e-p143">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|143}}</ref>
 
==History==
Fel'rekt was born female but at some point changed to being male. However, disgusted with the treatment of males in [[drow]] society, he joined [[Bregan D'aerthe]]. Almost immediately, Jarlaxle was impressed with Fel'rekt, who volunteered for all the most dangerous missions.<ref name="WDH5e-p201-202" />

In [[1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} if Jarlaxle managed to obtain the ''[[Stone of Golorr]]'', then Fel'rekt and Krebbyg confronted some adventurers first in [[Mistshore]], where they had the support of the [[Lantanese]] mechanical [[dragon turtle]] [[Big Belchy]],<ref name="WDH5e-p67">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|67}}</ref> and again in the [[Dock Ward]].<ref name="WDH5e-p72">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|72}}</ref>
 
==Personality==
Fel'rekt lacked the typical drow cruelty and killed opponents only if he had no other options. He was the most loyal lieutenant of Jarlaxle.<ref name="WDH5e-p201-202" />

==Relationship==
Fel'rekt and Krebbyg were a working team.<ref name="WDH5e-p201-202" />

==Possessions==
Like his fellow Soluun Xibrindas and Krebbyg Masq'il'yr, Fel'rekt was equipped with Lantanese [[firearm]]s.<ref name="WDH5e-p132">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|132}}</ref>

'''Xanathar's Thieves' Guild''', or simply the '''Xanathar Guild''', was a criminal and slaving organization in [[Waterdeep]] and [[Skullport]], run by a series of [[beholder|beholders]].<ref name="WatN-57">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|57}}</ref><ref name="FRCS-p281">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|281}}</ref><ref name="EtU-18">{{Cite book/Expedition to Undermountain|12}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p17">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|17}}</ref>

{{quote|I don’t know if the Xanathar really exists, but as long as I’m working in Waterdeep, I make sure he gets his cut. Why risk the alternative?|Termerin, cat burglar<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p1">{{Cite dungeon/206/The Xanathar: Crime Lord of Waterdeep|1}}</ref>}}

==Geography==
This criminal and slaver network was one of the most powerful on the Sword Coast, but often unintentionally acted as a buffer against other evil organizations. They operated out of [[Skullport]], but were otherwise active primarily in Waterdeep.<ref name="FRCS-p281"/>

The Xanathar Guild had many connections in [[Luskan]], [[Baldur's Gate]], and the surrounding [[Underdark]], mostly the Underdark north and west of Waterdeep.<ref name="Dungeon206-TXBCLoW-p5">{{Cite dungeon/206/The Xanathar: Crime Lord of Waterdeep|5}}</ref>

==Aims & Goals==
The Xanathar Guild worked to gain financial and political power through its portfolio of spies, mercenaries, slavers, and thieves.<ref name="C&D-p139">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|139}}</ref> They hoped to rule both Skullport and Waterdeep by installing Xanathar spies as Masked Lords in the City of Splendors.<ref name="C&D-p142">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|142}}</ref>

==Organization==
The Xanathar Guild had a loose method of organization due to the instability of its leader, and members inside regularly assassinated one another as desperate attempts to vie for the beholder's favor.<ref name="WDH-p220">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|220}}</ref><ref name="WDH-p17"/> During the reign of [[Xanathar (Zushaxx)|Zushaxx]] in {{Yearlink|1492}}, members could be killed at any time at the whim of the beholder.<ref name="WDH-p100">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|100}}</ref>

===Under Xanathar (original)===
During the original Xanathar's reign, the Guild was often populated by freelance thieves or mercenaries that were hired on a case-by-case basis.<ref name="CoS-WWiW-p88">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|88}}</ref> Xanathar's most senior agents were the Four Councilors, who were each assigned a domain to supervise: mercenaries, bookkeeping, thievery, and blackmail.<ref name="WatN-57"/>

== Summary ==

=== Prologue (1488 DR) ===
In the Year of Dwarvenkind Reborn, Regis races to Waterdeep through demon-infested forests, pursued by fiends and desperate to warn of a coming invasion. Entrusting his companions to defend the halfling town of Bleeding Vines, a valiant stand unfolds. Doregardo escapes to alert others while Showithal sacrifices himself. Lady Donnola and Clan Battlehammer lead an evacuation to Gauntlgrym, defended fiercely by Pikel and Ivan Bouldershoulder. ==Summary==
[[Liriel Baenre]] is the prized daughter of [[Gromph Baenre]] and therefore a princess of [[House Baenre]] in [[Menzoberranzan]]. Although Gromph has been able to train her in wizardry and keep her away from the wiles of the priestesses, [[Matron Mother]] [[Triel Baenre]] insists that she enter the clerical academy of [[Arach-Tinilith]] to become a priestess of [[Lolth]]. There her practical jokes gain her the enmity of [[Shakti Hunzrin]]. Her desire to visit the surface prompts her to study [[portal]] magic with her tutor, [[Kharza-kzad Xorlarrin]], and she is able to transport her self to the [[High Forest]], where she meets several followers of [[Eilistraee]].

Meanwhile, [[Fyodor]] seeks the [[Windwalker (artifact)|Windwalker]] amulet on a quest he received due to his inability to control his berserker rages. The amulet, which had formerly been held in a tower in [[Rashemen]], has been stolen by a wizard (later revealed to be [[Merdrith]]) and then stolen again by a band of [[drow]] mercenaries of the [[Dragon's Hoard]]. Fyodor follows them through a portal to the [[Underdark]] near Menzoberranzan. 

Liriel learns of the Windwalker through surface books obtained by [[Nisstyre]], leader of the [[Dragon's Hoard]], and during one of her jaunts out of the city she finds the amulet on the body of a dead drow killed by [[deep bat]]s. She plans to use the Windwalker to store her drow magic and allow her to travel to the surface. However, the priestesses of Menzoberranzan catch wind of her plan and plot her demise. Shakti allies with Nisstyre in this attempt. Liriel escapes to the surface, joining with Fyodor as she flees her pursuers. They travel to [[Skullport]], entering [[Waterdeep]] with the help of [[Danilo Thann]]. 

Once at the [[Promenade of the Dark Maiden]] near Skullport, Liriel and Fyodor join forces with the chosen of Eilistraee and with the [[deep dragon]] [[Zz'Pzora]] to assault and defeat the [[Dragon's Hoard]] headquarters. However, although they have temporarily waylaid their pursuers, Liriel and Fyodor still have far to travel to master the magic of the Windwalker to aid both of them in their goals.

'''''Dungeons & Dragons: Neverwinter''''' is a free-to-play massively multiplayer online roleplaying game developed by Cryptic Studios and published by Perfect World Entertainment. It was released on the 20th of June 2013, with additional content updates (called Modules) releasing every few months thereafter.

The game deals with the aftereffects of an attack on the city of [[Neverwinter]] by [[Valindra Shadowmantle]] and a [[dracolich]]. Several factions and individuals attempt to take advantage of the chaos caused by the attack. Later updates have generally followed [[Wizards of the Coast|Wizards]]' official story arcs.

==Main Plot==
===The Battle of Sleeping Dragon Bridge===
{{See also|The Siege of Neverwinter}}
Washing up on the beach in [[1479 DR]] after their ship was sunk by the dracolich, several [[adventurer]]s were thrust into battle against Valindra's [[undead]] minions while trying to reach the relative safety of Neverwinter's walls. The adventurers were joined by Private [[Wilfred]], a Neverwinter graycloak, who was killed by [[Valindra Shadowmantle|Valindra]] when he attempted to confront her directly on Sleeping Dragon Bridge. Meanwhile, a tiefling warlock named [[Makos]] dispatched the dracolich and used his magic to prevent its soul from returning to its phylactery to regenerate. For their valor in defending the city against the undead, the adventurers became known as the Heroes of Sleeping Dragon Bridge.

===The Crown of Neverwinter===
[[File:Neverwinter_MMO_-_Quest_-_The_Thief_of_The_Crown_(Cutscene).png|thumb|The theft of the crown]]
During an investigation of the vaults beneath the [[Hall of Justice]], the Heroes of Sleeping Dragon Bridge discovered that the [[Sons of Alagondar#Nashers|Nashers]] managed to sneak into the vault during Valindra's attack and steal the ''[[Crown of Neverwinter]]''. After tracking down the Nasher rogue [[Honor Reigh]], whom they saw stealing the crown with another Nasher, clues pointed to a Nasher base in the [[Neverwinter#Blacklake|Blacklake district]]. The heroes found the Blacklake District to not only be overrun with Nashers, but covered with a magical sludge that was bubbling up from the Blacklake itself. The heroes aided an alchemist in investigating it, ultimately confirming that it originated with a [[Chosen of Ghaunadaur|Chosen]] of [[Ghaunadaur]] living in the lake. After dispatching the [[Chosen]], the heroes continued searching for the other thief and eventually discovered he had been kidnapped by the [[Dead Rats]]. Down in the sewers beneath the city, the heroes located the other thief, [[Gothwan Cooper]], who claimed that his leader [[Karzov]] left him to die - gladly giving up Karzov's location in exchange for his life. The Nashers set fire to their safehouse when the heroes arrived and Karzov escaped to their headquarters, an old warehouse. The heroes followed and killed Karzov, bringing the Crown back to [[Neverwinter#Protector's Enclave|Protector's Enclave]].
