The '''Lords' Alliance''', also known as the '''Council of Lords''', was a partnership of merchant cities founded in the early 14{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="LEoF-p141">{{Cite book/Lost Empires of Faerûn|141}}</ref><ref name="GHotR-p137">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|137}}</ref> Its members were from the [[Sword Coast]], [[the North]] and [[Western Heartlands]],<ref name="OOtA-p128">{{Cite book/Out of the Abyss|128}}</ref> including [[Waterdeep]], [[Silverymoon]], [[Baldur's Gate]], and [[Neverwinter]], as well as other free cities and towns in the region, which made up the bulk of the organization.<ref name="RoT-pp13-14" /> It was formed to oppose the growing influence of the [[Zhentarim|Black Network]] in the North, the [[Shadow Thieves of Amn]],<ref name="SF-p9" /><ref name="FRCS3e-pp275-276" /><ref name="CoV-p102">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|102}}</ref> rampaging hordes of [[orc]]s, and [[Northlander]] raiders.<ref name="SCAG-p43">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|43}}</ref>

{{Quote|For a century and a half, and more, the Lords' Alliance has stood as the most important and influential group in the North. Its power has kept towns safe from the predations of larger powers, has kept the ambitions of Luskan in check, and has taught the rulers of many cities that it is better to cooperate, even for a time, then to merely shut one's doors and allow the storms to rage outside.|[[Andew Cururen]], a Lords' Alliance agent of note.<ref name="SCAG-p43" />}}

==Organization==
Depending on who was asked, the Lords' Alliance was considered either a strong coalition,<ref name="OOtA-p128" /> or unsteady pact of political powers. While the Alliance was said by some to be a collective of aggressive and self-serving figureheads,<ref name="PotA-p15-16">{{Cite book/Princes of the Apocalypse|15-16}}</ref><ref name="SKT-p12">{{Cite book/Storm King's Thunder|12}}</ref> others insisted they were among the most secure governing bodies in the west.<ref name="OOtA-p128" /> The truth, as it usually was, lay somewhere between the two extremes. While the members often held opposing views and held interests that conflicted with their peers, these rivalries were set aside when true danger emerged to threaten their collective safety and prosperity.<ref name="RoT-pp13-14">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|13-14}}</ref> The true strength of the alliance, it was said, manifested when its members banded together on collaborative endeavors.<ref name="OOtA-p128" /> 

While some of these cities and city-states held more influence than others,<ref name="RoT-pp13-14" /> they aligned their goals for the greater good and prosperity of the whole alliance.<ref name="OOtA-p128" /> Collectively they believed in the preservation of civilization for the better of their people.<ref name="SCAG-p43" />

The Lords' Alliance had the following ranks from lowest to highest: Cloak, Redknife, Stingblade, Warduke, and Lioncrown.<ref name="DMG-2014-p22">{{Cite book/Dungeon Master's Guide 5th edition|22}}</ref>

The alliance's emblem was a golden crown set over a field of red.<ref name="KGV.P13">{{Cite book/Keys from the Golden Vault/Prisoner 13|?}}</ref>

==Activities==
Member-states of the Lords' Alliance served to better the collective regions of the North and the Western Heartlands as a whole. They achieved this through well-wrought mercantile treaties and official pledges to ensure their collective safety,<ref name="CoV-p74">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|74}}</ref> especially along prominent trade routes.<ref name="PotA-p15-16" /><ref name="EFR-p68">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|68}}</ref> The collective group bargained with foreign trading partners and even regulated preferred relations and treatment of certain groups long-feared by many people of the Coast and the North, such as [[goblinoid]]s and [[orc]] tribes.<ref name="FRCS3e-pp275-276" /> 

The alliance often took charge whenever it was necessary to take military action in campaigns that arose across the North or the Heartlands.<ref name="RoT-pp13-14" />

The Lords Alliance also maintained the prison of [[Revel's End]], an isolated panopticon in the [[Frozenfar]] that housed some of most heinous criminals from across the North and Sword Coast.<ref name="RotF-p154">{{Cite book/Icewind Dale: Rime of the Frostmaiden|154}}</ref>

==Tactics==
The alliance usually hired [[adventurer]]s to work for them in the past for tasks ranging from gathering simple information, or even for use in raids against Zhentarim strongholds and outposts. Local adventurers could quickly gain status, as well as powerful friends by helping the alliance, but can just as easily make enemies of the sinister Zhentarim as a result.<ref name="SF-p9" /><ref name="FRCS3e-pp275-276" /> 

==Relationships==
[[File:Lords' Alliance signet ring.png|thumb|right|200px|''A 15{{th}} century DR Lords' Alliance signet [[ring]].'']]
Among the orders with which the Lords' Alliance maintained a pledge of mutual assistance in times of need were a group of spellcasters known as the [[Guardians of the Weave]].<ref name="CoV-p84">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|84}}</ref>

The [[Order of Tye]] was the prestigious knightly organization that served as guards for ranking Lords' Alliance members during their travels outside the walls of the allied cities and accompanied them on dangerous mission. [[Lord Peneshay]] led the organization ''circa'' [[1372 DR]].<ref name="NWN-DoD">{{Cite game/Neverwinter Nights: Darkness over Daggerford}}</ref>

While they did not view the [[Red Wizards]] of [[Thay]] as enemies, the Lords' Alliance treated the Thayan spellcasting society with much mistrust.<ref name="LoD-p60">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|60}}</ref>

==History==
===14{{th}} Century===
The Lords' Alliance was formed in the {{YearlinkName|1325}}, when a collective of towns and cities across the [[Sword Coast North]] banded together to oppose overreach from the powerful merchant houses of Amn.<ref name="GHotR-p137">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|137}}</ref> Interest in joining the alliance was strongest in the North at first, but gradually spread south over the course of the following years.<ref name="SCAG-p43" />

Just over three decades later, in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, the Lords' Alliance of Waterdeep expels [[Luskan]] troops from [[Ruathym]] by using both military and diplomatic pressures.<ref name="LEoF-p142">{{Cite book/Lost Empires of Faerûn|142}}</ref><ref name="GHotR-p142">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|142}}</ref> They were forced into outright military conflict just [[1361 DR|three years later]], when Luskan finally conquered the island off the coast.<ref name="GHotR-p146">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|146}}</ref><ref name="LoD-p109">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|109}}</ref>

The Lords' Alliance committed their forces to combat the forces of evil during the [[Second Dragonspear War]] in the {{YearlinkName|1363}}.<ref name="PoF-p27">{{Cite book/Power of Faerûn|27}}</ref> Following the war, the Lords' Alliance redoubled their efforts to make safe the [[Trade Way]], and ensure commerce along the coast went unimpeded.<ref name="VGSC-p65">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the Sword Coast|65}}</ref>

When a number of Waterdhavian vessels were sunk during their voyages from the Sword Coast to [[Maztica]], in the {{YearlinkName|1368}}, the Lords' Alliance worked diligently to collect evidence against the suspected culprits: the nation of Amn.<ref name="GHotR-p149">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|149}}</ref>

After the [[Sharn Wall]] beneath [[Evereska]] was breached in [[Nightal]] of the {{YearlinkName|1371}}, the Last Home of the elves was rapidly overrun. An army of the Lords' Alliance led by [[Laeral Silverhand]] marched to the city in relief. Unfortunately, they did not arrive at Evereska for some months.<ref name="GHotR-p151">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|151}}</ref>

===15{{th}} Century===
When the [[Cult of the Dragon]] plotted to release [[Tiamat]] in the 1480s DR,{{ToD-time}} the Alliance was one of the many factions who rose up to oppose the [[dragon]] goddess's attempt at freedom.<ref name="Factions">{{Cite web | author= [[Wizards of the Coast]]  | title=Heroes Isteval | publisher=[[Wizards of the Coast]] | date=2014 | work=What is D&D? |  url=http://dnd.wizards.com/dungeons-and-dragons/story/factions | accessdate=2014-12-09 }}</ref> During the crisis, the Lords' Alliance dispatched seven of its members to the [[Council of Waterdeep]].<ref name="RoT-pp13-14">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|13-14}}</ref>

Around that same time, members of the Alliance took note of strange goings-on in the regions of the [[Underdark]] beneath the North and the Sword Coast. Many places of note within ceased communication with each other and the surface world. The little information that did come out, revolved around cults of [[demon]]-worshipers.<ref name="OOtA-p128" />

When the [[Ordning (social structure)|ordning]] of the [[giant]]-kind of Faerûn collapsed, rumors circulated that the Lords' Alliance was responsible for the disappearance of [[Hekaton]], king of the [[storm giant]]s.<ref name="SKT-p12" />

Following the collapse of the [[Luruar|Silver Marches]], in the {{YearlinkName|1488}},<ref name="SCAG-p18">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|18}}</ref> the Lords' Alliance focused inwards and began restricting new members from beyond the borders of the North.<ref name="SCAG-p43" />

Some time during or around the {{YearlinkName|1491}}, Lords' Alliance agents operating in the [[Moonsea]] region began looking into unusual activities in and around [[Mulmaster]].<ref name="EE-p15">{{Cite adventurers league/Embers of Elmwood|15}}</ref> Their involvement in city business led production talks with members of the city's [[Blades (Mulmaster)|Blades]],<ref name="BYR-p7">{{Cite adventurers league/Breath of the Yellow Rose|7}}</ref> and the [[Hawks]],<ref name="OFI-p6">{{Cite adventurers league/Oubliette of Fort Iron|6}}</ref> which they hoped to leverage into the admittance of Mulmaster into the alliance, or at least adherence to its tenets.<ref name="SS-p6">{{Cite adventurers league/The Sword of Selfaril|6}}</ref> When the [[Cult of the Eternal Flame]] set off explosions in the city, agents of the Lords' Alliance sprang into action to help restore order.<ref name="MU-pp8-11">{{Cite adventurers league/Mulmaster Undone|8-11}}</ref>

'''Piergeiron Paladinson''' ({{Pronounce|peer|GEER|on|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-DM-p29" />}}) was the [[Lords of Waterdeep|Open Lord of Waterdeep]] throughout most of the 1300s [[DR]] and leader of the [[Lords' Alliance]].<ref name="SF-p9">{{Cite book/The Savage Frontier|9}}</ref> He lived in [[Piergeiron's Palace]] in [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="CoS.W-p53">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|53}}</ref> 

Usually, the identities of the Lords of Waterdeep were concealed from the people, but Piergeiron was one of only four whose identities were known as of his tenure. He followed in the footsteps of his predecessors, Open Lords [[Ahghairon]], [[Baeron]], and [[Lhestyn]].<ref name="CoS.CG-p67">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|67}}</ref>

==Description==
Piergeiron was a tall, muscular, and strikingly handsome man with a firm jaw, and dark eyes.<ref name="CoS.W-p53" /><ref name="HoH-p92">{{Cite book/Hall of Heroes|92}}</ref> His hair was curly slightly greying at the temples, adding to the man's regal aura.<ref name="HoH-p92" />

==Personality==
Piergeiron did not speak very often, understanding the tact of silence, and when he did, he spoke very slowly weighing his every word. This led to some of those around him nicknaming him "the Thickskull," although never in his presence.<ref name="CoS.W-p53" />

He was aware of the nickname, sometimes even laughing about it. Piergeiron used this misconception of his mental capabilities to his advantage. Having his political enemies underestimate him led to often made slip-ups in the negotiations, giving the Open Lord an upper hand.<ref name="HoH-p93">{{Cite book/Hall of Heroes|93}}</ref>

Piergeiron did not fear battle, he was quite capable of defending himself thanks to years of service prior to his life as a ruler. He preferred diplomacy to battle, but when he was pressed into a physical confrontation he fought with the knowledge he was blessed by his god.<ref name="HoH-p92" />

The '''Order of the Gauntlet''' was a coalition of morally upstanding warriors, knights, [[paladin]]s and [[cleric]]s who dedicated themselves to the destruction of [[evil]] in [[Faerûn]] in the 15{{th}} century [[DR]]. They were a unified group, bonded by either their fervent religious beliefs or staunch dedication to enforcing justice in the realms. To these brothers and sisters in arms, evil had to be dealt with and could not be ignored.<ref name="HDQ-p29">{{Cite book/Hoard of the Dragon Queen|29}}</ref><ref name="RoT-p12"/><ref name="SCAG=p147">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|147}}</ref><ref name="FO.OotG">{{cite web | author= [[Wizards of the Coast]]  | title=Factions — Order of the Gauntlet | publisher=[[Wizards of the Coast]] | date=2014 | work=What is D&D? | url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150208232119/http://dnd.wizards.com/dungeons-and-dragons/story/faction/ordergauntlet | accessdate=2014-12-11 }}</ref>

Members of the order were strong in their faith, using it to stand against evil forces.<ref name="RoT-p12"/> While this faith ranged from believing in one's personal god, it extended also to those considered close friends, and especially to oneself. Most often, Knights of the Gauntlet venerated either the God of Guardians, [[Helm]]; two of the deities of [[the Triad]], [[Torm]] and [[Tyr]];<ref name="TA-p30">{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|30}}</ref> as well as the Poet of Justice, [[Hoar]].<ref name="FO.OotG"/>

==Organization==
The Order of the Gauntlet had a number of chapters in various cities located across the western Realms of [[Faerûn]].<ref name="HDQ-p28">{{Cite book/Hoard of the Dragon Queen|28}}</ref><ref name="W.DH-p17"/><ref name="ToA-p47"/><ref name="SKT-p249"/><ref name="SKT-p101"/>

The Order had the following ranks from lowest to highest: Chevall, Marcheon, Whitehawk, Vindicator, and Righteous Hand.<ref name="DMG-2014-p22">{{Cite book/Dungeon Master's Guide 5th edition|22}}</ref>

==Activities==
As part of their duties the Order of the Gauntlet remained vigilant against any potential threats to the Realms, standing ready to stop any aggression. When necessary, they investigated potential threats or secretive organizations, and watched over those creatures they saw as inherently [[evil]].<ref name="FO.OotG"/>

===Beliefs===
The Order of the Gauntlet held three tenets:<ref name="FO.OotG"/>
* Faith was a member's most potent weapon against evil
* Great challenges required heroic demonstrations of both strength and valor
* It was justified to punish an act of evil, not a thought or feeling

==Tactics==
[[File:Valiant Endeavor.jpg|thumb|right|400px|''Knights of the Gauntlet undertaking a valiant endeavor.'']]
The Order was clear on its methods of operation, and would never preemptively attack an opponent. They forewent the approval of local leadership when striking against evil.<ref name="RoT-p12"/> They struck their foes fast and hard, seeking decisive victories.<ref name="Drgn+.1.PF">{{Cite dragon+/1/Player Factions: Our Heroes?}}</ref> They would never punish someone who had not committed an evil act nor those who merely expressed immoral thoughts.<ref name="FO.OotG"/><ref name="SCAG=p85">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|85}}</ref>

It was very uncommon to see a lone member of the Order as they almost always traveled in groups.<ref name="FO.OotG"/>

'''Luskan''', also known as the '''City of Sails''', was a port city at the mouth of the [[River Mirar]] on the [[Sword Coast North]].<ref name="VoloN-p111" /> Luskan, despite the presence of the [[Ten Towns]], was considered by many to be civilization's farthest reach.

Although once a bustling city, in the days prior to the [[Spellplague]], the city would suffer greatly at the hands of [[Arklem Greeth]]. The resulting disaster would allow the Spellplague to nearly annihilate the city, with it having only a fraction of its former populace.{{fact}}

{{quote|''Tucked like a gold filling (or some would say a pus-filled abscess) at the mouth of the River Mirar, Luskan is a city of merchant princesses, swarthy bandit princes, and the most diverse, cutthroat, and fractured cabal of pirates on Toril. Torn into factions, these piratical groups are known as "Ships."''|Excerpt from ''[[Corsairs of Luskan]]''<ref name="bg3">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

==Geography==
As of [[1370 DR]], the city was split into northern and southern halves by the River Mirar, and these were connected by the bridges [[Dalath's Span]], [[Harbor Cross]], and the [[Upstream Span]].<ref name="VoloN-p113">{{cite book/Volo's Guide to the North|113}}</ref> Other than the [[Host Tower of the Arcane]], the city was dominated by two- and three-story buildings.<ref name="RGLoD-142-143" />

The city and its five inhabited islands sat in the mouth of the [[River Mirar]], on the [[Mirarmouth Bay]].<ref name="grotto">{{Cite archive/Greenwood's Grotto/2024-05-10/Mirar Bay}}</ref> Three of these were inhabited: [[Cutlass Island]] (upon which lay the Host Tower of the Arcane, and which was connected to both sides of the river by bridges), [[Closeguard Island]] (which housed [[Ship Kurth]] and was connected to the southern bank by a bridge), and [[Blood Island]] (which housed Luskan's army garrison).<ref name="RGLoD-142-143" /><ref name="VoloN-p118-119">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the North|118-119}}</ref> The remaining uninhabited islands were [[Fang Island]] (named for its treacherous rocks) and [[Harbor Arm Island]] (which sheltered the city's harbor).<ref name="VoloN-p118" />

The '''Shadow Thieves''' was a secretive and powerful consortium of thieves' guild in [[west Faerûn|western Faerûn]] that were headquartered in the [[Amn]]ian city of [[Crimmor]]<ref name="FRCG-p93">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|93}}</ref> during the 15{{th}} century [[DR]], having relocated from their base in [[Athkatla]] the century prior. They were formerly the most prominent thieves' guild in [[Waterdeep]], but were routed from the city by the [[Lords of Waterdeep|Masked Lady]] [[Lhestyn|Lhestyn Arunsun]].<ref name="C&D-p103">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|103}}</ref> From the early-14{{th}} century onward they controlled many of the criminal operations along the [[Sword Coast]],<ref name="C&D-p101">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|101}}</ref><ref name="C&D-p103" /> growing into the largest and most prosperous thieves guild in all of [[Faerûn]],<ref name="PGtF-p74">{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|74}}</ref> with ultimate goal of further increasing their collective influence and wealth.<ref name="C&D-p102">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|102}}</ref> 

The organization operated under two oaths, which were written by its first Grandmaster, following their exile from Waterdeep:<ref name="C&D-p102" />
*''"The Shadow Thieves would never again be brought so low and disgraced as they were in their exile."''
*''"They would take their vengeance upon the city of Waterdeep and the [[Arunsun]] family."''

==Activities==
The Shadow Thieves were discreet in their activities, even in Amn, where they were known by just about every citizen of the nation. Even the lower-ranked members, who earned their gold as beggars, cut-purses, thugs and fences followed these rules as they went about their daily duties. Even these minor operations allowed the Shadow Thieves to insert itself into the daily lives of Amnian citizens. Thus, a friendly game of chance run by a con artist gamer could lead to higher and higher stakes and a great take of gold for the agent and overall guild.<ref name="C&D-p109">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|109}}</ref> 

Many citizens relied upon the manipulative and illicit services provided by the Shadow Thieves. Business people became reliant on their protection rackets, money lenders and the enforcers and thugs they hired out. Politicians and government officials would often hire the guild's spies and burglars to obtain guarded knowledge, or plant false information. The multitude of traders and merchants in [[Amn]] received a myriad of unique mercantile opportunities provided by the guild's smugglers and pirates.<ref name="C&D-p109" />

The agents of the guild were just as ruthless as they were secretive. If a situation required the use of violence, they would only take prisoners if a person was deemed worthy of capture, i.e. they possessed arcane, political or financial power. The captives were relocated to a safehouse until ransom could be collected or the person otherwise manipulated.<ref name="C&D-p104" /> 

===Outlook===
Secrecy was the key to the immense success of the Shadow Thieves, an outlook that began with the ruling [[Shadow Council]] and spread to every officer, agent and member. Only the upper echelon of the organization knew the full complexities of their hierarchical structure, with individual members only aware of their immediate associates. Members worked exclusively with their known comrades so that could never implicate more than a handful of other thieves. === LINKED WIKI PAGE (first 5000 chars): Marking the years

{{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

The '''Flaming Fist''', or '''Flaming Fists''', was a [[mercenary]] company based in the city of [[Baldur's Gate]] on the [[Sword Coast]], where its members served as city guards.<ref name="FRCG-p94-95">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|94-95}}</ref> They were one of the most powerful and expensive fighting forces in all the Realms during the 14{{th}} century [[DR]],<ref name="FRCS:DM-p34">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Set/DM's Sourcebook of the Realms|34}}</ref> motivated primarily by the promise of riches and the glory of victory, though they edged on the side of promoting order throughout the Realms.<ref name="G&G-p11"/>

{{TOC limit|3}}

==Organization==
[[File:Flaming Fist.png|thumb|left|300px|''A proud soldier of the Baldurian Flaming Fist.'']]
===Leadership===
During the 1300s, the company was led by 10 senior officers which included the master-tactician [[Hurbold Duethkatha|Scar]], the powerful mage Moruene and the company's founder and leader, [[Eltan|Grand Duke Eltan]].<ref name="FRCS:DM-p34"/><ref name="FRCS:DM-p35"/>

The other leaders included [[Beluarion]], [[Kulurauk]], [[Bellan]], [[Nenon]], [[Koruelve]], [[Desedrak]] and [[Yulimtul]]. These high-ranking Flaming Fist officers served as commanders on the battlefield, bodyguards during intense, high-risk negotiations and often as a protective bulwark for Eltan and Moruene when they forayed into combat.<ref name="FRCS:DM-p35">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Set/DM's Sourcebook of the Realms|35}}</ref>

Following the near-disastrous expeditions to [[Maztica]] and Anchorome during the early 1360's DR, over half of the senior officers were either killed, went missing or were absent from Baldur's Gate due to new posts from which they served. Along with the surviving Koruelve, A new generation of officers were inducted into the Flaming Fist including [[Lendali Firehair]], [[Herculus|Herculus the Hammer]], [[Skot-sar]], [[Delandria]] [[Kalvistalk|Kalvistalk the Huner]] and [[Morgan Redwing]].<ref name="G&G-p11"/>

During the mid-15{{th}} century [[DR]], [[Grand Duke]] [[Abdel Adrian]] served as the Marshal of the Flaming Fist until his death in the {{YearlinkName|1482}}.<ref name="MBG-p4">{{Cite book/Murder in Baldur's Gate/Adventure|4}}</ref> He was succeeded by [[Ulder Ravengard]], who also ascended to the office of Grand Duke of Baldur's Gate within a decade.<ref name="TRoT-p15">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|15}}</ref><ref name="SCAG-p45">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|45}}</ref>

'''Grand Duke Ulder Ravengard''' ({{Pronounce|UL|der|RAY|ven|gard|refs=<ref name="BG.DiA-p4">{{Cite book/Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus|4}}</ref>}}) was a lifelong soldier of the [[Flaming Fist]] who ascended to the rank of Marshal, and served on the [[Council of Four]] of [[Baldur's Gate]] in the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="SCAG-p7">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|7}}</ref>

{{Quote|Baldur's Gate holds a delicate balance that requires all of our precious resources to maintain. That's the greater good.|Grand Duke Ravengard to leaders of [[the Watch]].<ref name="LBG.2-p10">{{Cite comic/Legends of Baldur's Gate 2|10}}</ref>}}

==Description==
As a commander, Ravengard was a true warrior at heart<ref name="TRoT-p15" /> and always took quick and relentless action in battle.<ref name="MiBG.CG-p43" />

==Personality==
[[File:Duke Ulder Ravengard.jpg|thumb|''Duke Ravenguard defending Baldur's Gate.''|340x340px]]
Ulder Ravengard was highly disciplined and precise in his actions,<ref name="MiBG.CG-p43">{{Cite book/Murder in Baldur's Gate/Campaign Guide|43}}</ref> and possessed great martial acuity. Despite that, he did lack the sophistication and tact of other dukes.<ref name="TRoT-p15" /> While acted in the manner he always believed to be "[[good]]", he lost sight when it came to others who exhibited any sort of moral ambiguity.<ref name="MiBG.CG-p43" />

He had an unwavering commitment to his duty to uphold the law, often evaluating others in terms of how they gave and obeyed orders. He also had a great sense of duty to his city and wanted to restore Baldur's Gate's sense of pride.<ref name="TRoT-p15" />

==Abilities==
He dedicated much of his focus to stamping out every lingering presence of [[the Guild]] in Baldur's Gate.<ref name="MiBG.CG-p43" />

==Possessions==
His preferred weapon of choice was a [[bastard sword]].<ref name="MiBG.MS-p8">{{Cite book/Murder in Baldur's Gate/Monster Statistics|8}}</ref><ref name="MiBG.MS-p16">{{Cite book/Murder in Baldur's Gate/Monster Statistics|16}}</ref><ref name="MiBG.MS-p23">{{Cite book/Murder in Baldur's Gate/Monster Statistics|23}}</ref> As a Marshal of the Iron Fist, Ulder wore a ''[[marshal medallion]]'', a ''[[plate of high command]]'', and defended himself with the ''[[Aegis of Baldur's Gate]]'' – all [[enchanted]] items, and the ''Aegis'' was rumored to once had been wielded by [[Balduran|"Baldur"]] himself. During [[Elturel]]'s Descent into [[Avernus]], Ulder recovered an enchanted sword, ''[[Lathander's Might]]'', from the deity's chapel. Following the city's return to the [[Prime Material plane]], the Duke was gifted ''[[Hellrider dagger]]'' by one of the [[Hellriders]]. The imprisonment at the bottom of the [[Gray Harbor]], following Elturel's Descent, gave Ulder Ravengard a ''[[Helm of Torm's Sight]]''.<ref name="idle" />

==Activities==
As Blaze, Ulder Ravengard served as the Warden of the [[Wyrm's Rock]] fortress.

'''Daggerford''' was a small but consequential town located in the [[Delimbiyr Vale]] within the greater [[Sword Coast]].<ref name="NGSF-D-p2" /><ref name="SSC-p12" /><ref name="GCS-p134">{{Cite book/Ghosts of Dragonspear Castle|134}}</ref> While it was primarily a farming community, and considered by some to be a mere stopover, the town had great ambitions to grow and be seen as an alternative to their northern neighbor of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="NGSF-D-p3" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p227" /> 

Despite its modest size and somewhat inflated sense of importance, Daggerford was resilient. It was something of a relic from the old kingdom of [[Delimbiyr]],<ref name="SSC-p12">{{Cite book/Scourge of the Sword Coast|12}}</ref> too stubborn to fall even when a series of wars, crusades, and other conflicts threatened the entirety of the Sword Coast.<ref name="BG.SoD" /> Daggerford's coat-of-arms was a ''[[silver]] [[dagger]]'', covered in blood atop a field of dark blue.<ref name="VoloSC-p34" />

Mirabar also had an elected sceptrana,<ref name="tTO-p54" /> [[Shoudra Stargleam]] in 1370 DR.<ref name="tTO-p53">{{Cite book/The Thousand Orcs/MMP|53}}</ref>

The '''Frozenfar''' was the coldest, most remote region of [[the North]]. It comprised the [[Spine of the World]] mountains;<ref name="N.GSFW-p39" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p169">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|169}}</ref> the frigid lands to the north and west; including [[Icewind Dale]], the [[Cold Run]], the icebergs of the [[Sea of Moving Ice]], and the northeasternmost part of the [[Trackless Sea]].<ref name="N.GSFW-p39">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/The Wilderness|39}}</ref><ref name="N.GSFCC-p19">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/Cities & Civilization|19}}</ref><ref name="LCS.CG-p56">{{Cite book/Legacy of the Crystal Shard/Campaign Guide|56}}</ref><ref group="note">While ''[[The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier]]'' includes the islands of [[Purple Rocks]] and [[Gundarlun]] within the ''Frozenfar'' section, their status as sub-arctic isles (and their latitude) is inconsistent with the in-text description of the Frozenfar in various sources. Additionally, they are not included within the broad categories of the ''Frozenfar'' section of the more recent sourcebook, the [[Legacy of the Crystal Shard]].</ref>

{{TOC limit|3}}
==Usage==
The Frozenfar did not have definable boundaries. The term "Frozenfar" was used by people across [[Faerûn]] to include various areas. For example in [[Amn]] and the [[Inner Sea]], the term "Frozenfar" referred to the city of [[Mirabar]] and everything north, including the Spine of the World mountains.<ref name="VoloN-p7">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the North|7}}</ref> Sometimes the term "Frozenfar" also included the island of [[Ruathym]].<ref name="N.GSFW-p42">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/The Wilderness|42}}</ref>

However in the North, the term "Frozenfar" referred only to the lands north of the Spine of the World.<ref name="VoloN-p7"/> That was among the most commonly accepted usage.<ref name="FRCS3e-p169"/><ref name="SM-p32">{{Cite book/Silver Marches|32}}</ref>

Yet others used "Frozenfar" to refer to all the lands of the North within which individuals could literally freeze to death during their travels.<ref name="N.GSFCC-p19"/>

The '''Order of Tye''' was a prestigious knightly unit dedicated to guarding and escorting the members of the [[Lords' Alliance]] during travels outside the walls of [[Waterdeep]]. They were easy to spot by the Order's colors of azure blue.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

==Organization==
Based in the City of Splendors, the Order of Tye's reputation brought numerous aspiring applicants. The number of aspiring knights led to the Order's leaders to implement rigorous initiation tests. The applicants bore the title of Squire and only the best of them reached the end of the trials. The trials varied greatly with many of the youths losing their lives during exercises, to deadly traps, or when sent to stand against dangerous beasts. The final test was referred to as the Knight's Trial.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

==History==
[[Peneshay|Lord Peneshay]] was the leader of the Order of Tye ''circa'' [[1372 DR]]. Under his leadership the order flourished and gained significant prestige that increased the number of prospective members. In 1372 [[DR]], Squire [[Blancheflor]], Squire [[Launfal]], and Squire [[Camelon]] were facing their Knight's Trial. The trio was sent to the area known as the [[Lonely Barrens]], in [[Delimbiyr Vale]], to the east of [[Daggerford]]. The youths were ordered to slay a vicious and dangerous foe: the [[Mighty Rat of Cunning Demeanor]]. But the squires fled from the creature's lair at the sight of a swarm of hungry [[rat]]s that attempted to consume them. With the help of the individual known as the [[Hero of Daggerford]], the squires survived the ordeal and brought MRCD with them to the Order of Tye as a prisoner and the proof of their dubious valor.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

The '''Red Wizards of Thay''', colloquially known as the '''Red Magic Cult''' in the [[Eastern Heartlands]], were the notorious and nefarious magocratic ruling class of the inhospitable but well-inhabited country of [[Thay]].<ref name="SCAG-p141">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|141}}</ref> Historically they owed their power to being cruel slavers,<ref name="SB.CG-p14">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|14}}</ref><ref name="CoR-p63">{{Cite book/Champions of Ruin|63}}</ref> demonologists,<ref name="SB.CG-p17">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|17}}</ref><ref name="RoT-p7">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|7}}</ref> and arcane experimenters,<ref name="MotM-p60">{{Cite book/Mysteries of the Moonsea|60}}</ref> before most of their numbers wholly dedicated themselves to creating a nation of [[undeath|undead]].<ref name="SCAG-p13">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|13}}</ref>

==Organization==
For centuries, the leaders of the Red Wizards and in fact all of Thay was the [[Council of Zulkirs]].<ref name="FRCS2e.RTR-p27">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|27}}</ref> The ruling body comprised eight of the most powerful Red Wizards, each of whom represented one of the [[schools of magic]].<ref name="LoD-p56"/> Each of the zulkirs and their subordinates fomented intense rivalries with one another, leading to great feuds and near-crippling internal strife.<ref name="LoD-p60">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|60}}</ref>

In the years following the [[War of the Zulkirs]], the Red Wizards of Thay were led by the feared [[lich]] and supreme regent, [[Szass Tam]].<ref name="SCAG-p13"/><ref name="FRCG-p5">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|5}}</ref><ref name="DrM.366.RK">{{Cite dragon/366/Rose Keep}}</ref>

Since Szass Tam's takeover, the exiled Red Wizards have transformed enormously, becoming a loosely organized chain of merchant enclaves who specialized in procuring magical items.<ref name="DrM.366.RK"/>

===Factions===
The Red Wizards have historically been fraught with in-fighting and turmoil. As the stakes rose over the years, these conflicts became more dramatic and impactful to their operations.<ref name="SB.CG-p11">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|11}}</ref><ref name="SB.CG-p15">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|15}}</ref>

In the years before the mid–14{{th}} century [[DR]], the Red Wizards divided themselves into two separate ideological groups. The influential Imperialists sought to conquer Faerûn, by military might, subterfuge, or any other means necessary.<ref name="SB.CG-p8"/> The Researchers took a more isolationist stance, preferring to focus on arcane research within the safety of the [[Thayan Plateau]].<ref name="SB.CG-p9">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|9}}</ref>
