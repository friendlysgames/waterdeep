campaign/quests/act-ii/fireball/design-notes.md:5: ***Three Phases, Not One Chain.*** The original WDH investigation is a sequential chain: find the nimblewright → House of Inspired Hands → detector → Gralhund Villa. One missed check severs any link. The redesign distributes each critical revelation across three independent paths so no single failure blocks progress. Phase 1 (identify the construct) is crime-scene reading. Phase 2 (find all owners) is city-scale elimination. Phase 3 (determine guilt) is convergence under faction pressure. They teach different investigative skills in sequence without being mechanically dependent on each other.
campaign/quests/act-ii/fireball/design-notes.md:19: ***The Change That Makes the Investigation Work.*** In the original WDH, Floxin is the perpetrator and is identified quickly; the investigation ends at the crime scene. The Alexandrian change — nimblewright as actor, Floxin as witness — converts a person-chase (find Floxin, done) into a systematic elimination (identify the construct, find all owners, determine guilt). The Stone being at Gralhund Villa before the investigation begins means the party races against a closing window rather than chasing a mobile target.
campaign/quests/act-ii/fireball/design-notes.md:43: ***Frustration That Becomes Coherent.*** The Faire visit feels like a stall because it is one — the Gralhund nimblewright is not there. The payoff arrives the moment the party moves north and the detector changes behavior on Saerdoun Street. campaign/quests/act-ii/gralhund-villa/design-notes.md:1: # Design Notes: Gralhund Villa
campaign/quests/act-ii/gralhund-villa/design-notes.md:5: The published Dragon Heist resolves Gralhund Villa by having the Stone leave with a villain-specific NPC (Grinda Garloth or Fenerus Stormcastle, depending on the variant). This creates a structural problem: the party has spent an entire arc tracking the Stone to this estate, and it escapes before they can act on that intelligence. The result tends to produce frustration. The investigation was real effort, the address was earned, and the Stone was going to move regardless.
campaign/quests/act-ii/gralhund-villa/design-notes.md:21: The published Gralhund Villa chapter is a keyed dungeon: fixed NPC positions, a linear threat structure, a predetermined outcome. The Alexandrian's revision replaces this with adversary rosters: dynamic NPC positions that respond to PC actions and produce cascading reactions rather than static encounters.
campaign/quests/act-ii/gralhund-villa/design-notes.md:39: When the Manshoon reveal comes, it needs to land as a recontextualization of events the party has already lived through. A **Gralhund Villa** reveal produces a name they file away. A later reveal, after they have encountered his agents in multiple quests without connecting them to a single source, produces the retroactive click that defines a well-structured mystery.

campaign/structure/arc-e-faction-outposts.md:461: ***The Problem of Non-Linear Content in a Linear Format.*** Every quest through **Gralhund Villa** moved in a single investigative current — a question asked, an investigation run, a confrontation resolved. **Faction Outposts** cannot function this way. Ten outposts in any order, at the party's discretion, cannot be scripted into a sequence without defeating the point. The nine-scene architecture is the solution: three framing scenes that run in order (Stone attunement, faction consultation, operational briefing), four faction sections that function as a menu, and two closing scenes that apply after the menu has been used. The faction sections are labeled as scenes to maintain the document's structural vocabulary — each has a Purpose, Content, and Tone — but the GM should understand that "Scene 4" does not mean "fourth in sequence." It means "this is the Xanathar section."
campaign/structure/arc-e-faction-outposts.md:489: ***Chain Entry Points.*** Every faction chain has at least three independent paths to its first outpost: one from prior quest documents (**Gralhund Villa** fallout, **Fireball!** Faire visit), one from within the faction's chain (cross-outpost documents), and one requiring no prior relationship or document (street-level entry). The last path — Grinda's reputation in Mistshore, North Ward canvassing for Brindul Alley, Divine Sense near Coachlamp Lane, theater district playbills — ensures that a party who has avoided faction contacts and found no cross-outpost documents still has a way in. Three Clue Rule requires three independent paths that cannot all fail simultaneously; it does not require three equally prominent paths.
