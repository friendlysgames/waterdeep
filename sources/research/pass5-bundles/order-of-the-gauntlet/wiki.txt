The '''Order of the Gauntlet''' was a coalition of morally upstanding warriors, knights, [[paladin]]s and [[cleric]]s who dedicated themselves to the destruction of [[evil]] in [[Faerûn]] in the 15{{th}} century [[DR]]. They were a unified group, bonded by either their fervent religious beliefs or staunch dedication to enforcing justice in the realms. To these brothers and sisters in arms, evil had to be dealt with and could not be ignored.<ref name="HDQ-p29">{{Cite book/Hoard of the Dragon Queen|29}}</ref><ref name="RoT-p12"/><ref name="SCAG=p147">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|147}}</ref><ref name="FO.OotG">{{cite web | author= [[Wizards of the Coast]]  | title=Factions — Order of the Gauntlet | publisher=[[Wizards of the Coast]] | date=2014 | work=What is D&D? | url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150208232119/http://dnd.wizards.com/dungeons-and-dragons/story/faction/ordergauntlet | accessdate=2014-12-11 }}</ref>

Members of the order were strong in their faith, using it to stand against evil forces.<ref name="RoT-p12"/> While this faith ranged from believing in one's personal god, it extended also to those considered close friends, and especially to oneself. Most often, Knights of the Gauntlet venerated either the God of Guardians, [[Helm]]; two of the deities of [[the Triad]], [[Torm]] and [[Tyr]];<ref name="TA-p30">{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|30}}</ref> as well as the Poet of Justice, [[Hoar]].<ref name="FO.OotG"/>

==Organization==
The Order of the Gauntlet had a number of chapters in various cities located across the western Realms of [[Faerûn]].<ref name="HDQ-p28">{{Cite book/Hoard of the Dragon Queen|28}}</ref><ref name="W.DH-p17"/><ref name="ToA-p47"/><ref name="SKT-p249"/><ref name="SKT-p101"/>

The Order had the following ranks from lowest to highest: Chevall, Marcheon, Whitehawk, Vindicator, and Righteous Hand.<ref name="DMG-2014-p22">{{Cite book/Dungeon Master's Guide 5th edition|22}}</ref>

==Activities==
As part of their duties the Order of the Gauntlet remained vigilant against any potential threats to the Realms, standing ready to stop any aggression. When necessary, they investigated potential threats or secretive organizations, and watched over those creatures they saw as inherently [[evil]].<ref name="FO.OotG"/>

===Beliefs===
The Order of the Gauntlet held three tenets:<ref name="FO.OotG"/>
* Faith was a member's most potent weapon against evil
* Great challenges required heroic demonstrations of both strength and valor
* It was justified to punish an act of evil, not a thought or feeling

==Tactics==
[[File:Valiant Endeavor.jpg|thumb|right|400px|''Knights of the Gauntlet undertaking a valiant endeavor.'']]
The Order was clear on its methods of operation, and would never preemptively attack an opponent. They forewent the approval of local leadership when striking against evil.<ref name="RoT-p12"/> They struck their foes fast and hard, seeking decisive victories.<ref name="Drgn+.1.PF">{{Cite dragon+/1/Player Factions: Our Heroes?}}</ref> They would never punish someone who had not committed an evil act nor those who merely expressed immoral thoughts.<ref name="FO.OotG"/><ref name="SCAG=p85">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|85}}</ref>

It was very uncommon to see a lone member of the Order as they almost always traveled in groups.<ref name="FO.OotG"/>

==Base of Operations==
In [[the North]], the Order of the Gauntlet maintained a strong presence in the city of [[Neverwinter]],<ref name="SKT-p101">{{Cite book/Storm King's Thunder|101}}</ref> several members stationed in [[Waterdeep]],<ref name="W.DH-p17">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|17}}</ref> and a representative in the [[Ten Towns]] city of [[Bryn Shander]].<ref name="SKT-p249"/> In the [[Silver Marches]], they maintained a small watchtower near the [[Silverymoon Pass]] known as the [[Hawk's Nest]]. It was there they bred [[hippogriff]]s, that were trained as aerial mounts.<ref name="SKT-p156">{{Cite book/Storm King's Thunder|156}}</ref>

The order was well-represented in the [[Western Heartlands]]. In the devout city of [[Elturel]], their chapterhouse operated out of the tavern known as [[A Pair of Black Antlers]].<ref name="HDQ-p28"/> Order members in [[Baldur's Gate]] discreetly supported those vigilantes who fought out against lawlessness.<ref name="BG.DiA-p166">{{Cite book/Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus|166}}</ref>

Far south in the land of [[Chult]], the Order first settled in [[Camp Righteous]] near a Chultan shrine, until it was taken over by undead creatures. They then established [[Camp Vengeance]] further up the river, near the [[Aldani Basin]].<ref name="ToA-p47">{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|47}}</ref>

==Possessions==
Members of the order were known to proudly display the [[holy symbol]]s of their respective deities.<ref name="HDQ-p29"/>

==Relationships==
The Order of the Gauntlet counted a number of influential organizations among its allies. While they operated in very different ways, the [[Harpers]] and the Gauntlet ultimately shared the same goals for Faerûn.<ref name="RoT-p12"/>

As of the early 1490s, they sought to ally themselves with the [[Knights of Samular]] to conduct an allied campaign in the [[Savage Frontier]].<ref name="PotA-p15">{{Cite book/Princes of the Apocalypse|15}}</ref>

==History==
[[File:OrderoftheGauntlet Symbol HoF.png|thumb|right|300px|''The Order's symbol.'']]
As of [[1489 DR]], the Order of the Gauntlet was considered one of the newest power groups in Faerûn.<ref name="SCAG=p147"/>

When the [[Cult of the Dragon]] began amassing vast stockpiles of treasure in the 1480s, the Order of the Gauntlet went to investigate. Working with the Harpers, they tracked a cult shipments across the [[Sword Coast]] to learn more about their far-reaching schemes.<ref name="HDQ-p30">{{Cite book/Hoard of the Dragon Queen|30}}</ref> When the threat of the cult, and the summoning of [[Tiamat]] proved to required a coalition of power groups, the [[Council of Waterdeep]] was established.<ref name="RoT-p5">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|5}}</ref> The order's delegate to the council was the [[Torm]]ar paladin of [[Elturel]], [[Ontharr Frume]].<ref name="RoT-p12"/>

At some point in the late 15{{th}} century DR, the order sent a 50-man expedition to Chult so as to rid the jungle of its [[undead]] infestation.<ref name="ToA-p30">{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|30}}</ref><ref name="ToA-p36">{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|36}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|1491}}, the Order attempted to cultivate more political influence in the [[Sumber Hills]] region of the [[Dessarin Valley]]. They sought alliances with various local leaders, including the [[Waterbaron]] of [[Yartar]].<ref name="PotA-p15">{{Cite book/Princes of the Apocalypse|15}}</ref>

==Members==
Members of the Order were staunch comrades, never failing to trust in each other completely. While united, they were highly competitive and strived for excellence in all of their actions.<ref name="RoT-p12">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|12}}</ref><ref name="FO.OotG"/>

==Etymology==
The word "Faerûn" was a modified version of "Faerie", the name of the homeland of ancient [[elves]]. The term was coined by [[Durothil (prince)|Durothil]], prince of [[Tintageer]], [[Faerie]].<ref name="e:ioe-p97">{{Cite book/Evermeet: Island of Elves/MMP|97}}</ref>

==Geography==
{{main|Portal:Geography}}
The continent included terrain that was as varied as any other. Besides the exterior coastline to the west and south, the most dominant feature on the continent was the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]]. This was an irregular inland sea that kept the interior lands fertile, connected the west and east regions of Faerûn and served as a major trade route for many of the bordering nations.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /><ref name="FRCS-p142">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|142}}</ref>

Next in significance was the [[Shaar]], a broad region of grasslands in the south that,<ref name="FRCS-p196">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|196}}</ref> together with the Lake of Steam, separated the area around the inland sea from the coastal nations at the southern edge of the continent. To the east, Faerûn was bordered by a vast region of steppes from [[Kara-Tur]],<ref name="FRCS-p230">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|230}}</ref> and in the north were massive glaciers ([[Pelvuria]] and [[Reghed]]) and tundra.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /> South of the continent, separated by the [[Great Sea]], was the sub-tropical land of [[Zakhara]].<ref name="FRCS-p230"/>

The continent included the following geographic regions.<ref group="note">The following division of Faerûn by compass directions was developed for organizational purposes on the Forgotten Realms Wiki, and is not closely reflected by descriptions in source text. In particular, terms like "the North" and "the South" in the sources can be much more wide-ranging in scope, and as ambiguous, than what is defined here.</ref>

=== LINKED WIKI PAGE (first 5000 chars): Marking the years

{{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

The '''Knights of Samular''', also known as the '''Holy Order of the Knights of Samular''', were an order of [[paladins]] in the service of [[Tyr]], beginning in the 10{{th}} century [[DR]],<ref name="CS.W-p47">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|47}}</ref><ref name="PotA5e-p36-37">{{Cite book/Princes of the Apocalypse|36-37}}</ref> who focused on the supremacy of law.<ref name="CoV-p102">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|102}}</ref> Formerly based at [[Thornhold]], their main base of operations was [[Summit Hall]] in the [[Dessarin Valley]], which was supplemented by the [[Halls of Justice]] in [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="CS.W-p48">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|48}}</ref>

==Membership==
Admittance into the order was open to all [[human|men]] of the Realms, provided they completed a quest and undertook an oath to serve the ''Maimed God''.<ref name="CS.W-p48"/>

==History==
Formed in [[952 DR]] after the [[Troll Wars|Second Troll War]], the Knights of Samular traced their roots to three brothers who became champions of the war, [[Samular Caradoon]], [[Renwick Caradoon]], and [[Amphail the Just]]. While successfully stopping the siege of [[troll]]s through the use of the [[Rings of Samular]] and the magical siege engine known as the ''[[Kezefbane]]'', Amphail and Renwick were killed around the end of the war<ref name="CS.W-p47"/> (though the latter would become an [[archlich]] entombed at the [[Sumber Hills]]). The invading trolls were eventually routed by Samular and his growing group of Tyrran paladins. These knights would eventually come to be known as the Knights of Samular.<ref name="CS.W-p48"/>

After routing the warlord [[Brunyundar Margaster]] from the keep, the knights made Thornhold as their chapterhouse.<ref name="CS.W-p48"/> 

The Knights later lost their headquarters to the [[Zhentarim]] thanks to the treachery of [[Dag Zoreth]]. The keep was then confiscated by [[Bronwyn Caradoon]], who took up residence with her niece, but she did not have good relations with the Knights.<ref>{{Cite book/Thornhold}}</ref><ref name="CS.W-p74">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|74}}</ref>

After their loss of the keep, the Knights of Samular relocated to Summit Hall and kept a force within the [[Halls of Justice]] in the city of Waterdeep.<ref name="CS.W-p48"/>

The '''Savage Frontier''' was the region of [[northwest Faerûn]] north of the [[Delimbiyr|Delimbiyr River]], excluding the [[Sword Coast North]], the [[High Forest]] and the former nation of [[Luruar]], also known as the [[Silver Marches]].<ref name="FRCS3e-p168">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|168}}</ref> In contrast to the civilized cities found to the south and west, the Savage Frontier comprised rural farmsteads and rough settlements of miners and loggers.<ref name="FRCS3e-p169">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|169}}</ref>

It was a temperate land,<ref name="NGSF.W-p4">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/The Wilderness|4}}</ref> with rugged landscape that was rich with natural resources.<ref name="FRCS3e-p168" />

{{quote|The North has sinfully warm days late in the year, which some call elf summers, that merely bulwark the wary for the inevitable chills of winter to come.|An excerpt from ''[[My Travels]]''<ref name="BST">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower||1}}</ref>}}

==Geography==
Generally speaking the Savage Frontier comprised three greater regions: the expansive [[Delimbiyr Vale]] that encompassed its [[Delimbiyr River|namesake river]] along the eastern and southern borders of the High Forest;<ref name="NGSF.W-p63" /><ref name="SKT-p74" /> the [[Fallen Lands]], which rebuffed the [[Anauroch]] desert;<ref name="NGSF.W-p62" /><ref name="SKT-p83">{{Cite book/Storm King's Thunder|83}}</ref> and the lowlands of the [[Dessarin Valley]], that bordered the western trees of the High Forest.<ref name="NGSF.W-p46">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/The Wilderness|46}}</ref><ref name="SKT-p81">{{Cite book/Storm King's Thunder|81}}</ref>

The '''Council of Waterdeep''' was a conciliary body formed in the 1480s [[DR]],{{ToD-time}} tasked with meeting the rising threat the [[Cult of the Dragon]] posed to the people of the [[Sword Coast]] and [[the North]].<ref name="RoT-p6">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|6}}</ref>

==Organization==
The council was attended by delegates from five of the most influential and powerful factions of [[West Faerûn|the west]] and the North. These 'Five Factions'<ref name="ToD.9">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 9}}</ref> included the league of city-states known as the [[Lords' Alliance]],<ref name="RoT-p13" /> the knights of the [[Order of the Gauntlet]],<ref name="RoT-p12" /> [[druid]]s of the [[Emerald Enclave]],<ref name="RoT-p13">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|13}}</ref> agents of the [[Harpers]],<ref name="RoT-p11">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|11}}</ref> and the merchant-mercenaries of the [[Zhentarim]].<ref name="RoT-p16" />

As the largest and arguably most influential of the factions—representing several of the largest city-states of [[Faerûn]]—delegates of the Lords' Alliance held seven seats on the Council of Waterdeep.<ref name="RoT-p14">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|14}}</ref> The other factions held either a single seat,<ref name="RoT-p13" /> or only a few. Each delegate was accompanied by their own retinue of aides.<ref name="RoT-p19" />

==Activities==
The Council of Waterdeep held four sessions<ref name="RoT-p19" /> during which the cult,<ref name="RoT-p5" /> and their [[chromatic dragon]] allies arose as an ever-increasing threat to the Realms.<ref name="RoT-p5" /> During each of their meetings, representatives of each faction measured how the council's actions aligned with their own interests, and weighed what resources they could pledge to the cause. A single band of [[adventurer]]s served as an intermediary of sorts between the Five Factions, trying to balance the needs and ideologies of any one group with those of the others.<ref name="RoT-p19" />

==Base of Operations==
The council held their sessions within the [[Lords' Palace]] in [[Waterdeep/Castle Ward|Castle Ward]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="RoT-p20" />

==History==
===Formation===
The preliminary events that led to the Council of Waterdeep's founding was the campaign by the Cult of the Dragon to amass a hoard of wealth sufficient enough to appease [[Tiamat]], the Goddess of Dragons . Cult members and their [[storm giant]] accomplice [[Blagothkus]] attempted to transport that wealth aboard [[Skyreach Castle]] to the [[Well of Dragons]] in preparation for the [[ritual]] that would grant Tiamat admittance to [[Faerûn]].<ref name="HDQ-p77">{{Cite book/Hoard of the Dragon Queen|77}}</ref> Unfortunately for Tiamat and her cultists, a band of adventurers thwarted that plot and moved back the timeline for their ritual.<ref name="RoT-p20" />

Not long after Skyreach Castle was neutralized, agents of the cult sounded the ancient draconic artifact known as the ''[[Draakhorn]]''.<ref name="RoT-p19" /><ref name="RoT-p20" /> The horn's booming call was heard all across the Sword Coast and the North.<ref name="RoT-p19" /> It was perceived as a call-to-action for dragons across the wester,<ref name="RoT-p20" /> and was a grim sign of the conflict just on the horizon.<ref name="RoT-p19" />

The true catalyst of the council's formation however was the murder of the [[Masked Lord]] of Waterdeep, [[Arthagast Ulbrinter]], at the hands of the dragon cultists. Lord Ulbrinter was husband to [[Remi Haventree]], a devoted agent of the Harpers. The '''Dessarin Valley''', also known as the '''Gateway to the North''' by its residents, was a valley region situated between the [[Sword Mountains]] and the [[High Forest]].<ref name="SKT-p74"/> It was home to a diverse range of folk, although most inhabitants were either [[human]]s or [[halfling]]s. The main industry of the Dessarin Valley was agriculture,<ref name="SKT-p81"/> and [[Goldenfields]] played a large part in the agricultural economy of [[the North]].<ref name="PotA-p19">{{Cite book/Princes of the Apocalypse|19}}</ref>

The Dessarin was unlike the rest of the [[Savage Frontier]]. While it was largely unsettled, comprising many scattered, remote villages and farmsteads, the valley was much safer than the surrounding area had been in years past.<ref name="PotA-p18">{{Cite book/Princes of the Apocalypse|18}}</ref><ref name="SKT-p81"/>

==Geography==
The region of the Dessarin Valley was bordered on the east by the [[High Forest]] and the [[Forlorn Hills]], and on the west by the [[Long Road]].<ref name="SKT-p81">{{Cite book/Storm King's Thunder|81}}</ref>

'''Savra Belabranta''' ({{Pronounce|SAV|rah|_|bel|ah|BRAN|tah|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was a member of the [[Cult of the Howling Hatred]] during the [[Elemental Evil]] crisis of [[1491 DR]],<ref name="PotA-p46" /> but later became a member of the [[Order of the Gauntlet]] in [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="WDH5e-p39" />

==Personality==
While a member of the Cult, Savra was totally brainwashed by it and fully believed everything.<ref name="PotA-p46" /> However, she believed that they should use elemental fury only against [[Waterdeep]]'s enemies.<ref name="PotA-p50">{{Cite book/Princes of the Apocalypse|50}}</ref>

==Relationships==
Savra was a member of the [[Belabranta]]s, a noble Waterdhavian family whose main activity was raising [[griffon]]s to serve as mounts for the [[Griffon Cavalry]].<ref name="WDH5e-p39" />

==History==
Savra hailed from Waterdeep, where she joined the [[Feathergale Knights]], a cover group for the [[Cult of the Howling Hatred]]. There, she was indoctrinated into the worship of [[Yan-C-Bin]]. In [[1491 DR]], Savra showed some adventurers the [[Feathergale Spire]].<ref name="PotA-p46" />

Some time later, Savra repented from her cultist days and became a member of the [[Order of the Gauntlet]]. Fully reformed, she began to serve [[Tyr]] in an attempt to atone for the evil acts she committed and to regain her honor. She returned to Waterdeep and worked for the Order at the [[Halls of Justice]], assigning missions and swearing new members into the Order.<ref name="WDH5e-p39" />

'''Belabranta''' ({{Pronounce|BEL|ah|brahn|tah|refs=<ref name="CS-WW2e-p11">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|11}}</ref>}}) was among the oldest [[Nobility of Waterdeep|noble houses]] of [[Waterdeep]],<ref name="WatN-p46">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|46}}</ref> being officially ennobled in [[952 DR]]. They were famous [[griffon]] breeders, trainers and even hunters.<ref name="CoS.NHoW-p2" />

==Activities==
The head of House Belabranta was one of twelve noble heads permitted to choose new [[Lords of Waterdeep|Masked Lords]] in times of peril.<ref name="LLotR-17">{{Cite web/Ed Greenwood's Patreon/Lost Lore of the Realms 17}}</ref>

==Base of Operations==
[[File:Sea Ward-Assumbar-Cassalanter-Dezlentyr-Eirontalar Villa.png|thumb|right|270px|''Map showing the location of the Belabranta Villa (labeled <code>$52</code>) ''circa'' 1372 DR.'']]
The family owned a villa in the [[Waterdeep/Sea Ward|Sea Ward]], on the northeast corner of [[Mendever Street]] and [[Rough Road]].<ref name="CSW3e-p92,95">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|92,95}}</ref>

==History==
Before House Belabranta became an official noble house of Waterdeep it could claim one of its members as a hero of the city. Ilithrew Belabranta was a famous griffon rider and hero of the second [[Trollwars|Trollwar]]. During that time he amassed a small fortune by plundering slain [[troll]] hoards. With some of those funds he was able to commission the construction of the ''[[Griffontack of Belabranta]]'', a set of magical items passed down to future generations.<ref name="CoS.W-p133">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|133}}</ref>

During an attack by an orc horde, known as the [[Black Claw]], upon Waterdeep in [[1026 DR]], now Lord Palinor Belabranta took to the skies on his griffon steed, only to be brought low by a volley of orc arrows. He and his mount crashed into the orc ranks and it was thought the Griffontack of Belabranta was lost forever.<ref name="CoS.W-p133" />

For quite some time, Lord Huld had an affair with a courtesan at the [[House of Purple Silks]] within the city's [[Sea Ward of Waterdeep|Sea Ward]]. Unfortunately, he was unaware that his lover was actually a [[succubus]] named [[Elinaera]]. Some time before [[1372 DR]], the Lord and the fiend had a falling out and got into a fight. While the [[fiend]]ess was able to deal Lord Huld a mortal wound, the wizard of House Belabranta banished Elinaera from [[Toril]]. Just before he died, the lord's mind was consumed by a greater doppelganger of the [[Unseen]] who assumed his form and took over leadership of the noble house.<ref name="CoS.NHoW-p8">{{Cite web enhancement/Noble Houses of Waterdeep|8}}</ref>

Around 1372 DR, the ''Griffontack of Belabranta'' was recovered from the vault of [[House Ilvastarr]]. Lord Moedt Belabranta hired adventurers to break into the Ilvastarr Vault<ref name="CoS.W-p33" /> and prove the family’s age-long suspicion that a member of House Ilvastarr looted the artifacts from the fallen Lord Palinor.<ref name="CoS.W-p133" />

'''Barrowin Undurr''' was a devoted [[dwarf]] [[cleric]] of [[Gorm Gulthyn]].<ref name="Heroes" /> Along with several other [[adventurer]]s,<ref name="ToD.1" /><ref name="ToD.2" /> she played a vital role in thwarting the schemes of the [[Cult of the Dragon]] in the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="ToD.9" /><ref name="ToD.10" />

{{Quote|Love is like an axe through the heart|Barrowin.<ref name="Heroes" />}}

==Personality==
She had a dour outlook on life but would always stand against [[evil]], especially when it moved against dwarvenkind.<ref name="Heroes" />

==Possessions==
Barrowin took immaculate care of her weapons and armor. She never let them become rusty.<ref name="Heroes" />

She kept a single [[gem]] along with her during her travels, and had regular conversations with it. It was unknown whether or not the gem ever reciprocated.<ref name="Heroes" />

==History==
===Early Life===
Barrowin was born in the area of the [[Great Rift]], and she managed her family's business holdings in [[Ironmaster]] near [[Icewind Dale]]. She spent fifteen years there on assignment in [[Ironstar]]. Her life was not exciting enough for her, so she asked her family for relocation. When that request went unheard, she called on her deity to provide a new path.<ref name="Heroes" />

Gorm answered indirectly. Barrowin found a new life, by falling in love with [[Storn Skulldark]], a wealthy young Ironstar dwarf. Barrowin and Storn set out from Ironstar to build a fortress along the route from [[Neverwinter]] to Icewind Dale, within the [[Spine of the World]] mountain range.<ref name="Heroes" />

The project had many setbacks, chief of which was the sudden disappearance of Storn Skulldark's fortune in [[Ironmaster]]. Storn traveled back without Barrowin to find his missing gold. During his journey he was attacked and carried off by a [[white dragon]]. His fate was unknown. Barrowin was devastated by this loss. She left Ironmaster, and joined the [[Order of the Gauntlet]].<ref name="Heroes"/>

===Tyranny of Dragons===
[[File:ToD comic fighting cult.png|thumb|right|300px|Barrowin and her allies battling the [[Cult of the Dragon]] in [[Icewind Dale]].]]
Some time in the 1480s DR,{{ToD-time}} Barrowin served as the Order of the Gauntlet's representative on a mission to stop the Cult of the Dragon from retrieving the ''[[Red Dragon Mask]]''. Along with her new companions, [[Alaeros Margaster]] and [[Nymmestra Durothil]], Barrowin traveled from [[Neverwinter]] back to Icewind Dale to stop the Cult.<ref name="ToD.1">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 1}}</ref> There the trio picked up two more allies, [[Ratshadow]] and [[Talon Everhale]],<ref name="ToD.2">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 2}}</ref> and found out the cult had uncovered a stone-arch ''[[portal]]'' that granted them passageway to the isle of [[Tuern]]. Eventually, Barrowin and her four allies acquired full knowledge about the Cult of the Dragon's ultimate plan.<ref name="ToD.3">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 3}}</ref>

While on Tuern, Barrowin and her allies ventured into the [[Flame Fault]] and slew [[Lostmauth]],{{ToD-Lost}} one of the isle's three [[red dragon]] overlords.<ref name="ToD.4">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 4}}</ref> Their efforts were in vain however, as the cult's leader [[Severin Silrajin]] acquired the ''Red Dragon Mask'' and fled back to Neverwinter.<ref name="ToD.5">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 5}}</ref> 

While they were stranded for a short while,<ref name="ToD.5" /> eventually the group made it back to Neverwinter. While there, Ratshadow managed to steal the ''[[White Dragon Mask]]'' from the dragon cultists,<ref name="ToD.6">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 6}}</ref> and stashed it at [[Ulbrinter Villa]] in [[Waterdeep]] for his [[Zhentarim]] masters.<ref name="ToD.7">

It is not set in a particular year, but is assumed to be the [[1492 DR|Year of Three Ships Sailing, 1492 DR]] (see note).

'''''Dungeons & Dragons: Neverwinter''''' is a free-to-play massively multiplayer online roleplaying game developed by Cryptic Studios and published by Perfect World Entertainment. It was released on the 20th of June 2013, with additional content updates (called Modules) releasing every few months thereafter.

The game deals with the aftereffects of an attack on the city of [[Neverwinter]] by [[Valindra Shadowmantle]] and a [[dracolich]]. Several factions and individuals attempt to take advantage of the chaos caused by the attack. Later updates have generally followed [[Wizards of the Coast|Wizards]]' official story arcs.

==Main Plot==
===The Battle of Sleeping Dragon Bridge===
{{See also|The Siege of Neverwinter}}
Washing up on the beach in [[1479 DR]] after their ship was sunk by the dracolich, several [[adventurer]]s were thrust into battle against Valindra's [[undead]] minions while trying to reach the relative safety of Neverwinter's walls. The adventurers were joined by Private [[Wilfred]], a Neverwinter graycloak, who was killed by [[Valindra Shadowmantle|Valindra]] when he attempted to confront her directly on Sleeping Dragon Bridge. Meanwhile, a tiefling warlock named [[Makos]] dispatched the dracolich and used his magic to prevent its soul from returning to its phylactery to regenerate. For their valor in defending the city against the undead, the adventurers became known as the Heroes of Sleeping Dragon Bridge.

===The Crown of Neverwinter===
[[File:Neverwinter_MMO_-_Quest_-_The_Thief_of_The_Crown_(Cutscene).png|thumb|The theft of the crown]]
During an investigation of the vaults beneath the [[Hall of Justice]], the Heroes of Sleeping Dragon Bridge discovered that the [[Sons of Alagondar#Nashers|Nashers]] managed to sneak into the vault during Valindra's attack and steal the ''[[Crown of Neverwinter]]''. After tracking down the Nasher rogue [[Honor Reigh]], whom they saw stealing the crown with another Nasher, clues pointed to a Nasher base in the [[Neverwinter#Blacklake|Blacklake district]]. The heroes found the Blacklake District to not only be overrun with Nashers, but covered with a magical sludge that was bubbling up from the Blacklake itself. The heroes aided an alchemist in investigating it, ultimately confirming that it originated with a [[Chosen of Ghaunadaur|Chosen]] of [[Ghaunadaur]] living in the lake. After dispatching the [[Chosen]], the heroes continued searching for the other thief and eventually discovered he had been kidnapped by the [[Dead Rats]]. Down in the sewers beneath the city, the heroes located the other thief, [[Gothwan Cooper]], who claimed that his leader [[Karzov]] left him to die - gladly giving up Karzov's location in exchange for his life. The Nashers set fire to their safehouse when the heroes arrived and Karzov escaped to their headquarters, an old warehouse. The heroes followed and killed Karzov, bringing the Crown back to [[Neverwinter#Protector's Enclave|Protector's Enclave]].

===Orc Invasion===

While battling the orcs, the heroes learned that their leaders were operating from within the [[Neverwinter#Cloak Tower|Cloak Tower]].
