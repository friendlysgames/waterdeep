The '''Zhentarim''' ({{Pronounce|zz|HENT|ah|rim|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-CR-p92">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Set/Cyclopedia of the Realms|92}}</ref>}}) also known as the '''Black Network''',<ref name="FRCS3e-p281">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|281}}</ref> was a mercenary company and greater mercantile organization in [[Faerûn]],<ref name="SCAG-p78">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|78}}</ref> who, over their 200 years of existence, had a storied history as a cadre of self-serving [[thieves]], spies, [[assassin]]s, and malevolent [[wizard]]s,<ref name="SCAG-p77">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|77}}</ref> who for a time, were indentured by their leaders to serve dark gods [[Bane]]<ref name="FRCS3e-p282">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|282}}</ref> and [[Cyric]].<ref name="C&D-p9">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|9}}</ref> Over the decades, the organization experienced several leaps and bounds in terms of successes,<ref name="RoZK:CB-p34" /> but also major misfortune, particularly for their historical strongholds around the [[Moonsea]].<ref name="FRCG-p151">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|151}}</ref> As of [[1489 DR]] the Zhentarim was headquartered in the [[Western Heartlands]], at [[Darkhold|Darkhold Castle]], nestled in the [[Darkhold Vale|valley of the same name]].<ref name="SCAG-p78" />

==Aims & Goals==
As an extension of the will of the dark wizard [[Manshoon]], the Zhentarim was initially formed as a mercenary company to support his rule over the [[Moonsea]] with the aim of spreading that control over all of Faerûn.<ref name="CaD-p32">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|32}}</ref> After he recruited the faithful of Bane, unscrupulous merchants and lesser [[mage]]s to his cause,<ref name="RoZK:CB-p15">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|15}}</ref> they collectively sought to dominate all aspects of the Realms, either by manipulation, outright control, or, if they couldn't exert control over something, extinction.<ref name="C&D-p36">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|36}}</ref>

After the mass purge by the Cyricists, known as the [[Banedeath]], and the subsequent partial destruction of [[Zhentil Keep]] following the [[Cyrinishad]] debacle, the control of the Zhentarim passed to the Bane-worshiping [[Fzoul Chembryl]]. He made secret deals with leaders in the Moonsea to erect temples to [[Iyachtu Xvim]], Bane's divine son, within the capitals of their city-states. His aim to extend the will of the Lord of Tyrants across Toril was a dynamic factor behind many of the changes within the organization.<ref name="CaD-p32" />

By the late 15{{th}} century DR, the goals of the Zhentarim returned to their original ideals, despite their more benign public perception. By offering the ''best'' guards and mercenaries, with the final aim of an iron-fisted monopoly on protection services in the Realms, they sought to make the merchants of Faerûn, and thus the continental economy, completely reliant upon them as an organization. While many of the practices and services they offered were completely legal, they were not above spending a tremendous amount of gold to destroy their competitors and secure future profits to increase their wealth, influence, and power.<ref name="Factions">{{Cite web/Factions: The Zhentarim}}</ref>

==Activities==
{{quote|Ooh, the sneaky rascals. If it wasn't for the rampant egotism, random murdering, and foul gods, I might quite like these Zhentarim.|[[Purfbin Doogrick]]<ref name="nwndod">{{Cite game/Neverwinter Nights: Darkness over Daggerford}}</ref>}}
In order to achieve their financial goals, the Zhentarim agents often dealt in illicit goods and contraband, using their extensive mercantile contacts to move products throughout the Moonsea, neighboring lands and over Faerûn's more hazardous terrains. The types of commodities they dealt in were slaves, [[poison]], drugs and even [[smokepowder]] [[weapon]]s.<ref name="LoD-p97">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|97}}</ref>

As a means of eliminating their competition, the Zhentarim used the [[Zhentilar]] army of Zhentil Keep or recruited bands of monstrous [[humanoid]]s to take down rival caravans, often completing the route under their own banner to earn good faith and new business with merchant groups. They were not above resorting to sabotage, blackmail, and arson to outright murder to undermine their mercantile or political opponents. They were even known to purposefully upset the balance of natural beasts, stir up aggressive monster populations within the wild, and even have their [[mage]]s summon foul monstrosities to threaten isolated towns and villages, in effect forcing the residents to rely on the protection of the Zhentilar.<ref name="LoD-p97" />

Zhentarim wizards usually tasked their apprentices with dangerous missions in exchange for tutelage in [[the Art]] as opposed to more traditional modes of payment.<ref>{{Cite web/Ed Greenwood's Patreon/Little Lore 15}}</ref>

==Organization==
[[File:Naerth.png|thumb|227px|''Zhentarim often made use of [[flying snake]]s as messenger animals.'']]
The Zhentarim had the following ranks from lowest to highest: Fang, Wolf, Viper, Ardragon, and Dread Lord.<ref name="DMG-2014-p22">{{Cite book/Dungeon Master's Guide 5th edition|22}}</ref>

===Inner Circle===
From the early years of the organization until [[1369 DR]], the Zhentarim was led by the triumvirate of [[Manshoon]], the ultimate authority and Lord of [[Zhentil Keep]]; [[Sememmon]], his student in the arcane who presided over Darkhold; and [[Fzoul Chembryl]], the High Priest of Bane.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p32">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|32}}</ref> However, the Zhentarim was rife with mistrust, even amongst its upper echelons. While Manshoon had a bond of trust with his pupil Sememmon, his relationship with Fzoul was one of mutual disdain shrouded by public respect. While the three shared the same goal of increasing their collective wealth and power, it was at best an uneasy alliance that ultimately fell apart.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p34">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|34}}</ref>

====Manshoon====
In addition to his role as the ultimate authority of the Zhentarim, Manshoon held the title of High Lord of Zhentil keep, speaker of the city-state's [[Council of Lords (Zhentil Keep)|Council of Lords]], and had the full backing of the Zhentilar army.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p62">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|62}}</ref> He kept close control over the Moonsea Zhentarim, and controlled the other city states such as [[Hillsfar]], the Citadel of the Raven, and even [[Mulmaster]].<ref name="RoZK:CB-p42">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|42}}</ref> Prior to the partial destruction of Zhentil Keep, Manshoon moved his operations over to the Citadel.<ref name="RoZK:CG-p30">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|30}}</ref>

====Sememmon====
The obedient and skillful lieutenant of Manshoon long served as the Lord of Darkhold and unfailingly loyal servant of the Zhentarim.<ref name="FRCS3e-p229">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|229}}</ref> Due to its distance from Manshoon and the Moonsea Zhentarim, the mage-lord only recruited the most loyal and capable agents, about 1000 strong, to the dark keep hidden within the [[Far Hills]].<ref name="RoZK:CG-p35">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|35}}</ref> Sememmon's second-in-command was the powerful mage [[Ashemmi]], a former [[Harper]], his consort and true love.<ref name="FRCS3e-p229" /><ref name="RoD-p74">{{Cite dragon/357/Volo's Guide: Renegades of Darkhold|74}}</ref><ref name="C&D-p37" />

====Fzoul Chembryl====
Before he became the [[exarch]] of Bane following the [[Spellplague]],<ref name="FRCG-p81">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|81}}</ref> the faithful servant Fzoul served as the High Priest of his [[church of Bane|clergy]] at the [[Black Altar]] in Zhentil Keep.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p32" />

===Post Re-emergence of Bane===
After [[1370 DR]], the Black Network followed a strict linear hierarchy with Fzoul Chembryl serving as the Zhentarim's Grand Tyrant. Only the powerful lords would report directly to him, while lesser officers and agents would only report to their immediate superiors.<ref name="LoD-p96" />

The only exception to the inherent power structure of the Zhentarim was Manshoon. While he allowed Fzoul to act as the supreme leader of the Zhentarim, he maintained command over any of its agents or resources to further its goals or his own endeavors.<ref name="LoD-p96" />

==Relationships==
===Allies===
[[File:Zhentarim Agents.jpg|thumb|right|300px|''A rather intimidating group of Zhentarim agents.'']]
The Zhentarim had few allies, in comparison to their foes, but they were highly valued for their strategic value. The roster of these supporters were constantly changing along with interests and powers of the Zhentarim.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p40" /> They included:
*[[Beholder]]s: Ever since Manshoon first allied himself with [[Xantriph]] of the [[Temple in the Sky]], the Zhentarim maintained a mutually beneficial relationship with various [[beholder]]s across Faerûn, though they maintained their own personal agendas. Xulla, a longtime ally of Fzoul Chembryl, took over this post following Xantriph's death,<ref name="RoZK:CB-p40">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|40}}</ref> before becoming the Caravan Lord within the network.<ref name="EGpEFG-p72" /> [[Manxam]], the oldest and most powerful eye tyrant in [[West Faerûn|western Faerûn]], served as the ruler of [[Teshwave]] for decades,<ref name="C&D-p36" /> before eventually relocating to Darkhold the following century.<ref name="SCAG-p79">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|79}}</ref> Under the assumed name of "Xalval", the [[elder orb]] [[Vaxall]] of the [[Twisted Rune]] was an ally of Teldorn Darkhope and controlled the city of [[Kzelter]].<ref name="C&D-p36" /> [[Xualahuu]] controlled the oasis of [[Olomaa]] in [[Anauroch]]<ref name="Anaur-p52">{{Cite book/Anauroch|52}}</ref><ref name="RoZK:CB-p45">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|45}}</ref> and [[Zantiid]] oversaw the [[Stonelands]] in Cormyr.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p47">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|47}}</ref>
*[[Bloodswords]]: This [[Amn]]ian mercenary company drove away [[Cormyr]]ian patrols and non-Zhent caravans from the region of the [[High Road (Cormyr)|High Road]] west of [[Eagle Peak]] in the Storm Horns.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p40" />
*[[Harondalbar]]: The [[black dragon]] who maintained a lair near the [[Yellow Snake Pass]] kept non-Zhentarim forces from the area in return for the treasure, creatures, and adulation brought to him by his "keeper" [[Faren Starlight]].<ref name="RoZK:CB-p41">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|41}}</ref>
*[[Waterdeep]]: Unbeknownst to many, the Zhentarim had a tenuous peace with [[Khelben Arunsun|Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep during the mid-to-late 1300s [[DR]], thanks to Fzoul Chembryl. Khelben "Blackstaff" exchanged certain information to Fzoul in exchange for an assurance that the Black Network would not expand their influence west of the [[Thunder Peaks]] for 30 years. This bargain led to a split between the [[Harpers]] and Khelben's independently operating [[Moonstars]].<ref name="LoD-p98">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|98}}</ref>

===Foes===
Over the years, the Zhentarim drew the ire and hostility of a great number of peoples and organizations.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p37">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|37}}</ref> They opposed any group they perceived as a threat to their interests to grow their trade and influence across Faerûn, including the [[Iron Throne]] of the [[Sword Coast]], the [[Rundeen]] of [[Calimshan]], and the fanatical [[Cult of the Dragon]].<ref name="LoD-p98" /> They included:
*[[Harpers]]: Due to their diametrically opposed world views, the Harpers were at odds with the Zhentarim ever since they became aware of the network's existence. Many of the highest-ranking members of the Harpers, such as [[Elminster Aumar]], and several of the [[Seven Sisters]] had sizable bounties placed upon their heads by the Black Network.<ref name="LoD-p98" />
*[[Knights of the North]]: This group of resistance fighters was formed in [[1355 DR]] after Zhentil Keep betrayed the allied forces of the Moonsea by violently seizing the Citadel of the Raven. Following the death of Commander [[Galauntar Hawkhelm]], his fellow Ravenar soldiers banded together and launched a guerrilla campaign against Zhent holdings and Zhentarim operations within the Moonsea.<ref name="CoV-p93">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|93}}</ref>
*[[Iron Throne]]<ref name="LoD-p98" />
*[[Cult of the Dragon]]<ref name="LoD-p98" />
*[[Rundeen]]<ref name="LoD-p98" />
*[[Red Wizards of Thay]]<ref name="LoD-p98" />

===Founding===
The Zhentarim was founded in the {{YearlinkName|1261}}, by the power-hungry wizard Manshoon, the lord of Zhentil Keep, as a tool for his ambitions of expanding his realm of control.<ref name="LoD-p96" /><ref name="tGHotR-p128">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|128}}</ref><ref name="RoZK.CB-p14">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|14}}</ref><ref name="RoZK.CB-p28">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|28}}</ref> He was soon joined by the Banite [[cleric]] Fzoul Chembryl in the {{YearlinkName|1263}}, who broke away from the orthodox church in [[Mulmaster]], bringing with him a small army of his fellow [[priest]]s into the lesser ranks of the Zhentarim. He then disposed of the high-ranking priests within Zhentil Keep and converted the city's lesser clergy to the worship of Bane.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p15" /><ref name="RoZK:CB-p28">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|28}}</ref> Paranoid even in the early years of their alliance, Manshoon offset the possible threat of Fzoul's influence by concocting a plan with the beholder Xantriph. They had the Banite priests confess their sins and swear fealty to Manshoon alone. Xantriph's lair, the floating Temple in the Sky, became an outpost for Zhentilar troops and Banite priests in former [[Teshendale]].<ref name="RoZK:CB-p15" /><ref name="EGpEFG-p71-72">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|71-72}}</ref>

Manshoon planned on capturing the Citadel of the Raven during the first days of the Zhentarim, though they were not powerful enough as an organization to openly attack a city of the Moonsea. While it was openly discussed for years, many senior members of the Zhentarim advocated against assaulting the city, fearful of losing their investments that funded the Black Network.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p19" /> Putting that scheme on hold, they greatly strengthened their economic holdings during the [[Moonsea War]] of {{YearlinkName|1306}}. The allied forces of Zhentil Keep, [[Hillsfar]], [[Melvaunt]], [[Phlan]] and [[Sembia]] united together to defeat the city-state of [[Mulmaster]].<ref name="RoZK:CB-p17">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|17}}</ref> 

===Growth===
In the {{YearlinkName|1312}}, Manshoon seized the fortress of [[Darkhold]] from the [[Lich]]-Queen [[Varalla]] in order to extend his control into the [[Western Heartlands]].<ref name="FRCS3e-p227">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|227}}</ref> Four years later, they invaded and seized [[Teshendale]] in the {{YearlinkName|1336}}.<ref name="FRCS3e-p116">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|116}}</ref>

They then conquered and installed their own rulers in [[Daggerdale]] in [[1336 DR]].<ref name="GHR-p137">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|137}}</ref> Manshoon consolidated his rule over all Zhent lands and declared himself the High Lord of Zhentil Keep the [[1337 DR|following year]]. These actions caused the nations of the Moonsea to grow wary of the Zhentarim and Zhentil Keep.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p29">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|29}}</ref>

Also that year, Manshoon granted the previously occupied [[Yûlash]] its independence, and maneuvered this act of "good will" to call a meeting of the rulers of the Moonsea in the neutral town of [[Elmwood]]. He called for a garrison to be established at the Citadel of the Raven to protect the greater Moonsea from a horde of orcs that was stirring further north. He "graciously" offered to move the recently evicted Zhentilar soldiers from Yûlash to the city,<ref name="RoZK:CB-p16">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|16}}</ref> though this was merely part of a long-term scheme to take over the city.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p19" />

The Zhentarim saw significant losses in the next few years when [[Jyordhan]], puppet ruler of Shadowdale, was assassinated by [[Khelben Arunsun]];<ref name="tGHotR-p139">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|139}}</ref> Xantriph was killed by the [[Knights of Myth Drannor]];<ref name="SoE-?">{{Cite book/Swords of Eveningstar/Hardcover 2006|?}}</ref> and [[Malyk]], the puppet ruler of Daggerdale, was killed by [[Randal Morn]], leading to all-out civil war.<ref name="GHR-p140">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|140}}</ref>

Despite their losses, the time was right in the {{YearlinkName|1355}} for the Zhentarim to strike against their former allies in the Citadel of the Raven. They had their agents planted within the city kill as many as they could, using [[poison]] and magic. The orcs and [[Zhentilar]] poured into the city and claimed it for the Zhentarim. One of the few survivors within the Citadel was the mortally wounded [[Galauntar Hawkhelm]], whose eventual death would lead to the formation of the [[Knights of the North]].<ref name="RoZK:CB-p19">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|19}}</ref>

===Banedeath===
During the [[Time of Troubles]] of the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, Bane arrived in the [[Black Altar]].<ref name="RoZK:CB-p21">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|21}}</ref> He ordered his devoted priest Fzoul Chembryl and Manshoon's apprentice Sememmon to work together, much to their mutual displeasure,<ref name="RoZK:CB-p34" /> in an assault on the [[Twisted Tower of Ashaba]] in [[Shadowdale]] so that he could enter [[Morningdawn Hall]]. After the destruction of the Dark God's [[avatar]], the newly ascended deity Cyric, who took on his divine portfolio, became the focus of worship for a great number of the members of the Zhentarim. Fzoul Chembryl and a few others maintained their faith, while some others converted to the veneration of [[Iyachtu Xvim]], Bane's divine son.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p21" />

In the {{YearlinkName|1360}}, the Zhentarim began their campaign to take control of a trade route that ran through the desert of [[Anauroch]]. They were initially rebuffed by the unified tribes of the Bedine, aided by the Harper agent [[Lander]] and an exiled [[sorceress]] known as [[Ruha]].<ref name="PS">{{Cite book/The Parched Sea|?}}</ref>

The conflict between the opposing faiths embedded within the Zhentarim culminated in an event known as the [[Banedeath]], which began on [[Marpenoth 30]], the {{YearlinkName|1361}}. [[Xeno Mirrormane]],<ref name="RoZK:CB-p21" /> began a purge of all the remaining Banites within Zhentil Keep, eventually killing his brother and sparing none except for Fzoul Chembryl, due to his "special relationship" with Cyric. While the aid of the [[church of Cyric]] allowed Zhentil Keep to grow in power over the next few years,<ref name="RoZK:CB-p22">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|22}}</ref> Manshoon realized that Cyric was no replacement for the calculating cunning and patience of Bane.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p23">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|23}}</ref> 

Over a period of six months in the {{YearlinkName|1366}}, Manshoon quietly and discreetly shifted resources and personnel from Zhentil Keep to the Citadel of the Raven, being distrustful of the impulsive Cyric and his inept clergy members. Fzoul Chembryl saw these actions and remained neutral, still bitter from his loss of influence as the High Priest of Bane.<ref name="RoZK:CB-p23" />

===Cyrinishad===
Cyric was eventually undermined by a divine conspiracy involving High Lord Fzoul. The larger northern half of Zhentil Keep was left in ruins as a result. Some 1,500 Zhentilar and Zhentarim, many of whom were high-ranking officers, and all the [[orc]] troops fled the city.<ref name="RoZK-p-14-27">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|14-27}}</ref>

=== Manshoon Wars===
In the {{YearlinkName|1370}}, Fzoul and Lord [[Orgauth]] attacked and killed Manshoon. However, Manshoon lived on through clone bodies, many of whom fought and killed each other. Of these, one returned to the Zhentarim, conceded all leadership to Fzoul, and worked as an agent.<ref name="FRCS3e-p283">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|283}}</ref>

===Resurrection of Bane===
[[File:New_Zhentarim_Symbol.jpg|thumb|right|''Symbol of the Zhentarim under Fzoul ''circa'' 1372 DR.'']]
Bane was resurrected on [[Midwinter]] night in the {{YearlinkName|1372}}.<ref name="F&P">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|15}}</ref><ref name="FRCS">{{Cite book/FRCS|265}}</ref>

In the early morning hours of [[Nightal 16]], the {{YearlinkName|1374}}, a Zhentarim army led by Scyllua Darkhope overran [[Shadowdale]] from the north. They were aided by the church of [[Shar]] and [[House Dhuurniv]], who attacked the [[Twisted Tower]] from below. The [[Army of Myth Drannor]] was unable to respond as Zhentarim brigades to the east launched simultaneous attacks on several key elven fortifications.<ref name="S.TSotL-p4">{{Cite book/Shadowdale: The Scouring of the Land|4}}</ref><ref name="C.WAtT-p71">{{Cite dragon/Cormanthor: War Amidst the Trees|71}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|158}}</ref>

A Zhent named [[Karne]] attempted to take over the [[Onyx Tower]], but the plan ultimately failed.<ref name="BG-DA">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate: Dark Alliance}}</ref>

In the [[1383 DR|Year of the Vindicated Warrior, 1383 DR]], the [[Shadovar]] moved against Zhentil Keep during the [[Shadowbane War]], razing it along with the Citadel of the Raven, killing both Fzoul and the final clone of Manshoon, leaving only Darkhold left.<ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|150-151}}</ref><ref name="FRCG-p282">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|282}}</ref>

===15{{th}} century===
[[File:Zhentarim FRCG 4e.jpg|thumb|right|''Symbol ''circa'' 1479 DR.'']]
The Zhentarim was again taken over by the forces of Cyric, who engaged in battle with the forces of Bane. In the {{YearlinkName|1420}}, an evil mage named [[Rezlus]] took over the Banites and attempted to conquer Daggerdale. He failed and the Banites fell with only the forces of Cyric remaining.<ref name="D&D:D">{{Cite game/Dungeons and Dragons: Daggerdale}}</ref> 

Under the leadership of the Pereghost, the Zhentarim transitioned from a conquering force into a mercenary army that served others. As of [[1489 DR]], the Zhentarim were said to have greater numbers than ever before and had a reputation for service unlike any time in their history.<ref name="SCAG-p78" />

===Post–Second Sundering ===
[[File:5e Zhentarim badge.png|thumb|left|''Symbol ''circa'' 1490 DR.'']]
In the {{YearlinkName|1489}}, the Zhentarim sent an agent, the [[gnome]] assassin [[Jamna Gleamsilver]], to investigate the recent activities of the Cult of the Dragon,<ref name="HotDQ-p31">{{Cite book/Hoard of the Dragon Queen|31}}</ref> as they had been growing in power rather rapidly. They allied with several other factions, including those which would otherwise be enemies, to rise up against the Cult and [[Red Wizards of Thay|Red Wizards']] attempts to free the [[dragon]] god [[Tiamat]].<ref name="tRoT-p16">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|16}}</ref>

==Areas of Activity==
===The Moonsea===
*[[Zhentil Keep]]: This city-fortress was the main base of operation for the Zhentarim.<ref name="MotM p.123">{{Cite book/Mysteries of the Moonsea|123}}</ref>
*[[Citadel of the Raven]]: This isolated stronghold was a chain of interconnected fortresses straddling the [[Dragonspine Mountains]]. It was intended to stop any possible invasions from either [[The Ride]] or [[Thar]].<ref name="RoZK-CB-p36">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|36}}</ref> The Zhentarim stole it from the other Moonsea powers in 1355 DR,<ref name="CoV-p93" /> some fifteen years before Manshoon made it the stronghold from which he would pursue his arcane interests.<ref name="RoZK:CG-p30" />
*[[Melvaunt]]: Seemingly always at war with Zhentil Keep, Melvaunt was infiltrated by agents of the Zhentarim, in an attempt to take over their slave trade,<ref name="RoZK:CB-p45" /> including [[Lyran Nanther]] who was almost able to attain lordship of the city-state with the ''[[pendant of Ashaba]]''.<ref name="FRCS2eS-p7">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Shadowdale|7}}</ref>
*[[Mulmaster]]: This former enemy became an ally to the Zhentarim after the High Blade was secretly replaced by his twin brother in 1368 DR.<ref name="MotM-p94" />
*[[Phlan]]: Another long-time foe, the settlement was finally conquered in [[1380 DR]].<ref name="MotA-52">{{Cite dungeon/170/Monument of the Ancients|52}}</ref>
*[[Yûlash]]: The Zhentarim took this city in a long and bloody civil war, where they pitted their forces against those of [[Hillsfar]].<ref name="tGHotR-p140">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|140}}</ref><ref name="FRCS3e-p272">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|272}}</ref>

===Western Heartlands===
[[File:Zhentarim Bandit CLB.jpg|thumb|280px|''A halfling Zhent absconding with "liberated" treasure.'']]
*[[Darkhold]]: The castle-keep of Darkhold and the surrounding [[Far Hills]] were conquered by the Zhentarim in [[1312 DR]].<ref name="RoZK:CG-p35" /> The stronghold was the seat of power for the group in [[West Faerûn|the west]] and was ruled by the mage Sememmon until Bane's resurrection. It had strong ties to [[Church of Cyric|Cyricists]], as its two ruling lords were the enigmatic Pereghost<ref name="FRCS3e-p227" /> and the High Priest of the Black Sun, [[Dhamir Ercals]].<ref name="C&D-p37">{{Cite book/Cloak and Dagger|37}}</ref> In the following century, following the Spellplague, Darkhold would serve as headquarters for the restructured Zhentarim, led by the Pereghost.<ref name="SCAG-p78" />

===The Dalelands=== 
*[[Daggerdale]]: In 1336 DR, Zhentarim agents infiltrated [[Daggerdale]] and wrested control of [[Dagger Falls]] out of the rightful government's hands, and [[Randal Morn]] was forced to flee the city. The Zhentarim ruled the city for 33 years, though they were forced to regularly rebuff attacks from the [[Freedom Riders]] in a bloody civil war. Randal Morn and his guerrillas eventually reclaimed their lands in the [[battle for Dagger Falls]].<ref name="FRCS3e-p127">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|127}}</ref>
*[[Scardale]]: In a joint venture with Cormyr, Sembia, and the other Dalelands states, the Zhentarim sponsored a peacekeeping force in [[Scardale]] after [[Lashan Aumersair]] attempted to conquer the area during the [[second inter-dale war]].<ref name="FRCS3e-p118">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|118}}</ref> Support for the troops was withdrawn soon after they arrived in [[1357 DR]] though they remained in the Dale for at least another decade.<ref name="FRCS3e-p137">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|137}}</ref>
*[[Shadowdale]]: In the late 1330s DR, the Zhentarim managed to assassinate the lord of [[Shadowdale]], [[Aumry Obarskyr]],<ref name="FRCS2eS-p9">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Shadowdale|9}}</ref> and replace him with one of their agents, [[Jyordhan]], who ruled over the Dale for six years, until he was killed by [[Khelben Arunsun]].<ref name="tGHotR-p139" /> On [[Nightal 15]] [[1374 DR]], under the cover of darkness, an army of Zhentarim, led by [[Scyllua Darkhope]], invaded Shadowdale with assistance from the [[Church of Shar]] and [[House Dhuurniv]].<ref name="tGHotR-p158">{{cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|158}}</ref> It was only a few months before the citizens of Shadowdale, led by [[Azalar Falconhand]] and his [[fey]] allies, overthrew the occupiers.<ref name="S.TSotL-p115">{{Cite book/Shadowdale: The Scouring of the Land|115}}</ref>
*[[Teshendale]]: In [[1316 DR]], Zhentil Keep recruited a horde of [[orc]]s to overrun [[Teshendale]] and seized the city shortly after.<ref name="FRCS3e-p116" />

===Other Regions===
*[[Anauroch]]: The Zhentarim built the [[Black Road]] across Anauroch to allow trade with the North, including [[Waterdeep]]. They had control of a number of [[oases]] within the sands of the unforgiving desert, including [[Bhaerlith]],<ref name="RoZK:CB-p42" /> [[Hlaunga]],<ref name="RoZK:CB-p44">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|44}}</ref> [[Ma'atar]], [[Olomaa]]<ref name="RoZK:CB-p45" /> and [[Tel Badir]].<ref name="RoZK:CB-p47" /> By [[1479 DR]], the road was no longer in use.<ref name="FRPG-p100">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Player's Guide|100}}</ref>
*[[Chult]]: The Zhentarim forged a strong relationship with the [[Port Nyanzaru#Merchant Princes|merchant princes]] of [[Port Nyanzaru]], notably with [[Ifan Talro'a]] and [[Zhanthi]],<ref name="ToA-p25">{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|25,27}}</ref> and their mercenaries provided security for the city walls, mining and lumber activities, and even the princes' villas.<ref name="ToA-p25" /><ref name="ToA-p31">{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|31}}</ref> Their chief covert operative was one of the city's so-called "[[Port Nyanzaru/Old City#Government|beggar princes]]" known as [[Pock-Marked Po]].<ref name="DDEP01-p7">{{Cite adventurers league/Peril at the Port|7}}</ref> ''Circa'' [[1490 DR]], Zhent operatives had come to the region to hunt for [[Artus Cimber]].<ref name="ToA-p31" />
*[[Icewind Dale]]: In the late 1480s DR, a Zhentarim agent from Luskan named [[Naerth Maxildanarr]] became Speaker of [[Targos]] and thus gained a prominent position in the [[Ten-Towns]]. He was eager to secure a Zhentarim stranglehold on northern trade to and from Icewind Dale.<ref>{{Cite book/Icewind Dale: Rime of the Frostmaiden|86}}</ref>
*[[Savage Frontier]]: In the [[Delimbiyr Vale]], the town of [[Llorkh]]<ref name="WatN-p7">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|7}}</ref> and the village of [[Orlbar]]<ref name="N:GtSF-p62">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/Cities & Civilization|62}}</ref> were taken over by the Zhentarim. These settlements marked the terminus of the Black Road route and the gateway for Zhent trade in the North.<ref name="FRCS2e-Map">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition/Map}}</ref>
*[[Sword Coast]]: Through Karne, the Zhentarim were a shadowy organization that existed in [[Baldur's Gate]] and its surrounding areas. The [[Marsh of Chelimber]] was conquered by the Zhentarim through Karne and his [[lizardfolk]] servant, [[Sleyvas]].<ref name="BG-DA" />

==Terminology==
*''Zhent'': A person from Zhentil Keep. Not all Zhents were members of the Zhentarim.<ref name="FRA-p73">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|73}}</ref>
*''Zhentarim'': The greater organization itself; members of the organization, either singular or plural.<ref name="FRA-p73" />
*''Zhentilar'': The military of Zhentil Keep, used to distinguish them from members of the Zhentarim.<ref name="FRA-p73" /><ref name="FRA-p121">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|121}}</ref>
*''Zhentish'': An adjective for people and things of Zhentil Keep.<ref name="FRA-p73" />

'''Manshoon''' ({{Pronounce|man|SHOON|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-DM-p25-26" /><ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was the founder of the [[Zhentarim]],<ref name="FRCS-p282">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|282}}</ref> also known as the Black Network, until he was "slain" by [[Fzoul Chembryl]], his ally, and Lord [[Orgauth]] in [[1370 DR]]. However, it transpired that Manshoon had created a dozen or more [[clone]]s that the murderous duo then found themselves facing. Only three of these clones were known to have survived into the late 1370s [[DR]] and once more headed the evil organization known as the Zhentarim.<ref name="FRCS-p283">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|283}}</ref>

==Personality==
Completely fitting the archetype of the sly-tongued, sinister, and decadent master spellcaster, Manshoon ruthlessly crushed revolts within his own ranks of the Black Network, easily slaughtered his rivals and weeded the weak out the ranks of the young and ambitious by sending upstarts on nigh-impossible suicide missions. Hated and mistrusted by Fzoul, Manshoon manipulated him much as he did all others—remaining arrogant, untouchable in his power, and faintly amused by it all.<ref name="FRCS-p283" />

==Relations==
He had contacts with many creatures, mainly [[beholder]]s, such as [[Xarlandralath]] and [[Xualahuu]], some of whom held much respect and loyalty to him. For example, Xualahuu was deeply loyal to him and tried to advance the Black Network's plots.<ref name="A-p52">{{Cite book/Anauroch|52}}</ref>

==History==
===Early Life===
Manshoon was born in Zhentil Keep in the {{YearlinkName|1229}},<ref name="GHotR-p125">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|125}}</ref> to [[Harlshoon]], the first lord of Zhentil Keep. Manshoon, his brother [[Asmuth]]; and [[Chess]], the son of their father's chief rival, [[Calkontor]], were sent off together as [[adventurer]]s to prove themselves worthy of being lords like their fathers. While his sons were away, Harlshoon was poisoned by Calkontor, but, with his dying breaths, he threw Calkontor out of a tower window. The trio's fathers' places were usurped by a pair of opportunists, [[Theilon Greencloak]] and [[Ulsan Baneservant]], the deaths blamed on foreign assassins, while the unknowing lord-princes continued their adventures together.<ref name="RoZK-CB-p13">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|13-14}}</ref>

One such adventure was a quest to take a magical sword from a barrow, a goal shared by a trio of female [[Harpers]]. Asmuth was blinded and Manshoon had one of his hands blown off when he carelessly grabbed for the sword. Chess saved both of their lives, and the three fled when the sword animated itself, appearing to attack the Harpers. They came across a party of other adventurers and murdered them all in their sleep. Manshoon recovered a [[wand]] and a [[spellbook]]. He traded the wand for his hand to be restored but kept the spellbook as he secretly planned to study [[the Art]], having been inspired by the magic of the sword. After a few more adventures they headed home, learning about their slain sires and their usurped positions in an inn somewhere in the [[Dalelands]]. Manshoon made sure his blind brother Asmuth fell off a bridge to his death before they returned to the keep where he and Chess planned to dispose of the usurpers.<ref name="RoZK-CB-p13" />

Meanwhile, Theilon Greencloak had gone to fight some mages in [[Thar]] and died in the conflict while Ulsan Baneservant remained in the keep. Manshoon and Chess were attacked on arrival by [[assassin]]s acting on Ulsan's orders but were saved by Manshoon's magic. However, unknown to him, Chess defected in exchange for his father's seat on the council. It was, however a double cross: Chess, Manshoon, and their childhood friend Fzoul Chembryl killed the usurper. They claimed their rightful council seats and supported Fzoul's rise through the ranks of the Banite priesthood.<ref name="RoZK-CB-p13" />

===Building the Zhentarim===
Feeling exposed, Manshoon immediately set about creating a cabal of mages, his [[Black Cloaks]], to protect him, recruiting Fzoul early on. Not long afterward, in the {{YearlinkName|1261}}, he founded the Zhentarim. However, knowing the priests would obey Bane over anyone else and assuming the wizards were just as ambitious as he was, Manshoon held little trust for any of his new allies. He sent out spies and was determined to raise his powers in the arcane far above anyone else in Zhentil Keep. Some of his spies returned from [[Teshendale]] talking of a flying hollow rock. Manshoon investigated personally, hoping to find powerful magic. Instead, he discovered the [[beholder]] [[Xantriph]]. The two canny individuals joined forces, claiming the rock was a conduit to the gods. Banite priests flocked to the place where Xantriph instructed them all to do as Manshoon commanded,<ref name="RoZK-CB-p14-15">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|14-15}}</ref> joining the Zhentarim and leading them away from the decadent [[High Imperceptor of Bane|High Imperceptor]] of the church. However, in order to do this, Manshoon and his magelings had to pledge fealty to Bane, promising to lead the faith to even greater heights of power than the weakening High Imperceptor had. Furthermore, Manshoon promised control of the priesthood and Zhentil Keep to the beholders, or so it seemed.<ref name="EGpEFG-p71-72">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|71-72}}</ref>

In secret, Manshoon embarked on another plan for his organization. Having little interest in empire-building, he set his Zhentarim wizards the task of establishing trade routes between the Moonsea and the untapped markets of the [[Sword Coast]]. This allowed Manshoon to further dream of advancing his skill in the Art while manipulating both the beholders and the church of Bane from behind the scenes. During this period, Manshoon developed his ultimate insurance plan, the notorious ''[[stasis clone]]'' spell.<ref name="EGpEFG-p71-72" />

===The Clones and the Manshoon Wars===
Over the years Manshoon died many times, always coming back in a new body in half a day. [[Elminster]] claimed he had personally killed twenty-and-one Manshoon clones by the {{YearlinkName|1357}}. That was the year when Manshoon's next death occurred, during his hunt for [[Shandril Shessair]]'s [[spellfire]]. Severely weakened by Elminster and [[Storm Silverhand]], his ''[[contingency]] ''spell whisked him away to the last place he'd been teleported from—right in front of Shandril, who instantly obliterated him in revenge for the death of [[Delg Ironstar]].<ref name="CoF-ch12">{{Cite digital book/Crown of Fire||12}}</ref> The Black Network and [[Church of Bane]] widely believed that this was the last clone he had, until Manshoon eventually returned … again.<ref name="CoF-ep">{{Cite digital book/Crown of Fire||21 (Epilogue)}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|1370}}, Manshoon was "slain" again by Fzoul and Lord Orgauth, who then found themselves facing around a dozen clones that the wizard had created. Each of the clones was convinced that he was the real Manshoon and was inexplicably compelled to slay his rival clones. Most of these perished in the ensuing battles. The caches of magical items formerly hidden by Manshoon were scattered throughout the Realms.<ref name="FRCS-p283" /> These conflicts were known as the [[Manshoon Wars]].<ref name="C&D-p13">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|13}}</ref>

[[File:LaeralKyrianiManshoonWars.jpg|thumb|right|200px|''[[Laeral]] and [[Kyriani Agrivar]] witness the Manshoon Wars on top of [[Piergeiron's Palace]] in [[Waterdeep]]''.]]
Three clones, who mastered this killing compulsion by means of spells and devices, survived, with the one that returned to the Zhentarim appearing to be 'the' Manshoon.<ref name="FRCS-p283" />

One of the three remaining clones returned to the Zhentarim, conceding leadership to Fzoul and entering into some sort of accommodation with him.<ref name="FRCS-p282" /><ref name="FRCS-p283" /> He served as a member-at-large, a free agent with the power and authority to direct Zhentarim assets as he saw fit.<ref name="FRCS-p282" /> Used to ruling and manipulating from behind the scenes and relieved of responsibility of administering to the Black Network, the Zhentarim Manshoon seemed to have learned a lesson in caution and patience. By [[1372 DR]], he had redoubled his arcane research, seeking ways to transfer his consciousness from one clone to another at will.<ref name="FRCS-p283" />

The second clone assumed a position of power among the [[Night Masks]] of [[Westgate]], calling himself '''Orbakh'''<ref name="FRCS-p283" /> or '''Orlak II'''. This Manshoon awoke at the beginning of the [[Manshoon Wars]] only to find that he had been turned into a [[vampire]] and was not in the stasis chamber with the rest of the Manshoon clones (or the stash of magic items he had left for himself in the event of his death). He hunted down the vampire who had turned him, the Night King [[Orlak]], and slew him, taking the vampire lord's magic items and his title of Night King. Whilst venturing up to the surface to feed, he found himself in Westgate where the magic items influenced him to create some vampire underlings and, with them, he decided to take over and revitalize the Night Masks thieves' guild. Orbakh quickly took over the Night Masks as he arrived in the midst of a power vacuum left by the death of the guild's former leader. He restructured the guild to his tastes and planned to turn Westgate into the capital city of a vampire kingdom by wedding the last in the line of the defunct Westgate royalty.<ref name="C&D-p48-50">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|48-50}}</ref> This plot never saw fruition, however, because of the intervention of the [[Spellplague]].{{Speculation}}

The third took refuge in [[Undermountain]] with [[Halaster]].<ref name="FRCS-p283" />

Although most believed the Manshoon Wars persisted only a few short, chaotic years, they were actually waged over the course of at least a century. The surviving clones became increasingly entrenched, hoping to outlast their foes.<ref name="EGpEFG-p71-72" />

===Post-Spellplague===
When [[Returned Netheril]] learned of the Zhentarim's alliance with the [[phaerimm]], the [[shadovar]] destroyed Zhentil Keep,<ref name="FRCG-p282">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|282}}</ref> killing the Zhentarim Manshoon.<ref name="FRCG-p284-285" /> The one in Undermountain appeared to have died during the events of the [[Spellplague]] itself.<ref name="FRCG-p284-285" /> Now only the vampire Manshoon known as Orbakh remained.<ref name="FRCG-p284-285" /> He was now the [[Night King]], leader of the Night Masks and a powerful vampire. The [[Fire Knives]] though, purged Westgate of vampires.<ref name="FRCG-p284-285" />

Retaking his original name, the last Manshoon clone relocated to the haunted castle of [[Stormwatch]], and rebuilt the forces of the Zhentarim, adding his own undead thralls to those few living Zhents that remained.<ref name="FRCG-p284-285" /> Stormwatch and [[Darkhold]] were the main bases of these forces, but not having the resources to rebuild the organization to its former glory, the Manshoon's new Zhentarim was now merely a mercenary company, bolstered by priests of Cyric from Darkhold.<ref name="FRCG-p282" />

Decades after the collapse of the [[Weave]] and the devastating effects of the [[Spellplague]], the vampiric Manshoon began plotting to take rulership of [[Cormyr]]. To this end, he slowly began collecting information on items rumored to contain the spirits of [[the Nine]]. Known as [[blueflame ghost]]s, combining their power was rumored to be strong enough to breach the wards protecting the royal palace of [[Suzail]]. He also began to use his powerful mental techniques on the lowly and powerful alike, enslaving some, making others mind-thralls to be used or discarded as needed (without the person being any the wiser), and spying on the nobility and palace staff.<ref name="EMD">{{Cite book/Elminster Must Die/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

Manshoon also began to slowly whittle away at Elminster's defenses, killing an ally of the Old Sage's or removing one of the many contingency spells still tied to him whenever he felt like having some fun. Finally, in the {{YearlinkName|1479}}, he took revenge upon Elminster for the foiling of so many plans and the destruction of so many clones. Manshoon, using the body of a [[beholder]] held in mind-thrall, unleashed magics strong enough to reduce Elminster to dust. He departed, however, before discovering that Elminster survived as a pile of magical ash.<ref name="EMD" />

Manshoon continued to plot his overthrow of the [[Obarskyr]]s. He followed Elminster to the [[Simbul]]'s cave in [[Shadowdale]] with the intent of killing Elminster. However, Elminster and [[Storm Silverhand]] were able to restore the Simbul's sanity, and she forced Manshoon to yield and promise to work with Elminster to recover [[blueflame item]]s. After the Simbul [[teleport]]ed away, Manshoon once again blasted Elminster's human form to ashes but couldn't destroy him.<ref name="BED">{{Cite book/Bury Elminster Deep/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

Later, after Mystra's return around [[1480 DR]], Elminster regained his original body and destroyed the vampire Manshoon for good. However, Elminster knew that Manshoon was ready to awaken a new clone.<ref name="EE">{{Cite book/Elminster Enraged/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

===Post-Second Sundering===
Although intending to continue where his former clone left off, the new Manshoon, a vampire again, continuously found himself thwarted, from even attempting to begin his schemes, by the newly returned [[Mirt the Moneylender]], though the two of them regularly met for drinks and passive-aggressive conversations in [[Suzail]], Cormyr. When the events in [[Myth Drannor]] led to a disturbance in the Weave in the {{YearlinkName|1487}}, the vampire's guard dropped momentarily. With Mirt's help, he was arrested by [[War Wizards]] in [[Memories of Queen Fee]].<ref name="TH-19">{{Cite book/The Herald/MMP (2014)|?|19}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|1488}}, Manshoon, now no longer interested in direct confrontations with the [[Sage of Shadowdale]], was caught alongside several other [[archwizard]]s inside a [[spellstorm]] in Cormyr for the purpose of making a bid for the legendary [[Lost Spell]]. However, aside from an "alliance" made with [[Malchor Harpell]] (at the indirect request of Elminster and [[Mystra (Midnight)|Mystra]]), Manshoon eventually left empty-handed.<ref>{{Cite book/Spellstorm/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

By [[1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} the Undermountain Manshoon had resurfaced in Waterdeep, having survived the Spellplague and his clash with Halaster Blackcloak. During the latter battle, Manshoon's left arm was severed and proved impossible to magically regenerate, forcing him to use a metal prosthetic instead, while the original was kept and reanimated by Halaster.<ref>{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dungeon of the Mad Mage|119}}</ref> The ancient archwizard had infiltrated Waterdeep years before and had been hiding out in [[Kolat Towers]] ever since, plotting to replace the [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|City Watch]] with Black Network mercenaries that were loyal to him alone and to rule Waterdeep as Wizard-King.<ref>{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|208}}</ref>

'''Zhentil Keep''' ({{Pronounce|z|HENT|ill|_|keep|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-CR-p93">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Set/Cyclopedia of the Realms|93}}</ref>}}), also called '''Thargate Keep''',<ref name="RoZK.CB-p7" /> was once the most powerful city in the [[Moonsea]] region as well as the main base of operation for the [[Zhentarim]].<ref name="MotM p.123">{{Cite book/Mysteries of the Moonsea|123}}</ref> Along with the [[Citadel of the Raven]], Zhentil Keep was destroyed by the [[Shadovar]] in [[1383 DR]] during the [[Shadowbane War]].<ref>{{Cite dungeon/170/Monument of the Ancients|65}}</ref><ref name="FRCS4-p282">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|282}}</ref>

==History==
{{main|History of Zhentil Keep}}

===Early History===
Zhentil Keep began as a small trading camp located on the north side of the [[Moonsea]] around [[640 DR]].<ref>{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|93}}</ref>

In [[747 DR]], a [[Selgaunt|Chancelgauntian]] ship owner named [[Orlephar Flostren]], began hiring workers and mercenaries to construct fortifications and patrol the surrounding areas. By the next spring, a group of 12 Sembian merchants purchased the hold and began a major project of expansion. It was during this period that a large keep was raised in the northwestern quarter. <ref name="RoZK-p8">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|8}}</ref>

Following its completion, a merchant by the name of [[Elephstron]] quickly moved into the keep, titling himself "Lord of the Keep." In response to the objections of the other Sembian merchants, Elephstron proposed each be named a "Lord of the City," with the ability to collect taxes, enact laws, and establish militias for carrying out orders on their behalf. The eleven merchants agreed and the hold experienced a period of rapid growth as an increasing number of merchants moved in to buy and rent land.<ref name="RoZK-p8" />

Despite the measures taken by the Lords, monster attacks on caravans and nearby trade routes became more common and infighting between the merchants was growing increasingly dangerous. With the stability of the hold at stake, Elephstron sought out a [[wizard]] to aid him in combating these threats. In [[751 DR]], Elphestron met a wizard by the name of [[Zhentar]] in the city of [[Westgate]].<ref name="GHoTR-p101" /> Zhentar accepted Elephstron's request on the condition that the twelve Lords create a formal governing council and give him a seat with equal status in this new council. The twelve lords agreed to his terms and Zhentar relocated to the Hold as a Lord of the City.<ref name="RoZK-p8" />

The '''Lords' Alliance''', also known as the '''Council of Lords''', was a partnership of merchant cities founded in the early 14{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="LEoF-p141">{{Cite book/Lost Empires of Faerûn|141}}</ref><ref name="GHotR-p137">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|137}}</ref> Its members were from the [[Sword Coast]], [[the North]] and [[Western Heartlands]],<ref name="OOtA-p128">{{Cite book/Out of the Abyss|128}}</ref> including [[Waterdeep]], [[Silverymoon]], [[Baldur's Gate]], and [[Neverwinter]], as well as other free cities and towns in the region, which made up the bulk of the organization.<ref name="RoT-pp13-14" /> It was formed to oppose the growing influence of the [[Zhentarim|Black Network]] in the North, the [[Shadow Thieves of Amn]],<ref name="SF-p9" /><ref name="FRCS3e-pp275-276" /><ref name="CoV-p102">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|102}}</ref> rampaging hordes of [[orc]]s, and [[Northlander]] raiders.<ref name="SCAG-p43">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|43}}</ref>

{{Quote|For a century and a half, and more, the Lords' Alliance has stood as the most important and influential group in the North. Its power has kept towns safe from the predations of larger powers, has kept the ambitions of Luskan in check, and has taught the rulers of many cities that it is better to cooperate, even for a time, then to merely shut one's doors and allow the storms to rage outside.|[[Andew Cururen]], a Lords' Alliance agent of note.<ref name="SCAG-p43" />}}

==Organization==
Depending on who was asked, the Lords' Alliance was considered either a strong coalition,<ref name="OOtA-p128" /> or unsteady pact of political powers. While the Alliance was said by some to be a collective of aggressive and self-serving figureheads,<ref name="PotA-p15-16">{{Cite book/Princes of the Apocalypse|15-16}}</ref><ref name="SKT-p12">{{Cite book/Storm King's Thunder|12}}</ref> others insisted they were among the most secure governing bodies in the west.<ref name="OOtA-p128" /> The truth, as it usually was, lay somewhere between the two extremes. While the members often held opposing views and held interests that conflicted with their peers, these rivalries were set aside when true danger emerged to threaten their collective safety and prosperity.<ref name="RoT-pp13-14">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|13-14}}</ref> The true strength of the alliance, it was said, manifested when its members banded together on collaborative endeavors.<ref name="OOtA-p128" /> 

While some of these cities and city-states held more influence than others,<ref name="RoT-pp13-14" /> they aligned their goals for the greater good and prosperity of the whole alliance.<ref name="OOtA-p128" /> Collectively they believed in the preservation of civilization for the better of their people.<ref name="SCAG-p43" />

The Lords' Alliance had the following ranks from lowest to highest: Cloak, Redknife, Stingblade, Warduke, and Lioncrown.<ref name="DMG-2014-p22">{{Cite book/Dungeon Master's Guide 5th edition|22}}</ref>

The alliance's emblem was a golden crown set over a field of red.<ref name="KGV.P13">{{Cite book/Keys from the Golden Vault/Prisoner 13|?}}</ref>

The Rundeen had a relatively loose and informal hierarchy, but had a clear, layered structure.<ref name="C&D-p129">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|129}}</ref><ref name="EotSS-p181">{{Cite book/Empires of the Shining Sea|181}}</ref> These layers operated independently and even unaware of the others, with only the leadership grasping the full picture of the organization.<ref name="LoD-p170" /><ref name="LoD-p4">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|4}}</ref>

They were the secret inner circle of the organization, never identified nor directly observed and directing affairs covertly. The Yrshelem were in turn divided into two tiers;<ref name="EotSS-p181" /><ref name="C&D-p129" /> there were the five Grand Yrshelem and under them were the fifteen First Yrshelem.<ref name="EotSS-p181" /><ref name="C&D-p129" /><ref name="LoD-p170" /> Overall, the Yrshelem operated as an oligarchy.<ref name="EotSS-p181" /> The members of the Grand Yrshelem each had an area of specialty and authority, controlling a particular aspect of Rundeen business<ref name="EotSS-p181" /><ref name="C&D-p129" /><ref name="LoD-p170" />—caravans, raw goods, money-changing and loans, shipping, and slavery—but evenly divided both the responsibilities and the profits, aiding one another when it came necessary. ==Etymology==
The word "Faerûn" was a modified version of "Faerie", the name of the homeland of ancient [[elves]]. The term was coined by [[Durothil (prince)|Durothil]], prince of [[Tintageer]], [[Faerie]].<ref name="e:ioe-p97">{{Cite book/Evermeet: Island of Elves/MMP|97}}</ref>

==Geography==
{{main|Portal:Geography}}
The continent included terrain that was as varied as any other. Besides the exterior coastline to the west and south, the most dominant feature on the continent was the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]]. This was an irregular inland sea that kept the interior lands fertile, connected the west and east regions of Faerûn and served as a major trade route for many of the bordering nations.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /><ref name="FRCS-p142">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|142}}</ref>

Next in significance was the [[Shaar]], a broad region of grasslands in the south that,<ref name="FRCS-p196">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|196}}</ref> together with the Lake of Steam, separated the area around the inland sea from the coastal nations at the southern edge of the continent. To the east, Faerûn was bordered by a vast region of steppes from [[Kara-Tur]],<ref name="FRCS-p230">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|230}}</ref> and in the north were massive glaciers ([[Pelvuria]] and [[Reghed]]) and tundra.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /> South of the continent, separated by the [[Great Sea]], was the sub-tropical land of [[Zakhara]].<ref name="FRCS-p230"/>

The continent included the following geographic regions.<ref group="note">The following division of Faerûn by compass directions was developed for organizational purposes on the Forgotten Realms Wiki, and is not closely reflected by descriptions in source text. In particular, terms like "the North" and "the South" in the sources can be much more wide-ranging in scope, and as ambiguous, than what is defined here.</ref>

'''Purfbin Doogrick''' was a young [[gnome]] [[rogue]] companion of the [[Hero of Daggerford]] who rescued Great Duke [[Pwyll Daggerford]] from the [[Zhentarim]] abduction in [[1372 DR]].<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

{{quote|''My nose tells me there is "opportunity" in this cellar. Granda [[Retsotnert Doogrick|Retsotnert]] had a keen nose too. Bigger and hairier though.''|Purfbin Doogrick<ref name="NWN-DOD" />}}

==Description==
Purfbin was a portly young gnome with shaggy curly blond hair and black, slightly bulging eyes. One of his front teeth was noticeably chipped. Purfbin wore brightly colored jacket of many pockets, each overstuffed with valuables and gadgets.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

==Personality==
Purfbin Doogrick smiled often and possessed a mischievous streak. [[Trulit & Greegon's claret]] was one of Purfbin's favorite libations, he could detect its smell from a distance even if it was sealed in waxed casks.<ref name="NWN-DOD" /> Like many of his fellow gnomes, Purfbin loved [[cheese]], in moderation, of course, and hated feeling even slightest hint hunger.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

However cheerful and eccentric the gnome appeared to be, he missed his home and family greatly, often tearing up when talking bout the Doogrick clan burrows.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

His main motivator in life was avarice. He loved [[gold]], [[silver]], and had especial weakness for [[gem]]s.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

==Abilities==
Purfbin Doogrick considered himself to be a "professional treasure hunter", and not a thug or a thief. He honed his roguish skills in acquiring and recovering rare artifacts. That profession often brought him to antagonize with other thieves by stealing from them said powerful artifacts.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

Purfbin always tinkered with his weapons and armor, often actually making useful alterations.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

==Possessions==
''[[Purfbin's clothes]]'' were far from fashionable and were eccentrically dyed, but enchanted his roguish skills. ''[[Purfbin's crossbow]]'' was a powerful invention that could shoot a seemingly unlimited number of ''[[bolt of lightning|bolts of lightning]]'' along with regular [[crossbow]] ammunition. He also had a ''[[potion of healing|potion of cure light wounds]]'' handy.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

==Relationships==
Purfbin often said that the home was wherever he found a safe place to sleep, however he missed his mode and family greatly. The Doogrick clan burrows were located somewhere to the south of Daggerford. The family was typical for gnomish standards, however Purfbin's favorite family member was Retsotnert Doogrick, a famous and quite an eccentric inventor.<ref name="NWN-DOD" />

==Origins==
The most popular and widely accepted theory about the origins of the Spellplague was that this calamity was caused by the death of Mystra, the [[god]]dess of [[Magic]]. For eons, magic had been bound within Mystra's [[Weave]], the universal structure of arcane forces, and her death tore the Weave apart and unleashed the [[raw magic]] contained within it, creating the cataclysm that was later known as the Spellplague.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/><ref name="NV">{{Cite game/Neverwinter}}</ref>

Other scholars, however, didn't consider this to be a logical explanation. Historically, two earlier incarnations of Mystra had died before only to be reborn, and the Weave maintained its hold over the power of magic for the duration of her deaths. In these scholars' views, the truth would never be fully known to mortals, and perhaps not even to the [[god]]s.<ref name="NV"/><ref name="PoS-p79">{{Cite book/Plague of Spells|79}}</ref>

Around [[1396 DR]], the sentient [[construct]] [[Cynosure (construct)|Cynosure]] theorized that the escape of an entity named the [[Carnis|Traitor]] from its confinement in the dungeons of [[Stardeep]], around the same time Mystra was murdered, may have also influenced the Spellplague's virulent onslaught.<ref name="PoS-p79"/>

Around [[1479 DR]], scholars and sages who studied the [[Neverwinter/Chasm|Chasm]] of [[Neverwinter]], such as [[Rhazzad]], believed the [[aboleth]]s of the [[Abolethic Sovereignty]] may have been the actual creators of the Spellplague, although there were no definite proof.<ref name="NV"/>

The '''Time of Troubles''' was a cataclysmic time period in the chronology of [[Faerûn]]. Taking place during the {{YearlinkName|1358}},<ref name="FRCS-p271">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|271}}</ref> the Time of Troubles was a period during which the [[deities]] of [[Faerûn]] were forced to walk the earth in their mortal [[avatar]] forms. Several major deities died during the Time of Troubles (see [[#Deaths, Ascensions, and Resurrections|Deaths, Ascensions, and Resurrections]]) and a handful of mortals rose to divinity.<ref name="FRCS-p264">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|264}}</ref>

==Etymology==
Other names that this cataclysmic period was known by included '''the Arrival''',<ref name="FRCS-p271" /> the '''Avatar Crisis''', the '''Godswar''',<ref name="FRCS-p271" /><ref name="P&P-p3">{{Cite book/Powers & Pantheons|3}}</ref><ref name="F&A-p23">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|23}}</ref> the '''Time of the Avatars''', the '''Fall of the Gods''', and the '''Intervention of Ao'''.<ref name="P&P-p3" />

==Summary==
The Time of Troubles was precipitated by an attempt by the gods [[Bane]] and [[Myrkul]] to steal the [[Tablets of Fate]] from the overdeity [[Ao]]. Angry at the gods for their habitual pursuit of power and negligence toward their mortal faithful,<ref name="FRCS-p264"/> Lord Ao relegated every god (except for the guardian god [[Helm]], who was selected to protect the gates to the heavens<ref name="SD-p1">{{Cite book/Shadowdale/MMP (1989)|1-8|0}}</ref>) to walk among their followers on Toril.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/> This included the gods of [[pantheon]]s far removed from Faerûn, such as the [[Kara-Turan pantheon]] and [[Maztican pantheon]].<ref name="PBF">{{Cite web/theedverse/2018-10-13 - Time of Troubles - Pantheons Beyond Faerûn}}</ref>

The immediate effects of this edict were threefold. First, [[divine magic]] (spells granted to [[cleric]]s by their [[deity|patron deities]]) ceased to function altogether<ref name="Siege">{{Cite book/Siege of Darkness}}</ref> unless the cleric was within {{SI|1|mile|k|long=yes}} of their deity's avatar. Second, [[Magic|arcane magic]] (a force channeled from the [[Weave]] by [[wizard]]s and [[sorcerer]]s) ceased to be regulated by its steward, [[Mystra]], and became dangerously unpredictable.<ref name="FRCS-p264"/> Third, the characteristically immortal and aloof deities were now vulnerable (though still devastatingly powerful) and dwelling among the civilizations of Faerûn.<ref name="FRCS-p265">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|265}}</ref>

Machior had great influence over [[Dag Zoreth]] and [[Gareth Cormaeril]] thanks to a combination of blackmail and charisma.<ref name="CS.W-p38" />

==History==
Malchior was a [[Zhent]] of [[Darkhold]] sent to Waterdeep. By 1372 DR, he rarely went back to his home, where the [[Church of Bane]] had become more and more dominant.<ref name="CS.W-p38" />

'''Dag Zoreth''', born '''Brandon Caradoon''', was a [[cleric]] and [[strifeleader]] of [[Cyric]] at [[Darkhold]] in the mid-to-late 14{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="Th">{{Cite book/Thornhold}}</ref><ref name="Dr251">{{Cite dragon/251/Rogues Gallery: Thornhold-The Harpers at Twilight|86,88}}</ref><ref name="DDGttUd-p48">{{Cite book/Drizzt Do'Urden's Guide to the Underdark|48}}</ref><ref name="CS.W-p74">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|74}}</ref>

==History==
Dag was captured by the [[Zhentarim]] when he was a young boy and raised into the ways of evil, despite the fact that he was the son of a [[paladin]].<ref name="Th" /><ref name="Dr251" /> Eventually, Dag Zoreth would mastermind the attack on his father's keep and murder him in a sacrifice to the Dark Sun.<ref name="Th" />

At the head of a force of Zhentarim soldiers in the {{YearlinkName|1368}}, Dag Zoreth marched through the [[Clan Stoneshaft]] tunnels in the [[Sword Mountains]] to reach [[Thornhold]] on the [[Sword Coast North]]. On the way, they killed or enslaved many [[dwarves]], though Bronwyn Caradoon and [[Ebenezer Stoneshaft]] would later retake Thornhold.<ref name="Th" /><ref name="DDGttUd-p48" /> Thanks to the corrupt Sir Gareth of the [[Knights of Samular]], Dag Zoreth came close to possessing both ''[[Kezefbane]]'' and the ''[[rings of Samular]]'',<ref name="Th" /><ref name="CS.W-p49" /> only to be foiled by Bronwyn.<ref name="Th" /><ref name="Dr251" />

==Relations==
He was the estranged brother of [[Bronwyn Caradoon]].<ref name="Th" /><ref name="CS.W-p74" />

Dag Zoreth had a [[half-elven]] child with the [[elf]] [[Ashemmi]],<ref name="Dr251" /> named [[Cara]]. She was being raised by Bronwyn ''circa'' [[1372 DR]].<ref name="CS.W-p74"/>

[[Malchior]], a fellow Darkhold cleric of Cyric based in [[Waterdeep]], had great influence over Dag's decisions there, thanks to a combination of blackmail, subtle deceit, and charisma.<ref name="CS.W-p38">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|38}}</ref> Dag and Malchior were the only ones aware of Sir [[Gareth Cormaeril]]'s corruption and conversion to Cyric.<ref name="CS.W-p49">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|49}}</ref>

==Abilities==
As a cleric, he commanded the [[Trickery domain]].<ref name="CS.W-p74" />

A '''beholder''', sometimes called a '''sphere of many eyes''' or an '''eye tyrant''', was a large [[aberration]] normally found in the [[Underdark]]. These large, orb-shaped beings had ten eyestalks and one central eye, each containing powerful [[magic]]. Powerful and intelligent, beholders were among the greatest threats to the world.<ref name="MM3e-p24">{{Cite book/Monster Manual 3rd edition|24-25}}</ref>

{{Quote|Think ye weave cunning schemes and elaborate intrigues with fallback plans and positions? Beholders change, refine, discard, and spin anew scores of such plans all the time. To the average beholder, [[human]] intrigues are the fumbling of babies.|[[Elminster]]'s notes in ''[[Volo's Guide to Monsters (in-universe)|Volo's Guide to Monsters]]''.<ref name="VGtM-p6">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Monsters|6}}</ref>}}

==Description==
Beholders were immediately identifiable, being essentially a floating head with one single, [[cyclops]]-like eye surrounded by ten smaller eye stalks. Other than this, the main feature of a beholder's anatomy was its massive, gaping maw. Because of these features, beholders were occasionally known as "spheres of many eyes" or "eye tyrants,"<ref name="MM3e-p24" /> although the latter also referred to a specific type of beholder.<ref name="MM4e-p32">{{Cite book/Monster Manual 4th edition|32}}</ref>

Beholders of the Realms tended to be slightly larger than beholders found on other worlds, growing up to six feet in diameter where on other worlds they would average five feet wide. The majority of beholders living on Faerûn had skin colored in cool colors—purples and blues—on the top of their bodies that graduated into earth tones further down. Said skin had a pebbly texture. Most of Toril's beholders had nostrils and jointed, articulated eyestalks.<ref>{{Cite book/I, Tyrant|66}}</ref>

Because their entire body was covered in eyes, beholders had the capacity to see in all directions at once, making it nearly impossible to ambush them while also giving them an unusually high degree of perceptive ability. Although beholders lacked the capacity to see color, they had the ability to perceive even in the most darkened environment, under conditions in which a [[human]] or similar creature would be rendered completely blind.<ref name="MM3e-p24" />

Beholders were also capable of flight, in spite of their lack of wings or similar physical features, simply hovering above ground effortlessly. The effects of this flight resembled those of the [[wizard]] [[spell]] ''[[levitate|levitation]]''.<ref name="MM3e-p24" />

'''Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun''' ({{Pronounce|KEL|ben|_|AIR|un|sun|refs=<ref name="DMSotR-24" />}}) was the Lord Mage of [[Waterdeep]], a masked [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]], and powerful archmage.<ref name="ELH-pp299-300" /> He resided in [[Blackstaff Tower]] until his death.<ref name="CoWTM">{{Cite book/The City of Waterdeep Trail Map}}</ref> One of the few spellcasters to become one of [[Mystra's Chosen]],<ref name="B-p7-9">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|7-9|0}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p88">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|88}}</ref> and the last survivor of House [[Maerdrym]],<ref name="B-p62">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|62|6}}</ref> Khelben was a serious, imposing wizard and a grim proponent of law and order.<ref name="FRCS3e-274" />

==Description==
Khelben stood just over 6 feet (1.83 meters) tall and was well-muscled. He usually dressed in robes, wielding his trademark [[Blackstaff (staff)|Blackstaff]]. As of 1374 DR, he had a full beard, black hair with silver streaks, including a prominent one through his beard, and a distinguished, imposing manner.<ref name="B-p25">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|25|2}}</ref> He often dressed in black with a silver torque and a cape lined in gray fur.<ref name="ESh-p52-53">{{Cite book/Elfshadow/Paperback 2000|52-53}}</ref>

==Personality==
Often thought to be humorless and grave, Khelben was actually neither. He simply found it easier to deal with people if they were intimidated by or even frightened of him. He had numerous apprentices over the years and liked to encourage young people who displayed an aptitude for magic. Wielding [[magic|the Art]] could be a gravely dangerous business, however, and Khelben tried to instill an appreciation of that fact in his pupils by keeping up his "stern taskmaster" façade.<ref name=":1">{{Cite book

=== LINKED WIKI PAGE (first 5000 chars): Marking the years

{{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

The '''Moonstars''', also known as the '''Tel'Teukiira''', the '''Silverstars''', or the '''Twelvestars''', were a highly secretive group of individuals formed by [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] in the late 14{{th}} century [[DR]] with the aim of unifying the [[human]]s and other races (especially [[elves]]) against the forces of evil.<ref name="C&G-p25">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|25}}</ref><ref name="Cos.W-p76">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|75-76}}</ref>

==Outlook==
The Moonstars sought to preserve life and knowledge. They had a similar outlook to the [[Harpers]], but dedicated themselves to [[Mystra]], [[Oghma]], and [[Sehanine Moonbow]].<ref name="C&G-p25" /> Their leader, Khelben, was very much in tune with the beliefs and motivations of the Harpers, but preferred to keep tighter control of the agents under his control. He felt that he sacrificed much for the Harpers during his time in their service and was unfairly treated by the [[Twilight Trio]].<ref name="C&G-p26">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|26}}</ref>

==Prophecy==
A passage from [[Amagal's Tome]] (an ancient elven text from [[Cormanthor]]) declared:
{{Quote|''The Tel'Teukiira will come hidden and in many guises and faces. They dwell in shadow and speak in omens, yet they shall bring about waking dreams and save us from the Three Threats Who Wait in Darkness, the Prefects, and ourselves.''}}
It was believed that only Khelben understood the relevance of these words.<ref name="C&G-p25" />


==Activities==
Based in [[Waterdeep]], the Moonstars followed much the same methods as the Harpers did, though the Moonstars operated in more secrecy than the Harpers and members were less free to do what they wanted, being under the direct and relatively strict command of the Blackstaff himself.<ref name="Cos.W-p76"/><ref name="CaD-30">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|30}}</ref> Officially, the group's headquarters was [[Blackstaff Tower]], though very few even visited that prestigious place. Each member carried a badge that allowed them access to their real stronghold, [[Silver Safehold]], which was an extraplanar stronghold built by the [[Storm Heralds]] of [[Cormyr]].<ref name="C&G-p26"/><ref name="CaD-31"/><ref name="Cos.W-p76"/>

Of the gods who pledged their support to the Harpers, only three also supported the Moonstars: [[Mystra]], [[Oghma]], and [[Sehanine Moonbow]]. But even with the lack of the other Harper gods' blessings, Khelben and Laeral made up for it with their own spells.<ref name="C&G-p25" />

To aid them in their quests to vanquish evil, the Moonstars had several permanent ''[[portal]]s'' scattered around Faerûn to allow quick passage.<ref name="PG.PotM">{{Cite web/Perilous Gateways/Portals of the Moonstars}}</ref>

==History==
The Moonstars were officially formed in the {{YearlinkName|1370}}, but this was only a step in a much larger plan by the organization's founder, Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun, who had sought to create his own evil-fighting organization for centuries before.<ref name="C&G-p26" /> A strong supporter of the group, Lady [[Alathene Moonstar]], granted them permission to call themselves the Moonstars "in hopes that its members might be more worthy of the name than [[Vanrak Moonstar|some relations]] of mine."<ref name="CaD-31">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|31}}</ref><ref name="Cos.W-p76"/>

An offshoot of the Harpers, the Moonstars were formed when Khelben realized that he didn't have as much power over the Harpers as he once did. He discovered in the {{YearlinkName|816}}, that [[Alaundo|Alaundo the Seer]] had written prophecies about an as-yet-undiscovered threat to [[Faerûn]].
