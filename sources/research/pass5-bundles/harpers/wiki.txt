The '''Harpers''', or '''Those Who Harp''', were a semi-secret organization dedicated to preserving historical lore, maintaining the balance between nature and civilization,<ref name="CotH-p3-6">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|3-6}}</ref><ref name="FRA-p123-124" /><ref name="PGtF-p58">{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|58}}</ref> and defending the innocent from the forces of [[evil]] across the Realms.<ref name=SCAG-p123-124 /><ref name="FRCS-p46-47" /><ref name="CoV-p101">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|101}}</ref><ref name="CoS.W-p73">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|73}}</ref> The Harpers involved themselves in many world-changing events that helped shape the course of [[Faerûn]]'s destiny.<ref name="SCAG-p147">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|147}}</ref> Their power and influence waxed and waned over the years, as their order underwent a series of collapses and reformations.<ref name="RTR-p25-26">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|25-26}}</ref><ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" /> Their reputation amongst the people of the Realms varied just as wildly. They were often seen as wide-eyed idealists, but also just as often seen as insufferable meddlers who could not keep their business to themselves.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="CotH-p7" />
{{Quote|We are the Harpers. We are the Lord Protectors of the Realms, Fools, all―but the Gods look down and smile glory upon us. Weep for us, watch for us, and hope in us. We shall not fail thee.|Harper Chant, composed by [[Ailadrea Stars-in-mist]].<ref name="CotH-p3-6" />}}

{{TOC limit|3}}
==Organization==
[[File:Harpers tapestry.png|thumb|''A tapestry depicting Harpers, including a founding member, Elminster Aumar.'']]
The Harpers as an organization was actually anything but organized. Rather, it was a confederation of individuals from contrasting cultures and backgrounds who bonded over the shared goals of maintaining peace, preserving knowledge, and protecting the downtrodden. Some [[Harper agent]]s worked in small groups—often alongside friends and close allies—while others operated as lone operatives,<ref name="PGtF-p58" /><ref name="SKT-p12">{{Cite book/Storm King's Thunder|12}}</ref> who kept only informal ties with the group at large.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /> Throughout the many years it endured, the Harpers had disbanded and fallen apart several times, only to reform later in some new iteration.<ref name="RTR-p25-26" /><ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38">{{Cite dragon/430/The Harpers of Waterdeep|33-38}}</ref>

The Harpers' informality and lack of formal structure lent to its continuance as true secret society.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /> Some members took on specific roles, such as 'spymasters', 'handlers', and 'agents',<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" /> but maintained no hierarchy or ranking over their fellows. It was said that nothing could be truly "official" within the Harpers; the group was poorly defined, with contradictory operational standards, no formal roster, and a public face that was inconsistent from one locale to the next.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" />

However, faction ranks were reported to be in use around the late 15{{th}} century. From lowest to highest, they were Watcher, Harpshadow, Brightcandle, Wise Owl, and High Harper.<ref name="DMG-2014-p22">{{Cite book/Dungeon Master's Guide 5th edition|22}}</ref>

There did remain one consistent representation of the Harpers across the Realms, however: their symbol. The Harpers' symbol comprised a silver [[harp]] and crescent moon, set upon a field of black or royal blue.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /><ref name="RTR-p25-26" />

===Branches===
{{Quote|Two roads taken to the same gate; one seen more often, the other never late.|The bard [[Alither]] of [[Telflamm]], regarding the two branches of the Harpers.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" />}}
For much of its history, the Harpers were separated into two branches, each of which operated on either half of Faerûn's [[Heartlands]]. The western branch of the Harpers―the ''Order of the Silver Moon and Harp'', headquartered in [[Berdusk]]<ref name="CotH-p12-13" />―maintained a formal hierarchy, and orchestrated far-reaching plots many months in advance of their execution. The eastern branch―known as the ''Senior Harpers'' or ''Harpers in the Shadows'', based out of [[Shadowdale]]―preferred instead to sponsor parties of young [[adventurer]]s, wielding subtle influence rather than taking direct action.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="CotH-p36" /><ref name="CotD-p57" /> Both branches strived to achieve the same goals, and regularly shared intelligence with their comrades across the Realms. Beyond their operational differences, the collective members of the two branches each held contrasting attitudes and world-views.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" /><ref name="HL-p153" />

For a few decades, there existed an offshoot branch of the Harpers, known as the [[Moonstars]]. This splinter sect acted separately from the two branches of the Harpers, operating in the sprawling metropolis of [[Waterdeep]],<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p275">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|275}}</ref> and at times undermining the efforts of true Waterdhavian Harpers.<ref name="CoS.W-p73" />

===High Harpers===
The only true leadership of the Harpers was a small governing council known as the High Harpers. They were responsible for setting and planning most of the group's long-term plans and goals.<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" /> Members were elected through the means of secret ballots among the other High Harpers, with the criteria of long-term service and extreme discretion in the execution of their plans.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|274}}</ref> One of the High Harpers on the [[Sword Coast]] was [[Jaheira]] of [[Baldur's Gate]], who was granted the title sometime before [[1492 DR]].<ref name="bg3" />

===Master Harpers===
While the Harpers held no formal hierarchy, some members did enjoy the distinction of being named [[Master Harper]]s.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" /> These select few were blessed by the goddess [[Mystra]] and bestowed with [[Harpers#Abilities|divine boons]] associated with the Goddess of Magic and other deities that were present at the [[Harpers#First Reformation|First Reformation]].<ref name="CotH-p26" /><ref name="CotH-p40-41">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|40-41}}</ref> This term was sometimes confused with the unofficial name of the Harpers' eastern branch, the so-called [[Harpers#Branches|''Master Harpers'']], though usage in this manner was generally discouraged.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" />

If a Master Harper was slain or otherwise died with an unfinished quest or charge, they returned to the Realms as a [[spectral harpist]].<ref name="MCF-p89">{{Cite book/Monsters of Faerûn|89}}</ref> These incorporeal, [[undead]] Harpers retained the powers they held in life,<ref name="CotH-p121-122" /> serving the order as immortal guardians over the aptly named 'Harper haunts'.<ref name="CotH-p85-86" />

Among the Masters Harpers were four of Mystra's immortal daughters, [[Alustriel Silverhand|Endué Alustriel]], [[Dove Falconhand|Ambara Dove]], [[Laeral Silverhand|Anamanué Laeral]], and [[Storm Silverhand|Esheena "Storm" Silverhand]], as well as two of the goddess's favored [[chosen]], [[Elminster]] and [[Khelben Arunsun]]. Other known Master Harpers included:<ref name="CotH-p42-44">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|42-44}}</ref>
* [[Belhuar Thantarth]], an accomplished bard and the lord of Twilight Hall.<ref name="CotD-p57" /><ref name="CotH-p42-44" />
* [[Cylyria Dragonbreast]], High Lady of Berdusk and one-time ruler of the city.<ref name="CotH-p42-44" />
* [[Obslin Minstrelwish]], devoted servant of Lady Dragonbreast and seneschal of Twilight Hall.<ref name="CotD-p57" /><ref name="CotH-p50">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|50}}</ref>

==Beliefs==
{{quote|Right, then, good Lady Shandril, I shall try to tell thee something of what it is to be a Harper. A Harper holds peaceful sharing of the lands above all other goals. By sharing, we mean all the races living in and under the land, where each prefers to live, trading together where desired and need stir them to, and respecting each other's holds and ways—without the daily bloodletting that all too often holds sway in the Realms today.|[[Mirt the Moneylender]], explaining the Harpers' purpose to [[Shandril Shessair]].<ref name="CoF-p173-174">{{Cite book/Crown of Fire/Paperback 1994|173-174|9}}</ref>}}
The Harpers believed in the power of the individual over that of the sprawling kingdoms with laws that oppressed individual liberty. They sought to maintain balance between all things, civilization and nature, societal order and personal liberty.<ref name="DrM404.UEMH-p3" /> They considered the [[elven]] city of [[Myth Drannor]], with its rich culture, celebration of the arts, and promotion of education to be the most ideal expression of this balance by the free peoples of the Realms. They sought among other things to spread that belief, and recreate the world in the lost city's image.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /><ref name="CotH-p8-11" />

While they strived to eliminate tyranny and foster "[[good]]ness" in the Realms, not all Harpers agreed on how it was defined.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> Despite these differences of opinion, they did share the belief that current events could only be fully understood with a comprehensive knowledge of the history that preceded them.<ref name="DrM404.UEMH-p3">{{Cite dragon/404/Unearthed Arcana: A Matter of Honor|3}}</ref>

===Attitude===
{{Fq|text=True bards are another matter entirely. Part of a bard's training is memorizing the traditions and lore, which are passed down, precise and immutable, for generations. That's why so many Harpers are bards: to preserve a knowledge of our past.|source=[[Khelben Arunsun]]<ref name="ES-p17">{{Cite book/Elfsong (1994)|17|1}}</ref>}}
Considering the vastly different backgrounds and cultures from which Harper members were drawn, each cell or individual agent maintained their own, unique outlook on how to best conduct Harper business. Generally speaking, they took care of how their actions affected the greater world. Senior members tended to act with caution and circumspection, while younger Harpers often believed it was upon them to personally make the world a better place.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="CotH-p7" /> Yet all members were taught to uphold the virtues of dedication and comradery and honestly assess their own shortcomings. Harpers knew they could only earn respect not by attaining power or holding lofty titles, but by demonstrating sound judgement and acting with integrity.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> 

Some enemies of the Harpers accused them of treating their more manipulative schemes as some sort of grand game, that the people they directed were mere pieces on a [[Chess (game)|chess]] board. The Harpers demonstrated a type of altruistic arrogance, insisting that their machinations were taken for folks' own good and the overall betterment of the world.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" />

Harper agents were determined individuals. They never quit on their missions and took however long was needed to see them to completion.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> If an agent was slain in the field, their comrades would never forget their sacrifice and vowed to bring justice to their killer.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" />

===Culture===
It was said that the secretiveness of the Harpers extended beyond the organization as a whole, trickling down to its individual members and their personal lives. While often boisterous and lighthearted in public, Harpers tended not to share genuine sentiments with others–save for occasions when they were alone with their comrades.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" />

Overall, the Harpers were one of the most tolerant factions of the Realms. They accepted people from all walks of life, even those with unsavory pasts,<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" /> so long as they swore the Harper Code and did not violate its tenets.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" />

===Harper Code===
{{Quote|I shall do no less.|The response given to the Harpers' watchwords.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" />}}
The code of the Harpers was a series of outwardly simple statements that were in truth exceptionally difficult to maintain in earnest. They were not written down in any [[book]] or tome, but were expressed as a series of 'watchwords' used to swear in new members, an occasion known as the [[ritual of recognition]].<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" /><ref name="FRA-p123-124" /><ref name="HL-p153">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|153}}</ref><ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> In the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]], however, the Harper code was written in a book, upon which new members would swear their oaths. They would keep a copy as reminder.<ref name="HAT">{{Cite video/Honor Among Thieves}}</ref><ref name="TJN-c2">{{Cite book/Honor Among Thieves: The Junior Novelization|16-18|2}}</ref><ref name="TJN-c17">{{Cite book/Honor Among Thieves: The Junior Novelization|113-114|17}}</ref>

The Code of the Harpers, or the Harper Code, was as follows:<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" /><ref name="FRA-p123-124" /><ref name="HL-p153" /><ref name="CotH-p8-11" />
* "Harpers work against villainy and wickedness wherever they find it—but they work ever mindful of the consequences of what they do."<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="HL-p153" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" />
* "All beings should walk free of fear, with the right to live their lives as they wish."<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="HL-p153" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" />
* "The rule of law aids peace and fosters freedom, so long as the laws are just and those who enforce them lenient and understanding."<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="HL-p153" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" />
* "No extreme is good. For freedom to flourish, all must be in balance: the powers of realms, the reaches of the cities and the wilderlands into each other, and the influence of one being over another."<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="HL-p153" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" />
* "Whatever it takes, a Harper will do. Pride never rules the deeds of a true Harper."<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="HL-p153" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" />
* "Harpers can spare themselves less freedom than those they work to protect must have—but even a Harper must be free."<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="HL-p153" />
* "Harpers police their own. A Harper who hears the call of personal power can no longer hear the sweet song of the harp. A Harper who seizes power, and holds it above all else, is a traitor to the harp. Traitors must die, for freedom to live."<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="HL-p153" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" />
* "Without a past, no being can appreciate what they have, and where they may be going."<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="HL-p153" />
In summary and practice, it meant to oppose tyranny and evil; to protect the oppressed; to maintain freedom, peace, and order; to support fair and just laws; and to prevent extremes and imbalances of power, wealth, influence, and opportunity.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" /><ref name="FRA-p123-124" /><ref name="HL-p153" /><ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c2" /> They should do whatever they must, no matter how mundane or boring; no matter how hard, uncomfortable, or demeaning; and no matter how risky or life-altering.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /> They were expected to help one another without hesitation or concern for cost<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /> and to ask nothing in return for the works they did.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c2" /> They could freely disagree with or criticize each other, and were to police their own and punish treachery.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /> Finally, they were to accumulate knowledge for the good of everyone and to record and preserve the past,<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /> for it was said that history provided the light with which a Harper could see the future ahead.<ref name="WPR-p9-13" />

Throughout the course of history, the code of the Harpers spread beyond its members, influencing the shaping the laws of cities across the Heartlands and [[the North]].<ref name="CotH-p8-11" />

==Activities==
[[File:Harper Teleportation.png|thumb|right|350px|''The Harpers employed a network of teleportation circles in cities across the North, to ensure their movements remained shrouded in secrecy.'']]
{{Quote|True, we must fight, it seems often enough to keep our swords and our tempers both sharp enough. Yet, know ye; all of us fight when we must, or die. Moreover, ye only hear of blades drawn and death and spells hurled, and never know of the many, many times that a quiet word or a skillful deal has turned enemies aside from each other, forced a way clear where none was before, or distracted foes from the eager task of tearing each other's throats out. That is the true Harper way: subtle and quiet, behind the shouting. Trust and wisdom, and outfoxing others is what we deal in."|Mirt, continuing to Shandril.<ref name="CoF-p173-174" />}}
Conducting Harper business consisted of scheming, subtle nudging, and more than a little manipulation. More often than not, they helped guide events of the Realms rather than acting as their direct cause.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> Harpers tended to work as discreetly as possible, preferring the weapons of knowledge and influence, rather than those of more violent means.<ref name="WPR-p9-13">{{Cite book/Warriors and Priests of the Realms|9-13}}</ref><ref name="SCAG-p147" /><ref name="RoT-p11-12" />

So that the balance between civilization and the natural world could be maintained, the Harpers acted to keep kingdoms small and minimize the destruction of animal and plant life by [[human]]s or others.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /><ref name="FRA-p123-124" /> To that end, they cultivated public gardens and regularly re-planted crops in the wake of war and other crises.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> 

To preserve the knowledge of the past―and thus keep secure present and future prospects―the Harpers readily shared their wealth of knowledge and lore with the common folks of the Realms,<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="FRA-p123-124" /> often through music, stories, and song.<ref name="DrM404.UEMH-p3" /><ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> They never altered their performances to agree the whims of those who held power, but kept their works true to the tune of history.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /> When necessary, they used their performances to convey secret messages to allies and warn them of nearby dangers.<ref name="CotH-p126">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|126}}</ref> The simple alteration of a single chord or substituted phrase in a well-known folk song could instantly inform others to heed their warning.<ref name="EFR-p189">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|189}}</ref>

When the threats of destruction and tyranny presented themselves, the Harpers took decisive action. They moved against evil cults, thieves' guilds, and other malevolent forces that terrorized the people of the Realms.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /> Agents protected innocent folk from monstrous beings that sought to destroy civilization,<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> defended the weak against careless or malicious practitioners of [[the Art]],<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /><ref name="PA-p15">{{Cite book/Princes of the Apocalypse|15}}</ref> and kept at bay the horrors of the [[Underdark]], by sealing off passages that linked it to the surface world.<ref name="OOA-p126">{{Cite book/Out of the Abyss|126}}</ref>

===Operations===
The Harpers operated either individually or in small, close-knit groups. Senior members who wielded significant influence―known as spymasters<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" />―directed and aided young adventurers―known as Harper protégés<ref name="CoV-p23">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|23}}</ref>―who took on quests that furthered the Harpers' interests.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="WDH-p16" /> Yet other veteran Harpers lived the adventuring life themselves, taking it upon themselves to complete those very quests for the betterment of Faerûn.<ref name="CotH-p7">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|7}}</ref>

Special Harpers known as handlers served as intermediaries between spymasters orchestrating events, and other members completing field work. They were more often deployed in cities and regions that were considered more perilous for an agent's safety and well-being.<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" />

Most Harper agents lived some form of a double life.<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" /> They went about their business for much of their day, earning an honest (or not-so-honest) living in town or in the wilds, all while closely observing the activities of others. They took careful note of gossip and local tales, and compiled that information with that given to them by fellow members.<ref name="CotH-p7" /> They put on their best outgoing and charming personas, in order to get from others the most significant insights and information possible. Agents were of course allowed to pursue other interests outside of the organization, so long as they did not violate the Harpers' code.<ref name="CotH-p8-11">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|8-11}}</ref>

==Tactics==
{{Quote|Isn't that how the Harpers operate? They send one person to slip in and do a job where an army couldn't go. If the agent fails, they've lost only once. But if the agent succeeds...|[[Caledan Caldorien]]<ref name="CotH-p3-6" />}}
Whenever possible, Harpers made most of their moves outside of public view so they could not be tracked and studied and they acted "behind the scenes" either in places of power or at opportune moments.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" /> They analyzed power dynamics within a realm and took action when needed to prevent power imbalance or stop one faction from achieving domininance over others.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> These techniques were how Harpers ensured the greatest chance of success during their missions; it was on occasions when agents revealed themselves and acted openly that the group committed its greatest blunders.<ref name="FRA-p123-124" /> 

Known amongst their own as "weaponless Harpers", some lived as spies as informants<ref name="CotH-p7" /> often under aliases as traveling performers.<ref name="EFR-p36">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|36}}</ref> Some others acted as political manipulators and information brokers, working with commoners and merchants to spread rumors in aid of the Harpers' cause.<ref name="CotH-p7" /> Yet others fed intelligence to their more foolhardy comrades, focusing on deception and subterfuge over steel and spell.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /> 

In order to correct economic disparities that were prevalent across the Realms,<ref name="ToA-p30">{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|30}}</ref> 
the Harpers were known to occasionally rob wealthy nobles and merchants, in order to redistribute their wealth to poorer folk. If needed, those agents took some of their ill-gotten gains for themselves, to finance future Harper operations.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> To further support other members, the Harpers employed a network of drop boxes, hidden caches, and safe houses, at which their agents could find communiques, supplies, or even a safe place to rest.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" /> It was believed there were at least thirty such drop-offs in the city of Waterdeep alone.<ref name="DrM211.ISSDB-p29-34" />

Individual Harpers agents often used the grand, boisterous [[Midsummer]] celebrations sponsored by the [[church of Tymora]] as occasions to meet with their comrades.<ref name="FRCS3e-p253">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|253}}</ref><ref name="F&A-p167-168">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|167-168}}</ref>

====Communication====
{{Quote|Information and knowledge gained is half the battle won against any foe.|Harper maxim<ref name="DrM211.ISSDB-p29-34" />}}
Members shared information with each other in myriad ways, often employing magical means of communication or employing allies as intermediaries.<ref name="WPR-p9-13" /> 

Elder Harpers developed their own form of non-verbal cant, known as ''[[harpspeak]]''.<ref name="WPR-p9-13" /> The Harper sign language dated back to the years before the [[Era of Upheaval]], and fell into disuse during that era.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" />

[[File:Harper Symbols.png|thumb|right|300px|''Harper symbols were used to discreetly communicate messages to fellow members and allies. An additional dot added to a rune denoted it as false. This was used as a means to throw off foes familiar with the script.'']]
The Harpers also employed a secret, rudimentary language of [[rune]]s,<ref name="WPR-p9-13" /> each of which had a unique meaning to other Harpers. These runes were inscribed at certain locations to warn, attract, comfort, or otherwise notify other members―or anyone else that knew their meaning―about nearby dangers or local support. To mislead their foes, this system included extraneous marks that only other agents would know how to interpret and know to disregard them.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" />

They also utilized a series of passwords and code words to facilitate secret communication. Some of the known words and phrases included:<ref name="DuM188.WW-p4-6">{{Cite dungeon/188/Whispered Words|4-6}}</ref>
* ''Bad crops, as I recall'', used in the Eastern Heartlands to warn others of impending danger.<ref name="DuM188.WW-p4-6" />
* ''Bitter water in that well,'' a phrase used by [[Selgaunt]]an Harpers to solicit immediate privacy.<ref name="DuM188.WW-p4-6" />
* ''Dove'', which meant to take reconnaissance of an area.<ref name="DuM188.WW-p4-6" />
* ''Maskertander'', a nonsense word referring to cargo, used with "good" or "bad" to declare whether it could be utilized or should be avoided. <ref name="DuM188.WW-p4-6" />
* ''Rolling heads'', a term used in Cormyr and the Dales to convey the message that magic was not to be used. Conversely, ''wiser heads'' was used when agents had to prepare to defend themselves from impending magics.<ref name="DuM188.WW-p4-6" />
* ''Salaeriko'', the meaning of which was not entirely clear.<ref name="DuM188.WW-p4-6" />
* ''Sarkyn'', which identified others as Harper allies or otherwise trustworthy individuals.<ref name="DuM188.WW-p4-6" />
* ''Taland'', used by [[Athkatla]]n Harpers to tell others to go into hiding; also used by agents in [[Ormpur]] for agents to identify or otherwise reveal themselves.<ref name="DuM188.WW-p4-6" />

Some Harpers received magical [[tattoo]]s of the group's harp-and-moon symbol to allow themselves to be clearly identified by allies. These appeared as innocuous, mundane tattoos at first, but transformed into the Harpers' symbol when the proper [[activation word]] was spoken.<ref name="Adversary-p175-179">{{Cite book/The Adversary/Hardcover|175,179}}</ref>

==Base of Operations==
By all official records, the Harpers organization maintained no real base of operations in the Realms.<ref name="FRA-p123-124" /><ref name="RTR-p25-26" /> The operations of the Harpers' eastern branch remained largely decentralized,<ref name="N.GSF-p67" /> while the hub of its western branch, [[Twilight Hall]] in Berdusk, was the nearest thing the group had to a true headquarters.<ref name="CotH-p85-86">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|85-86}}</ref><ref name="FRA-p78">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|78}}</ref> Founded during a time when the [[Harpers#Corruption of the Harper King|Harpers were at war]], Twilight Hall served as a reminder of the Harpers' origins along with the reasons for which all Harpers were willing to fight.<ref name="CotH-p33" /><ref name="CotH-p34"/>

[[Korinn's Keep]] was an island stronghold somewhere in the [[Sea of Swords]] that was used in the late 1400s to store an assortment of valuable [[jewels]] and powerful and dangerous [[magic item]]s taken from the foes of the Harpers. Only a Harper, that is, a bearer of a ''Harper pin'', could enter.<ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c3">{{Cite book/Honor Among Thieves: The Junior Novelization|23-27|3}}</ref>

===Harper Refuges===
In the hinterlands of the Realms, elven allies and devotees of the nature deities maintained safe spaces for their friends among Those-Who-Harp. Known as [[Harper refuge]]s, these locations were kept secure and secret from non-Harpers and were protected by powerful [[magic]]al enchantments.<ref name="CotH-p93-95">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|93-95}}</ref>

===Regions===
In the so-called "civilized world", the Harpers stationed agents in many prominent cities, smaller towns, and even isolated hamlets all across Faerûn and beyond. They operated mainly in [[north Faerûn]], along the [[Sword Coast]], within the [[Eastern Heartlands|Eastern]] and the [[Western Heartlands]],<ref name="FRA-p123-124">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Adventures|123-124}}</ref><ref name="RTR-p25-26" /> and the [[Dalelands]].<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="N.GSF-p67">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/The Wilderness|67}}</ref> 

By the 15{{th}} century [[DR]], the Harpers were active across all of Faerûn,<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" /> and beyond. They had even set up operations on other [[planes of existence]], such as their place in the [[Radiant Citadel]]'s [[Radiant Citadel#Notable Locations|Court of Whispers]], within the [[Astral Plane]].<ref name="JRC-p8">{{Cite book/Journeys Through the Radiant Citadel|8}}</ref>

==Possessions==
[[File:Harper Agent 4e.jpg|thumb|left|250px|''A Harper agent blatantly and proudly displaying where her loyalties lie.'']]
{{Quote|See this? It means I'm one of the good guys. Secret mission. Hush-hush.|[[Olive Ruskettle|Stevie Nickelplate]], aspiring bard and "agent" of the Harpers flashing her Harper pin.<ref name="CAB-p51">{{Cite book/Curse of the Azure Bonds|51}}</ref>}}
The Harpers organization was one with significant resources and a surprising amount of wealth. As a whole, they maintained financial investments in holdings and establishments across [[west Faerûn]].<ref name="CotD-p26" /> The collective network of artisans, craftspeople, and merchants within their ranks allowed agents of the organization to purchase valuable [[item enchantment|magical items]] at a significant discount, helping increase the chances of success for their missions.<ref name="CoS.W-p73" />

Harpers across the Realms could be identified by the small, treasured token that became a symbol for aiding the downtrodden: the ''[[Harper pin]]''. Each of these badges proudly displayed a silver crescent moon and harp, and possessed potent [[magic]]al powers,<ref name="CotH-p104-105">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|104-105}}</ref><ref name="NWCS-p23-25" /> but varied depending on its creation.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /><ref name="HL-p150">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|150}}</ref> In broad terms, they were crafted in ''lesser'' and ''greater'' variations.<ref name="MoF-p159" /> Pins were carried at all times, except during missions where the exposure of their identity might interfere with its success. However, they would only wear them openly<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> when needing to be recognized by other Harpers,<ref name="NCS-p25">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|25}}</ref> when at Harper meetings,<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> or when in Harper bases.<ref name="CotH-p91" />

''[[Harper token]]s'' were small pieces of jewelry that possessed minor [[item enchantment|enchantments]], bestowed upon individuals that performed a service for the organization. Crafted from [[silver]], [[gold]], or other precious materials, they were often embossed with symbols favored by the Harpers.<ref name="CoV-p66-67">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|66-67}}</ref>

Traditionally, Harpers had no uniforms, both to maintain secrecy and to avoid needless pride.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> However, by the 1400s DR, some Harpers donned dark blue-green hooded [[cloak]]s attached with their ''Harper pins'' over their armor for use within their bases or in situations where they needed, or wanted, to be recognized.<ref name="NCS-p25" /><ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c2" /><ref name="TJN-c3" />

===Magic Items===
Harpers crafted<ref name="WDH-p16" /> and employed a variety of magical [[potion]]s, [[scroll]]s, and other items that were invaluable in times of danger. Notable among these were ''[[philter of timely invulnerability|philters of timely invulnerability]]'', which helped agents withstand mortal wounds and escape unharmed,<ref name="CotH-p104-105" /> and ''[[wandering star]]s'', gems of illumination that were linked to a single individual.<ref name="CotH-p115" /> Preferred weapons of the Harpers were [[implement]]s such as ''[[staff of wanderers|staves of wanderers]]'' and the unique, ''[[Pheljara's wand]]'',<ref name="CotH-p109-110">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|109-110}}</ref> along with ''[[shield of shadows|shields of shadow]]'',<ref name="CotH-p115">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|115}}</ref> and unique, Harper-crafted blades known as ''[[singing sword]]s''. When drawn, these enchanted greatswords let out resounding songs that bolstered the morale of their wielders and improved their combat prowess.<ref name="Magister-p59">{{Cite book/The Magister|59}}</ref><ref name="MoF-p144">{{Cite book/Magic of Faerûn|144}}</ref> Some two-score Harpers across the North were bestowed powerful ''[[Laeral's spell shield|spell shields]]'' that were crafted by the one-time Harper and Open Lord of Waterdeep, [[Laeral Silverhand]].<ref name="SS-p109">{{Cite book/The Seven Sisters|109}}</ref>

A ''[[scroll of the Harpers]]'' was a magical scroll that caused ''Harper pins'' to magically transform into actual harps for a short duration.<ref name="CotH-p104-105" />

Over the centuries, Harper craftspeople and [[enchanter]]s had made some of the finest instruments in the Realms, appropriately often in the form of magical harps. Notable among these instruments were ''[[Dunzrin's harp]]'', ''[[Jolora's harp]]'',<ref name="CotH-p111-112">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|111-112}}</ref> ''[[Murlyn's harp of charming]]'',<ref name="EM.IV-p1326">{{Cite book/Encyclopedia Magica Volume IV|1326}}</ref> ''[[Ruehar's flute]]'',<ref name="FMD-p53">{{Cite book/The Fall of Myth Drannor|53}}</ref> and ''[[Tallar's harp]]''.<ref name="CotH-p114">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|114}}</ref> Many Harpers' favored ''[[handharp]]s'', enchanted harps that could be held in one hand.<ref name="HHK-p49">{{Cite book/Halls of the High King|49}}</ref><ref name="MoF-p159">{{Cite book/Magic of Faerûn|159}}</ref>

==Abilities==
[[File:Harper Mage.jpg|thumb|right|250px|''A Harper Mage dutifully studying their spellbook.'']]
{{Quote|Know ye, lad―some Harpers have powers granted 'em by the gods, they do. Ye'd best kill them quick, before they can remember just what those powers are...|[[Gurth Aglathlyn]], Biting Blade of the [[Council of Zulkirs]].<ref name="CotH-p40-41" />}}
When Harpers were [[Harpers#History|founded for the first time]], several of those present were blessed by the Seven Gods of the Dancing Place,<ref name="DrM367-p7" /> deities that chose to manifest themselves on [[Toril]] on the momentous occasion. Later designated as the [[Harpers#Master Harpers|"Master Harpers"]], these select few were granted unique powers,<ref name="CotH-p40-41" /> not unlike those bestowed upon the divine [[chosen]],<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /> each associated with one of the Harpers' patron deities.<ref name="MoF-p28-31">{{Cite book/Magic of Faerûn|28-31}}</ref> Notable among those deities were [[Deneir]], the Lord of All Glyphs; [[Milil]], the Lord of Song; [[Lliira]], the Lady of Joy; Mystra, the Goddess of Magic; and [[Tymora]], the Lady of Luck.<ref name="CotH-p40-41" /><ref name="PGtF-p58" />

Later in the Harpers' history―sometime after the [[Time of Troubles]]―these boons became available to members outside the handful Master Harpers,<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /> namely to [[Harper mage]]s and [[Harper priest|priests]]. Notable among these powers were:<ref name="FRCS-p46-47" /> 
* ''Deneir's sight'', which allowed a Harper to see through the magic of glyphs, symbols, and runes.<ref name="CotH-p40-41" /><ref name="FRCS-p46-47" /><ref name="ELH-p293">{{Cite book/Epic Level Handbook|293}}</ref>
* ''Lliira's heart'', by which Harpers could steel their nerves in the face of fear and dread.<ref name="CotH-p40-41" /><ref name="FRCS-p46-47" /> 
* ''Mielikki's blessing'', which bolstered the determination of the Harper to take action against their foes.<ref name="DrM367-p7" />
* ''Mystra's grace'', the power to glean insight into the [[Weave]] to protect oneself from harmful magic.<ref name="MoF-p28-31">{{Cite book/Magic of Faerûn|28-31}}</ref>
* ''Oghma's insight'', the hunger for greater knowledge and understanding in even the most esoteric of scholarly fields.<ref name="MoF-p28-31">{{Cite book/Magic of Faerûn|28-31}}</ref>
* ''Tymora's smile'', which granted a little extra luck in times of need.<ref name="CotH-p40-41" /><ref name="FRCS-p46-47" /><ref name="DrM367-p7" /><ref name="ELH-p293" />

===Magic===
Experienced Harpers learned how to [[pass without trace|move without leaving a trace]], and do so once each day. As they furthered their career, they could perform the feat more often.<ref name="WPR-p9-13" />

Due to the bond formed with Mystra upon their founding, along with the numerous powerful [[archmage]]s that held membership amongst their numbers, the Harpers possessed knowledge of numerous spells that were not commonly known by casters in the Realms.<ref name="MoF-p28-31">{{Cite book/Magic of Faerûn|28-31}}</ref> Notable among these were ''[[delayed magic missile]]'',<ref name="CoS.CG-p87-89" /> ''[[Elminster's multiple mouths]]'',<ref name="SS-p72">{{Cite book/The Seven Sisters|72}}</ref> ''[[Gamalon's fiery backlash]]'',<ref name="CoS.CG-p87-89">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|87-89}}</ref> ''[[Ottar's mask]]'',<ref name="SS-p79">{{Cite book/The Seven Sisters|79}}</ref> and ''[[starharp]]'', among others.<ref name="HHK-p52">{{Cite book/Halls of the High King|52}}</ref>

==Relationships==
{{Quote|The trouble with Harpers is they'll never stop talking, even when you desperately wish they would.|[[Cormik]], proprietor of the [[Prince and Pauper]] gambling den.<ref name="CotH-p12-13">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|12-13}}</ref>}}
===Allies===
The Harpers regularly worked alongside the [[Heralds of Faerûn]] to maintain the history and cultures of the lands they loved.<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> Strongholds maintained by the Heralds served as hubs of communication for the Harpers, being sites where they could safely leave mail for fellow agents to receive later on.<ref name="CotH-p12-13" /> 

The group known as the [[High Heralds]] was founded by former Harper [[Aliost Oskrunnar]]. He called together leaders of some major powers in [[West Faerûn|the West]] to standardize their use of heraldic arms and prevent miscommunications that could adversely affect commerce in the region. The High Heralds remained staunch allies of the Harpers and worked alongside them on missions of shared interest.<ref name="CotH-p73-75">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|73-75}}</ref>

They counted among their allies some of the more prominent [[:Category:Bardic colleges|bardic colleges]], including [[Fochlucan]] in Silverymoon,<ref name="SM-p62">{{Cite book/Silver Marches|62}}</ref> and the [[College of the Herald]] in [[Herald's Holdfast]].<ref name=SCAG-p123-124 /> They were also allied with many of the [[druid circle]]s that established themselves across the North,<ref name=SCAG-p127>{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|127}}</ref> such as the [[Druids of Tall Trees]].<ref name="CoV-p75">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|75}}</ref> Political bodies and military orders, such as the [[Lords of Waterdeep]], the [[Lords' Alliance]]<ref name="SF-p9">{{Cite book/The Savage Frontier|9}}</ref> the [[Knights in Silver]],<ref name="SM-p91">{{Cite book/Silver Marches|91}}</ref> and the [[Order of the Gauntlet]], were aligned with the Harpers' mission to maintain order across the Realms.<ref name="RoT-p11-12" />

Other groups that the Harpers counted among their allies included the [[Fangshields]],<ref name="CoV-p78">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|78}}</ref> the [[Fellowship of the Purple Staff]],<ref name="CoV-p81">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|81}}</ref> the [[Sisterhood of Essembra]], the [[Talons of Justice]],<ref name="DoF-p99-100">{{Cite book/Dragons of Faerûn|99-100}}</ref> and even the [[galeb duhr]]s of [[Durgg-Gontag]].<ref name="Underdark-p147">{{Cite book/Underdark|147}}</ref>

===Moonstars===
{{Main|Moonstars}}
The one organization in Faerûn with the most complicated relationship with the Harpers was a splinter organization known as the Moonstars. Founded by ex-Harper Khelben Arunsun,<ref name=SCAG-p123-124 /> the sect differed greatly in their collective outlook on how to accomplish their shared goals,<ref name="CaD-p25-26">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|25-26}}</ref> but often still worked hand-in-hand with traditional Harper members.<ref name=SCAG-p123-124 /> On other occasions, the two groups operated as rivals of one another.<ref name="DrM335.WSC-p22-32" />

The Moonstars nearly completely fell apart following the death of their founder, with only a few select members of their inner circle continuing their work in the subsequent decades. They experienced a brief, but astounding reappearance in the Realms during the events of the [[Second Sundering]], before once again going silent.<ref name=SCAG-p123-124 /> 

==History==
[[File:Dancing-place.png|thumb|right|250px|''The Dancing Place was the beauteous glade in High Dale, in which the Harpers-in-Twilight was founded.'']]
{{Quote|There are many power groups, big and small, in Faerûn. Some aspire to conquest, and others long to defend the weak against such conquest. Of the latter category, I declare that none are as enigmatic (or as frustrating) as the Harpers.|[[Mendryll Belarod]].<ref name="WPR-p9-13" />}}

Accurate knowledge regarding the founding of the Harpers was said to be rather nebulous, and the history of the order overall was so convoluted that Elminster referred to it as "The Hidden Tale".<ref name="CotH-p20" /><ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38">{{Cite dragon/430/The Harpers of Waterdeep|33-38}}</ref> The original concept of the the Harpers was first conceived in the fabled city of Myth Drannor, by elven military leaders that worked in collusion with a few trusted human [[ranger]]s and [[druid]]s.<ref name="CotH-p20">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|20}}</ref> The [[mage]] [[Dathlue Mistwinter]] agreed to lead such a group in the {{YearlinkName|324}},<ref name="FRCS3e-p269-270">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|269-270}}</ref><ref name="CotD-p56"/><ref name="LEoF-p127">{{Cite book/Lost Empires of Faerûn|84-85}}</ref><ref name="GHR-p70">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|70}}</ref> at the head of a council that also included a (relatively) young Elminster Aumar. They took for their symbol Mistwinter's family crest—a silver harp between the horns of a crescent moon—and met at twilight at secret locations in the [[Elven Court]], earning them their moniker: the '''Harpers at Twilight'''.<ref name="CotH-p20"/><ref name="C.EoE-p38">{{Cite book/Cormanthyr: Empire of the Elves|38}}</ref>

The Harpers at Twilight dwindled in number over the next four centuries, victims of attrition at the hands of their enemies: bandits, slavers, [[drow]], [[illithid]]s, [[orc]]s, and evil spellcasters attracted to Myth Drannor's success.<ref name="CotH-p20"/> The final blow to the fabled City of Song was struck in the {{YearlinkName|712}}, at the culmination of the [[Weeping War]] when the [[Army of Darkness]] overwhelmed the defenders of Myth Drannor. In just [[Twilight's Falling|one battle]], more than half their number was lost.<ref name="FMD-p19">{{Cite book/The Fall of Myth Drannor|19}}</ref><ref name="FMD-p22-23" /><ref name="LEoF-p128">{{Cite book/Lost Empires of Faerûn|128}}</ref><ref name="GHR-p99">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|99}}</ref> By the end of the conflict, Dathlue herself was slain,<ref name="CotH-p21">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|21}}</ref> killed alongside a dozen of her fellow Harpers-at-Twilight.<ref name="CotH-p22">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|22}}</ref>

===First Reformation===
On the [[Flamerule 27|27{{th}} of Flamerule]] in the {{YearlinkName|720}},<ref name="RTR-p15-17">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|15-17}}</ref> a large congregation of [[dryad]]s arrived at the [[Dancing Place]] druid grove in [[High Dale]].<ref name="GHR-p99" /> Their arrival occurred at a time when dusk fell earlier than it should have and a bright moon shone when no moon should have been visible. The dryads bid the druids welcome the priests of many different gods who started to arrive before finally Elminster appeared to explain why they had all been called.<ref name="CotH-p21"/><ref name="FMD-p19" />

The elves had called for the support of the priests assembled to help fight back against the faithful of [[Bane]], [[Bhaal]], [[Loviatar]], [[Malar]], and [[Myrkul]], who were coming from the south and attacking the elves and performing abhorrent deeds.<ref name="CotH-p21"/> The priests argued but their deities—[[Corellon]], [[Mielikki]], Mystra, [[Oghma]], [[Selûne]], [[Silvanus]], and Tymora—directly possessed them and spoke through them, voicing their support in person and extending their blessings to this effort.<ref name="DrM367-p7"/><ref name="CotH-p22"/><ref name="FRCG-p111">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|111}}</ref> This night thus became known as the ''[[Gathering of the Gods]]''.<ref name="CotH-p22"/>

The remaining Harpers-at-Twilight set about recruiting new members and expanding their influence, though they did so very slowly and lost around twenty new recruits to conflict with their enemies.<ref name="CotH-p22"/> Over that time though, these new Harpers established an incredibly effective information network, and earned the respect of religious leaders by using that network to help their causes. In return, they were granted the use of fortified temples and [[monasteries]] wherein members could train and recuperate between missions.<ref name="CotH-p23">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|23}}</ref> 

===Long Years===
{{Quote|To be a Harper is to sacrifice a part of yourself for the greater good―to be the pebble in the charger's path, the knife that frays the hangman's rope, the barking dog that draws the hunter's eye. We do not act for glory, but for the good of all.'|Anonymous.<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" />}}
The next few centuries were known as the "Long Years" within the organization.<ref name="CotH-p22"/> During late 9{{th}} century, in the {{YearlinkName|886}},<ref name="GHR-p109">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|109}}</ref> the Harpers erected the wards around [[Hellgate Keep]] to prevent [[fiend]]s from overwhelming the surrounding lands.<ref name="HK-p8">{{Cite book/Hellgate Keep|8}}</ref><ref name="MCI-p105">{{Cite book/Menzoberranzan: City of Intrigue|105}}</ref><ref name="RoF-p123">{{Cite book/Races of Faerûn|123}}</ref> While the protections unfortunately lasted for only a few years,<ref name="LEoF-p84-85">{{Cite book/Lost Empires of Faerûn|84-85}}</ref> the Harpers spread rumors that attracted local adventuring groups to help slay the fiends that made it out of the ruins.<ref name="VGN-p212">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the North|212}}</ref>

Some decades later, in the {{YearlinkName|916}}, Harpers worked with [[Church of Lathander|clerics of Lathander]] to slay [[Sammaster]], founder of the Cult of the Dragon.<ref name="GHR-p109">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|109}}</ref><ref name="CotD-p18-19">{{Cite book/Cult of the Dragon|18-19}}</ref><ref name="VGATM-p96">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to All Things Magical|96}}</ref><ref name="VGD-p231">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the Dalelands|231}}</ref> The Harpers' increasingly public actions brought them into more frequent conflict with the church of Bane and the nation of [[Thay]].<ref name="CotH-p23"/><ref name="FRCS3e-p269-270" />

In the {{YearlinkName|996}},<ref name="GHR-p113">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|113}}</ref><ref group="note" name="992 DR">''[[Power of Faerûn]]'' and ''[[The Grand History of the Realms]]'' both state this occurred in [[992 DR]].</ref> the Harpers founded the [[Heralds of Faerûn]] to prevent the unscrupulous from blaming others for their misdeeds. The Heralds also used their power to provide Harpers with cover identities.<ref name="CotH-p23"/>

When the Harpers eradicated the [[Wearers of the Skull]] and drew the ire of the [[church of Myrkul]], the Lord of the Dead's followers dispatched [[lich]]es to seek out and assail the Harpers.<ref name="SB-p127">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|127}}</ref> The undead spellcasters were turned away and subsequently destroyed. The church then raised armies to hunt down them and the remaining Harper agents. The Harpers were finally forced to go underground in the {{YearlinkName|1021}}.<ref name="CotH-p23"/><ref name="RTR-p15-17" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p269-270" /><ref group="note" name="Myrkul/Thay">''Code of the Harpers'' makes no reference to Thay being the source of the army but rather intimates that it was also the [[Church of Myrkul]]. ''[[Spellbound]]'' and ''[[The Grand History of the Realms]]'' explicitly states Thay was responsible.</ref>

===Second Reformation===
After the loss of many members during the previous year, Elminster and Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun decided in the {{YearlinkName|1022}}, that new recruits were needed and that the revitalized Harpers would be an "underground army of adventurers".<ref name="CotH-p24">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|24}}</ref><ref name="RTR-p15-17" /><ref name="FRCS3e-p269-270" /><ref name="GHR-p115">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|115}}</ref> Recruits who met the pair's standards took time to be found, but eventually Elminster happened upon the [[Wanderers of Espar]], a band of a dozen [[bard]]s, druids, and rangers led by [[Finder Wyvernspur]] and [[Ulzund Hawkshield]] in [[Cormyr]]. Through a series of manipulations, these adventurers were introduced to surviving Harper veterans and ended up successfully battling many of the Harpers' enemies.<ref name="CotH-p24"/>

The fates aided the Harpers in these efforts in the {{YearlinkName|1065}}, when they acquired significant treasure after successfully delving into the lair of the undead dragon [[Alglaudyx]], and destroyed the mighty [[dracolich]]. They used their newfound riches to make wise investments, purchasing properties and buying up businesses in cities all along the Sword Coast.<ref name="CotD-p26">{{Cite book/Cult of the Dragon|26}}</ref><ref name="GHR-p117">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|117}}</ref>

The group continued to be quietly manipulated by Elminster and Khelben, who appointed certain members as "Master Harpers". These Harpers received directions from the duo, via dreams bestowed on them by Mystra, that led them to a cache of ''[[Harper pin]]s'' in [[Gochall's Tomb]]. Elminster then traveled across the Realms with two of Mystra's children, the sisters [[Dove Falconhand|Dove]] and Storm Silverhand, to bolster the ranks of the Harpers. They posed as traveling minstrels, cultivated an air of mystique about the order, and attracted new members with their music.<ref name="CotH-p26">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|26}}</ref>

Over time, the Harpers re-established their information network but were drawn into ever more public fights with evil churches, the Cult of the Dragon,<ref name="BoL-p35">{{Cite book/Book of Lairs|35}}</ref> and the nation of Thay, and other malign forces.<ref name="CotH-p28">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|28}}</ref> Casualties began to mount again and, to avoid a repeat of history, Khelben and Elminster had all senior Harpers go into hiding.<ref name="CotH-p30">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|30}}</ref> 

At some point, the legendary Harper bard Finder Wyvernspur was corrupted by his own vanity and hunger for artistic immortality. The "Nameless Bard," as he was known, was put on trial before the Harpers, who found him guilty of [[Harpers#Harper Code|allowing his pride to bring harm to others]] and imprisoned him within the [[Citadel of White Exile]].<ref name="CotH-p28"/><ref name="CAB-p80">{{Cite book/Curse of the Azure Bonds|80}}</ref><ref name="HoH-p97">{{Cite book/Hall of Heroes|97}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|1116}}, as the Harpers had begun more openly fighting using blade and spell rather than whisper and rumor, the Heralds decided they could no longer be openly associated with Those Who Harp. The High Heralds argued that their role must be seen as peaceful and neutral; they could not retain the trust of rulers and common folk across Faerûn if they were seen as part of just another power group, striving to make its own way in the Realms, meddling in the affairs of others. (A view reflected in the folk saying "Wound a Herald and ye'll find a bleeding Harper.") Khelben and Elminster regretfully knew they were right, and so in order to maintain the neutrality needed to perform their mission the Heralds cordially split from the Harpers, while retaining strong ties between the two groups; Harpers would still perform legwork as information-gathers for the Heralds, and Herald strongholds would always be open sanctuary to any Harper in need.<ref name="RTR-p15-17" /><ref name="CotH-p28"/><ref name="GHR-p119">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|119}}</ref><ref name="PoF-p105">{{Cite book/Power of Faerûn|105}}</ref>

Meanwhile, junior members grew tired of the new, low-key direction the group was headed in and started getting themselves killed in foolish fights. In the {{YearlinkName|1182}}, Elminster gave them a unified direction—by pulling them into his ongoing conflict with malaugrym, horrific shapechangers native to the [[Plane of Shadow]].<ref name="FRCS3e-p277">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|277}}</ref> The Harpers' ongoing secret war with the malevolent [[outsider]]s came to be known as the [[Harpstar Wars]].<ref name="CotH-p30"/> Only around forty of the Harpers involved in the war survived it and when they returned, they found their organization had strayed in a completely different direction.<ref name="CotH-p31">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|31}}</ref><ref name="GHR-p123">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|123}}</ref>

===Corruption of the Harper King===
A Harper bard named [[Rundorl Moonsklan]] had convinced himself that Elminster, Khelben, and the Harpers they'd taken with them during the Harpstar Wars had gotten themselves killed fighting on other [[planes]] and that senior Harpers in hiding had actually permanently retired. His ambition, therefore, was to replace the organization's leadership and reshape it to his own ends. He desired to be the power behind every throne in the North and while planning how to achieve this he happened to meet one [[Szass Tam]], a Red Wizard of Thay.<ref name="CotH-p31"/>

The two came to an agreement: Szass Tam would funnel Rundorl information on his rivals in Thay and Rundorl would gain prestige by sending Harpers to win victories over them. To justify this focus on Thay, a land previously neglected by the Harpers, Rundorl spread the story of "the spell of Undeath". According to information he'd 'discovered', the Red Wizards were developing a new spell capable of instantly turning the populations of whole cities into undead slaves with a single casting to quickly produce armies and threaten and enslave all of [[Faerûn]]. According to Rundorl, the only things stopping them were some issues in implementation—and the Harpers. The plan went perfectly, Rundorl led and sent his fellows into battle against those Red Wizards and even [[zulkir]]s whom he claimed had involvement in "the spell of Undeath" and both he and Tam advanced in power.<ref name="CotD-p57" /><ref name="CotH-p32">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|32}}</ref>

Eventually, though, more Harpers were dying than Thayans and Rundorl led a reluctant retreat out of Thay. His exhausted men were being assassinated and reanimated as undead, however, and Rundorl rightly suspected that Tam had betrayed him. Rundorl broke off the alliance, then appealed to another lich named [[Thavverdasz]]. He promised the Harpers' services in return for his help defeating his reanimated comrades. Thavverdasz agreed, but then dominated Rundorl's mind and read his thoughts, mockingly took for himself the name "Harper King" after learning of Rundorl's ambition, and made the man his undead minion. The undead Harpers were easily wrested from Tam's control but Thavverdasz betrayed his other allies, the Cult of the Dragon.<ref name="CotH-p32"/>

It was this situation that the surviving Harpstar veterans encountered when they returned to Faerûn in the {{YearlinkName|1222}}. The Cult of the Dragon had raised an army and sent it against Thavverdasz's Harpers while Szass Tam challenged the Harper King directly. Thavverdasz used a powerful magic item to defeat Tam, but Elminster surprised and assassinated the Harper King shortly thereafter.<ref name="CotH-p33">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|33}}</ref><ref name="CotD-p26" /><ref name="GHR-p125">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|125}}</ref>

===Third Reformation===
Grimly, Storm and Dove set about replacing their massive losses while Khelben and the remaining veterans licked their wounds.<ref group="note" name="Third Reformation">According to ''[[Hall of Heroes]]'' and ''[[The Seven Sisters (sourcebook)|The Seven Sisters]]'', Storm helped Syluné found the Harpers in the 13{{th}} century DR.</ref> Elminster pulled strings among the [[Wychlaran]] in [[Rashemen]] to keep the Thayans busy and turned the lair of the Harper King into a deathtrap to destroy the inevitable Cult of the Dragon reinforcements. Luckily, the Harper information network remained intact and largely ignorant of the organization's recent corruption.<ref name="CotH-p33"/>

In the {{YearlinkName|1230}}, the Harpers helped defend the great city of Silverymoon, when it was threatened by a rampaging hoard of orcs.<ref name="LEoF-p84-85" /> An apprentice of the city's High Mage by the name of [[Sepur]] was appointed as Silvermoon's new ruler, but only served for two years before abandoning its people. [[1235 DR|Five years]] after Sepur's ascension, the [[Black Horde]] of orcs assailed the city, while warlords and mages vied for rule over the city. Master Harpers Storm and [[Alustriel Silverhand]] led the Harpers in a [[Battle of Tumbleskulls|second battle]] in defense of Silverymoon and emerged as a heroes of the city. Alustriel was elected by the Silvaeren people to rule as High Lady Mage.<ref name="SM-p57">{{Cite book/Silver Marches|57}}</ref><ref name="GHR-p125-126">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|125-126}}</ref>

In gratitude for the Harpers help in defending the city, Storm Silverhand was granted permission to build Moongleam Tower in Everlund. It was around that time that Storm took on leadership of the Harpers' [[Harpers#Branches|eastern branch]].<ref name="CotH-p34"/>

{{Fq|text=Over long years, the Zhentarim became the main opposition for the Harpers, corrupting merchants and minor nobles across the greater Heartlands and becoming the prime force in the Moonsea.|float=left|source=[[Asgir Lefrenn]], Underscribe of Candlekeep.<ref name="DrM415.HCMDM-p1-2">{{Cite dragon/415/History Check: The Many Deaths of Manshoon|1-2}}</ref>}}
It was in the {{YearlinkName|1269}}, that the Harpers first received intelligence about an emerging threat in [[North Faerûn|northern Faerûn]]—the Zhentarim. Over the course of the next century, the Harpers and the Zhentarim would all but wage war on each other. Each faction seemed to gain an edge for a time, before the other struck back once again.<ref name="RZK.CB-p37">{{Cite book/Ruins of Zhentil Keep/Campaign Book|37}}</ref> While Khelben took over as leader of the Harpers on the Sword Coast,<ref name="GHR-p137" /> Elminster was left to counter the Zhentarim threat alone, engaging in a long-standing feud with [[Manshoon]].<ref name="CotH-p33"/> 

However, in the {{YearlinkName|1321}}, Khelben sponsored the Harpstar veteran [[Cylyria Dragonbreast]] in her bid to become High Lady of Berdusk,<ref name="GHR-p137">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|137}}</ref> and gave over to her leadership of the western branch of the Harpers. The Harpers of Twilight Hall, as they were colloquially known, acted openly against the Zhentarim and [[Amn]]ian interests and even came close to crippling the Rundeen.<ref name="CotH-p34"/> While these actions brought on open hostilities against the city of Berdusk, they also attracted many folks to join the Harpers organization, swelling its ranks with new members.<ref name="CotH-p36">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|36}}</ref> 

In the {{YearlinkName|1349}}, Harper agents managed to put an end to the Rundeen's financial stranglehold over [[Chult]]. They disrupted the organization's major operations in Calimshan and killed three of their leaders.<ref name="SK-p127">{{Cite book/Serpent Kingdoms|127}}</ref><ref name="GHR-p140">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|140}}</ref>

They suffered tremendous loss a few decades later, however. In the {{YearlinkName|1356}}, [[Syluné Silverhand]]―eldest of the [[Seven Sisters]]―and several Harpers were slain when a [[flight of dragons]] was called down upon Faerûn.<ref name="CotD-p57">{{Cite book/Cult of the Dragon|57}}</ref> But her [[spirit]] persisted in the mortal world as one of the group's famed [[spectral harpist]]s, and would appear when summoned to certain Harpers in times of peril.<ref name="WPR-p9-13" /><ref name="SS-p48">{{Cite book/The Seven Sisters|48}}</ref><ref name="HL-p120-121">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|120-121}}</ref>

===Early Era of Upheaval===
[[File:Harpers banner.png|thumb|right|130px|''The Harpers of Faerûn have lasted the test of time''.]]
{{Quote|Tis more important that the Shadowmasters have no benefit from what I've left undone than that the Harpers or Shandril―or Toril itself―gain strength by what we do.|Elminster the Sage.<ref name="SS-p42">{{Cite book/The Seven Sisters|42}}</ref>}}
In the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, the [[Avatar Crisis]] struck Faerûn when the gods above were forced to walk the Realms as mortals. Amidst the numerous conflicts and catastrophes, the Harpers fought to defend Shadowdale against the invading armies of the [[Zhentilar]], particularly in the [[Battle of Swords Creek]].<ref name="S.Adv-p34">{{Cite book/Shadowdale (adventure)|34}}</ref><ref name="F&P-p153">{{Cite book/Faiths and Pantheons|153}}</ref> Elminster and the spectral Syluné led a band of Harpers in a series of skirmishes against the [[Shadowmaster]] and his sect of the malaugrym.<ref name="HL-p120-121" /> These events however led to many deaths among the ranks of the Harpers. The aftermath of the crisis saw the return of Finder Wyvernspur to the Realms and his subsequent rise to godhood. It also led to much disruption among the Zhentarim, which brought enough of a reprieve for the Harpers to replace their losses.<ref name="CotH-p36"/>

Over the following decade, the Harpers engaged the Zhentarim in skirmishes across Faerûn. They fought an open war against the Zhents in the [[Sunset Vale]],<ref name="VGSC-p147">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the Sword Coast|147}}</ref> and helped unite the [[Bedine]] people against Black Network agents in the oases of the [[Anauroch]] desert.<ref name="PS-p?">{{Cite book/The Parched Sea|?}}</ref><ref name="GHR-p145">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|145}}</ref> A [[1361 DR|few years later]], Harper agents were systematically tracked down and slain by an individual known as the "Harper Assassin". In time, the Harpers discovered the deaths were the work of a [[Kymil Nimesin|disgruntled sun elf]] who had been working in league with the Zhentarim.<ref name="Elfshadow-p?">{{Cite book/Elfshadow/Paperback 1991}}</ref><ref name="HL-p18">{{Cite book/Heroes' Lorebook|18}}</ref> Conflicts between the two groups broke out in cities across the Heartlands, from [[Iriaebor]]<ref name="CoS-p?">{{Cite book/Crypt of the Shadowking}}</ref> to [[Hluthvar]] and even [[Baldur's Gate]].<ref name="CotH-p53" />

While the Harpers remained engaged in conflict with nefarious groups, it was observed by some they had been failing in their role as lore-keepers for quite some time. In the {{YearlinkName|1364}}, the disgruntled former Harper [[Iriador Wintermist]]―who subscribed to this belief―used a [[Morninglark|powerful magical instrument]] to curse the Harpers, altering the tales they told and ballads they sang. She focused her vengeance against the city of Waterdeep and its archmage, Khelben Arunsun. Fortunately for the storytellers and balladeers of Faerûn, her plot was foiled by Khelben and his Harper apprentice [[Danilo Thann]].<ref name="Elfsong-p?">{{Cite book/Elfsong (1994)|?}}</ref> As misguided as it was, Iriador's message was received in earnest by the people of Waterdeep. Soon after these events they refounded the bardic college of [[New Olamn]] in the City of Splendors.<ref name="CoS.W-p29">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|29}}</ref>

That same year the Harpers were forced to face off with the Cult of the Dragon yet again, in a series of engagements that culminated in a massive battle in the city of Elversult.<ref name="VD-p?">{{Cite book/The Veiled Dragon/Paperback (1996)}}</ref><ref name="CotD-p24">{{Cite book/Cult of the Dragon|24}}</ref> They struck out against the cult a few years later, working to prevent the cult from establishing [[Alasklerbanbastos|a dracolich]] as the monarch of the politically unstable nation of [[Chessenta]].<ref name="CotH-p37">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|37}}</ref> 

With the downfall of Bane and the rise of [[Cyricism]]―and its subsequent entanglement with the Zhentarim―new foes developed evil plots to which the Harpers could not ignore. Harper agents acted against the followers of the Dark Sun as they sought after murderous artifacts on the Sword Coast,<ref name="Thornhold">{{Cite book/Thornhold}}</ref> and chased down [[Malik el Sami|Cyric's chosen]] in order to bring him to justice.<ref name="Crucible-p168-173">{{Cite book/Crucible|168-173}}</ref><ref name="DrM.245-p65-68">{{Cite dragon/245/Rogues Gallery: The Heroes of the Trial of Cyric the Mad|65-68}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|1369}}, the Harpers were able to put an end to a major threat that imperiled much of the North. Their members utilized powerful magic to destroy Hellgate Keep, and send back to the Hells nearly all the [[baatezu]] that remained within.<ref name="HK-p7">{{Cite book/Hellgate Keep|7}}</ref><ref name="FRCS3e-p166">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|166}}</ref><ref name="LEoF-p90">{{Cite book/Lost Empires of Faerûn|90}}</ref><ref name="SM-p7">{{Cite book/Silver Marches|7}}</ref><ref name="GHR-p149">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|149}}</ref><ref group="note" name="Hellgate Keep">While the ''[[Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition]]'' states this occurred in [[1368 DR]], ''[[Lost Empires of Faerûn]]'', ''[[Silver Marches (sourcebook)|Silver Marches]]'', the blurb for the [[Hellgate Keep (adventure)|Hellgate Keep adventure]], and ''[[The Grand History of the Realms]]'' all state this occurred in [[1369 DR]].</ref>

===Harper Schism===
{{Fq|text=My most recent quarrel with the good archmage is this: He persists in regarding the restored bard college as a sort of stud barn established for the sole purpose of breeding his new brand of Harper. Since it would appear that he has had some success in this endeavor and since "Harper" and "bard" are words so often spoken in one breath―I will address the Harper schism before moving on to such trifles as music, magic, and scholarship.|source=[[Danilo Thann]], in a letter to Storm Silverhand, speaking of Khelben and the Moonstars.<ref name="DrM335.WSC-p22-32">{{Cite dragon/335/New Olamn Bard College|22-32}}</ref>}}
The Harpers were not safe from strife, however. In the {{YearlinkName|1370}}, investigators discovered evidence that implicated Khelben in the theft of an [[artifact]], the ''[[Scepter of the Sorcerer-Kings]]'', which had then found its way into the hands of the Zhentarim. Khelben admitted to all of the charges set against him. Khelben and Laeral both left the Harpers,<ref name="CaD-p22"/><ref name="CoS.W-p76">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|76}}</ref><ref name="GHR-p150">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|150}}</ref><ref name=SCAG-p123-124>{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|123-124}}</ref> along with Alustriel, who felt she could not uphold her commitment to the group, as High Lady of the [[Silver Marches]].<ref name="CaD-p22">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|22}}</ref> and many of Khelben's closest friends and allies, founding their own group: the Moonstars.<ref name=SCAG-p123-124 /><ref name="DrM335.WSC-p22-32" />

The government of Waterdeep refused to help the Harpers of Twilight Hall prosecute Khelben, so the Harpers proceeded to ward their stores against Khelben and Laeral, while Khelben erected a ward that prevented any Harper from approaching [[Blackstaff Tower]]. Influenced by [[Bran Skorlsun]], the Harpers of Twilight Hall went on a witch hunt to purge their ranks of perceived traitors while Storm's eastern branch tried to carry on with their business regardless of events in the west.<ref name="CaD-p22"/>

When the church of Bane reformed with the Zhentarim—following the wane of Cyricism and the [[return of Bane]] to godhood―the Harpers were once again targeted by agents of their longtime foes.<ref name="FRCS3e-p274" /> 

The Harpers were overwhelmed as a whole during the [[Wailing Years]], a series of disasters that followed the [[Spellplague]] that wracked the Realms in the {{YearlinkName|1385}}. Many Harpers died while others vanished. Those who survived were cut off from one another, so they focused solely on the perils that menaced their homes and neighbors. While the original organization was divided and its members scattered across the continent, the hope of returning the Harpers to their former glory didn't die.<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" />

===Fourth Reformation===
{{Quote|Yet I very much doubt it will surprise any of you to learn that the Harpers did not, in fact, cease to exist. An extremely secretive, underground fellowship of some two dozen Harpers continued―as they do to this very day.|[[Oskrul Meddanthyr]], [[1479 DR]].<ref name="EMD.chp25">{{Cite book/Elminster Must Die/Hardcover||25}}</ref>}}
One bastion of Harper strength remained, however: Moongleam Tower. As of the {{YearlinkName|1419}}, [[Eaerlraun Shadowlyn]] re-founded the group as the "Harpers of Luruar" to secretly counter the forces of returned [[Netheril]], hoping to keep the Harper ideals alive.<ref name="FRCG-p146"/> Following their example, other Harper cells formed throughout the Realms. Some of these groups were collectives that adopted the Harpers' name and traditional ideology, while others comprised former Harpers that had survived the collapse and had gone underground before returning to action.<ref name="NWCS-p23-25" /><ref name=DU188-p55>{{Cite dungeon/188/Whispered Words|55}}</ref> Notable among the latter were the "Harpers of Waterdeep" and Storm Silverhand's "Harpers of Cormyr and the Dales", which many felt were the spiritual successors of the original organization.<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" />

Several other groups who called themselves Harpers appeared in the following years. The "Harpers of [[Athkatla]]" worked against the [[Council of Five]] and other Amnian authorities, but although they claimed to share the ideals of the Harpers of old, they did not and merely claimed to in order to gain support. The "Harpers of Selgaunt" and the "Harpers of Ormpur" were also rebels who did not really cleave to Harper ideals.<ref name=DU188-p55/> These new Harpers brought with them enemies of their own, such as the [[Abolethic Sovereignty]], Szass Tam's Thay, the Zhentarim, and the [[Warlock Knights]] of [[Vaasa]].<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" />

At some point, Harpers seem to have acquired or stolen{{speculation}} the ''[[horn of beckoning death]]'' that had been used by Szass Tam to turn much of the population of Thay into undead servants some years before<ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c3" />—not unlike the false "spell of Undeath" story spread by Rundorl Moonsklan before the rise of the Harper King.<ref name="CotH-p32" /> They stored it within the vault of Korinn's Keep, far from the lich's reach.<ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c3" /><ref group="note">From ''Honor Among Thieves'', it is unknown how the horn managed to pass from Szass Tam's possession and into the Harpers', and when this occurred. Most likely, they stole it to prevent him using it again. While speculative, the event is included here for context.</ref>

By the {{YearlinkName|1475}}, the "Harpers of Luruar" had managed to cripple the efforts of the Netherese in significant ways.<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" /> Unfortunately, Eaerlraun was killed by shadovar assassins and the Harpers were forced to act in complete secrecy to prevent the loss of more members.<ref name="FRCG-p146"/> 

Within [[1479 DR|four years]], the Harpers of Waterdeep were led by a Harper agent known as [[Aron Vishter|Aron "the Fisher" Vishter]], who was actually a double agent working for the Zhentarim to undermine Harper operations. Although [[Tam Zawad]] and other Harpers were able to bring the Fisher to justice, he did great damage to the Harpers of Waterdeep. Even after [[1486 DR|seven years]], Zawad was still dealing with corrupt Harpers within his Harper cell.<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" />

The Harpers of Neverwinter also suffered a devastating blow that same year. The group's leader [[Cymril]] was in the middle of aiding the [[Sons of Alagondar]] openly oppose the rule of [[Dagult Neverember]]. Cymril was slain when her group was ambushed by [[Mintarn Mercenaries]]. After the attack, it was revealed that she was also working for Neverember.<ref name="NWCS-p23-25">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|23-25}}</ref> After her treachery was revealed, the reputation of the Harpers of Neverwinter significantly tarnished for some time.<ref name="NWCS-p119">{{Cite book/Neverwinter Campaign Setting|119}}</ref>

In the time of [[Mystra's Return]], in the {{YearlinkName|1479}},<ref name="EE-p387">{{Cite book/Elminster Enraged/MMP|387}}</ref> Storm Silverhand began to revitalize the Harper organization in Cormyr. She sought out people from all walks of life: smiths, merchants, leather workers, and even those who worked in brothels. Her revitalizing actions were known and tolerated by the [[War Wizards]] of Cormyr.<ref name="EE-p176">{{Cite book/Elminster Enraged/MMP|176}}</ref> The Harpers prevented the assassination of King's Lord [[Lothan Durncaskyn]] who was sheltering [[Mirt]]'s lady, [[Rensharra Ironstave]].<ref name="EE-p360-361">{{Cite book/Elminster Enraged/MMP|360-361}}</ref> Since then, the Harpers became an active organization across the Realms again.<ref>{{Cite web/Factions: The Harpers}}</ref>

===Second Sundering Era===
In the years following the [[Second Sundering]]—which occurred over the course of the 1480s DR―the Harpers bolstered their numbers, recovered their resources, and once again embarked upon secret missions to better the Realms.<ref name="DiT-p3">{{Cite book/Dead in Thay|3}}</ref> The network of spies and adventurers held no leadership as before, but remained scattered across the breadth of Faerûn. They focused their efforts on their long-standing mission to oppose tyrants and other purveyors of injustice.<ref name="DIP-p9">{{Cite book/Dungeons & Dragons Essentials Kit/Dragon of Icespire Peak|9}}</ref>

The Harpers in the Moonsea region began investigating a series of raids and kidnappings in Hillsfar and the surrounding farmlands.<ref name="SoB-p8">{{Cite adventurers league/Shackles of Blood|8}}</ref> They successfully helped foment rebellion in the city, to overthrow its cunning First Lord, [[Torin Nomerthal]].<ref name="DotW-p26">{{Cite adventurers league/Death on the Wall|26}}</ref> Meanwhile, in Hillsfar, they became aware of strange happenings in the Underdark.<ref name="HotM-p8">{{Cite adventurers league/Herald of the Moon|8}}</ref> They helped forge an alliance between the people of Hillsfar and the drow of [[Szith Morcane]] to meet the emergent danger of [[Graz'zt]] and other prominent [[demon lord]]s, all appearing on Toril at once.<ref name="AM-p80">{{Cite adventurers league/Assault on Maerimydra|80}}</ref>

Yet other agents began looking into rumors circulating about a new iteration of the old Cult of the Dragon.<ref name="RP-p68">{{Cite adventurers league/Reclamation of Phlan|68}}</ref> Rather than worshiping dracoliches, the new dragon cult devoted itself to [[Tiamat]], the evil Queen of Dragons. When the cultists attempted to bring their goddess to the [[Material Plane]], in the 1480s DR, the Harpers were among the many factions who rose up to oppose Tiamat's attempt at freedom. Their agents tracked cultist activity across the Sword Coast,<ref name="HDQ-p13-14">{{Cite book/Hoard of the Dragon Queen|13-14}}</ref> gathered leaders of the free cities in the [[Council of Waterdeep]],<ref name="RoT-p20" /> and fought alongside their allies in the final assault of the [[Well of Dragons]] fortress.<ref name="SKT-p12" /><ref name="N.ToD">{{Cite game/Neverwinter: Tyranny of Dragons}}</ref>

During their involvement with the Dragon Cult's schemes, the Harpers began to keep a closer eye on its strongest allies: the Red Wizards of Thay. The Red Wizards had made strong inroads into the North and the Sword Coast during their alliance with the Cult.<ref name="RoT-p76">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|76}}</ref> On one mission in the mid–1480s DR,{{HAT-time}} a trio of Harpers captured a Red Wizard in his treasure-stuffed lair. Among them was the recent recruit [[Edgin Darvis]],<ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c2" /> who stole a [[gold]] token, little thinking it might be magically marked. By tracing that gold,<ref name="HAT" /> four Red Wizards rode into [[Targos]] seeking revenge and murdered Edgin's wife [[Zia]]. Afterward, Edgin blamed himself, abandoned his oath to the Harpers, and burned his [[book]] of the Harper Code upon Zia's funeral pyre.<ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c2" />

Some years later, the cults of [[Elemental Evil]] arose in the [[Dessarin Valley]] and the [[Moonsea]], and were a threat to the people in and around those lands. The Harpers were among the factions of the west that acted against the cults, working against the [[Cult of the Black Earth]] in Mulmaster and their activities in nearby mines.<ref name="MEM-p12">{{Cite adventurers league/Mayhem in the Earthspur Mines|12}}</ref><ref name="BHV-p17,21" />

When the [[Ordning (social structure)|ordning]] of [[giant]]-kind was abolished in the latter half of the 1480s DR and giants of all kinds ran amok in the North, the Harpers went on alert to protect the towns and cities they called home.<ref name="SKT-p12" />

Some years later, the [[death curse]] struck the Realms, and [[divine magic]] could no longer bring back the [[soul]]s of the recently deceased and restore their bodies to life. Around that time, the Harpers of [[Port Nyanzaru]] in Chult worked alongside the [[Ytepka Society]] to uncover a hidden foe within their city.<ref name="CE-p3">{{Cite adventurers league/A City on the Edge|3}}</ref> Working with other groups, they established a base of operations in the jungles of Chult and began to investigate a possible source of the curse.<ref name="OE-p26">{{Cite adventurers league/Over the Edge|26}}</ref><ref name="RR-p3">{{Cite adventurers league/Rotting Roots|3}}</ref> They finally determined the curse originated from an unholy and otherworldly artifact known as the ''[[Soulmonger]]'', hidden deep within the jungles of Chult.<ref name="ToA-p5">{{Cite book/Tomb of Annihilation|5}}</ref>

They once again took actions against the ever-meddling Red Wizards of Thay, who had uncovered an [[Xorvintroth|ancient Abeiran city]] that was infused with the dark energies of the [[Far Realm]]. The Harpers set up a secluded safehouse within the city's ruins, and collected intelligence by spying on the malign spellcasters from Thay.<ref name="CSNB-p4">{{Cite adventurers league/The City That Should Not Be|4}}</ref><ref name="Upr-p2,5">{{Cite adventurers league/Uprising|2,5}}</ref><ref name="FW-p9-10">{{Cite adventurers league/Frozen Whispers|9-10}}</ref> Harper agents successfully freed the many slaves the Red Wizards used to uncover the lost city, and uncovered their plot to take control of the [[Supreme Forge]] within.<ref name="Upr-p2,5" /><ref name="Upr-p22">{{Cite adventurers league/Uprising|22}}</ref>

[[File:Korinn's Keep break-in.png|thumb|right|350px|''"To be honest, there were times when I began to question the 'ask nothing in return' part of my Harpers' oath."<br/> — Edgin Darvis'']]
In the mid-1490s DR,{{HAT-time}} ex-Harper turned thief Edgin Darvis betrayed the order by leading a band of thieves, including himself, [[Holga Kilgore]], [[Simon Aumar]], [[Forge Fitzwilliam]], and [[Sofina]], to Korinn's Keep to steal its reaches. For this robbery, he donned his old Harper uniform and ''Harper pin''. They triggered an alarm and fought with Harper guards, but Sofina betrayed the thieves and escaped with her prize—Szass Tam's ''horn of beckoning death''. Edgin and Holga were left at the scene of the crime and captured by the Harpers, convicted of crimes of skullduggery and grand larceny by the justice of the Lords' Alliance, and incarcerated in the [[Revel's End]] prison.<ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c3" /> Two years later, Sofina would be revealed as a Red Wizard of Thay and servant of Szass Tam, and used the ''horn of beckoning death'' in an attempt to transform Neverwinter's population into undead servants of Szass Tam, but was thankfully foiled by Edgin and his thieves.<ref name="HAT" />

==Members==
[[File:Harper Recruiter.png|thumb|left|350px|''A Harper agent recruiting new members.'']]
{{Quote|What makes a Harper? Well, different folk'll tell ye a lot of high and grand things, but t'me it seems the only crucial talent―the one they all have―is the ability to turn up at the wrong time in a place where they're neither wanted nor expected, and plunge right into whatever trouble's afoot...|[[Darblin Melorinrose]].<ref name="CotH-p3-6" />}}
While the Harpers regularly sponsored adventurers looking to prove themselves, the group maintained high standards for accepting full-fledged members.<ref name="CotH-p121-122">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|121-122}}</ref> Admittance into the Harpers was more often initiated by the group rather than the individual,<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" /> requiring sponsorship by a member in good standing,<ref name="FRA-p123-124" /><ref name="DrM170.EBFR-p96-100">{{Cite dragon/170/Endings and Beginnings: the Forgotten Realms series|96-100}}</ref> and approval of the High Harpers.<ref name="FRCS-p46-47" /><ref name="CoS.W-p73" /><ref name="MoF-p28-31" /> Recruits would be assigned a first mission, with silent accompaniment by a senior member, who did not make their presence known to the prospective agent.<ref name="WPR-p9-13" /><ref name="CotH-p121-122" />

Members could quit the Harpers and foreswear the Code if they wished, such as after a loss of faith in its ideals,<ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c2" /> though the organization still kept tabs on ex-members, as they would under the Code.<ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c16" /> A career in the Harpers was never expected to last long, however, often being cut short by retirement or a more unfortunate fate. It was not unheard of for dying Harpers to bestow their final, incomplete quest upon a trusted ally or new friend. If that ally successfully carried out the dead agent's last quest, they were brought into the Harpers as a full member in good standing, without argument or trial.<ref name="CotH-p121-122" />

Membership could be revoked from any member if they violated the Harper's code, purposefully or negligently endangered a fellow agent, or willfully turned away from the organization. Readmittance into the Harpers was possible if an individual personally petitioned the High Harpers and agreed to undertake a quest of great importance, as well as completed some manner of [[atonement|divine atonement]].<ref name="FRCS-p46-47">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|46-47}}</ref><ref name="PGtF-p59">{{Cite book/Player's Guide to Faerûn|59}}</ref>
{{quote|You may have foresworn your oath, but your oath hasn't foresworn you.|Harper ally [[Xenk Yendar]] to ex-Harper Edgin Darvis<ref name="HAT" /><ref name="TJN-c16" />}}

===Demographics===
[[File:5eR valor bard - Ignatius Budi.png|thumb|''A dragonborn bard of the valorous Harpers.'']]
Harper adventurers came from many walks of life.<ref name="DrM430.HW-p33-38" /> They held nearly every profession, though more than a few lived as merchants or [[courtesan (prostitute)|courtesans]],<ref name="CotH-p8-11" /> and served at any age, young or old. Harpers could be of any [[gender and sex|sex or gender]],<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /> and of any race.<ref name="NWCS-p23-25" /> It was noted that more women than others were found among the ranks of the Harpers. Humans, elves, and [[half-elves]] were the most commonly represented races, but some [[gnome]]s, [[halfling]]s, and [[dwarves]], and even woodland folk like [[dryad]]s and [[centaur]]s also joined.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /> 

Most members of the Harpers lived their lives as resourceful [[ranger]]s<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /><ref name="CAB-p9">{{Cite book/Curse of the Azure Bonds|9}}</ref><ref name="PoF-p114">{{Cite book/Power of Faerûn|114}}</ref> or captivating [[bards]],<ref name="SF-p5">{{Cite book/The Savage Frontier|5}}</ref>.<ref name="CoV-p9">{{Cite book/Champions of Valor|9}}</ref> though some dashing [[rogue]]s,<ref name="FRA-p123-124" /> mighty [[wizard]]s,<ref name="RoT-p11-12" /><ref name="WDH-p16">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|16}}</ref> stalwart [[fighter]]s and [[cleric]]s of benevolent deities served as well.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" /><ref name="WPR-p9-13" /> Throughout the Harpers' history, numerous [[spellsinger]]s had also joined its ranks. These unique [[spellcaster]]s had the power to tap into the Weave through song or dance.<ref name="CotH-p100">{{Cite book/The Code of the Harpers|100}}</ref>

Priests could follow any faith; [[Azuth]], [[Deneir]], [[Eldath]], [[Lliira]], [[Mielikki]], [[Milil]], [[Mystra (Midnight)|Mystra]], [[Oghma]], [[Selûne]], [[Silvanus]], [[Tymora]], and the [[Seldarine]] were all represented. This caused no issues of faith, though there was some friction with upper ranks of clergy. [[Druid]]s, particularly of the North, tended to be their willing allies rather than members.<ref name="CotH-p3-6" />

==Rumors & Legends==
One of the more decisive battles fought during the Weeping War came to be known as [[Twilight's Falling]]. While nearly all the information regarding the fighting was kept hidden as Harper lore, it was believed that nearly seven out of ten Harpers in Twilight Hall were slain during the length of the two-day battle.<ref name="FMD-p22-23">{{Cite book/The Fall of Myth Drannor|22-23}}</ref>

The "High Harp" badge of [[High Dale]] referred back to the [[Harpers#History|founding]] of the Harpers at the Dancing Place.<ref name="VGD-p157">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to the Dalelands|157}}</ref>

During the mid–to–late 14{{th}} century DR, there persisted rumors about a second Harper Hall that was to be founded in [[Ravens Bluff]], called "Ravens Hall".<ref name="LoD-p98" />

'''Mertonius''', better known as '''Mirt "the Moneylender"''' ({{Pronounce|MERT|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}} {{Pronounce|MEERT|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>|alt=yes}}), was a [[human]] [[rogue]] and retired [[adventurer]] and a [[mercenary]] general, when he was known as '''Mirt the Merciless''' or '''the Old Wolf'''<ref name="FRCS3e-p181-182" /> or '''Lord Walrus'''.<ref name="ED-p15">{{Cite book/Elminster's Daughter/MMP|15|1}}</ref> He became a member of the [[Harpers]] as well as a [[Lords of Waterdeep|Masked Lord]] of [[Waterdeep]], which was commonly known.<ref name="FRCS3e-p181-182" /> Mirt was also one of the most infamous [[factor]]s of Waterdeep.<ref name="EFR-p114">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|114}}</ref>

==Description==
As of the 1370s DR, he was old, fat, and wheezy.<ref name="FRCS3e-p181-182" />

==Personality==
Outwardly, Mirt appeared to be a loud and drunken braggart who enjoyed telling long-winded stories of his exploits, as well as roughhousing and tavern brawls. However, this was a façade concealing his cunning and good judge of character, as well as his surprising agility. These traits helped him grow much wealthier in his investments later in life than being an adventurer or mercenary ever had. He had a good heart and desired to make [[Faerûn]] a better place than he'd found it.<ref name="FRCS3e-p181-182"/>

Mirt was sometimes seen wearing multiple artificial [[roses]] on his shoulder, signaling his interest to find several romantic partners to spend the night with.<ref name="CF-LCF">{{Cite web/Candlekeep forum/Questions for Ed Greenwood (2004)/02-20 - Lingerie, Courtship and Foreplay}}</ref>

==Relationships==
He was one of the few trusted friends of [[Evermeet]] and had a ''[[portal]]'' that could give him access to the elven island when he so wished. He carried a ''[[Purple Dragon ring]]'', which allowed him to speak in King [[Azoun IV]]'s name.<ref>{{Cite book/Crown of Fire/Paperback 1994|230}}</ref>

===Family===
His wife and heir,<ref>{{Cite book/Spellfire}}</ref> [[Asper]], was an informal member of the [[Gray Hands]]. She preceded him in death.<ref name="CoS.W-p54-55" /><ref name="WDH5e-p210-211" />

==Possessions==
Mirt owned a [[mithral shirt]] that was magically [[glamered]] to look like ordinary clothing.<ref name="FRCS3e-p181">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|181}}</ref> As a Masked Lord, he also possessed a ''[[lord's ensemble]]''.<ref name="WDH5e-p210-211" />

Mirt owned a ship named the ''[[Morning Bird]]'', which was used at one time to transport [[moon elves]] from the [[Ardeep Forest]] to [[Evermeet]].<ref name="Cos.W-p4">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|4}}</ref>

Mirt also owned a mansion in the [[Castle Ward of Waterdeep]], simply called [[Mirt's Mansion]]. It had a roughly hexagon-shaped, two-story main building with three-story towers at four of the six corners.<ref name="FR.CS-map2">{{Cite book/City System|Map 2}}</ref> It was located on the south face of [[Mount Waterdeep]] near the harbor at the intersection of [[Coin Alley]] and [[Tarnished Silver Alley]]. The estate could also be accessed from [[Smuggler's Dock]] in the harbor.<ref name="VGtW-p11-12" />

==History==
[[File:Mirt character.jpg|thumb|right|250px|''Mirt the Moneylender circa 1372 DR.'']]
===Early Life===
Mirt was the son of a successful Coaster, a [[Sword Coast]] trader. He grew up hearing many tales and deeds of heroism which led him down the path of becoming a [[fighter]].<ref name="HoH-p86" />

In time, he became a great mercenary general of the [[North]] and earned himself the nickname "Mirt the Merciless". He was greedy and became quite wealthy, but grew unsatisfied with mere wealth. He actually craved peace and tranquility, and realized he could not simply buy it, but would have to create it in the world.<ref name="HoH-p86" />

During his mercenary days, he met Durnan the Wanderer and the two became close companions. The two of them decided to "retire" to Waterdeep.<ref name="HoH-p86" />

At some point, he met his future wife, Asper, whom he rescued as a baby from a sacked city.<ref name="HoH-p86" />

==="Retirement"===
With Durnan in tow, Mirt went to Waterdeep to live, but quickly became bored with peaceful living. Within a month of his arrival, he was approached by [[Khelben Arunsun]] and [[Sammereza Sulphontis]] with an invitation to become a Lord of Waterdeep. A few years later, he proposed to the other Lords that Durnan also be accepted as a Lord, and they agreed.<ref name="HoH-p86" />

Mirt (and Durnan) performed many covert tasks for the Lords of Waterdeep. Among them was Mirt's stint as a member of Waterdeep's [[thieves' guild]] under the alias '''Tonius'''. He used his disguise to feed the Lords information on the guild, which led to the guild's eventual eradication from the city.<ref name="HoH-p86" />

Mirt ran his money-lending business primarily out of his mansion in Waterdeep. Transactions would happen only in the mornings, and Mirt was said to be patient regarding their repayment.<ref name="VGtW-p11-12">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|11-12}}</ref><ref name="WatN-p22,31">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|22,31}}</ref>

===Undermountain===
At some point, Mirt actually adventured into Undermountain with several other Lords, including Durnan (of course) and Khelben.<ref name="SD-p4" />

An entrance to Undermountain was found near Mirt's mansion below an alley named [[the Slide]] in the sewers. It eventually led to an area of the dungeon known as [[the Grim Statue]].<ref name="RoU-p9">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Campaign Guide to Undermountain|9}}</ref> With his companions, they explored many areas of Undermountain—sometimes by choice, but other times by necessity. Some of those areas included the [[Crystal Labyrinth]]<ref name="USD-p4,17">{{Cite book/Undermountain: Stardock|4, 17}}</ref> and an area of the [[Dark Levels]] known as the [[Lost Level]], where they befriended [[Bandaerl]].<ref name="UMLL-19">{{Cite book/Undermountain: The Lost Level|19}}</ref> Mirt, Durnan, and Khelben were among the few adventurers to return alive from the [[Underhalls]].<ref name="SD-p4">{{Cite book/Undermountain: Stardock|4}}</ref>

Mirt was among the Lords tasked with the job of keeping tabs on Skullport. When he wasn't visiting the city himself, usually with Durnan and/or Asper alongside, he would call for aide from [[Force Grey]]. He and some of the other Lords also maintained a network of informants within Skullport as well. One of Mirt's informants actually included the [[Iron Ring]] member [[Transtra]].<ref name="SK-p20"/>

On one occasion, while Mirt, Durnan, and Asper were in Skullport, they ran afoul of the [[beholder]] [[Xuzoun]] and his minions. The fighting woke up the elder [[black dragon]] [[Vulharindauloth]] from his decades long sleep, and he had to be driven off by the defenses of the city itself.<ref name="SK-p74">{{Cite book/Skullport|74}}</ref>

It was well known that Mirt and Durnan emerged from Undermountain in the {{yearlinkName|1302}}, with a vast amount of wealth and fame.<ref name="FRCS3e-180">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|179-180}}</ref><ref name="CoS.W-54">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|54}}</ref> Mirt used his share to build his mansion near [[Mount Waterdeep]], while Durnan used his to erect the [[Yawning Portal]] tavern over the famous entrance.<ref name="CoS.W-54" /> However, their success encouraged others to venture into Undermountain and lose their lives.<ref name="FRCS3e-180" />

===Time of Troubles===
[[File:Mirt.jpg|thumb|right|250px|''Mirt in the mid-14{{th}} century DR.'']]
During the tumultuous [[Time of Troubles]] in [[1358 DR]], Mirt was dispatched to secure assistance from any ships friendly to Waterdeep. This included tasks such as supplying Waterdeep with much-needed goods, patrolling the seas for pirates, warning of other naval invaders, and providing a means of escape from the city should it become necessary. The job also led Mirt to the island of [[Mintarn]].<ref name="FRE3-p32-33">{{Cite book/Waterdeep (adventure)|32-33}}</ref>

In the 1360s DR, Mirt briefly came out of retirement again to seek out and destroy ''[[The Black Basilisk]]'', a ship crewed by [[Hosttower]] mages, and scattered their undead-crewed Dead Fleet. The [[Black Daggers]] pirate guild of the [[Nelanther Isles]] were so thankful they granted amnesty to Waterdhavian ships.<ref name="Poly68">{{Cite polyhedron/68/The Everwinking Eye: Goodbye, Mulmaster|28}}</ref>

In [[1368 DR]], [[Joshuan Havabuck]], a [[halfling]] crime lord, and businessman of many enterprises of the [[Waterdeep/Dock Ward|Dock Ward]] in Waterdeep received a sizable loan from Mirt. The ten million [[Golden lion (coin)|gold lion]] loan was to pay out the [[lottery]] winnings the halfing ran in the Docks. Amazingly, every single person to buy into the game picked the winning numbers. This freakish luck was caused by [[Tymora]]'s divine powers running amok across the Realms. Joshuan was ready to take on the monstrous loan and its interest in order to keep his reputation intact. The collection of coin took more than four hours of carting sacks of gold from Mirt's vaults and the rest of the day was spent in calculation of all the [[enchanted]] items, [[gem]]s, and [[art]] Mirt needed to sell temporarily to refill the cash reserves. The same night, Mirt was visited by one of the Masked Lords of Waterdeep informing him of several similar gargantuan loans taken by other gambling organizers of Waterdeep. The Lords were troubled by the sudden influx of coin in  the city of Waterdeep and the threat of inflation caused by it.<ref name="ty">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|101-104}}</ref>

===Post-Spellplague===
At some point, Mirt was imprisoned within a magical [[handaxe]]. In [[1479 DR]], a [[Cormyr]]ean noble named [[Marlin Stormserpent]] stole the axe from another noble family, mistakenly believing it was an artifact of [[the Nine]]. He called Mirt out of the axe and Mirt escaped into [[Suzail]].<ref name="EMD">{{Cite book/Elminster Must Die/MMP|343-346,357-358|33,34}}</ref>

In [[1487 DR]], Mirt made an effort to continuously thwart the scheming of [[Manshoon]], or rather from even attempting to begin his scheming. The two of them had several meetings for drinks and passive-aggressive conversations in Suzail.<ref>{{Cite book/The Herald/Hardcover|9-11,62-66,196-197,297-299,333-335,348-349|1,4,11,16,18,19}}</ref>

In [[1488 DR]], Mirt came to the aid of [[Elminster]], openly posing as a waiter and cook for several [[archwizard]]s trapped inside a [[spellstorm]] in Cormyr, thereby contributing to the protection of the mythical [[Lost Spell]].<ref>{{Cite book/Spellstorm/Hardcover|?}}</ref>

In [[1491 DR]], [[Elminster]] and Mirt returned to the city of Waterdeep, both aiding the newly appointed Open Lord of Waterdeep, [[Laeral Silverhand]], in uncovering the culprits behind a string of murders of Masked Lords. After the investigations, Mirt chose to stay with Laeral, serving as a personal agent for the Open Lord.<ref>{{Cite book/Death Masks|?}}</ref>

===Spelljamming Career===
Some time later, Mirt was forced to flee Waterdeep and sought refuge far to the south<ref name="SA-p1" />.  He founded, and subsequently became the head of [[Spelljammer Academy]], an institution for training [[spelljammer]] pilots located off the coast of [[Nimbral]].<ref name="SA-p1">{{Cite web/D&D Beyond/Spelljammer Academy|1}}</ref>

Lady '''Remallia Haventree''' ({{Pronounce|reh|MAW|lee|ah|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was [[Waterdhavian]] [[Nobility of Waterdeep|noblewoman]] and an agent of the [[Harpers]] during the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]]. She famously founded the [[Council of Waterdeep]], a conciliary body that united the [[Five Factions]] of the [[Sword Coast]] against the emergent threat of the [[Cult of the Dragon]] during the 1480s DR.<ref name="TRoT-p12">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|12}}</ref><ref name="ToD.9">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 9}}</ref>{{ToD-time}}

{{Quote|We have an evil wizard situation and [[Thay]] is involved. No surprises there.|Lady Remallia, in reference to the [[Red Wizards]].<ref name="SS-p3" />}}

==Description==
Remallia had distinctly bronze-colored skin.<ref name="UH-p16">{{Cite adventurers league/Unsafe Harborage|16}}</ref> Despite her [[elven]] heritage, she had aged considerably during a few short decades in her life.<ref name="Upr-p5" />

She dressed in elegant finery that included beauteous [[robe]]s and non-ostentatious jewelry.<ref name="UH-p16" />

==Personality==
Lady Remallia was honest and friendly. She understood the threats that faced the Realms, but understood the caution of her fellow Harpers. She also agreed to compromise with lesser evils to fight more nefarious, but not at all prices.<ref name="TRoT-p12" /> The untimely death of her husband at the Dragon Cult's hand served only to increase Remallia's willingness to fight against the forces of evil.<ref name="ToD.9" /><ref name="TRoT-p20">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|20}}</ref>

Not one to open dialogue, Remi let others speak first before saying her piece.<ref name="UH-p16">{{Cite adventurers league/Unsafe Harborage|16}}</ref><ref name="SS-p18" />

==Possessions==
Among her possessions was a large oil painting of her family, created before her husband's death some time during the mid—to—late 15{{th}} century.<ref name="Upr-p5">{{Cite adventurers league/Uprising|5}}</ref>

She was rather fond of [[ticklemint tea]],<ref name="Upr-p4">{{Cite adventurers league/Uprising|4}}</ref> which she served in [[silver]] kettle with [[porcelain]] teacups.<ref name="Upr-p5" />

==Abilities==
She was at the very least proficient in [[divination]] magic.<ref name="Upr-p5" />

==Relationships==
Lady Remallia was married [[Arthagast Ulbrinter]] of the noble [[Ulbrinter]] family. Together they had two children: [[Arhtius|Arthius]], their son that lived in [[Silverymoon]], and [[Serenore]], their daughter that lived with her family on the island of [[Alaron]] in the [[Moonshaes]].<ref name="WDH5e-p214-215" />

She notably worked with [[Zehira Deryan]], a protector of [[storm giant]] queen [[Serissa]].<ref name="SS-p3" /><ref name="SS-p18">{{Cite adventurers league/Shadows in the Stacks|18}}</ref>

==History==
[[File:Remi.png|thumb|right|300px|Lady Haventree was a skilled spellcaster.]]
Remallia was a noble sun elf living in [[Waterdeep]] in [[Ulbrinter Villa]], once the home of her husband Arthagast.<ref name="WDH5e-p37,215">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|37,215}}</ref> At some point she joined the Harpers.<ref name="TRoT-p20" /> 

===Tyranny of Dragons===
During the 1480s DR, the Cult of the Dragon raided Ulbrinter Villa in search of a [[White Dragon Mask|draconic artifact]] stolen by a [[Zhentarim]] agent,<ref name="ToD.7">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 7}}</ref> and killed Lord Arthagast.<ref name="ToD.8">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 8}}</ref> Lady Remallia took decisive action in response, methodically and purposefully dismantling the cult's operations with all the resources at her disposal.<ref name="ToD.8" /><ref name="RoT-p21">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|21}}</ref> She was the decisive force in uniting the Five Factions in the Council of Waterdeep, founded to combat the menace of the Cult of the Dragon. For her efforts she was chosen as the Harpers' representative on the Council.<ref name="TRoT-p20" /> While Remallia was proactive in fighting the Cult, she had to exercise caution due to the Harpers' precarious position of having just been recently reformed.<ref name="TRoT-p12" /> In the end, the Five Factions raised a massive army<ref name="ToD.9" /> and defeated the cult's goddess [[Tiamat]] at [[Tiamat's temple|her temple]] in the [[Well of Dragons]].<ref name="ToD.10">{{Cite web/Tyranny of Dragons: Online Comic 10}}</ref>

===Against the Red Wizards===
Lady Remallia then turned her attention towards the Cult of the Dragon's onetime allies, the [[Red Wizards]] of [[Thay]]. She worked with her fellow Harpers and a group of adventurers to investigate a Red Wizard safehouse in Waterdeep's [[Waterdeep/Dock Ward|Dock Ward]],<ref name="UH-p3">{{Cite adventurers league/Unsafe Harborage|3}}</ref> then directed them to take information they recovered to [[Candlekeep]] for interpretation.<ref name="SS-p3">{{Cite adventurers league/Shadows in the Stacks|3}}</ref> A short time later, she was summoned to the ''[[Maelstrom (fortress)|Maelstrom]]'' by the storm giant queen Serissa.<ref name="WCD-p4">{{Cite adventurers league/To Walk the Cold Dark|4}}</ref>

Remallia's investigation led to the discovery of the [[Tenebrous Creed]], a sect of [[Shar]]-worshiping cultists obsessed with the [[Far Realm]],<ref name="NT-p2" /> and the being known as [[Father Llymic]].<ref name="CSNB-p2">{{Cite adventurers league/The City That Should Not Be|2}}</ref> She worked with adventurers and Harpers in the [[Moonsea]] region to recover a ''[[living spell|living]] [[blade of disaster]]'' that was unearthed by the Red Wizards.<ref name="NT-p2">{{Cite adventurers league/Night Thieves|2}}</ref> Working with Zehira Deryan, Remallia directed the party to form a lookout post [[Abeir]]an necropolis of [[Xorvintroth]] deep underground in [[Icewind Dale]],<ref name="CSNB-p4">{{Cite adventurers league/The City That Should Not Be|4}}</ref><ref name="Upr-p2">{{Cite adventurers league/Uprising|2}}</ref>  and soon joined them in their efforts to spy on the Red Wizards.<ref name="FW-p9-10">{{Cite adventurers league/Frozen Whispers|9-10}}</ref> 

During her stay in the city, Remallia became afflicted with some unknown malady. She began to unknowingly harm herself, leaving wounds that created ichor-filled boils upon her skin.<ref name="FW-p9-10" /> She began muttering to herself, about strange "slivers" that communicated with one another.<ref name="FW-p11">{{Cite adventurers league/Frozen Whispers|11}}</ref> Despite this, she still managed to work with her adventurer allies to free the slaves the Red Wizards had made captive in Xorvintroth,<ref name="Upr-p5" /> and uncover what they truly sought out: the [[Supreme Forge]].<ref name="Upr-p22">{{Cite adventurers league/Uprising|22}}</ref>

===Return to Waterdeep===
In the {{YearlinkName|1491}}, the goddess [[Eilistraee]] returned to the Realms<ref name="SCAG">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|23, 108}}</ref> near the walls of Waterdeep. In turn, many of the goddess' [[drow]] followers traveled to the City of Splendors ''en masse''. Some of their numbers approached Remallia and requested aid in constructing a temple of Eilistraee in the city. Lady Remallia agreed to provide her support to their cause.<ref name="DM">{{Cite book/Death Masks}}</ref>{{Page needed}}

'''Harpers' Hold''' was a refuge hidden inside [[Mount Waterdeep]] that was used by the [[Harpers]] in the 1360s [[DR]].<ref name="Dragon211-p29-34" />

==Description==
Harpers' Hold was created after the fall of the [[Shadow Thieves]] in their former lair, known as the [[Citadel of the Bloody Hand]]. The Hold consisted of approximately fifteen separate rooms including a teleportation entry room, a teleportation exit room, a kitchen, a pantry, a dining room, a central meeting room, three sitting rooms, three bedchambers, and a hidden area exclusively used by the [[Chosen of Mystra]].<ref name="Dragon211-p29-34" />

The Hold could be reached by entering a cave accessed by a very treacherous path along the shore and going through areas of the former Citadel, but typically entrants used one of the many secret teleport stations located in [[Waterdeep]] or other areas of the Realms. The Hold had direct links from such places as [[Elminster's Tower]], [[Twilight Hall]], a cave on an island in the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]], and a hollow tree in the [[Ardeep Forest]]. It also could be used to teleport to areas in [[Suzail]], [[Llorbauth]], [[Tantras]], the [[High Forest]], [[Moongleam Tower]], and a few other places in the city of Waterdeep itself.<ref name="Dragon211-p29-34" />

The primary use of the hold was a place of respite for members of the Harpers. Healing magics worked exceptionally well there and wounds healed exceptionally fast on their own as well. Harpers also used the place to gather and exchange information about the goings-on around the Realms.<ref name="Dragon211-p29-34" />

The '''Moonstars''', also known as the '''Tel'Teukiira''', the '''Silverstars''', or the '''Twelvestars''', were a highly secretive group of individuals formed by [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] in the late 14{{th}} century [[DR]] with the aim of unifying the [[human]]s and other races (especially [[elves]]) against the forces of evil.<ref name="C&G-p25">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|25}}</ref><ref name="Cos.W-p76">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|75-76}}</ref>

==Outlook==
The Moonstars sought to preserve life and knowledge. They had a similar outlook to the [[Harpers]], but dedicated themselves to [[Mystra]], [[Oghma]], and [[Sehanine Moonbow]].<ref name="C&G-p25" /> Their leader, Khelben, was very much in tune with the beliefs and motivations of the Harpers, but preferred to keep tighter control of the agents under his control. He felt that he sacrificed much for the Harpers during his time in their service and was unfairly treated by the [[Twilight Trio]].<ref name="C&G-p26">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|26}}</ref>

==Prophecy==
A passage from [[Amagal's Tome]] (an ancient elven text from [[Cormanthor]]) declared:
{{Quote|''The Tel'Teukiira will come hidden and in many guises and faces. They dwell in shadow and speak in omens, yet they shall bring about waking dreams and save us from the Three Threats Who Wait in Darkness, the Prefects, and ourselves.''}}
It was believed that only Khelben understood the relevance of these words.<ref name="C&G-p25" />


==Activities==
Based in [[Waterdeep]], the Moonstars followed much the same methods as the Harpers did, though the Moonstars operated in more secrecy than the Harpers and members were less free to do what they wanted, being under the direct and relatively strict command of the Blackstaff himself.<ref name="Cos.W-p76"/><ref name="CaD-30">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|30}}</ref> Officially, the group's headquarters was [[Blackstaff Tower]], though very few even visited that prestigious place. Each member carried a badge that allowed them access to their real stronghold, [[Silver Safehold]], which was an extraplanar stronghold built by the [[Storm Heralds]] of [[Cormyr]].<ref name="C&G-p26"/><ref name="CaD-31"/><ref name="Cos.W-p76"/>

Of the gods who pledged their support to the Harpers, only three also supported the Moonstars: [[Mystra]], [[Oghma]], and [[Sehanine Moonbow]]. But even with the lack of the other Harper gods' blessings, Khelben and Laeral made up for it with their own spells.<ref name="C&G-p25" />

To aid them in their quests to vanquish evil, the Moonstars had several permanent ''[[portal]]s'' scattered around Faerûn to allow quick passage.<ref name="PG.PotM">{{Cite web/Perilous Gateways/Portals of the Moonstars}}</ref>

==History==
The Moonstars were officially formed in the {{YearlinkName|1370}}, but this was only a step in a much larger plan by the organization's founder, Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun, who had sought to create his own evil-fighting organization for centuries before.<ref name="C&G-p26" /> A strong supporter of the group, Lady [[Alathene Moonstar]], granted them permission to call themselves the Moonstars "in hopes that its members might be more worthy of the name than [[Vanrak Moonstar|some relations]] of mine."<ref name="CaD-31">{{Cite book/Cloak & Dagger|31}}</ref><ref name="Cos.W-p76"/>

An offshoot of the Harpers, the Moonstars were formed when Khelben realized that he didn't have as much power over the Harpers as he once did. He discovered in the {{YearlinkName|816}}, that [[Alaundo|Alaundo the Seer]] had written prophecies about an as-yet-undiscovered threat to [[Faerûn]]. The '''Red Wizards of Thay''', colloquially known as the '''Red Magic Cult''' in the [[Eastern Heartlands]], were the notorious and nefarious magocratic ruling class of the inhospitable but well-inhabited country of [[Thay]].<ref name="SCAG-p141">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|141}}</ref> Historically they owed their power to being cruel slavers,<ref name="SB.CG-p14">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|14}}</ref><ref name="CoR-p63">{{Cite book/Champions of Ruin|63}}</ref> demonologists,<ref name="SB.CG-p17">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|17}}</ref><ref name="RoT-p7">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|7}}</ref> and arcane experimenters,<ref name="MotM-p60">{{Cite book/Mysteries of the Moonsea|60}}</ref> before most of their numbers wholly dedicated themselves to creating a nation of [[undeath|undead]].<ref name="SCAG-p13">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|13}}</ref>

==Organization==
For centuries, the leaders of the Red Wizards and in fact all of Thay was the [[Council of Zulkirs]].<ref name="FRCS2e.RTR-p27">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|27}}</ref> The ruling body comprised eight of the most powerful Red Wizards, each of whom represented one of the [[schools of magic]].<ref name="LoD-p56"/> Each of the zulkirs and their subordinates fomented intense rivalries with one another, leading to great feuds and near-crippling internal strife.<ref name="LoD-p60">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|60}}</ref>

In the years following the [[War of the Zulkirs]], the Red Wizards of Thay were led by the feared [[lich]] and supreme regent, [[Szass Tam]].<ref name="SCAG-p13"/><ref name="FRCG-p5">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|5}}</ref><ref name="DrM.366.RK">{{Cite dragon/366/Rose Keep}}</ref>

Since Szass Tam's takeover, the exiled Red Wizards have transformed enormously, becoming a loosely organized chain of merchant enclaves who specialized in procuring magical items.<ref name="DrM.366.RK"/>

===Factions===
The Red Wizards have historically been fraught with in-fighting and turmoil. As the stakes rose over the years, these conflicts became more dramatic and impactful to their operations.<ref name="SB.CG-p11">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|11}}</ref><ref name="SB.CG-p15">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|15}}</ref>

In the years before the mid–14{{th}} century [[DR]], the Red Wizards divided themselves into two separate ideological groups. ==Etymology==
The word "Faerûn" was a modified version of "Faerie", the name of the homeland of ancient [[elves]]. The term was coined by [[Durothil (prince)|Durothil]], prince of [[Tintageer]], [[Faerie]].<ref name="e:ioe-p97">{{Cite book/Evermeet: Island of Elves/MMP|97}}</ref>

==Geography==
{{main|Portal:Geography}}
The continent included terrain that was as varied as any other. Besides the exterior coastline to the west and south, the most dominant feature on the continent was the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]]. This was an irregular inland sea that kept the interior lands fertile, connected the west and east regions of Faerûn and served as a major trade route for many of the bordering nations.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /><ref name="FRCS-p142">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|142}}</ref>

Next in significance was the [[Shaar]], a broad region of grasslands in the south that,<ref name="FRCS-p196">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|196}}</ref> together with the Lake of Steam, separated the area around the inland sea from the coastal nations at the southern edge of the continent. To the east, Faerûn was bordered by a vast region of steppes from [[Kara-Tur]],<ref name="FRCS-p230">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|230}}</ref> and in the north were massive glaciers ([[Pelvuria]] and [[Reghed]]) and tundra.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /> South of the continent, separated by the [[Great Sea]], was the sub-tropical land of [[Zakhara]].<ref name="FRCS-p230"/>

The continent included the following geographic regions.<ref group="note">The following division of Faerûn by compass directions was developed for organizational purposes on the Forgotten Realms Wiki, and is not closely reflected by descriptions in source text. In particular, terms like "the North" and "the South" in the sources can be much more wide-ranging in scope, and as ambiguous, than what is defined here.</ref>

'''Laeral Silverhand''' ({{Pronounce|LAIR|awl|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}), born '''Anamanué Silverhand''' and formerly known as the Witch-Queen of the North<ref name="WDH5e-p207" /> and later the Lady Mage of Waterdeep, was one of the [[Seven Sisters]], a [[Chosen of Mystra]],<ref name="FRCS3e-280" /> and the [[Open Lord]] of [[Waterdeep]] during the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="WDH5e-p207" /> Together with her partner [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]], they led the [[Moonstars]], a dissident faction of the [[Harpers]], which sought to guide the cities and countries of the [[North]] toward a shared destiny.<ref name="FRCS3e-280" />

==Personality==
Laeral was incredibly organized—it was the only way she could manage to fit all of her activities into a single day. She was friendly and accommodating, leading almost everyone who met her, even the haughty nobles of Waterdeep, to consider her a true friend. She was also proactive in her defense of the city, to the extent that certain Waterdhavians would stage calamitous events so that she would keep tabs on persons of interest for them without their needing to ask for her help or involve themselves directly.<ref name="SS-p117-118">{{Cite book/The Seven Sisters|117-118}}</ref>

Having been in thrall to the ''Crown of Horns'' and forced to commit heinous acts by it, she felt that she understood the nature of evil better than most<ref name="SS-p32">{{Cite book/The Seven Sisters|32}}</ref> and often mediated disputes with rogues, undesirables, and sentient monsters, especially in [[Skullport]] where she kept an alias identity in the form of [[Irusyl Eraneth]].<ref name="SS-p117-118" />

Laeral was infuriated by magic-users who employed their power to deceive and exert baleful influence over others. She disliked [[doppelganger]]s and slave-owners.<ref name="SS-p31" />

'''Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun''' ({{Pronounce|KEL|ben|_|AIR|un|sun|refs=<ref name="DMSotR-24" />}}) was the Lord Mage of [[Waterdeep]], a masked [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]], and powerful archmage.<ref name="ELH-pp299-300" /> He resided in [[Blackstaff Tower]] until his death.<ref name="CoWTM">{{Cite book/The City of Waterdeep Trail Map}}</ref> One of the few spellcasters to become one of [[Mystra's Chosen]],<ref name="B-p7-9">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|7-9|0}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p88">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|88}}</ref> and the last survivor of House [[Maerdrym]],<ref name="B-p62">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|62|6}}</ref> Khelben was a serious, imposing wizard and a grim proponent of law and order.<ref name="FRCS3e-274" />

==Description==
Khelben stood just over 6 feet (1.83 meters) tall and was well-muscled. He usually dressed in robes, wielding his trademark [[Blackstaff (staff)|Blackstaff]]. As of 1374 DR, he had a full beard, black hair with silver streaks, including a prominent one through his beard, and a distinguished, imposing manner.<ref name="B-p25">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|25|2}}</ref> He often dressed in black with a silver torque and a cape lined in gray fur.<ref name="ESh-p52-53">{{Cite book/Elfshadow/Paperback 2000|52-53}}</ref>

==Personality==
Often thought to be humorless and grave, Khelben was actually neither. He simply found it easier to deal with people if they were intimidated by or even frightened of him. He had numerous apprentices over the years and liked to encourage young people who displayed an aptitude for magic. Wielding [[magic|the Art]] could be a gravely dangerous business, however, and Khelben tried to instill an appreciation of that fact in his pupils by keeping up his "stern taskmaster" façade.<ref name=":1">{{Cite book

''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref
