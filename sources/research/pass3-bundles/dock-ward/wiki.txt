The '''Dock Ward of Waterdeep''' was situated on the ''Great Harbor'' of [[Waterdeep]]. It contained the docks, shipbuilding yards, and warehouses for the sea trade. Compared to the rest of the city it was exceptionally dirty, smelly, and particularly dangerous. In the words of [[Elminster]] himself, the Dock Ward was a "riotous, nigh-perpetual brawl that covers entire acres, interrupted only by small buildings, intermittent trade businesses, an errant dog or two, and a few brave watchguards, who manage to keep the chaos from spreading beyond the docks; the whole lot wallowing in the stench of rotting fish."

==History==
''Circa'' [[1368 DR]], an enterprising [[halfling]] [[Joshuan Havabuck]] was involved in numerous schemes and business in the Dock Ward. His main profitable business was the [[lottery]] where the folk of Waterdeep placed bets on the total daily tonnage that entered the harbor. The numbers were reported by the dockmaster and the winnings were distributed accordingly. One ticket was sold for a single [[Golden lion (coin)|gold lion]] and the take, on average, was ten thousand coins. Amazingly, in 1368 DR, not ten people, picked the correct number, not even thirty. Every single gambler guessed it correctly. This was due the goddess of luck - [[Tymora]]'s power running amok in [[Faerûn]]. Joshuan Havabuck resorted to loaning ten million gold lions with steep interest from [[Mirt the Moneylender]] and pay the winnings out.<ref name="ty">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|101-104}}</ref>

After the [[Spellplague]], the northern section of Waterdeep harbor became dilapidated with most of the harbor choked with sunken ship hulls. This section became known as [[Mistshore]].<ref>{{Cite book/Mistshore|?}}</ref>

==Etymology==
The word "Faerûn" was a modified version of "Faerie", the name of the homeland of ancient [[elves]]. The term was coined by [[Durothil (prince)|Durothil]], prince of [[Tintageer]], [[Faerie]].<ref name="e:ioe-p97">{{Cite book/Evermeet: Island of Elves/MMP|97}}</ref>

==Geography==
{{main|Portal:Geography}}
The continent included terrain that was as varied as any other. Besides the exterior coastline to the west and south, the most dominant feature on the continent was the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]]. This was an irregular inland sea that kept the interior lands fertile, connected the west and east regions of Faerûn and served as a major trade route for many of the bordering nations.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /><ref name="FRCS-p142">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|142}}</ref>

Next in significance was the [[Shaar]], a broad region of grasslands in the south that,<ref name="FRCS-p196">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|196}}</ref> together with the Lake of Steam, separated the area around the inland sea from the coastal nations at the southern edge of the continent. To the east, Faerûn was bordered by a vast region of steppes from [[Kara-Tur]],<ref name="FRCS-p230">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|230}}</ref> and in the north were massive glaciers ([[Pelvuria]] and [[Reghed]]) and tundra.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /> South of the continent, separated by the [[Great Sea]], was the sub-tropical land of [[Zakhara]].<ref name="FRCS-p230"/>

The continent included the following geographic regions.<ref group="note">The following division of Faerûn by compass directions was developed for organizational purposes on the Forgotten Realms Wiki, and is not closely reflected by descriptions in source text. In particular, terms like "the North" and "the South" in the sources can be much more wide-ranging in scope, and as ambiguous, than what is defined here.</ref>

'''Mertonius''', better known as '''Mirt "the Moneylender"''' ({{Pronounce|MERT|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}} {{Pronounce|MEERT|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>|alt=yes}}), was a [[human]] [[rogue]] and retired [[adventurer]] and a [[mercenary]] general, when he was known as '''Mirt the Merciless''' or '''the Old Wolf'''<ref name="FRCS3e-p181-182" /> or '''Lord Walrus'''.<ref name="ED-p15">{{Cite book/Elminster's Daughter/MMP|15|1}}</ref> He became a member of the [[Harpers]] as well as a [[Lords of Waterdeep|Masked Lord]] of [[Waterdeep]], which was commonly known.<ref name="FRCS3e-p181-182" /> Mirt was also one of the most infamous [[factor]]s of Waterdeep.<ref name="EFR-p114">{{Cite book/Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms|114}}</ref>

==Description==
As of the 1370s DR, he was old, fat, and wheezy.<ref name="FRCS3e-p181-182" />

==Personality==
Outwardly, Mirt appeared to be a loud and drunken braggart who enjoyed telling long-winded stories of his exploits, as well as roughhousing and tavern brawls. However, this was a façade concealing his cunning and good judge of character, as well as his surprising agility. These traits helped him grow much wealthier in his investments later in life than being an adventurer or mercenary ever had. He had a good heart and desired to make [[Faerûn]] a better place than he'd found it.<ref name="FRCS3e-p181-182"/>

Mirt was sometimes seen wearing multiple artificial [[roses]] on his shoulder, signaling his interest to find several romantic partners to spend the night with.<ref name="CF-LCF">{{Cite web/Candlekeep forum/Questions for Ed Greenwood (2004)/02-20 - Lingerie, Courtship and Foreplay}}</ref>

==Relationships==
He was one of the few trusted friends of [[Evermeet]] and had a ''[[portal]]'' that could give him access to the elven island when he so wished. He carried a ''[[Purple Dragon ring]]'', which allowed him to speak in King [[Azoun IV]]'s name.<ref>{{Cite book/Crown of Fire/Paperback 1994|230}}</ref>

The '''Spellplague''', called the '''Blue Breath of Change''' by the inhabitants of [[Abeir]],<ref name="FRCG-p200">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|200}}</ref> was a disaster that struck [[Realmspace]] and even the [[planes]] themselves on the date of [[Tarsakh 29]] in the {{YearlinkName|1385}}, and was caused by [[Mystra (Midnight)|Mystra]]'s assassination at the hands of [[Cyric]] and [[Shar]].<ref name="FRCG-p50">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|50}}</ref><ref name="Ud-p64">{{Cite book/Undead|64}}</ref> It continued for a decade, leading to the [[Wailing Years]], during which [[arcane magic]] ceased to function and the planet of [[Toril]] was transformed.<ref name="TWY">{{Cite dragon/362/Countdown to the Realms: Spellplague: The Wailing Years}}</ref>

But instead of merely breaking the goddess of magic's dominance, her death threw the Weave into utter chaos and collapse. Magic spells faltered, or failed entirely. Countless spellcasters were killed or driven insane. A vast storm of blue flame arose from the [[Mhair Jungles]] and swept the continent, churning and reshaping the land, and leaving whole regions devastated.|''[[Magical Histories]], Volume 2: The Spellplague''<ref name="BGIII">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

==Origins==
The most popular and widely accepted theory about the origins of the Spellplague was that this calamity was caused by the death of Mystra, the [[god]]dess of [[Magic]]. For eons, magic had been bound within Mystra's [[Weave]], the universal structure of arcane forces, and her death tore the Weave apart and unleashed the [[raw magic]] contained within it, creating the cataclysm that was later known as the Spellplague.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/><ref name="NV">{{Cite game/Neverwinter}}</ref>

Other scholars, however, didn't consider this to be a logical explanation. Historically, two earlier incarnations of Mystra had died before only to be reborn, and the Weave maintained its hold over the power of magic for the duration of her deaths. In these scholars' views, the truth would never be fully known to mortals, and perhaps not even to the [[god]]s.<ref name="NV"/><ref name="PoS-p79">{{Cite book/Plague of Spells|79}}</ref>

Around [[1396 DR]], the sentient [[construct]] [[Cynosure (construct)|Cynosure]] theorized that the escape of an entity named the [[Carnis|Traitor]] from its confinement in the dungeons of [[Stardeep]], around the same time Mystra was murdered, may have also influenced the Spellplague's virulent onslaught.<ref name="PoS-p79"/>

Around [[1479 DR]], scholars and sages who studied the [[Neverwinter/Chasm|Chasm]] of [[Neverwinter]], such as [[Rhazzad]], believed the [[aboleth]]s of the [[Abolethic Sovereignty]] may have been the actual creators of the Spellplague, although there were no definite proof.<ref name="NV"/>

'''Mistshore''' was the name given to the dilapidated north shore of [[Waterdeep]]'s harbor in the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="Mistshore">{{Cite book/Mistshore}}</ref>

==History==
After the [[Spellplague]] of {{Yearlink|1385}}, Waterdeep went into a decline and maintenance of the harbor was neglected. Many ships sank or were scuttled in the northern harbor and eventually Waterdeep's outcasts created a small community on the wrecked ship hulls. The harbor water was polluted and smelled horribly.<ref name="Mistshore"/>

In [[Mirtul]] of {{Yearlink|1491}}, Mistshore was largely destroyed in a massive fire, with most ships burning down to the waterline and having to be towed out of the harbor to prevent other vessels from running afoul the wreckage.<ref>{{Cite book/Death Masks|?}}</ref> By {{Yearlink|1492}}, most buildings in the neighborhood were still burned and abandoned.<ref name="WDH5e-p65-66" />{{Waterdeep-time}}

==Description==
Mistshore was considered so dangerous that the [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|City Watch]] refused to send patrols into the area.<ref name="Mistshore"/>

The '''Blushing Mermaid''' was a tavern, inn, and [[festhall]] in [[Waterdeep]] managed by Lady [[Alathene Moonstar]] ''circa'' the {{YearlinkName|1372}}.<ref name="CoSW-106" /> It was well-known for its luxurious atmosphere, privacy, and unique dining with [[merfolk]] servers.<ref name="VGtW-193" /><ref name="VGtW-194" />

==Location==
[[File:NetStreet-1372.png|thumb|right|270px|''The Blushing Mermaid (labeled <code>D36</code>) ''circa'' 1372 DR.'']]
This establishment was located on [[Net Street (south)|Net Street]] in the [[Waterdeep/Dock Ward|Dock Ward]], just one door up from [[Dock Street (Waterdeep)|Dock Street]] and its intersection with [[Keel Alley]].<ref name="CS-14">{{Cite book/City System|14}}</ref><ref name="CSmap07">{{Cite book/City System/Map07}}</ref><ref name="WatN-26">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|26}}</ref><ref name="VGtW-195">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|195}}</ref><ref name="VGtW-map">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep/Map}}</ref><ref name="CoS-CG-55">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|55}}</ref><ref name="CoS-map">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Map}}</ref><ref name="CoSW-105">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|105}}</ref>
[[File:SpicesStreet-VgtW-195.png|thumb|right|270px|''Map showing the Blushing Mermaid on Net Street, ''circa'' 1367 DR.'']]

==Structure==
The Blushing Mermaid was a converted villa<ref name="PP-156">{{Cite book/Powers & Pantheons|156}}</ref><ref name="CoSW-45">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|45}}</ref><ref name="V-2">{{Cite web enhancement/Vanrakdoom|2}}</ref> that consisted of three two- and three-story connected buildings.<ref name="VGtW-193" /><ref name="CoSW-106" /><ref name="CoS-CG-55" /> The lowest level was connected to a series of well-secured secret tunnels once used for smuggling. The merfolk staff used these to commute to work, swimming in from the harbor and exiting the same way when their shift was over.<ref name="VGtW-193" />

==Interior==
The rooms were luxuriously appointed and each one was a suite composed of a reception area that could also be an office, a private bedroom, and a private bathroom. The common areas were partitioned with thin curtains and had many cushions for comfortable lounging. The carpets were thick and sound-absorbent. The main attraction was a large heated pool filled with scented water where guests could soak while being served food and drink by mermaids and mermen.<ref name="VGtW-193" />

It was a well-known fact that the Blushing Mermaid was riddled with secret passages. Nearly every room had a sliding panel in the wall, but they were spell-locked so only Lady Alathene or a trusted staff member carrying one of four enchanted [[silver]] hands could operate them. Guests seldom if ever managed to circumvent Lady Alathene's spells.<ref name="VGtW-193" />

===Atmosphere===
The Blushing Mermaid was a refuge from the loud and noisome harbor. Many hanging lamps gave off pleasant scents and warm [[amber]] glows. The plush carpets and gauzy curtains made cozy nooks for socializing between guests and escorts. All the waitstaff, maids, and escorts (and even Lady Alathene herself<ref name="VGtW-194" />) wore black masks made of thin, light metal with black veils to hide their faces, adding an air of mystery.<ref name="VGtW-193" /> Even more macabre was the spiral staircase that wound from the main dining pool room up to a rooftop greenhouse or garden under a glass cupola. The railings and risers were decorated with human bones affixed by thin wire.<ref name="VGtW-196">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|196}}</r

'''Alathene Moonstar''' was the proprietress of the [[Blushing Mermaid (Waterdeep)|Blushing Mermaid]] inn, tavern, and festhall in Waterdeep ''circa'' the {{YearlinkName|1372}}.<ref name="CoS.W-p62" /> She was also an [[archlich]], the leader of the [[Maids of the Midnight Moon]], and a former matriarch of House [[Moonstar (family)|Moonstar]].<ref name="PP-156" />

==Description==
Lady Alathene was very old but maintained her stunning good looks by magical means. She always dressed in the formal style with long, elaborately decorated gowns, [[corset]]s that accentuated her bust, and veils on her head.<ref name="VGtW-194" /><ref name="PP-157" /> She had an aristocratic bearing and manner of speaking that gave the impression of royalty.<ref name="CoS.W-p62" /> When interacting with her staff or clients of the Blushing Mermaid, she usually donned the same black metal mask and face veil as her employees wore.<ref name="VGtW-194" />

==Personality==
Lady Alathene was always a devout worshiper of [[Selûne]] and a staunch supporter of her [[Church of Selûne|church]]. When her twin brother [[Vanrak Moonstar|Vanrak]] turned to [[Shar]], it began a family conflict that lasted for centuries. Alathene became obsessed with bringing her wayward brother to justice, even to the point of embracing [[undeath]] to carry on the quest for as long as it took.<ref name="PP-157" /> She was a [[wizard]] of great accomplishment and did not hesitate to use her powers when defending her home and her staff at the Blushing Mermaid.<ref name="VGtW-195">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|195}}</ref> She found joy and amusement watching young people enjoy the festivities in her establishment and monitored it closely for any signs of trouble.<ref name="VGtW-196">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|196}}</ref> She had few close friends and was always on the lookout for people she could trust with important tasks.<ref name="PP-157" /><ref name="VGtW-214" />

==Abilities==
She was a master of wizardry, able to cast spells from any [[school of magic]].<ref name="PP-157" /><ref name="VGtW-214" /> She was an [[arcane devotee]] of Selûne<ref name="CoS.W-p62" /> and created [[Arcane magic|arcane]] versions of many of the [[Divine magic|divine]] spells granted only to [[Specialty priest|favored priests]] of the Moonmaiden,<ref name="PP-157" /> such as ''[[moon blade]]'', ''[[wall of moonlight]]'', ''[[moon path]]'', and ''[[moonweb]]''.<ref name="FA-137-138">{{Cite book/Faiths & Avatars|137-138}}</ref>

Lady Alathene could control her undead powers, switching them on or off at will. She was immune to being [[Turn undead|turned]] by [[cleric]]s.<ref name="PP-157" /><ref name="VGtW-214" />

'''Eaengul Skullcrown''' was a tavern owner inhabiting the city of [[Waterdeep]] during the mid-to-late 14{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="VGtW-p201">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|201}}</ref>

==Description==
He was bald and towered over most men. He was rumored to be able to naturally see the auras of magical enchantments.<ref name="VGtW-p201"/>

==Activities==
Eaengul owned the [[Friendly Flounder]], a tavern located on [[Book Street]] in Waterdeep's [[Waterdeep/Dock Ward|Dock Ward]].<ref name="VGtW-p201"/>

'''Nauloene Tharantra''' was a notorious hedonist and businesswoman living in [[Waterdeep]] in the latter half of the [[:Category:14th century|14th century DR]].<ref name="WN">{{Cite web/Waterdeep News/Tharantra Husband-Hunting Again}}</ref>

==Description==
Judging by her nickname and her profession, she was probably a redhead who honored [[Sune]], but this is uncertain.{{Speculation}} She lived on [[Sevenlamps Cut]] in the [[Castle Ward of Waterdeep]]. Her last known public appearance was sometime around the [[1374 DR|Year of Lightning Storms, 1374 DR]], when she stood on the stage of the [[Splendid Falcon]] theater and dancehall (also in the Castle Ward) and announced that she was looking to marry again, stating she felt sufficient time had passed since enduring her last husband's cruel nature. She wore a sparkling [[gown]] made of chains in a crisscross pattern adorned with blue [[diamond]] teardrop pendants dangling from various locations.<ref name="WN" />

==Personality==
She was a lady of pleasure, known to be free-loving and free-spending.<ref name="WN" />

==Activities==
For many years, Nauloene was the manager and hostess of the [[Heartflame]] festhall on [[Snail Street]] in the [[Dock Ward of Waterdeep]] until it burned down on the [[Night of Pirates]] in [[Uktar]], the [[1367 DR|Year of the Shield, 1367 DR]].<ref name="WN" />

==History==
Nauloene was married nine times before her announcement on the stage of the Splendid Falcon theater. Five of her husbands died; two fled Waterdeep citing personality and lifestyle conflicts; one was exposed as a [[doppelganger]] and killed in the streets of Waterdeep; and the ninth husband, merchant [[Mharelk Thorlkan]] from [[Innarlith]], she threw out and paid to have the marriage dissolved.<ref name="WN" />

==Rumors and legends==
The rumors in Waterdeep were that Nauloene had a hand in the death of at least two of her husbands.<ref name="WN" />

=== LINKED WIKI PAGE (first 5000 chars): Night of Pirates

The '''Night of Pirates''' was an event that took place in the [[Dock Ward of Waterdeep]] late in the month of [[Uktar]] in the [[1367 DR|Year of the Shield, 1367 DR]].

A pirate-lord hailing from the [[Nelanther Isles]], [[Elro Starankh]], led ninety-six crewmen from three ships into the [[Heartflame]] festhall on [[Snail Street]]. He proceeded to offer the proprietress, famed madam [[Nauloene Tharantra]], a commission of twenty [[Dragon (coin)|dragons]] for each girl who agreed to marry one of the seadogs. Each bride was to receive ten thousand dragons as a dowry in reverse. Only two of the ladies of the evening turned down the offer and a wild celebration with drinking and dancing ensued.

At some point in the revels, Starankh set fire to the festhall and it burned beyond the point of salvation. The relatively fancy [[Arrowsar Apartments]] were eventually built on the site of the former festhall.<ref name="WN">{{Cite web/Waterdeep News/Tharantra Husband-Hunting Again}}</ref>
