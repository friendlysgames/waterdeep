The '''City of the Dead''' was a large cemetery and ward in [[Waterdeep]]. Most of the city's dead were buried here.<ref name="City of the Dead">{{Cite book/City of the Dead}}</ref> By day, it also doubled as the city's major public park picnic area.<ref name="CoS:W-p102">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|102}}</ref><ref name="WDDH-p179">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|179}}</ref>

==Activities==
Besides burials and funerals, the City of the Dead was host to a variety of activities among Waterdhavians. These included, but were not limited to:  being an exercise run, a dueling ground, a playground, a highsun dining area, a meeting-moot, a place where courting lovers could meet in private, and a performance venue for [[minstrel]]s (and would-be minstrels).<ref>{{Cite web/Realmslore/Dawntree, Part One: Bold Dreams}}</ref>

At night the City of the Dead was off-limits to the public, yet it was still used by many during those hours for more nefarious activities.<ref name="CoS:W-p102" />

==History==
The area that would eventually become the site of the City of the Dead was used as a burial site as far back as the days of [[Nimoar's Hold]].<ref>{{Cite adventurers league/Hero of the Troll Wars|5-7}}</ref>

When Waterdeep was much smaller, a cemetery was established on the end of the plateau overlooking the plains east of the city. A low wall was built around the cemetery in [[1064 DR]] first establishing its boundaries north and east of the [[Trades Ward of Waterdeep|Trades Ward]] and south of the [[North Ward of Waterdeep|North Ward]]. Since that time, higher walls were built and gates into the City of the Dead were established which were only opened to the public during the daylight hours.<ref name="CoS-CG-p56-57">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|56-57}}</ref>

Space soon became hard to come by in the cemetery itself. Many of those who could not afford to be buried there found themselves interred in an area known as [[Grave Alley]] (later called [[Ilisar’s Alley]]). Their coffins were placed vertically below the cobblestones. This led to issues of the [[undead]] rising from the streets themselves and the practice was soon abandoned.<ref name="VGtW-p165">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|165}}</ref>

In order to alleviate the overcrowding, in [[1250 DR]], [[Ahghairon]] and other mages of Waterdeep began to establish ''[[portals]]'' from tombs and mausoleums to uninhabited demiplanes of various types where the dead were laid to rest. Most notable among these wizards, except for Ahghairon himself, was the ''portal'' and ''teleportation'' specialist, [[Anacaster]]. Two of his more well known portals led from [[The House of the Homeless]] and [[Merchants’ Rest]].<ref name="CoS:W-p102" />

Most noble houses, reportedly less than half, did not keep family tombs in the City of the Dead. They instead had crypts below their villas or were buried in family estates outside of Waterdeep.<ref name="CoS-CG-p56-57" />
 
The cemetery suffered extensive damage during the [[Spellplague]].<ref name="BT.p107-8">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|107-8}}</ref>

The '''Trades Ward''' was one of the seven wards of the city of [[Waterdeep]]. It was the main shopping district of Waterdeep and housed many of the city's guildhalls.<ref name="VGtW-p105" /> 

==Geography==
The ward was bounded in the south by the [[Southern Ward of Waterdeep|Southern Ward]], in the west by [[Castle Ward]], in the north by [[North Ward of Waterdeep|North Ward]] and the [[City of the Dead (Waterdeep)|City of the Dead]] and in the east by the [[Trollwall]].<ref name="VGtW-p105" />

==History==
In the late 14{{th}} century [[DR]], over the course of a [[tenday]] and more, streets across the Trades Ward experienced a number of [[ghost]]ly sightings that left many citizens, scribes, and members of the [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|City Watch]] confused. Some speculated if a [[wizard]] experimenting with [[illusion]]s may have been the cause. Others speculated it was a wizard seeking to harm local businesses, drive down local property values, or simply spread fear&mdash;though all who put forward these theories admitted to having nothing to backup their claims.<ref name="WN.GS" />

Some interpreted the apparitions as being a message &mdash; [[Sambral the Soothsayer]] would claim the dead were warning Waterdhavians of impending battles from underground invaders, [[Sheireera|Sheireera of the Prophecies]] would claim that it was the dead crying out against immorality and lawlessness in Waterdeep, and [[priest]] [[Thalomaun]] of the [[Church of Lathander]] would make a proclamation on [[Virgin's Square]] that it was a sign from the dead for the living of the affected neighborhoods to do away with old businesses and make way for new ones.<ref name="WN.GS">{{Cite web/Waterdeep News/Ghost Sightings}}</ref>

The '''North Ward''' was the northeastern sector of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="WatN-p24" /><ref name="CoS-p92" /> It was famous for being the quietest ward of the city, consisting mostly of residential areas.<ref name="VGtW-p81" />

==Geography==
The North Ward was delimited by the walls of the [[Waterdeep/City of the Dead|City of the Dead]] to the southeast and the [[Trollwall]] to the east and north. Its southern border was at [[Andamaar's Street]] and [[Trader's Way]] up to [[Shield Street]], which marked the western border until [[Vondil Street]]. North of that, the western border was defined by the [[High Road]] the rest of the way to the Trollwall.<ref name="CoS.AG-12">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|12}}</ref>

The architectural style of the North Ward was dominated by rowhouses, which filled the gaps between the villas of lesser [[Nobility of Waterdeep|noble houses]]. The ward also counted with a variety of taverns and shops.<ref name="VGtW-p81" /><ref name="WDH5e-p176">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|176}}</ref>

===Geographical Features===
The most prominent feature of the North Ward was the [[Cliffwatch]], a section of the Waterdeep plateau that was so steep and high that it provided a natural defense, so the area was marked by an interruption of the Trollwall between the [[Endcliff Tower]] and the [[Watchway Tower]] in the City of the Dead. A walkway traversed the entire Cliffwatch, along which some of the best inns and taverns of Waterdeep were located, taking advantage of the beautiful landscape to the east.<ref name="WDH5e-p176" /><ref name="CS-CG2e-p43">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|43}}</ref>

==History==
The North Ward was officially included as one of the official wards of Waterdeep in the {{YearlinkName|1276}}, together with the [[Waterdeep/Sea Ward|Sea Ward]].<ref name="GHR3e-p128" /><ref name="CS-CG2e-p31" />

In [[1368 DR]], the noble [[Cassalanter]] family loaned two ten-million [[Golden lion (coin)|golden lions]] with a huge interest rate. The first loan was to [[Widow Silvermane]] who ran a [[lottery]] in the North Ward, and the second one went to the [[Field of Triumph]] Race Track in the [[Waterdeep/Sea Ward|Sea Ward]]. '''Ahghairon''' ({{Pronounce|ah|GAIR|awn|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}), born '''Ahghairon Belnoth Undrur''',<ref name="GG-Ahghairon's name" /> was a [[mage]] from [[the North]]<ref name="CoS.CG-p26">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|26}}</ref> and the first [[Lords of Waterdeep#Open Lord|Open Lord]] of [[Waterdeep]]. He created the [[Lords of Waterdeep]], the system by which Waterdeep had been ruled ever since, except for a six-year period immediately following his death.<ref name="CoS.CG-p27">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|27}}</ref> 

{{Quote|There shall come a time when our city and its deepwater bay shall grow in fame and fortune across many realms and many worlds. Folk shall know of Waterdeep, our City of Splendors, and sing its praises. I have seen it thus, and I endeavor to make it true.|Ahghairon, ''circa'' [[1032 DR]]<ref name="CoS.CG-p25">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|25}}</ref>''}}

==Personality==
Ahghairon was known by several names during his youth, but went by only his first name during his time in Waterdeep.<ref name="GG-Ahghairon's name" />

==History==
{{quote|The original splendors of Waterdeep were Ahghairon's secrets, which keep us safe today and always, despite the predations of lesser so-called lords.|[[Agnan Crohal]], ''[[Tales Told Tavernside]]'', [[1268 DR]]<ref name="BS">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower||23}}</ref>}}<br/>
Ahghairon was born on [[Midsummer]]'s Night, in the {{YearlinkName|920}}.<ref name="CoS.CG-p29" /> He was named after his great-grandfather.<ref name="GG-Ahghairon's name" /> Legends say that the symbol of [[Mystra]] was glowing in the sky at the time.<ref name="CoS.CG-p29">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|29}}</ref>

===The Trollwars===
The [[Trollwars#Second Trollwar|Second Trollwar]] broke out in [[940 DR]],<ref name="CoS.CG-p29" /> as [[troll]]s were pushed down into the area later known as the [[Evermoors|Trollmoors]] by [[orc]] uprisings further north. After a decade of battles, a young Ahghairon distinguished himself, using his magic to turn the war against the trolls.<ref name="CoS.CG-p26" /> First, the Waterdhavian defenders drove the trolls back from their walls. The human realms then united under Ahghairon, War Captain [[Aeroth of Silverymoon]], and [[Samular Caradoon]] of [[Tyr]], and together they eradicated trolls across the Evermoors, with war finally coming to an end in [[952 DR]].<ref name=GHR>{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|113}}</ref>

At some point during the 940's DR, Aghairon was in [[Waterdeep]], at the time called Nimoar's Hold. Fires set to hold of the trolls threatened to destroy a large part of the town, so Aghairon asked adventurers to find and bring him [[Halaster Blackcloak|Halaster]]'s wards to bolster a ritual that would allow him to stop the fires. (More info about this adventure can be found in [[Hero of the Troll Wars]])

A third Trollwar almost took place in [[963 DR]], when the traitors [[Aviss]] and [[Fellandar]] led an army of trolls out of the [[Mere of Dead Men]] against the city. They knew well the city's defenses, and [[Castle Waterdeep]] had only just begun construction. But Ahghairon and a band of [[wizard]]s hurled Aviss and Fellandar into an extra-dimensional prison, thwarting the attack.<ref name="ADDc-33-p16">{{Cite comic/Summer in the City|16}}</ref> <ref group="note">The comic "[[Summer in the City]]" actually refers to "great Aglairon", but by the context it seems clear this is meant to be Ahghairon.</ref>

===Rise to Power===
At this time, Waterdeep was ruled by the Warlord [[Rhaulor]], who wanted to use the city's power and wealth to create an empire spanning [[northwest Faerûn]]. Ahghairon strongly disagreed with this, and when he voiced his concerns, Rhaulor attacked him. Ahghairon  used his magic to turn Rhaulor's sword into a snake, which bit and poisoned him. Rhaulor died and Ahghairon assumed control of Waterdeep, proclaiming himself the first Open Lord, and establishing a form of government involving Masked Lords, whose identities would remain private.<ref name="CoS.CG-p27" />

In [[1007 DR]], Ahghairon constructed his [[Ahghairon's Tower|wizard tower]], but positioned it outside the walls of Waterdeep (which since expanded). The '''Spellplague''', called the '''Blue Breath of Change''' by the inhabitants of [[Abeir]],<ref name="FRCG-p200">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|200}}</ref> was a disaster that struck [[Realmspace]] and even the [[planes]] themselves on the date of [[Tarsakh 29]] in the {{YearlinkName|1385}}, and was caused by [[Mystra (Midnight)|Mystra]]'s assassination at the hands of [[Cyric]] and [[Shar]].<ref name="FRCG-p50">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|50}}</ref><ref name="Ud-p64">{{Cite book/Undead|64}}</ref> It continued for a decade, leading to the [[Wailing Years]], during which [[arcane magic]] ceased to function and the planet of [[Toril]] was transformed.<ref name="TWY">{{Cite dragon/362/Countdown to the Realms: Spellplague: The Wailing Years}}</ref>

{{Quote|In the infamous, calamitous year of [[1385 DR]], a conspiracy between the goddess of darkness, Shar, and the god of trickery, Cyric, sought to end Mystra's control over the Weave and influence over the realms by cravenly assassinating her. But instead of merely breaking the goddess of magic's dominance, her death threw the Weave into utter chaos and collapse. Magic spells faltered, or failed entirely. Countless spellcasters were killed or driven insane. A vast storm of blue flame arose from the [[Mhair Jungles]] and swept the continent, churning and reshaping the land, and leaving whole regions devastated.|''[[Magical Histories]], Volume 2: The Spellplague''<ref name="BGIII">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

==Omens==
{{Quote|''"The white queen is troubled but can't say why. The black queen hates the white and gives the assassin a black coat. The assassin steals upon the white queen. She can't see him gliding through the shadows. The sword screams. The white queen falls. Her city falls. Stones fall in the cavern to crush the soothsayer. The tree burns and thrashes in agony. Branches break. Branches twist and grow together..."''|A vision of the Spellplague from the [[diviner]] [[Yaphyll]]<ref name="Ud-p7">{{Cite book/Undead|7}}</ref>}}

In her vision, the diviner Yaphyll referred to Mystra as the white queen and to Shar, goddess of the night, as the black queen. Cyric, god of murder, was the assassin;<ref name="Ud-p75">{{Cite book/Undead|75}}</ref> [[Savras]], the god of divination, was the soothsayer,{{speculation}}, and [[Dweomerheart|the city]], cavern and [[World Tree cosmology|tree]] symbolized the ordered structures of magic crumbling into chaos.<ref name="Ud-p75"/>

==Origins==
The most popular and widely accepted theory about the origins of the Spellplague was that this calamity was caused by the death of Mystra, the [[god]]dess of [[Magic]]. For eons, magic had been bound within Mystra's [[Weave]], the universal structure of arcane forces, and her death tore the Weave apart and unleashed the [[raw magic]] contained within it, creating the cataclysm that was later known as the Spellplague.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/><ref name="NV">{{Cite game/Neverwinter}}</ref>

Other scholars, however, didn't consider this to be a logical explanation. Historically, two earlier incarnations of Mystra had died before only to be reborn, and the Weave maintained its hold over the power of magic for the duration of her deaths. In these scholars' views, the truth would never be fully known to mortals, and perhaps not even to the [[god]]s.<ref name="NV"/><ref name="PoS-p79">{{Cite book/Plague of Spells|79}}</ref>

Around [[1396 DR]], the sentient [[construct]] [[Cynosure (construct)|Cynosure]] theorized that the escape of an entity named the [[Carnis|Traitor]] from its confinement in the dungeons of [[Stardeep]], around the same time Mystra was murdered, may have also influenced the Spellplague's virulent onslaught.<ref name="PoS-p79"/>

Around [[1479 DR]], scholars and sages who studied the [[Neverwinter/Chasm|Chasm]] of [[Neverwinter]], such as [[Rhazzad]], believed the [[aboleth]]s of the [[Abolethic Sovereignty]] may have been the actual creators of the Spellplague, although there were no definite proof.<ref name="NV"/>

The '''Deepwinter Vault''' was a mausoleum in the [[Waterdeep/City of the Dead|City of the Dead]] in [[Waterdeep]] that belonged to the defunct noble [[Deepwinter (family)|Deepwinter]] family. It was also one of the entrances to the former lair of [[Artor Morlin]], the [[vampire]] lord of Waterdeep,<ref name="Dun127-66" /> and was the reason his subterranean structure was called the [[Dungeon of the Crypt]].<ref name="CoS-CG-10">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|10}}</ref>

==Location==
The City of the Dead was a sprawling walled cemetery on the eastern edge of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="WatN-cover">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North/Inside cover}}</ref><ref name="CoS-CG-9">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|9}}</ref><ref name="CoSW-102">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|102}}</ref><ref name="Dun127-65">{{Cite dungeon/127/Vampires of Waterdeep: Dungeon of the Crypt|65}}</ref> The Deepwinter Vault was located in the northern part of the cemetery near the wall that separated the burial grounds from [[Zendulth Street]],<ref name="CoSW-101,104">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|101,104}}</ref> set back from the main path by a short walkway.<ref name="Dun127-66" />

==Structure==
[[File:Dungeon127-Deepwinter Vault-landscape-Lazzaretti.png|thumb|right|270px|''Floorplan of Deepwinter Vault, ''circa'' 1372 DR.'']]
Deepwinter Vault was approximately {{SI|90|ft|long=yes}} square<ref name="Dun127-65" /> with two large wooden doors bound in iron in the center of the south wall, atop a wide, shallow staircase of five steps. As of the {{YearlinkName|1372}}, the one-story white [[marble]] structure showed signs of age with cracked masonry, [[lichen]] stains, and ivy-covered walls. The ivy hid carvings of landscapes covered in deep snow and the doors had broken locks and stood slightly ajar.<ref name="Dun127-66" />

==Interior==
The mausoleum was divided into three sections of equal measure: a forechamber, the Deepwinter crypts, and the Hall of the Patriarch.<ref name="Dun127-65" /><ref name="Du127WE-17">{{Cite web enhancement/Dungeon 127 Map and Handout Supplement|17}}</ref>

===Forechamber===
The double doors led into a grand chamber the full width of the structure and about {{SI|25|ft|long=yes}} deep.<ref name="Dun127-65" /><ref name="Du127WE-17" /> Its walls were filled with stone frescoes that revealed scenes of Deepwinter triumphs interspersed with symbols of the [[church of Auril]]. At any time of the year, the walls were coated with a thin film of ice and minor icicles hung from the ceiling—the remnants of a [[magical]] trap placed on the room in the days when the Deepwinters still thrived—that kept the room temperature at {{SI|32|F}}. Around the perimeter of the room were at least thirty short pillars once used as pedestals for displaying family heirlooms, but by 1372 DR most of them had been vandalized by treasure hunters looking for hidden caches. In the center of the floor was the crest of the Deepwinter family, a snow-capped mountain with a large, stylized snowflake above and to the left of the peak. The only other exit was a narrow door in the center of the north wall.<ref name="Dun127-66" />

===Deepwinter Crypts===
The middle third of the mausoleum was filled with individual crypts. The '''Lords of Waterdeep''' were the mysterious and predominantly anonymous ruling council of [[Waterdeep]], having governed the city from the 11{{th}} century onwards.<ref name="CoS.CG-p8">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|8}}</ref><ref name="CS.W-p52" /> They oversaw the regional economy,<ref name="CS.W-p17" /><ref name="CS.W-p60" /> maintained security,<ref name="CS.W-p37" /> performed diplomacy,<ref name="CS.W-p53" /> and held legal jurisdiction over the city<ref name="WatN-p18" /> and exert their will over the surrounding lands for approximately {{SI|100|mi|k}} beyond its walls.<ref name="FRCS3e-p299">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|299}}</ref>

==Organization==
A full council comprised 20 Lords, though this number might vary from 16 to 29.<ref>{{Cite book/Death Masks|87}}</ref> The full number of Lords was not always known and some Lords were kept secret 

===Open Lord===
[[File:LordsOfWaterdeep.png|thumb|right|300px|Open Lord [[Piergeiron the Paladinson|Piergeiron]] and a Masked Lord in official garments ''circa'' [[1372 DR]].]]
The Open Lord of Waterdeep (also called the "Unmasked Lord")<ref name="NGSF.CC-p9">{{Cite book/The North: Guide to the Savage Frontier/Cities & Civilization|9}}</ref> was the sole member of the Lords of Waterdeep whose identity was publicly known.<ref name="CS.W-p52" /> They held a number of additional titles including [[City Guard (Waterdeep)#Ranks|Warden]] of Waterdeep, Overmaster of the [[Waterdeep#Guilds|Guilds]], and Commander of [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|the Watch]].<ref name="CS.W-p53">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|53}}</ref> The Open Lord was also the city's chief diplomat.<ref name="CS.W-p53" />

There were seven [[Lords of Waterdeep#Open Lord|Open Lords]] of [[Waterdeep]] to [[1489 DR]]:<ref name="CS.W-p52" /><ref name="CoS.CG-p67">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|67}}</ref><ref name="DtU-M-p118">{{Cite game/Descent to Undermountain/Manual|118}}</ref><ref name="SCAG-p55" >{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|55}}</ref>
* [[Ahghairon]] the Mage
* [[Baeron Silmaeril]]
* [[Lhestyn]] the Masked Lady
* [[Piergeiron the Paladinson]]
* [[Caladorn Cassalanter]]
* [[Dagult Neverember]]
* [[Laeral Silverhand]]

=== Masked Lords ===
The Lords of Waterdeep were only seen in public when sitting in the Lord's Court or when appearing every four years at [[Shieldmeet]] Council when the Lord's Rule is reaffirmed.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The identities of the Lords, other than the Open Lord, were not supposed to be known to the public, although they were known to each other.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The intent of this was to ensure that the Lords were free of coercion and could be drawn from any social strata within the city.<ref name="CoS.CG-p67" /> While the masked Lords were considered to be equal to the Open Lord in authority, some executive authority was reserved for the Open Lord, including the vetting and selection of new Lords,<ref name="CS.W-p52" /> the naming of the next Open Lord,<ref>{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|31}}</ref> and determining the needed number of Lords.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" />

This '''guard tower''' was one of the major torchtowers of [[Waterdeep]]'s [[Trollwall]], located in the city's graveyard district, the [[Waterdeep/City of the Dead|City of the Dead]].<ref name="CoS.W-p101,104" /><ref group="note" name="torchtower">Every tower connected to the Trollwall falls under the designation of "torchtower."</ref>

==Location==
This tower was located on the southeastern corner of the City of the Dead. Outside of the City of the Dead, in the [[Trades Ward of Waterdeep]], its exterior stood along [[Wall Way]].<ref name="CoS-map" /><ref name="CoS.W-p101,104" />

==Structure==
This tower stood four stories tall.<ref name="CoS.W-p101,104" />
===Interior===
Being a torchtower,<ref group="note" name="torchtower" /> this structure had all the interior features that were common among them. This included a debriefing room, a small armory, holding cells, storage rooms for the holding of evidence or confiscated items, some bedchambers, and some garderobes.<ref name="edverse">{{Cite web/theedverse/2021-10-17 - Waterdeep's Torchtowers}}</ref>

==Activities==
Like all other torchtowers, this structure had three beacon-fires that were constantly kept on standby.<ref name="edverse" />

==Inhabitants==
Like all torchtowers, this structure was manned by members of the City Watch.<ref name="edverse" /><ref name="CoS.W-p35">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|35}}</ref> Four members of the Watch were always stationed inside of the tower, while one was posted outside to watch for any trouble in the nearby alleyways abutting the City of the Dead.<ref name="CoS.CG-p57">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|57}}</ref>

'''Warriors' Monument,'''<ref name="WatN-p24">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|24}}</ref><ref name="CoS.CG-p57">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|57}}</ref><ref name="CoS.W-p102">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|102}}</ref><ref name="W.DH-p179">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|179}}</ref> also occasionally known as the '''Warriors of Waterdeep''',<ref name="CS-p12">{{Cite book/City System|12}}</ref> was a monument in [[Waterdeep]]'s [[Waterdeep/City of the Dead|City of the Dead]] meant to honor all those who ever fought to defend the city.<ref name="WatN-p24" /><ref name="CoS.CG-p57" /><ref name="CoS.W-p102" /><ref name="W.DH-p179" />

==Description==
Carved of stone and standing roughly {{SI|60|ft|long=yes}} tall,<ref name="WatN-p24" /><ref name="CoS.CG-p57" /><ref name="CoS.W-p102" /><ref name="W.DH-p179" /><ref name="KotLD-p12" /> Warriors' Monument was one of the tallest structures in the City of the Dead.<ref name="KotLD-p12">{{Cite book/Knight of the Living Dead|12}}</ref> In addition to being an intricate statue, the monument acted as a fountain, with water gushing out from wounds on its stone figures.<ref name="W.DH-p179" />

It depicted a group of [[warrior]]s in a circular formation,<ref name="WatN-p24" /><ref name="CoS.CG-p57" /><ref name="CoS.W-p102" /><ref name="W.DH-p179" /> three men and two women,<ref name="CoS.CG-p57" /><ref name="CoS.W-p102" /> striking down hordes of enemies that were falling backward and outward from their circle. These enemies included [[barbarian]]s, [[hobgoblin]]s, [[orc]]s, [[troll]]s,<ref name="WatN-p24" /><ref name="CoS.CG-p57" /><ref name="CoS.W-p102" /><ref name="W.DH-p179" /> and [[bugbear]]s.<ref name="KotLD-p12" /> A later addition to the monument was a warrior riding upon a [[griffin]], high above the other stone figures, and [[spear]]ing<ref name="CoS.CG-p57" /><ref name="CoS.W-p102" /><ref name="W.DH-p179" /> a [[Night rider|skeletal knight]] with a [[breastplate]] bearing the symbol of [[Myrkul]].<ref name="W.DH-p179" />

==History==
In [[1361 DR]],<ref name="CoS.CG-p57" /> three years following the event known as the [[Time of Troubles]], Warriors' Monument was renovated and magically altered to feature a minion of Myrkul.<ref name="CoS.CG-p57" /><ref name="CoS.W-p102" /> This addition was meant to commemorate those who perished fighting against the death god's invasion of Waterdeep.<ref name="CoS.CG-p57" />
