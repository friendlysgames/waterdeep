The '''City Watch''' of [[Waterdeep]] served as the police force within the city.<ref name="ADDc-01">{{Cite comic/The Gathering}}</ref><ref name="CoS.W-p35" /> Its strict code of conduct and well trained operatives made the Watch one of the most trusted police forces in [[Faerûn]].<ref name="WDH5e-p168">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|168}}</ref>

{{Quote|To distinguish between Waterdeep's different groups of guardians, keep this handy mnemonic in mind: [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|the Guard]] guards the walls while the Watch watches all.|[[Volo]]<ref name="MM5eR-p162">{{Cite book/Monster Manual 5th edition (revised)|162}}</ref>}}

==Organization==
The Watch's purpose was not only to enforce the law, but also to ensure the well-being of Waterdhavians.<ref name="CoS.W-p35" />

It had a centralized command led by the Commander of the Watch, who supervised three watchlords,<ref name="THO2004">{{Cite web/Candlekeep forum/Questions for Ed Greenwood (2004)/Ranks of the Waterdeep City Guard and City Watch}}</ref><ref name="PoF-p29">{{Cite book/Power of Faerûn|29}}</ref> or senior commanders: the Mage Civilar in charge of the [[wizard]] corps; the Senior Armsmaster, who was in charge of supplies and reported directly to the Commander of the Watch; and the Grand Civilar, who commanded the ward civilars and held the same rank as the Mage Civilar.<ref name="CoS2e-p75-76" /><ref name="CoS.W-p35" /><ref name="WDH5e-p198" /> The Commander of the Watch reported directly to the [[Open Lord]].<ref name="WDH5e-p198" />

Each ward of Waterdeep was patrolled by a division of the Watch under the command of a ward civilar, or [[Generic City Watch major (Waterdeep)|major]], who oversaw all the senior civilars commanding the watch stations of their respective wards.<ref name="CoS2e-p75-76" /><ref name="CoS.W-p35" /><ref name="WDH5e-p198" />{{Border box float

==Activities==
[[File:Delzorin.png|thumb|300px|right|''A traffic warden gives directions at the corner of [[Delzorin Street]] and [[Whaelgond Way]].'']]
{{Quote|I have sworn to uphold the laws and the edicts of the Lords of the city. For much of my life, I've dedicated myself to that goal of keeping the peace, and I'm happy with that. In Waterdeep, if wrong is being done or the spirit of the law is broken, the situation will be corrected by me and by the Watch, worry not of that. Law and order—sometimes it's all that separates us from becoming Undermountain or Hellgate Keep.|[[Captain Rulathon]]<ref name="CoS-CG-p75">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|75}}</ref>}}

The City Watch performed everyday policing duties. They never acted outside the walls of the city<ref name="ADDc-01"/> and did not pay much attention to the city's sewers.<ref name="WDH5e-p27">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|27}}</ref> They were a peacekeeping force, also responsible for solving crimes and apprehending criminals.<ref name="WDH5e-p198" />

During particularly busy hours at important intersections, the Watch also employed traffic wardens to manage the flow of people and vehicles. Traffic wardens carried two flags: one blue, which signaled for traffic to proceed, and one yellow, which signaled for it to stop. They also employed whistles to call the attention of passersby, if necessary.<ref name="WDH5e-p171">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|171}}</ref>

==Tactics==
When within the walls of Waterdeep, the City Watch typically patrolled in groups of four, with two patrolmen, one armar, and one civilar.<ref name="ADDc-01"/><ref name="CoS.W-p35"/>

In calmer districts such as the [[Castle Ward of Waterdeep|Castle Ward]], those detachments were even smaller, often consisting of only two officers. Conversely, locations such as the [[Dock Ward of Waterdeep|Dock Ward]] were patrolled by groups of eight during the day and twelve at night.<ref name="WDH5e-p168" />

Many spellcasters within the City Watch were trained in the use of the spell ''[[trembling horn]]'', which was developed for the organization by [[Thyriellentha Snome|Thyri Snome]]. It allowed watchmen place an invisible, magical tracer that caused alarm-like effects that were widely recognizable among the Waterdhavian populace.<ref name="CoS.CG-p86"/>

==Base of Operations==
The Watch's main base of operations was the [[Palace of Waterdeep]].<ref name="CoS.W-p35" />

There were multiple City Watch posts in Waterdeep. They were usually not located on the main streets, but were all identified by a green and gold lantern that hung outside, permanently lit by a ''[[continual flame]]'' [[spell]]. These posts served as local headquarters, armories, stations for people to report crimes, and temporary jailhouses to hold prisoners until they were moved to a courthouse.<ref name="WDH5e-p168" /> Watch stations were always led by a captain.<ref name="WDH5e-p198" />

==Possessions==
[[File:Badge_of_the_watch-5e.jpg|thumb|200px|right|''A  captain's ''[[badge of the Watch]]''.'']]
Members of the City Watch typically wore [[leather armor|leather]] and [[chain mail|chain armor]] and carried sturdy rods like [[club]]s, [[short sword]]s, and [[dagger]]s.<ref name="ADDc-01"/><ref name="CoS.W-p35"/> Many of them also carried unique  sounding horns, that functioned along with [[trembling horn|specific spells]] unique to the members of their organization.<ref name="CoS.CG-p86">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|86}}</ref>

In the 14{{th}} century [[DR]], City Watch captains carried an ''[[amulet of the Watch]]'', a [[magic item]] that allowed the wearer to summon reinforcements from the Watch or from the [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]].<ref name="CoSW3e-p149">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|149}}</ref> In the 15{{th}} century DR, officers who achieved the rank of captain were awarded a ''[[badge of the Watch]]'', a magic item that provided the wearer with additional protection against attacks and allowed the [[Open Lord]] to immediately know the wearer's location. Only the Open Lord could award ''badges of the Watch''.<ref name="WDH5e-p189">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|189}}</ref>

In the late 14{{th}} century DR, some of the members of the Watch were given fashionable-looking ''[[cloak of protection versus evil|cloaks of protection versus evil]]'' by [[Khelben Arunsun]] and one of his apprentices who later opened his own shop that sold similar magic [[cloak]]s.<ref name="nwn">{{Cite game/Neverwinter Nights}}</ref>

==History==
By [[1479 DR]], the [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]] had been folded into the Watch, becoming a division of its organization.<ref name="FRCG-p197">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide}}</ref><ref name="DS-ch1">{{Cite digital book/Downshadow||1}}</ref> As the Guard was better equipped and trained, particularly dangerous tasks inside Waterdeep were given to the Guard instead, such as patrolling the [[Waterdeep/Sewers|sewers]] and [[Undermountain]].<ref name="DS-ch7">{{Cite digital book/Downshadow||7}}</ref>

The '''City Guard''' was a highly trained military organization of fully-armored soldiers responsible for defending the great city of [[Waterdeep]] and patrolling its surroundings.<ref name="CS.W-p33" /><ref name="ADDc-01">{{Cite comic/The Gathering}}</ref><ref name="CoS2e-p75-76" />

{{Quote|To distinguish between Waterdeep's different groups of guardians, keep this handy mnemonic in mind: the Guard guards the walls while [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|the Watch]] watches all.|[[Volo]]<ref name="MM5eR-p162">{{Cite book/Monster Manual 5th edition (revised)|162}}</ref>}}

==Organization==
The City Guard served many purposes related to defending the city. Within Waterdeep, the Guard protected the city walls, gates, and its important buildings, as well as provided escorts for government officials. It also patrolled the harbor and the roads leading from Waterdeep to [[Amphail (village)|Amphail]], [[Goldenfields]], and [[Daggerford]].<ref name="WDH5e-p197" />

The exact size of the City Guard was a secret only known to the [[Lords of Waterdeep]], but it was thought to be around 1,200 strong.<ref name="CoS2e-p75-76" /> In times of war, it could enlist mercenaries and grow to a contingent of up to 12,000 members.<ref name="CS.W-p33"/>

Members of the City Guard were paid a salary of 6 [[gp]] per month. No fee was required to join the ranks.<ref name="CS.W-p33"/>

A special aquatic division of the Guard was recruited from the underwater residents of [[Deepwater Harbor]].<ref name="CS.W-p19">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|19}}</ref> The [[merfolk]] of [[Tharqualnaar]] and the [[Aquatic elf|sea elves]] of [[T'Quession]] provided Guards who were responsible for defending Waterdeep against aquatic threats, recovering bodies, salvaging lost cargo, and patrolling a short distance outside the harbor.<ref name="CS.W-p111">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|111}}</ref> In particular, they guarded the perimeter of [[Umberlee's Cache]], with the underwater lighthouse [[Deepwater Beacon]] serving as a guiding light and rally point.<ref name="CS.W-p108">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|108}}</ref>

These underwater Guards were not provided with weapons and equipment by the city, but were given a monthly 25 gp stipend of food, medical supplies, and [[trade bar]]s as compensation.<ref name="CS.W-p111" />

===Ranks===
Like the [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|City Watch]], the City Guard had specific titles for the members of their forces. A City Guard patrol usually consisted of twelve members, whose commanding officer was called the civilar. A commanding officer holding the rank of [[generic City Guard captain (Waterdeep)|captain]] was known as an aumarr, or senior civilar, while one holding the rank of [[generic City Guard lieutenant (Waterdeep)|lieutenant]] was known as a shieldlar, or, simply, civilar. The patrol also contained one armar, or [[Generic City Guard sergeant (Waterdeep)|sergeant]], and ten [[Generic City Guard private (Waterdeep)|guardsmen or guardswomen]], or trusties.<ref name="CS.W-p33" /><ref name="THO2004">{{Cite web/Candlekeep forum/Questions for Ed Greenwood (2004)/Ranks of the Waterdeep City Guard and City Watch}}</ref><ref name="PF-p29">{{Cite book/Power of Faerûn|29}}</ref> A patrol could also be led by a sergeant.<ref name="ADDc-01" />

Other officer positions existed within the ranks of the Guard, not typically associated with regular patrols. In particular, the ranks of valabrar and torsin were responsible for escorting important individuals within the city, while also composing honor guards and bodyguards to diplomats and illustrious visitors to the city. Officers holding those positions outranked captains.<ref name="THO2004" /><ref name="PF-p29" /><ref name="CS.W-p33" />

The City Guard also had a special branch called the [[Griffon Cavalry]]. Its members were responsible for patrolling the skies of the city riding [[griffon]]s.<ref name="WDH5e-p197" />

The commanding positions of the Guard held specific individual titles. Some of those positions existed at all times, while others only during war. The perennial command positions were [[Seneschal of Castle Waterdeep]], [[Defender of the Harbor]], [[Master of the North Towers]], and [[Master of the South Towers]]. Command positions only activated during wartime were the [[Lords' Hand]] and [[Lords' Champion]]. The [[Warden of Waterdeep]] commanded the entire organization.<ref name="WDH5e-p197" /><ref name="PF-p29" />

==Activities==
The City Guard's duty was to investigate foreign threats to Waterdeep and to protect the city against them.<ref name="ADDc-01" /> Their duties included guarding the city's gates, important locations, and citizens.<ref name="WDH5e-p197" /><ref name="Waterdeep">{{Cite web|url=http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/fr/20010516c|title=Waterdeep|author=[[Rob Heinsoo]], [[Ed Greenwood]], [[Sean K Reynolds]]|date=|work=Sneak Peek|publisher=[[Wizards of the Coast]]|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20071113084854/http://www.wizards.com:80/default.asp?x=dnd/fr/20010516c |archivedate=2007-11-13|accessdate=2019-03-11}}</ref> City Guards could be found posted at [[Castle Waterdeep]], [[Piergeron's Palace]], the city walls, and its surrounding countryside.<ref name="CS.W-p33" />

City Guard patrols also kept watch on the roads leading from Waterdeep to [[Amphail (village)|Amphail]], [[Daggerford]], and [[Goldenfields]].<ref name="WDH5e-p197" /> As the [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|City Watch]] did not operate within the [[Waterdeep/Field Ward|Field Ward]], the guards assigned to the gates and walls were the only official form of protection. As it was technically outside of the city, they were obliged to assist anyone who was threatened while moving in or out of the city, just as they would when patrolling afield.<ref name="WDH5e-p180">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|180}}</ref>

==Possessions==
Around [[1357]] and [[1358 DR]], soldiers of the City Guard wore yellow [[scale armor]] under green cloaks and [[tabard]]s. They wore matching conical helms of green and gold with fur trim, chainmail neck-guards, and topped by a spike.<ref name="ADDc-01" /><ref name="ADDc-20" /> They could also carry a shield. Both tabard and shield bore the symbol of Waterdeep, a crescent moon reflected over a bay, surrounded by seven stars. They wielded [[longsword]]s, [[dagger]]s, and [[dart]]s coated with a paralytic [[poison]]. On missions outside, they were also armed with [[bow]]s.<ref name="ADDc-01" />

By [[1368 DR]], the standard uniform of the City Guard was silver scale mail under black, gold-trimmed tabards. Standard armament included daggers, [[rod]]s, [[shortbow]]s, and [[shortsword]]s, with the possibility of additional armament for specific assignments.<ref name="CoS2e-p75-76" />

Around [[1372 DR]], Waterdeep's City Guards wore a uniform of polished [[chainmail]] covered with a tabard in black cloth with gold trim. They carried various weapons as their posts would require, including longswords, shortbows, [[shortspear]]s, shortswords, daggers and [[club]]s.<ref name="CS.W-p33" />

Whenever Waterdeep reached highs of {{SIrange|80|89|F|long=yes}} in the summer season, the City Guard would dress down from sweltering full body armor into more appropriate protection. This included back and [[breastplate|breast]] plates, [[gorget]]s, metal [[codpiece]]s, and light helms. They would also abstain from wearing [[gauntlet]]s and dress in [[surcoat]]s that were of a white hue in order to minimize heat absorption.<ref>{{Cite web/theedverse/2019-05-16 - What the Waterdhavian Guard Wears in the Summer}}</ref>

==History==
[[File:City_guard_and_magister-5e.jpg|thumb|250px|''Members of the City Guard hold down a suspect while a [[Magister (Waterdeep)|magister]] dispenses judgment.'']]
One day in the {{YearlinkName|1357}}, a huntsman informed the City Guard of a magical tower erected within a single day, a full day's hard ride north of Waterdeep's [[Trollwall]]. A City Guard patrol, including [[Pern]], investigated and attempted to scale the tower. [[Imgig Zu]], master of the tower, animated his [[gargoyle]] minions to attack the guardsmen.<ref name="ADDc-01" />

In the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, when the skies over the city darkened and an interdimensional wormhole (from the [[Great Door]]) disgorged a swarm of [[gargoyle]]s and [[kenku (winged)|kenku]]s over the city, a City Guard troop and [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] arrived to hold them off. <ref name="ADDc-18">{{Cite comic/Day of the Darkening|5}}</ref> Shortly after, during the [[Time of Troubles]], members of the City Guard stationed in Castle Waterdeep during a party tried to stop a trio of the [[Dark Army of the Night]] from kidnapping a [[Shar|false avatar]] of [[Selûne]]. However, they were hindered by the Dark Army's utter darkness and then by blinding light. The avatar herself stopped the trio with a ''[[bind]]'' spell, and the City Guard arrested them. Later, Lord [[Piergeiron]] ordered [[Rathnar]] of the City Guard to arrest [[Kyriani]] after she fought with the avatar, but [[Khelben Arunsun]] took charge of the prisoner and let her go.<ref name="ADDc-20">{{Cite comic/Dark of the Moon|11-14,19-20}}</ref>

The 3{{rd}} company of the High Guards fought in the final battle of the [[Second Dragonspear War]] at the [[Dragonspear Castle|castle]] in the {{YearlinkName|1363}}.<ref name="HoD-p26">{{Cite book/Hordes of Dragonspear|26}}</ref>

When the [[Shining Crusade]] threatened the Sword Coast in the {{YearlinkName|1368}}, one hundred guardsmen joined the [[Coalition]] army under the command of [[Cedric deLancie|Torsin deLancie]].<ref name="BG.SoD">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate: Siege of Dragonspear}}</ref>

After the murder of Warden [[Ezender Drayth]] in [[1491 DR]], [[Elminster Aumar]] accepted the position of Warden of Waterdeep, answering directly to [[Open Lord]] [[Laeral Silverhand]].<ref name="WDH5e-p197" />

The '''Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors''' was the [[guild]] of [[wizard]]s and [[sorcerer]]s for the city of [[Waterdeep]]. However, while technically a guild, the Watchful Order functioned primarily as a mage academy. The Watchful Order also policed the use of [[magic]] within Waterdeep, encouraging wizards to use magic wisely.<ref name="CoS|30" />

==Activities==
The Order provided many services to its members, including a venue for trading [[spell]] components and [[magical item]]s. The Order served Waterdeep by providing spell wards for wealthy inhabitants and fire fighting through the use of [[water elemental]]s.<ref name="CoS|30">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|30}}</ref>

The Order also provided many services to the city. Members could earn extra money serving as a fire guard, firefighter, or spell guard. A fire guard was hired to stand watch over a building and squelch any fires that may start as well as alert the firefighters. A firefighter is just that - they are summoned to put out fires with [[magic]]. A spell guard serves as a bodyguard to counter magical attacks. Pay was typically 5, 10, and 10 [[Gold|gold pieces]] per night/day, respectively.<ref name="CoSW-WW-p56">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|56}}</ref>

The '''Griffon Cavalry''' was the air cavalry of the city of [[Waterdeep]] in the 13{{th}}, 14{{th}}, and 15{{th}} centuries [[DR]]. Part of the [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]], it was a militia organization made up of soldiers, and [[griffon]]s, responsible for defending and patrolling the great city.<ref name="CS.W-p33">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|33–34}}</ref><ref name="ADDc-01">{{Cite comic/The Gathering}}</ref>

==History==
The history of the Griffon Cavalry can perhaps be traced as far back as the second [[Trollwars]] ([[940 DR]] to [[952 DR]]) when [[Ilithrew Belabranta]] rode his griffon into battle. One of his later heirs, Lord [[Palinor Belabranta]], did the same versus the orc hordes of the Black Claw tribe in the {{YearlinkName|1026}}. Since then, House [[Belabranta]] was a noble family associated with the breeding and training of griffons.<ref name="CS.W-p133"/>

In the {{YearlinkName|1235}}, [[Lords of Waterdeep|Open Lord]] [[Ahghairon]] officially declared the Griffon Cavalry part of the City Guard during a huge orc siege on the city. The riders not only fought the orc hordes again, but were also used to shuttle in much needed supplies to the city.<ref name="CoS-CG-p30">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|30}}</ref>

The Griffon Cavalry later established a base of operations on the landward side of [[Mount Waterdeep]] called [[Peaktop Aerie]], or Peaktop Eyrie. There they stabled over twenty griffons with another half-dozen housed at [[Castle Waterdeep]].<ref name="CoS-WW-p54"/>

As of [[1479 DR]], however, the Griffon Cavalry had been disbanded for years, as the griffons died in combat but were never replaced.<ref name="FRCG-p197">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|197}}</ref>

Then, in the {{YearlinkName|1491}}, Open Lord [[Laeral Silverhand]] proposed refounding the Griffon Cavalry, and threatened to make [[Mirt]] do it.<ref name="DM-ch5">{{Cite book/Death Masks|?|5}}</ref> Either way, this goal was achieved by the {{YearlinkName|1492}}, with the newly procured griffons continuing to be supplied by the Belabranta family.<ref name="WDH-p39">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|39}}</ref>

The '''Palace of Waterdeep''', also known as the '''Lords' Palace'''<ref name="RoT-p19" /> and previously often called '''Piergeiron's Palace''',<ref name="FRCG-p198">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|198}}</ref> was the residence of the [[Open Lord]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="WatN-p15">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|15}}</ref><ref name="CS.W">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|96}}</ref>

==Location==
The white [[marble]] palace was located at the foot of [[Mount Waterdeep]] at the intersection of the [[Street of Silks]] and [[Waterdeep Way]]. In the courtyard in front of the palace stood [[Ahghairon's Tower]], and just to the north were the palace stables, paddock, and guard barracks.<ref name="CS.W"/>

==Structure==
The palace featured a [[Lantan]]ese "golemwork clock" called 'Timehands' that comprised dials on three faces of its highest tower. Two bells of different tones would ring out each hour, marking the hour with their number of rings.<ref name="FRCG-p198" />

==Interior==
Inside Piergeiron's Palace was located a rare place of [[Ao]]ist worship, with a spacious chapel lined with [[oak]]en pews. Bright painted [[plaster]] walls were decorated with [[gold]], [[silver]], and carved [[limestone]] and its marble floors were covered with exotic carpets from [[Shou Lung]] and illuminated with [[jewel]]ed chandeliers. At the center of the furthest wall stood a large intricate stained glass window—the Eye of Ao. It gazed down on the chapel's visitors in radiant indifference.<ref name="TD">{{Cite book/The Diamond||1}}</ref>

A deep well could be found in the prisons and dungeons of the Palace. It was gated with enchanted oaken doors, magically and physically barred, and ensorcelled with the message "DO NOT OPEN UNDER PAIN OF DEATH." The welled path connected the palace with [[Undermountain]].<ref name="TD-ch4">{{Cite book/The Diamond||4}}</ref>

==Activities==
The Palace served multiple functions for the administration of the city. It was the main office for many of the city's officials including  [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|City Watch]], [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]], and city clerks. The [[Lords' Court]] also met in a large chamber on the second floor.<ref name="CS.W"/>

There were embassies here from many other countries and cities, although they were not required to be on the palace grounds. Guest quarters for foreign dignitaries without embassies were also available.<ref name="CS.W"/>

==History==
The Palace was built over the ruins of an abbey dedicated to [[Chauntea]] that was destroyed during the [[Trollwars#Second_Trollwar|Second Trollwar]] in the mid–10{{th}} century [[DR]]. In the 1370s, relatively harmless [[ghost]]s of Chauntean monks and clerics could still be seen occasionally roaming halls and staircases that no longer existed, passing through the walls of the modern-day castle.<ref name="RBN-6">{{Cite web/Realms by Night/Part Six: The Monks of the Three Candles}}</ref>

The construction and instillation of 'Timehands' was completed in the {{YearlinkName|1445}}.<ref name="FRCG-p198" />

The four meetings of the [[Council of Waterdeep]] were hosted in the Lords' Palace some time during the 1480s DR.<ref name="RoT-p19">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|19}}</ref>{{ToD-time}}

=== Masked Lords ===
The Lords of Waterdeep were only seen in public when sitting in the Lord's Court or when appearing every four years at [[Shieldmeet]] Council when the Lord's Rule is reaffirmed.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The identities of the Lords, other than the Open Lord, were not supposed to be known to the public, although they were known to each other.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The intent of this was to ensure that the Lords were free of coercion and could be drawn from any social strata within the city.<ref name="CoS.CG-p67" /> While the masked Lords were considered to be equal to the Open Lord in authority, some executive authority was reserved for the Open Lord, including the vetting and selection of new Lords,<ref name="CS.W-p52" /> the naming of the next Open Lord,<ref>{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|31}}</ref> and determining the needed number of Lords.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" />

'''Faerûn''' ({{Pronounce|fay|ROON|refs=<ref name="FRCS2e-r-p4">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|4}}</ref>|audio=Faerûn.ogg}} {{Pronounce|FAY|er|OON|primary stress=3|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-DM-p4">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Set/DM's Sourcebook of the Realms|4}}</ref>|alt=yes}}) was a major continent on the planet of [[Toril]].<ref name="FRCS-p5">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|5}}</ref><ref name="TFRA-p1">{{Cite book/The Forgotten Realms Atlas|1-3}}</ref><ref group="note">As the key setting of the ''[[Forgotten Realms]]'', the majority of official ''Forgotten Realms'' material covers this continent, and it has had more attention than any other area of the planet.</ref>
{{quote|''Someone should have dared, long ago, and done so as often as it took to break you of this serenely wrong view of the ways of the world. Hear this, [[Narantha Crownsilver|Narantha]], and hear it well: Faerûn is not going to change to your will. Either you must change to dwell in it, or it will break you.''|[[Florin Falconhand]] of the [[Knights of Myth Drannor]]<ref name="soe-p72">{{Cite book/Swords of Eveningstar/Hardcover 2006|72}}</ref>}}

==Etymology==
The word "Faerûn" was a modified version of "Faerie", the name of the homeland of ancient [[elves]]. The term was coined by [[Durothil (prince)|Durothil]], prince of [[Tintageer]], [[Faerie]].<ref name="e:ioe-p97">{{Cite book/Evermeet: Island of Elves/MMP|97}}</ref>

==Geography==
{{main|Portal:Geography}}
The continent included terrain that was as varied as any other. Besides the exterior coastline to the west and south, the most dominant feature on the continent was the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]]. This was an irregular inland sea that kept the interior lands fertile, connected the west and east regions of Faerûn and served as a major trade route for many of the bordering nations.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /><ref name="FRCS-p142">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|142}}</ref>

Next in significance was the [[Shaar]], a broad region of grasslands in the south that,<ref name="FRCS-p196">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|196}}</ref> together with the Lake of Steam, separated the area around the inland sea from the coastal nations at the southern edge of the continent. To the east, Faerûn was bordered by a vast region of steppes from [[Kara-Tur]],<ref name="FRCS-p230">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|230}}</ref> and in the north were massive glaciers ([[Pelvuria]] and [[Reghed]]) and tundra.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /> South of the continent, separated by the [[Great Sea]], was the sub-tropical land of [[Zakhara]].<ref name="FRCS-p230"/>

The continent included the following geographic regions.<ref group="note">The following division of Faerûn by compass directions was developed for organizational purposes on the Forgotten Realms Wiki, and is not closely reflected by descriptions in source text. In particular, terms like "the North" and "the South" in the sources can be much more wide-ranging in scope, and as ambiguous, than what is defined here.</ref>

However, depending on their posting, they could also have [[shortspear]]s, [[short sword]]s, and [[dagger]]s. They also possessed two ''[[potion]]s of [[cure light wounds]]'' and an ''[[oil]] of [[Magic weapon (spell)|magic weapon]]''.<ref name="CoS.W-p34"/>

The '''Castle Ward''' was the central ward of the city of [[Waterdeep]]. It encompassed [[Mount Waterdeep]], [[Castle Waterdeep]], and much of the government of the city, which included the [[Palace of Waterdeep]], occupied by [[Piergeiron the Paladinson]] in the mid-14{{th}} century [[DR]], and the residences of many prosperous Waterdhavians.<ref name="CoSW-p96">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|96}}</ref> Among these citizens were celebrated retired adventurers such as [[Mirt the Moneylender]].<ref name="CoSW-p100"/>

==Geography==
The ward was delimited by [[Julthoon Street]] to the north, west of [[Shield Street]], and by [[Trader's Way]] between Shield Street and the [[High Road (Waterdeep)|High Road]], which delimited the ward's eastern border together with [[Snail Street]]. Much of the territory of the Castle Ward was taken up by Mount Waterdeep, which consisted mostly of unused land.<ref name="CoS-CG2e-p9" /><ref name="CoS-AG2e-p18" />

The '''Dock Ward of Waterdeep''' was situated on the ''Great Harbor'' of [[Waterdeep]]. It contained the docks, shipbuilding yards, and warehouses for the sea trade. Compared to the rest of the city it was exceptionally dirty, smelly, and particularly dangerous. In the words of [[Elminster]] himself, the Dock Ward was a "riotous, nigh-perpetual brawl that covers entire acres, interrupted only by small buildings, intermittent trade businesses, an errant dog or two, and a few brave watchguards, who manage to keep the chaos from spreading beyond the docks; the whole lot wallowing in the stench of rotting fish."

==History==
''Circa'' [[1368 DR]], an enterprising [[halfling]] [[Joshuan Havabuck]] was involved in numerous schemes and business in the Dock Ward. His main profitable business was the [[lottery]] where the folk of Waterdeep placed bets on the total daily tonnage that entered the harbor. The numbers were reported by the dockmaster and the winnings were distributed accordingly. One ticket was sold for a single [[Golden lion (coin)|gold lion]] and the take, on average, was ten thousand coins. Amazingly, in 1368 DR, not ten people, picked the correct number, not even thirty. Every single gambler guessed it correctly. This was due the goddess of luck - [[Tymora]]'s power running amok in [[Faerûn]]. Joshuan Havabuck resorted to loaning ten million gold lions with steep interest from [[Mirt the Moneylender]] and pay the winnings out.<ref name="ty">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|101-104}}</ref>

After the [[Spellplague]], the northern section of Waterdeep harbor became dilapidated with most of the harbor choked with sunken ship hulls. This section became known as [[Mistshore]].<ref>{{Cite book/Mistshore|?}}</ref>

=== LINKED WIKI PAGE (first 5000 chars): Marking the years

{{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

'''Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun''' ({{Pronounce|KEL|ben|_|AIR|un|sun|refs=<ref name="DMSotR-24" />}}) was the Lord Mage of [[Waterdeep]], a masked [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]], and powerful archmage.<ref name="ELH-pp299-300" /> He resided in [[Blackstaff Tower]] until his death.<ref name="CoWTM">{{Cite book/The City of Waterdeep Trail Map}}</ref> One of the few spellcasters to become one of [[Mystra's Chosen]],<ref name="B-p7-9">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|7-9|0}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p88">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|88}}</ref> and the last survivor of House [[Maerdrym]],<ref name="B-p62">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|62|6}}</ref> Khelben was a serious, imposing wizard and a grim proponent of law and order.<ref name="FRCS3e-274" />

==Description==
Khelben stood just over 6 feet (1.83 meters) tall and was well-muscled. He usually dressed in robes, wielding his trademark [[Blackstaff (staff)|Blackstaff]]. As of 1374 DR, he had a full beard, black hair with silver streaks, including a prominent one through his beard, and a distinguished, imposing manner.<ref name="B-p25">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|25|2}}</ref> He often dressed in black with a silver torque and a cape lined in gray fur.<ref name="ESh-p52-53">{{Cite book/Elfshadow/Paperback 2000|52-53}}</ref>

==Personality==
Often thought to be humorless and grave, Khelben was actually neither. He simply found it easier to deal with people if they were intimidated by or even frightened of him. He had numerous apprentices over the years and liked to encourage young people who displayed an aptitude for magic. Wielding [[magic|the Art]] could be a gravely dangerous business, however, and Khelben tried to instill an appreciation of that fact in his pupils by keeping up his "stern taskmaster" façade.<ref name=":1">{{Cite book

The '''sewers of Waterdeep''' were a sanitation system of tunnels beneath the city that connected to most locations throughout [[Waterdeep]], including its dungeons.<ref name ="WatN-p26">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|26}}</ref><ref name="TRoU-p9">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Campaign Guide to Undermountain|9}}</ref>

{{Quote|It’s a trip neither pretty nor pleasant, but delve the sewers if you truly want to learn what goes on in Waterdeep.|''[[Down the Drain: A Life in the Guild of Cellarers and Plumbers]]'', [[1356 DR]]<ref name="BST-p25">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|25}}</ref>}}

==Structure==
The depth of these sewers varied across Waterdeep,<ref name="SoW" /> with some areas having around two to three sub-levels.<ref>{{Cite game/Eye of the Beholder}}</ref><ref name="DtU">{{Cite game/Descent to Undermountain}}</ref> One reason for this was [[Mount Waterdeep]] and the plateau the northern portion of the city was built upon. Another was that the varied sizes and areas in which the sewer network was constructed at were both deliberate design decisions, made in order to optimally account for the ocean tides.<ref name="SoW" />

The tunnels of this sewer system did not match up with the routes of the city's streets above them by the 14{{th}} century [[DR]]. This was partially due to the ever changing layout of Waterdeep, but also because the sewer's layout was dictated in part by where Mount Waterdeep's springs rose and flowed down to the sea.<ref name="SoW" />

The [[Waterdeep/Field Ward|Field Ward]],<ref>{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|180}}</ref> the harbor islands, and the heart of Mount Waterdeep were the only areas in the city that the sewers did not run beneath.<ref name="CoS.W-p121">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|121}}</ref>
===Functionality===
Refuse, as well as air, were brought down into the sewers by numerous feeder pipes scattered throughout the city.<ref name="CoS.CG-p60">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|60}}</ref> These pipes measured less than {{SI|1|ft|long=yes}} in diameter.<ref name="CoS.W-p121" /> Thanks to the pipes, air supply was good in the sewers and those who traveled in them had little chance of suffocating.<ref name="dung30-p9">{{Cite dungeon/30/And a Dozen Eggs|9}}</ref>

==Features==
===Architecture===
Generally speaking, Undermountain's natural chambers boast high ceilings of at least {{SI|16|ft}} and smooth floors made of gray [[granite]]. Many halls, rooms, and galleries were created by carving out the natural rock and were generally decorated in a way that made them look uniform and mundane. That said, exceptions existed.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16">{{Cite book/The Ruins of Undermountain/Campaign Guide to Undermountain|16}}</ref>

A corridor in Undermountain was usually {{SI|10|ft}} wide and high. Stairs typically had no railings.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

===Magical Features===
Undermountain had lots of [[magical]] peculiarities that were created and utilized by Halaster. Undermountain's proprietor made it impossible to cast magic that teleported people into or out of Undermountain. Various forms of divination magic would also not function, though some magic objects and spells like wizard eye worked to some degree. This made it challenging if not impossible for would-be treasure hunters to gather information about what the dungeon had in store or what Halaster was doing. Summoning spells also had limitations; their functionality was limited to moving monsters that were already present in Undermountain, or trapping monsters from outside. However, magic items that summoned creatures worked, at least as a general rule.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

Destroying any wards or magical effects that managed these limitations was a task that was as good as impossible. A high understanding of magic was required to understand the many-layered incantations, particular knowledge to determine how to dissolve these effects, the skill to actually dispel these high level magics, combat prowess to protect oneself from monsters that were certainly attracted to people working to dissolve the layered magic, the ability to do this work at a speed that exceeded the speed at which the magic was restored, a lot of time, and the willingness to undertake this task despite the thankless and likely fruitless nature.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

Another effect of the many-layered magic of the Mad Mage was that most of the surfaces and features of the [[dungeon]] radiated some degree of magic, including the floor, ceilings, and walls. This made spells like ''[[detect magic]]'' largely useless because either the radiated magic was so strong that it was impossible to distinguish specific information or because the radiated magic masked the magical properties of other things.<ref name="RU.CGU-p16" />

The complex magics of Undermountain were intertwined with Halaster himself. In the event of the Mad Mage's death or removal from Undermountain, its enchantments and spells would go wild. Countless ''[[gate]]s'' and ''[[portal]]s'' would become destabilized and available for many powerful monsters to utilize. '''Brace Ulmemur''' ({{Pronounce|BRAY|sss|_|ull|MEM|urr|refs=<ref name="WatN-p50" />}}) was a member of the [[Waterdeep]] [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|City Watch]] in the 14{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="CS.W-p36" />

==Personality==
Brace was very loyal to friends but was liable to hunt a betrayer to the end of the world. He was a little paranoid and distrustful of people he didn't know.<ref name="CS.W-p36" />

==History==
Brace was an adventurer devoted to [[Tymora]] but every adventuring party he joined was decimated by monsters. In time, most adventurers said that Brace was "kissed by [[Beshaba]]" and nobody wanted him in their party any more. So Brace joined the City Watch to have a regular income and home.<ref name="CS.W-p36" />
