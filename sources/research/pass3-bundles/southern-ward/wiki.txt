'''Southern Ward''' was the southeastern sector of the city of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="WatN-p11" /><ref name="VGtW-p135" /><ref name="CoSW-p106" /> It was named for the many settlers from [[South Faerûn|southern Faerûn]] as well as its geographic location within the city. As of the [[1491 DR|Year of the Scarlet Witch, 1491 DR]], only outsiders called it '''South Ward''', according to famed author [[Volo]] in his ''[[Volo's Waterdeep Enchiridion|Waterdeep Enchiridion]]''.<ref name="WDH-178" /> Interestingly, this custom changed from just the opposite, as reported by Volo in his ''[[Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|Guide to Waterdeep]]'' written in the early 1360s [[DR]].<ref name="VGtW-p135" />

==Geography==
The northern boundary with [[Trades Ward of Waterdeep|Trades Ward]] lay across [[Telshambra's Street]] and [[Caravan Court]]. On the west and south side was [[Dock Ward of Waterdeep|Dock Ward]] across the [[Way of the Dragon (Waterdeep)|Way of the Dragon]]. To the east, the Ward was bounded by the city walls (circa [[1372 DR]]), specifically the "[[Trollwall]]", which included the southern inner gate leading to the [[Caravan Grounds]] and the [[South Gate (Waterdeep)|South Gate]] beyond.<ref name="VGtW-p135" /><ref name="WatN-cover">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North/Inside cover}}</ref><ref name="CoSW-93">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|93}}</ref><ref name="CS-???">{{Cite book/City System}}</ref>

The stretch of buildings and alleyways in this Ward that stood between the Way of the Dragon and the [[High Road (Waterdeep)|High Road]] was an area that locals referred to as the Tween Run.<ref name="VGtW-p155">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|155}}</ref>

==Inhabitants==
This was considered the "poor" ward and was often known as '''Caravan City''' due to the large numbers of caravan merchants, teamsters and guards that swelled the population of the ward most of the year.<ref name="VGtW-p135" /><ref name="CoSW-p106" />

Dust and mud were common on the streets and most buildings were old, multi-story tenements with shops or businesses on the ground floor.<ref name="VGtW-p135" /> Many visited the ward to experience the wonder of the [[Moon Sphere]]; a miraculous event occurring on the nights of the full [[Selûne|moon]] in [[Dancing Court]],<ref name="VGtW-p142">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|142}}</ref> adjacent to the Jade Dancer; one of the best-known festhalls in the city.<ref name="VGtW-p155" />

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

The '''Trades Ward''' was one of the seven wards of the city of [[Waterdeep]]. It was the main shopping district of Waterdeep and housed many of the city's guildhalls.<ref name="VGtW-p105" /> 

==Geography==
The ward was bounded in the south by the [[Southern Ward of Waterdeep|Southern Ward]], in the west by [[Castle Ward]], in the north by [[North Ward of Waterdeep|North Ward]] and the [[City of the Dead (Waterdeep)|City of the Dead]] and in the east by the [[Trollwall]].<ref name="VGtW-p105" />

==History==
In the late 14{{th}} century [[DR]], over the course of a [[tenday]] and more, streets across the Trades Ward experienced a number of [[ghost]]ly sightings that left many citizens, scribes, and members of the [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|City Watch]] confused. Some speculated if a [[wizard]] experimenting with [[illusion]]s may have been the cause. Others speculated it was a wizard seeking to harm local businesses, drive down local property values, or simply spread fear&mdash;though all who put forward these theories admitted to having nothing to backup their claims.<ref name="WN.GS" />

Some interpreted the apparitions as being a message &mdash; [[Sambral the Soothsayer]] would claim the dead were warning Waterdhavians of impending battles from underground invaders, [[Sheireera|Sheireera of the Prophecies]] would claim that it was the dead crying out against immorality and lawlessness in Waterdeep, and [[priest]] [[Thalomaun]] of the [[Church of Lathander]] would make a proclamation on [[Virgin's Square]] that it was a sign from the dead for the living of the affected neighborhoods to do away with old businesses and make way for new ones.<ref name="WN.GS">{{Cite web/Waterdeep News/Ghost Sightings}}</ref>

The '''Dock Ward of Waterdeep''' was situated on the ''Great Harbor'' of [[Waterdeep]]. It contained the docks, shipbuilding yards, and warehouses for the sea trade. Compared to the rest of the city it was exceptionally dirty, smelly, and particularly dangerous. In the words of [[Elminster]] himself, the Dock Ward was a "riotous, nigh-perpetual brawl that covers entire acres, interrupted only by small buildings, intermittent trade businesses, an errant dog or two, and a few brave watchguards, who manage to keep the chaos from spreading beyond the docks; the whole lot wallowing in the stench of rotting fish."

==History==
''Circa'' [[1368 DR]], an enterprising [[halfling]] [[Joshuan Havabuck]] was involved in numerous schemes and business in the Dock Ward. His main profitable business was the [[lottery]] where the folk of Waterdeep placed bets on the total daily tonnage that entered the harbor. The numbers were reported by the dockmaster and the winnings were distributed accordingly. One ticket was sold for a single [[Golden lion (coin)|gold lion]] and the take, on average, was ten thousand coins. Amazingly, in 1368 DR, not ten people, picked the correct number, not even thirty. Every single gambler guessed it correctly. This was due the goddess of luck - [[Tymora]]'s power running amok in [[Faerûn]]. Joshuan Havabuck resorted to loaning ten million gold lions with steep interest from [[Mirt the Moneylender]] and pay the winnings out.<ref name="ty">{{Cite book/Tymora's Luck|101-104}}</ref>

After the [[Spellplague]], the northern section of Waterdeep harbor became dilapidated with most of the harbor choked with sunken ship hulls. This section became known as [[Mistshore]].<ref>{{Cite book/Mistshore|?}}</ref>

The '''Trollwall''' was a defensive barrier that encircled nearly the entirety of the city of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="CoS.CG-p8" /><ref name="CoS.W-p18-19" /><ref name="WatN-p11">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|11}}</ref>

==Location==
The Trollwall fully surrounded Waterdeep's eastern perimeter, from its southern tip at [[Deepwater Harbor]] to the northern end of the [[City of the Dead]],<ref name="CoS.CG-p8" /><ref name="WatN-p11" /> along the way acting as the eastern boundary of the [[Waterdeep/Southern Ward|Southern Ward]].<ref>{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|135}}</ref> It stopped at the northeastern corner of the City of the Dead, at a small stretch of land known as the [[Cliffwatch]],<ref name="CoS.CG-p8" /><ref name="CoS.W-p18-19" /> whose steep {{SI|100|ft}} cliff of the [[Cliffwatch (cliff)|same name]] acted as a natural barrier.<ref name="CoS.CG-p8" /><ref name="WatN-p11" /><ref name="CoS.CG-p43" /><ref name="CoS.W-p94">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|94}}</ref> The Trollwall then continued around Waterdeep's northern perimeter,<ref name="CoS.CG-p8" /><ref name="WatN-p11" /> beginning at the point in which the Cliffwatch's height was negligible enough for attackers to scale it,<ref name="WatN-p11" /> and formed the southern boundary of the [[Waterdeep/Field Ward|Field Ward]]<ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Player's Guide|128}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|196}}</ref> before doubling back south to surrounded the western seacoast.<ref name="CoS.CG-p8" /><ref name="WatN-p11" />

==Structure==

The '''South Gate''' was the southern point of entrance through the [[Trollwall]] into [[Waterdeep]], City of Splendors.<ref name="CoSW-p107">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|107}}</ref>

==Description==
The gate featured a single tower on either side of the [[High Road (Waterdeep)|High Road]]. Each tower stood four stories tall.<ref name="CoSW-p107"/>

==Law & Order==
Any large caravan that wished to enter through South Gate had to register with one of the [[Magisters of Waterdeep]], who was always accompanied by members of the [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]]. The '''High Road''', largely known to citizens as just '''the High''',<ref name="drag410-p29">{{Cite dragon/410/Eye on the Realms: The Talking Door|29}}</ref> was one of the main streets of [[Waterdeep]], connecting its southern and northern regions. The High Road started at the [[South Gate (Waterdeep)|South Gate]] of Waterdeep, where it continued south as the [[Trade Way]]. In southern Waterdeep, the intersection between the High Road and the [[Way of the Dragon (Waterdeep)|Way of the Dragon]] was known as the [[Waymoot (Waterdeep)|Waymoot]]. The '''Moon Sphere''' was a magical radiance that appeared every full [[moon]] in the [[Dancing Court]] in the [[Waterdeep/Southern Ward|Southern Ward]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="WDH-p178">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|178}}</ref>

==Description==
The Moon Sphere appeared as a glowing field of blue light that hovered above the Dancing Court. The sphere was about {{SI|40|feet|long=yes}} in diameter, and allowed anyone within the field to fly about.<ref name="VGW-p137">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|137}}</ref> At its highest, the sphere extended {{SI|30|feet|long=yes}} above the court.<ref name="VGW-p143">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|143}}</ref>

The Moon Sphere would come into being as the full moon shone upon the Dancing Court, and would slowly fade as the moonlight waned.<ref name="VGW-p142"/>

==Location==
The Moon Sphere was located within the Dancing Court, which had been left empty to accommodate the sphere. In fact, the nearby [[Jade Dancer]] had been built in order to give their guests the best view of the field.<ref name="VGW-p142"/>

==Activities==
By the [[laws of Waterdeep]], none were allowed to charge for admittance into the Moon Sphere, nor restrict anyone from entering. Spellcasting was prohibited out of fear of disturbing the Moon Sphere's magic; certain spells cast within the sphere would result in bursts of [[wild magic]].<ref name="VGW-p142"/><ref name="VGW-p143"/> This law was enforced by a [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]] patrol, as well as a supervising member of the [[Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors]].<ref name="VGW-p142"/> They also kept a sharp eye for any visitor attempting to experiment with the sphere in order to learn its magic.<ref name="VGW-p143"/>

Courting couples often came to the Moon Sphere to dance and kiss one another, though there were tales of more passionate encounters. Guests of the Jade Dancer often continued their revelry by flying with drinks in hand—particularly daring guests would jump from the Jade Dancer's balcony into the Moon Sphere. Doing this without spilling their drink was considered a mark of exceeding sophistication.<ref name="VGW-p143"/>

Whenever the Moon Sphere appeared, the [[House of the Moon]] sent at least one priestess to dance and pray within the field's glow.<ref name="VGW-p145">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|145}}</ref>

The Moon Sphere was also used as a common temple by sailors throughout Waterdeep. Whenever the sphere appeared, able sailors journeyed across the city to see it. Traditionally, sailors extended a hand into the field and asked Selûne to guide them safely to their next destination.<ref name="VGW-p142"/>

A style of flying dance had been developed by Waterdhavians in order to perform within the sphere.<ref name="WDH-p178"/>

===Magical Effects===
Besides granting the ability to [[fly]], the sphere had many powerful magical effects. If the correct words were recited, the sphere could recharge some [[magical item]]s, including ''[[Wand of magic missiles|wands of magic missiles]]''.<ref name="VGW-p145"/>

Falling within the Moon Sphere was similar to the effects of a ''[[feather fall]]'' spell, though falling entirely outside of the sphere was governed by the normal laws of gravity. One person could not drag another person down by descending; the sphere's magic affected each person individually.<ref name="VGW-p143"/>

Devout worshipers of [[Selûne]] could also be granted healing of wounds, [[poison]], mental illness, [[disease]], and [[curse]]s after certain rituals were performed with the sphere.<ref name="VGW-p143"/>

[[Abjuration]], [[enchantment]], [[charm]], and [[divination]] spells cast by [[chaotic good]] [[wizard]]s or [[priest]]s of Selûne all worked to their fullest potential within the sphere, along with any spell connected to moonlight or starlight. Fire-based spells, in contrast, were reduced to their lowest potential within the sphere.<ref name="VGW-p143"/>

The ''' Full Cup''' was a tavern located in the city of [[Waterdeep]] during the mid-to-late 14{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="CoS.AGttC-p7">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|7}}</ref><ref name="VGtW-p161-163">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|161-163}}</ref>

==Location==
The tavern stood at the southeast intersection of [[Coach Street]] and [[Carter's Way]] in Waterdeep's [[Waterdeep/Southern Ward|Southern Ward]].<ref name="CS.M9">{{Cite book/City System/Map04}}</ref><ref name="WDH.M">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist/Map}}</ref><ref name="VGtW.M">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep/Map}}</ref><ref name="IA.W">{{Cite interactive atlas|Waterdeep}}</ref>

==Description==
The Full Cup was a one story building.<ref name="CoS.CG-p53">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|53}}</ref> The interior was small and poorly lit. A long bar dominated the main room. An impressive array of bottles adorned the wall behind the bar. The bottles were covered by an equally impressive layer of dust.<ref name="VGtW-p161-163"/>

==Activities==
The Full Cup's patrons were mostly caravan drovers. Brawls were a common occurance in this seedy tavern.<ref name="CoS.AGttC-p7"/><ref name="VGtW-p161-163"/>

==Services==
Aside from the frequent brawls and frigid winter drafts, the Full Cup was known for serving delicious buttered [[mushroom]]s for 1 [[cp]] per bowl. The mushrooms were actually grown in-house in the dung cellars beneath the tavern. The dung pile was quite deep and the Full Cup's proprietor would hide items beneath it for customers who knew how to ask for a fee of 5 [[gp]] for a month, after which time the items were forfeited if not claimed.<ref name="VGtW-p161-163"/>

==Inhabitants==
The Full Cup was owned by a man named [[Gulth Djanczo]].<ref name="VGtW-p161-163"/>

The ''' Red Gauntlet''' was a tavern located in the city of [[Waterdeep]] during the mid-to-late 14{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="VGtW-p160-161">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|160-161}}</ref>

==Location==
The tavern stood on the north side of the southern stretch of the [[Way of the Dragon (Waterdeep)|Way of the Dragon]] in the [[Tween Run]] of Waterdeep's [[Waterdeep/Southern Ward|Southern Ward]].<ref name="CS.M9">{{Cite book/City System/Map04}}</ref><ref name="WDH.M">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist/Map}}</ref><ref name="VGtW.M">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep/Map}}</ref><ref name="IA.W">{{Cite interactive atlas|Waterdeep}}</ref>

==Description==
The tavern stood two stories tall.<ref name="CoS.CG-p53">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|53}}</ref> The interior was poorly lit and shabby.<ref name="VGtW-p160-161"/>

==Services==
Food served here consisted mostly of dried fish, waybread, and rabbit-and-fowl stew. Drink consisted of a house [[wine]]. The fares were cheap in the Red Gauntlet with a plate of food running just 3 [[cp]] and a tankard 2 cp.<ref name="VGtW-p160-161"/>

==Activities==
The Red Gauntlet allowed patrons to drink themselves to sleep and stay, provided they did not snore too loudly. Anyone becoming rowdy or threatening was slipped a sleep syrup in their drink to placate them.<ref name="VGtW-p160-161"/>

==Inhabitants==
The Red Gauntlet was owned by a retired [[thief]] named [[Daunt Buirune]].<ref name="VGtW-p160-161"/>

The '''Spouting Fish''' was a popular tavern in the [[Waterdeep/Southern Ward|Southern Ward]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="VGW2e-p159-160" /><ref name="CS-AG2e-p7">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|7}}</ref><ref group="note">On p. 27 of ''[[Waterdeep: Dragon Heist]]'', it is claimed that the tavern is located in the Dock Ward, but this is incompatible with all previous sources, as well as all maps of the city across all previous editions. For that reason, we assume that the location given in ''Dragon Heist'' is in error.</ref>

==Location==
The tavern was located on the east side of the [[Way of the Dragon (Waterdeep)|Way of the Dragon]], at the corner where it turned north after branching west from the [[High Road (Waterdeep)|High Road]] at the [[Waymoot (Waterdeep)|Waymoot]].<ref name="CSW3e-p105-106">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|105-106}}</ref> Its popularity was largely due to its strategic location, which prompted many travelers entering the city from [[South Gate]] to stop there for a drink.<ref name="VGW2e-p159-160" />

==Structure==
The tavern was characterized by a large water fountain shaped like an upright spouting fish. The large building had several floors built with a labyrinthine structure.<ref name="VGW2e-p159-160" />

==Interior==
The interior of the Spouting Fish was brightly lit. All of the tavern's booths and benches, as well as the building's structural beams and posts were unpainted and, for that reason, very flammable. As a result of the fire hazard, two members of the [[Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors]] were hired to remain constantly on guard within the tavern.<ref name="VGW2e-p159-160" />

The tavern's cellar contained an entrance to a surface shaft that led to a small junction in the Waterdhavian sewer system.<ref name="CSW3e-p123-124">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|123-124}}</ref><ref name="CS-CG2e-p7">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|62}}</ref>

===Atmosphere===

| text   = A bit like drinking in the thick of a street brawl.

| float  =

The Spouting Fish was notoriously noisy<ref name="VGW2e-p159-160" /> and boisterous.<ref name="CS-CG2e-p7" />

==Services==
The tavern offered numerous drinks, including [[zzar]], [[wine]]s, and [[beer]]s. The food, which included roast fowl, sausages, and [[bread]], was heavily [[salt]]ed to induce patrons to consume more beverages.<ref name="VGW2e-p159-160" />

Another reason for the tavern's popularity was intense advertising performed by street criers across the city.<ref name="CS-CG2e-p7" />

==History==
By the {{YearlinkName|1492}},{{Waterdeep-time}} the sewer intersection that connected to the Spouting Fish's cellar was an access point to one of the [[Xanathar Guild]] hideouts. It was guarded by a [[gazer]].<ref name="WDH5e-p27">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|27}}</ref>

==Inhabitants==
In the mid‒14{{th}} century [[DR]], the Spouting Fish was owned by the fearless [[human]] [[Janess Imristar]].<ref name="VGW2e-p159-160" />
