She has been mapping the Brandath Crypts for three months and has located the vault entrance, but cannot open it — she has no Stone, no Eyes, no dragon to negotiate with. Her plan is to be present when someone else opens it and take the most portable, highest-value items before the gold conversation bogs down. She is not interested in the gold itself: 500,000 gp weighs ten thousand pounds and cannot be moved by a lone cat burglar. She is interested in whatever magical artifacts, gems, and documentation are inside. When the party arrives at the crypts in **Vault of Dragons**, Esvele is already there doing reconnaissance. campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:335: # Design Notes: Vault of Dragons
campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:339: **Xanathar's Lair** through **Kolat Towers** each tested a different genre and a different set of party capabilities — dungeon infiltration, social infiltration, caper improvisation, raid violence. **Vault of Dragons** is not a fifth genre in that sequence. It is the point where all four genres' consequences arrive at once. The party does not case the vault, because there is nothing left to case — they already know where it is and what guards it. What replaces the heist framework's dramatic engine (plan, execute, adapt) is accumulated consequence: every faction relationship, every renown level, every choice about who to trust and who to betray, all resolving simultaneously. A GM running **Vault of Dragons** should not look for a new structural trick. The trick was already played, nine times, across the quests that came before it.
campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:343: This is the single most important practical note in the document. **Xanathar's Lair** and **Cassalanter Villa** both defer to "the 3-Eye disclosure scene from **Vault of Dragons**" without knowing which lair will actually deliver the third Eye — the party's own choices determine that. **Sea Maidens Faire**, published earlier in the drafting sequence, wrote the disclosure inline rather than deferring, on the theory that a scene this significant deserved to exist wherever it naturally lands rather than as a forward reference to a document that didn't exist yet at the time. The result is a minor structural seam: **Sea Maidens Faire**'s inline text and this document's canonical text needed to agree closely enough that a GM reading both back to back would not notice a difference. They do. If **Vault of Dragons** is being run and the third Eye came from **Xanathar's Lair** or **Cassalanter Villa**, use Scene 1's Full Awakening text as written — it is the canonical version, and it was written to match **Sea Maidens Faire**'s language precisely so that whichever quest actually delivers the disclosure, the moment reads identically. Do not restage the disclosure a second time in **Vault of Dragons**'s own Scene 1 if it already happened in Scene 6 of whichever prior quest delivered the third Eye — reference that it happened, briefly, and move on.
campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:347: Every prior lair heist ends its Scene 6 by setting one or two flags for later use. **Vault of Dragons** is the quest where all of them get read at once. The Faction-State Summary Table in Scene 1 exists so a GM does not have to flip back through four other documents to reconstruct what's true — it is meant to be filled in once, before the session, and referenced throughout. The table's most important function is making Scene 5's roster assembly mechanical rather than improvised: a GM who knows Manshoon is Alive, Xanathar is Wounded, and the Cassalanters are Active-Adversarial can build the vault confrontation directly from the Roster-Assembly Table without guessing at what "feels right."
campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:351: The four resolution paths in Scene 4 are deliberately structured as a puzzle with real, discoverable clues rather than a GM's arbitrary choice between "talk" and "fight." Hlam's advantage on Persuasion, the Masked Lord's Writ as a literal instrument of authority, Renaer's standing as Neverember's actual son, and Dagult's public ousting as demonstrable legal fact are all clues the campaign has planted across multiple quests. A party that has been paying attention arrives at the vault already holding the tools for a peaceful resolution, without the GM needing to hint at it in the moment. A party that has not is not punished for it — combat remains available and mechanically fair, per the boss-design framework — but it is framed, correctly, as the outcome Aurinax himself did not want.
campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:353: Note on source material: the Alexandrian's own "Addendum: The Dragon of Dragon Heist" recommends replacing Aurinax with a straightforward red dragon, on the grounds that his backstory is convoluted continuity to introduce at the very end of a campaign. That concern does not apply here — this remix has been seeding Aurinax's existence, the dragonstaff's significance, and Dagult Neverember's role in the Grand Game since **Finding Floon**.
