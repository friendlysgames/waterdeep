}}'''The Walking Statues''' were mighty [[construct]]s built for the defense of holy temples, ancient vaults and the most important cities, castles and fortresses of [[Waterdeep]]. They often served as ethereal guardians that could attack other ethereal creatures approaching its guarded area. They were unswervingly loyal to their instructions or orders, carrying them out to the best of their abilities.<ref name="CSW3e-p139-140" />

==Description==
Unlike most constructs, walking statues were free-willed, thinking creatures who could reason, learn and remember. They were able to stand still for centuries at a time, remaining endlessly vigilant, but all the while they would be deep in thought. Walking statues were capable of understanding [[Common]] and [[Draconic language|Draconic]], but would speak only if they were commanded to do so by their creator or by any whom they recognized as an authority.<ref name="CSW3e-p139-140" />

==History==
[[File:Blackstaff of Waterdeep AFR.jpg|thumb|left|[[Vajra Safahr]] holding the [[Blackstaff (staff)|Blackstaff]] near one of the Walking Statues.]]
Originally there was only one visible statue in the city, later known as [[The Sahuagin Humbled]], while seven other statues remained hidden on the ethereal plane around the city. During [[Spellplague|The Spellplague]] ([[1385 DR]]) six more of the statues exited the ethereal plane and begun rampaging the city. [[Tsarra Chaadren]], the [[Blackstaff]] at the time, was unable to control them or banish them back to [[Ethereal plane|The Ethereal plane]]. Once their rampage ended, the city rebuilt around, on top of, and sometimes inside the statues. Much later in [[1479 DR]] the eighth statue, [[The Griffon]], appeared. It defended [[Ahghairon's Tower]] (again, free from the control of the ''Blackstaff'') before settling down near [[The Peaktop Aerie]] on [[Mount Waterdeep]]. As the statues are unable to be removed from the city, they've come to be used as landmarks to navigate.<ref name="W-DH-p182">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|(182)}}</ref>

'''Ahghairon''' ({{Pronounce|ah|GAIR|awn|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}), born '''Ahghairon Belnoth Undrur''',<ref name="GG-Ahghairon's name" /> was a [[mage]] from [[the North]]<ref name="CoS.CG-p26">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|26}}</ref> and the first [[Lords of Waterdeep#Open Lord|Open Lord]] of [[Waterdeep]]. He created the [[Lords of Waterdeep]], the system by which Waterdeep had been ruled ever since, except for a six-year period immediately following his death.<ref name="CoS.CG-p27">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|27}}</ref> 

{{Quote|There shall come a time when our city and its deepwater bay shall grow in fame and fortune across many realms and many worlds. Folk shall know of Waterdeep, our City of Splendors, and sing its praises. I have seen it thus, and I endeavor to make it true.|Ahghairon, ''circa'' [[1032 DR]]<ref name="CoS.CG-p25">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|25}}</ref>''}}

==Personality==
Ahghairon was known by several names during his youth, but went by only his first name during his time in Waterdeep.<ref name="GG-Ahghairon's name" />

==History==
{{quote|The original splendors of Waterdeep were Ahghairon's secrets, which keep us safe today and always, despite the predations of lesser so-called lords.|[[Agnan Crohal]], ''[[Tales Told Tavernside]]'', [[1268 DR]]<ref name="BS">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower||23}}</ref>}}<br/>
Ahghairon was born on [[Midsummer]]'s Night, in the {{YearlinkName|920}}.<ref name="CoS.CG-p29" /> He was named after his great-grandfather.<ref name="GG-Ahghairon's name" /> Legends say that the symbol of [[Mystra]] was glowing in the sky at the time.<ref name="CoS.CG-p29">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|29}}</ref>

===The Trollwars===
The [[Trollwars#Second Trollwar|Second Trollwar]] broke out in [[940 DR]],<ref name="CoS.CG-p29" /> as [[troll]]s were pushed down into the area later known as the [[Evermoors|Trollmoors]] by [[orc]] uprisings further north. After a decade of battles, a young Ahghairon distinguished himself, using his magic to turn the war against the trolls.<ref name="CoS.CG-p26" /> First, the Waterdhavian defenders drove the trolls back from their walls. The human realms then united under Ahghairon, War Captain [[Aeroth of Silverymoon]], and [[Samular Caradoon]] of [[Tyr]], and together they eradicated trolls across the Evermoors, with war finally coming to an end in [[952 DR]].<ref name=GHR>{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|113}}</ref>

At some point during the 940's DR, Aghairon was in [[Waterdeep]], at the time called Nimoar's Hold. Fires set to hold of the trolls threatened to destroy a large part of the town, so Aghairon asked adventurers to find and bring him [[Halaster Blackcloak|Halaster]]'s wards to bolster a ritual that would allow him to stop the fires. (More info about this adventure can be found in [[Hero of the Troll Wars]])

A third Trollwar almost took place in [[963 DR]], when the traitors [[Aviss]] and [[Fellandar]] led an army of trolls out of the [[Mere of Dead Men]] against the city. They knew well the city's defenses, and [[Castle Waterdeep]] had only just begun construction. But Ahghairon and a band of [[wizard]]s hurled Aviss and Fellandar into an extra-dimensional prison, thwarting the attack.<ref name="ADDc-33-p16">{{Cite comic/Summer in the City|16}}</ref> <ref group="note">The comic "[[Summer in the City]]" actually refers to "great Aglairon", but by the context it seems clear this is meant to be Ahghairon.</ref>

===Rise to Power===
At this time, Waterdeep was ruled by the Warlord [[Rhaulor]], who wanted to use the city's power and wealth to create an empire spanning [[northwest Faerûn]]. Ahghairon strongly disagreed with this, and when he voiced his concerns, Rhaulor attacked him. Ahghairon  used his magic to turn Rhaulor's sword into a snake, which bit and poisoned him. Rhaulor died and Ahghairon assumed control of Waterdeep, proclaiming himself the first Open Lord, and establishing a form of government involving Masked Lords, whose identities would remain private.<ref name="CoS.CG-p27" />

===Rule===
In [[1007 DR]], Ahghairon constructed his [[Ahghairon's Tower|wizard tower]], but positioned it outside the walls of Waterdeep (which since expanded). At the same time, he imposed magical barriers on [[Halaster's Hold]], which prevented the possibility of any dangers escaping into the city. He created a magical axe named ''[[Azuredge]]'' in [[1026 DR]].<ref name="CoS.CG-p30">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|30}}</ref>

During his rule, Ahghairon also banned [[slavery]]. Though this was later reinstated by the [[History of Waterdeep#Reign of Guildmasters|Lords Magister]], it was soon outlawed again by Open Lord [[Baeron Silmaeril]] and had remained illegal ever since.<ref name="CoS-WWiW-p6">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Who's Who in Waterdeep|6-7}}</ref>

===Death===
Ahghairon used ''[[potion of longevity|potions of longevity]]'' to remain in prime physical form even though he was growing old by human standards.<ref name="CoS.CG-p26" /> Eventually, his health failed in [[1256 DR]], and he died and was buried in his tower in Waterdeep. The tower was sealed off and protected by magic, and it was believed that his resting place remained undisturbed ever since.<ref name="CoS.CG-p27" /><ref name="CoS.CG-p30" />

'''Vajra Safahr''' ({{Pronounce|VAJ|rah|_|sah|FAR|refs=<ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was the seventh [[Blackstaff]] of [[Waterdeep]], a title she ascended to in [[1479 DR]] when her mentor and lover, [[Samark Dhanzscul]], was killed.<ref name="Blackstaff Tower">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower}}</ref> She was quite young when she became the Blackstaff,<ref name="BST-p4">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|4}}</ref><ref name="BST-p1">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|1}}</ref> and was the youngest Blackstaff in Waterdeep's history.<ref name="WoW">{{Cite game/Warriors of Waterdeep}}</ref>

==Description==
Vajra was diminutive, standing under {{SI|5|ft|c|long=yes}},<ref name="Schend-tweet">{{Cite web|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230628202441/https://twitter.com/StevenESchend/status/1674134481840529408?cxt=HHwWgICzhZ-c3LsuAAAA|title=Howdy and welcome to a quick "Steven clarifies something he wrote long ago"|accessdate=28-06-2023| date=28-06-2023}}</ref> with olive skin, short black hair, and brown eyes.<ref name="BST-p4" /><ref name="BST-p1" /> However, for a time, she had the ability to change her eye color, and the color switched depending on which of the six previous Blackstaffs controlled her body at any given time.<ref name="Schend-tweet" />

==Personality==
Vajra loved an intriguing [[arcane]] challenge. She was patient and known to pursue multiple plans at once, often requiring her to get her rivals to act in her best interests instead of their own.<ref name="WoW" /> Although she was prone to self-doubt when she first assumed the role of the Blackstaff,<ref name="DM-ch1">{{Cite book/Death Masks||1}}</ref> she soon learned to ignore those doubts and to dismiss the envies of those who were skeptical of her role.<ref name="WoW" />

==Activities==
She was focused on turning Waterdeep, and specifically her [[Blackstaff Academy]], into the center of [[magical]] study in [[Faerûn]]. She would wander the libraries and laboratories of [[Blackstaff Tower]] under magical silence so as not to disturb the other wizards, save for when she made a sudden appearance to aid them in their studies. She allowed her best students access to her own personal [[spellbook]], and was said to even allow some of them to add their own creations to it.<ref name="WoW" />

==Abilities==
Though having become the Blackstaff at a young age, Vajra was a skilled wizard, even capable of casting a lone spell while entirely [[paralyze|paralyzed]], and thus incapable of performing verbal incantations or hand-gestures. The effect of this spell was to fire forth a few rays of amber-colored energy from her eyes.<ref name="Blackstaff Tower">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower}}</ref>

The '''Blackstaff''' was the name for a series of powerful magical staves, created and used by [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] and/or his successor.<ref name="FRCS-p275">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|275}}</ref><ref name="BST-p260">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|260}}</ref>

==Description==
===Khelben's Blackstaff===
It was believed that Khelben Arunsun, who carried the first [[Blackstaff]] title, selected a new Blackstaff every time he left [[Blackstaff Tower]].<ref name="RGLoD-98">{{Cite book/A Reader's Guide to R. A. Salvatore's the Legend of Drizzt|98}}</ref> However, it always appeared as a long wooden staff. It was longer than an average human was tall, roughly seven feet from base to tip.  A ring of bulbs circled the staff at about eye level, and the staff was topped by a curved claw shape. The entire staff was pure black in color.<ref name="FRCS-p275" /> 

===Samark's Staff===
[[Samark Dhanzscul]], the sixth Blackstaff, carried a staff known as the Duskstaff, which was a twisted metal staff with a crystal on top that flashed various colors.<ref name="BST-p1">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|1}}</ref><ref name="BST-p139">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|139}}</ref> It was unknown whether this was an iteration of a Blackstaff or a different staff. The Duskstaff could only be moved by the current Blackstaff or by someone wielding [[Ncral's Ring]].<ref name="BST-p139">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|139}}</ref>

===Vajra's Blackstaff===
[[Vajra Safahr]], the seventh Blackstaff, carried a silver-shod Blackstaff.<ref name="BST-p252">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|252}}</ref> The Blackstaff she carried when proclaiming her status as the new Blackstaff was the "true blackstaff", a large rune-inscribed [[duskwood]] staff with silver veins. The whole staff was blackened by use. Atop the staff was a large axe head in the shape of a snarling wolf's head, which had glowing green eyes.<ref name="BST-p250">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|250}}</ref>

==Known powers==
The Blackstaff had all the powers of a [[staff of power]].<ref name="FRCS-p275" /> 

The staff also had a [[permanent]] [[blackstaff (spell)]] upon it<ref name="FRCS-p275" /> that could&hellip;
* &hellip;grant an [[enhancement]] when used in melee combat,<ref name="FRCS-p275" /> 
* &hellip;[[dispel magic]] with a touch,<ref name="FRCS-p275" />  
* &hellip;cause a spellcaster to lose a spell or expend a bit of their magical power,<ref name="FRCS-p275" /> and
* &hellip;channel various [[mind-affecting]] spells.<ref name="FRCS-p275" /> 

A Blackstaff's Heir needed to touch the Blackstaff in order to be centered in his or her new powers.<ref name="BST-p252">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|252}}</ref> Only a Blackstaff or the Blackstaff's Heir could safely carry and wield a Blackstaff. As long as a Blackstaff's Heir was touching the Blackstaff, the powers of the actual Blackstaff (person) could be channeled through the staff.<ref name="BST-p260">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|260}}</ref>

The [[Walking Statues of Waterdeep|Walking Statue]], known as the '''Sahuagin Humbled,''' was originally the only Walking Statue visible in [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="CoS.W-p21">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|21}}</ref><ref name="VGtW-9">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|9}}</ref>

==Description==
This Walking Statue was a {{SIAdj|90|ft|adj=tall}} stone colossus. It was one of a set of eight such [[construct]]s in Waterdeep.<ref name="CoS.W-p21" />

Before being reshaped into the image of a [[sahuagin]], the Walking Statue took the form of a regal human man, facing the [[Sea of Swords]].<ref name="VGtW-9"/>

==Location==
The statue was positioned on [[Gull Leap]] at the western end of [[Julthoon Street]] in Waterdeep by [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] (the creator of the statue and most of its brethren)<ref name="WatN-23">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|23}}</ref> to help defend the city.<ref name="CoS.W-p21" /><ref name="VGtW-9" />

==History==
In the 1360s, it was considered a minor crime under the Second Plaint to deface or vandalize the statue.<ref name="VGtW-9"/>

Around the same time, members of the [[Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors]] would cast ''[[light]]'' spells upon the statue during particularly rough storms, in order to guide passing ships.<ref name="VGtW-9"/>

After defending the city in the [[Deepwater War]] of {{Yearlink|1369}}, Khelben reshaped the statue into the image of a sahuagin, moved it to the base of [[Mount Waterdeep]], and had it pose down on one knee showing reverence toward the [[House of Heroes]] on the far side of the [[Field of Triumph]] to the north.<ref name="W-DH-p183" /><ref name="WDH-map" /><ref group="note">In ''[[Waterdeep: Dragon Heist]],'' a sahuagin invasion is described as occurring in {{yearlink|1370}}. However, it seems clear from context that they are referring to the Deepwater War, which occurred in {{yearlink|1369}}.</ref>

The '''Spellplague''', called the '''Blue Breath of Change''' by the inhabitants of [[Abeir]],<ref name="FRCG-p200">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|200}}</ref> was a disaster that struck [[Realmspace]] and even the [[planes]] themselves on the date of [[Tarsakh 29]] in the {{YearlinkName|1385}}, and was caused by [[Mystra (Midnight)|Mystra]]'s assassination at the hands of [[Cyric]] and [[Shar]].<ref name="FRCG-p50">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|50}}</ref><ref name="Ud-p64">{{Cite book/Undead|64}}</ref> It continued for a decade, leading to the [[Wailing Years]], during which [[arcane magic]] ceased to function and the planet of [[Toril]] was transformed.<ref name="TWY">{{Cite dragon/362/Countdown to the Realms: Spellplague: The Wailing Years}}</ref>

{{Quote|In the infamous, calamitous year of [[1385 DR]], a conspiracy between the goddess of darkness, Shar, and the god of trickery, Cyric, sought to end Mystra's control over the Weave and influence over the realms by cravenly assassinating her. But instead of merely breaking the goddess of magic's dominance, her death threw the Weave into utter chaos and collapse. Magic spells faltered, or failed entirely. Countless spellcasters were killed or driven insane. A vast storm of blue flame arose from the [[Mhair Jungles]] and swept the continent, churning and reshaping the land, and leaving whole regions devastated.|''[[Magical Histories]], Volume 2: The Spellplague''<ref name="BGIII">{{Cite game/Baldur's Gate III}}</ref>}}

Cyric, god of murder, was the assassin;<ref name="Ud-p75">{{Cite book/Undead|75}}</ref> [[Savras]], the god of divination, was the soothsayer,{{speculation}}, and [[Dweomerheart|the city]], cavern and [[World Tree cosmology|tree]] symbolized the ordered structures of magic crumbling into chaos.<ref name="Ud-p75"/>

==Origins==
The most popular and widely accepted theory about the origins of the Spellplague was that this calamity was caused by the death of Mystra, the [[god]]dess of [[Magic]]. For eons, magic had been bound within Mystra's [[Weave]], the universal structure of arcane forces, and her death tore the Weave apart and unleashed the [[raw magic]] contained within it, creating the cataclysm that was later known as the Spellplague.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/><ref name="NV">{{Cite game/Neverwinter}}</ref>

Other scholars, however, didn't consider this to be a logical explanation. Historically, two earlier incarnations of Mystra had died before only to be reborn, and the Weave maintained its hold over the power of magic for the duration of her deaths. In these scholars' views, the truth would never be fully known to mortals, and perhaps not even to the [[god]]s.<ref name="NV"/><ref name="PoS-p79">{{Cite book/Plague of Spells|79}}</ref>

Around [[1396 DR]], the sentient [[construct]] [[Cynosure (construct)|Cynosure]] theorized that the escape of an entity named the [[Carnis|Traitor]] from its confinement in the dungeons of [[Stardeep]], around the same time Mystra was murdered, may have also influenced the Spellplague's virulent onslaught.<ref name="PoS-p79"/>

Around [[1479 DR]], scholars and sages who studied the [[Neverwinter/Chasm|Chasm]] of [[Neverwinter]], such as [[Rhazzad]], believed the [[aboleth]]s of the [[Abolethic Sovereignty]] may have been the actual creators of the Spellplague, although there were no definite proof.<ref name="NV"/>

'''Tsarra Chaadren''' (known by the alias '''Autumnfire''') was a [[half-elf]] [[Sorcerer|sorceress]]<ref name="charprof"/> and the second [[Blackstaff]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="BlackstaffTower226">[[Maliantor]] of Waterdeep, ''My Eyes Open Always: Memories of the Blackstaff'', Year of the Enchanted Puppet ([[1416 DR]]). {{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|226}}</ref>

==Appearance==
Tsarra had long, curly auburn hair<ref name="charprof"/><ref name="B-p22">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|22}}</ref> Her eyes were hazel.<ref name="BST-p178">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|178}}</ref> As of 28 Uktar [[1374 DR]], [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] had attached a [[kiira (disambiguation)|''kiira'']] to Tsarra's forehead, which consisted of an intricate tattoo with a green gem at its center.<ref name="B-p46">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|46}}</ref>

==Personality==
Tsarra was a patient teacher, although she did have a temper, which flared up when hearing lies, or when people treated children or animals poorly. She was stubborn when she believed herself to be right.<ref name="charprof"/>

She preferred to spend time in the [[:Category:Woods|woods]] than in cities, but preferred cities at night.<ref name="B-p54">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|54}}</ref> She gained from her mother a knowledge of woodland herbs and their use in healing applications.<ref name="B-p92">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|92}}</ref>

==Possessions==
Tsarra inherited [[Mhaornathil]], a magical [[Rillifane Rallathil]]-blessed [[scimitar]] that could harm [[ghost]]s, from her mother, and she held a deep affection for it.<ref name="B-p22"/> Tsarra was given the [[Shield Belt of Storms]] to wear on 28 Uktar 1374 DR, through which she bore the majority of the spirit of her friend [[Danthra]].<ref name="B-p49">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|49}}</ref> As an [[arcane archer]],<ref name="B-p95">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|95}}</ref> Tsarra usually possessed a bow.<ref name="B-p99">{{Cite book/Blackstaff/MMP|99}}</ref>

==Relationships==
Tsarra's father was the human ranger [[Taalmuth Chaadren]]. Her mother was the sylvan elf [[Malruthiia Chaadren]] (originally an exiled bastard child of the [[Ilbaereth]] family).<ref name="charprof"/>

While on a hunt in the [[Ardeep Forest]] in [[1357 DR]] with her father, Taalmuth became involved in a spell battle against a suspected [[lich]] (the true identity of the assailant was not known) and was killed. Her mother pulled some favors and gained Tsarra an apprenticeship at [[Blackstaff Tower]]. Shortly afterwards, her mother was killed while protecting the [[Elfstone Tavern]] from an attack by [[Myrkul]]'s horde of undead. From losing both her parents to undead creatures, Tsarra formed a deep hatred for all manner of undead.<ref name="charprof"/>

'''Blackstaff''' was the title and name given to the master of the [[Blackstaff (staff)|eponymous staff]] and [[Blackstaff Tower]], including [[Blackstaff Academy]], as well as the [[Archmage]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="FRCG-p198">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|198}}</ref> "Blackstaff" was originally a nickname given to [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun|Khelben Arunsun]] because of his powerful staff of the same name,<ref name="FRCS3e-p275">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|275}}</ref> but after his death it became the title used by his successors.<ref name="FRCG-p198" />

==Blackstaffs==
Over time, it became customary for the Blackstaff to choose an heir,<ref>{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|?|22}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|?|24}}</ref> called the '''Blackstaff's Heir'''.<ref>{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|?|Prologue}}</ref>

The magical quasi-spirits of the former Blackstaffs resided within the tower and served as advisors to the Blackstaff. They were not true spirits but were green-tinged facsimiles produced by the green ''[[kiira N'Vaelahr]]'' whose magic was bound to the tower.<ref name="BlackstaffTower235">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|235|20}}</ref> The ''kiira N'Vaelhar'' once held the spirits and combined knowledge of the first three Blackstaffs—Khelben Arusun, [[Tsarra Chaadren]], and [[Kyriani Agrivar]]—but [[Krehlan Arunsun]] and the Grand Mages of [[Rhymanthiin]] dissipated the ''kiira'' in the [[1415 DR|Year of Staves Arcane, 1415 DR]], and bound its magic to Blackstaff Tower and its twin in Rhymanthiin, [[N'Vaerymanth]]. Thereafter, whenever someone became a Blackstaff or the heir, a template of their spirit, intellect, and knowledge became part of the Blackstaff and the tower (not the actual [[spirit]]s). These spirits could not leave the tower, but the Blackstaff could communicate with them or tap into their knowledge whenever he or she so wished. Krehlan merged the gem with the tower so that it could be the Blackstaff's advisor, rather than have all the combined consciousnesses of the previous Blackstaffs overwhelm the current Blackstaff.<ref name="BlackstaffTower252-254">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|252–254|22}}</ref>

The Blackstaff could also manipulate the tower at will, opening any door with a thought, and could listen in on the conversations of anyone within the tower. He or she also knew the location and nature of every [[magical item]] within the tower and could see the tower’s twin, N'Vaerymanth, in his or her mind.<ref name="BlackstaffTower252-254"/>

In public, she wore a magical illusion of Khelben so that others not close to the Blackstaff would believe Khelben was still alive. She could commune with Khelben’s spirit whenever she wished and had all the knowledge and memories of the previous Blackstaff. Tsarra was able to hold the [[Spellplague]] at bay the first time it struck Waterdeep, but the second time, when it surged from [[Undermountain]], it shattered her illusion as Khelben and revealed her true self before a crowd of gathered nobles at the palace. '''Ahghairon's Tower''' was the home and eventually the final resting place of [[Ahghairon]], the first [[Open Lord]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="CoS-CG-p27">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|27}}</ref>

==Location==
Ahghairon's Tower was located in a courtyard at the intersection of the [[Street of Silks]] and [[Waterdeep Way]] in the [[Castle Ward of Waterdeep]].<ref name="BT"/>

==Activities==
Ahghairon's Tower was haunted by several grim sights. One was the skeleton of [[Melkar|Melkar of Mirabar]], a wizard who had attempted to breach the tower. His skeleton stood inside the magical fields facing the north side of the tower, with its arms stretched wide.<ref name="VGW-p8">{{Cite book/Volo's Guide to Waterdeep|8}}</ref>

Local children made it a game to rearrange the bones inside the fields with a long stick, as the bones would always eventually drift back into position. The practice was eventually banned by the Open Lord, [[Piergeiron Paladinson]].<ref name="VGW-p8"/>

Another set of skeletal remains was displayed within the tower's magical fields. These had once belonged to the wizard [[Khondar Naomal]], who nearly breached its defenses in {{Yearlink|1479}}.<ref name="BT-p261-299">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|261-299}}</ref> Khondar's skeleton was condemned to forever be pursued by spectral wolves, torn apart, and then reformed.<ref name="BT-p300">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|300}}</ref>

The final, and perhaps strangest, sight was that of [[Ahghairon's Great Cloak]], which flew from the tower every 15{{th}} day of Ches to possess a chosen bearer. The Cloak was understood to be possessed by the spirit of Ahghairon, and its bearer would be under its command. The bearer could enter the tower if the Cloak so desired, though they would retain no memories of its interior after the Cloak removed itself from the bearer.<ref name="BT-290">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|290}}</ref><ref name="RBN-SoA">{{Cite web/Realms by Night/Part Nine: The Spirit of Ahghairon}}</ref>

==Defenses==
A series of six barriers were put in place to prevent entry into the tower. To successfully gain entry to the tower, all wards required keys. Any item that Ahghairon himself had bespelled worked as a key, including [[Ahghairon's Amulet]], [[Ahghairon's Ring]], [[Anthaorl's Fang]], and [[Ahghairon's Key]].<ref name="BT-257">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|257}}</ref><ref name="BT-216">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|216}}</ref><ref name="BT-223">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|223}}</ref> Opening each barrier gave a limited amount of time to open the next, as each would reactivate after a short time.<ref name="BT-p214">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|214}}</ref>

One of the wards was a permanent ''[[forcecage]]'' spanning ten feet out from the tower, invisible except for a slight shimmering in the air. Unless the ''forcecage'' was attacked, it would remain invisible. Within this ''forcecage'' was another ward, a ''[[prismatic sphere]]'' similarly enchanted to be invisible until attacked.<ref name="CoS-CG-p45">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|45}}</ref><ref name="WatN-p23">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|23}}</ref><ref name="VGW-p8"/><ref name="CoS:W-p115">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|115}}</ref>

Within the ''prismatic sphere'' was a field enchanted with the ''[[time stop]]'' spell, and within the ''time stop'' field was a field enchanted with ''[[spell turning]].'' Within the ''spell turning'' field was an ''[[alarm]]'' spell that would ring a loud alarm in the [[Palace of Waterdeep]] and [[Blackstaff Tower]], as well as [[Herald's Holdfast]]. Upon the alarm being triggered, the [[Griffon (Walking Statue)|Griffon Statue]] would be teleported from Mount Waterdeep in order to defend the tower. A [[cyclocone]] would also be activated, creating a whirlwind to swirl about the tower.<ref name="CoS-CG-p45"/><ref name="WatN-p23"/><ref name="CoS:W-p115"/>

==Geography==
Mount Waterdeep shielded the city from the worst coastal storm winds.<ref name="FRCS3e-p179" /><ref name="CS-CG2e-p19">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|19}}</ref> Inside the heart of the mountain there was a freshwater spring, that once spewed out and down its seaward rocks and into the sea below. Later on this spring was redirected into large-cistern caverns that were built into the upper reaches of the mountain, which then went down pipes into the city's [[Waterdeep/Sewers|sewer system]].<ref name="MWFS">{{Cite web/Candlekeep forum/Questions for Ed Greenwood (2014)/Mount Waterdeep's Freshwater System}}</ref>

==History==
There was a ballad that spoke an ancient city sunk into the sea and the gods raised a head stone for it to mark the grave. That was Mount Waterdeep.<ref name="WW-p15">{{Cite book/Windwalker/Hardcover|15}}</ref>

Mount Waterdeep was once home to the underhalls of [[Melairbode]], a shield dwarf hold.<ref name="WDMM5e-p86">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dungeon of the Mad Mage|86}}</ref>

==Notable Locations==
* Waterdeep's [[Peaktop Aerie]], which housed the [[Griffon Cavalry]] riders, sat on this mountain top.<ref name="WDH5e-p181">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|181}}</ref>
* A lookout tower.<ref name="CS-CG2e-p46">{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|46}}</ref>
* A [[Celestial Stairway]]<ref>{{Cite comic/Total Eclipse|15-16}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book/Waterdeep|328}}</ref>
* On its seaward side rested eleven gigantic triple catapults.<ref name="CoSW-p96" />
* Several sea caves existed on the western side of the mountain. These were all watched by the [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]].<ref name="CS-CG2e-p46" />
* The [[South Sea Caves (Waterdeep)|South Sea Caves]] were connected to Undermountain by a set of hoists large enough to move a sailing ship.<ref name="CoSW-p96" />
* The [[Cliffride]], a steep gravel path that ran along the western edge of Mount Waterdeep's northern spur, connecting the festhall [[Silavene's]], the rental villa of [[Fair Winds]], and, at its southern end, the [[bardic college]] of [[New Olamn]].<ref name="CS-CG2e-p46" />

==Inhabitants==
The reclusive [[monk]] [[Hlam]] lived in a cave halfway up the side of the mountain.<ref name="CSW3e-p49">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|49}}</ref><ref name="WDH5e-p204">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|204}}</ref>

The '''God Catcher''' was a tenement building in the [[Waterdeep/Castle Ward|Castle Ward]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="W-DH-p182">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|182}}</ref><ref name="tGC">{{Cite book/The God Catcher|?}}</ref>

==Location==
[[File:GodCatcher-Map.png|thumb|220px|''The God Catcher marked on a map.'']]
This structure was situated near The Market, at the intersection of [[Sul Street]] and [[Heard Lane]].<ref name="W-DH-p182" /><ref name="tGC" />

==History==
The building was originally one of the colossal [[Walking Statues of Waterdeep]] but in [[1385 DR]] the [[Spellplague]] animated all the guardians of the city and they walked around Waterdeep wreaking havoc. The [[Blackstaff]] at the time, [[Tsarra Chaadren]], turned the ground to mud underneath the statue and it sank partway into the ground. As mysteriously as it animated, it stopped and settled in the Castle Ward, just north of the Market at the intersection of Sul Street and Heard Lane.<ref name="W-DH-p182" /><ref name="tGC" />

Over time, the statue was hollowed out and partitioned into living quarters. The leg that sank into the ground penetrated a sewer line so a stairway was hollowed out inside of it allowing access to [[Waterdeep/Sewers|the sewer system]] of the city. As of [[1479 DR]], the statue/building was owned by the reclusive sorcerer [[Aundra Blacklock]], who lived in the hollowed-out stone sphere that floated above the statue's upraised right hand.<ref name="W-DH-p182" /><ref name="tGC" />

The '''Castle Ward''' was the central ward of the city of [[Waterdeep]]. It encompassed [[Mount Waterdeep]], [[Castle Waterdeep]], and much of the government of the city, which included the [[Palace of Waterdeep]], occupied by [[Piergeiron the Paladinson]] in the mid-14{{th}} century [[DR]], and the residences of many prosperous Waterdhavians.<ref name="CoSW-p96">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|96}}</ref> Among these citizens were celebrated retired adventurers such as [[Mirt the Moneylender]].<ref name="CoSW-p100"/>

==Geography==
The ward was delimited by [[Julthoon Street]] to the north, west of [[Shield Street]], and by [[Trader's Way]] between Shield Street and the [[High Road (Waterdeep)|High Road]], which delimited the ward's eastern border together with [[Snail Street]]. The southern border of the ward was delimited by [[Shesstra Street]], [[Gut Alley]], [[Belnimbra's Street]], and [[Lackpurse Lane]], until the ward met [[Deepwater Harbor]].<ref name="CoS-AG2e-p18">{{Template:Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|18}}</ref>

===Geographical Features===
Much of the territory of the Castle Ward was taken up by Mount Waterdeep, which consisted mostly of unused land.<ref name="CoS-CG2e-p9" /><ref name="CoS-AG2e-p18" />

==History==
The Castle Ward was one of the original wards of Waterdeep, established by [[Ahghairon]] in the {{Template:YearlinkName|1035}} together with the [[Waterdeep/Trades Ward|Trades Ward]], [[Waterdeep/Southern Ward|Temple Ward]], and [[Waterdeep/Dock Ward|Dock Ward]].<ref name="CoS-CG2e-p30" />

The '''Trades Ward''' was one of the seven wards of the city of [[Waterdeep]]. It was the main shopping district of Waterdeep and housed many of the city's guildhalls.<ref name="VGtW-p105" /> 

==Geography==
The ward was bounded in the south by the [[Southern Ward of Waterdeep|Southern Ward]], in the west by [[Castle Ward]], in the north by [[North Ward of Waterdeep|North Ward]] and the [[City of the Dead (Waterdeep)|City of the Dead]] and in the east by the [[Trollwall]].<ref name="VGtW-p105" />

==History==
In the late 14{{th}} century [[DR]], over the course of a [[tenday]] and more, streets across the Trades Ward experienced a number of [[ghost]]ly sightings that left many citizens, scribes, and members of the [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|City Watch]] confused. Some speculated if a [[wizard]] experimenting with [[illusion]]s may have been the cause. Others speculated it was a wizard seeking to harm local businesses, drive down local property values, or simply spread fear&mdash;though all who put forward these theories admitted to having nothing to backup their claims.<ref name="WN.GS" />

Some interpreted the apparitions as being a message &mdash; [[Sambral the Soothsayer]] would claim the dead were warning Waterdhavians of impending battles from underground invaders, [[Sheireera|Sheireera of the Prophecies]] would claim that it was the dead crying out against immorality and lawlessness in Waterdeep, and [[priest]] [[Thalomaun]] of the [[Church of Lathander]] would make a proclamation on [[Virgin's Square]] that it was a sign from the dead for the living of the affected neighborhoods to do away with old businesses and make way for new ones.<ref name="WN.GS">{{Cite web/Waterdeep News/Ghost Sightings}}</ref>
