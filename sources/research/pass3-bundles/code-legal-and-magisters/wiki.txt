=== WIKI PAGE: Magister

[[File:Talatha Vaerovree 3e.png|thumb|right|250px|''Talatha Vaerovree, a Magister, shows spells to children.'']]
The '''Magister''' was a personal champion of [[Mystra]], devoted to spreading the use of magic. It was a lonely office, whose holder was often besieged by challengers looking to take it for themselves.<ref name="SotM-p5"/>

==Duties==
The central task of the Magister was to spread the use and teaching of magic, encouraging its use, and promoting its availability and utility. Specific Magisters sometimes received different tasks, but the common thread was always spreading magic.<ref name="SotM-p5"/>

Though a number of magisters, from [[Shornthal Imynster]]<ref name="SotM-p30"/> up to [[Maldiglas Turntower]]<ref name="SotM-p72">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|72}}</ref> devoted notable amounts of effort to influencing rulers, by {{yearlink|1370}}, the Magister was strongly advised to steer clear of politics.<ref name="SotM-p5">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|5}}</ref>

==Succession==
The most common manner of acquiring the title of the Magister was by slaying the previous holder with magic. Duels by 1370 could be simple spell duels, or they could be nonfatal, when Azuth sought to shield the Magister or the challenger from death. In those cases, Azuth converted all their spells into a force that would force a green flame provided by the deity of wizardry closer or further to the challengers; a challenger touched by the flame would lose the office. Azuth would heal both mages, and then spirit away the loser, with their wits broken but otherwise protected from all things, to some remote location for about a month. At the end of this time, they would regain their wits.<ref name="SotM-p7_10"/>

The Magister was not allowed to refuse a duel, but Azuth could judge that a duel could not be fair, to prevent a succession of challengers from wearing down the incumbent. In such cases, the god himself or some [[shadowstaff|shadowstaves]] would cloak their location and perhaps ''teleport'' one or both parties to other locations.<ref name="SotM-p7_10"/>

Question duels were also an option; challengers could instead ask each other obscure magical trivia, and by quizzing each other, demonstrate their superiority in arcane knowledge. Azuth was the sole judge of such duels; clergy of Azuth and Savras were granted knowledge of the duel's questions and answers when it happened, but they did not receive knowledge of the challengers' identities.<ref name="SotM-p7_10"/>

By {{yearlink|1370}} trial by combat was still a means of acquiring the office,<ref name="SotM-p7_10">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|7-10}}</ref> but by {{yearlink|1372}}, Mystra and Azuth had completely abandoned trial by combat as a method of succession.<ref name="MoF-">{{cite book/Magic of Faerun}}</ref>

Whenever a Magister died to something other than another mage (or if the other mage slew that Magister and died in the process, or if their combat was far too one-sided for whatever reason), [[Azuth]] selected another mage whom he believed would work to spread magic, and offered them the office; it could be refused without disfavor.<ref name="SotM-p7">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|7}}</ref> Before the recorded Magisters, Mystra had made a number of offers herself.<ref name="SotM-p7"/>

Other Magisters had voluntarily sought release from the office, judging they were not suited to it. When Mystra judged this to be honest, the Magisters could be made into [[Chosen of Mystra]] instead, or thrust directly into [[Mystra's Choice]]. This path had been selected by both [[Alvaerele Tasundrym]] and [[Azuth]]. They still went through [[Mystra's Choice]] upon death.<ref name="SotM-p14">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|14}}</ref>

Whenever a new Magister was named, every mage casting a spell at the moment felt a sudden, violent sensation; priesthood of Mystra, Azuth or Savras found a manifestation in their temples, where the symbol of the Magister appeared on all scrying surfaces, afterwards transforming into the [[mage sigil]] employed by the mage who had gained the office, for about ten minutes. Magisters who eschewed a sigil or whose sigil was too close to that of another mage instead got their name spelled out in [[Common]].<ref name="SotM-p9_10">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|9-10}}</ref>

==Relationships==
Though the office made them targets for endless waves of ambitious mages, leading to the severing of many relationships, the Magister could count on a few groups to behave in specific ways. The clergy of [[Azuth]] and [[Mystra]] were generally allies of the Magister, while the clergy of [[Savras]] was unfriendly to them; nonetheless, even those of Savras took care not to antagonize the Magister, and might even be counted on to render a small amount of aid. The clergy of [[Velsharoon]] was a constant enemy, and they had attempted to trap, rather than kill, the Magister simply to prove the might of Velsharoon.<ref name="SotM-p5_6">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|5-6}}</ref>

Mystra ruled the Magister, though lightly, and seldom making pronouncements. Azuth, on the other hand, was known to advise the Magister, and appeared far more often than Mystra to them.<ref name="SotM-p5_6"/>

==Powers==
Mystra conferred a plethora of powers upon the Magister, which she changed on occassion, in response to specific events.

===Powers upon Succession===
By 1370 DR, immediately upon becoming the Magister, a mage would have their brow encircled by silver fire; this granted their weapons the ability to strike and harm just about any creature, though it did not really improve their attacks. Furthermore, they gained a short-term immunity to all magic, and protection equivalent to an ''[[ironguard]]'' spell. The ''ironguard'' spell faded after nine days; the immunity to all magic stopped protecting against one school every four to seven months, though never against ''[[enchantment]]''.<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/> Mystra began granting strong immunities against magic starting in the [[Night of Fourteen Magisters]], [[Eleasis 4]] of the {{YearlinkName|709}}.<ref name="SotM-p50">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|50}}</ref>

===Constant Powers===
By 1370 DR, Magisters were provided with ''magesight'': a special insight into the [[Weave]], allowing them to know when their own spellcasting was somehow impaired, when large magical discharges were nearby, or whenever anyone within {{SI|60|feet|long=yes}} cast a spell. They could also sense [[wild magic]] and [[dead magic]] areas, the boundaries of sorcerous wards, magical traps, and untriggered spells within {{SI|90|feet|long=yes}}.<ref name="SotM-p11_12">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|11-12}}</ref>

By 1370 DR, the Magister could employ ''[[dimension door]]'' and ''[[water walk]]'' six times a day, they were always protected by ''[[feather fall]]'' and could ''[[levitate]]'' at will, without the need for spells, for instance, preventing them from falling into a chasm suddenly opening under their feet,<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/> as was the cause of death of the Magister [[Horgyl Tusselvarr]] in the {{yearlinkName|1204}}.<ref name="SotM-p65">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|65}}</ref> By concentrating, Magisters could activate ''[[true seeing]]'' at will. These abilities were not spells, and could be used at the same time as a spell.<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/>

The Magister [[Onsilur Maerdrathom]] was imprisoned inside a ''[[kiira]]'' at some point prior to the {{yearlink|326}}.<ref name="SotM-p39"/> By 1370 DR, the Magister was immune to the spells ''[[imprisonment]]'' and ''[[sink]]''; the Magister was also undetectable by any [[divination]] spells,<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/> an ability that made some aspects of [[Athelzedar's rest]] redundant, developed by the Magister [[Imrul Athelzedar]] shortly before the {{yearlink|1354}}.<ref name="SotM-p82">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|82}}</ref>

By 1370 DR, the Magister was immune to all glyphs, runes, symbols, and other such wards based on enchanted inscriptions. They could also cross through any barriers generated by spells less powerful than a ''[[limited wish]]'', without the need to break them. They were also immune, much like the Chosen, to nine spells of their choice, which had to be of different power.<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/>

Magisters [[Shornthal Imynster]]<ref name="SotM-p30"/> and [[]] were recorded to have battled constructs during their time in office; by 1370 DR, the Magister was always intangible to all magical constructs and automatons.<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/> 

The Magister [[Tyvbolt Arsurath]] was struck insane by studying a magical mirror at some point before the {{yearlinkName|544}}.<ref name="SotM-p43">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|43}}</ref> After his fate, and that of a later Magister,<ref name="TWEET3">{{cite web/theedverse/2022-06-13 - Urboltar and Mind Shielding}}</ref> Mystra had granted the Magister absolute immunity to all mind-affecting magic, all [[enchantment]] spells, and all psionics, by 1370 DR. This ability exceeded the defense which Mystra granted her chosen, extending even to artifacts and to the powers of other divinities.<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/> It could only be bypassed by the Magister's own doing;<ref name="TWEET">{{cite web/theedverse/2022-06-13 - Magister Self-Enchantment}}</ref> Magister [[Veldrin Daerivyn]] had been known to accidentally drive himself near-catatonic shortly before his death.<ref name="SotM-p51_52">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|51-52}}</ref>

By 1370 DR, the Magister had a resistance to all magic that could nullify any spell just under half the time. At the Magister's option, the nullified spell could also be redirected back at the source or at a third party, or even turned into a surge of wild magic prior to redirection. No ''[[teleport]]'' spells or similar of theirs were subject to any chance of failure.<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/>

Finally, a Magister could hear their own name whenever it was spoken anywhere in Faerûn, as well as the title of the Magister, much like the [[Chosen of Mystra]]. They could turn this ability off, but it turned back on whenever they awoke, which allowed them to sleep from time to time.<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/>

===Spellcasting===
The spells of the Magister were also boosted in a number of ways. Those who would otherwise save themselves from the worst of the Magister's magic too narrowly were struck in full, due to the perfection granted to their magic. They also needed no [[material component]]s to cast spells, only through development. They were able to cast any spell that took less than a full wizard's title to cast without moving a muscle.<ref name="SotM-p12_13">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|12-13}}</ref>

They were also granted up to nine spells of varying power, that once per day, they could cast without having prepared before. The selection could be changed only yearly.<ref name="SotM-p12_13"/>

Magisters who specialized in a school of magic were able to change specialization with but three months of work. Most were not specialized in the first place, however.<ref name="SotM-p12_13"/> They also regained their spells within minutes of study,<ref name="SotM-p12_13"/> and were granted visions of any magic created by previous Magisters that resembled spells they were interested in developing. One specific spell, the ''[[mantle (spell)|mantle]]'', which used to cause permanent degradation of the user's ability to protect themselves, had this requirement waived for the Magister.<ref name="SotM-p12_13"/>

===Greater Powers===
A Magister could grant a target they touched the ability to cast a given spell. At their option, it could be cast thrice an hour for a tenday (a total of roughly 720 uses) or instead once for each year of their lives per year, with one less every year until fully fading. A third option was to allow it to be used twice a night, so long as the Moon was full. The Magister was forbidden, however, from informing the frequency bestowed, and the ability cost them a permanent impairment in their vitality.<ref name="SotM-p12_13"/> Magister [[Immué Dathril]] was known to have imbued ''[[fly]]'' spells upon an entire [[Calishite]] dancing establishment during her tenure, at some point between the {{yearlink|1066}} and the {{yearlink|1104}}.<ref name="SotM-p62_63">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|62-63}}</ref>

The apex of a Magister's powers, however, was the ''Magister's Ban'', the ability to substitute, Toril-wide, the effects of one spell for those of another. This ability, however, could only be used to further magic: Mystra immediately ended a ban used lightly or for the Magister's own gain. Such prohibitions had lasted for a tenday, or perhaps a month, though some had been recorded to last for a year. Mages usually found out the hard way that a spell's effect had been substituted.<ref name="SotM-p12_13"/>

The Magister had other powers, but only those two were revealed immediately to a new Magister.<ref name="SotM-p12_13">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|12-13}}</ref> For instance, they could be granted the [[metamagic]] powers of a [[Weavespinner]], were they to inquire about them.<ref name="SotM-p126">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|126}}</ref>

Magisters also had the ability to use the spells banned by [[Mystra]] after [[Karsus' Folly]], but this thrust them into [[Mystra's Choice]] immediately and attracted the attention of both her and [[Azuth]].<ref name="SotM-91">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|91}}</ref>

==History==
The office of the Magister was first occupied by [[Azuth]] during his mortal days; after his ascension to divinity, the office was given to a different mage, and held by a number of them over the years.<ref name="SotM-p4">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|4}}</ref>

There was no record of the Magisters prior to [[Shornthal Imynster]];<ref name="SotM-p30">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|30}}</ref> however, at least one such Magister employed a spell forbidden by [[Mystra]] to slay a city's inhabitants in [[Halruaa]] after they decided not to heed his warnings as to the use of magic, which Mystra immediately reverted, creating a zone of wild magic. One later Magister took to the long task of restoring magic in the city to normal.<ref name="SotM-p91">{{cite book/Secrets of the Magister|91}}</ref> By {{yearlink|1370}}, the Magister's duties specifically excluded maintenance of the Weave, save when specifically instructed.<ref name="SotM-p5"/>

During the early 1370s DR, the clergy of [[Velsharoon]] had managed to trap at least one Magister and attempted to convert them into undead. Mystra personally broke the Magister out of confinement.<ref name="SotM-p5_6"/><ref name="NN" group="note">Velsharoon ascended in 1369 DR; as the events referred to in Secrets of the Magister do not extend into 1372, this narrows down the time in which such an event could have happened.</ref>

Talatha Vaerovree was the last Magister recorded before the Spellplague of {{yearlink|1385}}, when she was slain by [[Orbakh]] of [[Westgate]].<ref name="RotD-MMoM">{{cite book/Realms of the Dead/The Many Murders of Manshoon}}</ref> Sages have claimed that the Magister's head exploded at the time of the Spellplague: there may have been another Magisters after her, whose career may have been too short for a proper record, or only the appearance of Azuth could carry the the entitlement.<ref name="TWEET2">{{cite web/theedverse/2022-05-25 - Magister and the Spellplague}}</ref>

There was no Magister after the [[Spellplague]], until the [[Second Sundering]].<ref name="TWEET2"></ref> In [[1442 DR]], [[Tannathra]] of [[Hardcastle]] was offered by [[Azuth]] to be Magister in the absence of Mystra. After Tannathra's retirement in [[1490 DR]], Mystra and Azuth appointed [[Valargrim Hond]] to be Magister, who died by battle in [[1496 DR]]. [[Ramarthant Horaerays]] was the Magister of the turn of the century.<ref name="Patreon-magister" /> The Magister's identity was not revealed as of the late 15th century [[DR]].<ref name="TWEET3"></ref>

==Rumors & Legends==
Though Mystra and Azuth were loath to give out any information on previous Magisters, it had been possible by 1370 to refute, amongst other things, that the Magister could command all [[dragon]]s in Faerûn, that they could teleport at will, that their spells were boosted by a permanent connection to the Weave, and that they could eavesdrop into the minds of all mages at will.<ref name="SotM-p11_12"/>

===Background===
A Magister NPC or even PC class may have been intended for the 4{{th}}-edition ''[[Forgotten Realms Player's Guide]]''. As discussed [https://www.deviantart.com/ironshod/art/Magister-96929729 here], artist [[Anne Stokes]] was commissioned to paint four illustrations for the FRPG, but only three were printed for paragon paths. The fourth is titled "Magister", possibly suggesting a Magister paragon path or epic destiny was intended.

The '''Castle Ward''' was the central ward of the city of [[Waterdeep]]. It encompassed [[Mount Waterdeep]], [[Castle Waterdeep]], and much of the government of the city, which included the [[Palace of Waterdeep]], occupied by [[Piergeiron the Paladinson]] in the mid-14{{th}} century [[DR]], and the residences of many prosperous Waterdhavians.<ref name="CoSW-p96">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|96}}</ref> Among these citizens were celebrated retired adventurers such as [[Mirt the Moneylender]].<ref name="CoSW-p100"/>

==Geography==
The ward was delimited by [[Julthoon Street]] to the north, west of [[Shield Street]], and by [[Trader's Way]] between Shield Street and the [[High Road (Waterdeep)|High Road]], which delimited the ward's eastern border together with [[Snail Street]]. The southern border of the ward was delimited by [[Shesstra Street]], [[Gut Alley]], [[Belnimbra's Street]], and [[Lackpurse Lane]], until the ward met [[Deepwater Harbor]].<ref name="CoS-AG2e-p18">{{Template:Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Adventurer's Guide to the City|18}}</ref>

===Geographical Features===
Much of the territory of the Castle Ward was taken up by Mount Waterdeep, which consisted mostly of unused land.<ref name="CoS-CG2e-p9" /><ref name="CoS-AG2e-p18" />

==History==
The Castle Ward was one of the original wards of Waterdeep, established by [[Ahghairon]] in the {{Template:YearlinkName|1035}} together with the [[Waterdeep/Trades Ward|Trades Ward]], [[Waterdeep/Southern Ward|Temple Ward]], and [[Waterdeep/Dock Ward|Dock Ward]].<ref name="CoS-CG2e-p30" />

: The massive peak around which Waterdeep was built<ref name="CoSW-p96"/> encompassed the southwest portion of the city.<ref name="CoSW-p93">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|93}}</ref> Atop its summit was the [[Peaktop Aerie]], which housed the city's famed [[Griffon Cavalry]], and its sea-facing flank featured 11 triple-[[catapult]]s armed with rock and flammable pots. ===Castles, keeps and towers===
;[[Castle Waterdeep]]
:The great fortress of Waterdeep loomed over the surrounding ward from the side of Mount Waterdeep. Its hanging banners and heraldry were so massive they signaled diplomatic arrivals and ceremonial announcements to those even in the southern wards of the city.<ref name="CoSW-p96"/> It was used by the [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|city guard]] and the [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|watch]] for training, housed the stables for some 70 [[horse|warhorse]]s and held several levels of subterranean dungeons.<ref name="WatN-p22">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|22}}</ref>

;[[Palace of Waterdeep]]
: The white marble palace of the city's [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lords]] stood as a beacon of the incorruptibility of their rule over the city. The shining tower housed the Lords' court, embassies from other city-states and nations and the offices of city officials, including those from the [[City Guard (Waterdeep)|City Guard]] and [[City Watch (Waterdeep)|Watch]].<ref name="CoSW-p96"/> 

*[[Ahghairon's Tower]]: This small, stone tower was the former residence of Waterdeep's first Lord Magister, [[Ahghairon]]. It had a modest appearance<ref name="WatN-p22">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|22}}</ref> but was protected by [[spell]]s like ''[[prismatic sphere]]'' and a ''[[forcecage]]''.<ref name="WatN-p23">{{Cite book/Waterdeep and the North|23}}</ref> As of [[1357 DR]], no one was believed to have entered its walls since Ahghairon's death.<ref name="WatN-p22"/>

'''Manshoon''' ({{Pronounce|man|SHOON|refs=<ref name="FRCS1e-DM-p25-26" /><ref name="WDH5e-p4">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|4}}</ref>}}) was the founder of the [[Zhentarim]],<ref name="FRCS-p282">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|282}}</ref> also known as the Black Network, until he was "slain" by [[Fzoul Chembryl]], his ally, and Lord [[Orgauth]] in [[1370 DR]]. However, it transpired that Manshoon had created a dozen or more [[clone]]s that the murderous duo then found themselves facing. Only three of these clones were known to have survived into the late 1370s [[DR]] and once more headed the evil organization known as the Zhentarim.<ref name="FRCS-p283">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|283}}</ref>

==Personality==
Completely fitting the archetype of the sly-tongued, sinister, and decadent master spellcaster, Manshoon ruthlessly crushed revolts within his own ranks of the Black Network, easily slaughtered his rivals and weeded the weak out the ranks of the young and ambitious by sending upstarts on nigh-impossible suicide missions. Hated and mistrusted by Fzoul, Manshoon manipulated him much as he did all others—remaining arrogant, untouchable in his power, and faintly amused by it all.<ref name="FRCS-p283" />

==Origins==
The most popular and widely accepted theory about the origins of the Spellplague was that this calamity was caused by the death of Mystra, the [[god]]dess of [[Magic]]. For eons, magic had been bound within Mystra's [[Weave]], the universal structure of arcane forces, and her death tore the Weave apart and unleashed the [[raw magic]] contained within it, creating the cataclysm that was later known as the Spellplague.<ref name="FRCG-p50"/><ref name="NV">{{Cite game/Neverwinter}}</ref>

Other scholars, however, didn't consider this to be a logical explanation. Historically, two earlier incarnations of Mystra had died before only to be reborn, and the Weave maintained its hold over the power of magic for the duration of her deaths. In these scholars' views, the truth would never be fully known to mortals, and perhaps not even to the [[god]]s.<ref name="NV"/><ref name="PoS-p79">{{Cite book/Plague of Spells|79}}</ref>

Around [[1396 DR]], the sentient [[construct]] [[Cynosure (construct)|Cynosure]] theorized that the escape of an entity named the [[Carnis|Traitor]] from its confinement in the dungeons of [[Stardeep]], around the same time Mystra was murdered, may have also influenced the Spellplague's virulent onslaught.<ref name="PoS-p79"/>

Around [[1479 DR]], scholars and sages who studied the [[Neverwinter/Chasm|Chasm]] of [[Neverwinter]], such as [[Rhazzad]], believed the [[aboleth]]s of the [[Abolethic Sovereignty]] may have been the actual creators of the Spellplague, although there were no definite proof.<ref name="NV"/>

=== LINKED WIKI PAGE (first 5000 chars): Marking the years

{{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

'''Tashalar''', also referred to as '''the Tashalar''', was a small, self-contained country located on a strip of coast along the southern [[Shining Sea]]. It lay just east of the [[Chult]] peninsula, and west of the magic realm of [[Halruaa]]. It was famous for beautiful weather, beautiful people, excellent crossbows, and the richest wine-producing region in the world.<ref name="FRCS-p105">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 3rd edition|105}}</ref><ref name="SK-p124" />
{{TOC limit|3}}

==Description==
The country was a lush and bountiful land of great natural wealth and goods that were considered exotic to many northern realms.<ref name="SK-p124" /> It comprised a number of small city-states,<ref name="FRCS-p105" /> scattered rural villages and vast jungle wilderness.<ref name="SK-p124" /><ref name="SK-p125" />

==Geography==
While "the Tashalar" was the name commonly used to refer to the entirety of the [[Tashtan Coast]], the country itself comprised the [[Tashalar Basin]] set within the [[Hazur Mountains]].<ref name="SK-p124" />

'''Eldrus Wands''' was a mage of the 4{{th}} and 5{{th}} centuries [[DR]], the [[Magister]] from [[409 DR]] to [[516 DR]], and the founder of the famous [[Wands]] noble house of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="SotM-p41">{{Cite book/Secrets of the Magister|41}}</ref>

==History==
Originally named '''Eldrus Sunstaff''', he was a [[wizard]] from another world who arrived on [[Toril]] in the {{YearlinkName|395}}. He changed his surname to "Wands" at some point.<ref name="CoS.W-p63">{{Cite book/City of Splendors: Waterdeep|63}}</ref>

In the {{YearlinkName|409}}, he was hired by Lord [[Irgult Wheivron]], ruler of [[Narubel]], to take Irgult's form and wait in his bedchamber for a prearranged tryst with the then-current Magister, [[Eleedra Nathchant]], who used her powers to seduce and influence the rulers of the Realms and kill those who defied her. Eldrus challenged Eleedra and killed her in the ensuing spell-duel. After slaying her, Eldrus inherited the position of Magister.<ref name="SotM-p41"/> 

In his early days, Eldrus discovered some handily positioned ''[[gate]]s'' that permitted him to swiftly and easily move up and down the [[Sword Coast]] and travel to the [[Vilhon Reach]]. He also found a wizard's tower that had been completely robbed, yet two chests full of potions, among them several ''[[potion of longevity|potions of longevity]]'' had been overlooked. These would aid him as Magister.<ref name="SotM-p41"/> 

He had many adventures in his time. For example, Eldrus once found a wrecked [[spelljammer]] ship in the [[Wood of Sharp Teeth]], and flew it to the bottom of the [[Lake of the Long Arm]] on a joyride. Another was when he destroyed the fortress of [[Sorlauv]] of [[Athkatla]] to "persuade" him to release the captured [[treant]]s he was experimenting on. Eldrus also thieved the ''[[Evard's black tentacles]]'' spell from [[Candlekeep]] and sold many copied [[scroll]]s of it across [[Faerûn]] in the guise of a poor and simple former adventurer, charging mere handfuls of [[copper piece]]s.<ref name="SotM-p41"/> 

One of his legacies as Magister was many standing stones around the Realms. When Eldrus found a wizard being pointlessly cruel or harsh, he sometimes transformed them to stone, followed by a second spell to sheathe them in an additional layer of rock. The result was a sort of stone prison, one that was protected from the elements, could not be identified as a petrified person, and was immune to the effects of a simple, accidental ''[[dispel magic]]'' (it would remove an outer layer of stone, but not undo the petrification).<ref name="SotM-p41"/>

Although he used a number of longevity magics, Eldrus survived into old age. His skill at concealing his true nature ensured that no challenging wizards came calling during his reign as Magister. He died in the {{YearlinkName|516}}, reportedly sick and raving whilst wandering alone in the winter snow near [[Hill's Edge]], presumably of fever, exposure, or [[heartstop]]. He was succeeded as Magister by [[Thardon Ilvynner]].<ref name="SotM-p41" />

===Legacy===
From his many children, Eldrus was the founder of what would become House Wands of Waterdeep.<ref name="CoS.W-p63"/><ref name="SotM-p41" />

==Personality==
As an old man, he was good-natured, interested more in jokes than wealth or power and using his magic for kindness.<ref name="SotM-p41" />

==Activities==
Eldrus focused primarily on protecting himself, and he maintained many houses and disguises around the [[Realms]]. He traveled regularly and randomly, albeit relatively calmly, between his homes and hideouts.<ref name="SotM-p41"/>

The heirloom was subsequently retrieved from the [[Tomb of Ankh-Kephra]] and returned to the Wands family.<ref name="Dec">{{Cite game/Descent to Undermountain}}</ref>

'''Jhesiyra Kestellharp''' was a [[human]] [[sorceress]] and one of [[Halaster]]'s apprentices, known as the [[Seven]], who accompanied him into [[Undermountain]]. She later reigned as [[Magister]] over [[658 DR|658]]–[[667 DR]].<ref name="SotM-p47">{{Cite book/Secrets of the Magister|47-48}}</ref><ref name="EtU-p8">{{Cite book/Expedition to Undermountain|8}}</ref> She was noted as the primary source of information on Halaster, as she was the only one of the Seven to leave his halls.<ref name="VLb-p30">{{Cite book/Villains' Lorebook|30}}</ref><ref name="EtU-p18" /> She transformed herself into an [[arcanaweave]], a ''[[wish]]'' [[living spell]]<ref name="EtU-p18">{{Cite book/Expedition to Undermountain|18}}</ref> and finally became fused with Undermountain itself.<ref name="DotMM-10">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dungeon of the Mad Mage|10}}</ref>

==Description==
In life, Jhesiyra was described by a fellow mage in [[Cormanthor]] as "short, broad-shouldered, curvaceous more than muscular, with snapping dark brown eyes and lustrous light brown hair, worn long but curbed by magic into graceful shapes, never a flurry before her eyes or a tangle anywhere." Another considered her "more a fierce flame, splendid in her energy, than a rose of placid beauty."<ref name="SotM-p47"/> A portrait in Undermountain made when she was still Halaster's apprentice depicted her as a young and plain woman with a faint hint of a smile, wearing yellow and blue robes.<ref name="DotMM-p300">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dungeon of the Mad Mage|300-301}}</ref>

As a living spell, she was but a web of magical energy, shimmering and silvery, with a blue-white glow at its heart.<ref name="EtU-p75"/>

==History==
Alongside the other six apprentices of Halaster, Jhesiyra departed from [[Halaster's Hold]] in the {{YearlinkName|307}}. They apparently all went underground, planning to establish their own underground holds.<ref name="EtU-p8"/><ref>{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|69}}</ref> But following Halaster's test, she left his service,<ref name="SotM-p47"/> and fled, knowing him to be utterly mad.<ref name="DotMM-10"/> Her writings afterward would tell the world what little was known of the Mad Mage.<ref name="VLb-p30"/>

'''Halaster Blackcloak''' (formerly '''Hilather Blackcloak''' {{Pronounce|HILL|a|thur|refs=<ref name="WitW-p12">{{Cite adventurers league/A Wrinkle in the Weave|12}}</ref>}}) was a notorious, ancient, and utterly insane [[wizard]] who resided within his lair, the infamous [[Undermountain]].<ref name="FRCS3e-p180-181" />

==Personality==
Halaster seemed, in a word, insane. He muttered and chuckled to himself ceaselessly and had difficulty holding the thread of any conversation or debate for very long. Nevertheless, he remained fully aware of threats and hazards, and reacted and defended himself with great speed and efficiency. While in Undermountain, he had only rare moments of lucidity, but if he was ever outside the dungeon, he became lucid for much of the time.<ref name="FRCS3e-p180-181" />

At these times, Halaster was cold, well-mannered, and dignified and fastidious and meticulous in his habits, though still thoroughly evil. He did not tolerate any insult or insolence in any case and remembered any offense or help given his way. He pushed to have his own way in all things, heedless of the havoc or harm done to others in the way.<ref name="FRCS3e-p180-181" />

His main hobbies were collecting new monsters and kinds of [[magic]]. However, having lived for millennia, he finds very little that he hasn't seen before. Thus, until such things came along, Halaster meddled in politics and goings-on in the world according to his whims for his own entertainment.<ref name="FRCS3e-p180-181" />

He was more gregarious as a young man, creating and hosting [[magefair]]s and [[spellmoot]]s, and taking many apprentices. However, as his power grew, he dealt more and more with beings from other [[planes]], which led to his growing madness.<ref name="TRoU-p3"/>

==Abilities==
As a mighty [[archmage]], Halaster Blackcloak had a vast array of spells and magical powers at his command. Among them, he could make use of ''[[contingency]]'' spells and [[clone]]s of himself to ensure his survival in any spell-battle.<ref name="FRCS3e-p180-181" />

Inside Undermountain, he could command all kinds of [[construct]]s, including [[crawling claw]]s, [[helmed horror]]s, and various [[golem]]s. He also made use of its countless traps and surprises, such as a "moving [[portal]]" that could pick up either himself or his victims and deposit them somewhere else.<ref name="FRCS3e-p180-181" />

In addition to his great magical prowess, Halaster was well-versed in many areas, from [[gem]]-cutting to engineering to the breeding, care, and containment of monsters, both alive and undead, particularly from those hailing from other [[plane]]s.<ref name="FRCS3e-p180-181" />

==Personality==
Ahghairon was known by several names during his youth, but went by only his first name during his time in Waterdeep.<ref name="GG-Ahghairon's name" />

==History==
{{quote|The original splendors of Waterdeep were Ahghairon's secrets, which keep us safe today and always, despite the predations of lesser so-called lords.|[[Agnan Crohal]], ''[[Tales Told Tavernside]]'', [[1268 DR]]<ref name="BS">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower||23}}</ref>}}<br/>

===The Trollwars===
The [[Trollwars#Second Trollwar|Second Trollwar]] broke out in [[940 DR]],<ref name="CoS.CG-p29" /> as [[troll]]s were pushed down into the area later known as the [[Evermoors|Trollmoors]] by [[orc]] uprisings further north. After a decade of battles, a young Ahghairon distinguished himself, using his magic to turn the war against the trolls.<ref name="CoS.CG-p26" /> First, the Waterdhavian defenders drove the trolls back from their walls. The human realms then united under Ahghairon, War Captain [[Aeroth of Silverymoon]], and [[Samular Caradoon]] of [[Tyr]], and together they eradicated trolls across the Evermoors, with war finally coming to an end in [[952 DR]].<ref name=GHR>{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|113}}</ref>

At some point during the 940's DR, Aghairon was in [[Waterdeep]], at the time called Nimoar's Hold. Fires set to hold of the trolls threatened to destroy a large part of the town, so Aghairon asked adventurers to find and bring him [[Halaster Blackcloak|Halaster]]'s wards to bolster a ritual that would allow him to stop the fires. (More info about this adventure can be found in [[Hero of the Troll Wars]])

A third Trollwar almost took place in [[963 DR]], when the traitors [[Aviss]] and [[Fellandar]] led an army of trolls out of the [[Mere of Dead Men]] against the city. They knew well the city's defenses, and [[Castle Waterdeep]] had only just begun construction. But Ahghairon and a band of [[wizard]]s hurled Aviss and Fellandar into an extra-dimensional prison, thwarting the attack.<ref name="ADDc-33-p16">{{Cite comic/Summer in the City|16}}</ref> <ref group="note">The comic "[[Summer in the City]]" actually refers to "great Aglairon", but by the context it seems clear this is meant to be Ahghairon.</ref>

===Rise to Power===
At this time, Waterdeep was ruled by the Warlord [[Rhaulor]], who wanted to use the city's power and wealth to create an empire spanning [[northwest Faerûn]]. Ahghairon strongly disagreed with this, and when he voiced his concerns, Rhaulor attacked him. Ahghairon  used his magic to turn Rhaulor's sword into a snake, which bit and poisoned him. Rhaulor died and Ahghairon assumed control of Waterdeep, proclaiming himself the first Open Lord, and establishing a form of government involving Masked Lords, whose identities would remain private.<ref name="CoS.CG-p27" />

The '''Red Wizards of Thay''', colloquially known as the '''Red Magic Cult''' in the [[Eastern Heartlands]], were the notorious and nefarious magocratic ruling class of the inhospitable but well-inhabited country of [[Thay]].<ref name="SCAG-p141">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|141}}</ref> Historically they owed their power to being cruel slavers,<ref name="SB.CG-p14">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|14}}</ref><ref name="CoR-p63">{{Cite book/Champions of Ruin|63}}</ref> demonologists,<ref name="SB.CG-p17">{{Cite book/Spellbound/Campaign Guide|17}}</ref><ref name="RoT-p7">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|7}}</ref> and arcane experimenters,<ref name="MotM-p60">{{Cite book/Mysteries of the Moonsea|60}}</ref> before most of their numbers wholly dedicated themselves to creating a nation of [[undeath|undead]].<ref name="SCAG-p13">{{Cite book/Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide|13}}</ref>

==Organization==
For centuries, the leaders of the Red Wizards and in fact all of Thay was the [[Council of Zulkirs]].<ref name="FRCS2e.RTR-p27">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|27}}</ref> The ruling body comprised eight of the most powerful Red Wizards, each of whom represented one of the [[schools of magic]].<ref name="LoD-p56"/> Each of the zulkirs and their subordinates fomented intense rivalries with one another, leading to great feuds and near-crippling internal strife.<ref name="LoD-p60">{{Cite book/Lords of Darkness|60}}</ref>

In the years following the [[War of the Zulkirs]], the Red Wizards of Thay were led by the feared [[lich]] and supreme regent, [[Szass Tam]].<ref name="SCAG-p13"/><ref name="FRCG-p5">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|5}}</ref><ref name="DrM.366.RK">{{Cite dragon/366/Rose Keep}}</ref>

Since Szass Tam's takeover, the exiled Red Wizards have transformed enormously, becoming a loosely organized chain of merchant enclaves who specialized in procuring magical items.<ref name="DrM.366.RK"/>

The '''Tunlands''', also known as the '''Tun Plain'''<ref name="Po-p20" /> or '''Plains of Tun'''<ref name="Ud-p166" /> was a region in the [[Western Heartlands]]<ref name="Western Heartlands" group="note" /> roughly encompassing the area between the [[Sunset Mountains]] and the [[Storm Horns]] north of [[Proskur]].<ref name="Dr365-p45">{{Cite dragon/365/Backdrop: Cormyr|45}}</ref>

==Description==
The Tunlands consisted largely of grassy steppes and large swathes of wetlands,<ref name="Po-p20" /> with most of the ground marshy and wet.<ref name="FRCS.2e.CotR-p49">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|49}}</ref> The soil was largely considered too damp and marshy for farming, especially since the plains were often flooded by the [[Tun River]].<ref name="EE.TCM-p3">{{Cite book/Elminster's Ecologies/The Cormyrean Marshes|3}}</ref>

==Geography==
Bounded by the Sunset Mountains and [[Far Hills]] to the west, the [[Sunset Foothills]] to the north, the Storm Horns to the east, and Proskur to the south,<ref name="Dr365-p45" /> the Tunlands were often described as the vale of the [[Tun River]]<ref name="FRCS.2e.RtR-p20">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/Running the Realms|20}}</ref> which originated in the Farsea Marshes<ref name="FRCS.2e.CotR-p49" /> and flowed south<ref name="EE.TCM-p3" /> and then east to reach the [[Dragonmere]].<ref name="Po-p20" />

The earliest recorded event in the Tunlands (known at the time as the God's Theater) was the [[Battle of the Gods' Theater]] ''circa'' {{Yearlink|-10750}}, one of the largest and deadliest battles of the [[Crown Wars]]. ==Etymology==
The word "Faerûn" was a modified version of "Faerie", the name of the homeland of ancient [[elves]]. The term was coined by [[Durothil (prince)|Durothil]], prince of [[Tintageer]], [[Faerie]].<ref name="e:ioe-p97">{{Cite book/Evermeet: Island of Elves/MMP|97}}</ref>

==Geography==
{{main|Portal:Geography}}
The continent included terrain that was as varied as any other. Besides the exterior coastline to the west and south, the most dominant feature on the continent was the [[Sea of Fallen Stars]]. This was an irregular inland sea that kept the interior lands fertile, connected the west and east regions of Faerûn and served as a major trade route for many of the bordering nations.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /><ref name="FRCS-p142">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|142}}</ref>

Next in significance was the [[Shaar]], a broad region of grasslands in the south that,<ref name="FRCS-p196">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|196}}</ref> together with the Lake of Steam, separated the area around the inland sea from the coastal nations at the southern edge of the continent. To the east, Faerûn was bordered by a vast region of steppes from [[Kara-Tur]],<ref name="FRCS-p230">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting|230}}</ref> and in the north were massive glaciers ([[Pelvuria]] and [[Reghed]]) and tundra.<ref name="TFRA-p1" /> South of the continent, separated by the [[Great Sea]], was the sub-tropical land of [[Zakhara]].<ref name="FRCS-p230"/>

The continent included the following geographic regions.<ref group="note">The following division of Faerûn by compass directions was developed for organizational purposes on the Forgotten Realms Wiki, and is not closely reflected by descriptions in source text. In particular, terms like "the North" and "the South" in the sources can be much more wide-ranging in scope, and as ambiguous, than what is defined here.</ref>
