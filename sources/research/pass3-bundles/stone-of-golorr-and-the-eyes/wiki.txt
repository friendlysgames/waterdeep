The '''''Stone of Golorr''''' was an [[artifact]] that contained the consciousness and memories of an [[aboleth]] named [[Golorr]].<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

==Description==
The artifact was a small and glossy greenish-gray stone that could fit in the palm of a [[humanoid]]'s hand.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

==Powers==
The power of the ''Stone of Golorr'' could be called upon to access the aboleth's memory in a similar way as the ''[[legend lore]]'' spell. This power could be used up to three times, until the ''stone'' recharged at the next dawn.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

However, knowledge given by the aboleth was fleeting. Once a creature broke the attunement to the ''Stone of Golorr'', there was a chance that any knowledge acquired via the stone, and even possession of the stone itself, was forgotten by the user. This magically induced amnesia could sometimes be removed by a ''[[remove curse]]'' spell, but more powerful spells such as ''[[greater restoration]]'' had a better chance of success.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

As the trapped creature inside, the ''Stone of Golorr'' hungered for knowledge and did not like to stay attuned to the same individual for too long. It had an alien personality and saw itself as a [[god]]. If its user ignored the ''stone's'' demands, it could attempt to seize control of the user.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

==History==
The ''Stone of Golorr'' was in fact an aboleth that was transformed into an object. It could be freed if the stone was destroyed via an ''[[antipathy/sympathy]]'' spell cast with the appropriate parameters. If Golorr were freed in this way, it could go berserk and attack any nearby creatures.<ref name="WDH5e-p192" />

In the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]], the ''Stone of Golorr'' was used to store the knowledge of the location of the [[Vault of Dragons]], which contained a treasure that had been embezzled by [[Dagult Neverember]] shortly before he was ousted as the [[Open Lord]] of [[Waterdeep]].<ref name="WDH5e-p192" /> At some point the stone fell into the hands of [[Xanathar]]. Then in [[1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} Lord Dagult ordered many of his agents to obtain the stone from Xanathar's lair, with one [[Dalakhar]] ultimately being the one to do so while the crime lord was in the middle of a meeting with the [[Zhentarim]].<ref name="WDH5e-p5">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|5}}</ref>

{{Roll of years|1|Sunrise|nocat=true}}
''Example conversion between the years for [[1 DR]]''

Years (winters)<ref name="GTotR-p22" /> are referred to by names, each name consistent across the Realms. Each kingdom or city-state numbers years differently, usually to measure the reign of a dynasty or the current monarch, or since the founding of the country. The result is a hodgepodge of overlapping numbers that serve to confuse the ordinary person and frustrate the sage. The widespread differing year dates include the following:

==Methods of Reckoning==
===Dalereckoning (DR)===
Dalereckoning is taken from the {{YearlinkName|1}}, when the [[Standing Stone]] was raised by the [[elves]] of [[Cormanthyr]] and the [[human]]s of the [[Dalelands]]. Since this time, humans were permitted by the [[Elven Court]] to settle in the more open regions of the forests. In some texts, primarily those which do not have direct ties to Dalesfolk history, Dalereckoning is called Freeman's Reckoning (FR). The calendar is widely used in [[Faerûn]] but has not spread beyond its shores.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Cormyr Reckoning (CR)===
[[Cormyr]] Reckoning begins at the foundation of [[House Obarskyr]], the dynasty that still rules that land. The 25-year gap between Cormyr Reckoning and Dalereckoning has caused much of the confusion regarding elder days. Timelines and calendars of the period often use DR designators, but place the founding of Cormyr at 1 DR instead of [[26 DR]]. This is understandable, given that the two reckonings are from two nearby parties and spread by a third (the merchants of Sembia), but it causes learned sages to slam their heads violently against their desks trying to figure things out.<ref name="GTotR-p22">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22}}</ref><ref name="TGHotR-p4">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|4}}</ref>

===Tethyreckoning (TR)===
Tethyreckoning begins with Founding Year, Year 0 TR, in {{Yearlink|-212}}, when [[Darrom Ithal]] of [[Clan Ithal]] retook the city of [[Myratma]] from the [[Calimshan|Calishites]] in the [[Battle of the Purple Marches]] and was crowned king of [[Tethyr]] by the clans. This dating system was rarely used outside of official legal documents in Tethyr.<ref name="LoI1-T-p25">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Tethyr|25}}</ref>

===Northreckoning (NR)===
Used in the City of [[Waterdeep]], Northreckoning dates from the year [[Ahghairon]] became the first [[Lords of Waterdeep|Lord of Waterdeep]] ([[1032 DR]]). A more archaic system called Waterdeep Years (WY) dates from the supposed first use of Waterdeep as a trading post. This reckoning is now largely abandoned except in ancient texts.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Mulhorand Calendar (MC)===
One of the oldest calendars in use in the Realms, this ancient scheme of record-keeping dates from the founding of [[Skuld]], the City of Shadows, reputedly by a [[Mulhorand]]i god.<ref name="GTotR-p22" /><ref name="TGHotR-p4" />

===Netheril Year Calendar (NY)===
Used by the [[Netherese]], it places 0 NY on {{Yearlink|-3859}}, when several villages on the shore of the [[Narrow Sea]] combined for mutual protection, forming the [[Alliance of Seventon]], which later became [[Netheril]].<ref name="NEoM-p5">{{Cite book/Netheril: Empire of Magic/The Winds of Netheril|5}}</ref> The Netheril Year calendar was created by [[Nether]] to celebrate the union.<ref name="TGHotR-27">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|27}}</ref>

===Present Reckoning (PR)===
In an attempt to get a handle on the various number systems in use, a new type of reckoning has been implemented with the approval of such worthies as [[Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun]] of Waterdeep and [[Vangerdahast]] of Cormyr and carried abroad by the [[Harpers]]. All year-dates trace back to the [[Time of Troubles]] in the {{YearlinkName|1358}}, as year 0.<ref name="GTotR-p22-23">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2nd edition (revised)/A Grand Tour of the Realms|22-23}}</ref>

===Amnian Calendar===
The local calendar of [[Amn]] was based on the founding of [[Athkatla]] and the opening of its market on [[Shieldmeet]] in {{YearlinkName|100}}, which served as year 0. However, the Amnian calendar was largely irrelevant and rarely used outside of some official documents, with the vast majority of the population using Dalereckoning instead.<ref name="LoI2-A-p19">{{Cite book/Lands of Intrigue/Amn|19}}</ref>

===Impilturan Calendar===
The old calendar of [[Impiltur]] was the primary reckoning tradition in the so-called [[Demonlands]], including [[Damara]], the [[Great Dale]], Impiltur, [[Narfell]], and [[Vaasa]]. It began with the founding of the kingdom of Impiltur, which in Dalereckoning occurred in the {{YearlinkName|-74}}.<ref name="DunMag.177-p77">{{Cite dungeon/177/Realmslore: Vaasa|77}}</ref><ref name="tGHotR-p58">{{Cite book/The Grand History of the Realms|58}}</ref

The '''Vault of Dragons''' was a hidden underground location in [[Waterdeep]] built long ago by [[dwarves]].<ref name="W:DH-p5">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|5}}</ref> As of the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]], the Vault could only be accessed using the ''[[Stone of Golorr]]'', which revealed its location as well as the keys needed to enter.<ref name="W:DH-p94">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|94}}</ref><ref name="W:DH-p5" />

==Location==
The exact location of the Vault of Dragons was unknown.<ref group="note">Per the ''[[Waterdeep: Dragon Heist]]'' adventure, the Vault of Dragons has four potential [[canon]] locations, with a fifth location given in ''[[Idle Champions of the Forgotten Realms]]''.</ref> It was said perhaps to be in the [[Castle Ward]] beneath the [[Pink Flumph]] theater,<ref name="W:DH-p58">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|58}}</ref><ref name="W:DH-p62">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|62}}</ref> in the [[Sea Ward]] under either an old tower owned by [[Esvele Rosznar]] or a [[windmill]] in which the artist [[Kalain]] lived,<ref name="W:DH-p60">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|60}}</ref> in the [[City of the Dead]] below the [[Brandath|Brandath family]] mausoleum,<ref name="W:DH-p61">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|61}}</ref><ref name="W:DH-p70">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|70}}</ref> or in the [[North Ward]] beneath a villa.<ref name="IC">{{Cite game/Idle Champions of the Forgotten Realms}}</ref>

==Structure==
The Vault was located underground with walls of [[mortar]]ed stone and doors made of solid stone slabs with stone handles and hinges. The only entrance was a {{SIAdj|20|foot|long=yes|adj=high}} corridor ending at a massive gate of solid [[adamantine]] inscribed with [[Dwarvish]] [[rune]]s that read "THE THREE KEYS. BRING THEM FORTH". This gate had no obvious handles or hinges, and opened by sliding into the wall only when three specific keys were brought near it.<ref name="W:DH-p94" />

==Interior==
Immediately inside the gates of the Vault was a tall foyer extending north with no sources of light. It featured three {{SIAdj|60|foot|long=yes|adj=tall}} columns supporting three crumbling bridges far overhead. The ground floor had with twelve [[iron]] doors embossed with images of dwarf warriors flanking the room. Of these doors, only the two in the far northeast corner of the room were real, with the other ten being immovable distractions. Behind the real doors was a chamber containing a {{SIAdj|20|foot|long=yes}} magical [[fresco]] of a battle between dwarves and [[goblin]]s that could [[charm]] any creatures who viewed it, causing them to gaze upon it until they collapsed from exhaustion.<ref name="W:DH-p94" /><ref name="W:DH-p95">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|95}}</ref> Across from this fresco was an expertly-carved staircase leading up {{SI|70|feet|long=yes}} to a long, {{SIAdj|20|foot|long=yes|adj=high}} corridor known as the Hall of [[Moradin]], from which the bridges above the foyer could be accessed via three archways along the eastern wall. This hall contained three pillars resembling upside-down [[warhammer]]s, and the western wall was adorned with an aged [[mosaic]] of a dwarven [[blacksmith]] crafting dwarves out of metal and [[diamond]]s. This mosaic concealed a secret door leading to a dust-filled room containing [[copper]] urns filled with [[coin]]s, [[gem]]s, and other treasures.<ref name="W:DH-p95" /><ref name="W:DH-p96">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|96}}</ref>

Neverember was tall, broad-shouldered,<ref name="RT-p14-15">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|14-15}}</ref> and strikingly good-looking,<ref name="W:DH-p215">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|215}}</ref> with angular features,<ref name="T-ch8">{{Cite book/Timeless||8}}</ref> a mane of silver-flecked "pumpkin-brown" hair, and deep-set but shining dark eyes.<ref name="LCoN-p22">{{Cite book/Lost Crown of Neverwinter|22}}</ref><ref name="BST-p218">{{Cite book/Blackstaff Tower|218}}</ref> He was often called a "lion of a man" for both his appearance and bearing,<ref name="BST-p218" /> and was said to have the look of a strong ruler.<ref name="FRCG-p198">{{Cite book/Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide|198}}</ref><ref name="RT-p14-15">{{Cite book/The Rise of Tiamat|14-15}}</ref>

By the end of his tenure as Open Lord, Neverember sported a short but thick beard<ref name="RT-p14-15"/><ref name="DDEK:DC">{{Cite book/Dungeons & Dragons Essentials Kit/Divine Contention}}</ref> that had gone grey.<ref name="DDEK:DC" /><ref name="AM-ch7">{{Cite book/Archmage/MMP (2016)||7}}</ref>

=== Masked Lords ===
The Lords of Waterdeep were only seen in public when sitting in the Lord's Court or when appearing every four years at [[Shieldmeet]] Council when the Lord's Rule is reaffirmed.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The identities of the Lords, other than the Open Lord, were not supposed to be known to the public, although they were known to each other.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" /> The intent of this was to ensure that the Lords were free of coercion and could be drawn from any social strata within the city.<ref name="CoS.CG-p67" /> While the masked Lords were considered to be equal to the Open Lord in authority, some executive authority was reserved for the Open Lord, including the vetting and selection of new Lords,<ref name="CS.W-p52" /> the naming of the next Open Lord,<ref>{{Cite book/City of Splendors (boxed set)/Campaign Guide|31}}</ref> and determining the needed number of Lords.<ref name="CoS.CG-p68" />

'''Dalakhar''' was a [[thief]] and spy in [[Waterdeep]] working for Lord [[Dagult Neverember]] in the late 15{{th}} century [[DR]].<ref name="WDH5e-p11">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|11}}</ref>

==History==
In [[1492 DR]],{{WDH-time}} Dalakhar was spying upon [[Renaer Neverember]], the estranged son of his master.<ref name="WDH5e-p49">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|49}}</ref> At one point, however, Lord Dagult ordered many of his agents, including Dalakhar, to obtain the ''[[stone of Golorr]]'' from the lair of the [[Xanathar]]. The rock gnome managed to steal the stone, ruining a meeting between Xanathar and the [[Zhentarim]].<ref name="WDH5e-p5">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|5}}</ref>

Later, Dalakhar was targeted by agents of the Zhentarim, the [[Xanathar's Thieves' Guild]], [[Bregan D'aerthe]], and others, making it impossible for him to get out of Waterdeep as planned.<ref name="WDH5e-p43-44" /> For the time being, Dalakhar decided to entrust the stone to some adventurers who had saved Renaer.<ref name="WDH5e-p46" /> However, Dalakhar was ambushed by some Zhentarim led by [[Urstul Floxin]] and hit by a ''[[fireball]]'' launched by the [[nimblewright]] of [[Gralhund|House Gralhund]], killing the rock gnome along with ten more people.<ref name="WDH5e-p43-44">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|43-44}}</ref>

==Personality==
Dalakhar was very loyal to his master, believing Dagult to be the rightful [[Open Lord]] of Waterdeep.<ref name="WDH5e-p46">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|46}}</ref>

==Chronology==
The adventure takes place after the events described in ''[[Hoard of the Dragon Queen]]'', ''[[The Rise of Tiamat]]'', ''[[Princes of the Apocalypse]]'',<ref name="WDH5e-p38-39">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|38-39}}</ref> and ''[[Tomb of Annihilation]]'',<ref name="WDH5e-p5">{{Cite book/Waterdeep: Dragon Heist|5}}</ref> but does not directly depend on them. It is not set in a particular year, but is assumed to be the [[1492 DR|Year of Three Ships Sailing, 1492 DR]] (see note).

'''''Idle Champions of the Forgotten Realms''''' is a free-to-play [[Wikipedia:Incremental game|idle game]] developed by [[Wikipedia:Codename Entertainment|Codename Entertainment]]. It was released for early access on September 7, 2017.

==Description==
The core gameplay of ''Idle Champions'' revolves around the player selecting "champions" (such as [[Bruenor Battlehammer|Bruenor]], [[Minsc]], and [[Celeste]], among others) to defeat waves of increasingly difficult monsters, thereby accumulating gold. This gold is used to upgrade the player's champions, or unlock new ones. Each individual champion has its own set of unique abilities that empower either themselves or other champions. The player needs to place these champions in a "formation" that facilitates the overall strength of the party to ensure progress can be made against higher-level waves of monsters. As players complete objectives, or reset their current adventure, they accumulate "Favor" with a specific deity, which increases the amount of gold they find in future sessions. Furthermore, players can collect character-specific equipment that permanently boost their champions further, either as random drops, or through purchasing "chests" filled with random items. Equipment comes in four quality tiers, where "Epic" is the highest. Furthermore, equipment can be further boosted by being made "Shiny" (a rare chance at permanent improvement) or "Golden" (an even bigger improvement but generally limited to real-world currency purchases). Finally, "Epic" equipment can be forged into "Legendary" quality, which does not empower the item itself, but rather provides an additional positive effect for the party.

The game can be automated to a degree using the "Modron Automation" system which allows the game to handle all necessary input to repeatedly play itself, in four separate instances, for a period of up to 7 days.

The game is segmented into several "campaigns", each with its own unique Favor, ensuring Favor progress does not carry over between campaigns. Each campaign has a unique formation pattern, forcing players to rethink how to arrange their champions. Some of the campaigns are permanent, while others are event-based and only available for a short time. Event campaigns reward unique champions that are only available to unlock during their respective events. Permanent campaigns are usually adaptations of a tabletop adventure, such as ''[[Tomb of Annihilation]]''. All the campaigns are further divided into "adventures", which are narrative frames for the core gameplay, for example recounting how the champions help rescue a farmer's daughter who has been kidnapped by [[werewolf|werewolves]]. Most adventures also have additional challenge modes, unlocked after meeting various criteria. These challenge modes present the player with new obstacles to overcome, for example through limiting the champions available to choose from. The "Patron" system allows for these challenges to be replayed with even more limitations imposed on the player. For example, selecting the [[Strahd von Zarovich|Strahd]] patron means players are restricted to only using champions with a high intelligence score.

The "end game" of ''Idle Champions'' is the "Trials of Mount Tiamat", a cooperative campaign where five players undertake missions to defeat [[Tiamat]]. Over the course of a week, players must succeed in daily missions to help reach their goal. Success yields "Scales of Tiamat", which is a currency used to forge "Legendary" equipment.

'''''Dungeons & Dragons Dice Masters: Adventures in Waterdeep''''' is a  24-card and 16-dice team pack for a dice-building game set in ''[[Forgotten Realms]]''. It utilizes WizKids Games' proprietary Dice Building Game platform in which two players take the role of warlords, collect and assemble their "party" of adventurer dice and battle in head-to-head gameplay.

{{Quote|''The Dungeons & Dragons Dice Masters: Adventures in Waterdeep Team Packfeatures the most beloved characters from Waterdeep, including Brawlwin Chainminer, the Black Viper, Jarlaxle, Durnan, and several characters making their very first appearances in Dice Masters!''}}

==Gameplay==
Each turn, players roll the dice to see what resources they have available, buy dice, send their party members into the field, and then strike at the enemy warlord. There are multiple cards available for each type of die so players get to choose which one to use. This lets players specialize dice to suit play style.
