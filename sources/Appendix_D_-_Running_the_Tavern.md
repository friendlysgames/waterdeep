# Appendix D: Running the Tavern

<aside class="quote-lg float-right">
  <p><q>A tavern is not a building. It is the sum of every conversation that has ever happened inside it, and every conversation that will. What you are doing, in reopening this place, is reopening all of those. I hope you're ready.</q></p>
  <p class="quote-author">—Broxley Fairkettle, <em>Fellowship of Innkeepers</em></p>
</aside>

---

Trollskull Tavern is more than a base of operations. It is the characters' home in Waterdeep — a place they have rebuilt with their own coin, their own labor, and (if they were wise) the reluctant blessing of a poltergeist bartender who has been waiting for new owners since 1479 DR. The systems in this appendix give that investment mechanical and narrative weight.

This appendix covers:

- **Operating Costs** — staff wages, guild fees, and supply costs, and how they interact.
- **The Reputation Track** — how the tavern's Fame rises, falls, and shapes weekly income.
- **Tavern Time** — a structured procedure for running a night at the bar.
- **Notable Patrons** — expanded profiles for regular guests.
- **Events** — random and scripted incidents that bring the common room to life.
- **Business Threats** — Faction Response Teams and how each faction interacts with the tavern as the Grand Game escalates.

---

## Part 1: Operating Costs

Once the doors are open, Trollskull Tavern incurs three categories of recurring expense every tenday. Two of these — guild fees and staff wages — form the base **60 gp/tenday** figure established in Act II. The third, supply costs, sits on top of that figure and scales with how busy the tavern is and what quality of product the characters choose to stock.

Characters who fall behind on any category of expenses accumulate **Unpaid Debt**. Each 1 gp of unpaid debt subtracts 1 from the next Revenue Roll (see Part 3).

---

### Category 1: Guild Fees (Fixed)

Guild fees are non-negotiable in the ordinary course of business. The guilds of Waterdeep have legal standing, collective muscle, and long institutional memories. Missing a payment results in a formal notice the first time, a supply disruption the second, and a license review the third.

The combined guild fee of **10 gp per tenday** covers the following obligations:

| Guild | Obligation | Tenday Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Fellowship of Innkeepers | Operating license; periodic inspection compliance | 3 gp |
| Launderers' Guild | Linen service for rooms and common area | 2 gp |
| Dungsweepers' Guild | Street cleanup contract (exterior maintenance) | 1 gp |
| Loyal Order of Street Laborers | General exterior upkeep | 1 gp |
| Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors | Fire-suppression ward maintenance | 2 gp |
| Stablemasters' and Farriers' Guild | Courtyard access for guests' mounts | 1 gp |
| **Total Guild Fees** | | **10 gp/tenday** |

### Category 2: Staff Wages (Variable)

Staff wages form the largest part of the base **50 gp/tenday** operational expense and are the category most directly within the characters' control. A functioning tavern requires at minimum one bartender, two servers, one cook, and a part-time cleaner. The characters may fill any of these roles themselves, but any role a character personally covers means they are unavailable for adventuring, investigation, or faction missions that tenday.

#### Minimum Staff (Standard Wages)

| Role | Wage (per tenday) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bartender | 10 gp | Lif serves this role for free if appeased |
| Server (×2) | 5 gp each | |
| Cook | 8 gp | Standard Bakers' Guild-referred cook |
| Cleaner | 4 gp | Lif handles some cleaning; a full cleaner is still recommended |
| General maintenance | 8 gp | Minor repairs, restocking, errands |
| **Standard Staff Total** | **40 gp/tenday** | **(30 gp/tenday if Lif tends bar)** |

The remaining 10 gp of the 50 gp staff/maintenance budget covers incidentals: broken glassware, a replaced keg tap, a tip slipped to the Dungsweepers' man who moves faster when he's motivated.

When the characters decide to hire for any position, word spreads through the guilds and the alley within a day. The following candidates turn up for each role. The DM should choose which candidates appear based on what the campaign needs, or roll 1d4 among options 1–4, with option 5 always appearing last if the position hasn't been filled after three days.

#### Lif the Bartender

If Lif (see Act II) has been appeased, he becomes the tavern's de facto bartender at no cost. He pours drinks with unsettling precision, keeps the bar spotless without instruction, and has memorized every regular's order after a single visit. He communicates through gesture, chalk on a small slate, and occasional atmospheric emphasis. He does not require wages, a bed, or meals — though he will occasionally leave a fresh drink at a specific table for a specific guest, with no explanation offered.

<aside class="notable">
    <h4>Lif's Signature Drink: The Half-Elf</h4>
    <p>Lif invented this drink in life and has continued refining it in death. A base spirit (varying nightly, chosen by Lif without consultation) with ale floated in a perfect layer on top. The two never mix, regardless of how much the glass is jostled — an effect no living bartender has successfully replicated. Regulars come specifically for it. The Half-Elf adds <strong>+2 to the Revenue Roll</strong> for any tenday in which Lif is actively tending bar and this drink is on the menu.</p>
</aside>

If Lif has *not* been appeased, or the characters want a living bartender regardless, five candidates present themselves.

##### Bartender Candidates (10 gp/tenday)

**Candidate 1 — Perella Dunwhistle**
*NG female rock gnome, Commoner. Genuinely excellent. Hire her.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>She is barely four feet tall and arrives carrying a folded leather apron over one arm and a small, extremely organized box of bartending tools over the other — a muddler, a straining cloth, a set of calibrated measuring spoons, all nestled in velvet-lined slots. She has warm brown skin, silver-streaked dark hair pinned back severely, and clever eyes that move across the taproom and inventory everything in it before she has said a single word. "The taps are misaligned," she says, by way of greeting. "Whoever installed them poured the secondary off the same line as the primary. You've been losing half a pint to foam on every pull. I can fix that this afternoon, if you'd like to see how I work before you make a decision."</p>
</div>

Perella worked the bar at the Yawning Portal for eleven years until Durnan replaced her with a cheaper hire during a lean season. She holds no grudge against Durnan — she understands the math — but she hasn't been back since. She is the best bartender the characters are likely to find in the North Ward. She knows every regular type on sight, spots watered drinks by smell, and maintains a mental ledger of every tab without writing anything down.

**Hidden Quality:** She and Durnan are on civil but cool terms. If she sees him at the bar, she will serve him without comment and charge him double the going rate. He will pay it without complaint. Neither will explain this.

**Faction Note:** No faction affiliation. Durnan's network gives her street-level information she occasionally shares with employers she respects — not intelligence, just Waterdeep gossip with good signal-to-noise ratio.

**Wage:** 12 gp/tenday. She knows she is the best option who will walk through the door and prices herself accordingly. Worth it.

**Mechanical Effect:** +3 to Revenue Roll per tenday. If Lif is also present, they develop a strange working rapport — Lif handles the ale side, Perella handles spirits, and the efficiency of this arrangement adds an additional +1 (total +4 with both).

**Candidate 2 — Orryn Tetch**
*CN male half-elf, Commoner. Charming, unreliable, probably stealing.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He is tall, languid, and beautiful in the specific way that makes people forgive things they shouldn't. Copper-toned skin, pale green eyes, a white linen shirt open at the collar. He sets his elbows on the bar, leans forward, and smiles at whoever is interviewing him as if they are the most interesting person he has encountered in months. "I've worked twelve bars in six cities," he says. "I'm very good at this. The reason I've worked twelve bars in six cities is complicated, and I'm happy to explain if you'd like, but the short version is: people keep offering me more interesting opportunities, and I keep taking them." He shrugs pleasantly. "I'm not offering you loyalty. I'm offering you competence, and as long as I'm here, I'll be excellent at this."</p>
</div>

Orryn is telling the truth on all counts. He is excellent at his job. He is also skimming approximately 2 sp per shift from the till — not enough to notice on any given night, but it adds up. He has no particular malice; it's simply habit. A DC 15 Investigation check of the cash records after a tenday catches the discrepancy.

**Hidden Quality:** Orryn is genuinely fond of the characters if they treat him well. If the characters catch him stealing and give him a choice between dismissal and a formal warning with consequences, he stops stealing entirely — not from fear, but because someone treating him as worth correcting rather than discarding is novel enough to affect him. From that point, he becomes fiercely protective of the till and will flag any other staff irregularities unprompted. He is, in this one regard, a reformed man.

**Faction Note:** No faction affiliation.

**Wage:** 10 gp/tenday (the standard rate; he does not negotiate).

**Mechanical Effect:** +1 to Revenue Roll per tenday. −2 gp from actual take each tenday (the skimming) until caught. After the warning, the −2 gp loss stops and he provides a passive +1 to Revenue Roll from genuine engagement with the work.

**Candidate 3 — Gosta Ironhelm**
*LN male shield dwarf, Commoner. Competent, joyless, and a City Watch informant.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He is broad, grey-bearded, and appears to have decided some years ago that smiling was not worth the effort. He wears a clean but unornamented tunic and carries a copy of the Fellowship of Innkeepers' standard service regulations, which he sets on the bar between you without explanation. "I have seventeen years of bar service," he says. "I do not drop things. I do not sample the product. I do not engage in extended conversation with patrons. I close on time. I open on time. The books will balance." He pauses. "I will need Tenthdays off for Watch liaison obligations. This is not negotiable."</p>
</div>

Gosta moonlights as an informal Watch informant — not a spy exactly, more a civic-minded citizen who has been filing regular reports on "establishments of interest" for the better part of a decade. He considers this entirely compatible with his employment. He doesn't report anything actionable; he reports atmospherics — who comes in, how often, whether the crowd skews toward any particular guild or faction. The Watch values him precisely because he is boring and consistent.

**Hidden Quality:** A DC 14 Insight check during the interview reveals he is withholding something about his other obligations. Asking him directly causes him to confirm, without shame, that he has "a civic reporting arrangement." He will not lie about it if asked directly; he simply will not volunteer it.

**Faction Note:** Informal City Watch informant. Not a planted spy — this is his own civic arrangement, predating any party involvement. Lords' Alliance party members who learn of this may find him unexpectedly useful: his reports are factual, consistent, and reach the right desks.

**Wage:** 10 gp/tenday. He does not negotiate. The wage is in the Fellowship regulations and he respects regulations.

**Mechanical Effect:** No bonus to Revenue Roll. Immunity to Watch harassment events (they already know what's happening here). If the characters are engaged in anything illegal, Gosta is a liability — but he will give one warning before filing a report, out of professional courtesy.

**Candidate 4 — Senna Vael**
*NE female tiefling, Commoner. A Zhentarim plant.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>She has deep red skin, small curved horns that she's painted gold at the tips, and a tail she keeps curled neatly around one ankle while seated. She smiles easily and makes eye contact without effort — the practiced warmth of someone who has learned that people are nervous around tieflings and has decided to do the work of putting them at ease rather than resenting the labor. "I've been working bars in the Dock Ward for three years," she says. "I'm good with difficult people. I'm fast. I'm honest about my references — two of my three employers have moved on, but the third will speak well of me." She slides a folded letter across the bar. The seal is a wine glass. "I want to work somewhere with a bit more... character."</p>
</div>

The letter is genuine and the reference will check out. Senna is, in fact, an excellent bartender. She is also reporting to Davil Starsong of the Zhentarim Doom Raiders — specifically on who visits the tavern, what they discuss within earshot of the bar, and whether the characters show any signs of significant income or valuable information. She is not aggressive; if the characters are not faction-active, she simply files dull reports and enjoys the work. If they become significant players in the Grand Game, her reports become consequential.

**Hidden Quality:** DC 17 Insight to detect during interview — she is too polished. If the characters investigate her Dock Ward references, a DC 13 Investigation check turns up a connection to a Zhentarim front business. Confronted with evidence, she does not deny it, and will offer to become a double agent for the characters in exchange for continued employment — which is itself a Zhentarim instruction she's been given in case of discovery.

**Faction Note:** Zhentarim (Doom Raiders) plant, reporting to Davil Starsong. If the party are Doom Raiders themselves, Davil will mention — casually, over drinks, when they've been open for a tenday — that he "took the liberty of placing someone helpful." He frames it as a courtesy. It is also a test of whether they object. Doom Raiders party members who accept this and keep her employed gain +1 Renown; those who dismiss her immediately lose no standing but Davil notes the preference.

**Wage:** 10 gp/tenday.

**Mechanical Effect:** +2 to Revenue Roll per tenday (she is legitimately skilled). If employed for more than two tendays, Davil Starsong has a detailed picture of the tavern's patron traffic. This may or may not matter, depending on how deep the characters are in the Grand Game.

**Candidate 5 — Tobrin Ashvale**
*NE male human, Commoner. A Xanathar Guild information plant — not a fighter, just a watcher.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He is forgettable in the precise way that suggests effort has gone into it: medium height, medium build, brown hair cut unremarkably, clothes that are clean but not distinctive. He smiles when he is supposed to smile and nods when he is supposed to nod. His references are impeccable — three separate establishments in the Castle Ward, all of which will confirm he worked there. "I heard you were hiring," he says. "I like the North Ward. Quiet. I'm looking for something steady." He glances around the taproom with the specific casual interest of someone memorizing a room.</p>
</div>

Tobrin has tended bar before — he is not Pix; he can actually do the job. He is here because the Xanathar Guild places low-level watchers in North Ward establishments when those establishments become points of interest. He has no combat mission. His role is to report on who visits, what they discuss within earshot of the bar, and whether the characters appear to be holding or moving any Golorr artifacts. He files reports through a contact in the Dock Ward.

**Hidden Quality:** DC 14 Insight detects that his professional confidence is real but his interest in the job is not — there is a watchful quality behind his eyes that does not match the casual manner. DC 16 Investigation of his Castle Ward references reveals that one establishment has been under Xanathar Guild protection for two years. There is no version of this the characters can turn to their advantage; the Guild placed him, and the Guild does not share.

**Faction Note:** Xanathar Guild — an adversary faction, not available to player characters. Tobrin is a Guild watcher, not a Guild ally. There is no version of this where Tobrin can be turned to the party's benefit; he is simply an ear in the taproom. The DC 12 Insight or DC 16 Investigation checks are the party's only warning before the Guild's attention becomes structural.

**Wage:** 10 gp/tenday.

**Mechanical Effect:** Functionally competent bartender; no Revenue Roll bonus or penalty. After two tendays of employment, the Xanathar Guild has a detailed profile of the tavern's regular patron traffic and the party's schedule. If the characters discover his affiliation and dismiss him, N'arl Xibrindas dispatches a replacement within three tendays — this time not applying for a bar position.

##### Server Candidates (5 gp/tenday each; hire two)

**Candidate 1 — Kassia Brenn**
*NG female human, Commoner. Hardworking, quick, exactly what you want.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>She arrives five minutes early, which you notice because she is standing outside the door when you open it. She is perhaps twenty, with a broad, freckled face and the posture of someone who has been carrying heavy trays since she was old enough to hold one — straight-backed and balanced. She does not sit during the interview but stands at a slight angle to the nearest table, as if already gauging the distance to the next. "I've been serving at Felzoun's Folly since I was sixteen," she says. "I know the North Ward crowd. I'm fast, I don't spill, I remember orders, and I know when to leave a table alone. I'd like to work somewhere that isn't Felzoun's, because Felzoun is cheap and loud and I'm neither."</p>

Kassia is excellent and has no hidden agenda. She will be loyal to the tavern in the specific way that good service staff are loyal — the place becomes part of her professional identity. She will also, within a tenday, identify which regulars tip well, which ones are trouble, and which ones are something more complicated, and she will quietly tell the characters what she's noticed if they ask.

**Faction Note:** No affiliation. After one tenday she will have noticed which patrons are regulars, which are unusual, and which watch exits — and she will tell the characters if they ask. She is not an intelligence asset; she is simply observant and helpful.

**Wage:** 5 gp/tenday.

**Mechanical Effect:** +1 to Revenue Roll per tenday she is employed.

**Candidate 2 — Pell Ashford**
*CG male halfling, Commoner. Enthusiastic, catastrophically clumsy, beloved by customers.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He is a stout halfling with curly red hair and the kind of enormous grin that looks like it might have its own structural requirements. He knocks over the chair he was about to sit in, catches it before it falls, sets it right, and sits down with enormous dignity. "I've been told I'm enthusiastic," he says. "Which I am. **Wage:** 4 gp/tenday. He genuinely underbids because he is excited about the job.

**Mechanical Effect:** +2 to Fame per tenday from customer goodwill. −1d6 gp per tenday in breakage. Net positive at most Fame levels — he is worth it.

**Candidate 3 — Ivrala Sorn**
*LE female human, Commoner. A Cassalanter household operative — here to listen, not to serve.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>She arrives on time, which is unremarkable, but she arrives having already observed the room through the window before she knocked, which is not. She is composed in the way of someone trained to be unobtrusive — not invisible, just easy to overlook. Perhaps forty, dark hair with silver at the temples, dressed practically but well. "I have household service experience," she says. "Neverember, Adarbrent, and most recently the Cassalanter family, four years. I can manage a floor, I know how to read a room, and I am extremely discreet." She meets your eyes with the directness of someone who has spent years making sure the people they work for feel looked after. "I would like something steady."</p>
</div>

Ivrala is a Cassalanter social operative: not a poisoner, not a fighter, but a meticulous observer trained to gather intelligence in domestic settings where staff are invisible. She is genuinely good at the work of serving — reading a room, managing difficult tables, anticipating what a patron needs before they ask. The Cassalanters assigned her to the tavern because the floor is where the real conversations happen, and household staff hear everything. She reports in writing, monthly, to Willifort Crowelle, through a drop at a tobacconist on the High Road.

She has no aggressive brief. She is building a picture: who visits, what they discuss, what the characters value, who they trust. If the Cassalanters determine the party is an asset, Ivrala's reports shape how Lady Ammalia approaches them in Act IV. If the party becomes a threat, the reports inform something else.

**Hidden Quality:** She is almost impossible to catch. DC 15 Insight during the interview detects that her attention to the room is architectural — she clocked the exits, the stairs, and the private alcove in the first thirty seconds. DC 17 Investigation of her Cassalanter reference reveals that household records list her departure as "temporary reassignment," not completion of service. Willifort Crowelle appears on this thread if the party is already investigating the Cassalanters. Sister Valdra (Bouncer candidate 4) has seen her at Cassalanter social functions and will recognise her with a DC 13 Perception check if both are in the tavern at the same time.

**Faction Note:** Cassalanter household — adversary faction. Her placement is intelligence-only. Every tenday she is employed, the Cassalanters receive one specific, accurate piece of intelligence about the party's relationships, current concerns, or plans. This intelligence is not immediately threatening; it becomes so when the Cassalanter arc enters its active phase and the family knows exactly which pressure points to press.

**Wage:** 5 gp/tenday. She asks for the standard rate. She would work for less. She is not here for the money.

**Combination Note:** If Ivrala is employed at the same time as Rella Asp (Master Cook candidate), the Cassalanters have both a kitchen-level and a floor-level view of the tavern. Their combined intelligence reports give Lord Victoro a detailed operational picture of the party's social world. This is not immediately actionable by the DM, but it should be tracked — by the time the party encounters the Cassalanters directly, the family will seem to know them implausibly well.

**Mechanical Effect:** +1 to Revenue Roll per tenday from her service quality. No Fame bonus — she is deliberately unremarkable. Intelligence reports filed as described above.

**Candidate 4 — Darvish**
*LG male human, Commoner. A former City Watch officer, recovering from injury, quietly excellent.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He moves with a slight hitch in his left leg — an old fracture, badly healed — and carries himself with the straight-backed habit of a man who spent years in uniform. He is perhaps forty, with a broad jaw and grey-threaded dark hair cropped close. He does not apologize for the limp when he notices you notice it. **Faction Note:** Formally retired City Watch. Lords' Alliance characters who know Jalester Silvermane will find that Darvish and Jalester have a professional acquaintance; Jalester will speak well of him if asked directly.

**Wage:** 6 gp/tenday. He is slightly underpaid for his value and does not complain about this.

**Combination Note:** If Darvish and Kassia Brenn are both employed, they work well together — Darvish handles the room-read, Kassia handles the fast service. Combined effect: +1 additional to Revenue Roll on Event nights specifically (their coordination reduces response time to any incident).

**Mechanical Effect:** +1 to Revenue Roll. When a Faction Response Team (Xanathar Guild specifically) causes a tavern Event, the party receives advance warning the round before it escalates.

**Candidate 5 — the Twins: Nessa and Corvin Vayle**
*CN female/male humans, Commoner. They come as a pair. One is a Harper courier.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">

He will not name the Harpers unless the characters already have Harper connections.

If Harper-affiliated characters hire them, Mirt considers the tavern a confirmed waypoint and will occasionally route sensitive documents through it. This is an asset and a liability.

**Wage:** 10 gp/tenday for both (5 gp each). They will not negotiate separately.

**Combination Note:** If the Vayle Twins are employed alongside Kassia Brenn (as a third server), the tavern runs at exceptional efficiency during busy nights: nightly covers increase by approximately 20% on Respected-tier and above, reflected as +5 to Revenue Roll on any tenday when all three are working full shifts.

**Mechanical Effect:** Counts as two servers for wage purposes. +2 to Revenue Roll from efficiency. If the characters are Harper-affiliated, this connection unlocks a minor information channel — Corvin can carry messages for them at no cost and will occasionally bring back relevant street intelligence.

##### Cook Candidates (8 gp/tenday)

**Candidate 3 — Whisper**
*N female kenku, Commoner. Copies recipes perfectly. Cannot invent anything new.*

<div class="fvtt narrative">

**Mechanical Effect:** +1 to Revenue Roll per tenday. If the characters introduce her to a new dish (e.g., from a patron's suggestion), she adds it to permanent rotation at no additional cost. Cannot cover for kitchen Events without explicit player instructions.

### Category 3: Supply Costs (Variable, Separate from Base Expenses)

Supply costs are not included in the 60 gp/tenday base figure. They represent the raw materials the tavern actually sells — ale, wine, spirits, bread, meat, and kitchen staples. Supply costs scale with volume, quality, and the tavern's current Fame level, and they are paid against revenue rather than as a flat overhead.

Trollskull's taproom is approximately 210 square feet — comfortably thirty-five seats, forty at a pinch, standing room aside. It is a medium-sized neighborhood tavern, not a grand hall. Its supply costs reflect this: peak nights fill the room once, maybe twice if there's fast table turnover. The numbers below assume ten nights of trading per tenday.

At the end of each tenday, before calculating profit, the characters pay supply costs based on how busy the tavern has been. Use the following table, cross-referenced with the tavern's current Fame tier:

| Fame Tier | Fame Score | Estimated Nightly Covers | Supply Cost per Tenday | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Condemned | −50 to −20 | 3–8 | 8–15 gp | A handful of the desperate and the unaware; stock only basics |
| Struggling | −19 to −1 | 8–18 | 15–30 gp | Local foot traffic only; no premium stock |
| Known | 0–30 | 18–35 | 30–50 gp | Neighbourhood establishment running near capacity on good nights |
| Respected | 31–80 | 30–40 | 48–65 gp | Consistently full; two seatings on busy nights; premium options viable |
| Fashionable | 81–150 | 35–40 | 62–80 gp | At or near capacity most nights; Hammond Kraddoc's featured spirits worth stocking |
| Renowned | 151–350 | 35–40 (+ overflow) | 75–95 gp | Turning people away on peak nights; kitchen needs second cook; consider overflow seating upstairs |
| Institution | 351+ | Fully booked nightly | 90–110 gp | Reservation system; supplier agreements should be locked in; quality is the only constraint |

Supply costs are paid out of gross revenue each tenday before net profit is calculated — they are part of the cost side of the ledger, not included in the Revenue Roll result. The supply cost figure matters for two mechanical reasons:

1. **Cash Flow:** The characters must have supply costs available at the *start* of each tenday, before trading begins. A tavern that cannot pay its suppliers operates at reduced capacity, imposing −15 to the Revenue Roll that tenday.
2. **Quality Investment:** If the characters spend 20% more than the baseline supply cost for their Fame tier on premium stock (e.g., Hammond Kraddoc's featured spirits, a special Bakers' Guild pastry selection), they gain +5 to the Revenue Roll that tenday and +1 to Fame at the end of the tenday if they do so for three consecutive tendays.

### Repaying Debts

Not all faction assistance is free. Mirt's loan, Istrid Horn's loan, and any personal debt to Renaer represent real financial obligations that the party carries into the operating life of the tavern. These should be tracked and repaid from the tavern's net revenue each tenday — not as an afterthought, but as a line item in the books.

**Loan Repayments come off the bottom line.** After calculating net profit (gross revenue − fixed costs − supply costs − incidentals), deduct the tenday's loan repayment before the party takes anything from the till. This keeps the math honest: the party sees their gross revenue, watches costs come off, and then watches the debt come off. What's left is actually theirs.

The following table covers the two coin loans the party may carry:

| Lender | Loan Amount | Repayment Type | Tenday Repayment | Payoff Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Istrid Horn** *(Doom Raiders)* | Up to 400 gp | Coin, no interest | Party's choice — minimum 20 gp/tenday recommended | 20 tendays at minimum; faster is better, Istrid notices |
| **Mirt the Moneylender** *(Harpers)* | Up to 500 gp | Favor, not coin | No scheduled repayment — Mirt calls it in when useful | Indefinite; the debt hangs until he needs something |
| **Renaer Neverember** | Up to 200 gp | Coin, no interest, "when convenient" | Party's choice; Renaer will never ask | Technically indefinite; morally, they should pay it |

## Part 2: The Reputation Track (Fame)

Fame represents Trollskull Tavern's standing in Waterdeep's social geography — how well-known it is, how fashionable, how safe, and how reliably good its ale is. Fame is tracked as a numerical score. It starts at **0** and can range from **−50** (condemned and actively avoided) to **351+** (a Waterdhavian institution).

Fame is checked at the end of each tenday and modifies the Revenue Roll. It is not a currency and cannot be spent; it is a condition that affects income, NPC behavior, and narrative events.

        <h4>Design Note: Why Fame Instead of Straight Advertising Spend?</h4>
        <p>The original <em>Dragon Heist</em> system allows characters to spend 1 gp/tenday on promotion and add 1 to the business roll — functional but colorless. The Fame track replaces this with a system that reflects the lived reputation of the tavern in its neighborhood. Famous taverns attract famous patrons; famous patrons cause famous events; famous events make the tavern more famous. It also means that faction incidents, staff choices, and what happens inside the common room on any given night have mechanical teeth beyond individual bad sessions.</p>
    </article>
</div>

### Starting Fame

When the Grand Opening occurs, roll 1d6 and add the result to any of the following bonuses the party has earned through preparation:

| Preparation Action | Fame Bonus |
|---|---|
| Lif is appeased and tending bar | +2 |
| Tavern has been promoted (flyers, word of mouth via NPCs) | +1 per PC-tenday spent (max +3) |
| A notable NPC was personally invited and attended | +1 per notable NPC (max +3) |
| Grand Opening featured live music | +1 |
| Grand Opening featured a special menu item | +1 |
| A public disruption or violent incident occurred during the Grand Opening | −3 |
| Lif caused a violent scene (was not appeased) | −2 |

The Grand Opening Fame total becomes the tavern's starting Fame score.

### Fame Thresholds

A well-staffed tavern with Lif tending bar, a skilled cook, a house bard, and a competent server corps can accumulate 3–5 Fame per tenday from staff contributions alone, before any events, patron praise, or active promotion. The early tiers reflect this natural momentum — a well-run tavern finds its feet quickly. The higher tiers slow dramatically: passive staff gains become a smaller proportion of the gap, and reaching Renowned and Institution requires the party to actively shape the tavern's story rather than simply running good shifts.

Staff-driven Fame gains reflect sustained employment of those staff members. They do not stack with single-night event bonuses, but they accumulate tenday over tenday.

| Fame Score | Status | Revenue Roll Modifier | Patron Reach | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| −50 to −20 | **Condemned** | Disadvantage | No one comes willingly | Guild representatives begin proceedings to revoke the license. City Watch checks in monthly. No positive staff contributions offset this — the problem is structural. |
| −19 to −1 | **Struggling** | −10 | Locals only; some avoid it | Regulars thin out. Patron Table rolls at −2. |
| 0–30 | **Known** | — | Neighbourhood draw | A functioning local establishment. Staff contributions start accumulating here. A well-staffed tavern reaches this ceiling in 5–10 tendays of good operation. |
| 31–80 | **Respected** | +5 | Ward-wide draw | Notable patrons arrive without prompting; roll d8 (not d6) for significant patron count. Sustained staff contributions push the tavern through this range over 10–15 tendays. |
| 81–150 | **Fashionable** | +10 | City-wide draw | Faction contacts begin using the tavern for private meetings. The occasional Masked Lord passes through. Reaching this tier requires deliberate investment — premium stock, hosted events, or a string of exceptional patron interactions — on top of good staffing. |
| 151–350 | **Renowned** | +15 | City-wide and beyond | The tavern appears in travel guides. Volo's write-up (if obtained) lands here. Faction Response Teams treat the address as politically sensitive. | 351+ | **Institution** | +20 | Waterdhavian landmark | Trollskull Tavern is part of the city's social fabric. Events here echo through noble drawing rooms by morning. <aside class="notable">
    <h4>Calibrating the Climb</h4>
    <p>At peak staff (Lif + Maren + Mattrim + Pell + Hadra in her first three tendays), the tavern gains roughly 5–6 Fame per tenday. A party that opens well and staffs well reaches <strong>Respected</strong> in about 5–10 tendays and <strong>Fashionable</strong> in another 10–15 — fast enough to feel like genuine momentum, slow enough that it means something when it happens.</p>
    <p>The climb changes character at <strong>Renowned</strong> (151+). Staff gains of 3–5 per tenday now represent a small fraction of the 200-point gap to Institution. Getting there requires hosted events (+2 each), notable patron relationships (+1 each), faction meetings (+1 each), and the kind of memorable nights that the players will talk about. A party that does nothing but run shifts will plateau somewhere in Fashionable and stay there. A party that treats the tavern as a social project — a place where things happen — can reach Institution by the end of the campaign.</p>
    <p>At 5 Fame/tenday average, with no events or special investment, the journey from 0 to Institution takes roughly 70 tendays. With active play — events, faction meetings, notable patron moments — it compresses to 50–60. Either way, it spans most of the campaign, which is the point.</p>
</aside>

### Gaining Fame

Fame grows slowly. Good nights, good staff, and good word-of-mouth accumulate over tendays and months. Most gains are +1 — because a satisfied customer tells one friend, maybe two, and that friend tells no one unless they come and are also satisfied. Momentum builds through consistency, not incidents.

| Event | Fame Change |
|---|---|
| A prominent NPC publicly praises the tavern | +1 |
| An Event night resolves positively without violence or Watch involvement | +1 |
| The characters resolve a tavern crisis smoothly and in public view | +1 |
| A Faction Contact uses the tavern for a private meeting | +1 |
| The tavern hosts a public event (celebration, fundraiser, ward feast) | +2 |
| A particularly memorable night (DM's discretion; once per tenday maximum) | +1 |
| Lif performs a spectacular or helpful act in public view | +1 |

| Hadra Stonebread joins as cook (opening) | +1 for first 3 tendays as word of the food spreads |

| Mattrim "Threestrings" Mereg is the house bard (sustained) | +1/tenday |
| Lysa of the White Lute passes her first performance | +1; then +1/tenday from the second tenday onward |

| The tavern gains a write-up in *Volo's Guide to Spirits and Specters* | +3 |
| Exceptional Revenue Roll result (111+) | +1 |

### Losing Fame

Fame falls fast and falls hard. A single bad night — the wrong incident, the wrong witness, the wrong rumor reaching the wrong person — can undo tendays of careful work. An unsatisfied customer tells ten people. An angry one tells a hundred. A frightened one never comes back and tells everyone why.

Where Gaining Fame caps most entries at +1 or +2, Losing Fame entries are weighted 3:1 against equivalent gains. Recovery is slow by design: the same event that costs −5 Fame takes five consistently good tendays to undo, and those good tendays require everything to go right. In a city as socially interconnected as Waterdeep, reputation is fragile.

<aside class="notable">
    <h4>Recovering Lost Fame</h4>
    <p>There is no special recovery mechanic. Fame comes back the same way it was built: one good tenday at a time. A party that takes a −10 Fame hit from a patron death needs roughly ten tendays of consistent positive play to recover — and that assumes nothing else goes wrong in the interim, which in Waterdeep is rarely a safe assumption.</p>
    <p>This is intentional. The tavern's reputation in the North Ward is a social object shared by hundreds of people who each have their own experience of it and their own network of people they talk to. The party cannot simply have a great night and expect the damage to disappear. They have to earn the trust back, one visit at a time, from people who are now primed to notice if anything goes wrong again.</p>
</aside>

        <h4>DM Tip: Fame as a Story Engine</h4>

At Fame 351+ (Institution), a Masked Lord has a <em>reserved</em> table. Halaster Blackcloak has heard of it. Events here echo through noble drawing rooms by morning. A tavern at Fame 120 (Fashionable) that suffers a Watch investigation and a patron death in the same tenday drops to roughly Fame 102, back into mid-Fashionable. That is a recoverable situation. But it will take a month of careful operation to undo. That month of careful operation is good campaign material.</p>

## Part 3: Revenue and Costs

At the end of every tenday, the characters determine how much the tavern took in, then subtract what it cost to run.

### The Revenue Roll

Roll **d100 + 10 + Fame modifier + Promotion modifier − Unpaid Debt modifier** and consult the Revenue Table below. The result is the tavern's **gross revenue** for the tenday — the total coin that came through the door before any expenses.

**Promotion Modifier:** For each 1 gp spent on promotion (flyers, bribes to city criers, drinks-on-the-house nights), add 1 to the roll, up to a maximum of +20. Promotion spending is not guaranteed to work — a low base roll with a high promotion modifier simply means the promotional effort reached people who weren't in the mood to come out.

**Unpaid Debt Modifier:** For each 1 gp of outstanding expenses, subtract 1 from the roll. Suppliers who haven't been paid deliver less; staff who haven't been paid work poorly.

**Faction Pressure Penalty:** When a faction actively disrupts the tavern's neighbourhood — Response Team confrontations, a planted spy exposed, a Watch investigation opened through faction interference — subtract 10 from the next 1d3 rolls. Foot traffic drops when something unsettling happens on the street.

### Revenue Table

| d100 + Modifiers | Gross Revenue | Notes |
|:---:|---|---|
| 1–20 | 2d6 × 5 gp (avg 35 gp) | A very bad stretch. The doors were open but almost no one came in. Staff called in sick, a barrel spoiled, word got around that something was wrong. |
| 21–30 | 3d6 × 5 gp (avg 52 gp) | Poor takings. A handful of regulars, thin nights, nothing to draw anyone in. |
| 31–40 | 4d6 × 5 gp (avg 70 gp) | Below average. The tavern is trading, but not filling. |
| 41–60 | 6d6 × 5 gp (avg 105 gp) | Average night. The common room fills most evenings; regulars spend normally. This is what the tavern looks like when nothing notable is happening. |
| 61–80 | 8d6 × 5 gp (avg 140 gp) | A solid tenday. Word is spreading; a few new faces each night; the till comes in ahead of expectation. |
| 81–90 | 10d8 × 5 gp (avg 225 gp) | Strong trading. The room fills early and stays full. Notable patrons spend freely; premium stock moves. |
| 91–110 | 10d10 × 5 gp (avg 275 gp) | Excellent trading. The tavern is the place to be this tenday. The party notices people making a point of coming here rather than anywhere else. |
| 111+ | 10d12 × 5 gp (avg 325 gp) | Exceptional. Gain +1 Fame. Something clicked — a night the whole ward will talk about, a run of remarkable patrons, a private booking that filled the upstairs and the common room both. These don't happen often. When they do, they matter. |

### Calculating Net Profit

After rolling revenue, subtract costs in this order:

**1. Fixed Costs (always 60 gp/tenday)**
Guild fees (10 gp) + staff wages and maintenance (50 gp). These do not vary. If the characters have faction leverage reducing guild fees, adjust the 10 gp figure accordingly.

**2. Variable Supply Costs (from the Supply Costs table, Part 1)**
Look up the current Fame tier and use the tenday supply cost for that tier. If the characters invested in premium stock this tenday (20% above baseline), note the higher figure.

**3. Incidental Losses (as they occur)**
These are tracked separately and deducted from revenue when they happen:
- Orryn Tetch skimming: −2 gp/tenday until caught
- Pell Ashford breakage: −1d6 gp/tenday
- Tavern brawl with no bouncer: −2d6 × 5 gp in furniture damage
- Rella Asp's commission (if active): no direct revenue loss, but Watch investigation may impose Faction Pressure Penalty
- Any staff dismissed mid-tenday: their wage is still owed for the full tenday

**Net Profit = Gross Revenue − Fixed Costs − Supply Costs − Incidental Losses**

A negative result means the shortfall becomes **Unpaid Debt**, which subtracts from the next Revenue Roll.

<aside class="notable">
    <h4>Putting the Numbers in Context</h4>
    <p>At Known tier (supply costs ~40 gp/tenday), total costs run about 100 gp/tenday. An average Revenue Roll (41–60, avg 105 gp) produces roughly 5 gp net profit — the tavern is alive but not thriving. A solid roll (61–80, avg 140 gp) produces ~40 gp net. A strong roll (81–90, avg 225 gp) produces ~125 gp net. These numbers improve as Fame rises and supply costs grow more slowly than revenue capacity — at Fashionable tier (supply ~70 gp, total costs ~130 gp), a solid roll produces ~10 gp net, but a strong roll produces ~95 gp and an excellent roll produces ~145 gp net.</p>
    <p>Startup costs are 1,250 gp. The tavern won't recoup that from average rolls in the early tiers — it exists as a home base and social engine during Acts II–III, not a profit center. Consistent strong rolling at Respected tier recovers the investment in roughly 15–20 tendays of good operation. Excellent rolling at Fashionable tier can do it in 8–10. Design sessions around that arc: the tavern starts as a responsibility and becomes an asset.</p>
    <p>The revenue model also makes staff and faction choices land directly. Hiring Perella Dunwhistle (+3 to Revenue Roll) costs 12 gp/tenday instead of 10 gp — 2 gp more, but her roll bonus shifts the average revenue band upward, paying for itself on any non-terrible tenday. Paying for the Lords' Alliance guild fee reduction (−2 gp/tenday on fees) saves 20 gp per tenday indefinitely. These are real decisions with real arithmetic, not abstracted modifiers.</p>
</aside>

<aside class="notable">
    <h4>Tracking in Play</h4>
    <p>You don't need to run the Revenue Roll every session. If the characters are deep in an investigation arc, run the roll in the background and report the result in a line: "While you were gone, the tavern had a solid tenday — Lif's been managing things, costs came to about 100 gp, and you find 40 gp net in the cash box." The roll matters most during the early game when money is tight, and during any period when faction pressure is actively disrupting the neighbourhood. After the party reaches Fame 31+ (Respected), the tavern largely sustains itself; hand-waive the roll unless something dramatic happened that tenday.</p>
</aside>

## Part 4: Tavern Time

For each significant night the characters spend at the tavern, use the following procedure to generate the evening's events and cast of characters.

### The Procedure

**Step 1 — Check for an Event.** Roll 1d6. On a 1, an Event occurs tonight (see the Event Table). At Fame 81+ (Fashionable), Events occur on a 1–2 as the tavern draws a more volatile and interesting clientele.

**Step 2 — Determine Significant Patrons.** Roll 1d6 to determine the number of significant patrons present this evening, then roll on the Patron Table for each. Duplicate results mean that patron is more central to the evening than usual — they've brought a friend, or they're having a bigger night than normal.

**Step 3 — Establish Topics and Agendas.** Look up the Topics/Agendas for each significant patron. Choose one topic per patron, or select a single topic to drive the evening. When in doubt, default to the first unplayed topic for that patron.

**Step 4 — Let It Run.** The characters decide how (and whether) to engage. Not every patron needs a scene. Two strong interactions are worth more than five rushed ones.

## Part 5: Events

When an Event is called for, roll 1d20 and consult the Event Table below.

### Event Resolution

Most events in Trollskull Tavern resolve through **roleplay or automatic outcomes**, not combat or dice. The tavern is not an adventure location — it is a home base. Events exist to generate conversation, character moments, and faction texture, not encounters. The exceptions are noted explicitly. Use the following guidance:

- **Auto-resolved (no roll needed) if staff conditions are met:** A Bouncer on duty resolves the Brawl (Event 12), the arm-wrestling competition (Event 1), and any physical altercation automatically without involving the characters. A Master Cook or experienced Cook handles kitchen Events (Event 16) without loss unless the specific "cook has quit" result occurs. Lif alone handles most drunk-patron disturbances.
- **Roleplay-resolved:** All social events — the socialite (17), the courier (19), the Masked Lord (14), the half-elf at the bar (15), the patron slipping something in a drink (4) — are fully in the players' hands. No roll is needed unless they choose to escalate or investigate.
- **Dice-optional:** The brawl (12), Stannis the pickpocket (5), and the kenku drug-sellers (2) have dice options if the players choose confrontation, but can also resolve entirely through good conversation.
- **The one exception:** Faction Response Team events (8) may, depending on campaign state and the team involved, escalate past social resolution. See Part 7.

### Event Notes

#### Traveler's Dust (Event 2)

Traveler's dust is a narcotic sold by kenku street gangs with ties to the Xanathar Guild. Tiny roseate crystals that dissolve when dropped in the eye, it produces a euphoric sensation paired with a subjective slowing of the world around the user. Regular users — "those walking the crimson road" — develop trembling hands, slurred speech, and blood-red eyes. The Xanathar Guild profits from distribution; the kenku sell it for them, taking a cut.

If the characters allow the sale, a regular patron becomes visibly affected and the Watch receives an anonymous tip within the tenday. Lose 1 Fame. If the characters drive the kenku out without violence, gain +1 Fame but make an enemy of the kenku flock. If a Bouncer is employed, they handle the expulsion without requiring character involvement — but the kenku flock's enmity still follows.

#### Stannis the Pickpocket (Event 5)

Stannis (CG male Illuskan human, Commoner) is twelve years old and genuinely good at his job. His handler Sabeen (N female half-orc, Tough) runs a crew of child pickpockets in the North Ward, sourced from the Field Ward's poorest families. She is not cruel to them, but she is also not kind.

If the characters catch Stannis and turn him over to the Watch, Sabeen escalates — her crew will target the tavern's patrons for the next tenday (−1 Fame). If they catch him and speak to Sabeen directly, she can become a low-level street contact who owes them a favor. If they employ him in some legitimate capacity, Stannis becomes a fixture — fiercely loyal and surprisingly useful.

#### Kyser Tameno (Event 9)

Kyser (CG male wood elf wizard) has a standing teleportation arrangement that passes through this location — it was a safe house for a now-dissolved wizard's society, and he hasn't updated his portal anchor. He is startled to find people here. If the characters are friendly, he becomes a genuine regular, arriving by portal once or twice a month. He can serve as a source of arcane gossip, a route to the Surveyors', Map & Chart Makers' Guild, or an eventual route to locations beyond Waterdeep's walls. If the characters are rude, he reanchors his portal elsewhere with visible offense.

#### Tavern Brawl (Event 12)

**If a Bouncer is employed:** The brawl is resolved automatically before it properly starts. The Bouncer physically separates the two groups — Torbo by standing between them until they feel foolish, Bram by announcing Watch protocol in a voice that tolerates no argument, Yagra by cracking her knuckles in the specific direction of whoever threw the first card. No damage, no Fame loss. Gain +1 Fame if this is the first time the tavern has demonstrated it keeps a firm house.

The following profiles cover the significant recurring patrons of Trollskull Tavern. Each profile includes a brief appearance note, roleplaying keys, and a list of Topics/Agendas — conversational gambits or needs the NPC brings to the table on a given night. Work through topics in order, returning to the beginning when exhausted.

### Tally Fellbranch
*The Bent Nail — CG, Commoner with half-elf traits*

**Appearance:** Short brown hair in a bowl cut; large blue eyes; leather vest with tool pockets. Rubs the tops of his ears as a nervous habit.

**Topics & Agendas:**
1. Introduces the characters to his brother, Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave contact), framing it as a casual visit that is clearly calculated.

4. Mentions that the oak tree outside the manor is "acting strange" — the roots are reaching toward the foundation. He thinks it's the ghost. He's wrong.

### Embric & Avi

**Appearance:** Embric has faint fire-colored veins visible on his forearms and smells faintly of hot metal. Avi always looks slightly damp and wears silver jewelry that shifts subtly.

4. They want to install a hearth upgrade in the kitchen — at cost, no charge for labor — as a thank-you for some specific kindness the characters showed. They will not accept payment.

### Fala Lefaliir
*Corellon's Crown — CG wood elf, Druid*

## Part 7: Business Threats — Faction Response Teams

The real threats to Trollskull Tavern are not a jealous competitor and his hired rats. By the time the characters are running a recognized establishment, the business threats that matter are the same ones driving the Grand Game: four factions who know where the party lives, and who will periodically remind them of this fact.

Faction Response Teams are detailed fully in a later chapter. What follows describes how each team behaves specifically in the context of the tavern — when they come, what they want, and what the interaction looks like at the bar rather than in an alley. As a rule of thumb: Response Teams do not attack the tavern unprovoked while customers are present. The factions are not suicidal; a public massacre in a licensed North Ward establishment would invite the City Guard, the Watchful Order, and potentially Vajra Safahr's personal attention within the hour. What they do instead is visit. And the manner of the visit tells the characters exactly where they stand.

<div class="fvtt advice">
    <figure class="icon">
        <img class="round" src="icons/sundries/books/book-open-turquoise.webp">
    </figure>
    <article>
        <h4>Design Note: Faction Teams at the Tavern</h4>
        <p>The visit structure below matches the arc structure of the campaign (see Arc Structure for full faction timelines). Teams show up at the tavern in proportion to how much the party has gotten in their way — or how useful they might be. A party that has stayed entirely neutral will see occasional curious visits and nothing more. A party that has hit two lairs and stolen an Eye will see a very different kind of customer.</p>
        <p>Use Event Table entry 8 ("A Faction Response Team enters for a drink") as the low-pressure version of these interactions. For escalated visits triggered by player action, use the full profiles below and skip the Event Table roll.</p>

### Xanathar Guild — Gorath's Duergar

*The Xanathar Guild does not negotiate. It sends lessons.*

**Who comes:** Gorath leads the opening visit: four duergar in unremarkable clothes, sitting at two tables, ordering the cheapest ale, and saying nothing. Gorath himself takes a seat facing the door. If Gorath has already been neutralized, Korgstrod Uxgulm's team is next — with a half-ogre who takes up too much space and a gazer floating at belt height under a heavy cloak. Noska Ur'gray's team (six bugbears plus a gazer) does not visit. They arrive with intent.

**What triggers a visit:**
- The party has discovered, looted, or interfered with a Xanathar Guild outpost or operation.
- A Guild spy planted as staff (Tobrin Ashvale, Grevik Nass, or any similar candidate) has been exposed.
- The party has the Stone of Golorr or an Eye in their possession and the Guild knows it.

**What the visit looks like — early campaign (Acts II–III):**

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Four dwarves come in at the same time, which is unusual enough to notice. They don't look at you directly. They order ale by pointing at the nearest tap. They sit at two tables without rearranging the chairs — which means they sat exactly where they could watch the door and the stair. One of them, the broadest, has a filed-tooth smile he uses once, when your eyes meet his. He does not look away first.</p>
    <p>They drink one round each and leave exact coin on the table. No tip. No trouble. They do not come back that night.</p>
</div>

**What this means:** The Guild knows where you live. They are not threatened yet. This visit is a receipt — confirmation that their files on the party now include this address.

**What the visit looks like — active campaign (Acts III–IV, after a heist):**

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Gorath sits at the bar — which is a choice, because it means his back is to the room. He orders something good, pays for it, and says, when there is a lull: "You cost us something." He doesn't specify what. "We want it back. Or we want something worth the same. You have until the new moon." He drinks the rest of his ale, sets the mug down precisely, and leaves. His team is outside. They walk east, together, without looking back.</p>
</div>

**What the characters can do:** Negotiate, pay back what they owe (if it's goods rather than information), or prepare for Korgstrod's team. There is no social skill check that makes the Xanathar Guild forget what they lost. There is only exchange or escalation.

**Fame Impact:** A duergar visit that is handled without public incident costs no Fame. If the characters are visibly intimidated and regulars notice, −1 Fame. If they handle the ultimatum calmly and the team leaves without trouble, +1 Fame (the kind of house that can manage difficult customers).

### Manshoon's Zhentarim — Vevette Blackwater

*Manshoon's people are the weakest response team in the Grand Game. This does not mean they are safe.*

**Who comes:** Vevette Blackwater — fiery red hair with two platinum streaks, CE swashbuckler, arrives alone or with two thugs in decent clothes. She is charming, laughs easily, and will absolutely try to extract information through conversation before she tries anything else. Urstul Floxin does not visit the tavern unless he is specifically hunting the characters for the Gralhund Villa incident. Agorn Fuoco may arrive as a secondary contact, especially if Vevette has already been neutralized.

**What triggers a visit:**
- The party has interfered with the Gralhund Villa operation or disrupted Manshoon's acquisition of the Stone.
- The party has been identified as potential sellers — Manshoon is looking for people who might deliver an Eye for coin.
- The party has allied with the Doom Raiders, and Manshoon wants to know how close that alliance is.

**What the visit looks like — early contact:**

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>She has been here before you noticed her, which means she came in either during a busy period or before you opened, and that is itself information. Red hair, two pale streaks, a long coat she hasn't taken off. She's on her second drink. When she catches you looking, she raises her glass with a smile that doesn't quite include her eyes. "Your bartender," she says, "makes a very good case for this place. I thought I'd see what the fuss was about." A pause. "The people I work for are curious about something you may have found recently. I'm told you're the sort of people who appreciate a direct conversation." She waits.</p>
</div>

**What the visit looks like — if the Doom Raiders are known allies:**

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>She sits down across from whichever party member is closest and sets a card on the table, face down. "I'm going to be honest with you," she says. "Davil Starsong is a decent man in an organization that has made some poor choices. The people I work for are not decent, and they have made excellent choices, and they are going to win." She taps the card. "There's a number on that. If you find what my employers are looking for, and you don't deliver it to Starsong first, that number becomes yours. No conditions. No faction." She stands. "Think about it." She leaves the card face down.</p>
</div>

**What the characters can do:** Engage with the offer, refuse it, or pass the card to Davil. Passing it to Davil earns +1 Doom Raiders renown. Engaging with the offer without following through is noticed by both factions within two tendays and costs standing with both.

**What the Doom Raiders do if they find out Vevette visited:** If party members are Doom Raiders, Davil will ask, politely, whether they had any unusual visitors recently. He will already know the answer. He wants to know if they'll tell him.

**Fame Impact:** Vevette's visit is unremarkable to other patrons — she is well-dressed and quiet. No Fame impact unless she causes a scene (which she won't, unless the characters escalate).

### The Cassalanters — "Willifort Crowelle"

*The Cassalanters do not send teams to taverns. They send individuals. The individual is worse.*

**Who comes:** "Willifort Crowelle" — in truth a doppelganger, the Cassalanters' most trusted lieutenant. He arrives in whatever identity is most useful: a friendly rival tavern-owner, a minor city functionary checking compliance paperwork, a Harper contact the characters haven't met before. He will not arrive as himself, because "himself" does not exist.

If the characters have become a serious threat to the Cassalanters' acquisition of the Gold, Seffia Naelryke's full team (cult fanatic + 2 spies + 1 veteran + 1 invisible imp) will instead conduct a quiet search of the premises during a busy night — not a confrontation, a reconnaissance.

**What triggers a visit:**
- The party has connected the Cassalanters to the Grand Game through investigation.
- The party has been to the Brandath Mausoleum or shown interest in the Third Eye's location.
- The party has publicly refused a Cassalanter social invitation or caused them visible embarrassment.
- A Cassalanter household operative placed as staff has been exposed — Rella Asp (Master Cook candidate) if she poisoned someone and was traced; Ivrala Sorn (Server candidate) if the party discovered her reporting arrangement. Either way, "Willifort" arrives to assess the damage.

**What the visit looks like — early contact, "Willifort" as a compliance officer:**

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>He has a badge from the Fellowship of Innkeepers, which is real — he had it made. He is polite, thorough, and asks questions that are approximately two degrees more specific than a compliance inspection requires. How often do you take deliveries? Do you have regular overnight guests? Is there a cellar access? He thanks you warmly and writes something in a small book that he does not show you. At the door, he turns back. "One more thing. Do you know a man named Dalakhar? Small, gnomish build? We've had a few reports of a person matching that description frequenting addresses in this ward." He watches your face. "No matter. Good evening."</p>
</div>

**What the visit looks like — Seffia's team during a busy night:**

This visit is invisible unless the characters succeed on a DC 15 passive Perception check or a DC 13 active check while in the cellar or upstairs rooms during service. The invisible imp examines the characters' personal belongings and looks for any Golorr artifacts. It does not steal; it reports. A DC 17 Detect Magic or a character with active True Seeing detects the imp.

**What the characters can do:** The Cassalanter arc is fundamentally a moral one (see Act IV). Their presence at the tavern is investigative, not threatening — until the party is in direct competition for the Gold. Once that threshold is crossed, Willifort shifts from information-gathering to active interference: impersonating party members to damage faction relationships, or arranging for a Cassalanter-connected Watch lieutenant to take a new interest in the tavern's compliance record.

**Fame Impact:** Willifort's visit causes no public Fame impact. Seffia's team, if detected during a busy service, causes −1 Fame as patrons notice that something is wrong and can't say what.

### Bregan D'aerthe — Fel'rekt Lafeen (or Another Lieutenant)

*Jarlaxle's people are the only faction that might genuinely enjoy the tavern.*

**Who comes:** One of Jarlaxle's three named lieutenants — Fel'rekt Lafeen most often in the early campaign, accompanied by 2–4 drow in surface disguise. Their disguises are excellent. They are also excellent at behaving like normal patrons: they order interesting things, tip well, and do not start trouble. Jarlaxle himself may appear in a different disguise on every visit (see Patron Profiles), always preceded by one of his lieutenants scoping the room first.

**What triggers a visit:**
- The party has interfered with Bregan D'aerthe's Eye operations or the Sea Maidens Faire.
- Jarlaxle has decided the party is interesting enough to monitor closely.
- A drow character or Bregan D'aerthe-affiliated character is part of the party (this accelerates his interest significantly — Jarlaxle takes a personal and territorial interest in drow operating in his city).

**What the visit looks like — reconnaissance:**

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>The four of them come in within a few minutes of each other, which you might not notice if you weren't looking. They are dressed well, in the surface fashion — well enough to pass, but with a slight hesitation in how they handle their cloaks, a slight uncertainty about which side to sit on. They are learning the room. One of them, long-limbed, with eyes that catch the light the wrong way, orders a Calishite spirit by its proper name and watches Lif pour it with something that might be professional appreciation. They stay for two hours and leave together. One of them leaves a tip in a coin you don't recognize. It is worth more than you expect.</p>
</div>

**What the visit looks like — if a drow PC is part of the party:**

<div class="fvtt narrative">
    <p>Fel'rekt does not disguise his heritage. He sits at the bar in the open, surface-dressed but with his coloring making no claim to anything it is not. He orders something specific — he knows what's good here, which means he has been before in a form you didn't recognize. He looks at the drow party member the way one professional looks at another who has found a very good hiding place. "Jarlaxle sends his regards," he says, "and wonders if you'd like to have dinner. He suggests Remalia's, Thursday. He'll know if you come." He finishes his drink and leaves. The coin is on the bar before he stands up.</p>
</div>

**What the characters can do:** Meet with Jarlaxle (a social encounter worth running as a full scene — see Patron Profiles), refuse him (he finds refusal interesting and is not offended), or attempt to tail Fel'rekt back to the Sea Maidens Faire (DC 16 Dexterity Stealth, contested by Fel'rekt's +6 Perception — achievable, but difficult).

**Fame Impact:** Bregan D'aerthe visits, handled without incident, add +1 Fame after the first two occurrences — not because anyone knows who these mysterious well-dressed patrons are, but because they tip extraordinarily well and word gets around that the Trollskull attracts interesting people.

<aside class="notable">
    <h4>When Response Teams Attack the Tavern</h4>
    <p>Direct attacks on Trollskull Tavern — not visits, not ultimatums, but violence inside the building — are rare before Act IV and require specific triggering conditions: the party has taken an Eye and a faction team has tracked it to this address, or the party has publicly humiliated a faction leader in a way that demands a visible response. Even then, the attack will come at night, after hours, when the tavern is empty or nearly so. Breaking into an occupied, licensed North Ward establishment in view of Trollwatch is not something any faction does casually.</p>
    <p>The exception is Noska Ur'gray's team of bugbears. Noska is Xanathar's enforcer, not his diplomat, and bugbears do not read the social calendar. If Noska is deployed, he will not wait for closing time.</p>
    <p>Full combat profiles for all Response Teams, including Noska's bugbear team, are detailed in the Faction Response Teams chapter.</p>
</aside>

## Part 8: The Bigger Picture

Trollskull Tavern does not exist in a vacuum. As the campaign progresses through Acts III, IV, and beyond, the tavern's status changes.

### The Tavern and the Grand Game

By the time the Grand Game (the multi-faction competition for Neverember's Enigma) reaches its active phase, Trollskull Tavern has become known enough that factions take an interest in it:

- **The Harpers** may use the tavern as a casual dead-drop point, with notes left inside specific books that Rishaal provides on a rotating basis.
- **The Zhentarim** (if not hostile to the characters) may send a Response Team to the bar not as a threat but as a show of normalcy — they drink, they tip, they leave. The message is: *we know where you live, and we are not currently bothered by this.*
- **The Xanathar Guild** has no interest in the tavern specifically, but a Guild operative may follow a target into the building. This is the Xanathar Guild's version of not caring about the tavern.
- **Bregan D'aerthe**, through Jarlaxle, finds the tavern genuinely charming. He has a table reserved — they don't know this yet — and will begin appearing occasionally, in a different disguise each time.

### The Tavern and Undermountain

When the campaign transitions into *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, Trollskull Tavern becomes an above-ground anchor — a reason to return to the surface, a source of income between dungeon runs, and a place where rumors about Undermountain's upper levels circulate. Durnan at the Yawning Portal is the professional; the Trollskull is where the amateurs talk.

NPCs who disappeared in the dungeon may turn up at the bar. Faction agents may request meetings there specifically because the dungeon entrance is close. Most importantly: the characters have something to protect now. That matters when Halaster Blackcloak starts paying attention.

    <h4>Halaster Takes Notice</h4>

They leave without incident. But Lif — who otherwise welcomes all paying customers with poltergeist efficiency — refused to serve them, retreating behind the bar and staying there. The stranger's seat, when examined afterward, holds a single copper piece, tails-up. Etched into the coin's surface, in handwriting too small to read without magnification: <em>Interesting.</em></p>
