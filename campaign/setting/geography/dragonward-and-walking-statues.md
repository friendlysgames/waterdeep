# The Dragonward and Walking Statues

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** The geography entry for Ahghairon's dragonward, which keeps dragons out of Waterdeep, and for the eight walking statues that stand in the city's streets.
> - **Where it is used:** The GM reads it before answering what the dragonward stops, who can pass it, or where a named statue stands and who lives in it.
> - **Quests and events:** **The Poisoned Delivery** (Doom Raiders Mission 2) hands a coffer over beside the God Catcher, and **Silencing Skeemo** (Doom Raiders Mission 4) refers back to that meeting. **The Handkerchief and the Girl** (Bregan D'aerthe Mission 1) puts a Castle Ward clerk at the foot of the Great Drunkard's stair during the Ches 21 parade. **Vault of Dragons** turns on the dragonstaff of Ahghairon and the dragon who holds it. The **Castle Ward** gazetteer page lists six of the eight statues and the ward's source.
> - **Location journals:** None. No mapped site sits on a statue or at the ward's source.
> - **Spoiler limit:** Everything above the `## Overview` is for the GM. The Overview names no holder of the dragonstaff and no vault.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM say exactly what the dragonward stops and who can pass it, and place and describe each of the eight walking statues without inventing a height, a ward or a second awakening that no source gives.

Ahghairon's dragonward is a permanent magical effect over Waterdeep that physically keeps dragons out of the city. The walking statues are eight enormous stone constructs that stand in its streets. The published text says they exist to fend off armies and insurmountable foes, and the city now uses them as landmarks.

Both features lead back to the Castle Ward. The dragonward begins beneath Ahghairon's Tower in the Palace courtyard, and six of the eight statues stand in the ward. The campaign treats them as setting rather than as party resources. The statues are sites in two faction events, and the dragonward is the reason the dragonstaff of Ahghairon is worth taking.

### The Dragonward

- **What it does:** Dragons and all other creatures of the dragon type are physically unable to enter Waterdeep or its sewers for as long as the ward persists. It does not depend on the Open Lord's permission, so no decree can invite a dragon in.
- **What it is:** An undispellable and permanent magical effect that blankets the city. Dragon Heist names it Ahghairon's dragonward, and the campaign's outline calls it Ahghairon's founding spell.
- **Where it begins:** Somewhere under Ahghairon's Tower, which stands in the Palace courtyard in the Castle Ward and holds the first Open Lord's tomb. The published text gives no more exact point.
- **Where it ends:** The ward does not extend to the harbor or into Undermountain, so a dragon in either place is outside it. The campaign's old outline says the ward matters because Jarlaxle moves by sea.
- **How a dragon gets past it:** A creature of the dragon type that is touched by the dragonstaff of Ahghairon can ignore the ward and move through the city freely. The effect lasts until the staff touches the creature again, or until the time set by whoever is attuned to the staff runs out. The published text names no other way in.
- **When it was raised:** Dragon Heist gives no date. The wiki places the ward in 1071 DR, after the adventurer Ranressa Shiard came back to Mount Waterdeep riding the copper wyrm Galadaeros and Ahghairon laid a mythal over the city in response. This date comes from the wiki alone, and the bundle holds no wiki page for the ward. `[research]`

### The Walking Statues

- **Before 1370 DR:** Over a century ago only one of the eight stood in view, on a bluff called Gull Leap at the northern foot of Mount Waterdeep. It was ninety feet tall and looked like a bald human staring out to sea. The other seven were hidden on the Ethereal Plane, to be called forth only in times of great peril, and many in the city doubted that such sapient constructs were real.
- **1370 DR:** After the statue helped defend the city against a sahuagin invasion, Khelben Blackstaff reshaped it into a kneeling sahuagin. It is now the Sahuagin Humbled.
- **1385 DR:** The Spellplague made the magical fields that protect Waterdeep unstable, and six more statues appeared in the city and rampaged during an earthquake. The Sahuagin Humbled stayed motionless. The authorities and citizens stopped three of them by breaking the Swordmaiden and the Hawk Man and sinking the God Catcher into the street. Then all of them stopped as suddenly as they had begun. Tsarra Chaadren, the Blackstaff at the time, could not command them back to the Ethereal Plane, so the city repaired itself and built up around them.
- **1479 DR:** The eighth statue, the Griffon, came out of the Ethereal Plane to defend Ahghairon's Tower against an intrusion. It roosted there for a time before it flew to its present position near Peaktop Aerie on Mount Waterdeep. This too seemed to be outside the Blackstaff's control.
- **Since then:** Dragon Heist says every statue has been dormant for well over a decade. Since the 1385 rampage, only the Griffon has woken.

### Who Can Animate Them

- **The Blackstaff:** Only the wielder of the Blackstaff can animate the seven statues that are still whole. **Vajra Safahr** holds the Blackstaff now. The Swordmaiden is too broken to be animated at all.
- **How reliable that is:** The statues moved on their own in 1385 and 1479, and the Blackstaff of the time could not command them either time. The published history records no case of a Blackstaff wielder commanding a statue.
- **When they are used:** Because the statues are so destructive, the city uses them only against armies and seemingly insurmountable foes.
- **What happens to what is built on them:** Any structure attached to a statue is destroyed the first time that statue animates. That includes the God Catcher tenement, Gralkyn's Tankard and Sparaunt Tower.
- **Constructed nature:** A statue needs no air, food, drink or sleep. All of them share one stat block, the Walking Statue of Waterdeep in Dragon Heist Appendix B, which the campaign Bestiary does not hold yet.
- **In the campaign:** No quest or faction event animates a statue, and the campaign writes no scene in which the party commands one.

### Heights

- **The original statue:** Ninety feet, and only the original. It is now the Sahuagin Humbled.
- **The Griffon:** Its back is twenty feet off the ground, which the adventure calls a mount fit for a storm giant.
- **The Honorable Knight:** Nine stories tall, twice the height of any building nearby.
- **The rest:** The God Catcher, the Great Drunkard, the Lady Dreaming, the Hawk Man and the Swordmaiden have no published height. Do not give them one.

### The Eight Statues

**The God Catcher** — Castle Ward
- A well-muscled and impassive male human with its left leg sunk to the hip in the street, the result of a spell the Blackstaff cast during the 1385 rampage. Its left hand and right foot press against the ground as if it were pulling itself out, and its right arm reaches skyward. A sphere of stone floats above the open palm, the statue gazes up at it, and bird droppings around its eyes make it look as though it weeps.
- It stands near the Market and is probably the most famous statue in the city. A tenement of the same name climbs its chest, knee and shoulders. The landlord, Aundra Blackcloak, is an unsociable sorcerer who lives in the floating sphere and comes and goes by a door carved in it.
- She appears unannounced on balconies and rooftops after dark, mostly to buy odd substances for magical purposes. She is polite, pays fair coin and never talks about her own affairs. No one has said they have seen the inside of her home.
- **Hook:** In **The Poisoned Delivery**, the party meets Esvele Rosznar, **The Black Viper**, in a hire-coach waiting in the shadow of the raised arm. The coach stands there from 17:45 to 19:00, and the exchange happens at 18:00. In **Silencing Skeemo**, **Tashlyn Yafeera** questions whoever spoke for the party at the God Catcher.

**The Griffon** — Castle Ward
- A granite griffon on four legs, with a back fully twenty feet off the ground. It has flown, spreading granite wings like a bird's, but now it stands in a regal pose near Peaktop Aerie atop Mount Waterdeep and looks southeast over the Dock Ward.
- Newcomers sometimes take it for a monument to the Griffon Cavalry, which flies from Peaktop Aerie, though Waterdhavians know otherwise.
- **Hook:** It is the only statue that has moved since 1385. The campaign's history says it rose to defend Ahghairon's Tower in 1479 DR, and no quest uses it.

**The Sahuagin Humbled** — Castle Ward
- The original walking statue, ninety feet tall and reshaped by Khelben Blackstaff after the 1370 DR sahuagin invasion. It kneels in the shape of a sahuagin and bows low toward the House of Heroes, which the city reads as thanks for everyone who fought in that war.
- Dragon Heist places it at the foot of Mount Waterdeep near the head of Julthoon Street, and also on the bluff called Gull Leap at the mountain's northern foot. It stayed still throughout the 1385 rampage.
- **Hook:** The House of Heroes is the Sea Ward temple of **Tempus**. No quest uses the statue.

**The Great Drunkard** — Castle Ward
- A giant that stopped when it reached the Market, fell backward onto a building and sat there. Its arms hang limp and its head rests on its chest, so it looks asleep. Its huge stone battleaxe still stands upright nearby with the blade half buried in the cobbles.
- Rubble from the crushed building became a broad stone stair with railings and a ramp, which climbs to the statue's lap. A two-story tavern called Gralkyn's Tankard fills the lap.
- The wiki puts the statue just south of the Market, and the campaign's events put it at the head of Bazaar Street.
- **Hook:** In **The Handkerchief and the Girl**, Maester Roderick Bartlethorpe, a Castle Ward clerk, stands at the foot of the stair for every parade. A window table in the Tankard costs 1 gp in tips and looks straight down on his spot.

**The Lady Dreaming** — Castle Ward
- A statue of a female elf whose hair and clothing seemed to flow as it walked the city during the Spellplague. When the statues stopped it toppled onto its side, and it now looks like a titanic sculpture of a noble lady asleep in her garden.
- Dragon Heist gives no site. The wiki puts it on the far northern slope of Mount Waterdeep, and the Castle Ward count in Dragon Heist agrees that it stands in the ward.
- **Hook:** None. No quest or faction event uses it.

**The Honorable Knight** — Trades Ward
- A male warrior in plate armor with a shield and a longsword. When the statues stopped it bowed to those who opposed it, straightened, sheathed its sword and set its shield point down by its side. It faces southwest toward the harbor like a castle guard at ease, and citizens of the southerly wards view it with respect.
- It stands in a block between Snail Street and the Way of the Dragon, near the place where four wards meet. Dragon Heist uses it to orient visitors. If it is south and west of you, you are in the Trades Ward. If it is north and west, you are in the Southern Ward. South and east is the Castle Ward, and north and east is the Dock Ward.
- The wiki settles the statue in the Trades Ward, and Dragon Heist does not name a ward.
- **Hook:** None. No quest or faction event uses it.

**The Hawk Man** — Castle Ward
- A winged, hawk-headed figure that resembles an aarakocra. Its wings are folded tight against its back and have never opened, so no one knows whether it can fly. It tilts toward the northeast because its right foot and right arm were broken up for rubble, and its left arm reaches out to the north with the palm forward in a gesture that says "Stop."
- The body is hollow and serves as a tower shared by several wealthy tenants, officially called Sparaunt Tower after its owner. A tunnel runs through the left arm to a courtyard on the north side. A visitor rings a bell there, and the door guard lowers a rope ladder, or a rope chair for guests who are infirm or heavily laden.
- **Hook:** None. No quest or faction event uses it.

**The Swordmaiden** — North Ward
- A statue that looks almost identical to the Honorable Knight, except for its female form and open-faced helm. It was felled in the Spellplague after causing great chaos and slaughter. North Ward residents took out their anger on it, and pieces of it now sit all over the ward in buildings and as freestanding sculpture.
- Its head sits among tall trees in the North Ward block bounded by Hassantyr's Street, Tarsar's Street, Whaelgond Way and Ussilbran Street. A door in place of the jaw leads into Thort's Findings. Undevvur Thort, a wizened ex-adventurer who leans on a cane that some locals say is more than a cane, lives in the small shop. It fills the hollow head with levels, stairs and landings and is crammed with oddments that adventurers sold to him.
- Each item carries a placard in Thort's flowing handwriting that names it or guesses at its origin. Nobles rent his wares as props for themed revels, and sages, alchemists and wizards visit often.
- **Hook:** None. It is the only statue that cannot be animated, and no quest or faction event uses it.

### Statues as Landmarks

- Over the years Waterdhavians have built homes, shops and towers around and on top of several statues, because they now treat them as little more than landmarks. The wiki adds that the statues cannot be removed from the city, so people use them to navigate.
- The Honorable Knight is the best orientation point in the city, because it stands near the place where four wards meet.
- The old outline called the statues "a civic reference point, not a player resource," and that stands. Dragon Heist describes them as beautiful, cyclopean reminders of Waterdeep's might.

## Overview

Waterdeep lies under Ahghairon's dragonward, a permanent magic that no spell can dispel and that keeps dragons from entering the city. It does not reach the harbor or the dungeons below. Eight giant walking statues stand about the city and serve as landmarks, six of them in the Castle Ward. They rampaged during the Spellplague, and only the Griffon has moved since, defending Ahghairon's Tower in 1479 DR.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Who holds the dragonstaff:** Aurinax, an adult gold dragon, holds the dragonstaff of Ahghairon and guards the hidden vault under the city. He can ignore the ward himself and lives in the city as the dwarf Barok Clanghammer. He stays in humanoid form until a fight starts, because he cannot easily carry the staff in dragon form.
> - **How he came by it:** Maaril, the archmage who last wielded the staff, was his friend and invited him to stay in the city's Dragon Tower. Shortly before the Spellplague Maaril went mad, left Waterdeep and gave Aurinax the staff. Dagult Neverember then let Aurinax keep it as payment for guarding the vault. Dragon Heist places that bargain when Neverember became Open Lord, and the campaign places it after he found the vault (see **The Open Lord's Fall**).
> - **What the staff is worth:** Whoever holds it decides which dragons the ward admits, one creature at a time. Jarlaxle wants it as leverage over Waterdeep. The structure notes for **Vault of Dragons** add that Manshoon would take it if he could and that Neverember still believes he has a claim to it.
> - **How Aurinax treats it:** He attacks only to defend himself, the staff or the gold. He will not give the staff up willingly, because it is his payment, but he will use its power on behalf of the city in exchange for more gemstones.
> - **Who knows:** Dragon Heist says nobody left in Waterdeep knows about Aurinax or where the staff is, and the vault is warded against scrying. **Vault of Dragons** settles who holds the staff afterward.
> - **Answering a player who asks whether a dragon can enter the city:** No. A dragon cannot enter unless the staff has touched it, and the Open Lord cannot waive the ward. A dragon in the harbor or in Undermountain is outside it.
> - **Answering a player who asks who can wake a statue:** Only the Blackstaff's wielder, and only seven of the eight. Say that the statues have moved on their own twice, in 1385 and 1479.
> - **The tomb and the Griffon:** The campaign's history reads Ahghairon's tomb as the ward's source and the 1479 DR intruder as the reason the Griffon rose. Dragon Heist says only that the ward begins somewhere under the tower.

**Tone:** Plain and civic. Waterdhavians treat the dragonward and the statues as fixtures, so describe them the way a local gives directions and save the awe for the God Catcher's pose and the Honorable Knight's size.
