**Purpose:** Lets the GM place any site, office or festival in the right ward and send each question to the page that answers it, without restating what the ward, governance and History pages hold.

Waterdeep is a walled port city on the Sword Coast, built on a plateau below Mount Waterdeep and known as the City of Splendors. The adventure calls it the most civilized city on the Sword Coast and the coast's grandest metropolis. Open Lord Laeral Silverhand governs it from the Palace beside the secret Masked Lords, the City Watch polices its streets, and the guilds touch every trade. The campaign opens at the Yawning Portal on Ches 1 and runs to Flamerule 11, with all four villain factions active in the city throughout, as **The Grand Game** sets out.

### The City at a Glance

- **Setting:** Mount Waterdeep stands across the skyline to the west, and its far slopes drop into the sea. A spur of the mountain juts inland, and Castle Waterdeep stands atop the spur's easternmost point. The City of the Dead lies opposite the mountain's northern ridge, which descends to the Field of Triumph. The adventure uses these three landmarks to orient visitors.
- **Population (city-wide):** No source gives a count for 1492 DR. `[research]` For scale, the wiki gives 1,347,680 across Waterdeep and the farmland it commands (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting* 3e, 1372 DR), of whom 132,661 count as the metropolis, and the wiki's own estimate for the city proper is about 200,000. The wiki adds that a plague in 1395 DR reduced the population greatly (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide* 4e).
- **Peoples (city-wide):** The wiki gives humans 64%, dwarves 10%, elves 10%, halflings 5%, half-elves 5%, gnomes 3%, half-orcs 2% and others 1%, with most elves moon elves and most halflings lightfoot. No source gives a ward breakdown, so the ward pages reuse these figures with a label. The adventure names the people of Waterdeep among its greatest splendors and calls the city a haven for those who define for themselves what it means to be a man or a woman.
- **Languages:** The wiki leaves the field blank. `[research]` The Palace keeps the Code Legal in several languages, and the magisters at the gates hand out the Common version.
- **Trade and coin:** The wiki calls Waterdeep the trading hub for the mineral-rich north, Amn and Calimshan to the south, the Inner Sea kingdoms to the east and the sea kingdoms to the west (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting* 3e). It exports ale, arms, cloth, fish and refined metals, among other goods. The city mints its own coins, and all taxes, fines and guild fees must be paid in Waterdhavian coin or in the coin of a Lords' Alliance settlement.
- **Taxes and fees:** Dagult Neverember set a monthly tax of 1 shard per person over ten in the first year of his reign, and the City Guard collects it door to door on the last day of each month. A person who pays a single dragon receives a writ that exempts them for twelve months. A ship that touches dock pays 5 dragons for a stay of up to fourteen days, and a stall in the Market rents for 1 nib a day. The roads charge no toll, and the adventure tells a traveler to report any Guard who asks for one.
- **Amenities:** Many buildings connect directly to the sewers, and the Dungsweepers' Guild collects waste where they do not and keeps the streets clean at every hour. Public wells and fountains supply clean water. *Continual flame* lights the signs and streetlamps of the wealthier streets, and the Guild of Chandlers and Lamplighters lights the rest except in the Field Ward and the most dangerous parts of the Dock Ward. Oghma's priests at the Font of Knowledge teach reading free, and more than thirty publishers print broadsheets.
- **Getting about:** The High Road and the Way of the Dragon meet at the Waymoot in the south and again in the Trades Ward, where the city is at its narrowest. The joined road then splits into the High Road north and Waterdeep Way west toward the Palace. Drays run the main boulevards for 2 to 4 nibs, hire-coaches seldom cost more than a half-dozen shards, and City Watch traffic wardens signal with blue and yellow flags. The Scriveners', Scribes', and Clerks' Guild labels the streets on the corner of each building, about a dozen feet above the ground.

### The Wards

Waterdeep has six official wards, and a visitor changes ward by crossing a street. Ahghairon founded four of them in 1035 DR, the Castle, Trades, Dock and Southern Wards, and the wiki calls the Southern Ward the Temple Ward. The Sea Ward and the North Ward became official wards in 1276 DR (wiki). Each ward has its own legends and traditions, and the wards compete in nearly every race and parade.

- **Colors and mascots:** The Castle Ward flies blue and purple with a gold griffon, the Sea Ward blue and gold with a sea lion, and the North Ward green and orange with a white dove. The Trades Ward flies green and purple with a mimic that is a different object every four years and is a tankard now. The Southern Ward flies red and white with a mule, and the Dock Ward burgundy and orange with a green swordfish. No source gives the Field Ward colors or a mascot.
- **Telling the wards apart:** The Honorable Knight stands nine stories tall in a block between Snail Street and the Way of the Dragon, near the place where four wards meet. If it stands south and west of you, you are in the Trades Ward. North and west puts you in the Southern Ward, south and east in the Castle Ward, and north and east in the Dock Ward.
- **Outside the wards:** The Field Ward lies between the two northernmost walls, and Undercliff is the rural farmland east of the city. The adventure files both under "Outside the City Proper" and says neither is an official ward, so neither pays the monthly tax and neither has the protection of the Watch or Guild Law.
- **The City of the Dead:** The adventure gives the walled cemetery its own entry and names no ward for it, and this page treats it as apart from the wards. The wiki calls it both a cemetery and a ward.

### Walls, Gates and Harbors

- **Walls:** The wiki says strong walls close the city on its landward sides and Mount Waterdeep protects the seaward side (*City of Splendors* boxed set, 2e). The ward pages place the Trollwall along the northern and eastern edges. The wiki adds that each ward once had its own walls, that most came down as the city grew, and that only the walls of the City of the Dead remain (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep* 3e). At the Cliffwatch the cliff is steep enough that the city wall breaks on either side of it.
- **Gates:** The City Guard keeps posts on the roads to the city, at Zundbridge on the Trade Way from the south and at the town of Rassalantar on the Long Road from the north. The gates are shut at night and in winter. The smaller West Gate opens onto the Mud Flats, where locals register with the Guard and no new arrivals are accepted, and the Waymoot signpost points toward the harbor and each city gate. A large caravan or ship registers with a magister at the gate or the harbor, and a small party registers after a tenday.
- **The air:** The Griffon Cavalry meets any flier over the city, and only licensed riders and mounts may fly over Waterdeep.
- **The harbor:** The adventure's ward text calls the water the Great Harbor, and the Castle Ward boundary and the Faire's pages call it Deepwater Harbor. The Dock Ward holds the docks, shipyards and warehouses, the City Navy's docks are in the Castle Ward, and the Naval Harbor lies near Mirt's mansion at the far end of the mountain. Mistshore is the burned harbor neighborhood on the north shore. The harbor magister registers arriving ships, and the wiki says merfolk keep the peace in their own city beneath the water (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep* 3e). The dragonward does not reach the harbor.

### The Market, the Sewers and Undermountain

- **The Market:** The Market in the Castle Ward is the largest market square of any city in the North. Six boulevards run north from Waterdeep Way to the great road around it, and five more continue beyond. The Cynosure, built as a temple to Ao and now rented for events, stands at its edge, and two walking statues stand near it. See **Castle Ward**.
- **The sewers:** The dragonward bars dragons from the sewers as it does from the city. Watch captains hold the keys to the sewer access points of their wards, and the Field Ward has no sewers at all. Two routes matter to the campaign, the Dock Ward grate that leads to **Xanathar Sewer Hideout** and the stair in the Castle Ward sewers that leads to the lair.
- **Undermountain:** The largest and deepest dungeon in the world lies beneath the plateau, and the well in the Yawning Portal is its best-known entrance. **Undermountain and Skullport** holds the levels, the outlaw port below and Halaster.

### Government and Law

- **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords:** Ahghairon founded the system in 1032 DR after he turned the warlord Raurlor's sword into a serpent and took the warlord's throne. He sat openly as lord while a council of near-equal lords ruled beside him, and their names are hidden even from one another. **Laeral Silverhand** holds the office now, since the other Lords voted **Dagult Neverember** out. A petitioner who is invited meets the Open Lord or the Masked Lords in the audience chamber of the Palace of Waterdeep, where Ahghairon's throne stands. Castle Waterdeep is a redoubt of last defense, the Palace is the seat of government, and no source gives the Masked Lords a seat or an office of their own. See **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords**.
- **Nobility:** A noble house has the right to retain up to seventy equipped soldiers in its colors, and everyone else, foreign envoys included, may retain sixteen. The wiki counts seventy-six noble families, most of them tracing their lines back to before the city's founding (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep* 3e).
- **The law:** Waterdeep's law is a library of custom and precedent, and the Code Legal holds the main body of it. Magisters wear black robes, travel with a City Guard escort and may pass sentence without a trial. Any Masked Lord can overturn a magister's ruling, though one is rarely nearby. See **The Code Legal and the Magisters**.
- **Public order:** No law forbids carrying weapons, but Waterdhavians go about unarmed, and dueling and assault are illegal. Everyone caught brawling is arrested and judged, whoever started it. Executions take place behind the walls of Castle Waterdeep and floggings in the nearest watch post. People convicted of minor offenses work off their debt at Amendsfarm, a prison farm in Undercliff.

### The Watch and the Guard

- **The City Watch:** The Watch is the police force. Its officers wear green-and-goldenrod doublets and tall steel helmets, and a green-and-gold lantern lit by *continual flame* marks each watch post even by day. A pair of operatives walks the Castle Ward and squads of eight to twelve walk the Dock Ward. The Watch does not patrol the Field Ward. The Commander of the Watch reports to the Open Lord. The adventure names no Watch headquarters, and **The City Watch** records the wiki's reading.
- **The City Guard:** Volo's mnemonic says the Guard guards the walls while the Watch watches all. The Guard is the city's army. It patrols the roads, watches the walls, gates and harbor, guards civic buildings, protects magisters and collects the monthly tax. Its Griffon Cavalry flies from Peaktop Aerie on Mount Waterdeep, and the City Navy docks in the Great Harbor.
- **The Watchful Order and Force Grey:** An arcane caster who stays in the city must register and is urged to join the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors, which the Blackstaff, **Vajra Safahr**, heads. The Open Lord can send Force Grey against threats that the Watch and the Guard cannot handle, and does so as a last resort.

### The Guilds

- **Scope:** More than forty guilds exist, and the adventure's list prints forty-four names. Almost every Waterdhavian belongs to one or works for someone who does. The Trades Ward holds the most guildhalls. The Dock Ward holds halls for the sea trades, and the Castle Ward holds the Map House of the Surveyors', Map- and Chart-makers' Guild.
- **Guild Law:** It sits outside the legal code, though the Watch and the magisters take a guild's traditions seriously. Joining is optional, but a non-member finds that guild members refuse their coin, which covers nearly everyone who sells the necessities of life. No thieves' guild is active in the city, and Xanathar's Guild is a faction with no guild standing.
- **Guild days:** Guildsmeet falls on Tarsakh 7, Leiruin on Tarsakh 10 and Guildhall Day on Kythorn 14. See **The Guilds**.

### Religion and Festivals

- **Temples:** Waterdeep honors many gods and no one temple speaks for the city. The Major Temples table in the adventure lists fifteen temples. Two stand in the North Ward, five in the Castle Ward (the Temple of the Seldarine, the Spires of the Morning, the Font of Knowledge, Tymora's Tower of Luck and the Halls of Justice) and six in the Sea Ward. Helm's Hall stands in the Southern Ward, and Umberlee's Queenspire stands on the beach. The table has no row for the Trades Ward, the Dock Ward or the Field Ward. The wiki lists Deneir, Mystra, Oghma and Tempus as the city's faiths. See **The Deities** for the temples table, with **Tyr**, **Tymora** and **Selûne** for the gods of the Halls of Justice, the Tower of Luck and the Tower of the Moon.
- **Pilgrims:** Pilgrims worship Mystra at her temple and pay homage to Kelemvor in the City of the Dead, where he has no temple.
- **Festivals:** The city keeps the Calendar of Harptos and fills it with public holidays. Fleetswake runs from Ches 21 to Ches 30, Waukeentide from Tarsakh 1 to Tarsakh 10, and Founders' Day falls on Flamerule 1, the party's real deadline. The Day of Wonders on Marpenoth 3 falls after the campaign window. The Plowing and Running sends ward racers to the finals at the Field of Triumph on Mirtul 9. See **Timekeeping and the Festival Calendar**.

### The Dragonward and the Walking Statues

- **The dragonward:** Dragons and other creatures of the dragon type are physically unable to enter Waterdeep or its sewers unless the dragonstaff of Ahghairon has touched them. The Open Lord's permission plays no part.
- **The statues:** Eight walking statues stand about the city, six of them in the Castle Ward. The Honorable Knight stands in the Trades Ward by the wiki's reading, and the Swordmaiden lies in pieces across the North Ward and cannot be animated. Only the original statue is 90 feet tall, and only the Griffon has moved since the 1385 DR rampage, when it woke in 1479 DR. See **The Dragonward and Walking Statues**.

## Overview

Waterdeep, the City of Splendors, is the grandest and most civilized city on the Sword Coast. Open Lord Laeral Silverhand rules in public beside the secret Masked Lords, and the City Watch polices the streets. Six wards fill the city, with the City of the Dead and the Field Ward beside them and Undermountain below. Ahghairon's dragonward keeps dragons out, and festivals such as Fleetswake and Founders' Day fill the calendar.
