**Purpose:** Lets the GM run the City of the Dead as a public park by day and a patrolled graveyard by night, and keeps the Brandath Mausoleum and the Brandath Crypts apart so that no scene calls the building the approach or the approach the building.

The City of the Dead is Waterdeep's cemetery, a walled park of grassy hills, flower beds and gravel paths where the city entombs its dead and honors them with sculpture. Dragon Heist calls it an open-air museum, and Waterdhavians treat it as the city's picnic ground until the gates close at dusk.

The campaign uses it in two ways. By day it is a public place where contacts meet on a bench or beside a statue, and by night it is a graveyard that Sir Ambrose Everdawn patrols and the party enters over a wall. Its northern section, by the campaign's account, also holds the Brandath Mausoleum, which the **Vault of Dragons** quest reaches by an approach called the Brandath Crypts.

### Place and Walls

- **Where it lies:** Dragon Heist says the City of the Dead lies opposite the northern ridge of Mount Waterdeep, which descends to the Field of Triumph. It also says the city is at its narrowest in the heart of the Trades Ward, bounded by Castle Waterdeep on its spur of the mountain and by the walls of the City of the Dead.
- **Its edges:** The wiki says a low wall went up in 1064 DR and set the boundaries north and east of the Trades Ward and south of the North Ward. Higher walls and gates followed, and the gates open to the public only in daylight (wiki, *City of Splendors* 2e).
- **Its gates:** The wiki names three public gates, the Andamarr Gate, the Coffinsmarch Gate and the Mhalsyymber Gate, and a hidden private one, the Dead End Gate, which the Carver family uses for burials and maintenance. The campaign's scenes use a south gate, a main gate with the Poplar Walk just inside it, and a central gate path. No source says which wiki gate is which, so the page does not match them.
- **Its ward:** The adventure gives the City of the Dead its own entry beside the ward entries and names no ward for it. The campaign's old outline lists it as a walled necropolis apart from the wards, and this page does the same. The wiki calls it both a cemetery and a ward.
- **Its size and residents:** No source gives an area, a population or a languages line, and nobody lives inside the walls. `[research]`

### The Dead and Their Halls

- **How the city buries:** Dragon Heist says Waterdavians long ago stopped burying their dead and entomb them in mausoleums instead. For centuries the major mausoleums have each been connected to an extradimensional space where the dead are taken, mourned and interred.
- **When that began:** The wiki dates the portals to 1250 DR, when Ahghairon and other mages of Waterdeep opened them from tombs to uninhabited demiplanes to relieve overcrowding. The Alexandrian gives the same year for the start of public mausoleums (wiki, *City of Splendors* 2e).
- **Who is buried here:** Most noble houses, by the wiki's count fewer than half, keep no tomb here and use crypts under their villas or family estates outside the city. Families who can afford it mark their dead with sculpture, and nobles and wealthy merchants have competed to raise the grandest markers.
- **Where the poor went:** Before the portals, the wiki says, some who could not afford a plot were buried upright beneath the cobbles of an alley, until the dead began to rise and the practice stopped. Asmagh's Alley in the **Castle Ward** carries a similar folk rhyme, and no source ties the two together.
- **No temples:** The wiki says the Lords forbade any place of worship here, so that no faith could claim the ground as sacred and charge fees for burial there (wiki, *City of Splendors* boxed set). Kelemvor therefore has no temple or shrine in the grounds, although pilgrims pay him homage here. See **Kelemvor**.
- **Damage:** The wiki says the cemetery suffered extensive damage in the Spellplague.

### By Day

- **Opening hours:** The grounds are open to the public from dawn until dusk. Gardeners rake the gravel between the mausoleums, and families lay flowers at the feet of marble statues.
- **What people do:** Dragon Heist tells visitors to pack a midday feast, picnic and stroll. The wiki adds that Waterdhavians also run, duel, court, play and perform here (wiki, citing a Realmslore column).
- **Ahghairon's Day:** On Eleasis 1 commoners leave violets on Ahghairon's Statue. The festival falls after the campaign window.
- **Faction use:** Doom Raiders contacts meet just inside the main gate, and Istrid Horn chose the open hour at the statue because a public park has too many witnesses for anyone to try anything. The Emerald Enclave's informant network includes gardeners who work here, and Sorrel Hartwick, a druid in the network, has tended the margins of the grounds for twenty years.

### By Night

- **Closure:** The City of the Dead is closed at night, with two City Guard soldiers stationed at each entry gate. A character who slips over a wall unseen needs a successful DC 15 Dexterity (Stealth) check.
- **Driftglobes:** At dusk hundreds of driftglobes leave the inhabited city and gather among the mausoleums, and they return to the waking city at dawn. Dragon Heist says no one knows why, and the campaign gives no answer.
- **Sir Ambrose:** He patrols from sunset to sunrise and chases off grave robbers. A character who creeps around in the dark has a 30 percent chance of meeting him, and he escorts trespassers out and alerts the City Guard only if they refuse to leave.
- **Permits:** A Force Grey member who has reached Senior Griffon can ask Vajra Safahr for a written authorization to enter at night. It is given once per tenday, never while a villain faction is at Lockdown, and it does not cover a crime committed inside.
- **Who else is here:** The wiki says people use the grounds after dark for nefarious ends, and the campaign's outline calls the place patrolled but not well staffed. No source gives a patrol strength beyond the gate guards and Sir Ambrose.

### The Keepers

**Sir Ambrose Everdawn (Lawful Neutral, Tethyrian Human, he/him)** is the senior Kelemvorite patrol knight. He is about sixty, has walked these grounds for fifteen years, and wears a silver gauntlet pin at his collar. He knows every family plot and burial custom and expects the living to treat the grounds with respect. He speaks in full sentences without contractions and calls Kelemvor "the Judge".

- **Alone in the dark:** He cannot watch the whole cemetery, so in **Ten Nights in the City of the Dead** he takes the south and asks the Enclave to cover the north. He draws his blade only in defense of the dead or the living, in that order.
- **What he shares:** He does not offer what he knows of the Brandath crypts unasked. He tells a party who gives him a reason, and the mission's last dawn has him name them unasked if the party reported the pattern.
- **The Carver family:** The wiki says the Carvers are coffin-builders and stone carvers who live at Dead End House, which stands outside the walls and in no ward, and that they conduct burials and maintain the tombs. No campaign file uses them.
- **The towers:** The wiki says a City Watch torchtower stood at the cemetery's southeastern corner and another at Watchway Tower, both part of the Trollwall.

### Landmarks

**Warriors' Monument** — Fountain sculpture
- A sixty-foot circle of men and women strikes down trolls, orcs, hobgoblins, bugbears and barbarians, who fall backward and outward around them. Above them a griffon rider spears a skeletal knight whose breastplate bears the symbol of Myrkul, and the wounds of every figure gush water.
- The wiki says the monument honors everyone who fought to defend the city and that the Myrkul figure was added in 1361 DR to commemorate those who died against his invasion (wiki, *City of Splendors* 2e). Dragon Heist does not say what the monument commemorates.
- **Hook:** Waterdhavians picnic around it. No quest uses it, and **Myrkul** carries the god's side of the story.

**Ahghairon's Statue** — Monument
- A tall marble statue of a bearded, robed wizard stands on concentric steps and faces west toward the skyline, his hands outstretched and a broad smile on his face. The wiki puts it in the central section near the entryway and says torches lit the steps at night.
- Dragon Heist adds a female dwarf in plate armor standing at its foot, and does not say whether she is carved or living.
- **Hook:** Istrid Horn asks the party to meet her here after **Gralhund Villa**. The statue is the site for her request to be hidden for a tenday.

**The Poplar Walk bench** — Meeting place
- Two rows of tall narrow trees line a gravel walk just inside the main gate, and a stone bench sits in the shade at the foot of it.
- **Hook:** In **Davil's Arrest** Tashlyn Yafeera meets the Doom Raiders members here on Ches 28, an hour before dusk, and Yagra Stonefist waits under the last poplar. **Wolf** brings the same meeting back at the same place. The design notes call the bench a small invented detail so the meeting has somewhere to happen.

**The Brandath Mausoleum** — Family tomb
- The crumbling stone mausoleum is sealed, and the name BRANDATH is etched above its entrance. Elms and birches grow around it, and the largest tree by the main entrance is a treant.
- The treant wakes as anyone approaches and growls, "Only those of Brandath blood are welcome here! Begone!" If someone without Brandath blood tries to open the mausoleum, it animates two nearby trees and attacks. It lets **Renaer Neverember**, who has Brandath blood, and his companions pass, and tells him in passing that his mother was a lovely person.
- The Brandath family is an old Waterdhavian family. Lord Dagult Neverember married into it, and Lady Alethea Brandath was Renaer's mother.
- **Hook:** The mausoleum is the destination of **Vault of Dragons** Scene 2. The Gamemaster's Notes below set out the building, the approach and the way the two names are used.

## Overview

The City of the Dead is Waterdeep's cemetery, a walled park of grassy hills, flower beds and gravel paths. Waterdhavians entomb their dead in mausoleums, and the major ones open onto extradimensional spaces where the dead are laid to rest. The gates close at dusk, when hundreds of driftglobes drift in for reasons no one knows. Sir Ambrose Everdawn, a knight of Kelemvor, patrols the grounds until sunrise and escorts out anyone he finds.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Secret
> - **What lies below:** The Vault of Dragons lies beneath the Brandath family mausoleum, and the Stone of Golorr names it when the party attunes to the Stone. Neverember found the vault while preparing his wife Alethea's interment and used it to hide his embezzled gold. See **The Vault of Dragons and Neverember's Embezzlement**.
> - **The Eye:** Neverember hid one of the Stone's three Eyes in a crypt near Alethea's tomb. The Cassalanters found it there through their infernal divination and took it before the campaign opens. It is Eye #2, and they hold it.
> - **The walking dead:** No necromancer is raising the skeletons in **Ten Nights in the City of the Dead**. The vault presses against its wards, and the dead nearest the Brandath grove walk toward it. Ambrose feels the wrongness and does not know its cause.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **The two names:** The page uses **Brandath Mausoleum** for the family's building, with the name over its door, and **Brandath Crypts** for the approach, meaning the complex of mausoleums, Lady Alethea's Tomb and the Old Crypts beneath them down to the vault door. Dragon Heist keys one mausoleum with a ground level and an underground crypt and never writes "Brandath Crypts". The Alexandrian builds the complex as mausoleums A to F around a Grand Crypt, titles its map "The Brandath Crypts", and carves BR_N__TH CRY_TS in Dethek letters above the First Crypt. The campaign's Scene 2 is titled The Brandath Crypts.
> - **Doors:** The stone doors of the mausoleums are locked unless an encounter says otherwise. A character picks a lock with a successful DC 15 Dexterity check using thieves' tools, or forces a door with a successful DC 25 Strength (Athletics) check.
> - **The approach:** The route runs through the complex to Lady Alethea's Tomb, where a successful DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check finds a false south wall hiding a stair to the Old Crypts. At the end of the old passage an illusion hides an intact archway, and beyond it a ten-foot-wide passage slopes down to the vault doors. The keyed areas and the vault door belong to **Vault of Dragons**.
> - **Ambrose at the Crypts:** If the party enters after dark, it meets him. A plausible cover story, such as a Brandath-blood companion, a Watch escort or a writ from Laeral, earns a warning to be careful and quick. If the party enters by day, he does not appear, because his patrol is nocturnal. A party that marked **Brandath Crypts Visited** is waved through.
> - **How the party learns the way:** Any one of these suffices. The windmill map from **Faction Outposts**, Esvele Rosznar's tunnels from **Cassalanter Villa**, Kalain's letter and Dagult's papers from **Fireball!**, Manshoon's Report from **Kolat Towers** (E9), or the Stone's Full Awakening. Brimel's pencilled note in **The Theater's Back Room** points to the same map.
> - **The gate on the last day:** Savra Belabranta holds the main gate for a Whitehawk member, Ziraj watches the approach from the rooftops if he survived **Ziraj's Last Hunt**, and a Doom Raiders crew with four wagons waits at the gate in **Dread Lord**. Dagult Neverember arrives at the gate afterward with hired muscle and Watch loyalists (see **The Open Lord's Fall**).
> - **The Black Viper:** The Notable Figures page says her Viper Den sits above the Brandath Crypts and that she has mapped the crypt access for three months. She cannot open the vault and wants whatever is portable when someone else does.

**Tone:** Green, still and orderly. Treat the grounds as a place Waterdhavians love and picnic in, and let the unease come from Sir Ambrose's patrol, the driftglobes at dusk and the treant at the Brandath door.
