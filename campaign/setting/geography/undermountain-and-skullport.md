# Undermountain and Skullport

**Purpose:** Lets the GM say how deep Undermountain goes, how the party gets down, where Skullport sits and who holds it, and what Halaster is doing, without inventing a level, a route or a Mad Mage scene that no source gives.

Undermountain is the largest and deepest dungeon in the world, a maze of halls, tunnels and caverns beneath the city and Mount Waterdeep. Dragon Heist says it plunges as many as twenty levels, and Dungeon of the Mad Mage maps twenty-three. The Melairkyn dwarves first dug the tunnels, the drow are said to have dug up from below, and the mad wizard Halaster Blackcloak and his apprentices claimed and enlarged all of it.

Skullport is a grim, cutthroat town in a cavern on the River Sargauth, connected to Undermountain's third level. Xanathar's Guild holds it, and the campaign places it outside the city's jurisdiction. Few people from the surface know the way there, and the people who live there prefer it like that.

The best-known way down is the well in the Yawning Portal, a tavern in the Castle Ward, and the dragonward does not reach below it. The campaign treats Undermountain as a presence under the Dragon Heist quests, with a few threads that run down into the dungeon and a few that come up from it.

### The Dungeon

- **Builders:** The Melairkyn dwarves excavated the first tunnels, called the Underhalls, around a mithral mine beneath Mount Waterdeep. By the time Halaster broke into them the clan had been killed or dispersed, and warring duergar and drow had settled in the ruins.
- **Halaster's work:** Halaster came to the foot of Mount Waterdeep more than a thousand years ago with seven apprentices and built a tower there. He fought the drow and the duergar, tunneled "ever downward", and filled the dungeon with prisoners, servants and guardians that he collected from other planes. Some say he came from the Cradlelands, others place him among the early wizards of Netheril, and no one has settled the question.
- **The city above:** Waterdeep grew up around the ruins of his tower. Early settlers sent criminals into Undermountain as punishment, until an adventurer named Durnan climbed out alive with riches and tales, tore down what was left of the tower and built the Yawning Portal over the well.
- **The depth:** Dragon Heist says the dungeon plunges as many as twenty levels. Dungeon of the Mad Mage keys twenty-three, from Level 1 (the Dungeon Level) to Level 23 (the Mad Wizard's Lair).
- **The allure:** Adventurers still go down, and some come back with treasure. The taproom treats it as sport, and patrons place bets on who returns.

### The Well at the Yawning Portal

- **The shaft:** A well 40 feet across stands in the middle of the taproom and drops 140 feet to a dark room on Level 1. Its walls are sheer, old mortared stone. Dragon Heist calls it the outer shell of a sunken stone tower, and Dungeon of the Mad Mage says the tower was Halaster's.
- **The descent:** A winch with a rope-and-pulley mechanism hangs beside the well. **Durnan** works it himself and lowers one adventurer at a time. A round trip takes 10 rounds, and the rope is stained with old blood.
- **The price:** Durnan charges 1 gp each to descend, whether the adventurer uses the rope or not. The return trip costs another 1 gp, sent up in a bucket in advance.
- **The climb:** The walls are crumbly and have plenty of handholds and footholds. A character who scales them without climbing gear succeeds on a DC 15 Strength (Athletics) check. Riding the rope needs no check.
- **Where it stands:** The Yawning Portal is a Castle Ward landmark, and the **Castle Ward** page holds its entry.

### Other Ways Down

- **The Castle Ward sewers:** A spiral staircase behind a secret door in the sewers leads to Xanathar's lair. Dragon Heist says it bypasses a harder route through Undermountain and Skullport, which is why most parties never walk that route.
- **The tunnel from the lair:** The lair connects to Skullport by a long tunnel. A magically lit passage runs 300 feet east off the lair's map to a staircase that climbs 20 feet to the Guts & Garters Inn in Skullport.
- **The river and five passages:** The River Sargauth joins Level 3 to Skullport, and so do five winding passageways: Beggar's Rest Pass, Shadow Pass, Steamfall Pass, Taglath's Gap and Whisperhaunt Pass.
- **The sea caves:** A chain of natural caverns and magic locks runs from the South Sea Caves on Waterdeep's shore to the Sargauth far below the mountain, and vessels descend hundreds of feet as the locks lower them. The outer caves hold dozens of wrecks, home to sea hags and merrows. A ruined ten-story hoist once lifted ships over a retaining wall to the river 100 feet below, and crews now haul rowboats and other small craft over it with their own ropes. Skullport lies a few hundred yards farther along the river.

### What the Dungeon Does to Magic

- **Travel:** No spell other than *wish* can be used to enter Undermountain, leave it or move from one level to another. *Astral projection*, *teleport*, *plane shift*, *word of recall* and similar spells fail, and so do effects that banish a creature to another plane. Magic items with those properties fail too.
- **The exception:** *Etherealness* and other magic that reaches the Border Ethereal still works, but a creature that enters the Border Ethereal from Undermountain is pulled back into the dungeon when it leaves.
- **What still works:** Magic that summons creatures or objects from other planes works normally, and so does magic that uses an extradimensional space. Characters who receive spells from deities or otherworldly patrons keep receiving them, and spells that contact beings on other planes function normally.
- **The walls:** Spells cannot destroy or reshape Undermountain's ceilings, pillars, columns, walls or floors, so *earthquake* causes no collapse. Doors and furnishings are not protected.
- **Sending:** Halaster cannot be reached by a *sending* spell inside the dungeon. The spell is redirected to his nothic secretary, which answers in Undercommon with a string of expletives.
- **The dragonward:** The ward "doesn't extend to the harbor or into Undermountain," so a dragon in the dungeon is outside it. See **The Dragonward and Walking Statues**.

### Halaster Blackcloak

- **Who he is:** Halaster's true form is a tall, gaunt human male, though he uses magic to take many other shapes. In any form he giggles and mutters constantly, and Dungeon of the Mad Mage notes that he is nonetheless alert and attentive to everyone near him.
- **Is he alive:** Yes. Dragon Heist says he and his apprentices "are believed to dwell in the dungeons to this day," and Dungeon of the Mad Mage writes him in the present tense.
- **What he does:** Most often he watches adventurers from a distance with amusement. Sometimes he appears to them as a spectral eye or another magical force. The adventure gives him several possible goals and says his goal can change at any time. One of them is a search for new apprentices, and he is cultivating talent on Level 9 while paying close attention to other arcane spellcasters who enter the dungeon.
- **His enemies:** Dragon Heist lists him among Manshoon's powerful enemies. The Manshoon who now lives in Waterdeep hid in Undermountain, ran afoul of Halaster, and lost his left arm in a brief spell duel. Dungeon of the Mad Mage keeps that arm on Level 9 as an undead guardian that attacks every intruder until Halaster, or a creature that looks like him, waves it off.

### Skullport

- **The town:** Skullport fills an immense cavern. It has three levels of its own, a lower, a middle and a top level, which have nothing to do with Undermountain's levels. A stone bridge joins the southern end of the town to Skull Island, where the River Sargauth flows around a fortress garrisoned by Xanathar's minions.
- **Skull Island:** Its half-ogre commander, Sundeth, lives in a hollowed-out spire with his wyvern mount. The island has two natural harbors, each 60 feet deep and lined with rotted docks. Steel augers at each harbor mouth can be raised on chains to pierce the hulls of passing ships.
- **Who lives there:** Few people have permanent homes in Skullport, and most local businesses are shut. The ones still open pay Xanathar for protection. Adventurers can find food, drink, shelter and supplies, or a quick death if they antagonize the locals.
- **The flameskulls:** Thirteen flameskulls haunt the town and have defended it since its founding. They are all that remains of the Sargauth Enclave, a settlement of Netherese wizards, and they consider themselves the true rulers. Their minds drift between the present and the past, and they rarely speak, often in a dead language.
- **Prices and light:** The town has no general store, and prices run five times higher than normal. Most of the lanterns are gone, so visitors carry their own light or rely on darkvision, and the flameskulls drifting through the streets look like bobbing torches from a distance.
- **The Guild's hold:** Xanathar's Guild members haunt the dilapidated buildings. Xanathar itself lives in a dungeon complex under the town, which Dragon Heist describes. The Guild controls access to Skullport's smuggling networks and gladiatorial circuits.
- **Jurisdiction (campaign only):** The campaign places Skullport outside the city's jurisdiction. No published text says so. The only published limit on the city's reach is the dragonward line quoted above.
- **History:** The town's founding and its chain of masters are on **The Long Shadow**.

### Who Holds What Below

- **Xanathar's Guild:** The Guild holds Skullport and "is trying to secure the uppermost levels of Undermountain." On Level 2 it keeps two watch posts, led by a drow named Shunn "Spider Eyes" Shurreth in the north and a human berserker named Nadia the Unbent in the south.
- **Intellect devourers:** **Xanathar** is served by **Nihiloor**, which spends its days making intellect devourers and releasing them in the sewers. Dragon Heist says an intellect devourer in league with Xanathar ate **Meloon Wardragon**'s brain several months ago, and that Meloon had joined a fledgling adventuring band on an expedition into Undermountain three months before the adventure. On Level 3 a mind flayer named Ulquess, Skullport's ambassador to Stromkuhldur, implants intellect devourers in goblinoids to make spies loyal to Xanathar.
- **The Zhentarim:** The adventure's Black Network is trying to drive the Guild out of Skullport. Rizzeryl and a gang of wererats from Skullport harass the Guild's forces on Level 2.
- **House Auvryndar:** A low-ranking drow house from Menzoberranzan is slowly and secretly consolidating its power in Undermountain. Its invasion of Level 3 is meant as a first step toward taking Skullport. Dragon Heist holds two of its drow in Xanathar's lair, Zaibon Kyszalt, captured in Skullport, and his superior Raelyn Auvryndar, and **Nihiloor** has learned of the house's plot to conquer the town.
- **The Harpers:** Dalagor's Fortress, a Harper hideout, stands on the uppermost level of Skullport, guarded by a dragonborn mage named Felrax. **Thorvin Twinbeard** serves the Harpers as a paid informant and holds secret meetings with Harper spies in Skullport.
- **The Lords' Alliance:** Joroth Brighthelm, a dwarf spy for the Lords' Alliance, visits the Yawning Portal after news of Kressando Rosznar's death reaches the broadsheets.

## Overview

Undermountain is the largest and deepest dungeon in the world, said to plunge as many as twenty levels beneath Waterdeep. Its best-known entrance is the Yawning Portal's well, and the mad wizard Halaster is believed to dwell below. Skullport, a grim smugglers' town in a vast cavern on the River Sargauth, connects to the dungeon's third level. Xanathar's Guild is said to rule it, outside the reach of the city's laws.

> #### GM Notes
> - **Answering a player who asks how deep it goes:** Say twenty levels, because that is the figure Waterdhavians repeat. The dungeon has twenty-three, and the GM can say so once a character has descended past the first few.
> - **Answering a player who asks who rules Skullport:** The Guild does, through its fortress on Skull Island. The thirteen flameskulls still consider themselves the true rulers, and the campaign puts the town outside the city's jurisdiction.
> - **The knot in the Weave (published):** Dungeon of the Mad Mage says Aelinthaldaar, the elven capital that stood where Waterdeep stands, was erased by a spell that left "a permanent knot in the Weave" deep underground. It breeds an obsession with the dungeon in anyone who lives near it too long. "Halaster and several of his apprentices fell prey to this obsession, as did many Melairkyn dwarves and Durnan, the proprietor of the Yawning Portal." No campaign page mentions the knot yet.
> - **Halaster takes notice (campaign only):** The Trollskull Alley design notes plant Undermountain threads that "accumulate below the surface until the Dragon Heist resolution makes Halaster's awareness of Waterdeep's events feel retrospectively obvious rather than bolted on." One of them is Durnan's message through Mattrim Mereg at the grand opening: "More activity than usual below. Nothing you need to worry about. Yet." Halaster himself appears after **Vault of Dragons**. In the first tenday after it, at Fame 15 or higher, a stranger in a battered traveling cloak nurses a drink at **Trollskull Manor**, and Lif will not serve them. The seat holds "a single copper piece, tails-up", with the word *Interesting* etched into it (`campaign/guides/trollskull-manor/01-overview.md:38`, `06-tavern-time.md:35`).
> - **Illuun on Level 4 (campaign only):** "Jeryth has held the dreaming presence back from the Castle Ward water table for months. It is Illuun, an aboleth on Undermountain Level 4, and the Stone of Golorr woke it." (`campaign/setting/notable-figures/emerald-enclave/02-jeryth-phaulkon.md:33`). The published adventure supports the aboleth: "Illuun plans to take over the entire level as a step toward gaining control of Undermountain and then Waterdeep" (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:11344`). No Enclave speaker knows its name. **Jeryth Phaulkon** has held it back for months in **The Dreamer's Reach**, and the Harper seer in **The Stone's Other Master** felt it answer when the Stone woke.
> - **Nihiloor and Level 17 (campaign only):** "Nihiloor was not in his domain at the time and escapes to Undermountain Level 17 with his three projects intact" (`campaign/structure/arc-f-xanathars-lair.md:111`). The published support is thin. Halaster worries that "the conflict between the githyanki on level 16 and the mind flayers on level 17 could spiral out of control" (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:569`), and Level 17 is Seadeeps. The Level 17 text itself was not checked.
> - **Joroth Brighthelm's team (campaign only):** Laeral tells a Lioncrown: "Six tendays ago I sent a man named Joroth Brighthelm into Undermountain. He took a second agent and three mapmakers, and the last dispatch came up from the third level. Nothing has reached me since." (`campaign/quests/faction-events/lords-alliance/r50-lioncrown/ev-01-lioncrown.md:313`). The same event says the dispatch named Level 10, Falkir's Fist and the Eye of the Spider, and Dungeon of the Mad Mage puts both on Level 10 (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:447`). The team's fate is left to the Mad Mage pass.
> - **Bregan D'aerthe's eyes (campaign only):** A drow woman at the Yawning Portal's balcony table "has been at that table every evening for two weeks, cataloguing who descends into Undermountain and who comes back" (`campaign/quests/act-i/finding-floon/ev-01-yawning-portal.md:29`). She is **Ryvarra**, and the Finding Floon design notes say that Jarlaxle has had eyes on the party "since their first night in the city" (`design-notes.md:11`). The company's Skullport drop in **Houseless Noble** is a ropewalk on the lower quay: "Xanathar's Guild holds the whole place, so say nothing of us in the street" (`campaign/quests/faction-events/bregan-daerthe/r50-houseless-noble/ev-01-houseless-noble.md:284`).
> - **Routes the campaign adds (campaign only):** A trapdoor under the kitchen rug at the Terasse Estate in the Castle Ward "leads to an underground dock and on to Skullport. Gladiators pass through on their way to Xanathar's lair" (`campaign/gazetteer/waterdeep/castle-ward.md:89`). The Emerald Enclave keeps three sewer routes in **Autumnreaver**. The event says it plainly: "Every route runs to **Level 1** of Undermountain and no deeper" (`emerald-enclave/r10-autumnreaver/ev-01-autumnreaver.md:267`). The routes start in the Sea Ward, the Southern Ward and the Dock Ward. The Dock Ward route ends at the Grell Hideout, where the grells of **The Grells in the Dock Ward** came from after the Guild's cargo tunnel broke through.
> - **Manshoon's arm (published, with campaign):** Dragon Heist says the Manshoon now in Waterdeep "escaped death by hiding out in Undermountain, where he eventually ran afoul of Halaster Blackcloak." The campaign keeps that account (`campaign/setting/history/04-the-founding-of-chains.md:47`) and has him flee Undermountain after the duel and take Kolat Towers as a base (`history/08-the-players-arrive.md:33`). The severed hand on Level 9 is the published proof.
> - **No help below (campaign only):** The campaign's faction contacts stop at the surface. "Sending works anywhere below, except to Halaster. Vajra casts the reserve above ground because she will not go below" (`force-grey/r50-force-grey-commander/ev-01-force-grey-commander.md:231`). **Vajra Safahr** matches the published rule that Halaster cannot be reached by *sending*. No spell carries a Lords' Alliance summons to a member below ground (`lords-alliance/r50-lioncrown/ev-01-lioncrown.md:16`), the Emerald Enclave's sprig does not work there (`emerald-enclave/r10-autumnreaver/ev-01-autumnreaver.md:134`), and the Veterans of **Warduke** "do not go down the Portal's well or into Undermountain" (`lords-alliance/r25-warduke/ev-01-warduke.md:115`).

**Tone:** Plain and local. Waterdhavians treat Undermountain as tavern talk and a wager, and Skullport as a rumor they have never checked, so voice both the way a regular repeats a story told to them by someone who came back.
