# The Guilds

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the institution entry for Waterdeep's guilds, and the last of the four governance and guilds pages. It covers what the guilds are, what Guild Law is and is not, how membership and dues work, and which guild functions the campaign has added to the published adventure.
> - **Where it is used:** read it before you answer a player who asks whether the party must join a guild, before you run the permit events for **Trollskull Manor**, and before you voice any guild representative.
> - **Featured in:** **Trollskull Alley** (The Guild Gauntlet), **Fireball!** (Kalain's Tower, where Neverember's letters to Hammond Kraddoc are found), **Cassalanter Villa** (the loan contracts in Victoro's office), and **Kolat Towers** (Jelenn Urmbrusk's votes in the Council of Guildmasters, which only the structure document carries).
> - **Faction events:** **Lords' Alliance First Meeting**, **The Long Watch**, **The Exile's Return**, **Stingblade**, **Three Nights** and **Harpers First Meeting**.
> - **Location journals:** **Trollskull Manor** (the kitchen, cellar and upper floors carry the guild inspections) and **Cassalanter Villa** (Victoro's office). The Map House, which is the Surveyors' guildhall, is described on **Castle Ward**.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` is for the GM only. The Overview is what an ordinary Waterdhavian knows, and it names no faction and no guild leader.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM say how the guilds work and what a guild can and cannot do to the party, and keeps the campaign's added guild functions apart from the published adventure and the wiki so that no later page invents a guild count, a power or an operative that no source gives.

The guilds of Waterdeep are the trade associations through which almost every Waterdhavian earns a living, and the published adventure counts more than forty of them. Virtually every profession has one, and hardly a citizen belongs to none or works for someone who belongs to none. The Guilds of Waterdeep list in the adventure prints forty-four names, from the Bakers' Guild to the Wheelwrights' Guild, and it includes the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors. Guild Law is not technically in the legal code of the city, but guilds appear in the oldest surviving legal documents, which Ahghairon wrote himself. By law no thieves' guild is active in Waterdeep. The founding of the guilds and the wars that followed are on **Ahghairon's Gifts**.

### Tenets

- **Every Trade Has a Guild:** The published adventure says no aspect of life in Waterdeep goes untouched by at least one guild. The wiki adds that the city had a guild for almost every trade, and that sages, thieves, bounty hunters and legal advocates were the exceptions (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e). The adventure confirms the last: no guild of solicitors exists.
- **Guild Law Stands Beside the Code Legal:** Guild Law sits outside the legal code, yet the rules are respected by wise city folk, and the City Watch and the magisters take a guild's laws as seriously as the guild does. Someone who flouts a guild's traditions can expect public scorn and a visit from enforcers of the law. The wiki adds that Guild Law could never override civic law (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e). See **The Code Legal and the Magisters**.
- **Joining Is Optional and Staying Out Costs Money:** Working at a guild trade without membership is not illegal, and a guild has no lawful recourse against someone who refuses to join. Word still spreads, and the coin of a non-member is refused by every guild member, which covers nearly everyone who sells the necessities of life or rents a bed. The adventure adds that guilds try to entice members and go out of their way to hinder independents, up to driving them out of business.
- **Guilds Judge Their Own:** Many guilds keep their own codes of accusation, trial and punishment. A Baker who sells bread in the wrong shape is drenched with water and coated in his own flour. Heckling a member of the Jesters' Guild brings public jeering from at least four guild members for four days. A ship that unloads without the Guild of Watermen has its cargo seized or thrown into the harbor.
- **Codes Entwine:** A job that touches several trades must pass through several guilds in order. The adventure's example is a new building, which needs the Surveyors', Map-, and Chart-makers' Guild for the plat and the plan, then the Cellarers' and Plumbers' Guild for the site, and only then the Carpenters', Roofers', and Plaisterers' Guild for the structure. Carvers, masons, smiths, glaziers and the plumbers again follow before the work counts as complete.
- **No Guild for Thieves:** The adventure says Xanathar's Guild is a faction and not an actual guild, and that despite its name it has no official guild status in Waterdeep. It appears on no guild list. See **Xanathar's Guild**.
- **No Monopolies (wiki):** The wiki says the laws of Waterdeep forbid a guild to restrict trade to its members, and that the Lords and the Black Robes enforced this strictly (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e). It also says a new guild needs the Lords' approval and that such applications were seldom granted, particularly when the trade had a guild of its own (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e).
- **The Open Lord Is Overmaster of the Guilds (wiki):** The wiki gives the Open Lord the title Overmaster of the Guilds, with the authority to override any Guild Law (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e). No published adventure line and no campaign file uses the title, so the campaign treats it as unconfirmed. `[research]` See **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords**.

### Operations

- **Joining:** A guild wants a background, a proficiency or a status it values, and the adventure's example is a character with the sailor background joining the Master Mariners' Guild. A character with the guild artisan background has free membership in one Waterdeep guild of the player's choice. Most people may belong to several guilds, and the wiki says only guildmasters are limited to one (wiki; The City of Splendors: A Waterdeep Novel).
- **Dues and expulsion:** The adventure suggests 1 gp per month or 10 gp for a year paid in advance, and says the real figure varies. A guild can expel a member for any number of reasons, and failing to pay dues is one of them. All taxes, fines and guild fees are paid in Waterdavian coin or in the currency of any Lords' Alliance member settlement.
- **The guild calendar:** Guildsmeet falls on Tarsakh 7, when members gather in their halls for new policies and a gala from dusk to dawn. Leiruin falls on Tarsakh 10, when members pay annual dues and guildmasters meet the Lords of Waterdeep to renew their charters. Guildhall Day falls on Kythorn 14, when most shops close for trade fairs and many guilds recruit. The wiki prints Kythorn 11 for Guildhall Day, and this page follows the adventure. See **Timekeeping and the Festival Calendar**.
- **Ranks (wiki):** A guild usually has a guildmaster, then a council of Elders, then a Speaker who deals with the public, then the members, then apprentices. A guild without a Speaker uses the guildmaster. Someone who is not a Lord or a noble has trouble getting an audience with a guildmaster and usually works through the Speaker (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e).
- **Guildhalls (wiki):** Every guild has a hall that serves as a meeting place, a showcase and an office, and visitors who are not members wait in a meeting room and may not wander. The Trades Ward holds the most halls, and the wiki counts thirteen there (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e). The wiki says the **City of the Dead** gives all the guilds a shared burial ground called Guildbones (wiki; The City of Splendors: A Waterdeep Novel). The campaign adds the Map House on the **Castle Ward**, which is the Surveyors' guildhall, and a haulers' guildhouse on the quay in the **Dock Ward** (campaign-only). See **Trades Ward**.
- **Where a visitor learns the rules:** The adventure tells a stranger to ask someone on duty at the guild headquarters or a senior guild member. A visitor who does business as anything other than a buyer is advised to hire a local solicitor, and the adventure says the solicitor should come highly recommended.
- **Services the guilds provide:** The Scriveners', Scribes', and Clerks' Guild is the reason Waterdeep labels its streets, often on the corner of a building about a dozen feet above the ground. A member of the Guild of Trusted Pewterers and Casters or of the Jewelers' Guild changes foreign coin faster than the Palace exchequers, with reliable scales and guild-wide rates. The Guild of Chandlers and Lamplighters repairs the Dock Ward streetlamps at the start of each season, and the adventure calls the effort halfhearted. The Dungsweepers' Guild does not serve the Field Ward.
- **The Watchful Order:** The adventure lists the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors among the guilds, and arcane spellcasters who live in the city must register and are strongly encouraged to join. The Blackstaff heads the Order, and its members act as temporary members of the City Watch or City Guard and help after fires and other disasters when called. The wiki says Waterdhavians do not think of every group on its broad guild list as a guild (wiki; City of Splendors: Waterdeep, 3e). See **The City Watch**.
- **Trollskull Manor permits (campaign-only):** The **Trollskull Manor** guide's Operating Costs page sets six recurring guild fees at 10 gp a tenday and says they are "Non-negotiable in ordinary business. Missing a payment escalates: formal notice → supply disruption → license review." The six are the Fellowship of Innkeepers (operating license, 3 gp), the Launderers' Guild (linen service, 2 gp), the Dungsweepers' Guild (street cleanup, 1 gp), the Loyal Order of Street Laborers (exterior upkeep, 1 gp), the Watchful Order (fire-suppression ward, 2 gp) and the Stablemasters' and Farriers' Guild (courtyard access, 1 gp). The Fame table adds that each tenday of unpaid guild fees costs 2 Fame, and that at Condemned "Guild license proceedings begin."
- **The Guild Gauntlet (campaign-only):** In **Trollskull Alley**, Broxley Fairkettle and Hammond Kraddoc give the party a checklist, which reads "Required guilds: Carpenters', Roofers', and Plaisterers' Guild (structural work); Cellarers' and Plumbers' Guild (basement and plumbing); Guild of Butchers (meat supply); Bakers' Guild (bread and pastries); Launderers' Guild (linens); Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors (fire suppression ward)." Each needs a site inspection and a fee.

### Key Members

**Broxley Fairkettle** (Lawful Good, Strongheart Halfling, he/him) is the Fellowship of Innkeepers representative for the North Ward and a father of nine. He calls on the party's tavern often to push for guild registration, because he fears the haunted building will bring trouble to the alley. The published adventure has him ask the party to host the Deadwinter Day feast, which the Fellowship arranges with the Bakers', Butchers' and Vintners' guilds.

**Hammond Kraddoc** (Neutral, Illuskan Human, he/him) represents the Vintners', Distillers', and Brewers' Guild and wants to keep its hold on the North Ward's spirit trade. He arrives with his scribe Jinny, a young tiefling, to inspect the party's tavern, and he keeps visiting after the inspections end because he has nobody else to drink with.

**Vajra Safahr** (Neutral, Tethyrian Human, she/her) is the Blackstaff, and the published adventure says the Watchful Order is headed by the Blackstaff. Her Notable Figures page does not mention the Order. **Rishaal the Page-Turner** (Neutral, Gold Dragonborn, he/him) and **Ulkoria Stonemarrow** (Neutral Good, Shield Dwarf, she/her) are Order members in Trollskull Alley, and in The Guild Gauntlet either of them can sign the written statement that the poltergeist Lif poses no public danger.

**Embric** (Neutral Good, Fire Genasi, he/him) and **Avi** (Neutral Good, Water Genasi, she/her) co-own the forge Steam and Steel. Embric belongs to the Most Careful Order of Skilled Smiths and Metalforgers, and Avi belongs to the Splendid Order of Armorers, Locksmiths, and Finesmiths. **Fala Lefaliir** (Chaotic Good, Wood Elf, they/them) is an herbalist and a member of the Guild of Apothecaries and Physicians.

**Emmek Frewn** (Neutral Evil, Illuskan Human, he/him) runs Frewn's Brews, and the published adventure says he is in trouble with two guilds. He fixed his own roof against the rules of the Carpenters', Roofers', and Plaisterers' Guild, and he insulted a dwarf of the Cellarers' and Plumbers' Guild by comparing the dwarf's beard to barnacles. In the campaign, his early sabotage imposes a -2 penalty on the guild inspection DCs for the first week of The Guild Gauntlet (campaign-only).

**Fenerus Stormcastle** (Neutral Evil, Human, he/him) founded the Blood Hawk Posse, was drummed out of the gang after a botched raid, and then joined the Guild of Chandlers and Lamplighters in Waterdeep.

Several more names have no Notable Figures page, so they stay as plain text. The adventure names three guild representatives who meet Esvele Rosznar to estimate repairs on the tower she now owns. They are Sembra Vashir, a lawful neutral Calishite human of the Carpenters', Roofers', and Plaisterers' Guild, Pynt Oomtrowl, a chaotic good rock gnome of the Cellarers' and Plumbers' Guild, and Jarbokken Frostbeard, a lawful good shield dwarf of the Guild of Stonecutters, Masons, Potters, and Tile-makers. Justyn Rassk of the Guild of Butchers delivered the meat that Thrakkus paid to have delivered, and Morana Huldark, a dwarf of the Guild of Watermen, supervises a character who must scrape barnacles off the docks for a tenday. The campaign adds Mareva Coile, Berna, Herath and Delvon Harsk of the Dungsweepers' Guild in **The Long Watch**, and Piers Hobb, who leads the Dock Ward haulers' guild, in **Stingblade** (campaign-only).

## Overview

Waterdeep has more than forty guilds, and nearly every trade has one. Most citizens belong to a guild or work for someone who does. Guild Law is separate from the Code Legal, though the Watch and the magisters respect it. Each guild keeps its own customs and punishments. Nobody has to join, but an independent trader soon finds that guild members refuse to deal with them.

> - **Campaign-only guild functions:** The functions below are the campaign's own and are not in the published adventure. Each is tied to a scene or a document, and none of them is common knowledge.
> - **Cassalanter loans:** Victoro's office holds thirteen loan contracts, "the Cassalanter banking leverage over Waterdeep's guilds". A character who makes a DC 12 Intelligence (History) check on them learns they amount to control of the Chandlers', Coopers' and Carpenters' guilds, among others (campaign-only). This replaces the retired claim that the Carpenters' maintain the villa, for which no file gives a source.
> - **Neverember and the Vintners':** In Kalain's Tower the party finds letters to Hammond Kraddoc that confirm large bribes paid to cover a contaminated-liquor scandal in the Dock Ward. They are Neverember's private leverage over the Vintners', Distillers', and Brewers' Guild (campaign-only).
> - **Manshoon and the Council of Guildmasters:** The Kolat Towers structure document says Jelenn Urmbrusk's votes in the Council of Guildmasters follow Manshoon's preferences without anyone asking her directly (campaign-only, structure document pending conversion). No other file mentions a Council of Guildmasters, and the document writes "his votes" for a woman. See **Manshoon's Zhentarim** and **Jelenn Urmbrusk**.
> - **The Dungsweepers' Guild:** In the published adventure, members of the guild or of Xanathar's Guild erase the chalk symbols in the sewers, and four Dungsweepers carrying intellect devourers visit the tavern on the second night of a Bregan D'aerthe renown mission. The campaign builds on both. In **The Long Watch**, Delvon Harsk sells the crew's timings to Pirn, a street agent of Xanathar's Guild, to pay a 300 gp debt, and Berna's crew is clean. In **Three Nights**, four Dungsweepers' workers sit down at the back table in the evening and say nothing. In **The Exile's Return**, a Dungsweepers' night man is one of three sources who name the rope warehouse in Mudwagon Court (campaign-only).
> - **Bregan D'aerthe in the guilds:** Fenerus Stormcastle is a lamplighter by day and a Bregan D'aerthe cut-out at night. His Notable Figures page says he believes his contacts are Luskan agents, and the Bregan D'aerthe page says he once followed Krebbyg and knows the messages go to the **Sea Maidens Faire**. Among the messages that Krebbyg collects from his house is a note about the Council of Musicians' objection to the Faire, resolved through the Jesters' Guild, and another that ends "We have eyes up in the Watchful Order." (campaign-only).
> - **Faction help with Trollskull permits (campaign-only):** The manor guide lets four factions bend the guild fees. A Harper at Renown 3 or higher has **Mirt** take 1 gp off the Launderers' line. A Lords' Alliance member gets **Jalester Silvermane's** civic designation, which cuts the Fellowship of Innkeepers line from 3 gp to 1 gp until a Watch citation resets it. The Emerald Enclave's druids replace the Watchful Order ward and remove its 2 gp. **Savra Belabranta** of the Order of the Gauntlet walks the party to one guildhall in Order insignia, and the permit queue closes within a day. **Davil Starsong** of the Doom Raiders gets bulk agreements through a bypass of the Vintners' Guild, which cuts about 100 gp from the opening stock.
> - **Faction operatives in guild leadership:** The retired lore page said most major guilds have at least one faction operative embedded in leadership. No file names one, and no scene uses one. The published adventure says only that various nobles and guildmasters are Harper sympathizers, that Davil can arrange meetings with members of city guilds, and that the Doom Raiders have forged alliances with various nobles and guilds. Treat the ties listed in these notes as the complete set until a quest adds another.
> - **Faction cover and laundering (retired claims):** The retired page called the League of Skinners and Tanners a cover for Zhentarim material transit and the Jewelers' Guild a vector for money laundering. No other file says either. Beyond the list, the adventure mentions the League once, as the guild Emmek Frewn disliked dealing with, and it gives the Jewelers' Guild only a currency-exchange role. Neither claim has a scene, so neither is in play. `[research]`
> - **Never reveal:** The Cassalanter loans, Neverember's bribes, Delvon Harsk's sale of timings and any faction tie in these notes before a quest scene gives it. A player who asks about the guilds gets the Overview.

**Tone:** Practical and slightly wry, in the manner of a city full of people who charge dues. Give each campaign addition and each wiki claim its tag, so that the GM can see which statements are published, which are campaign choices and which are borrowed.
