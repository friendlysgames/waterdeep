# The City Watch

> Structuring draft.

> - **What this page is:** the institution entry for Waterdeep's police force, and the third of the four governance pages. **Purpose:** Lets the GM say who polices Waterdeep, who answers to whom and how the Watch reacts when the party makes noise. It keeps the wiki's claims, the campaign's additions and the published adventure apart, so that no later page invents a headquarters, a headcount or a corrupt officer that no source gives.

The City Watch is Waterdeep's police force, charged with keeping the peace and apprehending criminals inside the city. Its officers wear a green-and-goldenrod doublet and a tall steel helmet, and each carries a truncheon, a dagger and a buckler. Because most citizens carry no weapon, that gear is deterrent enough. The published adventure calls the Watch one of the most trusted police forces anywhere, after paladin-patrolled Elturgard, and tells a visitor who keeps the law to expect to be left alone.

Newcomers confuse the Watch with the City Guard, and the published adventure offers Volo's mnemonic to keep them apart: the Guard guards the walls while the Watch watches all. The Guard is the city's army, and the Watch walks the streets that the Guard's walls enclose. The Commander of the Watch reports to the Open Lord. No source gives the Watch's total strength, so this page gives none.

### Tenets

- **Arrests Without a Scene:** The Watch makes every effort to take people into custody without disturbing other citizens, and bystanders return the favor by giving an arrest a wide berth. Floggings are carried out in the watch post nearest the sentencing.
- **A Strict Code, Mostly Kept:** The published adventure says a visitor can trust the Watch to do its duty diligently and that its strict code of conduct makes it one of the most trusted forces anywhere. The same text names eight crooked Watch veterans in the pay of a Masked Lord, so the code is real and a few officers have stepped outside it.
- **The Streets Belong to the Watch:** Every officer knows the streets they patrol and the people who live on them better than a visitor will in ten seasons. Running from the Watch is a time-honored tradition among local miscreants, and it rarely works for newcomers.
- **Above the Sewers, Inside the Walls:** Watch officers care more about the city above than the sewers below, and "Keep the blood off the streets" is a common saying among them. The wiki adds that the Watch never acted outside the city walls and paid little attention to the sewers (wiki, citing Dragon Heist). The Field Ward is the plainest gap, since the Watch does not patrol it and many crimes there go uninvestigated.

### Operations

- **Watch posts:** The Watch keeps posts throughout the city, usually off the main thoroughfares in small courtyards or at cross streets. A green-and-gold lantern burns outside each one, lit even by day with a *continual flame* spell. A post serves as a local headquarters and armory. It takes crime reports when no constable can be found, and its few cells hold the arrested until they are marched to a courthouse jail. A captain leads each post.
- **Where the Watch is based:** The wiki gives the Watch's main base as the Palace of Waterdeep, where the city's officials, the Guard and the clerks also keep their main offices (wiki; City of Splendors: Waterdeep, 3e). The published adventure names no headquarters, and no source puts a separate Watch headquarters building in the Castle Ward. The Palace is the Open Lord's seat and is described on **Castle Ward**.
- **Ranks:** Officers rise from constable to sergeant (armar), lieutenant (civilar), captain (senior civilar, who leads a watch post) and major (ward civilar, one for each ward), and the Commander of the Watch stands above them. A Senior Armsmaster in charge of supplies reports to the Commander. The wiki gives the Commander three senior watchlords, the Mage Civilar who leads the wizard corps, the Senior Armsmaster and the Grand Civilar who commands the ward civilars (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e).
- **Patrols:** Squads leave the posts on day and night rounds and on special assignments of protection or investigation, and a patrol runs four to twelve officers. A pair of officers walks the Castle Ward, and squads of eight walk the Dock Ward, rising to a dozen at night. A patrol expecting trouble may bring a priest on loan from a local temple or a mage from the Watchful Order. When officers meet trouble they cannot handle, they blow tin whistles that summon more of the Watch and alert nearby citizens.
- **Who the officers are:** Most Watch members are veterans, and some of the highest-ranked are knights. All wear helmets and carry clubs on duty. The published adventure also uses the guard stat block for Watch constables at street scenes.
- **Watch talk:** Officers use a slang that tells a visitor how the encounter is going. "What befalls?" asks what is happening, "Hold!" means do not move, "Down arms!" means drop your weapons and "Talk truth!" demands the whole story. "Robes" means a magister, and "Do we need robes here?" is a threat.
- **Sewer keys:** Watch captains hold keys to the sewer access points that match their assigned wards. A party that needs a sewer door opened without a lockpick can find a captain who has the key.
- **The dead:** After the fireball, the dead go to a Watch station in the North Ward and are kept in a cellar morgue. Clerics from local temples cast *gentle repose* on them while the investigation runs.
- **Badge of the Watch (wiki):** The wiki, citing Dragon Heist, says an officer who reaches captain may be given a badge of the Watch. The badge adds protection against attacks and tells the Open Lord where the wearer is, and only the Open Lord can award one. The published adventure gives a badge to Jalester Silvermane and says it returns to Laeral if he loses it.
- **When the party breaks the law:** Characters who engage in overt crime are quickly cornered and arrested by the Watch and brought before a magister. Advocates may speak for those who have allied themselves with influential people. Lords' Alliance agents are more likely to be excused if the Open Lord has cause to let them off. The magisters and the Code Legal are covered on **The Code Legal and the Magisters**.

### The Services Beside the Watch

**The City Guard.** The Guard is Waterdeep's army, charged with the walls and gates, the government buildings, the harbor and the city's officials, and it patrols the roads to Amphail, Goldenfields and Daggerford. Its ranks run private, sergeant (armar), lieutenant (civilar) and captain (senior civilar), then command posts such as the Seneschal of Castle Waterdeep, the Defender of the Harbor and the Master Armorer, with the Warden of Waterdeep at the top. Elminster is the current Warden and answers to the Open Lord. Privates and sergeants use the guard stat block, and lieutenants and above are veterans. The Guard seals the fireball scene before the Watch arrives. It collects the monthly tax door to door on the last day of the month, posts two soldiers at each City of the Dead gate at night and runs the Amendsfarm labor camp among the farms of Undercliff. It oversees the Field Ward from the walls around it and acts there only when someone moving in or out of the city is threatened. The wiki gives Castle Waterdeep a garrison of 1,400 City Guard in 1372 DR (wiki; City of Splendors: Waterdeep, 3e), and **Castle Ward** lists the rest of it. The wiki also says the Guard had been folded into the Watch as a division by 1479 DR, and that dangerous work such as patrolling the sewers and Undermountain then went to the Guard (wiki; Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide, 4e, and Downshadow). The published adventure still gives the Guard and the Watch separate ranks and duties in 1492 DR, and play follows the adventure.

**The Griffon Cavalry.** The Cavalry is a special branch of the City Guard whose riders are veteran soldiers who fly griffons from the Peaktop Aerie atop Mount Waterdeep. Each rider has a *ring of feather falling* and drops from the saddle to strike flying threats such as manticores, harpies and outlaw wizards, then floats until the griffon swoops in. The riders stay above the rooftops, because the smell of so much horseflesh in the streets can drive a griffon into a frenzy. A rider circles over Trollskull Alley after the fireball, and two riders hover above Gralhund Villa when the Watch arrives. The Belabranta family raises the griffons, and **Savra Belabranta** (Lawful Good, Human, she/her) belongs to it. The wiki says the Cavalry had been disbanded for years by 1479 DR because its griffons died and were never replaced (wiki; Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide, 4e), and that Laeral proposed refounding it in 1491 DR (wiki; Death Masks). The published adventure shows it flying in 1492 DR. Its present size is `[research]`.

**The City Navy.** The published adventure places the Navy's docks in the Great Harbor, inside the Castle Ward, and says the ward's race champions often come from the Guard, the Navy or the Cavalry. Mirt's mansion stands near the Naval Harbor. The wiki says Ahghairon restructured Raurlor's army and navy into the Watch and the Guard (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e), and it describes an aquatic division of the Guard recruited from the merfolk and sea elves of Deepwater Harbor (wiki; City of Splendors: Waterdeep, 3e). No source in the pass gives the Navy's size, ships or commander, and no campaign file uses it, so all three are `[research]`.

**The Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors.** Waterdeep requires arcane spellcasters who plan to stay to register with the city, and it strongly encourages them to join the Watchful Order, which the published adventure says the Blackstaff heads. Members are expected to serve as temporary members of the Watch or the Guard when called. Their expertise helps investigators decide whether magic was used in a crime, and they are tapped after fires and disasters. The Order also watches its own members, since any havoc a spellcaster causes risks the wrath of the Lords. The published adventure lists the Order among the city's guilds, and the wiki calls it technically a guild that works mainly as a mage academy, formed in 1317 DR (wiki; City of Splendors boxed set, 2e). **The Guilds** covers its place among the trades.

### Key Members

**Laeral Silverhand** (Chaotic Good, Moon Elf, she/her) is the Open Lord, and the Commander of the Watch reports to her. She is the Guard's Warden's superior as well, since Elminster answers to her. The published adventure gives her no species, and the moon elf reading is campaign-only (see **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords**).

**The Commander of the Watch** is an office, and the published adventure gives it no name. The wiki names Captain Rulathon as Commander around 1372 DR (wiki; City of Splendors: Waterdeep, 3e). The 1492 DR holder is `[research]`.

**Saeth Cromley** (Lawful Good, Illuskan Human, he/him) is a retired sergeant of the Watch who assists Barnibus Blastwind in many of his investigations. In the fireball scene he escorts Barnibus to the crime scene and sends twenty constables door to door. Between cases he sits in the sergeants' room at the Halls of Justice in the afternoons, and a visitor who buys him a drink finds him easy to talk to.

**Barnibus Blastwind** (Lawful Good, Illuskan Human, he/him) is a Watchful Order investigator of crimes that involve magic. He is prickly and secretive, confides only in Cromley and finds violence appalling. He is a lifelong bachelor who inherited a small tidy estate in the Sea Ward from his grandmother and spends his free days in its library. The published adventure gives him no magistrate's office.

**Vajra Safahr** (Neutral, Tethyrian Human, she/her) is the Blackstaff and, by the published adventure, the head of the Watchful Order. She commands **Force Grey**, whose work is the threats that the Watch and the Guard cannot or should not touch. The Open Lord alone can strip her of her title.

**Jalester Silvermane** (Lawful Good, Cormyrean Human, he/him) is a Lords' Alliance field agent who reports to Laeral and carries a badge of the Watch. He knows the Watch's patrol schedules and files better than anyone else the party is likely to meet, and he reads the Watch's file ledger each tenday.

**Lady Gondafrey** (Lawful Good, Gargoyle, she/her) served in the City Watch before Duhlark Kolat captured her in 1379 DR and fused her form with a gargoyle's. Her Notable Figures page makes her a captain, and she keeps fragments of her memories and her sense of duty. Kolat Towers holds her prisoner.

**Tashlyn Yafeera** (Lawful Evil, Calishite Human, she/her) is the Doom Raiders' Master of Arms and Mercenaries. She holds the rank of captain in the City Guard as cover and watches the traffic through the South Gate.

Several more officers have no Notable Figures page, so they stay as plain text. Hyustus Staget (Lawful Good, Illuskan Human, he/him) is the uptight captain who keeps the peace in the Dock Ward and has been dragging his heels on the Zhentarim and Xanathar's Guild, since he reasons that they may as well destroy each other. If he has died, the published adventure replaces him with Cressa Galavarco (Lawful Good, Tethyrian Human, she/her), a veteran. Officers Jonn Bannock (Lawful Neutral, Waterdhavian Human, he/him) and Ysa Terrow (Lawful Neutral, Waterdhavian Human, she/her) work the late shift at the Brandath grove in **Ten Nights in the City of the Dead**.

## Overview

The City Watch is Waterdeep's police force, and its officers wear green-and-goldenrod doublets and tall steel helmets. Squads of four to twelve patrol from watch posts marked by a green-and-gold lantern that burns day and night. The Commander of the Watch reports to the Open Lord. Locals keep the Watch apart from the City Guard with a rhyme: the Guard guards the walls while the Watch watches all.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **How the Watch fits the Grand Game:** The Watch is trying to do its job, and it knows that some cases are politically untouchable. Most officers are not corrupt by rule and can be pressed from above. The Watch has informants in every ward, and every faction has informants in the Watch. Laeral's information comes largely from the Watch and from Jalester, and it is about three moves behind (see **A City Watching Itself**).
> - **How fast the Watch answers:** Any audible commotion outside the walls of Gralhund Villa brings one mage and six veterans, one of them a sergeant, within 5 to 10 minutes. The Watch does not answer sounds from inside the villa during the raid unless outside noise draws it. A party that watches Saerdoun Street for 30 minutes sees a Watch patrol pass about every 20 minutes, and Jalester or Cromley will say so without the wait. In **The Extraction**, a neighbor who calls the Watch brings a patrol of two guards in 10 minutes. After the fireball, six City Guard soldiers seal Trollskull Alley within 5 minutes, and Barnibus and Cromley arrive with twenty constables after 20.
> - **Who can be bought:** Corylus Thann, a Masked Lord whom Manshoon's cell blackmails, keeps eight crooked Watch veterans who crack down on anyone who annoys him, and he clears any charge against them. If **Cultists Escaped** is marked in **The Fouled Channel**, the Splinter pays Watch sergeants to ignore reports about the Trades Ward cisterns, and **Vault of Dragons** reads this as reduced Watch cooperation in that ward. Mirt and Jalester both report that Watch coverage around Kolat Towers has been informally diminished, and Jalester suspects Masked Lord influence that he cannot yet prove.
> - **Who the Watch cannot reach:** Jalester says the Watch cannot touch the Cassalanters without evidence because they are too well connected. Mirt suspects, without proof, that Cassalanter money stood behind the Howling Hatred cult three years ago, since the Watch released two of its members after a word from someone at Cassalanter Villa. The Cassalanters' first response to the party is leverage, and a threat of Watch involvement is part of it. Willifort Crowelle may also arrange for a Cassalanter-connected Watch lieutenant to take a new interest in the party's tavern.
> - **Vajra and the Watch:** Vajra cannot act in the Trades Ward off the books. If a neighbor calls the Watch, a patrol sees the extraction or Orvyn dies in **The Extraction**, the Watch opens a review of the Trades Ward magistracy for 14 days and logs every request the Tower makes to it.
> - **Faction benefits that name the Watch:** A Gray Hand is Friendly to any Watch officer who sees the letter of authorization, and an officer who has watched the member commit a crime is no longer Friendly. A Stingblade can read the Watch's files, which takes one hour for standard files and four hours and a DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) check for classified ones. A High Harper can ask Mirt for Watch cooperation, which delivers a sealed instruction to the responsible station three days after Mirt accepts, gives access to the relevant records and assigns a liaison, and raises no new squad. A Lords' Alliance member earns 1 renown for helping the Watch in a crisis beyond its capacity. A Bregan D'aerthe Commander can arrange passage in or out of Waterdeep without the Watch's knowledge.
> - **After Gralhund Villa:** The Watch arrives at the front gate as Fel'rekt's team withdraws, and by the morning of Ches 25 it has sealed the villa and begun processing twenty-three bodies. Davil Starsong is arrested two days after the villa and held at Castle Waterdeep, and the Watch cracks down on the Black Network. **Davil's Arrest** and **Davil's Return** carry the consequences, and Tashlyn deals with a constable by showing the badge of a City Guard captain.
> - **At Trollskull Manor:** Response Teams do not attack the tavern while customers are present, because a public massacre in a licensed North Ward establishment brings the City Guard, the Watchful Order and possibly Vajra within the hour. From Ches 21 to 30 the Watch sweeps the North Ward for fireball witnesses, and a patrol may arrest a patron. Gosta Ironhelm, a bartender the party can hire, reports tavern gossip to an informal Watch contact and gives one warning before he files a report.
> - **At the dock:** At the Sea Maidens Faire, Jalester notes that its private docking arrangements get around standard Watch inspection protocols. He raises it as an irregularity that someone should eventually care about.
> - **Never reveal:** The names of Corylus Thann's crooked veterans do not exist, and nobody in the Watch knows that the Splinter pays sergeants. A player who asks whether the Watch can be bought gets the Overview, plus whatever the party has seen.

**Tone:** Plain and procedural. Give each wiki claim its tag, so that the GM can see which statements are published, which are campaign choices and which are borrowed, and keep the Watch a tired, mostly decent institution under pressure rather than a villain.
