# Manshoon's Zhentarim

> Structuring draft.

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> - **Campaign role:** Villain faction; pursues the Stone of Golorr for the Weave knowledge encoded in its aboleth memory.
> - **Contacts:** **Avareen Windrivver**, **Agorn Fuoco**, **Vevette Blackwater** (field operatives); **Sidra Romeir** (Kolat Towers)
> - **Villain profile:** see **Manshoon** in Villains
> - **Mechanics:** response teams, outposts, escalation and revelation list are on the **Manshoon's Zhentarim** page in the Factions Guide
> - **Featured in:** **Fireball!**, **Gralhund Villa**, **Faction Outposts**, **Kolat Towers**

**Purpose:** Gives the GM Manshoon's Zhentarim as a working cell: where it came from, what its members believe, how it is built and how it works, who its friends and enemies are, and who serves it. The page keeps lore only, and the **Manshoon** page in Villains owns his phases, goals and relationships. Everything above the Overview is for the GM.

Manshoon's Zhentarim, which outsiders call the Splinter, is a cell of the Black Network that answers only to Manshoon, who rules it from Kolat Towers in the Southern Ward.

> [!gamemaster]**The Two Zhentarims**
>
> Two cells operate in Waterdeep, and Davil Starsong's **Doom Raiders** are the player-joinable one, covered on the organization page **The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders)**. Manshoon's splinter is a completely separate organization that peeled off from the Doom Raiders' local network when he returned to Waterdeep `[research]`, and the two groups dislike each other. Anyone who deals with the Zhentarim through Davil is dealing with the Doom Raiders and not with Manshoon. The villains on this page answer only to Manshoon.

## History

The entries run from the founding of the Black Network to Ches 1, 1492 DR. The wider Network's story is on **The Founding of Chains**, and this page follows the thread that leads to Kolat Towers.

- **1261 DR, Manshoon founds the Black Network:** Manshoon was born in Zhentil Keep in 1229 DR and founded the Zhentarim, also called the Black Network, in 1261 DR, and the Banite priest Fzoul Chembryl joined him in 1263 DR (**The Founding of Chains**). The wiki says he set his wizards to open trade routes between the Moonsea and the Sword Coast, and that he and his magelings pledged fealty to Bane so that the Banite priests would follow him (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Manshoon*, citing *Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms*, edition not tagged). The published adventure calls him "one of the founders" of the Zhentarim (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). Design tag: the wiki makes him the founder, and the campaign keeps the published adventure's wording.
- **1370 DR, the death and the Manshoon Wars:** Fzoul and Lord Orgauth killed Manshoon in 1370 DR and found themselves facing a dozen or more clones that he had made. Each clone believed he was the real Manshoon and felt driven to kill the others, and the fights are the Manshoon Wars. Three clones survived. One rejoined the Zhentarim and conceded leadership to Fzoul, one became the vampire Orbakh in Westgate, and one took refuge in Undermountain with Halaster (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Manshoon*, citing the *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). The published adventure says a mishap woke every clone at once, that at least three survive, and that the one now in Waterdeep is the one who hid in Undermountain (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **1370 DR to 1489 DR, the Network without its founder:** The Zhentarim carried on under Fzoul, and Zhentil Keep fell in stages until the Shadovar razed what remained in 1383 DR (**The Founding of Chains**, citing the Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Zhentil Keep*, 4e). By 1489 DR the Zhentarim were a mercenary company based at Darkhold under a leader called the Pereghost, with more members than ever and a reputation for service (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Zhentarim*, citing the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e). The Zhentarim are reforming a council in 1492 DR, and efforts to reclaim and rebuild Zhentil Keep are underway. Manshoon's cell works independently of this surviving leadership (**Manshoon**). Design tag: *Heroes of Faerûn* and the wiki describe the Keep as rebuilt, and the campaign holds that this has not happened yet in 1492 DR.
- **The late fourteenth century to about 1394 DR, Kolat Towers:** The wizard brothers Duhlark and Alcedor Kolat built the towers, and Duhlark added a force field that covers the whole structure. In 1379 DR the City Watch captain Lady Gondafrey tried to breach the towers and was taken prisoner. Alcedor died there in 1385 DR, and Duhlark died about nine years later with the force field still running, after which the towers stood abandoned (**The Founding of Chains**). Design tag: the published adventure says Alcedor left the towers and was never heard from again, and the campaign has him die inside them.
- **1489 DR, the Doom Raiders arrive first:** Five retired adventurers, the Doom Raiders, reached Waterdeep in 1489 DR to found the Black Network's local foothold, and they hold the older Zhentarim cell in the city (**The Founding of Chains**; **The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders)**). Design tag: the published adventure says only that they came "three years ago" (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **About 1490 DR, the clone takes the towers:** The Undermountain clone had run afoul of Halaster Blackcloak, who captured him after a brief spell duel and took his left arm at the elbow. Magic could not regrow it, so he made an artificial arm and hand for himself (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). He fled Undermountain, found Kolat Towers abandoned with its force field intact, and took the amulet-making instructions from Duhlark's study. He then spent about two years building the Splinter, and in that time he peeled off a large part of the local network that the Doom Raiders had built (**The Founding of Chains**). Through that network he learned that Lord Neverember had used the local Zhentarim's resources to embezzle the vault's gold (Alexandrian remix, *The Villains*, p. 16). `[research]` No source states the year, which is derived from "about two years". Design tag: the published adventure says only that the clone infiltrated Waterdeep years ago, and the campaign places his arrival after the Doom Raiders'.
- **Less than a year before Ches 1, 1492 DR, House Gralhund:** Zhentarim agents loyal to Manshoon approached House Gralhund, and the two formed an alliance. The house gives coin and the use of its villa as a refuge, and it receives the Black Network's intelligence in return, including what the Zhents learn about the Masked Lords (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **House Gralhund**). Neither Orond Gralhund nor any of his family knows about the wizard's clone (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **About Nightal 1491 DR, the envoys and the street war:** Manshoon's agents stole the Neverwinter Eye from the Protector's Enclave, and his envoys carried it to Xanathar in Skullport as the opening of an alliance. Xanathar killed the envoys and kept the Eye, and the Zhentarim answered by attacking Guild outposts (**Manshoon**; **Running the Villains**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). The street war that followed runs through the Dock Ward and is now a standing feature of the ward (**Dock Ward**). Design tag: the published adventure has the Stone vanish while the two sides negotiate, and the campaign has the envoys deliver an Eye as a gift.
- **The night before Ches 1, 1492 DR, Renaer Neverember:** Five Zhentarim thugs working for Urstul Floxin seized Renaer Neverember to learn what he knew about his father's hidden gold, and they took Floon Blagmaar in the same attack. House Gralhund paid for the operation (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **House Gralhund**). Xanathar's Guild raided the Candle Lane warehouse where the thugs held both men and killed the cell's agents there (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Ches 1, 1492 DR, the campaign opens:** The cell holds Kolat Towers, Yellowspire and the Interrogation House, owns no Eye of the Stone, and blackmails two Masked Lords. It fights Xanathar's Guild in the Dock Ward and works to dismantle the Doom Raiders and the network they built (**Manshoon**).

## Beliefs

- **Take Prisoners, Not Corpses:** Manshoon prefers to capture rather than kill, because prisoners answer questions and corpses do not. The cell wants intelligence more than body counts, and it finds an underestimated enemy more useful than a dead one. Captives go to the Interrogation House on Brindul Alley in the North Ward. Design tag: the published adventure has Manshoon treat all his followers as expendable, and the campaign follows his Notable Figures page instead.
- **Stay Unseen:** Manshoon's plans depend on not drawing the attention of enemies who would move against him. He posts no visible guards at Kolat Towers so that neighbors take the towers for abandoned or haunted, and he travels by teleportation circle so that nobody sees him come or go. The same caution keeps the cell's field teams light on muscle. The published adventure names the enemies he fears as the Harpers, Elminster of Shadowdale, Halaster Blackcloak and the Blackstaff of Waterdeep, and it says he must operate in the shadows for the time being (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Leverage Before Force:** When the cell wants influence in the city, it reaches for blackmail and debt before violence. Corylus Thann is held over a scandal and Jelenn Urmbrusk through a debt to a Splinter front, and Manshoon's forces rely on both of them to cover up the cell's crimes. The wiki describes the older Zhentarim as willing to use sabotage, blackmail and arson against mercantile and political rivals before they turned to murder (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Zhentarim*, citing *Lords of Darkness*, edition not tagged).
- **Trust Is Issued and Revoked:** Access to Kolat Towers is something Manshoon grants and can withdraw. A pass-amulet system lets him revoke anyone's access from the towers, and his brass teleporter rings, each bearing a stylized M, go only to his most trusted servants. Sidra Romeir holds the rings when lieutenants leave the Towers and hands them to replacements when a holder does not come back. The wiki says Manshoon held little trust for any of his allies even in the Black Network's early years, and that he set his spies on them (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Manshoon*, citing *Ruins of Zhentil Keep*, edition not tagged).
- **Nobody Names the Master:** The street crews will not say who gives the orders, and few in the cell know their master's name. Urstul Floxin will not divulge it unless magically compelled, and he sends messages by black flying snake because he trusts animals more than people. Outsiders call the cell the Splinter.
- **One True Manshoon:** Every surviving clone claims to be the one true Manshoon, and this one holds the belief with absolute certainty. Loyalty to him runs from devotion to calculation. Havia Quickknife and Mookie Plush serve him completely, and Kaevja Cynavern serves him because he is the most capable operator she has met. Agorn Fuoco would trade whatever he knows to save himself. The published adventure says Havia and Mookie hold that Manshoon is the rightful leader of the Zhentarim and will sacrifice themselves to see his plans succeed (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Power Goes to Those Who Deserve It:** The Zhentarim credo, as the published adventure gives it through Agorn, is that power comes to those who deserve it, and members come to see the Black Network as a new family (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The wiki adds that Zhentarim wizards trained their apprentices by sending them on dangerous missions in place of a fee (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Zhentarim*, citing Ed Greenwood's Patreon, edition not tagged).
- **The Doom Raiders Are Deserters:** The cell regards the Doom Raiders as deserters who stole its name `[research]`, will not cooperate with them, and works to dismantle their people and the network they built.
- **Doctrine and Rites:** `[research]` The campaign files give the cell no creed, oath or rites. Amath Sercent keeps Yellowspire as a Banite cell that supports Manshoon's operations where they serve Bane's interests. The wiki says Manshoon and his magelings pledged fealty to Bane to win over the Banite priests, and it lists Bane as the Zhentarim's favored deity, formerly Cyric (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Manshoon* and *Zhentarim*, edition not tagged). Nothing in the campaign makes Bane the faith of the cell as a whole. The cell's emblem is a black winged snake biting a coin. It hangs on banners in Manshoon's audience chamber and is inlaid in the floor of his sanctum, and the street crews wear it as a tattoo on the neck or forearm (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).

## Structure

The cell is a hub with spokes around one man. Beneath his lieutenants it uses street crews, hired thugs and a few planted spies, and no source counts them.

- **Leadership:** Manshoon is a centuries-old wizard, a clone of a clone of one of the Zhentarim's founders, who claims, as all surviving Manshoon clones do, to be the one true Manshoon. He keeps a simulacrum in Kolat Towers as his contingency against assassination, and it fights as his decoy while he escapes. His insurance against dying is the stasis clone spell, which is a separate tool. Nobody stands between him and his lieutenants, and the cell takes its orders from him alone.
- **The hub and spokes:** The Splinter is built as a hub with spokes. Kolat Towers in the Southern Ward is the command center, and the Interrogation House on Brindul Alley in the North Ward and Yellowspire in the Castle Ward are its field offices. Yellowspire holds a teleportation circle that leads to the Towers, and the Towers stand in the Southern Ward. Teleportation circles also link the Towers to the Interrogation House (**Southern Ward**).
- **Kolat Towers:** A dilapidated pair of towers stands one block north of Phaulkonmere, the Emerald Enclave's estate, behind a force field with the properties of a wall of force. Light from *continual flame* spells shows in the windows, and most neighbors believe the place is abandoned or haunted by the Kolat brothers (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Southern Ward**). Sidra Romeir runs the ground floor like a barracks, Manafret Cherryport cooks for the household, and Manshoon's three apprentices, Ered Payno, Havi Termock and Savara Firethorn, sleep in the old apprentices' quarters and wear teleporter rings (**Sidra Romeir**; **Manafret Cherryport**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). Lady Gondafrey is still a prisoner inside (**Southern Ward**).
- **The sanctum:** Behind the towers lies an extradimensional sanctum in a finite demiplane, which the original Manshoon created and his clones all know. A teleporter ring and the circle in the towers are the way in (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). Kaevja Cynavern watches the entrance and is Manshoon's early-warning mechanism, Havia Quickknife and Mookie Plush keep his audience chamber, and Yorn the Terror is the enforcer who maintains the household's fear of its master (**Kaevja Cynavern**; **Havia Quickknife**; **Mookie Plush**; **Yorn the Terror**).
- **Yellowspire:** An old tower of pale yellow brick in the Castle Ward, where Amath Sercent and four acolytes are converting the building into a temple of Bane. Black handprints cover the interior walls, and neighbors take the place for a private home (**Amath Sercent**; **Bane**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). The circle in its basement is a second way into Kolat Towers (**Castle Ward**).
- **The Interrogation House:** A windowless house on Brindul Alley in the North Ward, kept by Avareen Windrivver and Zorbog Jyarkoth. A rack and an arcane circle fill the back room, and three black flying snakes nest near the ceiling (**North Ward**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Lieutenants and field teams:** Urstul Floxin is the chief field operative and leads the thugs who work the streets. Vevette Blackwater coordinates strike teams from Kolat Towers and leads the forces that fight Xanathar's Guild, and in the published adventure Agorn Fuoco leads those that fight the Zhents who are not loyal to Manshoon (**Vevette Blackwater**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). Vira Solkan is a plant inside Blackstaff Tower (**Castle Ward**).
- **Street holdings:** Crews with flying snake tattoos hold the Skewered Dragon in the Dock Ward as an informal ward headquarters, and the published adventure calls the tavern Zhentarim-owned (**Dock Ward**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). The cell keeps safe houses on Shesstra Street and Nethpranter Street in the Trades Ward and pays gang leaders in the Field Ward (**Trades Ward**; **Field Ward**). It also used a warehouse on Candle Lane to recruit, train and equip sellswords (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Size and ranks:** `[research]` No source counts the cell's members or gives it rank titles beyond "lieutenant". The wiki's ranks, from Fang to Dread Lord, belong to the Doom Raiders in the campaign.

## Methods and Tactics

- **Messengers:** Black flying snakes carry the cell's orders between its sites, and three of them nest at the Interrogation House while Urstul keeps a collection of his own (**North Ward**; **Urstul Floxin**). The wiki says the Zhentarim often used flying snakes as messenger animals (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Zhentarim*, edition not tagged), and Davil Starsong's briefings also arrive by flying snake (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). Kaevja Cynavern relies on the *sending* spell for the sanctum's alarms (**Kaevja Cynavern**).
- **What Manshoon Wants:** The gold means nothing to him as wealth. He wants the Stone of Golorr for what it knows, because the Stone is a transformed aboleth and its centuries of absorbed memory encode ancient knowledge of the Weave's structure. Manshoon intends to use that knowledge to absorb the Weave and supplant Mystra, so the vault is an instrument and the Stone is the goal.
- **Funding:** The cell pays for its daily work from the sellsword trade, from Gralhund coin and from the hush money that Corylus Thann pays (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The wiki describes the older Network as a trader in illicit goods and contraband (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Zhentarim*, citing *Lords of Darkness*, edition not tagged).
- **Blackmail and bribery:** The 500,000 gold dragons matter for what they buy, which is a private army, bribed magisters and time. Two Masked Lords on the Council are compromised, Corylus Thann through scandal and Jelenn Urmbrusk through debt, and they vote Manshoon's way without knowing why their interests have aligned. This leverage is his most carefully guarded intelligence. Evidence of the blackmail, if it reached Laeral Silverhand or the full Lords' Council, would collapse Manshoon's political position immediately, which is why the documentation lives at Kolat Towers and nowhere else. The scandal is that Corylus once hired Zhentarim thugs to attack non-humans who sought political office, and the debt is a loan that Jelenn took from the Zhentarim because she feared the gossip a noble lender would cause (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Capture and interrogation:** The cell takes prisoners and questions them at the Interrogation House, and Yorn the Terror is the asset it uses when a conversation has stopped working (**Yorn the Terror**). Manshoon prefers a prisoner to a corpse because a prisoner answers questions (**Manshoon**).
- **Intelligence and infiltration:** The Splinter has infiltrated the local Harper cell, and Vira Solkan sits inside Blackstaff Tower as a junior arcanist (**The Harpers**; **Vira Solkan**). Manshoon bought the Doom Raiders' Master of Magic, Skeemo Weirdbottle, with rare alchemical components and protection from Watch inspections, and Skeemo passes what he hears by paper bird (**Skeemo Weirdbottle**). The cell also draws on House Gralhund's intelligence about the Masked Lords (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Control of minds:** Manshoon's spellbook holds *dominate monster*, *geas* and *mass suggestion*, and Amath Sercent uses a Banite rite built to break a captive's will, which she has used to turn broadsheet publishers as part of a plan to control the flow of information in the city (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The GM Guide says the cell maintains mind-controlled servants, and no Notable Figures page names one (**Structural Rules**). `[research]` The wiki says another Manshoon clone, a vampire, enslaved some people and made others mind-thralls without their knowledge (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Manshoon*, citing *Elminster Must Die*, edition not tagged).
- **Recruiting:** The Black Network has an open recruitment policy that prizes tenacity and loyalty without requiring either, but the published adventure says adventurers cannot join Manshoon's cause (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). His lieutenants rise by impressing him, and Agorn Fuoco was raised quickly after he made a good impression on the wizard (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). Kaevja Cynavern was a Red Wizard of Thay who gave up her Thayan head tattoos and red robes to study under him (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Restraint:** Manshoon keeps a low profile because his success hinges on not attracting the attention of his enemies, and the published adventure says he avoids unnecessary confrontations with adventurers (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). His approach reflects his temperament, which favors precision and patience and avoids unnecessary violence. The Zhentarim response teams are the weakest in raw muscle of any faction, and this is deliberate, since drawing too much attention is the one mistake Manshoon won't make twice. In a fight he lets the simulacrum take the opening volley and casts from behind it (**Manshoon**).
- **What the cell will not do:** It will not cooperate with the Doom Raiders or share information with them, and it will not name its master. It will not post guards where they would draw attention, and it does not take up offers from houses it suspects of setting a trap. Vevette Blackwater turned down a Cassalanter approach for that reason (**Vevette Blackwater**).

The cell's response teams, outposts and escalation are on the **Manshoon's Zhentarim** page in the Factions Guide.

## Relationships

Each entry gives the stance on Ches 1, 1492 DR.

- **The Harpers (enemy):** Manshoon counts the Harpers among the powerful enemies who would move against him if they learned he was in Waterdeep (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The Splinter has infiltrated the local Harper cell and acts on Harper information, and the Harpers do not know that the Splinter is Manshoon's cell (**The Harpers**). The wiki says the two organizations have been at odds for as long as the Harpers have known of the Black Network (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Zhentarim*, citing *Lords of Darkness*, edition not tagged).
- **The Lords' Alliance (enemy):** The Alliance is alarmed by the Splinter, whose agents are trying to seize Waterdeep's institutions from within, and Jalester Silvermane calls the cell "not Davil's people" (**The Lords' Alliance**). The published adventure names the Alliance among the powers that would move against Manshoon once they had proof that he hides in the city (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Emerald Enclave (rival, at a distance):** Phaulkonmere stands one block south of Kolat Towers, and Melannor Fellbranch describes the Splinter as the other cell of an old network and says he does not know who leads it (**The Emerald Enclave**; **Southern Ward**). The wiki records that the Vilhon order of the Enclave opposed Zhentarim mages for fear that they would abuse the Art (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Emerald Enclave*, edition not stated).
- **The Order of the Gauntlet (enemy):** The Order knows the cell as the Splinter or the other cell and names no master for it, and the cell pays gang leaders in the Field Ward (**The Order of the Gauntlet**; **Field Ward**). The published adventure counts the Order among the powers that would act against Manshoon once they had proof (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Force Grey (enemy):** Force Grey speakers call the cell the Splinter or the other cell, and the cell has planted Vira Solkan inside Blackstaff Tower (**Force Grey**; **Castle Ward**). Manshoon counts the Blackstaff of Waterdeep among his enemies (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders) (enemy):** The Splinter peeled off a large part of the Doom Raiders' network, and the two cells despise each other and will not cooperate (**The Founding of Chains**). Manshoon regards the Doom Raiders as deserters who stole his name `[research]`, and they believe Urstul Floxin leads the cell, so they do not know whom it serves (**The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders)**). The published adventure calls the Zhentarim in Waterdeep a fractured organization, with Manshoon's supporters set on destroying Xanathar and seizing the city and his opponents set on exposing him (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Bregan D'aerthe (wary rival):** Manshoon treats Jarlaxle as the only Grand Game player he takes seriously, and the two keep a fragile non-interference arrangement (**Manshoon**). `[research]` The Villains page tags the arrangement and the rating as unsourced. Manshoon has marked Fenerus Stormcastle, whom he has identified as a Bregan D'aerthe cut-out, as a soft target (**Trades Ward**; **Running the Villains**).
- **Xanathar's Guild (enemy):** Xanathar killed Manshoon's envoys and kept the Eye they brought, and the street war in the Dock Ward has run since then (**Xanathar's Guild**). Xanathar's kill order on Manshoon has failed twice, and Manshoon knows who killed his envoys (**Running the Villains**). Xanathar wants to wipe out anyone it takes for a Zhentarim operative (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Cassalanters (neutral, mutual distrust):** Manshoon shares the city's suspicion of a family that is too rich and too pleasant, and an arrangement with them could have given him leverage `[research]` (**Manshoon**). Seffia Naelryke approached Vevette Blackwater to open a communication channel, and Vevette refused, suspecting a trap. Neither side has made contact since (**Running the Villains**).
- **House Gralhund (ally of convenience):** The Zhentarim use the villa as a shield and refuge, and the house uses them to learn the Masked Lords' identities and weaknesses (**House Gralhund**). Yalah Gralhund treats Urstul Floxin as an underling and does not know that Manshoon is the cell's master (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Open Lord's Watch (enemy):** The Watch has arrested a few of the tattooed crews in street brawls, and none of them will say who gives the orders (**The Open Lord's Watch**). Captain Staget lets the Dock Ward war run on, reasoning that if Xanathar's Guild and the Zhentarim want to destroy each other, the Watch may as well let them (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords (enemy, working from within):** The cell blackmails Corylus Thann and Jelenn Urmbrusk and makes them cover up crimes by its forces, and Manshoon keeps a list of five Masked Lords whose identities he knows (**The Open Lord and the Masked Lords**). No source records a dealing between Laeral Silverhand and the cell. The published adventure has Laeral act against Manshoon once she holds proof (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).

## Overview

Zhentarim crews with flying snake tattoos work Waterdeep's back streets, and they are hostile to Davil Starsong's Doom Raiders. The crews take prisoners, and the Watch has arrested a few of them in street brawls. None of them will say who gives the orders, and street talk says one master stands behind them.

> [!gamemaster]**GM Notes**
>
> - **The name:** Outsiders call the cell the Splinter. Few in the cell know their master's name, and the street crews do not use it.
> - **The Eyes:** The cell holds no Eye of the Stone, because its envoys brought Eye #1 to Xanathar as a gift and Xanathar killed them and kept the Eye. Manshoon therefore needs the Stone and all three Eyes.

**Tone:** Plain and procedural in the GM sections, and guarded in the Overview, where the cell is a street presence with no face. Play the members as patient professionals who keep their master's name to themselves.
