# The Lords' Alliance

> Structuring draft.

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> - **Campaign role:** Player faction. Political operatives committed to recovering the 500,000 stolen dragons for Waterdeep's treasury.
> - **Contacts:** **Jalester Silvermane** (primary); **Laeral Silverhand** (does not hand out assignments herself)
> - **Mechanics:** how the Alliance recruits, ranks, disciplines and rewards its members is on the **Lords' Alliance** page in the Factions Guide
> - **Featured in:** **Fireball!**, **Gralhund Villa**, **Faction Outposts**, **Cassalanter Villa**, **Sea Maidens Faire**, **Vault of Dragons**, **Lords' Alliance First Meeting**, **The Long Watch**, **The Exile's Return**, **The Red Wizard's Price**, **The Archer Above**, **Fentree's Ledger**, **An Audience with the Open Lord**, **Suspension and Dismissal**, **Escalation to the Open Lord**, **Redknife**, **Stingblade**, **Warduke**, **Lioncrown**

**Purpose:** Gives the GM the Lords' Alliance as an institution: where it came from, what its members believe, how the Waterdeep chapter is built and works, who stands with it and against it, and what it knows and does not know. The page keeps lore only. Everything above the Overview is for the GM.

The Lords' Alliance is a coalition of rulers and political operatives from Waterdeep and the allied cities of the North, and in Waterdeep it is the Open Lord's network of paid field agents.

## History

Dates up to 1491 DR follow the Forgotten Realms Wiki unless a campaign file is named, and each wiki entry gives the edition of the book the wiki cites.

- **1325 DR, the founding.** A collective of towns and cities in the Sword Coast North banded together to resist overreach by the merchant houses of Amn, and interest in joining was strongest in the North before it spread south (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e, and the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e). The wiki's introduction names a wider set of rivals, among them the growing Black Network, the Shadow Thieves of Amn, orc hordes and Northlander raiders, and gives the coalition a second name, the Council of Lords (same page; the clause on orcs and raiders cites the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e, and the rest names no edition).
- **1358 DR to 1361 DR, the first war with Luskan.** In 1358 DR the Alliance drove Luskan's troops off the island of Ruathym by combining military and diplomatic pressure, and in 1361 DR it was forced into open fighting when Luskan conquered the island (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e, and *Lost Empires of Faerûn*, 3.5e).
- **1363 DR and 1368 DR, Dragonspear and Amn.** The Alliance committed its forces to the Second Dragonspear War in 1363 DR and afterward worked to keep the Trade Way open to commerce. In 1368 DR it gathered evidence that Amn had sunk Waterdhavian ships bound for Maztica (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*; the 1368 DR entry cites *The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e, and the 1363 DR entries cite books whose editions the wiki does not state).
- **1371 DR, Evereska.** After the Sharn Wall beneath Evereska was breached, an Alliance army led by Laeral Silverhand marched to relieve the city and arrived some months late (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e). This is the wiki's earliest record of Laeral acting for the Alliance.
- **1480s DR, the Council of Waterdeep.** When the Cult of the Dragon plotted to free Tiamat, the Alliance sent seven delegates to the Council of Waterdeep, which gave it more seats than any of the other four factions at the table, and the council met in the Lords' Palace in the Castle Ward (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance* and *Council of Waterdeep*, citing *The Rise of Tiamat*, 5e). `[research: wiki only]`
- **1488 DR, the turn inward.** After the Silver Marches collapsed, the Alliance focused on its own affairs and began restricting new members from beyond the North (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e).
- **About 1489 DR, Laeral takes the seat.** The Masked Lords voted Dagult Neverember out of the Open Lord's seat and asked Laeral Silverhand to take it, and the Alliance's lead in Waterdeep passed to her with the office (**A City Watching Itself**; the wiki dates her leadership of the Alliance to 1488 DR, see Draft Notes). Neverember kept his place on the Alliance's rolls as the Lord Protector of Neverwinter (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, list of members as of 1489 DR, citing the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e; **Dagult Neverember**).
- **Around 1491 DR, Mulmaster.** Alliance agents in the Moonsea region began looking into unusual activity in and around Mulmaster (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *Embers of Elmwood*, a 5e Adventurers League module). `[research: wiki only]`
- **Last year, about 1491 DR, Faerrel Dunblade.** Faerrel was a former mercenary of the Steel Shadows whom Laeral had put to work beside Jalester as deputies and spies, and he died while exposing a plot against the government. Jalester stayed in Waterdeep as one of the Open Lord's field operatives (**Jalester Silvermane**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e, for the recruitment and the plot; the Trollskull Manor guide for "last year"). Design: the published adventure gives no date for the death, and the campaign places it last year.

## Beliefs

*"Civilization is the most fragile thing we have built. We will not let it fall."*

- **Civilization Must Be Actively Defended:** The Alliance's motto says that civilization is the most fragile thing its members have built and that they will not let it fall. In Waterdeep this makes the Alliance a pro-city intelligence and security apparatus, which pays agents to watch for threats to the city's stability both outside the walls and within them. The wiki gives the same belief to the whole coalition, which held to the preservation of civilization for the good of its people (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, 5e). An Alliance agent quoted in the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide* credits the coalition with keeping towns safe from larger powers and with teaching the rulers of many cities that cooperating, even for a while, beats closing the gates and waiting out the storm (same page, 5e).
- **Agents, Not Adventurers:** The Alliance is the most demanding of the factions in professional conduct, and Jalester holds that assignments from the Alliance are not optional. The published adventure agrees that the rule is special to this faction, since a member of any other faction can turn down a mission without consequence (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Gold Belongs to the City:** In Laeral's framing the 500,000 dragons were taxed from Waterdhavian citizens and merchants, and Neverember's ouster does not change who owns them. The Alliance wants them recovered and properly disbursed and will credit those who make that possible, but it will not bend institutional procedure to get them back.
- **The Alliance Works for the Open Lord:** The Alliance reports to Laeral Silverhand, Jalester is her field agent, and its members act under his direction. The published adventure says that Laeral asked him to spy on adventurers who might aid or imperil the city (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). She does not play the Grand Game herself, and the Alliance acts on her behalf. Jalester treats working for the Alliance and working for Waterdeep as one job (Alexandrian remix, Appendix B).
- **Field People Who Are Neither Watch nor Guard:** Jalester recruits capable people who can work where neither the Watch nor the Guard can or should appear, and he offers resources, backing and the city's gratitude in return. The wiki says the Alliance hired adventurers for tasks from gathering information to raiding Zhentarim strongholds, and that its agents came from every background (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*).
- **Loyalty to the City Outweighs a Record:** A candidate must be a Waterdhavian citizen, and a criminal record does not bar anyone who can show allegiance to the city (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The wiki repeats the rule (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, 5e).
- **Allies Answer the Call:** Members of the Alliance must come to one another's aid in times of need (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The wiki says the members often hold opposing views and conflicting interests, that they set their rivalries aside when real danger threatens their shared safety, and that the coalition shows its strength when they act together (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *The Rise of Tiamat* and *Out of the Abyss*, 5e).
- **Luskan Is Not Trusted:** The Alliance's leaders have voiced their opposition to admitting Luskan, and a few have declared that the city will never be welcome (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The wiki lists Luskan among the Alliance's enemies (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, edition not stated), and the same agent quoted in the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide* says the coalition has kept Luskan's ambitions in check (5e). Laeral thinks of Luskan as a greedy pirate state that cannot be trusted under any circumstances (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Splinter Is the Threat:** The Alliance is alarmed by the Splinter, whose agents are trying to seize Waterdeep's institutions from within. Jalester does not take the Splinter for Davil Starsong's cell and calls it "not Davil's people". The coalition's own origin story lists the Black Network's growing reach in the North among the reasons it formed (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, edition not stated).
- **Doctrine and Rites:** [research] The campaign files give no oath, rite or creed beyond the motto. The wiki gives an emblem, a golden crown set over a field of red (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *Keys from the Golden Vault*, 5e), and the five ranks Cloak, Redknife, Stingblade, Warduke and Lioncrown (same page, citing the 2014 *Dungeon Master's Guide*, 5e).

## Structure

The Lords' Alliance is a coalition of rulers and political operatives from Waterdeep and the allied cities of the North, among them Neverwinter, Baldur's Gate and Silverymoon. They are united by the conviction that civilization must be actively defended. In Waterdeep, the Alliance works as a pro-city intelligence and security apparatus and pays field agents to watch for threats to the city's stability, both outside the walls and within them.

The wiki names no headquarters for the coalition, and its infobox lists the leaders as Piergeiron Paladinson as of 1358 DR, Dagult Neverember from about 1467 DR and Laeral Silverhand since 1488 DR, each of whom was Waterdeep's Open Lord while he or she led it (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, edition not stated). The campaign keeps Laeral as the Alliance's highest authority in the city, although her Notable Figures page files her under the City of Waterdeep.

The wiki's list of members as of 1489 DR names Amphail, Baldur's Gate, Daggerford, Longsaddle, Mithral Hall, Mirabar, Neverwinter, Silverymoon, Waterdeep and Yartar, each under its own ruler (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e). The published adventure calls Waterdeep one of the most influential and most invested members, and it says that Waterdeep takes taxes, fines and guild fees in its own coin or in the currency of any member settlement (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The wiki adds that some members hold more influence than others, and that depending on who is asked the Alliance is a strong coalition or an unsteady pact of political powers (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *Out of the Abyss* and *Princes of the Apocalypse*, 5e). Outside Waterdeep the wiki gives the Alliance the prison of Revel's End, an isolated panopticon in the Frozenfar that holds some of the worst criminals of the North and the Sword Coast (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *Icewind Dale: Rime of the Frostmaiden*, 5e). `[research: wiki only]`

In Waterdeep the chapter is a set of field agents who answer to Jalester Silvermane, who reports to Laeral (Alexandrian remix, Appendix B). Laeral spends most of her time in the Palace of Waterdeep in the Castle Ward, and government residences within walking distance are set aside for the representatives of the other Alliance cities and towns (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Castle Ward**). Its agents hold one of five ranks, Cloak, Redknife, Stingblade, Warduke or Lioncrown, and the Factions Guide holds the terms of each. The chapter keeps at least one safe house, two rooms above a shut dyer's workshop at 14 Ironwick Lane in the Trades Ward, which a lame former Watch quartermaster named Garrick Tull manages (**Trades Ward**). No source gives the number of agents the Alliance keeps in the city. `[research]`

Its members are contacted in person, at the corner table beside the well at the Yawning Portal. Jalester takes that table most evenings, and anyone at the Portal's bar can point to it.

**Primary Contact:** Jalester Silvermane, a Cormyte field agent who reports directly to Open Lord Laeral Silverhand. He is professional, precise and direct, and he has little patience for pleasantries.

**Secondary Contact:** Laeral Silverhand, Open Lord of Waterdeep. She sends no assignments of her own.

## Methods and Tactics

The Alliance's position on the Grand Game is plain: the 500,000 stolen dragons belong to the city, and it wants them recovered and returned. It is also alarmed by the Splinter, whose agents are trying to seize Waterdeep's institutions from within.

**Recruiting.** Jalester offers membership in person to those who qualify, and he spends much of his time in the Yawning Portal and in the other taverns that adventurers frequent (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The published adventure invites people who place the security of the city and the realm ahead of their own interests, and it asks that recruits be residents of Waterdeep. The wiki says the Alliance drew agents from every background, among them bards, warriors, spellcasters and paladins (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e, and *The Rise of Tiamat*, 5e). Every recruit hears the same warning, that assignments from the Alliance are not optional (Alexandrian remix, Appendix B).

**Funding.** No source gives the Alliance a payroll or a treasury. `[research]` It pays its agents in backing, and the published adventure lists the ways. City Watch officials are told that the agents are on official business and are asked to help where they can, Mirt invites them to dinner and shares rumors, and Laeral grants them a brief audience (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).

**Information.** Its intelligence comes from Jalester's field reports and the Watch's contacts, and it runs about three moves behind the factions (**A City Watching Itself**). Laeral's own spies keep watch on the adventurers in the city, and she rewards those who put Waterdeep ahead of themselves (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). Jalester reads the Watch's file ledger each tenday (**The City Watch**).

**Fighting.** The Alliance seldom fights in its own name. The published adventure says it uses field operatives, whom it describes as diplomats, spies and assassins, and the wiki says it hired adventurers for raids on Zhentarim strongholds (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, edition not stated). Its agents take the work that the Watch and the Guard cannot be seen doing (Alexandrian remix, Appendix B). When a campaign calls for open war, the wiki says, the member cities have often taken charge of it together (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *The Rise of Tiamat*, 5e).

**Bargaining.** The wiki says the coalition bargained with foreign trading partners and set how certain groups, among them goblinoids and orc tribes, were to be treated, through mercantile treaties and pledges of mutual safety (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, edition not stated). In the Grand Game the Alliance trades credit for the gold's recovery and will not trade away procedure (**Lords' Alliance** in the Factions Guide).

The Alliance is the most demanding of the factions in professional conduct. Members are expected to behave like agents and not like adventurers, and a member who refuses an assignment or breaks the Alliance's rules can be suspended or dismissed.

**What it will not do.** The Alliance will not bend institutional procedure to recover the gold, and it will not let anyone who recovers the gold keep it (**Lords' Alliance** in the Factions Guide; Alexandrian remix, Appendix B). It does not play the Grand Game in its own name, because Laeral does not and it acts for her (**A City Watching Itself**).

How the Alliance recruits, ranks, disciplines and rewards its members is on the **Lords' Alliance** page in the Factions Guide.

## Relationships

These stances hold on Ches 1, 1492 DR.

- **The Harpers** (ally): Mirt, a senior Harper and a Masked Lord, is Laeral's closest advisor, and the wiki lists the Harpers among the Alliance's allegiances as of 1488 DR (**Mirt**; **Laeral Silverhand**; Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *The Rise of Tiamat*, 5e). The Harpers still count the Alliance among the powers whose hold on the gold they watch (**The Harpers**).
- **The Emerald Enclave** (neutral): Jeryth gives every question about the hoard the same answer, that it is for the Lords to settle, so the Enclave takes no side in the Alliance's claim on the gold (**The Emerald Enclave**). The wiki's infobox lists the Enclave among the Alliance's allegiances as of 1488 DR (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *The Rise of Tiamat*, 5e), and no campaign file gives the two a shared task.
- **The Order of the Gauntlet** (neutral): The wiki lists the Order among the Alliance's allegiances as of 1488 DR (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *The Rise of Tiamat*, 5e). The two work differently, since the Order strikes at evil without waiting for local leaders to approve (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, citing *The Rise of Tiamat*, 5e), while the Alliance will not bend institutional procedure (**Lords' Alliance** in the Factions Guide).
- **Force Grey** (ally): Both answer to the Open Lord, who can order Vajra Safahr to unleash Force Grey, and Vajra passes what she learns of the Grand Game to Laeral (**Force Grey**; **A City Watching Itself**). Force Grey wants the Open Lord's outcomes handled without fuss and without the Alliance's politics (**Player Factions Overview** in the GM Guide).
- **The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders)** (neutral): Jalester separates Davil Starsong's cell from the Splinter with the words "not Davil's people", and the Watch already keeps a file on the cell (**Lords' Alliance** in the Factions Guide; **The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders)**). The wiki's infobox lists the Zhentarim as an Alliance ally as of 1488 DR (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing *The Rise of Tiamat*, 5e). Design: the campaign splits that single Zhentarim into the Splinter and Davil's cell, so the line fits neither.
- **Bregan D'aerthe** (rival): Jarlaxle wants Luskan admitted to the Alliance, and the Alliance's leaders oppose it (**Jarlaxle Baenre**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). Only one side knows about the contest, since nobody has named Jarlaxle to Laeral (**A City Watching Itself**).
- **Xanathar's Guild** (enemy): The Guild is the largest criminal organization in Waterdeep and rules from a lair below the city, and the Alliance exists to watch for threats to the city's stability (**Xanathar's Guild**). Jalester does not know where the lair is and would have to ask Laeral (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). No source states an Alliance policy toward the Guild, so the stance follows from the Alliance's remit. `[research]`
- **Manshoon's Zhentarim** (enemy): The Alliance is alarmed by the Splinter and treats it as the threat to Waterdeep's institutions (**Lords' Alliance** in the Factions Guide). The wiki says the coalition formed partly to oppose the Black Network and lists the Zhentarim among its enemies in the 14th century (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, edition not stated). The Splinter holds two Masked Lords through blackmail (**Manshoon**), which is the capture from within that the Alliance fears, and no source says the Alliance knows of it. `[research]`
- **The Cassalanters** (neutral, suspicious): The Alliance has no proof against the family and knows nothing of a pact. Its speakers think the family too rich and too pleasant, and the Watch has closed three complaints against the house (**The Cassalanters**; **Lords' Alliance** in the Factions Guide).
- **House Gralhund** (neutral): Yalah presents herself as a gracious noblewoman and hides her Black Network ties (**Yalah Gralhund**), and no source gives the Alliance a day-one dealing with the house. `[research]`
- **The Open Lord's Watch** (ally): Jalester carries a badge of the Watch and reads its file ledger, and the Commander of the Watch reports to the Open Lord, whom the Alliance serves (**The City Watch**; **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords**). Alliance agents work where the Watch cannot be seen to act, and the magisters are more likely to excuse them if Laeral has cause to let them off (Alexandrian remix, Appendix B; *Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords** (ally): Laeral is the Alliance's highest authority in the city, and the Alliance is her instrument for acting outside the Palace (**A City Watching Itself**). The published adventure says the Masked Lords' identities are hidden even from one another, and it makes Mirt, her closest advisor, one of the Lords (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Neverwinter and Dagult Neverember** (member, at odds): Neverwinter is one of the Alliance's named cities, and its Lord Protector is the former Open Lord whose hidden gold the Alliance wants back for the treasury (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Dagult Neverember**; Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Lords' Alliance*, citing the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e). Jarlaxle wants to oust Neverwinter and its lord from the coalition (**Jarlaxle Baenre**).

## Key Members

- **Jalester Silvermane** — Chondathan human field operative from Cormyr who reports to Laeral Silverhand and gives the Alliance's assignments in person. See the **Jalester Silvermane** page in Notable Figures.
- **Laeral Silverhand** — Open Lord of Waterdeep and the Alliance's ultimate authority in the city. Her Notable Figures page lists her affiliation as the City of Waterdeep. She does not play the Grand Game herself, and the Alliance acts on her behalf. See the **Laeral Silverhand** page in Notable Figures.
- **Sevel Dastar** — A middle-aged Chondathan human in a plain brown coat who works for the Alliance as a spy.

## Grand Game Agenda

The Alliance wants the gold home. Laeral wants it recovered and properly disbursed, but at campaign start Jalester's reports place the vault in Neverwinter, so the Alliance has nothing to act on yet. The Alliance will not let anyone keep the gold. In exchange for returning it, it offers renown and political standing that no other patron in the city can match. Anyone who pockets the gold and claims ignorance will find that Laeral is a thorough investigator.

## Overview

The Lords' Alliance is a coalition of rulers and operatives from Waterdeep and the allied cities of the North. Its members hold that civilization must be actively defended. In Waterdeep it works as a pro-city intelligence and security apparatus with direct reach to the Open Lord. Its agents are paid to watch for threats to the city's stability, both outside the walls and within them.

> [!gamemaster]**GM Notes**
>
> - **Laeral's intelligence:** Laeral believes the vault is in Neverwinter, and she does not know the Stone has resurfaced or that four villain factions are active. Her information comes largely from the Watch and from Jalester, and it runs about three moves behind the factions.
> - **The Splinter and Manshoon's name:** Few in the cell know their master's name, and outsiders call the cell the Splinter. The Alliance uses that name and has no name for the cell's leader.
> - **The Cassalanters:** The Alliance has no proof against the Cassalanters and knows nothing of a pact. Its speakers think the family is too rich and too pleasant, and the Watch has closed three complaints against the house.
> - **Jalester:** He carries a badge of the City Watch and reads the Watch's file ledger each tenday. He is grieving his partner, Faerrel Dunblade, who died last year exposing a plot against the government, and he fills the silence with work.
> - **Luskan:** Bregan D'aerthe wants the Alliance to admit Luskan, and the Alliance's leaders have voiced their opposition. Jarlaxle means to change that by persuading Laeral to champion the city.

**Tone:** Formal and exact. The Alliance is sincere in its civic duty and rigid in its habits, and the narrator neither mocks it nor flatters it.
