# The Cassalanters

> Structuring draft.

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> - **Campaign role:** Villain faction; racing to acquire the vault's gold before the twins' ninth birthday, Flamerule 11, when their soul pact with Asmodeus comes due; Founders' Day (Flamerule 1) is the date the family has chosen for its feast.
> - **Contacts:** **Victoro Cassalanter**, **Ammalia Cassalanter**, **Willifort Crowelle** (villa-facing); **Seffia Naelryke** (field operative)
> - **Villain profile:** see **Cassalanters** in Villains
> - **Mechanics:** response teams, outposts, escalation and revelation list are on the **Cassalanters** page in the Factions Guide
> - **Featured in:** **Fireball!**, **Faction Outposts**, **Cassalanter Villa**

**Purpose:** Gives the GM the Cassalanters as a household and a cult: where the family came from, what it believes, how it is built and how it works, who stands for or against it, and what it hides. The page keeps lore only and presents the family as it stands on Ches 1, 1492 DR. Everything above the Overview is for the GM.

The Cassalanters are a Waterdhavian noble family of banking wealth, several generations on the noble registry, whose public life is generous charity and whose private business is a contract with Asmodeus.

## History

The entries below follow the Cassalanters from their ennoblement to the morning the campaign opens. **A Debt in Blood** in History holds the full account of the bargain, and these entries summarize it.

- **1248 DR, ennoblement:** The Lords of Waterdeep recognized several merchant houses and granted them noble titles and seals, and House Cassalanter was one of them (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *City of Splendors: Waterdeep*, 3e). The campaign keeps the date and says only that the name has stood on the noble registry for several generations (**A Debt in Blood**).
- **The 14th and 15th centuries DR, a banking fortune and a Masked Lord:** The wiki says the house made its fortune in banking, money-lending and rumor-mongering for the highest payer, and names Ohrl Cassalanter as its patriarch (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Cassalanter*, 1e) `[research: wiki only]`. Ohrl's son Caladorn set the family name aside until he had done something worthy of it (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Caladorn Cassalanter*, 1e and 3e). He served as a Masked Lord, earned the title of hero of the North and fathered Victoro (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). His ghost now waits in the family crypt below the villa, where it attacks anything with a fiendish taint (**Caladorn Cassalanter**). Campaign change: the wiki also makes him an Open Lord, and the campaign keeps him a Masked Lord only (**The Open Lord and the Masked Lords**).
- **1480 and 1483 DR, the children are born (derived):** Osvaldo was born in 1480 DR, and the twins, Terenzio and Elzerina, were born on Flamerule 11, 1483 DR. No source states either year. Osvaldo's comes from his ninth year in 1489 DR, and the twins' comes from their ninth birthday on Flamerule 11, 1492 DR (**A Debt in Blood**).
- **About 1489 DR, the ruin:** The family's banking was near bankruptcy. The Masked Lords voted Dagult Neverember out of the Open Lord's seat in 1489 DR (**The Open Lord's Fall**), and his embezzlement had emptied the civic funds that Victoro's loans were written against. Campaign change: the published adventure gives the near bankruptcy and no cause, and the campaign ties it to Neverember, as the Alexandrian author's backstory does (**A Debt in Blood**; `campaign/structure/arc-g-cassalanter-villa.md` line 11).
- **About 1489 DR, the bargain:** Victoro and Ammalia Cassalanter are Waterdeep aristocrats who made a devil's bargain. The details vary depending on who is speaking. Three years ago, they forged a pact with Asmodeus, trading their children's souls for power and prosperity. Ammalia found the way through infernal research, and a pit fiend carried the contract to the family. Victoro and Ammalia signed it together in the temple beneath their villa, after they had read the terms, and the deal also bought good health and long life (**A Debt in Blood**; `arc-g-cassalanter-villa.md` line 11).
- **About 1489 DR, Osvaldo:** The contract claims each child at nine, and Osvaldo, who was nine, was taken at once. His soul went to Asmodeus and his body became a chain devil, which the family keeps in the soundproofed attic of the villa, room C24, under the watch of two imps in spider form. Ammalia visits weekly and sits in the room outside the pentagram, and Victoro has not entered in months. The city hears that Osvaldo studies in a faraway city, and the twins have been told the same (**A Debt in Blood**; **Osvaldo Cassalanter**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). Campaign change: every child is taken at nine (user ruling, Session 47).
- **Before Ches 1, 1492 DR, undated, the temple and the cult:** The temple of Siamorphe that lay beneath the estate was desecrated by the family's devil-worship and given to Asmodeus, and its thirty-foot statue was carved from an image of Siamorphe (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **The Cassalanters** in Villains). Jandar Chergoba's debt to Victoro turned into cult service three years ago, which places some of the cult's household recruiting at the time of the bargain (`arc-g-cassalanter-villa.md` line 98). No source dates the temple's conversion, the cult's founding or the arrival of its other members `[research]`.
- **1489 to 1492 DR, the rise:** The family built the charitable reputation, the political connections and the public warmth that make it Waterdeep's most admired household, and none of it is faked (**A Debt in Blood**). Ammalia's charity in the Field Ward feeds hundreds, and Victoro's banking underwrites half the guilds in the city (`arc-g-cassalanter-villa.md` line 17). The Harpers have watched the charity for years and found it clean (**A Debt in Blood**).
- **Before Ches 1, 1492 DR, undated, the loophole and the Eye:** The Cassalanters found a loophole. A ritual can buy out the children's souls for "one shy of a million gold coins" and ninety-nine souls. They hold just over half the gold. They need Neverember's vault to cover the remainder. An infernal divination placed Eye #2 of the Stone of Golorr in the Brandath Crypts, and Victoro sent Seffia Naelryke and Arn Xalrondar to take it and to silence three cult members who knew too much. The two killed two of them, and the third, a florist named Vaelle Lurval, survived without Victoro knowing it (**Seffia Naelryke**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). The family keeps Eye #2 secured in the temple (**The Cassalanters** in Villains). Campaign change: the published adventure sends the pair after the Stone itself, and the campaign changes the object to Eye #2.
- **Ches 1, 1492 DR, the campaign opens:** Osvaldo is in the attic, and the family holds Eye #2 and about 500,000 gp. It knows that the vault's entrance lies in the Brandath family crypts and does not know that the Stone is in play (**What Each Faction Knows at Start**). On their ninth birthday, Flamerule 11, the pact comes due. Founders' Day falls ten days before, on Flamerule 1; that is when the Cassalanters plan to collect the ninety-nine souls.

## Beliefs

- **A Good Name Is Worth Keeping:** The charity is real. The family funds temples, orphanages and civic improvements, and Victoro is known as a worshipper of Lathander. Their warmth, including their warmth toward tieflings, is specific and genuine. Victoro keeps his philanthropist reputation as a working part of the plan, and the Harpers, who watch the family's charitable work, have found nothing wrong with it for years. The published adventure says Victoro's devotion to Asmodeus is a secret known only to his wife and his closest friends, and that most Waterdhavians know him as a banker, a philanthropist and a worshipper of Lathander whose profits help to feed and shelter the poor (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Family Comes First:** The love for the twins is real and drives every decision. Victoro and Ammalia would do anything to save Terenzio and Elzerina. Neither twin belongs to the cult, and both have been told that Osvaldo attends school in a faraway city (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The published adventure adds that Victoro's plan to win back the twins is meant to ease his guilt over Osvaldo (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Take Any Genuine Alternative:** The family has settled on the Founders' Day feast because it has found no other way to save the twins. Victoro and Ammalia are desperate parents, not zealots, and they would prefer any genuine alternative to the feast. The buy-out is the one exit the family has found, and the published adventure says that reneging on a contract with Asmodeus is a luxury no mortal can afford (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **A Contract Is a Contract:** Asmodeus does not negotiate with mortals who have already signed, so the family works inside the terms. They hunt for loopholes, never for release. Victoro is the family's priest and Ammalia makes the final calls. The god they signed with sells pleasure and power beyond a buyer's imagination, and his bargains are known to cost more than the buyer expects (**Asmodeus** in Deities).
- **Tell the Truth and Leave Out the Price:** Everything the Cassalanters tell outsiders about their situation is technically true. They never mention that the ritual also requires poisoning 99 people at the Founders' Day feast.
- **Keep the Temple Hidden:** An illegal temple to Asmodeus lies beneath the villa, and discovery of it is a capital offense under the Code Legal once a magistrate has ruled it unlawful, and it ruins the plan. Willifort Crowelle manages household security. Devil worship alone is not illegal in Waterdeep, so what the family guards is the temple and what is done in it (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Asmodeus** in Deities). The published adventure also says the family relies on its noble standing for protection and that the last thing it wants is the City Watch at its door (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Service Is Worship:** The cult's members fold devotion into the work they do for the family. Tissina Khyret attends Ammalia, Nana Rosse minds the twins, Jandar Chergoba cooks and Seffia Naelryke runs field operations, and none of them sees a conflict between the job and the god. They came to the faith by different roads: Tissina out of loyalty to Ammalia, Nana Rosse to stay close to the twins, and Jandar through a debt to Victoro that became cult service (`arc-g-cassalanter-villa.md` lines 96-98).
- **Rank Is Kept:** The cult runs on a strict order. Victoro leads the worship and Ammalia pours the wine, the head servants keep the other members in line, and Seffia is a true believer in the cult's hierarchy (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Seffia Naelryke**). The **Asmodeus** page says his priests obey his Chosen as lesser devils obey greater ones, and no file names a Chosen in the campaign.
- **Status Draws the Faithful:** The cult's members include evil-minded nobles and deluded commoners lured by the prospect of wealth and status. The gatherings give the Cassalanters a place to show their power to the people below them (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Doctrine and Rites:** The cult meets in the temple beneath the villa at midnight on the first day of every tenday. Members wear crimson robes and golden masks and carry golden sacrificial daggers. They pledge devotion to Asmodeus, call out the names of their enemies and ask him to visit ruin on them, and they drink wine, share gossip and now and then offer a sacrifice (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The one sacrament the campaign names is the font of grey water at the Asmodean Shrine, which is enchanted to mimic the River Styx (**The Cassalanters** in the Factions Guide). [research] The campaign files do not give the cult's creed. The **Asmodeus** page holds candidate doctrine, including the Fugue Plane pitch of comfort from a devil or a rank in the infernal host, and no file says this cult teaches it.

## Structure

- **The family:** Victoro and Ammalia head the house, and Victoro leads the cult as its priest. Their twins, Terenzio and Elzerina, are eight years old. The twins are tutored privately, rarely leave the estate and belong to no cult. Their eldest brother Osvaldo is the chain devil in the attic (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Osvaldo Cassalanter**).
- **The villa:** Cassalanter Villa stands in the Sea Ward, where the wiki puts it on the southwest corner of Delzorin Street and Diamond Street (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Cassalanter*, 3e). White walls and a crimson roof with turrets rise behind a wrought-iron gate, and the campaign journal gives the house four stories above ground, a basement and a sub-basement (**Cassalanter Villa**, location). Hired guards in house livery hold the front gate and the garden gate. Only the family, its personal attendants and its guards live in the house, and the other servants arrive before dawn from homes in the Dock Ward and the Field Ward (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The temple and the crypt:** The temple lies below the wine cellar, behind a secret door marked with a sun symbol and down a spiral stair, and a thirty-foot statue of Asmodeus fills its ceremonial hall (**The Cassalanters** in Villains). The family crypt opens off the temple's entry. Twelve stone sarcophagi bear the family crest, a sun over a scale in the Siamorphe heraldic tradition, and Caladorn's ghost holds the crypt (**Cassalanter Villa**, location).
- **The cult:** Victoro leads it, and the whole cult gathers in the temple at midnight on the first day of every tenday. About thirty members attend when it meets in force, roughly half of them nobles and the rest Waterdhavian commoners (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). Willifort, Tissina Khyret, Nana Rosse and Jandar Chergoba keep order at the rites while Ammalia pours the wine. The cult reaches beyond the household. Yalah Gralhund keeps Asmodean symbols and robes with Cassalanter embroidery in a trunk, and her groundskeeper Hurv Taldred wears a Cassalanter devotional mark (**House Gralhund**).
- **Chain of command:** Ammalia makes the final calls for the family and the cult, and Victoro, who leads the worship, has stopped questioning her. Willifort Crowelle links the family to everyone who works for it. He runs household security, reports to Ammalia and directs the spy and the poisoner. Seffia Naelryke commands the field team and answers to Victoro, and the head servants keep order at the rites.
- **Household staff:** The villa employs sixteen named staff, and ten of them know nothing about the temple (`arc-g-cassalanter-villa.md` line 91). The six cult members are Willifort the butler, Tissina Khyret the attendant, Nana Rosse the twins' caretaker, Jandar Chergoba the head chef, and the field pair Seffia Naelryke and Arn Xalrondar. The other ten are Corvo (footman), Pela (laundress), Marchello (gardener), Fiorella (cook's assistant), Doro (stable hand), Lissette (upstairs maid), Rennet (scullion), Maesta (housekeeper), Tomas (night watchman) and Bressa (nursemaid). Maesta manages the non-cult staff and is loyal to the household's reputation, not to the Cassalanters themselves.
- **Field operatives and outposts:** Seffia Naelryke commands the family's precise field team from the Converted Windmill on Coachlamp Lane in the Southern Ward, where Arn Xalrondar lives with her in plain dress. Willifort leads a second, blunter team that the family draws on once discretion has failed. The family also owns the Asmodean Shrine, a converted residence on Aveen Street in the North Ward that it bought for the Hell cyst below it (**The Cassalanters** in the Factions Guide). Rella Asp and Ivrala Sorn work under the covers of a cook and a server and report to Willifort (their pages in Notable Figures).
- **Imps and devils:** Six imps in raven form roost in the butterfly garden, where Ammalia uses them as spies and sentries, and two imps in spider form guard Osvaldo's door (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The cult also calls devils to its work. Seffia's team calls spined devils, and Willifort's team can call a bearded devil (**Seffia Naelryke**; **The Cassalanters** in the Factions Guide).
- **Money and fronts:** The family banks through the Cassalanter Counting House in the Sea Ward, which holds accounts across the North Ward, and the Cassalanter Foundation runs an orphan fund (**Sea Ward**, Gazetteer). The family holds about 500,000 gp of its own, and the outposts gather gold and ritual components toward the payment (**A Debt in Blood**).

The family's response teams, outposts, escalation and revelation list are on the **Cassalanters** page in the Factions Guide.

## Methods and Tactics

The Cassalanters work through goodwill, money and information, and they use force only when those tools fail.

- **Galas and dinners:** The family hosts public galas during Waukeentide, the tenday from Tarsakh 1 to 10, and its social events peak then (**Sea Ward**, Gazetteer; **Timekeeping and the Festival Calendar**). Sea Ward dinner parties serve as intelligence operations and the galas as recruitment screens `[research]` (**The Grand Game**).
- **Charity:** Ammalia's charity in the Field Ward feeds hundreds, and part of Victoro's profits goes to feeding and sheltering the poor (`arc-g-cassalanter-villa.md` line 17; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). The same charity is the means for Founders' Day. Ammalia intends to invite one hundred of Waterdeep's poorest to the villa's lawn (**A Debt in Blood**).
- **Bargaining:** Ammalia is known for driving a hard bargain, and she has banked enough goodwill in Waterdeep to win favor with a magistrate (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). Victoro's office holds thirteen loan contracts with nobles, merchants and guilds, and they amount to control of the Chandlers', Coopers' and Carpenters' guilds, among others (**The Guilds**). Everything the Cassalanters tell outsiders about their situation is technically true. The ritual requires the sacrifice. The twins are in danger. The Cassalanters love their children and would do anything to save them. They don't mention that the ritual also requires poisoning 99 people at the Founders' Day feast.
- **Intelligence:** Willifort makes a point of knowing everything that happens in the villa, and he escorts every guest for the whole visit and steers nosy ones away from anything that could link the family to the cult (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). Ammalia sends her raven imps on errands across the city. Rella Asp collects intelligence on the patrons where she cooks, and Ivrala Sorn files reports on the regulars where she serves and on who drinks with whom (their pages in Notable Figures). The wiki says the house made part of its fortune in rumor-mongering (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Cassalanter*, 1e). Victoro has asked to be told when the Stone is found, and the family does not hunt for it with agents (**A Debt in Blood**; GM Notes below).
- **Poison:** Ammalia keeps ten vials of the poison called midnight tears in her private study, and she is immune to poison (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). She means to serve ninety-nine of the one hundred guests wine laced with it, so that they die within a day and nothing connects them to the house (**The Cassalanters** in Villains). Jandar Chergoba sees that the poison is distributed correctly. Rella Asp is a poisoner under a cook's cover (**Rella Asp**).
- **Pressure before force:** The published adventure says the family tries to misdirect and discredit its opponents and not to murder them (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The family uses the city's own leverage first, in the form of Watch complaints, noble pressure and guild sanctions, and infernal force comes last (**The Cassalanters** in Villains). Willifort is the exception to that restraint, and he burns bridges, gathers intelligence and, when necessary, solves problems permanently (**Willifort Crowelle**).
- **Recruiting:** The cult draws nobles and commoners with the promise of wealth and status (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). It also binds people through debt, as with Jandar, through loyalty, as with Tissina, and through closeness to the twins, as with Nana Rosse (`arc-g-cassalanter-villa.md` lines 96-98). The **Asmodeus** page says his priests exploit greed and impatience, and the wiki says his cult works as a mutual-aid society for merchants and nobles who raise one another in rank (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Asmodeus*, 3e) `[research]`.
- **Funding:** The family lives on banking and money-lending, and the Counting House and the guild loans carry most of it. The family holds about 500,000 gp of its own and needs the vault's gold for the rest of the price (**A Debt in Blood**).
- **Fighting:** Victoro and Ammalia are formidable spellcasters who are devoted to one another, and if either is assailed the other comes as fast as possible (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). A badly hurt Cassalanter tries to negotiate or bribe before fighting on (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Victoro Cassalanter**).
- **What the family will not do:** The Cassalanters will not renegotiate with Asmodeus, because the terms are fixed once signed (**A Debt in Blood**). They will not bring the Watch to their door or murder when misdirection will serve (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). They will not tell the twins about Osvaldo, and they do not speak of him to guests (**A Debt in Blood**; **Osvaldo Cassalanter**). They do not compete with the other factions for the Stone and wait for someone to open the vault (**A Debt in Blood**).

## Relationships

Each entry gives the stance on Ches 1, 1492 DR. Outside the household and the cult, nobody knows about the pact, so every faction below only suspects the family, and Jarlaxle is the one exception.

- **The Harpers (neutral, watching):** The Harpers have watched the family's charitable work for years and found nothing, which they find suspicious, and Ammalia knows they watch (**A Debt in Blood**; **The Cassalanters** in Villains, `[research]`). Mirt keeps a dossier and suspects that Cassalanter money stood behind the Howling Hatred cult, but the Harpers do not know what the family hides (**The Harpers**).
- **The Lords' Alliance (neutral, suspicious):** The Alliance has no proof and knows nothing of a pact. Its speakers think the family too rich and too pleasant, and Jalester Silvermane says the Watch cannot touch the Cassalanters without evidence because they are too well connected (**The Lords' Alliance**; **The Open Lord's Watch**).
- **The Emerald Enclave (neutral, suspicious at most):** Jeryth Phaulkon senses an infernal influence in the Southern Ward, and the Enclave does not know whose it is. Her reports place a wound in the ley lines near Coachlamp Lane, where the family's windmill stands, and Enclave speakers call the Cassalanters a rich house and nothing worse (**The Emerald Enclave**).
- **The Order of the Gauntlet (enemy, on suspicion only):** Savra Belabranta has suspected the family for months and has no legal evidence, and the Order will move aggressively once it has some. Sister Valdra holds three independent pieces of Cassalanter intelligence, and nobody in the Order knows what the family worships (**The Order of the Gauntlet**).
- **Force Grey (neutral, suspicious):** Scrying from Blackstaff Tower has found conjuration and abjuration residue beneath the villa. Vajra Safahr cannot say what makes it, and nobody in Force Grey knows of a pact (**Force Grey**; **The Cassalanters** in Villains).
- **The Doom Raiders (neutral, rival lender):** Istrid Horn lends at rates comparable to the family's, and the published adventure counts the Cassalanters among her competitors (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Istrid Horn**). No source gives the cell any dealing with the family `[research]`.
- **Bregan D'aerthe (neutral, informed):** Jarlaxle knows about the pact through Vessa, who sits in the family's social circle under a permanent human cover, and he is the only outsider who does. He has no wish to act unless he sees a strategic gain, and he tells no member of the company unless the member works it out (**The Cassalanters** in Villains; **Jarlaxle Baenre**; **Vessa**).
- **Xanathar's Guild (neutral):** No source gives a dealing between them. The Guild holds Eye #1, which the family needs, and nothing says the family knows where it lies (**What Each Faction Knows at Start**) `[research]`.
- **Manshoon's Zhentarim (neutral, rebuffed):** Seffia Naelryke approached Vevette Blackwater to open a communication channel, and Vevette refused, suspecting a trap. Neither side has made contact since, and Manshoon is as suspicious of the family's wealth and spotless name as anyone `[research]` (**Manshoon**; **Vevette Blackwater**).
- **House Gralhund (ally, hidden co-religionists):** The Gralhund household and the Cassalanter household belong to the same Asmodean cult, and a Cassalanter journal lists "Inform Y.G." beside the Cassalanter mausoleum. Nobody outside the household and the cult knows this (**House Gralhund**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Open Lord's Watch (neutral, unaware):** The Watch has closed three complaints against the house and has no hint of a pact. It keeps a watch box at the corner outside the gate and posts a guard at the service gate (**The Open Lord's Watch**; **Sea Ward**, Gazetteer).
- **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords (neutral):** The Lords of Waterdeep ennobled the house in 1248 DR, and Caladorn sat among the Masked Lords, so the family still shows his portrait as a sign of civic virtue (**The Open Lord and the Masked Lords**). The family's ruin traces to Dagult Neverember, the Open Lord before Laeral Silverhand, and no source gives a dealing between Laeral and the house `[research]`.

## Overview

The Cassalanters are a noble family of Waterdeep and among the city's most prominent philanthropists. Victoro, a half-elf banker and worshipper of Lathander, and Ammalia, a wizard known for driving a hard bargain and for keeping the most beautiful butterfly garden in Waterdeep, fund temples, orphanages and civic improvements across the city. The family lives in a villa in the Sea Ward, is publicly known for its ancestral devotion to Siamorphe, and hosts public galas during Waukeentide, and the younger twins, Terenzio and Elzerina, are often seen at play in the gardens. Their eldest son is said to be at school in a faraway city, and more than one Waterdhavian has wondered aloud how a family can be so gracious, so prosperous and so fortunate all at once.

> [!gamemaster]**GM Notes**
>
> - **Origins:** The Cassalanter name has stood in Waterdeep's noble registry for several generations. The Lords of Waterdeep ennobled the house in 1248 DR, about 244 years before the campaign, when they granted titles to several merchant houses (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *City of Splendors: Waterdeep*, 3e). The wiki lists Waukeen, Siamorphe and Asmodeus as the house's favored deities. Financial ruin was the trigger for the bargain, which was made three years ago, about 1489 DR. The deal also bought power, good health and long life.
> - **The public face is true:** The family built its charitable reputation, political connections and public warmth in the years since the bargain, and none of it is faked. They became what they presented themselves as while also accumulating what Asmodeus requires.
> - **Social cover:** Sea Ward dinner parties are intelligence operations, and the Cassalanters' charitable galas are recruitment screens. A charitable hospital serves as the family's front business.
> - **Chain of command:** Ammalia makes the final calls and Victoro has stopped questioning this. Willifort Crowelle reports to Ammalia on everything that happens in the villa. Seffia Naelryke's response team answers to Lord Cassalanter, and Rella Asp and Ivrala Sorn report to Willifort.
> - **Who suspects:** Everybody is suspicious of the Cassalanters because they are too nice to be this rich and this purebred, and nobody outside the household and the cult knows they are infernalists.
>   - The Order of the Gauntlet is suspicious of the family and will move against the family once it has solid evidence. Sister Valdra holds three independent pieces of Cassalanter intelligence.
>   - The Emerald Enclave senses infernal influence in the Southern Ward through Jeryth and does not know whose it is.
>   - Vevette Blackwater of the Zhentarim rebuffed Seffia's approach to open a communication channel, suspecting a trap.
> - **The one exception:** Jarlaxle knows about the pact through **Vessa**, his doppelganger spy in the Cassalanter social circle. He never tells Bregan D'aerthe members unless they work it out themselves.
> - **Caladorn Cassalanter:** Victoro's father. The wiki has him a Masked Lord and later Open Lord of Waterdeep, by 1399 DR (*Blackstaff Tower*, 2e and 3e). The family uses the portrait of the former Masked Lord and hero of the North as a symbol of civic virtue. His ghost waits in the family crypt below the villa.
> - **The Eye:** The Cassalanters hold Eye #2 of the Stone of Golorr, which Seffia's infernal divination found in the Brandath Crypts. They still need the Stone and Eyes #1 and #3, so the family waits with a standing request to be told when the Stone is found, and moves on the vault the moment someone activates it.

> [!gamemaster]**Secret**
>
> - **The pact:** Victoro and Ammalia traded the souls of their children to Asmodeus for wealth compressed from three generations into one and for political access that normally takes a family centuries to build. They discussed it, understood what they were signing, and signed, three years ago. The pact covers three children: Osvaldo, already taken, and the twins.
> - **Osvaldo:** The eldest son's soul was claimed first, when he was nine, the age at which the contract claims each child, and he is now twelve and a chain devil held in the soundproofed attic of the villa, room C24, never the basement or the temple. Ammalia visits weekly, sitting in the room outside the pentagram to read to him while he curses her and weeps. Victoro has not visited in months. Willifort knows. Two imps are always present. Terenzio and Elzerina have been told Osvaldo is at school. Had the contract run its course, the twins were to become lemures (Forgotten Realms Wiki, Victoro Cassalanter, from *Dragon Heist*, 5e).
> - **The feast:** On Founders' Day, Flamerule 1, the family plans to poison 99 guests at a feast to collect the ninety-nine souls. Ammalia is the architect of the feast and Jandar Chergoba, the villa's head chef, is the person who sees that the poison is distributed correctly.
> - **The deadline:** The pact comes due on Flamerule 11, the twins' ninth birthday. Founders' Day on Flamerule 1 is the feast and Ammalia's real deadline. It is her operational window, chosen for the civic cover and for the time left to verify the terms, and it does not appear in the contract.
> - **The loophole:** A ritual can buy out the children's souls. The contract requires 999,999 gp and ninety-nine souls, both, and the souls are the guests poisoned at the Founders' Day feast. The family holds about 500,000 gp of its own, and the Vault's 500,000 gp is the shortfall.

**Tone:** Warm and civil in the public lines, grave and exact in the Secret. The narrator never gloats over the family and does not excuse them either.
