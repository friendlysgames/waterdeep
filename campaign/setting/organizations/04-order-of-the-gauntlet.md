# The Order of the Gauntlet

> Structuring draft.

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> - **Campaign role:** Player faction; martial agents hunting diabolists and aberrant evil across the city, and suspicious of the Cassalanters.
> - **Contacts:** **Savra Belabranta** (primary)
> - **Savra's past:** She is in her late twenties and looks older, as people do who have been through something and come out the other side. She wears her dark hair back, has a strong jaw and speaks with the patient, measured manner of someone who learned to think before speaking and found the habit worth keeping. She spent three years in the Howling Hatred elemental cult and left it in 1491 DR, about a year ago, before she was an Order knight. She does not hide that she belonged to the cult, and she speaks of what she did there only with people she has come to trust. She is making amends, and the Order is the work she found that made that meaningful.
> - **Mechanics:** the Grand Game stance, quest hooks, first meeting, renown, ranks and missions are on the **Order of the Gauntlet** page in the Factions Guide
> - **Featured in:** **Fireball!**, **Gralhund Villa**, **Xanathar's Lair**, **Cassalanter Villa**, **Vault of Dragons**

**Purpose:** Gives the GM the Order of the Gauntlet as a faith-driven order: where it came from, what it believes, how its Waterdeep chapter is built and works, who its friends and enemies are, and what it suspects about one noble family. The page keeps lore only. Everything above the Overview is for the GM.

The Order of the Gauntlet is a fellowship of paladins, clerics and devout martial warriors who hunt evil before it reaches the city, and its small Waterdeep chapter works out of the Halls of Justice under Savra Belabranta.

## History

No source gives the Order a founding date or a founder, and this page invents neither. The entries below run from the 1480s to the day the campaign starts.

- **1480s DR, the Cult of the Dragon:** When the Cult of the Dragon began to amass vast stockpiles of treasure, the Order went to investigate. Working with the Harpers, it tracked the cult's shipments across the Sword Coast to learn how far the scheme reached. The cult's plan to summon Tiamat then forced the powers of the west and the North into a coalition, and the Council of Waterdeep met with the Order as one of its five factions. The Order's delegate was Ontharr Frume, a paladin of Torm from Elturel (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet* and *Council of Waterdeep*, 5e).
- **Late 15th century DR, the Chult expedition:** The Order sent an expedition of fifty members to Chult to clear the jungle of undead, and the wiki calls the venture ill-fated. It was led by Niles Breakbone, a nobleman the wiki calls incompetent. The first camp, Camp Righteous, was taken over by undead, and the Order then founded Camp Vengeance farther up the river near the Aldani Basin. The sources give no year (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e).
- **1487 DR, the Second Sundering ends:** The mergers of the Spellplague era are undone, and Tyr, Torm and Helm stand again as separate, living gods, so all three powers the Order honors are present in 1492 DR (**Tyr**, **Torm**, **Helm**). The 4e abdication and deaths of Tyr and Helm do not hold in the campaign.
- **1489 DR, a young power:** The *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide* counts the Order among the newest power groups in Faerûn as of this year (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e). About three years before the campaign, the Waterdeep chapter took a cache of magic weapons from a diabolist's cellar in the Dock Ward and put it in the armory (**Vindicator**; the year is derived from "three years ago").
- **Early 1490s DR, the Order in the North:** The Order sought an alliance with the Knights of Samular for a joint campaign in the Savage Frontier. In 1491 DR it tried to gain political influence in the Sumber Hills of the Dessarin Valley, and it courted local leaders, among them the Waterbaron of Yartar (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, citing *Princes of the Apocalypse*, 5e).
- **1491 DR, the Howling Hatred falls:** The Howling Hatred was an elemental air cult that served Yan-C-Bin, the Prince of Evil Air. It had worked in Waterdeep and met in a derelict Dock Ward tower for its last two years. The cult was beaten in the north, and the Order's Waterdeep chapter arrested its remaining agents within a tenday. The high priest was never found, and no Watch record names who paid for the tower (**Savra's Past**). Savra Belabranta had spent three years in the cult. She left it that year, and the Order took her in (**Savra Belabranta**). Design tag: *Princes of the Apocalypse* has the cult indoctrinate her, and the wiki has her join its cover group, the Feathergale Knights (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Savra Belabranta*, 5e). The campaign has her leave in 1491 DR, a year before it starts (**Castle Ward**).
- **1491 to 1492 DR, Savra takes the Waterdeep chapter:** After she left the cult, Savra became a knight of the Order and began to serve Tyr to regain her honor (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). She works from the Halls of Justice, where she assigns the chapter's work and swears in new members (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Savra Belabranta*, 5e). About a dozen Knights and Priests answer to her (**Castle Ward**).
- **About six months before the campaign, a servant's story:** A servant tells the Watch a story about the Cassalanters, and the Watch dismisses it. About three months before the campaign, Sister Valdra, a half-orc cleric of Tyr, begins to build a case from that story on her own time. She acts without orders and without telling Savra (**Sister Valdra**).
- **Ches 1, 1492 DR, the campaign opens:** Savra has suspected the Cassalanters for months. She has no legal evidence and no investigators able to produce any, and the chapter's file records that the Watch closed every complaint against the family without findings (**The Order of the Gauntlet** in the Factions Guide; **Sea Ward**).

## Beliefs

*"Evil does not rest. Neither do we."*

- **Seek Evil Out:** The Order's mission is to find and destroy evil before it gains a foothold, and it does not wait for evil to come to it (*Dragon Heist*). In Waterdeep, the surge of violence sends its members looking for adventurers who can help return peace to the city. The wiki adds that the Order stays vigilant against threats to the Realms and investigates secretive organizations when it has to, and that it holds evil must be dealt with and cannot be ignored (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e). Every member binds themselves to this work by the oath described under Doctrine and Rites.
- **Faith Is the Weapon:** Members hold that faith is their most potent weapon against evil, and the wiki says it may rest in a personal god, in close friends or in oneself (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e). The same source says great challenges call for heroic demonstrations of strength and valor, and that members are bound by fervent belief or by a staunch dedication to enforcing justice. Savra asks the same of anyone who would serve with her: the willingness to act when acting is difficult, and the integrity to know justice from the wish to hurt someone (Alexandrian remix, Appendix B).
- **Judge the Deed:** The wiki gives the Order a rule that it is justified to punish an act of evil and never a thought or a feeling (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e). In the campaign this becomes a demand for proof. Savra files only on paper a magistrate would read, such as a ledger, a signed statement or a witness who will swear on the record, and Order agents may not enter a place of worship without a magistrate's warrant.
- **Honesty Over Management:** Savra cannot work with people who manage around her, and she knows when it is happening. She wants to build an Order chapter that values honesty above all. A member who breaks the oath answers to conscience and then to Savra, and the Order does not hunt its own, though it expects the member to say so (**Order of the Gauntlet First Meeting**).
- **Atonement Through Work:** Savra serves Tyr to regain her honor and to atone for the evil she did as a member of the Howling Hatred (*Dragon Heist*). The Order took her in after she left the cult, and her goodness is hard-won in a way that makes it more convincing. Savra holds this tenet most firmly, and no source makes it a rule for every member.
- **Diabolism First:** The Order ranks the threats it hunts in a fixed order, with diabolism first, aberrant evil second and everything else after. Savra counts Xanathar's Guild's use of intellect devourers as an abomination in her theological framework. She also wants to learn what the Cassalanters conceal behind their charity, and her years in the cult left her attuned to the signs of infernal operations (**Savra Belabranta**).
- **Hunt the Evil in Front of You:** The Order stays outside the Grand Game and does not position for the vault or compete for faction influence. Savra cares about the vault gold only as a question of who might seize it. She fears that 500,000 gp would fund whatever she suspects the Sea Ward family of doing, and she thinks the Splinter or Xanathar's Guild would be nearly as bad. She has no objection to the city treasury holding it.
- **Give Back from the Haul:** Knights live on less than they earn and give a quarter of their share of a large haul to a cause that looks after people who cannot look after themselves (see Methods and Tactics). The Alexandrian remix sets the share at a quarter of a hoard. In the Field Ward, where the Watch does not patrol, the Order fills part of the gap, and Savra wants the ward to see that someone is paying attention.
- **The Temples Are Friends:** The Order treats the Halls of Justice, the Temple of Helm and the Chapel of the Steadfast Shield as friendly to its members, and the priests of the three temples help the Order without belonging to it. Worship of Helm, Torm or Tyr only decides whom Savra addresses first, and nobody is tested for faith at the door.
- **Trust and Excellence:** Members are staunch comrades who trust one another completely, and they compete with each other and strive for excellence in all they do (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, citing *The Rise of Tiamat*, 5e).
- **Doctrine and Rites:** Recruits swear an oath to find and destroy evil in all its forms, to protect those who cannot protect themselves and to stand when standing is required. They speak it while wearing a silver gauntlet, and a priest of Tyr seals it with a brief invocation. The silver gauntlet is the Order's symbol and passes among its members, and the wiki gives the Order's emblem as a gauntlet grasping a sword by the blade (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e). Members display the holy symbols of their gods (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e). Torm's own sign is a white gauntlet raised palm forward, and at an investiture the words are "Tyr, Helm and Torm are witness to it" (**Torm**). Savra calls Tyr the Maimed God, while the priests at the Halls of Justice call him the Just God and the Even-Handed (**Tyr**). [research] The campaign files give no written creed, prayer or holy day beyond the oath and the motto.

## Structure

The Waterdeep chapter is small. It is Savra, a clerk's office in the east annex of the Halls of Justice, and a rotation of about a dozen Knights and Priests who answer to her. The Halls of Justice is the temple of Tyr in the Castle Ward, and the priests of its three temples help the Order without belonging to it.

**Primary Contact:** Savra Belabranta — a knight of the Order from a noble family known for raising griffons for the Griffon Cavalry. She spent three years in the Howling Hatred elemental cult and left it in 1491 DR, about a year ago, and she speaks of it only with people she trusts. She has channeled that shame into an iron commitment to justice.

- **Above the chapter:** No source names a head of the whole Order, and Savra leads the Waterdeep chapter only. The wiki lists chapters in several western cities, including Neverwinter, Bryn Shander and Baldur's Gate, and it names heads for single chapters, such as Ontharr Frume at Elturel (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e).
- **Membership:** Any non-evil character can join in Waterdeep, and clerics, monks and paladins are especially welcome (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). A member can also serve in the clergy or be a knight pledged to one god or temple. Some members are native Waterdhavians, and others come to the city from distant settlements on temple business (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Ranks and titles:** The ranks run from Chevall to Marcheon, Whitehawk, Vindicator and Righteous Hand (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, citing the 2014 *Dungeon Master's Guide*, 5e). A recruit becomes a Chevall when the oath is sworn, and the high priest of Tyr invests a Righteous Hand in the Halls of Justice. Savra has never held the highest rank (**The Order of the Gauntlet** in the Factions Guide; **Tyr**; **Savra Belabranta**).
- **Working posts:** Knights and Priests do the chapter's work. Priest Acolytes carry its notes and Scouts watch the places it names (**The Order of the Gauntlet** in the Factions Guide). Warden Osric Wickersham keeps the armory in the east annex. Three Knights are known by name, Dame Rhosyn Cullane, Sir Anselm Greave and Sir Merrick Tollwood (**Player Factions Overview**).
- **The Halls of Justice:** The Halls is the temple of Tyr and a seat of the courts, west of the Market in the Castle Ward, and its main hall holds sixty people. The Order's east annex holds the clerk's office, the armory and a narrow chapel, where a wooden statue of Tyr stands with its right hand carved away at the wrist. Oaths are sworn in an antechamber off the main nave, which is open every day from the second bell of morning to dusk. Father Corwen Thale, a gray-haired priest of Tyr with a missing left hand, has presided over the Order's oaths for twenty years. Isaldric Veyne is the high priest of Tyr in Waterdeep, and Brother Haldric Wence, the door-warden, keeps messages for Savra (**Tyr**; **Castle Ward**).
- **The three friendly temples:** The Halls of Justice, the Temple of Helm (Helm's Hall) in the Southern Ward and the Chapel of the Steadfast Shield in the Dock Ward treat Order members as friends. The Temple of Helm treats the Watch's poor for free and keeps a hospice wing for Watch veterans and their families. The Chapel of the Steadfast Shield is a Torm chapel, and the files give it no size, staff or street. The Hospice of St. Laupsenn in the North Ward belongs to Ilmater, and no source counts it among the Order's temples (**Helm**; **Torm**; **Ilmater**). The published adventure adds that a member can arrange meetings with local priests of non-evil gods (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Territory and holdings:** The chapter works from the Castle Ward and looks hardest at the Field Ward and at one family in the Sea Ward. In the Field Ward, Tyr's clergy argue for residents who cannot afford a hearing before the Lords, through the Legal Aid Fund of the Halls of Justice (**Field Ward**). No source gives the chapter an estate, a front business or a treasury beyond the east annex.

## Methods and Tactics

- **Hunting evil:** The Order of the Gauntlet is a fellowship of paladins, clerics, and devout martial warriors who pursue and destroy evil actively — without waiting for it to come to them. The Order strikes fast and hard and looks for decisive victories (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, citing *Dragon+*, 5e). In a fight Savra takes the place where the line is thinnest and holds it, and the diabolists die first (**Savra Belabranta**).
- **Law and the Watch:** In Waterdeep, they function as a quasi-religious law-enforcement body: more aggressive and less subtle than the City Watch, and considerably less interested in political nuance. They are exactly the allies you want when facing devil-worshippers or a mind-flayer-backed crime lord.
- **Proof before action:** Devil worship is not itself illegal in Waterdeep (*Dragon Heist*, 5e), so the chapter needs evidence of an act before it can move. Savra cannot file on a compelled answer alone, and the warrant rule sends the chapter to a magistrate whenever a trail leads into a temple. The Order's watchers observe from outside, and they do not enter private buildings or follow a target underground (**Player Factions Overview**; **The Code Legal and the Magisters**).
- **Watching a family:** The Order has a particular interest in the Cassalanters — a Sea Ward noble family whose impeccable public piety and conspicuous generosity the Order finds deeply suspicious. Months of quiet surveillance have produced no actionable evidence. Something is concealed behind the charity and the temple attendance; the Order cannot yet prove what. The Order grows more impatient for hard evidence it can act on. Savra names no household until a document or a sworn answer does (**Field Ward**).
- **Gathering information:** The chapter draws on Savra's field experience of devils and how they fight. The priests of its three temples brief members on patterns of trouble in a ward, and those briefings name nobody (**The Order of the Gauntlet** in the Factions Guide; **Player Factions Overview**). A priest of Tyr arranges *speak with dead* on a body the Watch holds, through the door-warden at the Halls of Justice (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Castle Ward**). When a member is held by the Watch, the published adventure has an Order member put in a good word and tend the member's affairs (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **Recruiting:** Savra recruits in person and visits a candidate's residence herself (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). She looks for people who fight evil in all its forms, and she speaks first to worshipers of Helm, Torm or Tyr (**The Deities**). The Order does not share members with other factions (**Order of the Gauntlet First Meeting**). Savra offers help in kind and never offers money (Alexandrian remix, Appendix D).
- **Funding:** The Order also expects its knights to live on less than they earn, and it asks them to give a quarter of their share of a large haul to a cause that looks after people who cannot look after themselves. No source names another income for the chapter. [research]
- **What it will not do:** The Order will not punish a thought or a feeling, because it holds that only an act of evil justifies punishment (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e). It will not enter a place of worship without a warrant, and it will not name a household it cannot back with paper. It does not position for the vault or compete for faction influence in the Grand Game, and it does not offer money to those it recruits.

## Relationships

- **The Harpers (ally):** The Order and the Harpers work in very different ways and share the same goals for Faerûn, and in the 1480s they tracked Cult of the Dragon shipments together (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Order of the Gauntlet*, 5e). The Harpers have watched the Cassalanters for years and found nothing, which matches the Order's own file, and no source says either body has shared its suspicion with the other (**The Harpers**).
- **The Lords' Alliance (neutral):** The Alliance and the Order sat among the five factions of the Council of Waterdeep, where the Alliance held seven seats and the other factions held a single seat or a few (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Council of Waterdeep*, 5e). No source gives a day-one dealing between them. [research]
- **The Emerald Enclave (neutral):** The Enclave also sat on the Council of Waterdeep beside the Order (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Council of Waterdeep*, 5e). Jeryth Phaulkon feels an infernal influence in the Southern Ward, where the Temple of Helm stands, and the Enclave cannot trace it to a house, while the Order hunts infernal cults (**Sea Ward**; **The Cassalanters**). No source gives a day-one dealing between them. [research]
- **Force Grey (neutral):** The published adventure counts Force Grey and the Order among the respectable factions whose members can plead for leniency before a magistrate (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). Vajra Safahr, the Blackstaff, has scried conjuration and abjuration residue beneath the Cassalanter villa (**The Cassalanters** in Villains). No source says she has told the Order, and no source gives a day-one dealing between Force Grey and the Order. [research]
- **The Doom Raiders (neutral):** The Order treats Davil Starsong's Doom Raiders and the Splinter as separate bodies and keeps them apart (**The Order of the Gauntlet** in the Factions Guide). The Watch keeps a file on Davil's cell, and no source gives the Order a day-one dealing with it (**The Open Lord's Watch**). [research]
- **Bregan D'aerthe (neutral, unaware):** The Order knows the Sea Maidens Faire's ships only as a carnival fleet and does not know who owns them (**The Order of the Gauntlet** in the Factions Guide). No source gives a day-one dealing. [research]
- **Xanathar's Guild (enemy):** The Order counts the Guild's use of intellect devourers and enslaved creatures as an abomination (Alexandrian remix, Appendix B). Guild dealers work the Field Ward, where the Order fills part of the gap the Watch leaves (**Field Ward**).
- **Manshoon's Zhentarim (enemy):** The Splinter pays gang leaders in the Field Ward (**Field Ward**). The Order knows the cell as the Splinter or the other cell and names no master for it (**The Order of the Gauntlet** in the Factions Guide). The published adventure counts the Order among the bodies that would join an effort to oust Manshoon if given proof that he hides in the city (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).
- **The Cassalanters (suspected enemy):** The Order finds the family's public piety and generosity deeply suspicious. Savra and her chapter suspect a diabolist or infernalist hand behind the family and name no pact, and nobody in the Order knows what the family worships. The chapter holds no legal evidence, and Sister Valdra holds three pieces of intelligence of her own (**The Cassalanters**; **Sister Valdra**). Mirt suspects Cassalanter money stood behind the Howling Hatred, and no source says Savra has heard it (**The Cassalanters** in Villains).
- **House Gralhund (neutral):** No source gives a day-one dealing between the Order and the Gralhunds (**House Gralhund**). [research]
- **The Open Lord's Watch (wary neutral):** The Order's knights are more aggressive and less subtle than the Watch. The Watch has closed every complaint against one Sea Ward family, and it does not patrol the Field Ward, where the Order fills part of the gap (**The Open Lord's Watch**; **Sea Ward**; **Field Ward**).
- **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords (neutral):** The Order takes its cases to magistrates, and any Masked Lord can overturn a magistrate's ruling (**The Code Legal and the Magisters**). No source gives Laeral Silverhand or the Lords a day-one dealing with the Order. [research]
- **Hlam and the Order of the Even-Handed (ally):** The Order considers Hlam a staunch ally, and he leads a small monastic group devoted to Tyr from a cave on Mount Waterdeep (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Hlam**; **Tyr**).

## Key Members

- **Savra Belabranta** — Neutral Good Tethyrian human knight; Order faction contact, atonement-driven. See the **Savra Belabranta** page in Notable Figures.
- **Sister Valdra** — Lawful Good half-orc cleric of Tyr; unofficial Order operative (unsanctioned by Savra); holds three independent pieces of Cassalanter intelligence. See the **Sister Valdra** page in Notable Figures.

## Grand Game Agenda

The Order will move aggressively against the Cassalanters once they have solid evidence of whatever the family is hiding. Savra goes well out of her way for knights who give generously.

## Overview

The Order of the Gauntlet is a fellowship of paladins, clerics, and devout martial warriors who pursue and destroy evil actively. In Waterdeep, a small chapter works from the Halls of Justice, the temple of Tyr in the Castle Ward. Its knights are more aggressive and less subtle than the City Watch, and they hunt diabolists wherever the trail leads. They watch one Sea Ward noble family whose generosity seems too good to be true, though they have found nothing yet.

> [!gamemaster]**GM Notes**
>
> - **Savra's past:** The Howling Hatred was an elemental air cult that served Yan-C-Bin, the Prince of Evil Air, and it is not active during the campaign. *Princes of the Apocalypse* has the cult indoctrinate Savra, and the wiki has her join the Feathergale Knights, a cover group for the cult (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Savra Belabranta*, 5e). The campaign has her leave in 1491 DR, a year before the campaign starts.
> - **What the Order suspects:** Savra and her chapter suspect a diabolist or infernalist hand behind the Cassalanters, and they name no pact. Nobody in the Order knows what the family worships. Their file holds no legal evidence, and it records that the Watch closed every complaint against the family without findings.
> - **Sister Valdra:** She is a half-orc cleric of Tyr who built a case against the Cassalanters on her own time, from a servant's story the Watch dismissed. Savra does not know her. She holds the same suspicion as the Order and none of its proof.
> - **A campaign-only rule:** Order agents may not enter a place of worship without a magistrate's warrant. The published Code Legal does not state this rule, so voice it as ordinary law and do not attribute it to the handout.

**Tone:** Earnest and direct, with a dry edge for the Order's impatience with politics. The narrator respects Savra's conviction and treats her past plainly, and it neither excuses the Order's bluntness nor mocks it.
