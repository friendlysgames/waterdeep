## What the Grand Game Is

Periodically throughout Waterdeep's history, the city's byzantine factions become collectively fixated on a single objective, and the city's chroniclers call each of these episodes a Grand Game. A Grand Game breeds subterfuge, violence and all manner of covert maneuvering, and most of it passes without the population noticing anything unusual at all. In the criminal and political underground the same phrase names the ongoing competition between power blocs for influence, territory and profit.

No governing body organizes a Grand Game, and nobody keeps a roll of the players. It is called a game because everyone who plays it understands that they are playing it.

The current Grand Game revolves around Neverember's Enigma, the 500,000 gold dragons that Dagult Neverember embezzled from the city and hid in a vault. Four factions are competing for it when the campaign begins: **Xanathar's Guild**, **Manshoon's Zhentarim**, **The Cassalanters** and **Bregan D'aerthe**. The party stumbles into the middle of the contest, and its choices decide who wins, who loses and what the vault's gold accomplishes. **The Vault of Dragons and Neverember's Embezzlement** and **The Stone of Golorr and the Eyes** hold the prize and the key.

Other powers play at the edges. The Doom Raiders and House Gralhund have stakes in the contest and get a short note on **What Each Faction Knows at Start**, and the Lords' Alliance acts for an Open Lord who does not play herself.

## How Information Moves

Front businesses, informants, social events and the taverns carry most of what the factions learn about one another, and each channel has a named owner you can point to when the party asks how someone found out.

- **Front businesses:** A front gives a faction a reason to be somewhere and a way to meet people it could not meet openly. Jarlaxle Baenre runs the Sea Maidens Faire, a traveling carnival, as the cover for his intelligence operations, and he lairs aboard a ship in the harbor with a submarine, the *Scarlet Marpenoth*, beneath it. He plays the carnival's master as Zardoz Zord, and the party learns who Zord is at **Sea Maidens Faire**. Istrid Horn runs the Doom Raiders' illegal lending operation from a heavily guarded Dock Ward warehouse. Skeemo Weirdbottle keeps his cover in the Trades Ward as the owner of Weirdbottle's Concoctions, a cramped shop that sells mostly nonmagical potions and elixirs. The Cassalanters use a charitable hospital as their front business (campaign only).
- **Informants in the Watch:** The Watch keeps informants in every ward, and every faction keeps informants in the Watch (campaign only). When the party wonders how a faction learned of a raid so quickly, a Watch informant is the first answer to give, because it needs no further explanation.
- **Jarlaxle's listeners:** Jarlaxle has two named spies inside rival circles. Nar'l Xibrindas advises Xanathar and reports to him by *Sending*, and Vessa, a doppelganger, attends Cassalanter gatherings under a permanent human cover (campaign only). Each nimblewright he sells to a wealthy family also feeds a crystal ball aboard the *Scarlet Marpenoth*, so every sale is a listening post. Vessa is the reason Jarlaxle alone outside the household and the cult knows what the Cassalanters have done, and he tells no member of Bregan D'aerthe unless they work it out themselves.
- **The Harpers' man in the Guild:** Thorvin Twinbeard, Xanathar's chief engineer, is a paid Harper informant who keeps the Harpers apprised of Xanathar's plans as well as he can.
- **The Doom Raiders' traitor:** Skeemo Weirdbottle, the Doom Raiders' Master of Magic, sells intelligence to Manshoon's cell and to House Gralhund. Manshoon pays him in rare alchemical components and protection from Watch inspections, and he messages both buyers by paper bird.
- **Social events:** Sea Ward dinner parties serve as intelligence operations, and the Cassalanters' charitable galas screen guests for recruitment (campaign only). The family holds its public galas during Waukeentide, from Tarsakh 1 to 10. A party invited to a Sea Ward dinner should expect that someone at the table is listening for the faction ties of the other guests.
- **The Yawning Portal:** Durnan, the retired adventurer who runs the tavern, can direct adventurers toward regulars who might help them or offer useful information. The Harpers prefer to do business in busy inns such as the Yawning Portal, Jalester Silvermane spends much of his time there, and Davil Starsong's recruitment pitch tells listeners to find him there. When Waterdeep is genuinely threatened, Durnan tends to know about it before the Watch does.

## The City Watch as a Character

The Watch is Waterdeep's police force, and **The City Watch** covers its ranks, posts and patrols. In the Grand Game it plays one role: a mostly decent institution that learns what it knows from informants and answers to people who are playing.

- **What it is trying to do:** The Watch is genuinely trying to do its job most of the time, and it knows that some cases are politically untouchable (campaign only). The published adventure says a visitor can trust the Watch to do its duty diligently, and a party that keeps the law can expect to be left alone.
- **How it bends:** Most officers are not corrupt by rule, and pressure from above can still move them (campaign only). The published adventure names eight crooked Watch veterans who answer to Corylus Thann, a Masked Lord, so the code is real and a few officers have stepped outside it. **The City Watch** sets out who can be bought and who cannot be reached.
- **What it misses:** The Watch works above the sewers and inside the walls. It does not patrol the Field Ward, and many crimes there go uninvestigated.
- **Saeth Cromley** (Lawful Good, Illuskan Human, he/him) is a retired sergeant of the Watch who assists Barnibus in many of his investigations. He sits in the sergeants' room at the Halls of Justice in the afternoons, and a visitor who buys him a drink finds him easy to talk to. He gives the party its easiest way to learn what the Watch knows about a case.
- **Barnibus Blastwind** (Lawful Good, Illuskan Human, he/him) is an investigator of crimes that involve magic for the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors, and he is not an officer of the Watch. He is meticulous and prickly, he confides only in Cromley, and he prefers to keep his conclusions to himself, sharing them only with other members of the Order. He and Cromley will not make an arrest without ironclad evidence and reliable testimony, so a party that wants the Watch to act on a hunch will wait on them. He finds violence appalling and never uses his magic to harm anyone.

## The Open Lord's Place

Laeral Silverhand does not play the Grand Game, and the Lords' Alliance acts on her behalf. She believes the vault lies in Neverwinter, and she does not know that the Stone of Golorr has resurfaced or that four factions are moving on the city. Her information comes largely from the Watch and from Jalester Silvermane, and it runs about three moves behind. **A City Watching Itself** gives her side in full.

## Overview

The city's chroniclers use the phrase Grand Game for the seasons when Waterdeep's great houses, guilds and shadier powers fix on the same prize and begin maneuvering against one another. Most citizens see only the results, such as a merchant ruined overnight or a fight in the Dock Ward that nobody will explain. The City Watch walks every ward and hears a good deal of talk along the way. Rumor travels fastest through the taverns, and the Yawning Portal is one where adventurers swap news and listen for work.

**Tone:** Practical and in-world, with the grave register of the History pages for the frame and plain procedure for the Gamemaster notes. Play the Watch as a decent institution that bends under pressure from above, never as a villain, and give each claim its tag so you can see which ones the published adventure makes and which ones are campaign choices.
