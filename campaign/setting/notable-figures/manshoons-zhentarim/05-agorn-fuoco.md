**Agorn Fuoco** (Neutral evil, Turami human, he/him)

## Background

Agorn Fuoco is a Turami human bard and one of Manshoon's lieutenants. The published adventure says that Manshoon was so impressed with him that he was raised quickly through the ranks, though his life is otherwise full of disappointment, since he has not found the fame he craved or the wealth he thought he was owed (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). He has taken up the Zhentarim credo that power comes to those who deserve it, and he has come to see the Black Network as a new family (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The wiki adds that he dreamed of becoming a famous bard, had no success and so devoted himself to the Zhentarim (Forgotten Realms Wiki, *Agorn Fuoco*, citing *Dragon Heist*, 5e) `[research: wiki only]`. He used to live and play music in Mistshore, where the local children still know his name, and he now sleeps in the Zhent quarters behind Kolat Towers (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).

Agorn grew up in a Turami family that had come to the Sword Coast by ship from the Sea of Fallen Stars `[design]`. His father rigged ships in the harbor and was away from the house more often than not, and he stopped coming home when Agorn was nine `[design]`. His mother, Marta (*Dragon Heist*, 5e), mended sailcloth for the chandlers and pawned her wedding bracelet to buy him a secondhand lyre `[design]`. From the age of eleven he played for coppers in the dockside taverns and on the quay, and the crowds liked him, which taught him early that the approval of the room was the thing he wanted most `[design]`. He learned the songs that were asked for, dropped the ones that were not, and fell into the habit of agreeing with whoever was loudest at the table `[design]`.

At twenty a patron who liked his playing arranged a hearing with the manager of a playhouse, who offered him a place in the pit and promised him an understudy's part `[design]`. On the night he was first called to stand in, Agorn left by the back door an hour before the curtain and did not return `[design]`. The manager never made the offer again, and Agorn told his mother that the company had folded `[design]`. He still goes to the theater, and he takes a seat near the door `[design]`. The fire that gutted Mistshore in Mirtul 1491 DR (**Dock Ward**) took the rooms where he had grown up, and he was at a supper party across the harbor that night and heard of it the next morning `[design]`. His mother, who is ill and lives in abject poverty (*Dragon Heist*, 5e) `[research]`, now keeps a burned-out shell with a sail for a roof, in a stretch of the shore that the fire left standing `[design]`.

Agorn took work as a house musician and played private dinners for merchants and factors, some of whom did business with the Black Network's local brokers `[design]`. He was agreeable to everyone at the table and remembered who had said what, and one broker began paying him to carry invitations and to listen `[design]`. About two years before Ches 1, 1492 DR, Manshoon's cell took a large part of the local network away from the older Zhentarim (**The Founding of Chains**) `[research]`, and Agorn went with the part he knew `[design]`. Manshoon heard him play at a gathering of the cell's officers and afterward asked what he had heard at the tables `[design]`. Agorn repeated it all in order, with the names of the speakers, and told Manshoon what he guessed Manshoon wished to hear about the rest `[design]`. The wizard raised him to lieutenant within months (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Manshoon's Zhentarim**).

Agorn met Amath Sercent when Manshoon sent him to Yellowspire with a message, and he came back the next evening without one `[design]`. She is the most persuasive person he knows, and he has never argued with her `[design]`. On Ches 1, 1492 DR he is her romantic partner (**Manshoon's Zhentarim**, Organizations). He carries a pass-amulet to Kolat Towers and sleeps in the Zhent quarters behind it alongside Vevette Blackwater and others (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Manshoon's Zhentarim**, Factions Guide). He leads one of the cell's three field teams, a bard with three or four thugs, and he files field reports that Urstul Floxin reviews (**Manshoon's Zhentarim**, Factions Guide; **Running the Villains**). On some evenings he sits by the ground-level fire at Yellowspire from dusk to midnight and tunes a lute while he waits for Amath (**Manshoon's Zhentarim**, Factions Guide). He crosses the city to see his mother when he can `[research]`, and she believes he plays for rich households and knows nothing about his work for the Zhentarim `[design]`.

**Personal goals.** Agorn wants to move his mother out of Mistshore and into dry rooms with a healer, and he leaves coin each tenday with a neighbor to bring her bread `[design]`. He spends the rest on tailoring and theater seats, and each month he tells himself that next month he will find her a place `[design]`. He also wants the two women he loves to meet, and he has found no way to bring either of them to the other's door `[design]`. Each time he leaves one, he rehearses how he will describe her to the other `[design]`. His third want is to play a whole evening of his own songs in a proper hall, with a paying audience and his own name on the bill `[design]`. He has nine songs finished and no tenth, and the tune he hums when he is nervous is the tenth `[design]`. In the version of that evening he pictures, a hall in a small quiet town far from the city, both women sit in the front row `[design]`.

## Personality

To most people who have met him, Agorn Fuoco is a smooth and agreeable bard with an easy tenor, who talks about the theater and the company he keeps. He carries himself as though the situation is never quite as bad as others seem to think. He does occasional work for the Zhentarim that he does not ask about too carefully, and he does not discuss it.

In the field he sends his thugs ahead and hangs back himself, and he leaves at the first sign of a fight. He hums when he is nervous, offers information before anyone asks and mentions his mother at inappropriate moments. He agrees with whoever is most persuasive in the room, and he would rather be playing music.

## Overview

Agorn Fuoco is a Turami bard who loves the theater and keeps excellent company. He used to live and play music in Mistshore, and the children there still know his name. He does occasional work that he never discusses. He carries himself as though nothing is ever quite as bad as others think.

## Roleplaying Information

**Resonance.** Agorn should inspire pity when you realize he is genuinely out of his depth; contempt when he starts trading people to buy his own escape; and wry surprise when his mother comes up and he becomes, briefly, comprehensible.

**Emotions.** Agorn most often feels artistic self-absorption, the particular anxiety of someone who has made too many decisions based on mood, passive compliance with whoever is most immediately persuasive, genuine love of music that sits embarrassingly alongside everything else, and deep filial attachment to his mother.

**Motivations.** Agorn wants to avoid consequences for his Zhentarim work, use whatever he knows (Amath's identity, his pass-amulet, Manshoon's operational structure) to buy his freedom, and eventually retire somewhere quieter.

**Inspirations.** When playing Agorn, channel Pete Campbell (*Mad Men*), Zaphod Beeblebrox (*The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*), and Tom Ripley (*The Talented Mr. Ripley*, the man always one step behind the situation).

### Voice

*A vain, out-of-his-depth bard who starts selling everyone out the moment he's threatened.*

- **Sound:** A smooth, musical, slightly affected tenor. It cracks into a whine under pressure.
- **Sentence shape:** Easy, self-absorbed small talk about art and society. Threatened, a rapid stream of offers, from least to most useful.
- **Word choice:** Artistic and social: performances, "delightful company", "a small favour". He calls his mother "Mama", and becomes briefly comprehensible when she comes up.
- **Swearing:** *Casual · Plain · Phrase.* Whining curses ("Oh, fuck, oh, this is bad, this is so bad").
- **Personality in speech:** Artistic self-absorption and passive compliance. He agrees with whoever is most persuasive in the room. His self-preservation is shameless.
- **Quirks:** He hums when nervous. He offers information before anyone asks. He mentions his mother at inappropriate moments.
- **Signature phrases:** "Let's be civilised." / "I can give you something better." / "Mama will be so upset."
- **Never:** He never fights, never keeps a secret under pressure, and never admits he knew what he was doing.
- **Sample lines:**
  - *Greeting:* "Ah, theatre-lovers? No? Pity. Delightful to meet you anyway."
  - *Business (threatened):* "Here, take the amulet. And I know an address. Amath, Yellowspire. And there's more, lots more, just don't hurt me."
  - *Under pressure:* "Oh, fuck, fuck, fuck, let's be civilised, please."

## Character Information

**Persona.** To the world, Agorn Fuoco is a bard who attends the theater, keeps excellent company, and does occasional work for the Zhentarim that he does not ask about too carefully. To anyone who threatens him, he offers his pass-amulet, Amath Sercent's address, Manshoon's general operational structure, and whatever else occurs to him — starting immediately and working outward from least to most useful, in the order most likely to buy another minute. Deep down, he would rather be playing music.

**Morale.** In a fight, Agorn attempts to flee immediately. If he is cornered, he begins trading information before anyone asks.

**Relationships.** Agorn is a Manshoon lieutenant and bard, the partner of Amath Sercent, whom he visits at Yellowspire in the evenings, a pass-amulet holder for Kolat Towers, and a man whose mother lives in Mistshore and knows nothing about his work for the Zhentarim.

> [!gamemaster]**Secret**
>
> Agorn is a lieutenant of Manshoon's Zhentarim, and the Overview shows only a bard. Few outside the cell know it. Yellowspire, where his partner Amath Sercent keeps a Banite cell, is also unknown to the public, and he carries the pass-amulet that opens Kolat Towers to him.

**Tone:** Plain and procedural in the GM text, with a dry, rueful regard for Agorn's vanity in Background and Personality. The narrator treats his love for his mother and for music as real and does not play his cowardice for a joke.
