**Yorn the Terror** (Neutral evil, half-orc, he/him)

## Background

Yorn the Terror is a half-orc enforcer of Manshoon's household in Kolat Towers and the sanctum behind it, where he keeps the household afraid of its master (**Manshoon**, Notable Figures; **Manshoon's Zhentarim**, Organizations). The cell uses him when a conversation has stopped working (**Manshoon's Zhentarim**, Organizations). The published adventure gives him black leather armor, a pouch of coin with a gem-studded ivory toothpick, and an autographed copy of *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, inscribed by the author with a hope that he will find the orc chapter illuminating (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). It also says that he values his own well-being above all else and that he speaks Common and Orc (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). No source records where he was born, who gave him his epithet or how he came to Manshoon `[research]`.

Yorn is the son of a human farmhand and an orc who worked the same barley farm, in a village at the edge of the Sword Mountains `[design]`. His father taught him Orc at night and told him little else, and the father died the winter before Yorn was ten `[design]`. That year a harvest failed, and the village blamed the half-orc boy for the missing grain `[design]`. He stood before the village moot and explained for an hour that he had taken nothing, and the moot drove him and his mother out the next morning `[design]`. He concluded from this that explaining does not change what people have already decided `[design]`.

His mother took work as a cook in a tavern on the Sword Coast road that kept a fighting pit behind it, and the retired fighter who ran the pit, a human woman, noticed that the boy never answered back `[design]`. She began training him at fourteen and taught him to wait until the other man moved first `[design]`. His mother remarried and left the road when he was sixteen, and the trainer kept him `[design]`. Yorn fought in the pits for six years `[design]`. He took off his leather gloves before each bout, and the crowd learned that the gloves coming off meant it was starting `[design]`. The announcers called him the Terror because the crowd went silent when he walked out, and he has never used the name himself `[design]`.

At twenty a promoter sold Yorn's next bout to the crowd's favorite and told him to lie down in the third round `[design]`. Yorn did not lie down, the promoter's men came for him in the cellar that night, and he broke three of them and left the pits that week `[design]`. He has put his own survival ahead of every employer since then `[design]`.

A Black Network broker who had bet on his bouts hired him as muscle for the local network `[design]`. About two years before Ches 1, 1492 DR, Manshoon's cell took a large part of that network away from the older Zhentarim (**The Founding of Chains**) `[research]`, and the broker brought Yorn with it `[design]`. Manshoon wanted a fighter who could keep a household afraid without making noise that the cell could not afford, and a man who answered questions with silence suited the work `[design]`. Yorn took the post because it paid a fixed wage, gave him a room and put no crowd between him and the work `[design]`. On Ches 1, 1492 DR he is stationed in the sanctum, and he reads the autographed book there when the household is quiet `[design]`.

**Personal goals.** Yorn wants to keep his old trainer in a dry room until she dies `[design]`. She is old and lame and rents a room above a chandler's shop in Waterdeep, and he pays the rent on the first of every month and sits with her for an hour without speaking, because she has never needed him to `[design]`. He also wants to learn which orc band his father came from `[design]`. His father wore a pattern of scars across one forearm and never explained it, and Yorn checks every plate of the orc chapter in his book for the pattern and has not found it `[design]`.

## Personality

To most people who have met him, Yorn the Terror is a burly half-orc in black leather armor who says almost nothing. He removes his gloves before he speaks, and he does not introduce himself, threaten anyone or explain what comes next. The household around him watches him closely and behaves accordingly.

## Connections

- **Manshoon:** The master whose household Yorn keeps afraid. Yorn serves for a fixed wage and a room, and he does the work well enough that his value is not questioned `[design]`.
- **The household:** **Kaevja Cynavern**, **Havia Quickknife**, **Mookie Plush** and **Manafret Cherryport**, whom Yorn keeps afraid of their master (**Manshoon**, Notable Figures). No source records how any of them treat him `[research]`.
- **His old trainer:** A human woman who ran the fighting pit where he learned to fight and who now lives on his rent money in Waterdeep `[design]`.
- **His father's people:** An orc band that Yorn has never met and cannot name `[design]`.

## Overview

Yorn the Terror is a burly half-orc enforcer who wears black leather and does not introduce himself. He speaks very little, and people who know him say that others gave him the name.

## Roleplaying Information

**Resonance.** Yorn should inspire dread disproportionate to any initial description — the epithet "the Terror" was not self-applied, and the people who gave it to him are professionals who know what they are talking about.

**Emotions.** Yorn most often feels steady patience, a physical readiness he carries the way other people carry stillness, satisfaction in his particular utility to Manshoon's operation, and a focused clarity that descends once he understands a situation is no longer going to be avoided.

**Motivations.** Yorn wants to do the work Manshoon brought him in for — enforcement, extraction, the maintenance of internal fear as much as external threat — and to do it well enough that his value is never questioned.

**Inspirations.** When playing Yorn, channel Anton Chigurh (*No Country for Old Men*), Victor Creed (*X-Men*), and Sandor Clegane (*Game of Thrones*).

### Voice

*An enforcer who doesn't introduce himself, doesn't monologue, and doesn't need to.*

- **Sound:** A low, flat, patient voice, rarely heard.
- **Sentence shape:** Almost nothing: a word or two before it begins, deliberately (a break from `ember-voice` speech).
- **Word choice:** Final and plain. He never threatens, only states.
- **Swearing:** *Stingy · Plain · Clipped.* Rarely: one flat word that means the conversation is over.
- **Personality in speech:** Steady patience and focused clarity once talk has failed. His silence is the terror.
- **Quirks:** He removes his gloves before he speaks.
- **Signature phrases:** "Now." / "It's time."
- **Never:** He never explains himself, never hurries, and never gloats.
- **Sample lines:**
  - *Greeting:* (silence, and he removes his gloves)
  - *Business:* "It's time."
  - *Under pressure:* "Now."

## Character Information

**Persona.** To the world — specifically to anyone brought into the sanctum who is about to be questioned — Yorn the Terror is the fact of what is going to happen. He does not monologue. He does not explain himself. To Manshoon's lieutenants, he is the asset deployed when a situation has moved past the point at which conversation is the primary tool. Deep down, he has been called "the Terror" long enough that he has stopped thinking of it as a name and started thinking of it as a description.

**Morale.** Yorn does not retreat and does not stop. He uses his physicality as a weapon before he draws a weapon, positioning himself to cut off exits and force opponents into a space where his strength is most useful. He does not negotiate mid-combat, but a party that can match his damage output can defeat him; a party that attempts to escape will discover that the corridor behind them has become a different kind of problem.

**Relationships.** Yorn is a Manshoon Splinter enforcer stationed in the extradimensional sanctum whose role in the organization is the maintenance of internal fear as much as the exercise of direct violence, and the asset the cell uses when a conversation has stopped working.

**Tone:** Plain and procedural in the GM text, with a dry, level regard for Yorn's silence in Background and Personality. The narrator treats his trainer, his father's scars and his refusal to explain himself as real and plays none of them for a joke.
