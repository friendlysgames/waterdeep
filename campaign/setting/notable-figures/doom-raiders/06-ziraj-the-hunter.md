**Ziraj the Hunter** (Lawful evil, half-orc, he/him)

## Background

Ziraj the Hunter is a half-orc who wields an oversized bow that shoots correspondingly large arrows, and he is the Master of Assassination for the Black Network (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The City Watch cannot say where he comes from or whether he has a home at all (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). The account below is the GM's alone, because Ziraj has told it whole to nobody `[design]`.

Ziraj was left as an infant at the door of a trapper's cabin in forested hill country, and the old man who lived there raised him `[design]`. The trapper taught him to track, to make a bow and to take only what a table needs, and by the age of fourteen Ziraj stood a head taller than his teacher `[design]`. The nearest village stared whenever he came down for salt, and its children threw stones, so he learned to cross it by the ridgeline and the rooftops instead of the road `[design]`. When the old man died, Ziraj buried him behind the cabin, took his bow and his knife, and left the hills without telling anyone in the village `[design]`.

For some years afterward he hired out as a scout and bowman, and he killed for coin `[design]`. One contract ended with a family dead who should not have been, and he has taken no coin for a killing since `[design]`. He decided then that he would kill only for the sake of someone he was loyal to and never on his own account `[design]`.

He met Davil Starsong while he was hired to clear tombs, and through Davil he came to know the three others who became the Doom Raiders `[design]`. The oversized longbow came out of a lich's guard room, where it had been made for a giant, and Ziraj was the only one of the five who could draw it `[design]`. He has restrung it four times, and he blackens the shafts of his arrows with soot and pitch himself so that they do not flash on a roof at night `[design]`. In the late 1480s DR he joined the Zhentarim with Davil and the rest, and he came to Waterdeep with them in 1489 DR (**The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders)**; *Dragon Heist*, 5e). Ziraj names no price when an outsider hires him, and the undisclosed favor that the hirer owes is called in later (*Dragon Heist*, 5e) `[design]`.

Ziraj saved Fala Lefaliir's life, and Fala has set aside a room for him on the second floor of Corellon's Crown (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). He has never told anyone how it happened, and Fala has never asked him to `[design]`. Years ago he let Yagra Stonefist follow him on a hunt `[design]`, and he taught her the marks he leaves when he is wounded (**The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders)**; **Yagra Stonefist**). She asked to learn them, and he has never asked her why `[design]`.

On Ches 1, 1492 DR Ziraj sleeps in Fala's second-floor room on the nights when he is in the North Ward, and he spends the others on rooftops chosen for their sightlines `[design]`. He takes his meals and gives his reports at the back table of Corellon's Crown, where Fala shelters him (**The Zhentarim (Doom Raiders)**). Because of the war with the other Zhentarim cell, he spends most nights on the roofs above the Doom Raiders' safe houses and fronts `[design]`.

**Personal goals.** Ziraj wants one season in which nobody asks him to kill `[design]`. He keeps a tally of the days since his last killing, scratched inside the lid of a small wooden box, and his longest run is ninety-one days `[design]`. He also wants to climb back to the trapper's cabin once and see whether the roof still stands `[design]`. He has not been back since the burial, and so far he has not found a night that he could spare `[design]`. Last, he wants to bring a night-blooming plant to flower on the sill of his room at Corellon's Crown `[design]`. Fala gave him the cutting, and two earlier cuttings have died on him `[design]`.

## Personality

To most people who have heard of him, Ziraj is a hulking figure on the rooftops whom the City Watch cannot place from one night to the next. Those who see him up close find a half-orc of conspicuous size, broad across the shoulders, with a face that has been in a number of fights. He answers only what he is asked, in two to five words, and he gives his reports as counts and never as names. He nods in place of saying yes.

Among the people he trusts he is patient and still, and his loyalty shows in what he does and rarely in what he says. If someone asks him why he killed, he says who asked. With Fala he speaks whole sentences, and sometimes two in a row, about the garden or about how little Fala sleeps. He never boasts, never explains a kill and never raises his voice.

## Overview

Ziraj the Hunter is a half-orc of conspicuous size and the Doom Raiders' Master of Assassination. The City Watch has heard of a hulking figure on the rooftops whose long black arrows kill from a distance. He rarely speaks, and when he comes down to street level it is to the back table at Corellon's Crown.

## Roleplaying Information

**Resonance.** Ziraj should inspire hushed awe with his physical presence and lethal reputation, endearment through his friendship with Fala (the one relationship where he is gentle), and respect for a killer who only kills because someone he cares about asked.

**Emotions.** Ziraj most often feels patient stillness, watchful calm, fierce loyalty to the Doom Raiders, gentle ease in Fala's company, grim hunter's focus, and a silence that communicates more than most people's speeches.

**Motivations.** Ziraj wants to protect the Doom Raiders by eliminating threats before they arrive, keep Fala Lefaliir safe, and remain unseen by the rest of the world.

**Inspirations.** When playing Ziraj, channel Boba Fett (*The Mandalorian*), Geralt of Rivia (*The Witcher*), and Anton Chigurh (*No Country for Old Men*).

### Voice

*A huge, near-silent assassin whose reports are a few words long and whose gentleness is saved for one friend.*

- **Sound:** A very deep, quiet rumble, barely above a whisper. With Fala, it softens and slows.
- **Sentence shape:** Two to five words, always a deliberate break from `ember-voice` speech. He answers only what was asked. Long silences in between.
- **Word choice:** Hunter's words: tracks, marks, numbers, directions. No names for enemies, only counts. He calls Fala by name, everyone else nothing.
- **Swearing:** *Stingy · Plain · Clipped.* Rare, and a single word: "Shit." It means the plan has changed.
- **Personality in speech:** Patient stillness. His loyalty shows in action, not words. The rare longer sentence is always about someone he protects. He kills because he's asked, and says so if asked why ("Davil asked.").
- **Quirks:** He reports as a count ("Three left."). He nods instead of saying yes. With Fala, he'll finish a whole sentence and sometimes two.
- **Signature phrases:** "Three left." / "Davil asked." / "Mm."
- **Never:** He never boasts, never explains a kill, and never raises his voice.
- **Sample lines:**
  - *Greeting:* (a nod)
  - *Business:* "Two on the roof. One in the alley. Mine."
  - *Under pressure:* "Go. I'll hold them."
  - *With Fala:* "The garden looks good this year. You should rest more."

## Character Information

**Persona.** To the world, Ziraj is a hulking shadow that glares from its perch, rains down death with an oversized bow, and slinks off without a whisper. Where he comes from remains a mystery. To those he trusts, he uses the quiet back table at Corellon's Crown and says "Three left" when asked for a report. Deep down, he kills because someone he cares about asked him to, and if no one asked, he would stop.

**Morale.** In a fight, Ziraj fires from elevation with his oversized longbow (requires Strength 18, deals 2d6 + modifier), repositions silently, and never engages in melee unless cornered. If wounded, he leaves intentional trail marks that he taught Yagra years ago.

**Relationships.** Ziraj is the Doom Raiders' Master of Assassination, a close friend of Fala Lefaliir (who shelters him at Corellon's Crown), a colleague whose wounded-hunter marks Yagra knows, and a presence on the rooftops around Trollskull Alley that most people never notice.

**Tone:** Plain and procedural in the GM text, with a dry, gentle humor about how few words he spends in Personality and the Overview. The narrator treats his gentleness with Fala as real and does not explain his kills.
