**Malkolm Brizzenbright** (Neutral, Chondathan `[research]` human ghost, he/him)

## Background

Malkolm Brizzenbright founded the Seven Masks Theater in the Dock Ward, a public house for a lower-class audience where ship captains and sailors are admitted free (*Dragon Heist*, 5e; **Dock Ward**). He has been dead for over thirty years (**Bregan D'aerthe**), and his soul could not bear to leave the building (*Dragon Heist*, 5e). No file gives the year or the cause of his death, his birthplace or his age `[research]`.

Malkolm was born into a family of players who worked the coast road, and he was carrying scripts and prompting from the wings before he was ten `[design]`. He was a middling actor and a very good manager, and he came to Waterdeep by ship as a young man, with a troupe of six players and a trunk of masks `[design]`. Each mask was a stock character, and with the manager's own that made seven, which is where the theater's name comes from `[design]`. He chose the Dock Ward because sailors will watch a play to the end if the second act is good, and he let captains and crews in free so that the house was never empty `[design]`.

His wife kept the box office and sewed the costumes, and she died of a winter fever some years before he did `[design]`. They had one daughter, who grew up in the wings and learned every part in the house `[design]`. Malkolm kept a dressing room at the back for himself, and in it he wrote a play of his own, which he rewrote for years and never staged `[design]`. The script he still carries is a script that was never performed (**Faction Outposts**, structure notes), and it is that play `[design]`. He died of a lung fever in that room during a cold season, with the last act still unfinished on the table `[design]`. The theater's debts then fell on his daughter, who sold the house to settle them and left Waterdeep with a touring company `[design]`.

Malkolm stayed in the building, and he has been moving props since the first rehearsal after his death, when a stagehand missed a cue and he nudged a flat into place `[design]`. He hisses at actors who do not project, and the casts have put the noise down to drafts for as long as anyone can remember `[design]`. The house changed hands several times after the sale, and about two years ago it came to the owner who calls himself Rongquan Mystere, whose changes Malkolm has watched and does not approve of (**Faction Outposts**, structure notes). Drow now come and go at irregular hours, a booking manager who calls himself Kreb Sorrush runs the schedules, and Malkolm's own dressing room is kept empty as a safe house (**Bregan D'aerthe**; **Dock Ward**). Malkolm knows that "Rongquan" is a false name, and he once saw him speaking with drow in the lobby who mentioned something called the Sea Maidens Faire (**Bregan D'aerthe**). He has told nobody who was worth telling `[design]`.

**Personal goals.** Malkolm wants to hear his play performed once, from the first line to the last, by actors who project `[design]`. He finished the last act in his head in the years after he died, and he would settle for a reading on the bare stage if he could find someone he trusted with the script `[design]`. He also wants news of his daughter `[design]`. He cannot leave the building, so he listens in the lobby for the names of touring companies and for anyone who mentions a woman from the Seven Masks, and he does not know whether she is alive `[design]`.

## Personality

To the Dock Ward, Malkolm Brizzenbright is the ghost of the Seven Masks, a figure in a suit and a director's coat with unkempt hair, who floats in the lobby and the backstage corridors and moves props during rehearsals. He greets visitors with a stock welcome that begins "Let not this harried visage diminish you, gentlesirs and beautiful ladies" (*Dragon Heist*, 5e).

Those who stop to talk find a fussy, proud old manager who is anxious about the building and thrilled to be taken seriously. He is not hostile, and he is a witness more than a haunting.

## Connections

Malkolm answers to nobody. He dislikes the theater's owner, the false "Rongquan Mystere" (**Jarlaxle Baenre / "Zardoz Zord"**), and the drow who use his dressing room. He also clashes with the booking manager **Krebbyg Masq'il'yr**, who runs the house as "Kreb Sorrush" and wants to run it without the ghost interfering. His daughter is the one person he wants to hear about `[design]`.

## Overview

Malkolm Brizzenbright is the ghost of the Seven Masks Theater in the Dock Ward, which he founded. He has been dead for over thirty years. He moves props during rehearsals and hisses at actors who project poorly.

## Roleplaying Information

**Resonance.** Malkolm should inspire pity for a man who loved his theater enough to haunt it; endearment with his fussy pride in the building's artistic integrity; and useful surprise when his grievance against the new management turns out to be exactly what a visitor needs to know.

**Emotions.** Malkolm most often feels proprietary outrage at the drow who use his dressing room, anxious concern about the theater's reputation, a director's eye that notices everything that has changed, and the specific unhappiness of a ghost who died too early to see what his theater would become.

**Motivations.** Malkolm wants to protect the Seven Masks Theater's reputation, convince someone worth convincing that "Rongquan Mystere" is not who he claims, and move props in a way that communicates his displeasure without being dismissed as a poltergeist.

**Inspirations.** When playing Malkolm, channel Nearly Headless Nick (*Harry Potter*), Casper (*Casper*), and Albert Brooks (*any comedy where he plays a man who is right about everything and cannot make anyone listen*).

### Voice

*The Seven Masks' fussy founding ghost, outraged about everything and desperate to be heard.*

- **Sound:** A reedy, theatrical, hollow voice with an actor-manager's diction, and a hiss when someone projects poorly.
- **Sentence shape:** Long, aggrieved, theatrical complaints full of stage terminology. When someone finally listens, he gets eager and specific.
- **Word choice:** Old theatre vocabulary: "the house", "blocking", "my stage", "philistines". He calls the drow "the new management" with disdain.
- **Swearing:** *Stingy · Euphemist · Clipped.* Old-fashioned and prim ("Blasted", "confounded"), then one real "damn them" he's proud of.
- **Personality in speech:** Proprietary outrage and anxiety. He's specific about grievances: the drow who come and go at irregular hours, the dressing room they use, the false name on the playbills. He's thrilled when anyone takes him seriously.
- **Quirks:** He critiques the party's diction. He moves props to make a point. He sighs dramatically.
- **Signature phrases:** "My stage!" / "Project, for pity's sake!" / "Finally, someone who listens."
- **Never:** He never lets bad acting pass, never leaves the theatre, and never believes "Rongquan Mystere".
- **Sample lines:**
  - *Greeting:* "You there! Project! I can't hear you from the gallery, and I've no body, so that's saying something."
  - *Business:* "There are drow in my dressing room at all hours, and a man on the playbills whose name is no more real than I am alive."
  - *Under pressure:* "Not my stage! Not my blasted stage!"

## Character Information

**Persona.** To the world, the Seven Masks Theater has a ghost that moves props during rehearsals and occasionally hisses at actors who project poorly. To those who speak with him, Malkolm Brizzenbright is the theater's founder — anxious that his theater not be used for nefarious purposes, certain that "Rongquan Mystere" is a false name, and able to point to the empty dressing room where drow agents in trouble hide. Deep down, he has been waiting for someone to tell, and the only question is whether they are worth telling.

**Morale.** Malkolm is a ghost. He passes through walls, can terrify intruders, and moves props. He avoids combat because the theater cannot afford the damage.

**Relationships.** Malkolm Brizzenbright is the founding ghost of the Seven Masks Theater, the one who knows "Rongquan Mystere" is a fake identity, the witness who can identify the dressing room Bregan D'aerthe keeps as a safe house, and the man who once saw "Rongquan" speaking with drow in the lobby during a performance of *The Mad Mage's Seven Riddles*, when they mentioned something called the Sea Maidens Faire.

> [!gamemaster]**Secret**
>
> - **What he knows:** "Rongquan Mystere" is a false name, and Malkolm cannot say whose face is behind it. He is an unknowing asset of Bregan D'aerthe, so Jarlaxle's company does not count him a member `[research]`.
> - **What he has seen:** He has watched Soluun Xibrindas pick elven and half-elven victims from the audience, and he can describe the pattern of the seats. Jarlaxle knows of the killings and has not acted on them.
> - **Why he stays quiet:** He tells these things only to a visitor who treats him with respect. Anyone who talks down to him gets complaints about the new management and nothing more.

**Tone:** Plain and procedural in the GM text, with a gentle humor about the ghost's fussy pride in Personality and the Overview. The narrator treats his grief and his daughter as real, and the page does not mock him.
