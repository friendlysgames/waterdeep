> - **Where it is used:** Cyric appears in no quest, faction page or Notable Figure. The campaign names him once, in the history of Zhentil Keep, and the published *Dragon Heist* names him in the story of Mount Waterdeep and in the Gods' Day bonfire.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Cyric, and states what the campaign and the published adventure have tied to him so that no later page invents more. It also records how the page handles his imprisonment in the wiki's history.

*The Prince of Lies*

- **Title:** God of Lies (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Dark Sun, the Prince of Lies (FRHoF); the Mad God, the Black Sun, Sirhivatizangpo among the Gugari and N'asr among the Bedine (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Lies, strife, malicious deception, intrigue, murder (derived from the FRHoF text and the wiki's 2e and 3e portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Trickery (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** White jawless skull on a black or purple sunburst (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Pandemonium (Cocytus) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Liars, manipulators, power-hungry folk (FRHoF)

> Every bond withers in the end, so trust no one.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Cyric is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and its youngest member. FRHoF calls him a recent arrival who rose from mortal life during the Time of Troubles. The wiki calls him a greater god in its 2e, 3e and 4e sources and a deity in its 5e row.
- **What the faith asks:** FRHoF says the faith is strong wherever evil plots flourish, and that people who want control over others flock to it. The faithful believe he will not hear a prayer unless they first make a blood sacrifice. Good folk do not speak his name, even when they are trying to deceive someone, and call on Leira instead.
- **What the priests teach:** His priests reject the charge that he is a god of madness, strife and deceit. They preach a god of dark majesty who proves that every bond between people corrupts and withers in the end (SCAG, 2014). Those who take him as patron tend to be sadists, con artists and power-mad connivers, and other folk pray to him when they want to do wrong without being found out (SCAG, 2014).
- **Open worship:** Temples to him are forbidden in most nations and thrive in Amn, where his teaching suits a culture of ambition, self-reliance and "buyer beware" (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). In the early years of his godhood the Moonsea around Zhentil Keep was the center of his worship, and the wiki says the Zhentarim served then as an extension of his church (2e and 3e sources). No campaign file carries that tie into 1492 DR.
- **Clergy:** Priests wear black or dark purple robes trimmed with silver, with belts or jewelry stamped with the skull-and-sunburst symbol. They spend much of their time scheming against one another, and each tries to strengthen their own power (FRHoF). The wiki adds that his clerics prayed for spells at night after moonrise, often trained as rogues or assassins, and that his specialty priests were called strifeleaders (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Cyric. FRHoF says the lies of his Chosen go undetected even by powerful magic, as if they had cast *Glibness*, and spells such as *Zone of Truth* have no effect on them. One Chosen at a time guards the *Cyrinishad*, a tome that turns its readers into fanatical devotees of the Prince of Lies.
- **Messengers:** He whispers to his followers in haze-filled dreams and sends undead riding Nightmares as emissaries (FRHoF). The wiki lists dreams of bloody wraiths, clouds of poisonous smoke and dark spaces full of whirling phantom skulls as his manifestations (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** His church kept few. It did not mark the date of his ascension, because the date was also Midnight's. When a temple acquired a victim important enough to sacrifice, its high priest declared a Day of the Dark Sun, and eclipses were holy occasions with feasts, prayers and bloody sacrifices (wiki; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). No file ties a city festival to him.
- **Temples the wiki names:** Darkhold, the Zhentarim fortress in the Western Heartlands (*Volo's Guide to the Sword Coast*, 5e); the House of Cyric, a subterranean temple beneath Skullport (*Serpent Kingdoms*, 3e); and the Twin Towers of the Eternal Eclipse, the center of his worship in Amn (*Lands of Intrigue: Amn*, 3e). None is in Waterdeep.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book tagged where the wiki gives one.
  - He looked down on most of the other powers of Toril and hated Mystra, Kelemvor and Bane above the rest. He claimed credit for Bane's death and briefly took over his portfolio (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - Tyr, Torm, Azuth, Deneir and Leira were among his enemies (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). FRHoF names him as the foremost enemy of the Knights of the Mystic Fire, who seek out his shrines to destroy them.
  - Jergal served him as seneschal while he ruled the dead, and could not steer him (*Prince of Lies*, novel). He partnered with Mask for a time, and the two became bitter enemies when Mask revealed himself as Cyric's sword Godsbane. He later allied with Shar, who used him to her own ends (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He began as a human thief and adventurer from Zhentil Keep, and he traveled with Midnight, Kelemvor and Adon in the search for the Tablets of Fate (the Avatar novels *Shadowdale*, *Tantras* and *Waterdeep*).
  - FRHoF says he rose to godhood during the Time of Troubles by killing Bhaal, and went on to slay other gods and steal their portfolios. The wiki dates his ascension to Marpenoth 15, 1358 DR, when he took Strife, Murder and Death and most of the worshipers of Bane, Bhaal and Myrkul (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). In the novel *Waterdeep* he takes the tablets at Blackstaff Tower and pleads his case to Ao on the Celestial Stairway atop Mount Waterdeep. *Dragon Heist* records the grant of godhood to Kelemvor, Midnight and Cyric on the slopes of that mountain.
  - Some months later he and Mask killed Leira and took her portfolios of Deception and Illusion (*Prince of Lies*, novel; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - In 1368 DR his inquisitors, armored by **Gond**, began the Second Banedeath against the worshipers of Bane in the Moonsea cities, and his clergy wrote the *Cyrinishad* to bind the Realms to his faith. Reading it drove him mad (*Prince of Lies*; *The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - Kelemvor then led the dead of the City of Strife in a revolt, ran Cyric through with half of Godsbane and took the dominion over the dead. Cyric withdrew to Pandemonium and built the Supreme Throne (*Prince of Lies*; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - In 1371 DR the Circle of Twelve tried him for neglecting the Balance. The court found him guilty and let him keep the portfolio of Strife (*Crucible: The Trial of Cyric the Mad*, novel).
  - In 1373 DR he allied with Shar to weaken Mystra's hold on the Weave (*The Grand History of the Realms*). In 1384 DR he arranged Helm's death at Tyr's hands (*Dragon* 381, 4e). In 1385 DR he and Shar killed Mystra with Azuth's staff, and the Spellplague followed (*The Fractured Sky*, novel; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - SCAG (2014) calls the murder of Mystra a legend. *Dragon Heist* says it has since been divined that his long hatred of her boiled over into her murder.
  - In retaliation, Lathander, Tyr and Sune trapped him in the Supreme Throne for a thousand years. His power waned over the next century, and the worship of Bane flourished in its place (*The Grand History of the Realms*; *The Crystal Mountain*; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). Even there he sent a skull-faced manifestation to order his chosen Malik in 1486 DR, during the Second Sundering (*The Sentinel*, novel).
- **In the campaign:** Cyric is present and active in 1492 DR, as every power is. FRHoF, the current canon, has him whispering to his followers in their dreams and says many of the gods he slew have returned and taken back their portfolios. The wiki's imprisonment of 1385 DR is history on this page, and it would otherwise have 893 years left to run (derived). The page uses neither the imprisonment nor the murder of Mystra in play (see Draft Notes).

> #### Using Cyric as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as a god nobody names lightly. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Prince of Lies, a god of deceit and strife who was once a mortal adventurer, and that he became a god on the slopes of Mount Waterdeep at the end of the Time of Troubles. They add that he is blamed for the murder of Mystra and the Spellplague, that his worship is banned in most lands and flourishes in Amn, and that a good person who means to lie to someone calls on Leira instead of him. Faerûnians use "the Dark Sun" as a warning that intrigue and infighting have gotten out of hand in a household (SCAG, 2014), but whether Waterdhavians do is [research]. Nothing in this conversation ties him to a faction in the city.
> - **Waterdeep:** Cyric has no temple, shrine or priest in the city in any source.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Cyric row. It lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple for all deities, and no source says his faithful use it.
>   - Mount Waterdeep is part of his story. *Dragon Heist* says Ao was last seen on its slopes when he granted godhood to the human heroes Kelemvor, Midnight and Cyric (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30483`). The mountain lies inside the Castle Ward.
>   - Gods' Day, Marpenoth 15, marks the end of the Godswar in 1358 DR. At night the Griffon Cavalry lights a bonfire on the peak of Mount Waterdeep for the fallen and risen gods who appeared there, and the text names Myrkul, Cyric, Kelemvor, Mystra, Helm and Ao (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31536`). The festival falls after the campaign window ends on Flamerule 11 (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:111`).
>   - The wiki dates his ascension to Marpenoth 15, 1358 DR, which is the date of Gods' Day (derived).
>   - Liar's Night, Marpenoth 30, pays tribute to Leira and Mask, and the text does not name Cyric (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31545`). No source ties it to him.
>   - Whether Waterdeep forbids his worship is unsourced. [research]
>   - The nearest temple a source names is the wiki's House of Cyric beneath Skullport, which lies three levels down in Undermountain (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:21`). Undermountain is future work, so treat it as a seed.
> - **A cleric PC:** Trickery Domain (derived; Player's Handbook, 2024). It is his own domain (SCAG, 2014), so it is the direct fit. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because the faith runs on blood sacrifice, prayer at night and scheming more than open fighting. His temples are forbidden in most nations, so a Cyricist cleric in Waterdeep has no temple to turn to.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oathbreaker (derived; Dungeon Master's Guide, 2014). A faith that teaches every bond must wither suits a paladin who has broken a sacred oath to serve an evil power. FRHoF lists no paladins among his followers, so the fit is loose. Oath of Vengeance (Player's Handbook, 2024) is the alternative for a zealot who punishes enemies. A paladin who opposes the faith fits Vengeance better, as the Knights of the Mystic Fire do when they hunt his shrines (see **Mystra**).
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No quest, faction or NPC uses this power. One campaign line names him, in the history of the Black Network: "Zhentil Keep fell in stages. Its northern half was ruined about 1369 DR after Cyric's fall, and the Shadovar razed the whole city in 1383 DR over the Zhentarim's alliance with the phaerimm" (`campaign/setting/history/04-the-founding-of-chains.md:35`). No campaign file ties the Cassalanters, the Zhentarim or the Doom Raiders to Cyric, and this page adds no tie. The pages for Tyr, Lathander, Helm, Torm, Oghma, Deneir and Siamorphe list him among the powers they oppose.

## Overview

Cyric is the god of lies, called the Prince of Lies and the Dark Sun, and he is the youngest god of the Faerûnian pantheon. He began as a human adventurer and became a god on the slopes of Mount Waterdeep at the end of the Time of Troubles, alongside Kelemvor and Midnight. Legend blames him for the murder of Mystra and the Spellplague that followed, and good folk avoid speaking his name. His worship is forbidden in most lands and thrives openly in Amn, and Waterdeep has no temple of his.

**Tone:** Cold and wary. Cyric is a god of lies whose priests call him a god of dark majesty, so describe what they promise before the suspicion that follows his name. Keep him at a distance, since no campaign scene brings him or his faith into Waterdeep.
