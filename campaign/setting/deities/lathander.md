# Lathander

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Lathander, the Morninglord and god of dawn and renewal, and the reference for the faith practiced at the Spires of the Morning.
> - **Where it is used:** Lathander never appears in person. His name matters in Waterdeep because Victoro Cassalanter is publicly a worshiper, and because his holiday, Rhyestertide, falls on the day the campaign opens.
> - **Featured in:** **Finding Floon** (Rhyestertide on Ches 1), **The Theater's Back Room** (a Bregan D'aerthe mission), and every public scene with Victoro Cassalanter.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier and names Victoro as a worshiper, which is his public reputation. The Secret box holds what that reputation covers.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Lathander, and sets out the few places where the campaign has tied him to the story so that no later page invents more.

*The Morninglord*

- **Title:** God of Dawn and Renewal (FRHoF). The 2014 Player's Handbook prints "God of birth and renewal".
- **Other Names:** Inspiration's Dawn, the Rose-and-Gold God (FRHoF). The wiki adds Commander of Creativity (2e) and, from the video game *Eye of the Beholder III*, Bringer of the Dawn and Lord of Birth and Renewal.
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Dawn, beginnings, birth, renewal, creativity, spring, youth (derived from the FRHoF text and the wiki's 5e portfolio; neither lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Life, Light (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A road traveling into a sunrise (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Neutral Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Elysium (Eronia) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Aristocrats, athletes, merchants, youths (FRHoF)

> Greet each dawn as a new start, and help others begin again.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Lathander is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki calls him a greater god in its 1e, 2e and 3e rows, and says he is among the oldest gods of Faerûn (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Temperament:** He is an optimist who prefers hope for the future to brooding over present failures. FRHoF adds that he is occasionally given to excess, abundant enthusiasm and vanity, and the 3e books say his critics find his zeal reckless, since he attacks a problem directly and ignores the consequences (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **What he governs:** He is the god of the dawn, which stands for a new day full of potential, and SCAG (2014) says he is not the god of the sun itself. FRHoF places spring, birth, youth, creativity and renewal under his care.
- **What the faith teaches:** Priests tell the faithful that life comes from death and that every morning offers a chance to turn a setback into a success. His favored sayings are "From death, life", "There is always another dawn" and "In the dawn, beauty reigns, and the way is clearer" (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e, via the wiki).
- **What the faith offers:** People pray to him before a journey or an undertaking, and his priests officiate at weddings and births. His name is invoked to seal alliances and launch new ventures and companies, which has made him popular with merchants and made his church wealthy (FRHoF). Temples also host dawn weddings, announce civic projects and, where possible, let a mother give birth so that the baby has good fortune (SCAG, 2014).
- **Growth and civilization:** The faithful welcome new communities and the growth of civilization, as long as that civilization gives everyone a chance to succeed (SCAG, 2014).
- **Undead:** His priests and followers despise undead. They see them as a corruption of the natural order and a refusal of new beginnings, because the undead cling to their old existence instead of moving on (FRHoF).
- **Who follows:** Aristocrats, athletes, merchants and youths (FRHoF). The wiki's 3e row adds artists and Sun Soul monks. Followers often dress in flamboyant fashions, and his priests wear rose pink, orange and gold, the colors of the dawn (FRHoF).
- **Temples:** They are wealthy and unafraid to show it, sometimes to the point of gaudiness. Some have stained-glass windows on every wall, others have parapets shaped like the spread wings of a rising phoenix, and many pay the best artists in Faerûn to fill them with colorful statues and murals (FRHoF).
- **Worship and clergy:** Most ceremonies are held at dawn, and agreements made at sunrise are said to be blessed by him. Funerals are held at dusk and followed by a wake that lasts until dawn. Novices were called the Awakened and clerics Dawnbringers, and a full priest took a new name in his service (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e, via the wiki).
- **Clergy titles:** In ascending order they are Dawngreeter, Dawnlord, High Dawnlord, Dawnmaster, Morninglord, High Morninglord, Mornmaster, High Mornmaster and Sunrise Lord (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e, via the wiki). The 2e church also required its followers to be neutral to good in alignment.
- **The Dawnbringers (FRHoF):** FRHoF treats the Dawnbringers as an order of Lathander devoted to helping survivors start anew after destruction falls on a town or village. They find refugees new homes, teach them skills and cultivate friendly town leaders. They wear tabards with a dark gray back and a front dyed in sunrise colors, and many initiates shed their old name for a new one. They find people in need through word of mouth, divination or omens, and they hire adventurers or mercenaries when they need help to reach them.
- **Other orders:** The wiki names the Order of the Aster, a small order of paladins and fighters sponsored by his church (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e), and the Order of the Sun Soul, a monastic brotherhood with links to Sune and Selûne (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). His first prophet was Rhyester of Silverymoon.
- **Holy days:** The temples hold the Song of Dawn on Midsummer morning and at the equinoxes, when priests and the faithful sing praises to the Morninglord (FRHoF). The wiki's 2e and 3e rows add any dawn. *Dragon Heist* names Rhyestertide on Ches 1 as his holiday, and Sornyn, on Flamerule 3 to 5, is a festival he shares with **Waukeen**.
- **Messengers and omens:** An early morning visit from an exuberant songbird can be a good omen. He also sends pixies with iridescent wings in the colors of sunrise (FRHoF).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Lathander. FRHoF says his Chosen wield powerful magic of light and life and spend their time lending these services to other good deities on his behalf. The wiki names Stedd Whitehorn as his Chosen and prophet when he returned in 1486 DR (*The Reaver*, novel).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book tagged where the wiki gives one.
  - He and **Chauntea** were romantically connected for centuries and saw each other as kindred spirits. Before her his lover was Tyche, the ancient goddess of fortune who later split into **Tymora** and **Beshaba** (*Tymora's Luck*, novel).
  - His allies were **Gond**, **Tymora**, **Tyr**, **Torm**, **Ilmater**, **Sune**, **Selûne**, **Oghma**, **Kelemvor** and **Mielikki**. His foes were **Cyric**, **Talos** and **Shar** (*Tymora's Luck*; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - **Helm** held a great deal of resentment for him, because the Dawn Cataclysm indirectly caused the death of Helm's lover, Murdane (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - FRHoF says the Dawnbringers count Selûne's followers among their staunchest allies, which sets them against the forces of Shar, and that they despise **Bane** and **Talos** for the destruction those gods' faithful wreak on innocent folk.
  - In the wiki's account of 1385 DR, Cyric and Shar killed Mystra and brought on the Spellplague, and Sune and **Tyr**, with Lathander's aid, captured Cyric and imprisoned him for a thousand years (*The Grand History of the Realms*; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). The Tyr page tells the same story.
  - **Zariel** began as a solar under his command in the Seven Heavens of Mount Celestia, and the wiki lists him as her former patron deity (*Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*, 5e). The Zariel page tells her fall.
  - **Amaunator** is the sun god of the old Netherese empire. Some of Lathander's followers held that he was Amaunator reborn (the Risen Sun heresy, 3e), and in the 4e books he is an aspect of Amaunator, revealed as such just after the Spellplague. By 1491 DR the two were worshiped as separate beings again (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e), and the wiki says it is unknown whether they were truly separate.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, and FRHoF for the Cataclysm.
  - He was the central figure of the Dawn Cataclysm, an eons-old attempt to reshape the Faerûnian pantheon in his image. Several gods died in the events that followed, among them Murdane (FRHoF; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). FRHoF says some theorize that his Chosen serve other good gods as an apology for it.
  - Tyche refused to share in his grab for power and left him. She then picked up a rose that was a seed of Moander, and Selûne split her to save her, which made Tymora and Beshaba (*Tymora's Luck*).
  - In 916 DR, enraged by the undead-centered philosophy of the Cult of the Dragon, he sent an avatar to Cormanthyr to fight Sammaster, destroyed him and was wounded in the battle (*Volo's Guide to the Dalelands*; *The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - In 1345 DR his worshipers, with those of Selûne, Shar and Tempus, took part in religious unrest in Waterdeep that ended in the Night of Temple Fires (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep*, 2e).
  - The wiki says he returned in the Second Sundering, in 1486 DR, through the efforts of his Chosen and prophet Stedd Whitehorn (*The Reaver*).
- **In the campaign:** Lathander is alive and present in 1492 DR. The Spires of the Morning is a working temple, and no file treats him as absent. The wiki's accounts of his absence and return are history and carry no weight in play.

> **[GM]**

> #### Using Lathander as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god of fresh starts. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Morninglord, the god of dawn, birth and renewal, and that you pray to him before a journey, a wedding or a new business. They add that his priests dress in rose, orange and gold, that his temples are rich and a little gaudy, and that his faithful have no use for undead. The temple they name is the Spires of the Morning in the Castle Ward. On Rhyestertide, the first of Ches, they say the faithful cover one eye until the next dawn, and a newcomer who catches a celebrant's eye should wink. Most Waterdhavians know Victoro Cassalanter, the banker and philanthropist, as a worshiper of his.
> - **Waterdeep:** Lathander has one named temple in the city, the Spires of the Morning in the Castle Ward.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Lathander, Spires of the Morning, Castle Ward". The text of the ward calls it "one of Waterdeep's most beautiful temples", and the Castle Ward gazetteer repeats that line.
>   - The wiki sets the temple on the northern spur of Mount Waterdeep (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep*, 2e). *Dragon Heist* puts all of Mount Waterdeep inside the Castle Ward, so the two agree.
>   - The Rhyestertide entry says the holy event of Rhyester's cure happened near Silverymoon, "but Lathander has long had a much larger temple in Waterdeep, and a following to match."
>   - The table also lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple for all deities, which includes Lathander.
>   - Rhyestertide falls on Ches 1, and the faithful wear bright garb in sunrise hues. Sornyn, on Flamerule 3 to 5, is a festival of Waukeen and Lathander for planning business, making treaties and agreements, and receiving envoys.
>   - No campaign file names a priest of the Spires, a Dawnbringer in the city or a hook at the temple, and the gazetteer marks the Spires as having no campaign hook yet. [research]
>   - No other shrine or temple of Lathander exists in the campaign files.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Life and Light (SCAG, 2014), and the 2024 Player's Handbook has both as subclasses. Light Domain is the closer fit (derived), because the faith is about the dawn and the wiki's 5e entry lists *Radiance of the Dawn* among his Channel Divinity options. Life Domain suits a healer of births and fresh starts (derived). The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), since the faith runs on weddings, naming rites and the Song of Dawn. A cleric in the Order of the Aster tradition, who fights beside paladins, would take Protector instead.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived). The wiki names the Order of the Aster as paladins and fighters sponsored by his church, and Devotion is the oath of a good paladin in a church's service. Oath of Glory (derived) fits an athlete in pursuit of self-perfection, since FRHoF lists athletes among his followers and the wiki lists athletics and self-perfection in his portfolio.
> - **A warlock PC:** The Celestial patron is a loose fit (derived). No source names a warlock of Lathander. The sources do give him celestial servants, since the wiki has Zariel serving as a solar under him, and Chosen who wield magic of light and life.
> - **Campaign ties:** Victoro Cassalanter, the opening day and one Bregan D'aerthe mission.
>   - Victoro is publicly a worshiper of Lathander. The Cassalanters organization page says the family funds temples, orphanages and civic improvements, and that Victoro is known as a worshiper of Lathander. No file says which temples the family funds, so any link to the Spires is [research].
>   - The campaign calendar notes that Rhyestertide, "a Lathander holiday", falls on Ches 1, the day **Finding Floon** opens at the Yawning Portal.
>   - In **The Theater's Back Room**, Brimel Crestfall, a steward in the Cassalanter household, says "Lathander keep me" whenever he is afraid. The mission file says he is afraid often.
>   - In the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, the vampire cleric Keresta leads Shar's cult in Vanrakdoom and plans to send the group to destroy the Spires of the Morning, which her map marks together with Selûne's temple. Undermountain is future work, so treat it as a seed.
> - **Never reveal:** What Victoro's worship of Lathander covers. The Secret box below holds it, and a player who asks about Lathander gets the Overview.

## Overview

Lathander, the Morninglord, is the god of dawn, birth and renewal. People across Faerûn pray to him before journeys, weddings and new ventures. His temple in Waterdeep is the Spires of the Morning in the Castle Ward, one of the city's most beautiful. His priests dress in the rose, orange and gold of sunrise. On Rhyestertide, the first of Ches, the faithful wear the same colors and cover one eye until the next dawn. Most Waterdhavians know the banker and philanthropist Victoro Cassalanter as one of his worshipers.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Rhyestertide and the opening:** The campaign opens on Ches 1, the day of Rhyestertide, so celebrants in sunrise colors with one eye covered are ordinary street dressing when the party arrives (derived from the festival entry). The festival gives **Finding Floon** a background detail and no scene.
> - **Sornyn and the ten days:** Sornyn, on Flamerule 3 to 5, falls in the ten days after Founders' Day. The festival calendar uses it as cover for envoys and strangers, and no file ties Lathander's faith to any scene on those days.
> - **No Chosen, no intervention:** No file has Lathander appear or speak, and none names a Chosen of his. The wiki's accounts of the Dawn Cataclysm and the Second Sundering stay out of play.
> - **Zariel:** The only link the sources give between Lathander and an archdevil is that she once served him as a solar. No campaign file uses it, and the Zariel page is a patron option for a warlock.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Secret
> - **What the cover hides:** Victoro is a priest of Asmodeus, and his devotion to the Lord of the Nine Hells is a secret known only to his wife and his closest friends (*Dragon Heist*). Most Waterdhavians know him as a banker, philanthropist and worshiper of Lathander. The pact behind it is set out on the **Asmodeus** page.
> - **The cover is deliberate:** The campaign gives Victoro Lathander and the house Siamorphe as public cover, which matches *Dragon Heist*. The philanthropist's reputation is a working part of the plan, and the family's charity is real. See **Siamorphe** for the other half of the cover.
> - **Lathander takes no part:** No file shows a priest of the Spires, a Dawnbringer or the god himself noticing the cover. Whether Victoro attends the Spires, gives to it or knows its priests is [research].
> - **Who knows (secrecy rule):** Nobody outside the Cassalanter household and cult knows the family worships Asmodeus before the party finds out. Jarlaxle knows through Vessa, and he tells Bregan D'aerthe members nothing unless they work it out. A player who asks about Lathander gets the Overview, and the party's own evidence adds the rest.

**Tone:** Warm and a little vain. Lathander is a god of fresh starts, so describe what he offers a person who is beginning something before describing his temples' wealth, and leave the Cassalanters out of everything above the Secret.
