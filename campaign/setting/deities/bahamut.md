# Bahamut

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Bahamut, the Platinum Dragon and god of the good dragons, for a GM who needs a power behind a gold or bronze dragon, a dragonborn character or a paladin, and for a player who wants to build a follower of his.
> - **Where it is used:** Bahamut never appears in person. The campaign names him in one scene rule, a bonus on the Persuasion check against Aurinax, and in one seed, a wall hanging in Undermountain.
> - **Featured in:** **Vault of Dragons** (the check against Aurinax). No other quest uses him. The Force Grey mission **The Dragon in the Harbor** concerns a bronze dragon and names no god.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier and never mentions Aurinax or the vault. Nothing about Bahamut himself is secret, but the vault and the dragon who guards it are.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Bahamut, and states that the campaign gives him one Persuasion bonus and one wall hanging and links him to no dragon in the files, so that no later page invents a tie.

*The Platinum Dragon*

- **Title:** Dragon god of good (PHB, 2014). The wiki's title row lists many epithets and no single title.
- **Other Names:** The King of the Good Dragons, the Justicemaker, the God of Dragons, the Grandfather of Dragons, the Angel of the Seven Heavens, Draco Paladin and the Lord of the North Wind (wiki title row, citing *Draconomicon*, *Cult of the Dragon*, *Manual of the Planes* and *Dragon* 38 and 378). The wiki also gives Xymor, his name among gold dragons in the oldest accounts, Marduk, his alias in the Untheric pantheon, and Fizban the Fabulous, the name mortals gave his old-man guise.
- **Pantheon:** Nonhuman (PHB, 2014). The wiki lists the Faerûnian pantheon, the Draconic pantheon, in which he was Xymor before he joined the Faerûnian, and, for a time, the Untheric pantheon. No SCAG, MTF or FRHoF entry exists for him.
- **Areas of Concern:** Good dragons and metallic dragons, justice tempered with mercy, wisdom, and the protection of the weak (derived from the wiki's portfolios and dogma; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Life, War (PHB, 2014; the wiki's 5e row agrees). The wiki's 4e row gives Hope, Justice, Strength and the Torm domain, and its 3e row gives Air, Cold, Dragon, Good, Luck, Nobility, Pride, Protection and Storm.
- **Symbol:** Dragon's head in profile (PHB, 2014; the wiki's 4e and 5e rows agree). The wiki's 2e row gives a polar star above a milky nebula, or a reptilian eye over a square of gold, and its 3e row gives a star above a milky nebula.
- **Alignment:** Lawful Good (PHB, 2014). The wiki gives lawful good in its 1e, 2e, 3e and 5e rows and good in its 4e row.
- **Plane:** Celestia, in Bahamut's Palace (wiki, 4e and 5e rows; the 2e row reads Mount Celestia and Mercuria). The PHB gives no plane.
- **Typical Followers:** Metallic dragons and dragonborn (wiki, 5e row). The wiki's 2e row gives good dragons, and its 3e row adds anyone seeking protection from evil dragons.

> Protect the weak, and let justice be tempered with mercy.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Bahamut is a lesser deity in every edition row of the wiki, and the good dragons call him their king. Sages once debated whether he was a god at all. Some thought him the archetype of good dragonkind, an avatar of another power, or a mortal dragon so strong that other dragons worshiped him (*Draconomicon*, 1990; *Races of the Dragon*). Sages of the 15th century DR hold that his origins tie him to the Material Plane, so he differs in kind from the gods of the Outer Planes (*Fizban's Treasury of Dragons*, 5e).
- **Nature:** He is stern and accepts no excuse for evil, and he is also counted among the most compassionate beings in the multiverse, with endless sympathy for the downtrodden (*Draconomicon*, 3e; *Monster Mythology*, 2e). He would sooner polymorph an offender than kill, and he would sooner talk an opponent into surrender than fight (*Monster Mythology*, 2e; *Races of the Dragon*). By draconic standards he cares little for treasure, and he spends his hoard on those in need while he prizes wisdom, prophecy and song (*Monster Mythology*, 2e; *Polyhedron* 73).
- **What the faith asks:** His dogma rests on justice, nobility, protection, honor, mercy and just authority. He teaches his followers to hold to the highest ideals of honor, to stay watchful against evil on every front, and to protect the weak, free the oppressed and defend just order (*Player's Handbook*, 4e). He also taught a greater justice that includes toppling oppressive regimes, and he rewards strength of purpose and character (*Dragon* 378, 4e).
- **The Ptarian Code:** His dragon adepts follow a draconic code of honor made by the gold dragon Ptaris, which was first meant for the lords who attended the King of Justice and later spread among gold and silver dragons. Its first precept is justice and good above all, and it also requires honor and protection for the lesser races (*Dragons of Faerûn*; *Draconomicon*, 1990). It pays homage to the draconic gods Lendys and Tamara as well as to Bahamut.
- **Who follows:** Metallic dragons and dragonborn (wiki, 5e row). All good dragons revere him, and gold, silver and brass dragons hold him in particular regard (*Races of the Dragon*). His worship grew after the Time of Troubles, when non-dragons began to take an interest in his teachings (*Dragons of Faerûn*). After the Spellplague many worshipers of Torm paid him homage too (*The Spectral Blaze*). His followers often take quests against Tiamat and her servants (*Races of the Dragon*).
- **Clergy:** He accepts only good clerics, whether dragons, dragonborn, half-dragons or others drawn to his philosophy. They take constant but subtle action for good and do as little harm as they can in the process (*Races of the Dragon*). A dragon cleric is raised from birth by a senior cleric, usually a parent or a friend of the parents, and carries a small dragon claw of platinum as a holy symbol. Losing it is a blasphemy (*Dragon* 86). A dragon cleric is a role model for other good dragons, an emissary in one region, and a spreader of his will (*Dragon* 86). A cleric who is not a dragon usually learns at the foot of a gold or silver wyrm in humanoid form, and many human and demihuman priests travel with trained canaries to copy their god (*Complete Divine*, 3e; *Draconomicon: Metallic Dragons*).
- **Orders:** The Talons of Justice are dragon paladins who follow the Ptarian Code. They probably number in the scores and live in towns across Faerûn in human and demihuman form (*Dragons of Faerûn*; *Draconomicon*, 1990). The Platinum Cadre are dragonborn knights of Tymanther who hold that not all dragons are evil and that dragonborn are the ancient children of Bahamut. Tymantheran society ridicules them and still admits they are among its best warriors (*Forgotten Realms Player's Guide*, 4e; *The Spectral Blaze*).
- **Temples and shrines:** Temples are extremely rare, because he prefers to be honored by deeds and not by objects. Gold, silver and brass dragons often keep a small shrine in the lair, usually his holy symbol engraved on a wall. The few temples are elegant buildings with simple furniture and no ornament, with meeting rooms where followers plan their campaigns against Tiamat and small rooms for private prayer (*Races of the Dragon*).
- **Rites:** His worshipers keep no formal rituals and believe in praying through their deeds. The one rite the wiki names is the Rite of Rebirth, which turns a non-dragon humanoid into a dragonborn (*Races of the Dragon*).
- **Holy days:** None. The wiki's holy-day rows are blank, and no file ties a city festival to him.
- **Omens:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Servants and messengers:** Seven ancient gold dragons form his court. They are his closest friends, they act as emissaries, and they sit as a jury when he judges evildoers. As of the 15th century DR they are Borkadd the Claw, Kurya the Eye, Sonngrad the Wing, Gruemar the Voice, Marroshok the Tail, Troannaxia the Presence and Urgala the Fang (*Draconomicon: Metallic Dragons*). Kuyutha, a dragonborn demigod, is his best known exarch (*Draconomicon: Metallic Dragons*). After the Second Sundering the greatwyrm Aasterinian served as his messenger (*Fizban's Treasury of Dragons*, 5e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Bahamut.
- **How he appears:** His natural form is a platinum dragon about 180 feet long with a tail as long as his body, and the scales have a faint blue sheen (*Draconomicon*, 3e; *Polyhedron* 73). Many sages call him the only platinum dragon (*Draconomicon*, 1990). When he walks the mortal world he usually takes the shape of an old human or demihuman wizard in peasant robes, followed by seven canaries (*Draconomicon*, 1990; *Monster Manual*, 5e). Mortals call this persona Fizban the Fabulous (*Fizban's Treasury of Dragons*, 5e). Other guises he has worn also come in sevens, among them a prince with a carriage drawn by seven horses, an urchin with seven friends and a beggar followed by seven dogs (*Polyhedron* 73).
- **How he spends his time:** He spends half of it traveling the worlds of the Material Plane, Toril among them, in the old man's guise, offering advice and judging the worth of everyone he meets (*Draconomicon: Metallic Dragons*; *Fizban's Treasury of Dragons*, 5e). The rest he spends at court in his palace on Celestia, where good clerics and paladins make pilgrimages (*Monster Manual*, 5e). He acts in mortal affairs only to thwart Tiamat, but he cannot turn away good creatures in need, and he helps them by indirect means such as information, advice, refuge and healing (*Monster Manual*, 5e; *Deities and Demigods*, 3e).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - In the 4e account he was a servant deity to Torm, in the position of a knight to a lord, and the two fought side by side against evil gods and devils (*Dragon* 381, 4e; *Whisper of Venom*).
  - His sister and eternal opposite is **Tiamat**, and the enmity runs through the worshipers of each (*Dragons of Faerûn*; *Draconomicon*, 3e; *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e). He is also at odds with Falazure, the evil aspect of Null (*Cult of the Dragon*; *Monster Mythology*, 2e).
  - His followers are allies of the worshipers of **Torm**, **Moradin**, **Yondalla** and other lawful good gods (*Races of the Dragon*; *The Spectral Blaze*).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - The accounts of his birth disagree. Commonly he is the son of Asgorath and the brother of Tiamat and Null (*Cult of the Dragon*, 2e). Gold dragon tradition makes him the son of Lendys and Tamara (*Draconomicon*, 1990). Dragonborn of Abeir say that he and Tiamat were born from the sundered corpse of Io, their name for Asgorath, after he died in the Dawn War (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e). The draconic myth *Elegy for the First World* says both existed before the Material Plane (*Fizban's Treasury of Dragons*, 5e). Some gold dragons believe he was once a gold dragon whose goodness carried him beyond his kind (*Polyhedron* 73).
  - According to the *Elegy*, he and Tiamat made the First World from primordial chaos. When the gods of the Outer Planes invaded he resisted and lost, and the First World broke apart. He fled to Celestia, was accepted there as a god, and later began to travel the Material Plane, mourning Tiamat after she was imprisoned (*Fizban's Treasury of Dragons*, 5e; revised *Dungeon Master's Guide*, 5e).
  - After the Time of Dragons the religious factions of dragons fought the Draco Holy Wars. In one of them the younger gold dragons gave up Lendys and Tamara for Bahamut and set themselves against the older golds, which split gold dragon society without open violence (*Draconomicon*, 1990). His followers and Tiamat's kept fighting in the Dragonfall War, and he made the dragonborn known as the Ux Bahamuti to oppose her dragonspawn (*Races of the Dragon*; *Dragons of Faerûn*).
  - The last intense fighting ran from -2087 DR to -1071 DR. In that time he took the alias Marduk in the Untheric pantheon, and the two dragon gods fought each other with neither gaining the upper hand. In -1071 DR, in the Battle of the Gods, Marduk killed Tiamat's Untheric aspect to save the god-king Gilgeam and died in doing it. Both lost their divine power, and Bahamut became a celestial paragon who lived in the upper planes or wandered the Material Plane doing what good he could (*Dragons of Faerûn*; *Draconomicon*, 1990).
  - In 1359 DR Gareth Dragonsbane came back from the Abyss with the Tree-Gem, which Bahamut had given back as a sign of his covenant to protect Damara from demons. Non-dragons took a new interest in his worship, and he regained the rank of lesser deity (*Dragons of Faerûn*).
  - Before 1373 DR he was trapped in a prison tied to the Dracorage mythal. When the mythal ended after Sammaster's destruction he freed himself, reappeared on Celestia and soon invaded Tiamat's demesne in Dragon Eyrie (*The Grand History of the Realms*). He gave his most devout followers the power to become dragonborn of Bahamut, so that the war could go on between mortals (*Dragons of Faerûn*).
  - In 1385 DR, after the Spellplague, demons led by the balor lord Axithar invaded the House of the Triad, and Bahamut answered the calls of Tyr and Torm by sending his legions (*The Crystal Mountain*). Torm then promoted him to the place Torm had once held under Tyr, and he became a temporary member of the Triad, replacing Tyr only until Tyr returned in the 1480s DR (*Dragon* 381, 4e; *Storm King's Thunder*, 5e; the **Tyr** page dates the return to the Second Sundering from the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*). The **Tyr** page records that in the 4e account Tyr's portfolio of justice passed to Bahamut. That transfer ended with his Triad seat, and in 1492 DR Tyr holds both. He also gained a few followers among the dragonborn of Tymanther (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - After Tiamat's failed escape from Avernus in the late 1480s DR, he sent the copper dragon Balarystul to Avernus to watch her followers (*Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Bahamut is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. The wiki's date of death (-1071 DR, "allegedly") belongs to his Untheric alias, and his place in the Triad and his stand-in role as god of justice ended when Tyr returned, so all three are history. In 1492 DR Tyr holds the justice portfolio and the Triad seat. The Halls of Justice is a working temple of Tyr (see **Tyr**), and no file shows Bahamut acting in Waterdeep.

> #### Using Bahamut as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the distant platinum dragon of the old tales. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says Bahamut is the lawful good god of the good dragons, whose symbol is a dragon's head in profile. They add that the gold, silver and brass dragons honor him, that he stands against his sister Tiamat, and that he judges people by what they do, so he has few temples anywhere. Bards tell of an old man in peasant robes with seven canaries who helps travelers on the road, and his faithful say the old man is the god himself. Nobody in this conversation mentions Aurinax or the vault. Which of his names the city uses, and how much a Waterdhavian really knows of him, is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Bahamut has no temple, shrine, priest or festival in the city in any source.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Bahamut row (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30921-30996`). Its "All deities" row, Holyhands House in the North Ward (`:30923-30925`), is the only listing that could cover him, and no source says his faithful use it.
>   - The wiki says his temples are extremely rare and that dragons keep his symbol engraved on a lair wall, and it has no Waterdeep text for him. A follower in the city therefore has a private shrine at most.
>   - Dragondown on Kythorn 20 parades and burns effigies of the dragons the city has faced (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:107`). No file ties it to him, and it treats dragons as threats the city survived.
>   - Undermountain lies below the city (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:21`) and holds his only likeness in the published adventures, a wall hanging in the Tiamat Shrine on Level 16. It shows him and is not a place of worship, and Undermountain is not in a ward.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Life and War (PHB, 2014). Life Domain and War Domain (both derived; Player's Handbook 2024) are each an exact fit by name. Life Domain suits his mercy and his sympathy for the downtrodden, and War Domain suits the Talons of Justice and the Platinum Cadre, so choose by whether the character heals or fights. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because his faithful defend the weak and the orders are knights. A cleric who keeps his wisdom and prophecy, as the Seven Gold Wyrms do, would take Thaumaturge instead (derived). He accepts only good clerics, and a cleric of his finds no temple to join in the city.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived; Player's Handbook 2024), a good fit. The wiki names two orders of his knights, the dragon paladins of the Talons of Justice and the dragonborn of the Platinum Cadre, and it says good clerics and paladins make pilgrimages to his palace. The oath's theme of justice and order and its knight archetype match the Ptarian Code's honor and protection of the lesser races. Oath of Redemption (derived; Xanathar's Guide to Everything) is the alternative, for a follower who leads with mercy, since the oath asks a paladin to use violence only as a last resort and he would sooner talk an opponent into surrender than fight.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** **Vault of Dragons** is the only quest that uses him. In the published text, the Persuasion check against Aurinax is made "with advantage if the characters brought a prominent city official with them, such as Mirt or Vajra Safahr, or if one of the characters professes to be a worshiper of Bahamut" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:14753`, with the creature tags removed), and the campaign keeps the clause. No file states that Aurinax follows Bahamut, serves him or has met him. Zelifarn, the bronze dragon of the Force Grey mission **The Dragon in the Harbor**, has no god in his files, and neither do the campaign's two dragonborn, Valetta and Rishaal. The details are in the GM Notes below.
> - **Never reveal:** That a gold dragon guards the Vault of Dragons, or that a character who professes to worship Bahamut gets advantage on the Persuasion check to move him. A player who asks about Bahamut gets the Overview, and the party's own evidence is the only thing that adds to it.

## Overview

Bahamut is the Platinum Dragon, the lawful good god of the good dragons, and his symbol is a dragon's head in profile. Gold, silver and brass dragons honor him, and so do many dragonborn, paladins and knights who hold to justice tempered with mercy. His faithful oppose his sister Tiamat, queen of the chromatic dragons, and they shelter the weak wherever they find them. Tales say he travels as an old man in peasant robes with seven canaries, and Waterdeep has no temple of his.

The published text also gives advantage when Hlam is present, because he and Aurinax met long ago and are on fair speaking terms (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:14846`; `campaign/setting/notable-figures/independents-allies/14-hlam.md:26`). Hlam serves Tyr, not Bahamut.

His page gives him an oath to Dagult Neverember and the dragonstaff of Ahghairon as payment, and it names no god. The wiki says gold, silver and brass dragons hold Bahamut in particular regard (*Races of the Dragon*), which suits the check but does not tie Aurinax to him. The sources give the advantage and no reason for it, and this page supplies none.
> - **The Tiamat Shrine (seed):** On Level 16 of Undermountain, the Crystal Labyrinth, area 12b lies inside the Dragons' Domain, where the red dragon Ashtyrranthor and her six children live (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:44031`, `:45963` and `:45990`). One wall hanging shows Tiamat fighting twelve storm giants. The other shows her fighting "a bearded and bloodied old man in wizard robes with seven golden canaries circling his head", and a DC 15 Intelligence (Religion) check identifies him as Bahamut in human form and the canaries as his polymorphed gold dragon advisors (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:46002`). Each wall hanging is worth 750 gp. The old man matches the wiki's account of his guise and of the Seven Gold Wyrms. Undermountain is future work, so treat the shrine as a seed.
> - **Zelifarn:** He is a young bronze dragon, lawful good, who lives in Deepwater Harbor and appears in **Sea Maidens Faire**, **Kolat Towers**, **Vault of Dragons** and **The Dragon in the Harbor** (`campaign/setting/notable-figures/bregan-daerthe/05-zelifarn.md:5` and `:7`). Bronze is a metallic dragon, and the wiki lists metallic dragons as Bahamut's chief worshipers. His page and the mission name no god, and he took Umberlee's offerings because he thought nobody guarded them (`campaign/quests/faction-events/force-grey/m02-the-dragon-in-the-harbor/overview.md:21`).
> - **Dragonborn in the files:** Valetta is a bronze dragonborn priest of Gond at the House of Inspired Hands (`campaign/setting/notable-figures/independents-allies/07-valetta.md:6`). Rishaal the Page-Turner is a gold dragonborn mage who keeps Book Wyrm's Treasure in Trollskull Alley (`campaign/setting/notable-figures/trollskull-community/06-rishaal-the-page-turner.md:6`). The wiki lists dragonborn among Bahamut's followers, and neither page names a god for Rishaal or ties either character to Bahamut.

**Tone:** Grave and plain. Bahamut is a god of honor and mercy, so describe what his faithful do for other people before the dragon, and keep him at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he acts. Keep the old man with the canaries a story that bards tell, and keep every dragon in the files free of him, since no file ties one to him.

The Triad seat and the 4e transfer of justice (`tyr.md:51`) are therefore history. The wiki's 5e portfolio row for Bahamut is "Justice" (`wiki.txt:80`), cited to *Baldur's Gate III*, a video game. This page does not adopt that row, and Bahamut holds no justice portfolio in the campaign. His epithet the Justicemaker and his dogma of justice tempered with mercy stay as character and are not a portfolio.

Mirt appears in the structure doc's Notable Figures list (`arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:327`), so the omission looks unintended. The page names both and does not edit either file.
