*The Lady of Strategy*

- **Title:** God of Strategy (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Lady of Strategy, the Crimson General, the Grandmaster of the Lanceboard (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014); the Red Lady and the Red Lord (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Strategy, planning, tactics, the game of lanceboard (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Strategy, planning, tactics", and no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** War (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A red knight lanceboard piece with stars for eyes (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Arcadia (Buxenus) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Fighters, game players, strategists (FRHoF)

> Win the war by planning it, and take no single lost battle for the end of it.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** The Red Knight is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon, and FRHoF calls her the god of strategy. The wiki's rank for her changes with the edition: a demipower in 2e (*Powers & Pantheons*), a demigod in 3e, an exarch of Tempus in 4e (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*) and a deity in its 5e rows.
- **What the faith asks:** Her followers call themselves the Red Fellowship, and they hold that the best planning, strategy and tactics win wars. Other war gods care about bravery, duty or the pain of battle, and she cares only about the art of tactics. Her military genius lets her and her followers see the whole of a war and not just the battles won and lost (FRHoF).
- **Sayings of the faith:** The Fellowship teaches that a lost battle does not lose a war, that a soldier plans for peace in wartime and for war in peacetime, and that allies are best sought among an enemy's enemies (SCAG, 2014).
- **Temperament:** She is calm and logical, and she is not without compassion (FRHoF). The wiki adds that she rarely raises her voice, loves a good joke and answers it with a throaty laugh, and that she will send her worshipers to their deaths if her plans require it. She dislikes anyone who switches allegiance on a whim (*Powers & Pantheons*).
- **Who follows:** Worshipers are rare in the general population. They are most often high-ranking army commanders, instructors in colleges of war, quartermasters and the authors of tomes on tactics (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). Her name is seldom invoked outside battle, except in games of strategy such as lanceboard (FRHoF). The wiki's 3e row adds paladins, monks and other lawful-minded warriors.
- **Temples:** Each temple to the Red Knight includes an altar to Tempus, so mercenaries and soldiers tend to frequent it. A vast pavilion and courtyard surround the temple, and companies of soldiers rent it to drill. Her priests believe drilling troops there is a form of worship she looks on with favor (SCAG, 2014). The greatest temples hold an enchanted war table that visiting generals can rent to visualize their tactics (FRHoF).
- **Clergy:** The wiki calls her clerics "holy strategists". They pray for their spells at night, before sleep, so that they are ready for the next day (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of the Red Knight. FRHoF says her Chosen ride her crimson pegasi into combat, but that their most important work is in war rooms far from the front, where they advise powerful military forces and predict a foe's next move with eerie accuracy.
- **Aid and omens:** She helps mortal tacticians by shaping boulders into red-veined stone golems in the form of lanceboard pieces, by sending crimson pegasi from the sky, and by animating lanceboard pieces to demonstrate a maneuver (FRHoF). No source gives a separate omen.
- **Holy days:** The wiki gives two beyond those of Tempus, whose days the Fellowship also keeps (*Faiths & Pantheons*; the wiki's 2e and 3e rows). On the Retreat, which falls on Midwinter, the clergy meet to go over the past year's campaigns, analyze battles and work the lessons into church teaching. On the Queen's Gambit, on Tarsakh 1, they feast and play day-long lanceboard tournaments, and the winners receive recognition, titles of merit, promotions and sometimes a gift from the temple armory. The wiki's 5e row is blank.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book named on each fact.
  - She looks on Tempus as a father, and he treats her as a fond and protective daughter. He sponsored her rise to demigod, encouraged her worship, and is the only one who knows her true name (*Powers & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Avatars*). FRHoF also calls her a wise, daughter-like figure to him in myth.
  - She keeps her true name secret, because anyone who learned it would gain insight into every strategy and war plan throughout the planes (*Powers & Pantheons*).
  - She sees Valkur, the god of sailors, as her best ally in naval conflicts, but he is chiefly concerned with protecting sailors and she sometimes wants more of him (*Powers & Pantheons*).
  - She is fond of Torm. The two are alike in temper and share an interest in fighting and the duties of warriors, and whether they are friends or lovers is their own business (*Powers & Pantheons*). Torm began building a close friendship with her at Tyr's urging, in the hope that justice would temper her appetite for war (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - Her greatest animosity is for Cyric, whom she would judge a poor planner even if she did not despise him as a traitor. She dislikes Garagos, who resents her and Tempus, but she has never fought him (*Powers & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced and FRHoF, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Worship of the Red Knight arose among a hero-venerating monastic order of Tempus in Tethyr shortly after the Time of Troubles. She came to those monks in a dream and gave them the Great Stratagem, a charge to travel to the places of war and teach generals and monarchs strategy and tactics. The Fellowship's priests did so across southwestern Faerûn. Many leaders turned them away at first, but those who took their counsel gained a clear tactical edge, and grateful victors built her temples (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
  - The wiki lists her as a formerly mortal human. A text in *Baldur's Gate III* says she was a devout paladin who toured armies as student and teacher before Tempus raised her.
  - In the Time of Troubles, in 1358 DR, her avatar possessed Lady Kaitlin Tindall Bloodhawk. With her adventuring band Bloodhawk crossed into Tethyr and drove back a large army of monsters, and the nation was grateful. She then found her clan's stronghold destroyed, rebuilt it as a temple to the Red Lady and named it the Citadel of Strategic Militancy, which became the center of her worship (*Powers & Pantheons*). Bloodhawk went on to lead the Order of the Red Falcon, a small knightly order that trained officers in tactics and military history (*Champions of Valor*).
  - In Ankhapur she was patron of the ruling family and was called the Red Lady, and some there honored her as the Red Lord, a male god. Her clergy kept the Cup and the Knife, the traditional symbols of the king's authority. The patronage ended in 1366 DR, when Janol Famisso took the throne and chose **Lathander** (*Empires of the Shining Sea*).
  - In the wiki's 4e account she is an exarch of Tempus with a dominion in Warrior's Rest. Valkur became an exarch of Tempus during the Spellplague (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*).
- **In the campaign:** The Red Knight is alive and present in 1492 DR as a god of the Faerûnian pantheon, as FRHoF has her. The 4e exarchy is wiki history that this page does not use as her present rank (see Draft Notes).

> **[GM]**

> #### Using the Red Knight as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as a soldier's goddess who is rarely prayed to in a city at peace. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is the Lady of Strategy, a patient planner whom the old stories call a daughter-like helper to Tempus, and that her sign is a red knight from a lanceboard set with stars for eyes. They add that commanders, quartermasters and instructors in the colleges of war are the people who revere her, and that her name is seldom heard away from a battle except over a game of lanceboard. Nobody in the city can point to a temple of hers.
> - **Waterdeep:** The sources place no temple, shrine, priest or festival of the Red Knight in the city.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Red Knight row. Its only generic row is "All deities", which names Holyhands House in the North Ward and no power (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30922-30926`).
>   - Tempus has the House of Heroes in the Sea Ward (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30978-30980`). Every Red Knight temple carries an altar to Tempus (SCAG, 2014), but no source says the House of Heroes holds an altar of hers, so this page adds none.
>   - The Queen's Gambit falls on Tarsakh 1, inside the campaign window. The city's calendar gives that day to Caravance, the gift-giving day of Waukeentide, and no file ties a Red Knight observance to it. The Retreat falls on Midwinter, before the campaign begins on Ches 1.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is War (SCAG, 2014), and War Domain (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the best fit. It is a close fit, since she is a god of war and her priests drill troops in a temple courtyard. Knowledge Domain (derived; Heroes of Faerûn) is the alternative for a cleric who plays the war-room adviser. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), for a cleric who drills and fights beside the troops. A cleric who plays the war-room adviser could take Thaumaturge instead (derived), because FRHoF puts the most important work of her Chosen in the war rooms.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of the Crown (derived; Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide), a loose fit. Her Fellowship was charged to teach generals and monarchs strategy, and the oath is sworn to civilization, a nation or fealty to a sovereign, so a paladin who serves a crown's army suits her. Oath of Devotion (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the alternative for her lawful, disciplined faith. FRHoF does not list paladins among her followers. The wiki's 3e row does, and the *Baldur's Gate III* text calls her a devout paladin before her rise.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power.

## Overview

The Red Knight is the Lady of Strategy, a lawful neutral god of planning and tactics. Her symbol is a red lanceboard knight with stars for eyes, and her followers call themselves the Red Fellowship. Few people outside the armies revere her, mostly commanders, instructors at war colleges, quartermasters and the authors of strategy books. Her name is rarely heard away from battle except over a game of lanceboard, and none of Waterdeep's major temples is hers.

**Tone:** Calm and orderly. The Red Knight is a god of planning, so describe the preparation before the fight and let her followers sound patient and exact. Keep her in the present tense and at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she acts, and keep Waterdeep out of her story, because no source puts her in the city.
