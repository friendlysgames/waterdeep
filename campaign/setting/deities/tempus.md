*The Lord of Battles*

- **Title:** God of War (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Foehammer (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014); the Battle Lord, the Usurper (so named by worshipers of Garagos), Tempos (among the barbarians of Icewind Dale), the Butcher (so named by followers of Eilistraee) (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** War, battle, warriors, brave conduct in war, the rules of war (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Battle, War, Warriors" in its 2e and 3e rows and "War" in 5e; no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** War (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Upright flaming sword (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Limbo (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Fighters, mercenaries, warriors (FRHoF)

> Fight bravely by the rules of war, and leave the innocent out of it.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Tempus is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and its only god of war (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). The wiki calls him a greater deity in its 2e and 3e rows, and the FRHoF and 5e rows give no rank.
- **Random favor:** FRHoF says his chaotic nature favors all sides equally, so he may be an army's ally one day and its enemy the next. When he manifests before a battle he can appear to either side.
- **Omen:** A rider seen before a battle is read as a sign. If Tempus rides Veiros, his white mare, the army will succeed, and if he rides Deiros, his black stallion, defeat is certain. He most often appears with one foot in each mount's stirrup, which stands for the unpredictable nature of battle (FRHoF).
- **Tempus's Honor:** His priests have spread a common code that encourages rules of war, respect for reputation and professional behavior on the battlefield, so that conflicts stay brief, decisive and as safe as possible for bystanders. The code tells followers to arm anyone who has need of a weapon, to disparage no foe, to acquit themselves with bravery, to train all for battle and to avoid feuds. Anyone who poisons wells, taints fields, kills noncombatants or tortures in the name of war is an enemy of the faith (FRHoF). The wiki gives an older and shorter dogma of three orders: be fearless, never turn away from a fight, and obey the rules of war (*Forgotten Realms Player's Guide*, no edition tagged).
- **What the priests teach:** War conducted properly is fair, because it falls on all sides equally and every soldier stands ready for both death and glory. Mortals should see war as a natural force, the storm that civilization brings by its existence (FRHoF).
- **Who follows:** Fighters, mercenaries and warriors (FRHoF). The wiki's 5e row says soldiers, and its 3e row adds barbarians, rangers and half-orcs. It also says nearly everyone who drew a sword in Faerûn had fought beside a cleric of Tempus and just as many had fought against one (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Clergy:** His priests are tacticians skilled in the art of war, and many serve as battlefield chaplains with armies and mercenary companies, encouraging fellow soldiers with word and blade (FRHoF). Priests turn up on both sides of any fight, so the church has no central authority, though within a single temple or order there is a strict chain of command (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Ranks:** The wiki says every priest is a Hammer and gives the ranks in order as acolyte, stalwart, hardhar, arahar, rauthat and direhar. The high priest is the warlyon, who wears gilded magic plate mail that lets him fly (*Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms*).
- **Temples:** The wiki describes his temples as looking more like military fortresses than temples, with barracks, mess halls, armories and training grounds (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Waterdeep's House of Heroes is described differently in the Using box below.
- **Orders:** The Order of the Broken Blade honors warriors and clergy who can no longer fight at their best, and they keep serving at his shrines and temples. The Order of the Steel Fang is an elite group that takes the most dangerous missions under battle-tested clergy (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Stormknives:** Mercenaries loyal to Tempus have long marked themselves with a rusty dagger dripping four drops of blood, and in recent years they have organized under a code of their own as Stormknives. A warrior joins by declaring it. A Stormknife carries a dagger at all times, wears a patch of four drops of blood, and adds four red marks to the signature on any contract tied to war (FRHoF).
- **The four drops:** Each drop is a loyalty, ranked from least to most important. The first is the sword, which means seeking battle and choosing violence whenever there is a choice. The second is the client, whose wishes the Stormknife obeys even to the point of standing down. The third is the contract, whose letter outranks the client's wishes. The fourth is Tempus, whose will overrides even a written contract. Each Stormknife decides what that will is, and some use the fourth drop as an excuse to break a contract (FRHoF).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Tempus. FRHoF says his Chosen are tacticians, fighters and leaders who reach battlefields so reliably that many suspect their presence starts the fight.
- **Messengers:** When he speaks to a mortal directly his voice rings with a clash of weapons, or he sends a Celestial berserker to carry his will. In visions he is a powerfully built warrior dressed for battle in the style of those who see him (FRHoF).
- **Holy days:** None sourced. The wiki's holy-day fields are blank in every edition. His faithful close a deed that pleases him with the words "Tempus thanks you", and the answer is "and I thank Tempus" (*Cormyr: A Novel*).
- **Realm:** The wiki puts his realm, Warrior's Rest, in Limbo, where battles never stop and the "dead" lie where they fell until they heal and then fight for whoever beat them. Permanent death is the punishment for cowardice (*On Hallowed Ground*; *Player's Guide to Faerûn*).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced.
  - He keeps to himself among the other gods, pursues no alliance or romance for long, and is feared by the others as likely to turn on them (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *On Hallowed Ground*).
  - He sponsored **The Red Knight**'s ascension, encouraged her worship, and alone knew her true name. She looks to him as a father figure, and the wiki likens them to a protective father and a brilliant daughter in the family business of war (FRHoF; *Powers & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Some sages said he raised her as a counterbalance to Garagos (*Powers & Pantheons*).
  - **Gond** is a friend for his war machines (FRHoF; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e), and **Beshaba** was once his lover (FRHoF; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). He had casual friendships with Nobanion and **Valkur** (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - Uthgar, the god of the Uthgardt barbarians, was his only ally. Tempus sponsored his divinity and counseled him against declaring war on gods who would have retaliated together (*Powers & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - He has very few enemies. **Sune** counts him a foe for his destruction of beautiful things, and he thinks her too flighty to be worth the quarrel (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). **Eldath** is his opposite, and he thinks her weak and naive but respects her conviction and punishes followers who harm her priests (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Pantheons*). Followers of **Eilistraee** call him the Butcher (*Silverfall*).
  - His one acknowledged enemy is Garagos, the Blood-Reaver, whom he beat and spared and who still resents him. Tempus lets him live in Warrior's Rest, and the sages do not agree why (*Powers & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - In the wiki's 4e rows, Beshaba, Garagos, the Red Knight, Uthgar and Valkur all serve him as intermediate deity or exarchs (*Dragon* 378; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He began as one of many war gods that emerged from the primordial clashes between **Selûne** and **Shar**. The war gods fought one another and the victors absorbed the defeated, until Tempus stood as the only war god of the Faerûnian pantheon. He spared Garagos alone (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - As a young demigod he is said to have beaten Garagos, the former primary war god of West Faerûn, in a battle that lasted a century (*Powers & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - The barbarians of Icewind Dale claim his original name was Tempos (*Icewind Dale*, video game).
  - In the Time of Troubles of 1358 DR his avatar appeared in a ruined castle in Battledale. Eldan Ambrose, an Amnian cleric of Tempus, followed the god's trail there and had a vision of the site's holiness. He and his allies rebuilt the castle as the Abbey of the Sword (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - In 1345 DR his worshipers, with those of **Lathander**, Selûne and Shar, took part in religious unrest in Waterdeep that ended in the Night of Temple Fires (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep*, 2e, as recorded on the Lathander page).
- **In the campaign:** Tempus is alive and present in 1492 DR. The House of Heroes is a working temple, and no file treats him as dead, absent or changed. The Spellplague, the Gruumsh merge and his relations with the Triad are not part of the sources behind this page (see Draft Notes).

> #### Using Tempus as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god of the fighting man and of the champion. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says Tempus is the Lord of Battles, the Foehammer, shown as an upright flaming sword, and that soldiers and mercenaries carry his name in their hearts. They add that he favors no side, so a white mare ridden before a battle means victory and a black stallion means defeat. His priests keep a code that tells a fighter to arm anyone who needs a weapon, to insult no foe and to leave the innocent out of the war. In Waterdeep they point to the House of Heroes across from the Field of Triumph, where the winners of the ward competitions are paraded after their victories.
> - **Waterdeep:** Tempus has one temple in the city, the House of Heroes in the Sea Ward, and the Dragon Heist text calls it the largest in Waterdeep.
>   - The Sea Ward text reads: "Must-see locations in the Sea Ward begin, of course, with the Field of Triumph, but just across the street is the no less remarkable House of Heroes-the largest temple in the city. Dedicated to Tempus, its many grand halls celebrate the city's champions of both battle and sport. The winners of ward competitions are paraded here after their victories, often carried on shoulders or passed from hand to hand over the heads of a crowd." (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30879`)
>   - The Major Temples of Waterdeep table (caption at line 30910) agrees: "Tempus", "House of Heroes", "Sea Ward" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30977-30981`).
>   - The walking statue of Waterdeep bows toward the temple. Since Khelben Blackstaff reshaped it into a sahuagin after the 1370 DR invasion, "It now bows low toward the House of Heroes on bended knee-a gesture of obeisance to the city, and an acknowledgment of the sacrifice of all who fought for the city in that war." (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31229`)
>   - The Plowing and Running (Mirtul 6 to 9) sends the ward races to the Field of Triumph. The published entry invites the reader to compete: "Who knows? Your name or visage might soon have a place in the House of Heroes." (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31414`, in the entry named at line 31410)
>   - The campaign never names the House of Heroes. Its Sea Ward lines are "noble estates, major temples, the Field of Triumph" (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:15`) and "Major temples in Sea Ward: Tymora (Luck; popular with adventurers and criminals alike), Selûne (Moon; widely attended)" (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:36`). The Sea Ward has no gazetteer file yet.
>   - No source describes the interior, names a priest or says who runs the Field of Triumph. No other shrine or temple of Tempus exists in the files, and no city festival is tied to him.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is War (SCAG, 2014), and War Domain is the matching 2024 Player's Handbook subclass (derived). It is an exact fit. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because his priests serve as battlefield chaplains who fight in armor beside the army.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Glory (derived). The House of Heroes honors champions of battle and sport, and his code asks for bravery in the field, so the fit is a paladin who seeks renown through deeds. FRHoF does not list paladins among his followers, and the fit has no sourced backing.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The only campaign line that names Tempus is a note in the Castle Ward gazetteer listing him among the wiki's city-wide faiths (`campaign/gazetteer/waterdeep/castle-ward.md:31`). The place the Dragon Heist text pairs with his temple does appear in play, as the GM Notes below show.

## Overview

Tempus is the god of war, called the Lord of Battles and the Foehammer, and his symbol is an upright flaming sword. He favors no side, so an army may count him an ally one day and an enemy the next. His priests teach a code of fair conduct called Tempus's Honor, and soldiers and mercenaries are his most common worshipers. In Waterdeep his temple is the House of Heroes in the Sea Ward, the largest in the city, across the street from the Field of Triumph.

> #### GM Notes
> - **The Field of Triumph:** Dragon Heist calls it "the city's great coliseum" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30692`), and the campaign places it in the Sea Ward (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:15`). No campaign file connects it to Tempus or to the House of Heroes, and the Twin Parades Event has no line about either.
> - **Trollskull Alley:** The Field of Triumph Event is the one scene set there. Zardoz Zord, the Faire's captain, has reserved gladiatorial slots. The format is "a free-for-all qualification round, then a four-team bracket. Nonlethal rules throughout; participants yield, they do not die." (`campaign/quests/act-i/trollskull-alley/ev-05-the-field-of-triumph.md:23`) The arena "holds three thousand people when it's full" (`ev-05-the-field-of-triumph.md:115`), and the final round is against Meloon Wardragon's Force Grey team (`campaign/quests/act-i/trollskull-alley/overview.md:56`).
> - **No temple scene:** The Event never mentions Tempus or the House of Heroes, and no file sends a party to the temple after the tournament. The published invitation to win "a place in the House of Heroes" is for the Plowing and Running, which belongs to the wards and not to Zord's slots.
> - **Fireball!:** The Event at the Sea Maidens Faire asks only whether the party attended: "A party that attended the Field of Triumph (Trollskull Alley) recognizes the private-box figure." (`campaign/quests/act-ii/fireball/ev-04-the-sea-maidens-faire.md:34`)
> - **Festival dates:** The Plowing and Running falls on Mirtul 6 to 9 and sends the ward winners to the Field (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:104`). The calendar also puts the Guildsmeet gala of Waukeentide in the Field (`09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:117`), and on Founders' Day it hosts "illusory displays that chronicle Waterdeep's history, and the City Guard gives martial exhibitions" (`09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:90`).

**Tone:** Plain and martial, with respect for the code. Tempus is a god who treats war as a fair and natural force, so describe the rules his fighters keep before the blood. Keep him in the present tense and at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he speaks, and keep the House of Heroes a place of civic celebration, which is all the Dragon Heist text gives it.
