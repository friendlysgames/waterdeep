# Mask

> **[GM]**

> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Mask, the Lord of Shadows and god of thieves.
> - **Where it is used:** Mask never appears in person, and no quest, faction or NPC in the campaign names him. The page serves a player who wants a cleric or devotee of the god of thieves, and it covers Liar's Night, the one Waterdeep holiday that names him.
> - **Featured in:** No campaign quest. The published *Dragon Heist* names him in the Liar's Night entry, and the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* names him in Slitherswamp (Undermountain Level 8).
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Mask is secret, and no source ties him to Xanathar's Guild, either Zhentarim, Bregan D'aerthe or any other criminal power in the city.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Mask, and records what the sources do and do not tie him to in Waterdeep, so that no later page invents a Mask cult or a Mask temple in the city.

*The Lord of Shadows*

- **Title:** God of Thieves (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Master of All Thieves (FRHoF); the Shadowlord and Blackheart (wiki, citing the novels *Prince of Lies* and *Crucible*)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Thieves, shadows, stealth, spies and intrigue, trickery (derived from the FRHoF text). No entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Trickery (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A black mask (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Hades (Niflheim) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Assassins, rogues, thieves, tricksters (FRHoF)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Mask is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon (FRHoF). The wiki rates him a lesser power or deity in its 1e, 2e and 3e sources, a god in its 5e row, and a dead power in 4e.
- **What he stands for:** FRHoF calls him a trickster god and the patron of ne'er-do-wells, spies and thieves, with everything that happens in shadow in his purview. People whisper a prayer to him when stealth is needed or intrigue is afoot, and courtiers and diplomats invoke his name in the hope of a smooth negotiation (FRHoF).
- **Everyday worship:** Ordinary folk pray that he will avert his eyes from their valuables. The cautious wear "Mask's purse," a cheap cloth pouch in plain sight that holds a small offering of coin. By convention a pickpocket who lifts one counts it as the god's gift, and the owner is grateful that Mask accepted the sacrifice. Anyone robbed several times in one outing has probably earned his anger (FRHoF).
- **What the faith teaches:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Forgotten Realms Adventures*).
  - Wealth belongs to those who can acquire it, and owning a thing only requires holding it. The faith also says to steal only what is necessary.
  - Honesty is for fools, but the appearance of honesty is valuable, and subtlety is everything. Manipulation is better than brute force, and dexterity is prized.
  - Mask sees everything that happens in the dark, so the faithful trust the dark and avoid walking into plain light.
- **Clergy:** Priests of Mask are thieves by profession and serve as higher-ups in the local underworld or a criminal syndicate. They hold the title demarche and wear veil-masks when they act as priests (FRHoF). SCAG (2014) adds the feminine form, demarchess.
  - The wiki calls the clergy Maskarrans and says they addressed one another as Brother or Sister Shadow, whatever their rank. A priest who finished an especially dangerous heist or a complex act of manipulation was often admitted to the Circle of the Gray Ribbon (*Faiths & Avatars*).
  - Where thievery was tolerated, the veil-masks were sheer fabric. Throughout much of the North they were thick wool that hid the wearer, and a saying held that the degree of law in a town could be read on the face of a cleric of Mask (*Forgotten Realms Adventures*). No source says what a Maskarran wears in Waterdeep.
- **Temples and organization:** The foremost organization serving Mask is the Shadowmasters of Telflamm, a criminal syndicate in Thesk. They are based at his largest temple, the House of Shadow in Telflamm, and keep safe houses throughout Thesk (FRHoF).
  - The wiki says his priesthood ran independently from city to city, so that action against one enclave would not endanger the others. Where one thieves' guild dominated a region, a temple or shrine of Mask was often joined to the guild hall by hidden tunnels. In a larger city with rival guilds, his temple stood underground on ground that every faction accepted as neutral (*Forgotten Realms Adventures*).
  - The wiki's text names no city for this practice, and this page does not apply it to Waterdeep.
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. FRHoF says his Chosen are master thieves, manipulative spies and assassins who rarely walk in the light. No file names a Chosen of Mask, and the wiki's named Chosen stay out.
- **How he appears and speaks:** He takes the form of a quick masculine being with two shadowy blades, his identity hidden by a mask, and sometimes he takes the form of masks that others might wear. He speaks to his followers by claiming a mask as his temporary face, or he sends a living shadow, a Neutral Evil Shadow Demon (FRHoF).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** Liar's Night, Marpenoth 30, which the published *Dragon Heist* gives to Leira and Mask together. The wiki's holy day rows for Mask are blank.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (3e unless a book is named).
  - He is a loner who enjoys his own company. He often allied with **Bane**, and their shared hatred of **Cyric** gave them common ground (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - He lost his intrigue portfolio to Cyric, which left an enduring hatred and made Mask more direct than in his earlier plots (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - He is at direct odds with **Waukeen**, and guardians of light, knowledge and duty oppose him. Among them is **Selûne**, whose light tends to reveal his faithful at their work. The Waukeen page records her enmity from the FRHoF side, and the pages for **Tyr**, **Helm**, **Oghma** and **Deneir** each list him among the powers they oppose.
  - He was allied with Vhaeraun, the drow god of thieves, in a businesslike arrangement. Non-drow on the surface often confused the two, and the wiki says Mask meant to make up his losses to Cyric with Vhaeraun's divinity (*Faiths and Pantheons*; *Demihuman Deities*; *Lords of Darkness*).
  - **Shar** resents him for holding the shadows and thieves she thinks should be hers (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e, as recorded on her page).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - During the Time of Troubles in 1358 DR he took the shape of a powerful blade, Godsbane. Cyric, then a mortal, wielded it after stealing it from a halfling named Sneakabout (*Faiths & Avatars*). The Kelemvor page records the wiki's novel account in which Cyric killed Kelemvor atop Blackstaff Tower with Godsbane.
  - Some years after the Time of Troubles he released the hound Kezef to kill Cyric, and the hound turned on him and bit off one of his legs. Mystra then gave him the weapon Houndsbane to defend himself (*Crucible*; *Champions of Ruin*).
  - In 1374 DR he gave his divinity and his life to Shar to repay a debt that was millennia old. The wiki says she was also his "mother," and she named his true name as Lessinor just before his death. His divinity was split among mortals and the archdevil **Mephistopheles**, and it was later gathered back into Drasek Riven, his former Second of Five (*Shadowrealm*, 4e; *The Godborn*).
- **In the campaign:** Mask is present in 1492 DR, as every power is, and FRHoF lists him as a current god. The wiki's death in 1374 DR and its 4e rank of dead power are history and change nothing in play (see Draft Notes).

> #### Using Mask as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as a god you deal with by staying out of his notice. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says Mask is the Lord of Shadows and the god of thieves, shown as a black mask, and that people whisper to him when they need to move unseen. Shopkeepers hope he looks away from their tills, and the careful wear a cheap coin pouch in plain sight as his share. Everyone knows that Waterdeep has no legal thieves' guild. A regular asked where Mask is worshiped in the city has no address to give, because no source places a temple there. On Liar's Night people put on masks and costumes to turn his notice, and Leira's, away from themselves.
> - **Waterdeep:** The sources place no temple, shrine or priest of Mask in the city. One holiday names him, and one passage names Waterdeep's law on thieves' guilds.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30910`) has no Mask row. Its only generic row is Holyhands House in the North Ward, "All deities," and no source says that Mask's followers use it.
>   - Waterdeep's laws: "By law, there are no active thieves' guilds in Waterdeep" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:1102`). The same sentence describes Xanathar's Guild as a faction.
>   - Liar's Night, Marpenoth 30 (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31545`): "This holy day pays tribute to Leira and Mask. To placate those deities and ward away their attention, folk of all walks of life don masks and costumes (magical or mundane) to disguise themselves and play at being other than what they are." The common mask styles include the black mask symbol of Mask.
>   - The festivities start in the evening with candles in carved gourds. Pickpockets are rife, so few people carry much coin, and the custom is that a pickpocket takes candy and leaves a small token (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31548`). By custom no deals are made and no contracts are signed on the night (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31549`).
>   - Liar's Night falls after Flamerule 11, the end of the campaign window, so it comes up only if the table plays past that date (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:111`).
>   - The wiki's Mask page names no city, and it says nothing about Waterdeep's thieves' guilds beyond the general practice quoted under Temples and organization. Its only Waterdeep tie is indirect: the Kelemvor page records Godsbane, Mask in the form of a sword, atop Blackstaff Tower in the wiki's novel account.
>   - Whether Waterdeep outlaws the worship of Mask is unsourced. [research]
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Trickery (SCAG, 2014; the 2014 Player's Handbook repeats it). The 2024 subclass is Trickery Domain, an exact fit (derived). The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because FRHoF describes his priests as thieves and syndicate higher-ups, who work through spells and cunning and do not serve as soldiers. A cleric of Mask finds no temple to join in the city.
> - **A paladin PC:** A weak fit. FRHoF lists no paladins among his followers. If a player insists, Oath of Glory is the closest of the available oaths (derived), because the wiki says the faith prizes dexterity, and the oath's Peerless Athlete feature rewards agility and athletics. No later oath matches a god of thieves better.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. Two lines come near him, and neither makes him a power in the story.
>   - The Players' Guide lists a background in its FRHoF table: "Shadowmasters Exile | Strong | Your old underworld connections still have reach into Waterdeep's black market." (`campaign/guides/players-guide/character-creation.md:62`). FRHoF names the Shadowmasters of Telflamm as the foremost organization serving Mask. No campaign file says that the Shadowmasters worship him or that they run anything in Waterdeep. A character with this background is a natural Mask devotee (derived).
>   - The published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* (Slitherswamp, Temple of the Great Snake, `sources/adventure-wdmm.json:23786`) says of the ghost Yoastal: "The ghost claims to have once been a cleric of Mask, cursed to remain in Slitherswamp until the nagas are destroyed." The claim is the ghost's own, and the text goes on to say her information is slanted. Undermountain is future work, so treat this as a seed.
> - **Criminal powers:** No campaign file names Mask in connection with Xanathar's Guild, either Zhentarim or Bregan D'aerthe, so this page makes no such link.

## Overview

Mask is the Lord of Shadows and the god of thieves, a chaotic neutral power whom Waterdhavians picture as a black mask. Ordinary folk ask him to look away from their purses, and thieves, spies and tricksters pray to him for stealth and a smooth tongue. By law Waterdeep has no active thieves' guild, and no temple of his is known in the city. On Liar's Night, Marpenoth 30, people put on masks and costumes to turn both his attention and Leira's away from themselves.

**Tone:** Dry and practical. Mask is a god people deal with by staying out of his notice, so describe the habits (a pouch worn in plain sight, a mask on Liar's Night) and leave out temples and cults, which no source supplies. Keep him in the present tense and at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he appears.
