> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Umberlee, the sea god, and the reference for the faith practiced at the Queenspire, her temple on the Waterdeep waterfront.
> - **Where it is used:** Umberlee never appears in person. Her temple and its Dread High Priest, Meritide Blackfin, set the deadline in the Force Grey mission **The Dragon in the Harbor**, and her festival fills the last tenday of Ches.
> - **Featured in:** **The Dragon in the Harbor** (Force Grey Mission 2), the **Junior Griffon** rank event (Meritide's grudge), and the Fleetswake and Fair Seas Festival entries in the festival calendar. **Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Umberlee, and records what the sources place in the city for her (the Queenspire, Fleetswake and the Fair Seas Festival) so that no later page invents more.

*The Queen of the Depths*

- **Title:** God of the Sea (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014, prints "Goddess of the sea")
- **Other Names:** The Wavemother (FRHoF); the Bitch Queen (SCAG, 2014, which calls it her most common moniker); the Great Queen of the Sea, Goddess of Oceans, Sea Queen, Stormgoddess of the Seas, Sea Bitch (wiki). Her worshipers are Umberlants (wiki).
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian, and one of the Gods of Fury (FRHoF, Talos entry)
- **Areas of Concern:** The sea, storms at sea, voyages and sea trade, shipwreck and drowning, tribute paid to the water (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Sea" in its 5e rows and "Currents, Oceans, Sea Winds, Waves" in 3e; no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Tempest (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A wave curling left and right (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** The Abyss (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Coastal dwellers, sailors (FRHoF)

> Pay the sea what it is owed before you sail, or it will collect for itself.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Umberlee is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and one of the Gods of Fury, who serve Talos (FRHoF). The wiki calls her an intermediate deity in its 2e and 3e rows and a deity in its 5e rows, and its 5e infobox lists her as serving Talos (FRHoF).
- **What the faith asks:** She is worshiped out of fear instead of adoration. Ship crews toss gems over the side to calm storm-tossed waters, because she is capricious and cruel and those who travel the sea must pay the price of challenging her domain (FRHoF).
- **What the faith offers:** She is mercurial but can be generous to those who honor her, as a great queen should be. Coastal villages and towns hold festivals to win her favor and avoid disaster at sea (FRHoF). The wiki adds that her mercy is bought with offerings, which bring fair winds (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Her character:** The wiki describes her as malicious, petty, greedy and vain. She does not feel bound by a promise that no longer benefits her, she rarely favors an individual mortal, and she will not let one priest grow famous enough to overshadow her church (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms*, no edition tagged).
- **Who follows:** Coastal dwellers and sailors (FRHoF). Some druids worship her as nature in its cruelest form (FRHoF), and the wiki counts her among the First Circle of nature gods, with Chauntea, Eldath, Mielikki, Silvanus, Auril, Malar and Talos (SCAG, 2014, as the wiki cites it). The wiki also lists krakens, sahuagin, water genasi and other evil sea creatures among her worshipers (*Stormwrack*; *Races of Faerûn*), and weresharks, whom she made to spite Selûne (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Clergy:** Her priesthood has almost no organization. Her priests roam coastal cities warning of doom and demanding free passage on ships, in return for ensuring her pleasure or at least her indifference. They dress in the colors of waves and wear sea trophies such as a necklace of shark teeth or seaweed wrapped around a bone. The preserved hand of a drowned person is a particularly holy object, and priests use severed hands as holy symbols (FRHoF).
- **How the clergy behaves:** The wiki says the church is disorganized, widely disliked and kept alive by fear, and that disputes are settled in favor of the strongest (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Its doctrine is that all should tremble before the sea, and priests collect a high tithe for any service. A priest who travels with a ship will see it through safely for a price, and a crew that is wrecked despite the promise has been known to kill the priest before the ship sinks. The final directive is to slay anyone who claims the storms at sea are Talos's doing (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Rites:** A novice, called Untaken, is drowned in a rite named the Drowning and is confirmed in service if the novice survives (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Shrines and offerings:** Sailors leave flowers or small candies at Umberlee's shrines in coastal cities, in the hope that she will spare them on the next voyage (FRHoF). SCAG adds that she has a large number of shrines in the coastal cities (SCAG, 2014). The wiki adds that the clergy remove the offerings when nobody is watching and wash the altar with seawater and kelp, to show that the goddess has taken her tithe (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Temples:** Waterdeep and Baldur's Gate both have temples dedicated to her, staffed by priests whose loved ones were lost at sea (FRHoF). SCAG puts it as staffed largely by the widows of lost sailors (SCAG, 2014).
- **Messengers:** When she speaks through emissaries she sends an air elemental, a water elemental, an awakened giant shark or a kraken (FRHoF).
- **Omens:** The wiki says her usual signs are the waves and winds themselves. Sailors who hear cold laughter or hissing words carried on a storm know that she is present (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **How she appears:** As a woman colored like the sea in blues and greens, with taloned hands and fins on her elbows. She wears a cape of purple jellyfish and carries a trident, and she rises from the sea to terrify sailors (FRHoF).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Umberlee. FRHoF says she makes aquatic people and creatures her Chosen, among them mighty sahuagin warlords and krakens, and that they have power over the tides and storms.
- **Holy days:** The wiki gives First Tide, held when the ice breaks up in a harbor. A parade with flutes and drums carries a caged animal to the water, where it is tied to a rock and thrown in, and an animal that survives is healed and treated as sacred (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). The wiki's 5e holy-day field is blank. In Waterdeep her festival is the Fair Seas Festival, described in the Using box.
- **Sailors' superstitions:** Seafarers of the Sea of Fallen Stars believe that cutting hair or trimming nails at sea angers her, because she takes grooming as an offering to her enemy Sune. They avoid saying the word "drown" aboard, and they hold that a person who falls overboard should not fight her will (*Sea of Fallen Stars*, no edition tagged). No campaign file says Waterdhavian sailors share these beliefs. [research]
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Talos is her superior and patron. He keeps trying to fold her sea storms into his own portfolio, so she flirts with him to stay in his favor, and she would destroy him if she could and take destruction for herself (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Auril is the closest thing she has to an ally, and Umberlee calls on her when she needs help (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). In the late fifteenth century DR, Auril's cold froze Umberlee's tides, and Umberlee turned to Talos and Malar, who helped her force Auril to retreat (*Icewind Dale: Rime of the Frostmaiden*, 5e). The wiki's Auril page says Auril fell back to the Sea of Moving Ice, and its Talos page says Icewind Dale in the late 1480s DR (see Draft Notes).
  - She openly fights Selûne, whose moonlight guides navigators, Chauntea, whom she hates for ruling the land, Sune, whose beauty makes her jealous, and Valkur, to whom sailors pray for a safe return (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Player's Guide to Faerûn*, 3e).
  - On Kelthann, an island in the Sea of Fallen Stars, the islanders believed winter storms at sea came from a rivalry between Umberlee and Grumbar. She tried to wash them off the island during the Time of Troubles, failed, and strengthened their faith in him (*Pirates of the Fallen Stars*, no edition tagged).
  - The wiki's Akadi page says Akadi can ask the wind to change but cannot make or end true storms, which belong to Talos and to Umberlee (the wiki cites it only as "FA"; see Draft Notes).
  - In the 4e Deep Wilds, Silvanus ranked above her as the ruler of the realm, though she did not see herself as his servant (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e; a 2008 designer forum post cited by the wiki).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - In 37 DR, after more than a century of calm seas had dulled the devotion of Calimshan, she sent five great waves against Almraiven, Manshaka, Teshburl, Memnon and Calimport. Between a third and two thirds of each city flooded, and the Calishites renewed their worship. Every coastal settlement in Calimshan has since kept at least two structures for her, for fear of the "Vengeance Waves" (*Empires of the Shining Sea*; *Star of Cursrah*; no edition tagged, see Draft Notes).
  - In 1358 DR, during the Time of Troubles, her avatar entered the Sea of Fallen Stars and destroyed pirate islands with storms and sea monsters (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Afterward she lived in Blood Tor, the flooded thirteenth layer of the Abyss (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Merchants, coastal cities and the Iron Throne appeased her, and the sea was calm again by 1360 DR to 1361 DR (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - After the Spellplague she lived in the Deep Wilds, in its deep dark ocean, and the 4e rows give her the Sea and Storm domains (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e; *Dragon* 378). The 3e rows put her home in Fury's Heart (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - In 1479 DR she and her sea monsters claimed the abandoned town of Port Llast (*Neverwinter Campaign Setting*). The same year, a northlander calling herself the Storm Maiden led followers on a failed bid to take the Sea of Swords and vanished with her ship (SCAG, 2014).
  - In 1484 DR she struck Neverwinter with a devastating storm after Dagult Neverember restricted the regular sacrifices to her (*Storm over Neverwinter*, no edition tagged). No campaign file mentions it.
  - In the 1490s DR she empowered Gar Shatterkeel as her Chosen and gave him the trident called the Wave, with which he opened a rift to the Elemental Plane of Water off Turmish (*Locathah Rising*). No campaign file mentions it.
- **In the campaign:** Umberlee is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. FRHoF describes her in the present tense, and the Queenspire is a working temple. The 3e, 2e and 4e homes and the Spellplague account are history, and Draft Notes records the difference.

> **[GM]**

> #### Using Umberlee as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as a queen who has to be paid. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says Umberlee is the Queen of the Depths and the Wavemother, the god of the sea, whose symbol is a wave curling left and right. They say sailors fear her more than they love her, that crews throw gems and coin overboard to calm a storm, and that she can be generous to anyone who honors her. In Waterdeep they point to the Queenspire on the beach by the east entrance to the Great Harbor, and to the Fair Seas Festival at the end of Fleetswake, when guards go from tavern to tavern collecting offerings and the coin is dumped in the harbor at sunset. A regular tells the story of the thieves who stole from the collection and tried to leave the city by ship. A squall rose as soon as their ship cleared the harbor, and a huge wave shaped like a hand swept them overboard and spared the ship and its crew. Some sailors call her the Bitch Queen, which SCAG gives as her most common name, but whether Waterdhavians say it in front of her priests is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Umberlee has one temple in the city, the Queenspire, and the Dragon Heist text places it on the beach and names no ward.
>   - The Major Temples of Waterdeep table lists "Umberlee, The Queenspire, Beach" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30993-30995`). The Fleetswake entry calls it "her temple on the beach by the east entrance to the Great Harbor" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31394`). The wiki describes a temple complex fronting the harbor, largely staffed by seafarers' widows (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep*; SCAG, 2014).
>   - Fleetswake runs Ches 21 to 30. The winners of the boat races and the other competitions hand their trophies and earnings to the priests of Umberlee at the Queenspire when the festival ends (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31394`). The campaign calendar lists Umberlee first among the sea gods the festival honors, ahead of Deep Sashelas and Valkur (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:82`).
>   - The Fair Seas Festival fills the last two days of Fleetswake, Ches 29 and 30. Feasts of seafood are held, flower petals cover the harbor, and City Guards collect offerings for Umberlee from tavern to tavern, with collection boxes at large gatherings. At sunset on Ches 30 the collected coin is sealed in chests and dumped into the deepest part of the harbor (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31395`; `campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:67`).
>   - The sunken wealth lies in Umberlee's Cache. Merfolk guardians watch it under standing orders to kill anyone who disturbs it, and rumor says the chests carry magical protections (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31396`).
>   - The wiki adds two older city episodes. In 1363 DR a mechanical dragon turtle sank in the harbor during the Day of Wonders and joined the Cache. In 1368 DR a merfolk shaman named Thur Aquarvol demanded that the Fleetswake coin be smelted into an underwater statue of Umberlee. The Lords' Alliance refused and commissioned a marble statue the following year (*City of Splendors* boxed set).
>   - In the campaign the Queenspire has a stone basin at the foot of the temple, where sailors toss copper during Fleetswake, a salt-crusted altar inside, and a water gate where offerings are delivered. Locathah messengers carry wax tablets marked with the temple's seal, and the temple keeps boatmen (`campaign/quests/faction-events/force-grey/m02-the-dragon-in-the-harbor/ev-01-the-dragon-in-the-harbor.md:35`, `:37`, `:299`; `campaign/quests/faction-events/force-grey/r03-junior-griffon/ev-01-junior-griffon.md:90`; Renaer's line at `ev-01-the-dragon-in-the-harbor.md:67`).
>   - No file places the Queenspire in a ward, gives the number of its priests or describes its rites. No other shrine of Umberlee exists in the campaign files.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Tempest (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Tempest Domain, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is War Domain (derived). The fit is loose, because War Domain has no storm or sea magic and is only the one of the four built for fighting. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), since the 2014 Tempest Domain trained clerics in heavy armor and martial weapons, which Protector grants. A cleric who follows the pattern the wiki describes joins a ship for a fee, and the Queenspire is the only temple the files give such a cleric in Waterdeep.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Vengeance (derived). The wiki's accounts of the Vengeance Waves of 37 DR and her directive to slay anyone who credits Talos with her storms give her faith its closest tie to retribution. The fit is loose. No source lists paladins among her followers, and her chaotic evil alignment suits none of the four oaths well.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** The Force Grey mission **The Dragon in the Harbor** and the Fleetswake calendar. The details are in the GM Notes below. No campaign file shows Umberlee acting, and no file names a priest of hers other than Meritide Blackfin.

## Overview

Umberlee is the chaotic evil god of the sea, called the Queen of the Depths and the Wavemother. Sailors and coastal folk worship her out of fear, and crews throw gems and coin overboard to calm a storm. In Waterdeep her temple is the Queenspire, on the beach by the east entrance to the harbor. Fleetswake ends with the Fair Seas Festival, when the city sinks its collected offerings in the deepest part of the harbor.

>
> #### GM Notes
> - **The mission:** **The Dragon in the Harbor** becomes available when a Force Grey member reaches Renown 3 and 3rd level (`campaign/quests/faction-events/force-grey/m02-the-dragon-in-the-harbor/overview.md:5`). On Ches 21 to 30 a locathah messenger brings the party to the Queenspire, where Meritide Blackfin gives them 48 hours to find whoever is stealing Umberlee's offerings. On any other date Vajra Safahr's *Sending* opens the mission, and Meritide meets the party at the harbor quay (`overview.md:17`).
> - **Meritide Blackfin:** A Chaotic Evil Darfellan, he/him, and the Dread High Priest of the Queenspire. He is formal, cold and tired, speaks slowly because he dislikes repeating himself, and does not threaten the party. He wants the offerings back and the thief named, in that order, and he refuses to discuss the goddess's moods, the Queenspire's rites or the temple's wealth (`ev-01-the-dragon-in-the-harbor.md:43-53`). His warning about the deadline is "The Queen of the Depths calls a wave into the harbor, and I will not stand between her and the docks. Find the thief before then, and I may not have to." (`ev-01-the-dragon-in-the-harbor.md:65`)
> - **What was taken:** Captains and churches leave silver cups, casks of boat-race coin and sealed candles at the deep moorings. Zelifarn, a young bronze dragon, found them unguarded and carried them to his cave under an elvish wreck forty feet down, believing they had been abandoned. The cave holds fourteen silver cups, three split casks of boat-race coin, six jars of sealed candles and a ship's bell, worth about 400 gp in coin and silver (`ev-01-the-dragon-in-the-harbor.md:280`).> - **Umberlee does not act:** Umberlee does nothing on screen in the mission, and the only risk is Meritide's grudge (`ev-01-the-dragon-in-the-harbor.md:21`). No wave comes into the harbor.
> - **Outcomes:** A party that returns the offerings marks **Offerings Returned**, and Meritide says, "The Queenspire will remember who brought it back." A party that keeps them marks **Offerings Kept**. If the party lies to Meritide and the Deception check fails, he says he will not strike them in his own house and tells them to remember that he let them leave (`ev-01-the-dragon-in-the-harbor.md:305`, `:333`, `:460-468`).
> - **The grudge:** In the **Junior Griffon** rank event, a party that marked **Offerings Kept** hears Vajra say that the Queenspire has written to her twice about the harbor, that she answered the matter was closed, and that Meritide has the party's names and has not forgotten them (`campaign/quests/faction-events/force-grey/r03-junior-griffon/ev-01-junior-griffon.md:92`).
> - **Fleetswake and the Faire:** The Sea Maidens Faire's carnival fleet arrives in harbor on Ches 21, the first day of Fleetswake, and the festival is cover for Faire reconnaissance (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:82`). The Dragon in the Harbor links the two through Zelifarn and the vessel under the *Eyecatcher*, but no file ties Umberlee or the Queenspire to the Faire.

**Tone:** Grave and plain. Umberlee is a queen who has to be paid, so describe what sailors give her before what she takes, and let the habits of the harbor carry the fear. Keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she acts, and keep the Queenspire a working temple with a tired, formal priest.
