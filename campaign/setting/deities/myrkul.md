# Myrkul

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**

> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Myrkul, the god of death and decay, and the reference for the one place he holds in Waterdeep, the city's memory of his invasion in the Time of Troubles.
> - **Where it is used:** Myrkul never appears in person, and no quest, faction or Notable Figure uses him. The city keeps him alive through the Gods' Day bonfire and a statue in the City of the Dead.
> - **Featured in:** No quest. The published *Dragon Heist* names him in its Waterdeep history, in the Warriors' Monument entry and in the Gods' Day entry.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Myrkul is secret in the campaign.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Myrkul, and states what the published adventure and the gazetteer tie to him so that no later page invents a temple, a priest or a cult in the city.

*The Lord of Bones*

- **Title:** God of Death (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** Old Lord Skull, the Reaper (FRHoF); Myrkul Bey al-Kursi, his mortal name (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Death, decay, old age, exhaustion, dusk, autumn, corpses and souls (derived from the FRHoF portfolio text; neither FRHoF nor SCAG lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Death (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** White human skull (SCAG, 2014). The wiki describes the skull set into a black triangle.
- **Alignment:** Neutral Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Hades (Oinos) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Necromancers, undead (FRHoF). The FRHoF text adds undertakers and morticians.

> Remember that death is waiting for everyone. (derived)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Myrkul is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and one of the Dead Three, with Bane and Bhaal (FRHoF). The wiki's rank changes with the edition. It lists a greater power in its 1e row, a dead power in its 2e and 3e rows, a deity in its 5eR (*Heroes of Faerûn*) row, and a quasi-deity in its 5e row (*Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*).
- **What the faith is:** Mortals fear him as the ending of things and as a symbol of hopelessness. People do not pray to him. They dread him and blame him for aching bones and fading vision (FRHoF). His faithful are morose and taciturn, and FRHoF says he is passionless and uncaring even to his most devout worshipers.
- **What he claims:** FRHoF gives him power over corpses and souls, and says he cares only for the acquisition of knowledge. He can call forth the soul of any slain creature and bind it into a wax skull worked with a ground piece of the body. While the soul is bound, the eye sockets burn with cold blue flame, and he can compel it to answer his questions. The skull melts a little with each service until it collapses and the soul goes free.
- **Who follows:** Necromancers and undead (FRHoF). Wizards and other spellcasters who want to master necromancy follow his teachings, as do undertakers and morticians (FRHoF). The wiki adds evil mages and cultists (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Clergy:** His priests serve as undertakers, like many followers of Kelemvor and Jergal, and they typically keep their patron's identity secret (SCAG, 2014). The wiki calls the church meager and unorganized. It says his priests spread the rumor that touching one of them brings certain death, so that mortals will both fear and respect death (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Shrines and temples:** Shrines and engravings of his holy symbol appear where people bury their dead, and full temples are rare (FRHoF). The few temples are hallowed places where the dead from hundreds of miles around are brought for interment, even if they were not of his faith. They set aside little room for the living, usually a single modest shrine, while the catacombs and ossuaries around it may be vast (FRHoF).
- **The doomwarden:** In the deepest chamber of each temple a throne holds the doomwarden, the preserved corpse of the temple's most revered saint, often its founder. An initiate kneels before it and spends a night and a day fasting and meditating in complete darkness (SCAG, 2014).
- **Cults:** FRHoF puts his wicked cults in Baldur's Gate, where they plague the city alongside other followers of the Dead Three. It places none in Waterdeep.
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. FRHoF says his Chosen are imbued with terrifying necromantic might and are taught the secret of binding souls and ways to siphon life from others to make themselves immortal. No campaign file names one.
- **Messengers:** When he speaks to his followers he sends a Wraith or the Ghost of someone known to the mortal (FRHoF).
- **How he appears:** The wiki describes his avatar as the skeleton of a man in flowing black robes, with wrinkled, lesioned skin and a high whisper that never carries any feeling (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). His face is a white skull shrouded in a black cowl.
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** The wiki's 2e row lists the Day the Dead Are Most With Us, and its link ties the day to the Feast of the Moon. The other rows are blank. The campaign calendar places the Feast of the Moon between Uktar 30 and Nightal 1, after the campaign window.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition tagged.
  - He and Bane and Bhaal kept a complicated kinship from their mortal lives into godhood (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Shar is his ally, and Chauntea, Lathander and Mielikki are among his foes. The wiki says the list of foes was likely much longer (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). FRHoF adds that the Order of the Dark Moon finds allies in his cult, as the **Shar** page records.
  - The wiki's 5e row has him serving Kelemvor, and SCAG says his faithful see him as the Reaper, who lays claim to souls and brings them to Kelemvor to be judged (SCAG, 2014). Even so, Kelemvor's followers are at odds with necromancers and priests of Myrkul (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
  - The wiki's 2e row lists Jergal as serving him. Its 5e account says the two share aspects of the domain of death in service of Kelemvor, despite the opposing views of their faithful (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He was Myrkul Bey al-Kursi, a human necromancer and the crown prince of Murghôm. He adventured with Bane and Bhaal in the Shadowed Age of Netheril, and each of the three sought godhood (*Dungeon* 170; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - SCAG says that Jergal grew weary of his divine duties and divided his influence among the three, and that Myrkul became the god of death and the dead (SCAG, 2014). The wiki says the three played knucklebones for Jergal's throne. Bane won and chose to be a tyrant, and Myrkul, who came second, took the rule of the dead (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - In the Time of Troubles he joined Bane in stealing the Tablets of Fate from Ao (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). *Dragon Heist* gives the same cause and says Bhaal joined them later. Ao cast the gods down to Toril as avatars in 1358 DR.
  - The wiki's account of that year brings him to Waterdeep. He took one Tablet to his realm and sent his night riders after the mortal Midnight, who held the other. He followed her through the Pool of Loss beneath the Yawning Portal and rode through the city with a horde of undead and fiendish minions. He found her and the Tablet in Blackstaff Tower, joined the two Tablets and climbed the spire toward the Celestial Stairway. A griffon distracted him, Midnight disintegrated him, and his avatar was destroyed in an eruption of plague and death (the *Waterdeep* adventure, 2e, as the wiki cites it).
  - Cyric took his portfolio and his home, the Bone Castle, and both later passed to Kelemvor (*Lost Empires of Faerûn*, 3e). Many of his worshipers went over to Cyric, and the change of his mausoleums into Cyricist temples was smooth (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). The **Mielikki** page records that she took the portfolio of autumn while he was dead.
  - His last remnants entered the Crown of Horns, an ancient Netherese artifact locked in a vault in Khelben's tower. It held what was left of his mind for a decade, then reformed and passed from host to host across Faerûn (*Volo's Guide to All Things Magical*).
  - A few minor shrines survived the Spellplague and the Second Sundering, after which the wiki says he was restored as one of the gods of death (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e). SCAG puts it in his own words: "In time Myrkul returned, for can death itself truly ever die?"
- **In the campaign:** Myrkul is present in 1492 DR, as FRHoF and SCAG present him, and the city dreads him and does not worship him. His death in 1358 DR is history. The wiki's "dead power" ranks and the *Dragon Heist* phrase "the fallen and the risen gods" do not make him absent in play (see Draft Notes).

> **[GM]**

> #### Using Myrkul as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the dread at the end of every road. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Lord of Bones, the god of death and decay, and that few people pray to him and many dread him. They blame him for aching bones and fading eyes. They tell how he and Bane stole the Tablets of Fate in the Time of Troubles, and how his invasion of Waterdeep set fire to the Southern, Dock and Castle Wards. A sage adds that he fell in that same crisis and that his followers now claim he has returned. Priests of Kelemvor say that necromancers favor him. Anyone who has walked the City of the Dead has seen his skull on the breastplate of a skeletal knight in the Warriors' Monument.
> - **Waterdeep:** Myrkul has no temple, shrine or priest in the campaign files, and the city has no festival of his own. What it keeps is the memory of his invasion.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Myrkul row (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30910-30997`).
>   - Gods' Day, Marpenoth 15, ends with a Griffon Cavalry bonfire on the peak of Mount Waterdeep, "honoring the fallen and the risen gods Myrkul, Cyric, Kelemvor, Mystra, Helm, and Ao who appeared here." The same entry says "In thanks for their defense during Myrkul's invasion and the resulting fires that raged through the Southern, Dock, and Castle Wards, Gods' Day is also a semiofficial 'Be Kind to the Guard and Watch Day' in Waterdeep" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31536`). The Castle Ward gazetteer repeats the bonfire "in memory of Myrkul's fires" (`campaign/gazetteer/waterdeep/castle-ward.md:195`). Gods' Day falls after the campaign window (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:111`).
>   - The City of the Dead holds the Warriors' Monument. *Dragon Heist* describes "a circle of women and men striking down trolls, orcs, hobgoblins, bugbears, and barbarians" and then says: "Above all of them, a flying griffon rider spears a skeletal knight whose breastplate bears the symbol of Myrkul, god of the dead." The statue is also a fountain, and the wounds on the figures gush water (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31077`). No source says what event the monument commemorates. The City of the Dead is a walled necropolis outside the wards (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:20`).
>   - In the wiki's account of 1358 DR he rode through the city and was destroyed on the roof of Blackstaff Tower in the Castle Ward. This is history and not a campaign scene.
>   - Whether any undertaker in the city serves him is unsourced. [research] SCAG says his priests typically keep their patron's identity secret, so none would announce it (derived).
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Death (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Death subclass, and none of Life, Light, Trickery and War suits an evil god of death well. Trickery Domain is the nearest (derived), and the fit is loose. SCAG says his priests keep their patron's identity secret, and Trickery rewards concealment. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because FRHoF says spellcasters who study necromancy follow his teachings. Waterdeep has no temple of his, so a cleric of Myrkul there works alone.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Vengeance, a poor fit (derived). FRHoF lists no paladins among his followers, and a god who is passionless and cares only for knowledge matches none of the four oaths. Vengeance pursues its target to the end, which is the closest match to his patience.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No quest, faction or Notable Figure file uses this power. The Castle Ward gazetteer names him once, in the Gods' Day line quoted above, and the published *Dragon Heist* names him in its Waterdeep history, which says "the crisis began with the theft of the Tablets of Fate by the vile and ambitious gods Bane and Myrkul, later joined by Bhaal" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30482`), as well as in the two entries above. The published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* has nothing on him.

## Overview

Myrkul is the god of death and decay, called the Lord of Bones and the Reaper, and his symbol is a white human skull. Few people pray to him, but many dread him and blame him for aching bones and fading eyesight. The city remembers him best from the Time of Troubles, when his invasion set fire to the Southern, Dock and Castle Wards. No temple of his stands in Waterdeep, and his priests, who are usually undertakers, seldom admit to the faith.

**Tone:** Cold and unhurried. Myrkul is dreaded more than he is worshiped, so describe the fear he inspires before any rite, and keep him at a distance. He reaches Waterdeep only as a memory of the Time of Troubles, and the campaign writes no scene in which he appears or speaks.
