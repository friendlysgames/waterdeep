> - **Where it is used:** Leira never appears in person, and no quest, faction or NPC in the campaign names her. The page serves a player who wants an illusionist, a rogue or a cleric devoted to her, and it covers the two Waterdeep festivals that name her, Leiruin and Liar's Night.

*The Lady of the Mists*

- **Title:** God of Illusion (FRHoF); SCAG (2014) writes "Goddess of illusion"
- **Other Names:** The Lady of Deception, the Lady of the Mists, Mistshadow (FRHoF); the Guardian of Liars and the Mother of Illusionists (wiki, *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Illusion, disguise, deception, secrets, lies (derived). The wiki portfolio is illusions, disguises and deception (5e, *Heroes of Faerûn*); no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Trickery (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A point-down triangle containing a swirl of mist (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Limbo (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Actors, con artists, illusionists, rogues (FRHoF)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Leira is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon (FRHoF). The wiki rates her a demigod in its 1e source, a lesser deity presumed dead in 2e, a dead power in 3e and a deity in its 5e rows.
- **What the faith asks:** Her faithful keep their secrets close and refrain from telling the truth when a falsehood or a half-truth serves them better. They feel comfortable speaking plainly only among fellow worshipers (FRHoF). Some folk swirl a finger behind their backs when they tell a lie, as a way of asking her for help (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Temperament:** The faith does not see her as malicious or as a trickster. SCAG (2014) calls her enigmatic, quiet and retiring, and FRHoF calls her a mysterious recluse who seldom speaks to her followers or appears on the Material Plane. SCAG (2014) credits her with inventing Ruathlek, the language of illusionists and the spoken tongue of Nimbral.
- **Everyday worship:** Her faithful seem to be scarce, though SCAG (2014) says it is hard to know because they rarely admit it. Illusionists pray to her for potency in their magic, and con artists treat her as a champion. Most other people pray to her when they hope to keep something secret, or to placate her before an important decision when they fear being deceived.
- **Lies and heinous lies:** Faerûnians now blame everyday dishonesty on Leira and file particularly heinous deception under Cyric, though the boundary between the two is rarely clear (FRHoF). A good person who means to lie calls on her and not on him (see **Cyric**).
- **Temples and shrines:** Her temples have no images or statues, because her priests hold that nobody can presume to know what a god of illusion looks like. They are disguised as other sites and marked with signs that only the faithful recognize, and they fill with a white mist that the high priest can raise and thicken to hide the room (FRHoF). SCAG (2014) says that temples to her exist only in Nimbral, and that her shrines elsewhere on the continent are disguised the same way.
- **Clergy:** Her priests wear vestments of white and mist gray, and their faces are covered by smooth, featureless masks. They are loath to show their faces to the unfaithful and sometimes use sleep-inducing poisons (FRHoF).
  - The wiki calls them Leirans, or Worshipers of the Mistshadow. It says they wore silver masks, often disguised themselves, spoke the truth only to other Leirans, and named their high-level clerics Mistcallers (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Leirans lay face down and prayed every morning and every moonlit night, and walked through any mist they found while they prayed. Their most revered rite was the Unmasking, held when a new follower joined or a follower changed rank, in which the follower walked barefaced among the others while the clergy held up candles and reflective surfaces (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - The Mistcallers were specialty priests with limited powers of illusion. A sect called the Mistwalkers appeared after the Time of Troubles and stayed deliberately vague about whether their divine magic came from a living Leira or from Cyric (*Warriors and Priests of the Realms*).
- **Nimbral:** The wiki says her faith was the state religion of the island of Nimbral and of its vassal state Samarach. When Nimbral rebelled against her clergy after her apparent death and banned state religions, the priests fled to Samarach and kept their influence there (a web article on Nimbral and *Serpent Kingdoms*, 3e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Leira. FRHoF says her Chosen can alter their appearance at will and that little else is known about them, which is how they like it.
- **Omen:** Displacer beasts are sometimes taken as omens of her will, especially when they turn up in places beyond the ordinary reach of fey influence (FRHoF).
- **Holy days:** The wiki's holy day rows for her are blank in every edition. The published *Dragon Heist* gives Liar's Night, Marpenoth 30, to Leira and Mask together. It also gives Waterdeep a festival named for her, Leiruin on Tarsakh 10, though the text does not call that day her holy day.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced.
  - Her allies were Mask, who betrayed her, and Azuth. She had no enemies, and Oghma opposed her because her lies hindered knowledge (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Cyric's faithful once taught that he killed her and now teach that she is his daughter (SCAG, 2014). A rumor that **Lliira** hunted her down and murdered her, because the two names are so alike, went around after the Time of Troubles. The wiki calls it false, since no evidence of it has ever turned up.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Months after the Time of Troubles, Cyric killed her at the village of Blackfeather Bridge with the sword Godsbane, which was Mask in the form of a blade, and absorbed her portfolios (*Lost Empires of Faerûn*, 3e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Her realm in Limbo was the Courts of Illusion, and her body was thought to be adrift on the Astral Plane (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Her divine power burst out at her death and reached her places of worship across Faerûn, and motes of it became incorporeal beings called glamers (*Dragon* 299).
  - Some believed she was alive and had used her death as a grand illusion, but the general view was that she was a dead power. Leiran traditionalists on Nimbral who held that she lived were in fact gaining their spells from Cyric (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Shining South*).
  - By the late fifteenth century DR it became known that she was alive and delighted in the confusion that the rumors of her death and return had caused (SCAG, 2014). FRHoF says she reclaimed most of her portfolio on her return, but not all of it.
- **In the campaign:** Leira is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. FRHoF and SCAG (2014) both have her living, and the wiki notes that the 5e *Player's Handbook* lists her. The murder at Blackfeather Bridge is history on this page. The *Dragon Heist* festival tale below is a second account of her being punished, and no source says how the two fit together.

> #### Using Leira as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as a goddess people keep on good terms with and do not look at too closely. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is the Lady of the Mists, the goddess of illusion and deception and the patron of illusionists and liars, and that she is a quiet figure and no trickster. They say folk pray to her to keep a secret, or before a choice where they fear being cheated, and that a swirl of the finger behind the back is how you ask her to cover a lie. A sage adds that she has been reported dead more than once, and that some tales give Cyric the killing. On Tarsakh 10 they tell the Leiruin story, in which Waukeen caught Leira trying to cheat her in a deal and buried her under a mountain of molten gold. Nobody can name a temple of hers in the city.
> - **Waterdeep:** The sources place no temple, shrine or priest of Leira in Waterdeep, and name two festivals.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30910`) has no Leira row. Its only generic row is Holyhands House in the North Ward, "All deities," and no source says that Leira's followers use it.
>   - SCAG (2014) says that the only temples to her are in Nimbral and that her shrines elsewhere are disguised and marked for the faithful only. No source places a disguised shrine in Waterdeep, so whether one exists is [research].
>   - Leiruin, Tarsakh 10, is the last day of Waukeentide and falls inside the campaign window (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:83`). The published text says: "In times long past, Waukeen caught Leira, the goddess of illusions and deception, attempting to cheat her in a deal, and buried her under a mountain of molten gold as punishment." It adds that the festival commemorates the event, and that it is the day for guild members to pay their annual dues and for guildmasters to meet the Lords of Waterdeep and renew their charters for another year (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31405`). The **Waukeen** page tells the same tale.
>   - Liar's Night, Marpenoth 30, pays tribute to Leira and Mask. People of all walks of life wear masks and costumes to ward off the attention of both, and the common mask styles include "the mirror face of the priests of Leira" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31545`). By custom no deals are made and no contracts are signed that night (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31549`). It falls after Flamerule 11, so it comes up only if the table plays past the campaign window (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:111`).
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Trickery (SCAG, 2014), and Trickery Domain is the direct fit in the 2024 Player's Handbook (derived). The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because her faith works through illusion, secrecy and masks and does not take the field. A cleric of Leira finds no temple in the city, and an open devotee must expect to hide the faith, as her priests do.
> - **A paladin PC:** None of the four oaths fits (derived). FRHoF lists no paladins among her followers, and her faithful lie by habit, which sits badly with an oath.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power as a character, a faction or a quest. Two calendar lines name her. The Waukeentide row in the festival calendar lists "Leiruin on Tarsakh 10 (guild dues paid and charters renewed with the Lords)", and the after-window line lists Liar's Night (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:83` and `:111`).

## Overview

Leira is the Lady of the Mists, a chaotic neutral power of illusion and deception whom illusionists, actors, rogues and con artists look to as their patron. She is a quiet and secretive goddess, and her faithful are happy to keep it that way. Waterdhavians know her chiefly from two holidays. On Leiruin, Tarsakh 10, the guilds pay their dues and renew their charters. The old tale behind it says Waukeen buried her under molten gold for cheating her in a deal. On Liar's Night, Marpenoth 30, people wear masks to turn her notice, and Mask's, away from themselves.

**Tone:** Soft and evasive. Leira is a goddess who prefers not to be looked at, so describe what her followers do (the finger behind the back, the mist, the masks) and say little about what she wants. Keep her in the present tense and at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she appears.
