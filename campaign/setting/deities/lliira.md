**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Lliira, and states which festivals the sources tie to her so that no later page invents a temple, a priest or a worshiper.

*Our Lady of Joy*

- **Title:** God of Joy (FRHoF). The 2014 Player's Handbook and SCAG print "Goddess of joy", and every source uses "she".
- **Other Names:** Mistress of Revels, Our Lady of Joy (FRHoF); Joybringer (SCAG, 2014); the Lady of Joy (*Dragon Heist*); the Everchanging Goddess, the Goddess of Joy (wiki, *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Joy, festivals, dance, contentment, freedom, hospitality (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki's 2e portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Life (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Triangle of three six-pointed stars (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Arborea (Arvandor) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Bards, dancers, poets, revelers, singers (FRHoF)

> Give joy freely, and keep the celebration free of violence.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Lliira is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. FRHoF lists her simply as a deity. The wiki calls her a lesser deity in its 2e and 3e rows and a demigoddess in 1e, and in its 4e row she is a good-aligned exarch of Sune (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*).
- **What she embodies:** Joy, contentment, dance and freedom. She is ever-changing and constantly moving, and as the patron of festivals she is honored at any celebration (FRHoF). SCAG (2014) adds happiness and release to the list.
- **How she is worshiped:** Dance is the primary way to worship her (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). Dancers and hedonists are among her most devoted worshipers, and some train in the bardic techniques of the College of Dance to express their devotion (FRHoF). The College of Dance is also a bard subclass in the 2024 Player's Handbook (derived).
- **What the faith asks:** Her followers wear at least one item of clothing in a bright, cheerful color (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). Rubies and sapphires are sacred to her, and her priests bless anyone they see wearing them (SCAG, 2014). The wiki gives her colors as orange, red and yellow (2e).
- **Not frivolous:** Her followers hold that divine joy is a real gift that mortals badly need, so they fight those who bring misery to others. They are fierce against their foes and joyous revelers when the work is done (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **What she forbids:** She abhors violence. Any fighting or drawing of weapons at a celebration, except in ceremony, makes her withhold her favor (SCAG, 2014). The wiki adds that a sword drawn in anything but ceremony drives her away, and that she simply leaves if combat breaks out (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Temperament:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). She enjoys life without a care, stays somewhat detached from everyday events, and has a flair for performance. She is neither ambitious nor greedy. She refuses to harm anyone unless it is unavoidable, though she will temporarily transform or move a troublemaker elsewhere, and she plays pranks, such as swapping wine for sour milk, as a joke or in revenge for an insult.
- **Clergy:** Her priests are joybringers, and they take it as their mission to make other people happy, even for a moment (SCAG, 2014). Their vestments resemble festival attire more than somber ecclesiastical garments, they throw excellent parties, and they are among the most beloved clergy in Faerûn. They are prone to jokes but never pranks (FRHoF). The wiki adds that from 1365 DR the specialty priests took the title joydancer, because the clergy had grown confused when clerics and specialty priests shared one name (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Orders:** Wiki-sourced. The orders of the church include the Order of the Leaping Stag and the Order of the White Violet (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). The Order of the Scarlet Mummers was formed to avenge the murder of the church's High Revelmistress in Selgaunt, and its duties were to protect Lliiran temples and to fight the followers of **Loviatar** (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Lliira. FRHoF says her Chosen fight those who bring misery to others, master mirthful spells such as *Tasha's Hideous Laughter* and *Otto's Irresistible Dance*, and use them against evil forces like sharpened weapons. They battle fiercely and then revel when their work is done.
- **Manifestations and messengers:** She appears on the Material Plane as a cluster of brightly whirling lights, a single light that leaves a glittering trail, or peals of joyous laughter. Will-o'-wisps and the *Dancing Lights* spell are easily mistaken for signs of her, and necromancers and charlatans use the likeness to fool victims. Joyful empyrean iotas or dancing pixies are her messengers of choice (FRHoF). The wiki adds a dancing light that can speak, a sudden radiance on an object with joyous laughter or wordless singing, and rainbows (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Satyrs:** She is especially popular with satyrs, and several of the songs, dances and prayers associated with her trace back to satyr communities (FRHoF).
- **How she appears:** Artists depict her as a woman soaring through the sky in elated dance (FRHoF).
- **Omen:** No source gives an omen apart from the manifestations above. [research]
- **Holy days:** *Dragon Heist* gives her two festivals.
  - Lliira's Night falls on Flamerule 7. It began as a celebration held only in Waterdeep and has since spread up and down the Sword Coast. The night honors the Lady of Joy with dances and balls across the city, and pink drinks, from healthy juices to deadly strong spirits, go round all night. A recent custom from Baldur's Gate has the City Guard set off smokepowder fireworks, so the boom and crackle go on until dawn. The wiki's 2e row lists Lliira's Night as her holy day and cites *City of Splendors: Waterdeep*.
  - The Grand Revel falls on Alturiak 14 and is led by the clergy of Sune, Sharess and Lliira. It is a day of dancing, music and sweet treats. Some of the dancing is wanton and performed for show, and large ring dances in the street for all ages are also popular. The dancing ends at dusk, bards and minstrels then perform at "love feasts" for families, and couples slip away to exchange promises and small tokens, often rings blessed by clergy with prayers of faithfulness. It falls before the campaign opens on Ches 1 (derived from the festival calendar).
  - The wiki's 5e holy-day row is blank.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (2e and 3e sources).
  - Her best friend among the Faerûnian powers is **Milil**, who shares her love of revelry. She is allied with **Deneir** and **Oghma**, though she refuses to treat Oghma as her superior (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Sharess is another good friend, for the same reason. The two fell out when Sharess came under the corruption of **Shar**, and the friendship was rekindled after Sune saved her in the Time of Troubles (*Powers & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - **Waukeen** was a good friend before the Time of Troubles. When Lliira answered the prayers of Waukeen's followers during her absence, many of them assumed their goddess was dead and turned to Lliira, and Waukeen came back jealous and upset. The friendship soured slightly (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Running the Realms*, 2e). The **Waukeen** page tells the same story.
  - In the 3e books she shares Brightwater with **Sune** and **Tymora** (*Book of Chaos*), and the wiki's 2e row has her serving Sune.
  - After the Time of Troubles a rumor said she hunted down and murdered **Leira** because the names are alike. The wiki calls the rumor false, since no evidence of it has ever turned up (*Forgotten Realms Adventures*; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book named on each fact. The wiki gives no account of her origin before the Time of Troubles. [research]
  - When Lord Ao cast the gods out as mortal avatars in the Time of Troubles (1358 DR), the first being she met was the avatar of Waukeen. The two stayed together, climbed the Celestial Stairway from Shadowdale and tried to bribe **Helm** to let them back into the Outer Planes. Helm refused (*For Duty & Deity*; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Waukeen then decided that her own divinity bound her to the world, and Lliira promised to hold Waukeen's power and portfolio in stewardship until she could reclaim it. During this time Lliira took Chlanna Asjros, her High Revelmistress of Selgaunt, as a lover, and a local cult of Loviatar later murdered the priestess (*For Duty & Deity*; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Waukeen was trapped in the Abyss. In 1365 DR Lliira took control of the Church of Waukeen in order to preserve it for her friend, and she acted as regent for the portfolio, answering prayers for spells. She felt tempted to fold Waukeen's power into her own and resisted out of loyalty to a promise. When Waukeen returned, Lliira gave back her mantle, and the church had flourished under the regency (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *For Duty & Deity*).
  - After the Spellplague she became an exarch of Sune and moved to the Gates of the Moon with her (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **In the campaign:** Lliira is alive and present in 1492 DR. The regency and the exarch rank are history, and no file treats her as subordinate to Sune or as absent.

> #### Using Lliira as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as the lady of the dance floor. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is the goddess of joy, dance and festivals, that her priests, the joybringers, dress like guests at a ball and throw excellent parties, and that her faithful wear something bright. They add that she turns away from any feast where a blade is drawn. Every Flamerule 7 the city keeps her night with dances, balls, pink drinks and fireworks until dawn, and Waterdhavians say the custom began here before it spread along the Sword Coast. How they feel about her clergy outside the festivals is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Lliira has no temple or shrine of her own in the city in any source, and she has two festivals.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Lliira row. The same table lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple for all deities, which includes her.
>   - No campaign file or ward gazetteer page names a shrine, a priest or a joybringer of hers. The Grand Revel text speaks of "the clergy of" Lliira, which implies that her clergy are in the city, but it names no temple or person. Whether a joybringer lives in Waterdeep is [research].
>   - Lliira's Night (Flamerule 7) is a nightlong festival of dances and balls, with smokepowder fireworks set off by the City Guard. It falls in the ten days between Founders' Day and Flamerule 11.
>   - The Grand Revel (Alturiak 14) is led by the clergy of Sune, Sharess and Lliira. It falls before the campaign opens.
>   - The wiki's temples of Lliira are the House of Happiness in Hillsfar, the Palace of Holy Festivals in Selgaunt and the Theater of Joy in Crimmor (*Lands of Intrigue: Amn*, for Crimmor). None is in Waterdeep.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Life (SCAG, 2014). Life Domain is the matching 2024 subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War (derived), a close fit. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because SCAG says her followers fight those who bring misery to others and are fierce against their foes. A cleric of hers finds no temple to join in Waterdeep, and the sources place no joybringer in the city. A bard with the College of Dance is the follower FRHoF names first.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of the Ancients (derived), as a loose fit. The 2014 Player's Handbook version of the oath tells its paladins to delight in song and laughter, in beauty and art, which matches a faith of dance and joy, and the faith also fights those who bring misery. FRHoF lists no paladins among her followers.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources. FRHoF says satyrs love her, but no source presents her as a patron.
> - **Campaign ties:** Lliira's Night and nothing else. The festival calendar lists "Flamerule 7 | Lliira's Night | Dances and balls through the night, with smokepowder fireworks set off by the City Guard until dawn." No NPC, faction or quest names her. The details are in the GM Notes below.

## Overview

Lliira, Our Lady of Joy, is the goddess of joy, dance and festivals, and people honor her at any celebration. Her priests, the joybringers, wear festival clothes and throw parties, and they are among the most beloved clergy in Faerûn. Her faithful wear at least one bright, cheerful color, and she turns her favor away from any revel where fighting breaks out or weapons are drawn. Waterdeep keeps her night on Flamerule 7 with dances and balls across the city and smokepowder fireworks until dawn.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Lliira's Night and the ten days:** The festival falls on Flamerule 7, six days after Founders' Day and four days before the twins' ninth birthday on Flamerule 11 (derived). The festival calendar lists it and Sornyn as the two festivals inside the ten days between the feast and the birthday. Its hook is cover. Envoys and strangers are expected in the city and fireworks boom until dawn, so unfamiliar faces and loud noises draw no notice.
> - **No worshiper, no Chosen, no intervention:** No file has Lliira appear or speak, and none names a Chosen or a worshiper of hers. Her reach in the story is the festival date, and no scene is written for it.

**Tone:** Warm and light, in the brisk reference register. Lliira is a goddess of celebration, so describe what the party can enjoy before what she forbids, and keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she speaks.
