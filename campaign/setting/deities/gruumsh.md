# Gruumsh

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Gruumsh, the One-Eyed God and head of the orc pantheon, for players who want an orc, a half-orc or a war-minded cleric, and for a GM who needs a power behind an orc raid, a shaman or a half-orc's family faith.
> - **Where it is used:** Gruumsh never appears in person, and no campaign file or published adventure names him. The page records the half-orcs who do appear in the files, so that no later page ties them to him.
> - **Featured in:** No quest. His name appears only on other Deities pages, among them **Corellon Larethian**, **Lolth**, **Moradin**, **Grumbar**, **Talos** and **Auril**.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Gruumsh is secret.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Gruumsh, and says plainly that no source gives him a temple, a shrine or a worshiper in Waterdeep.

*The One-Eyed God*

- **Title:** God of storms and war (SCAG, 2014). The PHB, 2014, prints "Orc god of storms and war", and VGM, 2014, gives He Who Watches. This page follows the 2014 (5e) printings, in which Talos is a separate and living god. The 4e books note Talos as an aspect of Gruumsh, and that merger is tagged history below.
- **Other Names:** He Who Watches (VGM, 2014). The One-Eye, the Cursed One, the First Power of the Orcs and He Who Never Sleeps (wiki). The adjective for his followers and rites is Gruuman (wiki, *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **Pantheon:** Orc (SCAG, 2014), and its head. The PHB, 2014, files him under "Nonhuman".
- **Areas of Concern:** War, conquest, territory, strength, survival and destruction (derived from the VGM text and the wiki's 5e portfolio of conquest, destruction, storms, strength, survival and war; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Tempest, War (SCAG, 2014; the PHB, 2014, agrees)
- **Symbol:** Unblinking eye (SCAG, 2014; the PHB, 2014, agrees)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Acheron, on the layer called Nishrek (VGM, 2014; wiki, 5e)
- **Typical Followers:** Orcs, half-orcs (wiki, 5e row; VGM, 2014, names orcs)

> Take the world that the other gods cheated the orcs of.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Gruumsh is the undisputed ruler of the orc pantheon (VGM, 2014). The wiki, citing SCAG (5e), calls that pantheon the Tribe of He Who Watches, and it rates him a greater deity in its 1e to 4e rows. Its 5e rank is blank.
- **His pantheon:** The wiki's 5e row names his servants as Bahgtru, Ilneval, Luthic, Shargaas and Yurtrus. Luthic the Cave Mother, goddess of healing and home, is his mate, and Bahgtru, god of might and loyalty, is their son. Ilneval, god of war and strategy, is his lieutenant, though Gruumsh does not trust him. Shargaas is the god of darkness and stealth, and Yurtrus is the god of death and disease (wiki; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
- **What he wants:** He pushes his children to increase their numbers so that they can be his instrument of revenge against the realms of elves, humans and dwarves. To spite the gods who spurned him, he leads the orcs in ceaseless slaughter, driven by a rage that wants to lay waste to the civilized world. Orcs are chaotic and unorganized by nature, and only the charismatic ones he has touched can control a tribe (VGM, 2014).
- **The chosen few:** An orc can claim allegiance to Gruumsh, but only those who have proven themselves in feats of strength and ferocity in war count as true worshipers. He marks each of them with a powerful dream or vision, and the visit changes them in mind and often in body. Some are driven to the brink of madness and mutter about omens and prophecies, while others gain supernatural power and rise to lead (VGM, 2014).
- **Eyes of Gruumsh:** A few of the touched carry a small part of his rage into battle as magic that strengthens weapons and helps the tribe succeed. An orc chosen for this must gouge out one of its eyes as a sign of devotion, and the tribe treats these Eyes of Gruumsh with respect even when they are old and infirm (VGM, 2014). The wiki adds that tribes disagree about which eye is taken (*Monster Mythology*).
- **Worship and clergy:** Clerics and adepts pray for their spells in the dark of night. Holy days fall on the new moon, and the anniversaries of great battles against dwarves, elves or rival orc tribes are holy as well (wiki; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Legends & Lore*, 1e; *Demihuman Deities*, 2e). The same sources say proper worship calls for blood every month, preferably human or demihuman and above all elven. His clergy spread the faith through intimidation and inspiration, and they become war leaders or advisers to war leaders (wiki; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Monster Mythology*).
- **Holy day:** The wiki's 4e row gives Gharfek'taaz, the Feast of the Bloodied Stones, on the last day of Marpenoth. It marks his rise to master of Nishrek, and new shamans are ordained that day in rites of sacrifice and torture (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). The wiki's 3e row gives the new moon, and its 5e row is blank. The date falls after the Dragon Heist calendar ends on Flamerule 11 (derived).
- **Prayer and rite:** Every prayer to him opens with *kharg-hark*, which means revenge in Orcish. The best known rite is the Ritual of the Poles, in which the victors drive longspears point up along the border of newly conquered land and set the head of a fallen foe on each one (wiki; *Complete Divine*).
- **Omens and dreams:** His omens range from a drifting cloud of toxic smoke to the sudden snapping of a young shaman's neck, and the second kind is the usual one (*Monster Mythology*). He also gives orders to his devoted in dreams (*Sons of Gruumsh*).
- **Messengers:** He sends powerful fiendish orc barbarians to act as his heralds on the Material Plane (*Complete Divine*). His other proxies include orc champions, patchwork teams of fiends and powerful undead (*Planes of Law*).
- **Storms:** The wiki's 4e account says orcs saw his face in avalanches, took earthquakes for his anger and heard his voice in howling storms. It adds that he raised thunderheads in front of his hordes by calling on his Talos aspect (*Monster Vault*; *Dragon* 378, 4e).
- **Afterlife:** Orcs who die with honor go to Nishrek in Acheron, where they join his army in the endless war against the goblinoid followers of Maglubiyet (VGM, 2014). Nishrek is one face of a giant cube, and the goblinoid realm of Clangor holds the opposite face (wiki; *Planes of Law*). Priests of Yurtrus perform the funeral rites for a fallen hero. The head is severed, boiled or smoked, and punched at the bridge of the nose, which leaves a skull with a single eye socket (wiki; VGM, 2014).
- **How the orc peoples differ:** Gray orcs honor the whole pantheon and pick the god closest to their interests. Almost all mountain orc clerics follow Gruumsh, and deep orcs keep his worship while forgetting the rest of the pantheon (*Races of Faerûn*, 3e). Half-orcs raised in orc tribes normally follow him. Other half-orcs, barbarians and fighters among them, may worship him as a war god without being evil, and those who want to avoid mistrust keep the faith private (*Races of Destiny*; *Player's Handbook*, 3e).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Luthic holds the pantheon together and takes the longer view of the war with Maglubiyet, urging the orcs to have many children so the goblinoids cannot outnumber them. Gruumsh sees the same war as a chance for the orcs to prove their worth, and he relishes every short-term victory. The archmage Tzunk predicts that Luthic will outlast him as the last god standing (*Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
  - He holds a particular hatred for the elven gods, above all Corellon, and one of his dictates is to destroy elves along with their homes and lands (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Sehanine Moonbow earns a special enmity because her subtle, pale and feminine ways offend his sense of strength (*Evermeet: Island of Elves*).
  - His hatred of the dwarven gods, Moradin above all, roughly equals his hatred of the elven ones, and every orc tribe dreams of taking a dwarven stronghold. Moradin loathes him in return (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes*, 5e; *Demihuman Deities*). The gnome gods Garl Glittergold and Baervan Wildwanderer annoy him with pranks (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes*, 5e).
  - Maglubiyet is in many ways his twin, an iron-fisted tyrant who reveres war. Gruumsh wants the territory and Maglubiyet wants the killing, and Maglubiyet's smaller pantheon is more divided and has no trusted right hand. Gruumsh lets his own gods squabble, which the wiki counts among his weaknesses (*Monster Mythology*; *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
  - Lolth once colluded with him against Corellon, and he refused to work with her again after she betrayed him. He now hates her as an elf (*Dragon* 408, 4e era; *Evermeet: Island of Elves*).
  - Asmodeus and his vassals have long held pacts with evil gods, Gruumsh among them, so devils are often found serving him (*Monster Manual*, 4e). He opposes the demons as undisciplined rabble (*Hellbound: The Blood War*).
  - He treats no non-orc as a true ally (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). The wiki names the kobold god Kurtulmak as a past partner (*Monster Mythology*) and the troll and ogre god Vaprak as an ally he brought under his thumb around the Spellplague (*The Plane Above*, 4e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Gruumsh created the orcs and ruled them and their gods as an unquestioned patriarch (*Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e; *Monster Mythology*). Some elven tales say orcs arose instead from his blood where it soaked the earth after his duel with Corellon (*Dragon* 408, 4e era; *Evermeet: Island of Elves*).
  - In the orc telling, the gods drew lots for the lands of the world. When Gruumsh's turn came the good places were gone, and the lots had been rigged. He pierced the mountains, laid waste to the hills, gouged the meadows and burned the forests to make homes for his people, and he swore that the orcs would take other lands and one day rule the world (*Monster Mythology*; *Monster Manual*, 5e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Orc religion denies that Corellon took his eye and treats the claim as blasphemy (*Monster Mythology*). In the elven telling, Corellon's blade pinned Gruumsh's arm so that his own dagger put out the eye, and Corellon then spared a wounded and unarmed foe. The **Corellon Larethian** page tells the duel (*Evermeet: Island of Elves*).
  - His pantheon long had no home. It was driven out of Gehenna and the Nine Hells and settled in Acheron, where Maglubiyet already lived (*Manual of the Planes*, 1e; *Planes of Law*).
  - In -1076 DR a horde of orcs came through a gate that the wizard Thayd had opened on the Plateau of Thay and overran Mulhorand and Unther. Gruumsh and his allies killed Re, the god-king of Mulhorand, in the first deicide in the Realms, and the orc side lost many gods in the war (*Powers & Pantheons*; *Races of Faerûn*, 3e).
  - In the Spellplague era, around 1385 DR, the orc population grew and he put his own pantheon and the minor gods of savage peoples under his control (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). Talos was noted as one of his aspects (*Dragon* 378, 4e era), and Maglubiyet was forced under the tyranny of **Bane** (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). The **Talos** page records an event called the Stormstar Requiem, in which Talos was said to have revealed himself as Gruumsh (*Dragon* 367, 4e era).
  - In the Second Sundering he lost his Talos aspect (SCAG, 5e), and Maglubiyet broke free of Bane, so the eternal war in Acheron began again (*Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*, 5e; *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e). His hold on his own pantheon weakened when Luthic's secret romance with **Grumbar** came out (*The Sentinel*, novel).
- **In the campaign:** Gruumsh is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. The page follows the 2014 (5e) printings, where he and Talos are separate gods, and keeps the 4e Talos merger as history. Draft Notes records the overlap in their printed titles.

> #### Using Gruumsh as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god that orcs follow and everyone else fears them for. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the One-Eyed God of the orcs, a god of war and conquest whose symbol is an unblinking eye, and that he watches every orc and judges what it is worth. They add that orcs believe the other gods cheated them out of a place in the world and mean to take one by force, that his chosen priests cut out one of their own eyes for him, and that an orc who dies well goes to Acheron to fight the goblinoid gods' armies forever. A sage would cite *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, which covers the Eyes, Nishrek and the war with the goblinoids. How Waterdhavians feel about orcs and half-orcs, and which of his names the city uses, is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Gruumsh has no temple, shrine, priest or festival in the city in any source.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no orc row (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30921-30996`). Its "All deities" row, Holyhands House in the North Ward (`:30923-30925`), is the only listing that could cover him, and no source says his faithful use it.
>   - The Dragon Heist city pamphlet tells visitors that they have nothing to fear from the City Guard "unless you lead a rampaging army of orcs, a horde of gnolls, or similar" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30441`). It is the only line found in either adventure that treats orcs as a danger to the city.

>   - No source puts an orc tribe, a shaman or an Eye of Gruumsh near Waterdeep. The wiki's one geographic note is a gallery caption for an Eye of Gruumsh orc from the Sword Mountains, and no source says how far that is from the city. [research]
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Tempest and War (SCAG, 2014; the PHB, 2014, agrees). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Tempest Domain, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is War Domain (derived). It is also his second domain by name, and it leaves out the storm magic, which Talos holds as the Storm Lord. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because his clergy lead armies and the 2014 Tempest Domain also trained clerics in heavy armor and martial weapons, which Protector grants. An orc or half-orc cleric raised in a tribe normally follows him, and a half-orc who worships him as a war god while avoiding evil keeps the faith private to avoid mistrust. A character who wants a war god without an evil faith can look at **Tempus**, whose wiki row also lists half-orcs (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e), or at **Hoar**, to whom half-orcs often turn (*Races of Faerûn*, 3e).
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Vengeance (derived), a loose fit. The oath is built around retribution, and his prayers all begin with the word for revenge. No source lists paladins among his followers, and his chaotic evil alignment suits none of the four 2024 oaths well.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The nearest links are the half-orcs in the files, none of whom has Gruumsh named as a god, and a few orcs in Undermountain.

Grum'shar, the half-orc wizard who holds Floon in the sewer hideout in **Finding Floon**, has no Notable Figures page. No page ties any of them to the orc gods.

The campaign's Notable Figure makes him human (see Draft Notes), so the book is a seed for the Kolat Towers room and not a half-orc detail.

## Overview

Gruumsh, the One-Eyed God, is the chaotic evil god of war and conquest and the ruler of the orc pantheon. Orcs and half-orcs follow him, and his symbol is an unblinking eye. Orcs hold that the other gods cheated them of a place in the world, and his priests drive each tribe to take one by force, and the orcs he chooses as his Eyes gouge out one of their own eyes in his honor. Waterdeep has no temple of his.

**Tone:** Grave and plain, with no relish for the violence. Gruumsh is a god of grievance and appetite, so describe what the orcs believe they were owed before the raiding it excuses, and keep him at a distance. The campaign writes no scene in which he acts.
