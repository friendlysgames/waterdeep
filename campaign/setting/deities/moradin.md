# Moradin

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Moradin, the All-Father and head of the dwarven pantheon, for players who want a dwarf cleric, a paladin or a devout smith, and for a GM who needs a power behind a forge, a stone hall or a dwarven oath.
> - **Where it is used:** Moradin never appears in person. His name is carried by rooms: the Hall of Moradin in the Vault of Dragons, and two sets of statues in Undermountain that the campaign treats as seeds.
> - **Featured in:** **Vault of Dragons** (the Hall of Moradin and the statuette of a dwarf priest of Moradin).
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier and never mentions the vault. Nothing about Moradin himself is secret, but the location of the Hall of Moradin is.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Moradin, and says plainly that no source gives him a temple, a shrine or a festival in Waterdeep, so that no later page invents one.

*The All-Father*

- **Title:** God of creation (SCAG, 2014). The PHB, 2014, prints "Dwarf god of creation", and the MTF entry has no title field.
- **Other Names:** The All-Father (wiki, citing *Demihuman Deities*, SCAG and the DMG); the Dwarffather, the Soul Forger, the Creator (wiki, citing *Demihuman Deities*)
- **Pantheon:** Dwarven, the Morndinsamman (SCAG, 2014), and its head. MTF spells the name "Mordinsamman".
- **Areas of Concern:** Creation, the dwarven crafts, smithing and stonework, the protection of dwarves (derived from the MTF text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Forge, Knowledge (SCAG, 2014; the PHB, 2014, and MTF agree)
- **Symbol:** Hammer and anvil (SCAG, 2014; the PHB, 2014, and MTF agree)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Erackinor, in Solania on Mount Celestia (wiki, 2e); Dwarfhome (wiki, 3e and 4e). SCAG, the PHB and MTF give no plane.
- **Typical Followers:** Dwarves, smiths, miners, engineers, fighters (wiki; no 5etools entry has a worshipers field)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Moradin heads the Morndinsamman, and MTF files him under it. The wiki calls him the chief deity of the dwarves and rates him a greater deity in its 1e, 2e and 3e rows. Its 4e and 5e rows give no rank.
- **Character:** He is gruff and stern and almost incapable of compromise, and he defends his people, their values and his principles of good and law without tiring (*Demihuman Deities*; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). A good heart is needed for his acceptance, but only quality results earn his approval (*Monster Mythology*). The wiki's opening describes a harsh but fair judge who weighed the goodness of a work before the intention behind it.
- **Craftiness:** Contrary to rumor he is very crafty, and he lets others think him dull so that they drop their guard (*On Hallowed Ground*).
- **How he appears:** As a dwarf twenty feet tall with a powerful upper body, a stern face, and a mane and beard that reach his knees. He wears a smith's leather leggings and apron with gold bracers, his clothes become plate armor and a large shield when he needs them, and a faint white radiance surrounds him that he can hide (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e).
- **What the faith asks:** He expects his children to follow in his footsteps, studying his techniques until they can one day match his expertise (MTF). He is the master of every craft the dwarves practice and the patron of artisans.
- **Priests:** A priest of Moradin judges the work of a stronghold's artisans. Priests keep great volumes describing crafting techniques and use them to rate individual pieces. They also evaluate young dwarves to decide their vocations, and the dwarves accept those decisions without question (MTF).
- **Clergy:** The wiki calls his clerics Sonnlinor. They are usually drawn from family lines, like most dwarven trades, and they wear earthy colors with chain mail and silvered helms (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **The church:** It guides the morals of dwarven communities and ties his hand to mining, smithing and engineering, and it invokes his blessing when such work begins. It led the push to found new dwarven kingdoms and to raise the dwarves' standing among surface peoples. Moradin charged his followers to wipe out the orc kingdoms and the followers of **Gruumsh** (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **Orders:** The Hammers of Moradin are an elite military order of crusaders and fighting clerics, with chapters in nearly every dwarven stronghold and members from every clan. They command dwarven armies and serve as a strike force against orc hordes, great wyrms and fiends, and they defend existing holdings while carving out new ones. Each chapter acts on its own, and in a great crisis a Grand Council assembles to plan strategy and divine his will (*Player's Guide to Faerûn*, 3e).
- **Holy days and worship:** He is worshiped at forges and hearths, and the wiki puts his holy day on the crescent moon without citing a book. Many dwarven communities celebrated Hammer 1, believing that day in 1306 DR to be a blessing from him (*Faiths & Pantheons*). The wiki's holy-day fields are blank, and no source gives a holy day or a festival in Waterdeep.
- **Messengers and omens:** No source names a messenger or an omen. [research] The wiki lists a faint white radiance as his manifestation, and among his associated creatures it names azers, galeb duhr, hammer golems, archons, and gold and silver dragons (*Races of Stone*; *Demihuman Deities*; *Deity Do's and Don'ts*).
- **When he acts:** He prefers manifestations to avatars, because he believes dwarves must earn their own place and salvation, and he seldom appears in the Realms. When he does, it is to help dwarves against an overwhelming force, and he may bless favored warriors with *strength* before the battle (*Monster Mythology*; *Demihuman Deities*). His other interventions guide his people through a crisis, inspire dwarves who will serve their kin later, or move outsiders to help the dwarves (*Demihuman Deities*).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He keeps a strategic but friendly alliance with **Gond**, **Kossuth**, **Helm**, **Torm**, **Tyr** and the heads of the elven, gnome and halfling pantheons, who on this roster are **Corellon Larethian**, **Garl Glittergold** and **Yondalla**. He opposes the gods of the goblinoids, the orcs, the evil giants and the banished dwarves (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - He loathes **Maglubiyet** more than any other god of the goblinkin (*Demihuman Deities*).
  - The *Demihuman Deities* lists Abbathor, Berronar Truesilver, Clangeddin Silverbeard, Dugmaren Brightmantle, Dumathoin, Gorm Gulthyn, Haela Brightaxe, Marthammor Duin, Sharindlar, Thard Harr and Vergadain as gods who serve him, and Laduguer as estranged (2e). The 4e account keeps Berronar Truesilver as a god and makes Clangeddin Silverbeard, Dugmaren Brightmantle, Marthammor Duin, Thard Harr and Vergadain his exarchs (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - No source ties him to **Grumbar**.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced. The wiki gives no date for his origin and records no death or abdication.
  - Dwarven myth holds that he was incarnated from rock, stone and metal, and that his soul is an ember of fire. He forged the first dwarves from metals and gems and breathed souls into them as he cooled them (*Faiths & Pantheons*). MTF tells the same creation.
  - He banished the evil gods of the derro and the duergar from the surface (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - His avatar appeared when the Ironstar clan fled orcs at the Stone Bridge, after Daurvos Frostbeard made a desperate appeal that was his last act. It helped them defeat the orcs and reach Ironford, and so the Fallen Kingdom was founded (*Dwarves Deep*, 2e).
- **In the campaign:** Moradin is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. MTF describes him in the present tense, and the wiki's past tense is edition history.

> #### Using Moradin as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the maker of the dwarves. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the All-Father, the dwarven god of creation, whose symbol is a hammer and anvil. They add that he forged the first dwarves from metal and gems and breathed souls into them as the metal cooled, that smiths, miners and stone-workers honor him, and that his priests rate a craftsman's work and decide what a young dwarf should become. Dwarves, they say, take his judgment without argument. His priests serve dwarven strongholds, and Waterdeep has no temple of his. Which of his names the city uses, and how much a Waterdhavian knows of him, is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Moradin has no temple, shrine, priest or festival in the city in any source.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no dwarven row (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30921-30996`). Its "All deities" row, Holyhands House in the North Ward (`:30923-30925`), is the only listing that could cover him, and no source says his faithful use it.
>   - The one place the campaign and the published adventure give him a room is the Vault of Dragons, under the Brandath family mausoleum in the City of the Dead (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:9778`; `campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:20`). The Hall of Moradin is a room in a sealed vault, not a place of worship.
>   - Undermountain lies below the city (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:21`) and holds two sets of statues of him, described under Campaign ties. Neither is in a ward.
>   - The wiki has no Waterdeep text for him. Its one temple image is a gallery caption for a temple of Moradin in Neverwinter (`research/deities/moradin/wiki.txt:180`).
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Forge and Knowledge (SCAG, 2014). Neither is in the 2024 Player's Handbook, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is War Domain (derived), through Forge. The fit is loose, because Knowledge has no counterpart among the four. War Domain's armor and weapon training suits the fighting clerics of the Hammers of Moradin and the chain mail and silvered helms the wiki gives his clergy. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), for the same reason. A cleric modeled on the priests who judge craftwork and keep the volumes of technique would take Thaumaturge instead (derived).
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived), a loose fit. The wiki lists fighters among his followers and calls the Hammers of Moradin crusaders and fighting clerics, which is the nearest any source comes to naming paladins. The oath's Duty tenet matches the loyalty to clan and hold that his church preaches. Oath of Glory (derived) is the second fit, for a character who treats the work as "a challenge to achieve greatness", a line the wiki quotes from the dwarf Balifra Eversharp in MTF p.67.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** The **Vault of Dragons** quest. The details are in the GM Notes below. No Notable Figure names Moradin. Istrid Horn, a shield dwarf moneylender, worships Vergadain (`campaign/setting/notable-figures/doom-raiders/03-istrid-horn.md:14`), whom the wiki lists among the gods who serve Moradin (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e). In the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, the Lost Level (Level 6) holds statues of Moradin and three other dwarven gods, and the Obstacle Course (Level 15) holds a 12-foot statue of him in area 26. Undermountain is future work, so treat both as seeds.
> - **Never reveal:** That the Hall of Moradin lies under the Brandath mausoleum, or that the mausoleum hides a vault of old dwarven work, before the party has attuned the Stone of Golorr. A player who asks about Moradin gets the Overview.

## Overview

Moradin is the lawful good god of creation and the All-Father of the dwarves, and he heads their pantheon, the Morndinsamman. Dwarven tales say he forged the first dwarves from metal and gems and gave them souls with his breath as the metal cooled. Smiths, miners and artisans honor him, and his symbol is a hammer and anvil. Waterdeep has no temple of his.

> #### GM Notes
> - **The vault in outline:** Scene 4 of **Vault of Dragons** (`campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:134-156`, an active structure doc; no quest journal exists yet). The vault's stone "changes to unmistakably dwarven work" fifteen feet down the sloping hallway, and a small cartouche with the runes "HN" marks the work of Harlsnod Darkshine, the vault's original architect, whose "secret offerings" to Dumathoin are scattered through it (`:140`). The party reaches the vault after attuning the Stone of Golorr (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:9778`).
> - **The Hall of Moradin (V4):** The campaign text reads: "Three hammer-shaped pillars run the length of this hall. A cracked mosaic depicting a dwarf smith crafting figures from black metal and diamond conceals a secret door — DC 17 Wisdom (Perception) to find, opening automatically for a dwarf or on a DC 17 Strength (Athletics) check for anyone else. A black pudding lurks in a break in the wall roughly halfway down the hall and attacks anyone who investigates the damage." (`campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:148`)
> - **What the published room adds:** The pillars are "carved to resemble warhammers, with their square heads pressed against the floor", and the hall is 20 feet high (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:14586-14597`). Three archways in the east wall lead to crumbling bridges over the entrance foyer, and the bridges end at adamantine doors to the rooms beyond. The secret door in the south end of the west wall opens at a dwarf's touch, including a creature transformed into a dwarf by *alter self* (`:14614`).
> - **The mosaic:** It shows a smith at a forge crafting dwarves from black metal and diamonds, which matches the creation myth the faith tells (derived). A dwarf PC who studies it recognizes the image of Moradin the maker. No source gives a check for this.
> - **The Secrets Room:** The next room holds "a small silver statuette of a dwarf priest of Moradin". In the campaign the urns are empty and the story of sealed secrets "is a legend with nothing behind it", so the statuette is the only thing to find (`campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:150`). The published room has treasure in five urns, among them a 9-inch silver statuette with amethyst eyes worth 250 gp (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:14625-14632`). The campaign file wins.
> - **Dumathoin's Secret (V7):** The room beyond is the vault's real path down. "A DC 14 Intelligence (Religion) check identifies Dumathoin as the dwarven god of secrets — automatic for any dwarf character." (`campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:156`; `sources/adventure-wdh.json:14674-14693`). The Melairkyn dwarves' notes in **Fireball!** call the vault "a Dumathoin ceremonial site" (`campaign/quests/act-ii/fireball/ev-07-kalainsalley-tower.md:61`), so Moradin is honored in the Hall as the maker while Dumathoin holds the vault.
> - **The Broken Circle of Gods:** The vault's frescoes show Dumathoin, Ilsensine and Laduguer in a circle of friendship that "official dwarven doctrine insists never existed", from before a falling-out "that the Morndinsamman has never fully explained" (`campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:154`). Moradin is not among the figures. The wiki's 2e account calls Laduguer estranged from him (*Demihuman Deities*), and the page does not connect the two.
> - **Statues on Level 6 of Undermountain (seed):** In the Lost Level, area 19 (Mountain Shrine) has four statues of Moradin (god of creation), Berronar Truesilver, Clangeddin Silverbeard and Marthammor Duin in niches at the corners (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:18631`). Area 29d (Gods and Demons) has four 9-foot statues of the same gods with a demon trapped in each, and a barlgura in the statue of Moradin (`:18866-18879`). The Melairkyn dwarves trapped them there.
> - **The statue on Level 15 of Undermountain (seed):** In the Obstacle Course, area 26 is "dominated by a 12-foot-tall stone statue of Moradin, the great dwarven god of the forge". It is veined with mithral and raises a stone hammer carved with glowing runes, and a steel plaque at its base reads in Dwarvish, "All that is made can be unmade." (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:43276`). The statue animates and swings at a creature teleported in from another teleport trap in the level, for 40 (6d10 + 7) force damage on a failed DC 17 Dexterity save (`:43316`).

**Tone:** Steady and plain, in the brisk reference register. Moradin is a maker who judges work by its results, so describe what dwarves build and how it is judged before the god, and keep him at a distance, since he seldom appears and the campaign writes no scene in which he does.
