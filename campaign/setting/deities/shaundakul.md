*The Rider of the Winds*

- **Title:** God of Travel (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Rider of the Winds, the Helping Hand (FRHoF)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Travel, exploration, caravans, portals, miners (derived from the FRHoF text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Neither FRHoF nor SCAG gives any. The wiki lists Air, Chaos, Portal, Protection, Trade and Travel (wiki, 3e).
- **Symbol:** A humanlike figure walking on the wind, chiseled on his stone shrines (FRHoF). The wiki gives an upright silver left hand, palm out, its wrist fading into rippling winds on a black or deep purple ground (2e), and a wind-walking bearded man in a traveler's cape and boots (3e).
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Neutral (wiki, 2e and 3e; FRHoF and SCAG give none)
- **Plane:** Ysgard (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Guides, explorers, rangers, travelers (FRHoF)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Shaundakul is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. FRHoF gives him no rank, and the wiki calls him a deity in its 5e row and a lesser deity in its 2e and 3e rows. Its 2e row adds that he was formerly a demigod and an intermediate deity.
- **What he governs:** He is the god of journeys and travelers, and both ordinary and magical travel fall under his sphere (FRHoF). The wiki's portfolio is travel (5e), then caravans, exploration, miners and portals (3e), and long-range traders and windghosts (2e).
- **Who follows:** Guides, explorers, rangers and travelers (FRHoF). The wiki's 3e row adds caravaneers, half-elves and portal-walkers.
- **How he is worshiped:** Travelers pray to him before they set out on a long trek, and spellcasters call on his name when they cast *Teleport* in the hope that he will carry them safely to their destination. Beyond those brief invocations, lay worship of him is rare in the Realms (FRHoF).
- **Decline of the faith:** He is an ancient god, but his worship dwindled after the fall of Myth Drannor, where his chief temple stood. A small and dedicated group of scattered priests keeps the faith alive (FRHoF). The wiki adds that most of his worshipers and all of his clergy died in the last battle of the Weeping War (*The Ruins of Myth Drannor: Campaign Guide to Myth Drannor*).
- **Clergy:** His priests dress for hard travel and each wears a gauntlet or glove on the left hand, with silk lace, gauze or another thin cloth trailing from the wrist (FRHoF).
- **Temple:** FRHoF says his only remaining temple is Shaundakul's Throne, in the ruins of Myth Drannor. The ruins are so dangerous that his priests seldom gather there, and instead they work to find and protect older shrines of his (FRHoF).
- **Portal-shrines:** His old stone shrines carry his chiseled symbol, and each one is linked to another shrine somewhere in Faerûn. A traveler who stands before a shrine and speaks the right command word opens a two-way portal of swirling air between the two places. Unless the command words are displayed at a shrine, his priests must search ancient dungeons for them or ask adventurers to do it (FRHoF).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Shaundakul. FRHoF says his Chosen fly through spells such as *Fly* and *Wind Walk*, help his lesser priests make the dangerous pilgrimage through Myth Drannor to the Throne, and in some cases vow to clear the ruins of monsters.
- **How he appears:** The wiki describes a handsome man of regal bearing, anywhere from six to eighteen feet tall, in plain leather armor and a dark cloak that whips about as if in a gale. His boots never quite touch the ground, so he walks in silence while the wind keens around him (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Manner:** He is stern and kind, speaks little and lets his actions speak for him. He dislikes secrecy, enjoys company, and likes to spread word of hidden places and trade jokes with a rugged sense of humor. The wiki says he sometimes grows lonely (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Messengers:** He speaks to his remaining followers as an elderly voice carried on a crisp breeze (FRHoF). The wiki lists glowing hands surrounded by winds, and walls of wind, as his manifestations (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*).
- **Omen:** No source gives a formal omen. [research]
- **Holy days:** No source gives one. The wiki's holy-day fields are blank. [research]
- **Divine realm:** The wiki places his realm of Shaunidaur on the first layer of Ysgard (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e), and in its 3e row puts his home plane at the Gates of the Moon.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - His portfolio is broad, and it overlaps with many gods who will not tolerate a rival on their ground, including friendly gods of air, travel, nature and night (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - Most rangers venerated **Mielikki**, and she took over his following among them when he fell from power. She was later friendly toward him (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - He lost his following among merchants to **Waukeen** during the same decline, then won much of it back while **Lliira** held her portfolio. Lliira remained a close ally after Waukeen returned, because she saw their domains as complementary (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - He took a good deal of **Tymora**'s ground. They stayed allies and once had a brief dalliance (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). He was also said to have had a romance with **Selûne**, another god with a broad portfolio that included navigation (*Polyhedron* 117).
  - Since the Time of Troubles he has been romantically linked with the elven goddess Aerdrie Faenya, an ally of long standing (*Demihuman Deities*). His other allies are Lurue, Nobanion, Shevarash, Solonor and **Akadi** (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - **Helm** is neither his ally nor his enemy, though Shaundakul's wide portfolio threatens his own. His dislike of secrets sets him against **Shar** (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - **Beshaba** is his enemy. He once spurned her, and in revenge she posed as him under the name the Treacherous Lurker in the Sands, making trouble for the Bedine nomads and spoiling his good name (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact. The wiki gives no account of how he became a god.
  - He was already a god before the Dawn Cataclysm (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). His main temple stood in Myth Drannor, and most of his worshipers and all of his clergy died in the last battle of the Weeping War (*The Ruins of Myth Drannor: Campaign Guide to Myth Drannor*).
  - By 1358 DR he had only six priests across Faerûn. He appeared in manifestations or in his avatar to those who worshiped him or badly needed help, in an effort to grow his flock (*The Ruins of Myth Drannor: Campaign Guide to Myth Drannor*).
  - In the decade after Waukeen vanished he gained many caravaneers, and he kept quite a few of them after she returned. His church grew steadily from then on (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - In 1385 DR he disappeared without a trace in the Spellplague. At least one temple outside Arabel kept receiving pilgrims for the next century (*Temple of the Sky God*).
  - After the Spellplague settled he returned to Toril at the head of a crowd of people and creatures who had been lost on other worlds. He has since traveled in mortal disguise and reveals his identity only to his most devout followers and priests (Ed Greenwood, *Sage Advice*, 2020, as cited by the wiki).
- **In the campaign:** Shaundakul is alive and present in 1492 DR. FRHoF describes him and his priests in the present tense, and the wiki's 1385 DR disappearance and his return are history. Draft Notes records the conflicts.

A well-read sage can describe his old stone shrines, which open a portal to a twin shrine somewhere else in Faerûn when a traveler speaks the right command word. The best fit among the available subclasses is Tempest Domain (derived; Player's Handbook 2014), a loose fit. He is the Rider of the Winds and a god of air, and the subclass is built on storm features (Wrath of the Storm, Thunderbolt Strike), though it leans on thunder more than wind. Trickery Domain (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the alternative, for his Chaos and Portal domains. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because his priests search ancient dungeons for command words and tend magical shrines. A cleric who guards pilgrims on the road would take Protector (derived). The faith has no temple in Waterdeep for a cleric to join.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Glory (derived; Player's Handbook 2024), a loose fit. The wiki says he speaks little and lets his actions speak for him, and the oath is about glory won through deeds. Oath of the Noble Genies (derived; Heroes of Faerûn) is the alternative for his air portfolio, since its spells include *fly* and *gaseous form*. No source lists paladins among his followers or ties an oath to him.

## Overview

Shaundakul, the Rider of the Winds, is the god of travel, and guides, explorers and rangers look to him. Travelers pray to him before a long trek, and spellcasters call on his name when they cast *Teleport*. His worship has faded since his chief temple was lost at Myth Drannor, and a few scattered priests keep it alive by tending old stone shrines that open portals to far places. Waterdeep has no temple of his.

**Tone:** Plain and a little wistful, in the brisk reference register. Shaundakul is a god whose best days are behind him, so describe the road and the shrines before the god, and keep him at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he speaks.
