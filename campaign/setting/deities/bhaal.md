> - **Where it is used:** Bhaal appears in no quest, faction page or Notable Figure. The published *Dragon Heist* names him once, in its history of the Time of Troubles.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Bhaal, and states what the campaign and the published adventure have tied to him so that no later page invents a cult or an assassin's guild in his name.

*The Lord of Murder*

- **Title:** God of Murder (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Lord of Murder (FRHoF); the Dread Lord, and Niynjushigampo among the Gugari (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Murder, assassination, violence and ritual killing, the fear a killer leaves behind (derived from the FRHoF text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Death (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A skull surrounded by a ring of bloody droplets (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Neutral Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Gehenna (Khalas) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Assassins, murderers (FRHoF)

> Kill in the dead of night, and let the fear do the rest.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Bhaal is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and one of the Dead Three, with Bane and Myrkul (FRHoF). The wiki ranks him a lesser deity in 1e and an intermediate deity in 2e and 3e. Its 5e row calls him a quasi-deity, effectively a mortal (*Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*), which conflicts with FRHoF (see Draft Notes).
- **What he wants:** He lends power to those whose slaughters inspire fear and dread. FRHoF says the killing is only half of it, and the other half is the suspicion and paranoia that an unstoppable killer leaves behind. He is feared most for killing people who seem beyond the reach of death.
- **Who prays to him:** Folk of Faerûn do not normally pray to him or acknowledge him, and they see him as a deeply evil and destructive god who hungers for unlawful death (SCAG, 2014). Some propitiate him when they want to commit a murder. A person who cannot redress an injustice by lawful means may have an understandable reason, but prayers to him come far more often from murderers moved by jealousy, greed or wrath. It is rare for anyone but assassins or compulsive killers to take him as a patron (FRHoF).
- **Cults and shrines:** Murder cults of Bhaal have arisen in the past, each led by a charismatic, self-styled priest, but organized worship is extremely uncommon, and temples and shrines are rare. Someone who raises a shrine usually does it to thank him for a successful murder. It shows a skull or a severed head ringed with drops of blood, both taken from the victim (FRHoF).
- **Baldur's Gate:** FRHoF says his cult has infiltrated Baldur's Gate on the Sword Coast, where a vast temple to the Lord of Murder is said to brood in the tunnels beneath the city. It is the only temple the sources name.
- **Clergy:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). His priests were called Bhaalists, or Bhaalyn in the lands east of the Dragon Reach. They formed a disorderly network of local hierarchies, with the urban and rural branches distant from each other. They held murder to be both a duty to their god and a game, and each cleric was expected to kill at least once every tenday in the darkest part of the night.
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Bhaal. FRHoF calls them blood-soaked terrors with little or no empathy, whose divine gifts hone their killing skills.
- **How he appears:** He always carries a red handkerchief somewhere on his manifested form. His alternate form is a hairless, hulking figure with a long curved dagger that drips blood, covered in bleeding cuts, with a flayed skull for a face and a halo of blood around it (FRHoF).
- **Messengers:** He usually sends a doppelganger in the guise of someone who, the recipient later learns, was dead during the conversation. Otherwise he sends a vicious mezzoloth (FRHoF).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** The Feast of the Moon (wiki, 2e; the other rows are blank). The campaign calendar places it between Uktar 30 and Nightal 1, after the campaign window. No file ties a city festival to him.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Most of the pantheon reviled him. His foes included Chauntea, Helm, Ilmater, Lathander, Lliira and Tyr.
  - Talona and Loviatar served him, and he served Bane.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book named on each fact.
  - He began as a power-hungry mortal adventurer who traveled with Bane and Myrkul Bey al-Kursi, and the three sought the portfolio of Jergal, the god of the dead. Jergal offered his realm, and they played a game of knucklebones to divide his power. Bane won first choice, Myrkul chose rule over the dead, and Bhaal took death (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Lost Empires of Faerûn*). The **Jergal** page gives the SCAG version of the same game.
  - In the Time of Troubles he joined Bane and Myrkul in the theft of the Tablets of Fate. On Eleint 16, 1358 DR, powerless and walking the Realms like the other gods, he was killed by the mortal Cyric, who wielded Mask's avatar in the form of the sword Godsbane. Cyric took his divinity and portfolio. His blood ran into the Winding Water around Boareskyr Bridge, and some of his divinity stayed there (*Lost Empires of Faerûn*; *Faiths & Avatars*).
  - Before he died he fathered many mortal children, the Bhaalspawn, to carry his essence (*Lost Empires of Faerûn*).
  - The city-dwelling Bhaalists soon turned to Cyric, whom they called Cyric-Bhaal. The rural priests kept the faith for years, claiming they still received their spells after their nightly prayers. The two sides fought each other until about 1367 DR, and the remaining Bhaalists lost their powers and drifted into the churches of Cyric or Iyachtu Xvim (*Faiths & Avatars*).
  - While he was dead, scattered cult groups, the best known being the Deathstalkers of Bhaal, tried to bring him back (*Faiths & Avatars*).
  - After the last Bhaalspawn died, his essence was freed. He was revived and took back the murder domain from Cyric (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e). The wiki adds that he returned as a quasi-deity, like Bane and Myrkul, and that in 1492 DR cultists of the Dead Three murdered people in Baldur's Gate (*Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Bhaal is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. FRHoF describes him in the present tense and says many of the gods Cyric slew have returned. His death and the Bhaalspawn are history on this page (see Draft Notes).

> #### Using Bhaal as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as a god nobody admits to praying to. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Lord of Murder, the god of assassins and killers, and one of the vile gods who, with Bane and Myrkul, stole the Tablets of Fate and brought the Time of Troubles down on the Realms. They add that decent folk never pray to him, that murder cults have risen now and then under self-styled priests, and that a skull ringed with blood drops is the sign of his killers. A well-read priest says his worshipers are said to keep a great temple under Baldur's Gate. Nobody in this conversation points to a cult in Waterdeep, because no source places one there.
> - **Waterdeep:** Bhaal has no temple, shrine or priest in the city in any source.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Bhaal row. It lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple for all deities, and no source says his faithful use it.
>   - The one published line is in the city's history: "the crisis began with the theft of the Tablets of Fate by the vile and ambitious gods Bane and Myrkul, later joined by Bhaal" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30482`). The text puts Waterdeep "at the center of events" in the Time of Troubles.
>   - Gods' Day, Marpenoth 15, marks the end of the Godswar. The Griffon Cavalry lights a bonfire on Mount Waterdeep for the gods "Myrkul, Cyric, Kelemvor, Mystra, Helm, and Ao", and the text does not name Bhaal (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31536`). It falls after the campaign window ends on Flamerule 11.
>   - Whether Waterdeep forbids his worship, and whether any murder in the city has been laid at his door, is unsourced. [research]
> - **A cleric PC:** Death Domain (derived; Dungeon Master's Guide, 2014). It is his own domain (SCAG, 2014), and he is the god of murder and of the fear a killer leaves behind. Trickery Domain (Player's Handbook, 2024) is the alternative for an assassin-priest who kills by stealth and disguise. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because Protector's heavy armor suits a night killer badly. This is an evil character, and a cleric of Bhaal in Waterdeep has no temple to turn to.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oathbreaker (derived; Dungeon Master's Guide, 2014). It is the oath of a paladin who breaks sacred vows to serve an evil power, which is the only way a paladin serves a god of unlawful death. FRHoF lists no paladins among his followers, so the fit is loose. Oath of Vengeance (Player's Handbook, 2024) is the alternative, through the FRHoF line about people who cannot redress an injustice by lawful means. A paladin who hunts murder cults fits Vengeance better.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power.

## Overview

Bhaal is the Lord of Murder, the god of assassins and killers, and one of the Dead Three with Bane and Myrkul. Waterdhavians know that he joined the theft of the Tablets of Fate that brought the gods down in the Time of Troubles. Few people pray to him, and those who do are murderers who raise a skull ringed with blood drops to thank him for a kill. Waterdeep has no temple of his, and the cult said to lurk beneath Baldur's Gate belongs to another city.

**Tone:** Cold and plain. Bhaal is a god of killing that no one admits to following, so describe the fear he leaves behind before the blade. Keep him at a distance, since the campaign writes no cult of his in Waterdeep.
