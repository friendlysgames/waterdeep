**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Eilistraee, and states what the sources place in Waterdeep and Undermountain so that no later page invents a temple, a priestess or a Bregan D'aerthe worshiper.

*The Dark Maiden*

- **Title:** God of Song and Moonlight (FRHoF). SCAG (2014) prints "Goddess of song and moonlight".
- **Other Names:** The Dark Maiden, the Lady of the Dance, Lady Silverhair (FRHoF). The wiki adds the Dark Dancer and the Dancing Goddess, the Masked Lady for the years she held her brother's portfolio (1375 DR to 1379 DR), and Darkfire of Love, a nickname from the Seven Sisters.
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian (FRHoF). SCAG and MTF (2014) file her under the Drow pantheon, the Dark Seldarine. The wiki says she belonged to the Seldarine before her exile.
- **Areas of Concern:** Song, dance, moonlight, freedom from tyranny, swordwork, the hunt, drow who leave the Underdark (derived from the FRHoF and MTF text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Light, Nature (SCAG, 2014). MTF (2014) adds Life.
- **Symbol:** A sword-wielding, dancing drow female silhouetted against the full moon (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Arborea, in Arvandor (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Bladesingers, elves, performing artists (FRHoF). The wiki's 5e row adds drow exiles and drow seeking an alternative to their society.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Eilistraee is a lesser deity (wiki; 2e, 3e and 5e rows) and the daughter of Lolth and Corellon Larethian (FRHoF). FRHoF calls her the drow god of dance, song, beauty, moonlight, swordplay and hunting. The wiki says she gained freedom as a portfolio after her return (SCAG and MTF, 5e).
- **Her message:** Where the worship of Lolth is strong, drow and other folk who oppose the Spider Queen turn to Eilistraee as a beacon of hope and freedom. She resents her mother's grip on cities such as Menzoberranzan, and she encourages her followers to pursue their own destinies, which may mean leaving the Underdark for the surface (FRHoF). Her message has begun to reach beyond drow society, and people in lands torn by conflict or oppression may take up her worship after hearing a story of a hopeful future (FRHoF).
- **What she asks and offers:** Her call reaches drow who feel like outsiders, who recoil from what their kin do, or who fall in love with the stars on a trip to the surface. She promises no safety and no welcome in the world above, but she opens her followers' hearts to the wonder of the night, and her songs and signs show a drow how to endure there (MTF, 2014). She speaks to her worshipers through dance-like dreams awash in gentle moonlight (FRHoF).
- **How the call comes:** Her call often arrives in music that has no other source, such as a parent's lullaby or a rousing chorus carried on the mind (MTF, 2014). The wiki adds that she sends dreams and visions to every drow, from the highest matron mother to the lowest slave (MTF, 5e, as the wiki cites it).
- **The drow view:** Most drow know nothing of her, and the matron mothers of the most powerful houses guard the scrolls that record her, so that they can recognize the enemy, and they tell raiders on the surface to retreat if they see the moon or hear music they find appealing (MTF, 2014). The fate of her followers' souls is uncertain, and drow she loves sometimes seem to vanish when they die, as the body dissolves into pale light (MTF, 2014). The wiki says Lolth ordered the persecution of her worshipers and the suppression of every record of her (MTF, 5e, as the wiki cites it).
- **Worship:** Her followers thank her by dancing beneath every full moon (FRHoF). The wiki says worship is song and dance under the moon on the surface, preferably in wooded places, and usually ends with a feast (*The Drow of the Underdark* and *Demihuman Deities*, 2e). Each day the faithful also hold a ritual called the Evensong, in which she listens to their wordless messages (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e).
- **Clergy:** Her priests are passionate advocates of the arts, especially song and dance. They practice sword dancing, a variant of bladesinging that blends it with the bardic arts of the College of Dance, and her temples are among the few places that teach bladesinging, so travelers come from afar to learn it (FRHoF). The wiki calls her clerics Sword Dancers and says they act as teachers, protectresses and diplomats under a loose hierarchy (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). It adds that her communities are often matriarchal, in a nurturing way meant to empower males and females alike (Ed Greenwood, as the wiki cites him).
- **Temples:** Her Underdark temples are refuges from the region's perils, hidden by powerful magic and opened only by a sword-shaped key (FRHoF). The wiki names one temple, the Promenade of the Dark Maiden, where she led drow refugees from Darkmaiden's Leap in the High Forest to safety during the Time of Troubles in 1358 DR (*Champions of Valor*, 3e).
- **Orders:** Two adventuring orders follow her. The Silverhair Knights demonstrate the strength of mercy and compassion, fight only when they must and use only nonlethal techniques. The Darksong Knights are a smaller order that hunts and destroys yochlols and Lolth's other demonic agents (FRHoF).
- **Chosen:** Her Chosen are diplomats who travel the surface and the Underdark and risk their lives to broker understanding in unlikely places (FRHoF), and the campaign has none.
- **Messengers and signs:** The wiki lists many servants, among them aasimar, lythari, pixies, silver dragons and silver dogs. Silver moths, silver-striped tabby cats and animals with solid silver eyes can carry her messages (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e). Her most common sign is a silvery radiance, sometimes with a snatch of song or a few notes of a harp (*The Drow of the Underdark* and *Demihuman Deities*, 2e).
- **Holy days:** None. The wiki's holy-day rows are blank for every edition. Dancing beneath each full moon is an observance and gives no date. [research]
- **Where she is worshiped:** The wiki places her worshipers in Cormanthor among the drow, in the Dalelands among half-elves (*Player's Guide to Faerûn*, 3e), and in Waterdeep and Skullport (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e). It also lists followers in and around Silverymoon (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e) and, since the Second Sundering, among the Aevendrow of Callidae (*Glacier's Edge*).
- **How others see her faithful:** They are little known and poorly understood. Some surface dwellers believe they are the vanguard of the Spider Queen's plan to take the surface, and drow who follow Lolth take them for surface elf spies in disguise. Surface elves are uneasy about a drow goddess at all, because they hold that dark elves are wholly to blame for the Crown Wars (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Demihuman Deities*, 2e).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (2e and 3e sources unless noted).
  - Her allies are the elven gods of the Seldarine, **Mystra**, **Selûne**, **Mielikki** and the other good gods of the Underdark races, among them Lurue, Haela Brightaxe and Callarduran Smoothhands (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Demihuman Deities*, 2e). Her ties to the Seldarine are uneasy, because many elves distrust their dark kin, and only Erevan Ilesere is close to her (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e).
  - She is close to Mystra through Qilué Veladorn, the seventh of the Seven Sisters, who served both goddesses as a Chosen (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e). **The Harpers** also have her goodwill, and they support her church on the surface (*The Drow of the Underdark*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Her chief enemy is **Lolth**, whose evil and corruption infuriate her. She also opposes Kiaransalee, **Ghaunadaur**, Selvetarm, the other evil gods of the Underdark, and **Malar**, the evil god of the hunt on the surface. Vhaeraun opposed her until 1489 DR (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Demihuman Deities*, 2e).
  - Followers of Eilistraee call **Tempus** the Butcher (*Silverfall*, as the Tempus page records it).
  - Ghaunadaur holds a grudge against her because she defeated its avatar and trapped it near the Promenade of the Dark Maiden, which the wiki says protected the city of Waterdeep (*The Seven Sisters*, no edition tagged).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - She was born to Araushnee, who later took the name Lolth, and to Corellon Larethian, and she is the sister of Vhaeraun (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Demihuman Deities*, 2e). When evil gods attacked Arvandor, Araushnee's treachery almost tricked her into killing her own father. Corellon banished the dark elven gods, and although she was cleared of guilt she insisted on sharing the exile, because she foresaw that the drow would need her. She swore never to use a ranged weapon again, and the sword became her only weapon (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - The Seldarine cursed and exiled the dark elves around -10000 DR, and Lolth and Ghaunadaur gained most of them. Her faith stayed marginal for millennia (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e; *Demihuman Deities*, 2e).
  - In the 1370s DR her realm moved to Arvandor (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). In 1375 DR Vhaeraun tried to kill her and failed, and she took his portfolio and the title of the Masked Lady for a time (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e).
  - She was defeated and disappeared in 1379 DR, in her long conflict with Lolth over the drow (*Ascendancy of the Last*; the wiki's 4e row calls her a dead power). She returned in the Second Sundering, in Flamerule 1489 DR, three years before the campaign (derived), and the wiki says she has been seen dancing in many places along the Sword Coast (SCAG and MTF, 5e; Ed Greenwood, as the wiki cites him).
  - After her return she and Vhaeraun reached an understanding, though some of their followers still fight each other (Ed Greenwood, as the wiki cites him).
- **In the campaign:** Eilistraee is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. FRHoF describes her in the present tense, and the published Undermountain adventure gives her a temple. Her death and disappearance in 1379 DR are history, and Draft Notes records the difference.

## Overview

Eilistraee, the Dark Maiden, is the chaotic good goddess of song, dance and moonlight. Her symbol is a drow woman dancing with a sword before the full moon. She is the daughter of Lolth and Corellon Larethian, and she calls drow to leave their cities and live under the open sky. Her followers include drow exiles, elves, bladesingers and performers who dance beneath the full moon, and Waterdeep has no temple of hers.

**Tone:** Warm and plain. Eilistraee is a goddess who offers a way out and promises no safety, so describe the music and the moonlight first and the danger of the road second. Keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she acts.
