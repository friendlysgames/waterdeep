# Beshaba

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Beshaba, the goddess of misfortune, and a reference for characters who ward off bad luck or serve her.
> - **Where it is used:** Beshaba never appears in person. No campaign file names her and she has no temple in Waterdeep. The only place the city honors her is the Reign of Misrule, a festival in the published *Dragon Heist* calendar that falls on Marpenoth 10, after the campaign's main window.
> - **Featured in:** no quest. The **Tymora** and **Lathander** pages in this journal mention her as Tyche's second half.
> - **Spoiler limit:** nothing about Beshaba is secret. Everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Beshaba, gives a player what the faith asks, and records the one festival the published adventure ties to her so that no later page invents a temple or a cult.

*Lady Doom*

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Beshaba is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and the counterpoint to her sister Tymora, as often acknowledged in daily life (FRHoF). The wiki calls her a chaotic evil intermediate deity (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e) and a member of Tempus's pantheon during the Spellplague (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **Her nature:** She is cruel and capricious and must be placated to avoid her ruinous attention. She is petty and spiteful, and her whims puzzle even her followers (FRHoF). The wiki says her chief motive is jealousy of her sister's warmer reception, so she demands equal lip service and curses those who withhold it (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **What the faith asks:** People worship her out of fear and not out of faith. Her doctrine holds that bad luck falls on everybody and that worship is the only way to avoid it (wiki, *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **When she is invoked:** Mortals call on her name when bad luck strikes, whether the trouble is a stubbed toe or a fall from a cliff. They also ask her to hold off her attentions when sudden bad luck could mean disaster, such as when crossing a rickety bridge (FRHoF). Someone rolling dice calls on Tymora, and someone crossing a bridge asks Beshaba to keep it standing (SCAG, 2014).
- **Welcoming her at ceremonies:** Most people tremble at the thought of her presence at any event. Even so, the opening speeches at marriages, coronations, contests and naming ceremonies welcome and propitiate her, because a goddess left uninvited might take offense and wreak misfortune on everyone there (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **The warding sign:** Folk fold in their thumbs and extend their fingers on one or both hands, like the horns of her symbol, to ward off misfortune. The same gesture raised to the head is a salute, and pointed at a person it wishes them ill favor (SCAG, 2014).
- **Who follows:** Gamblers, Red Wizards and rogues (FRHoF). The wiki's 5e row lists druids and the Red Wizards of Thay. SCAG says many druids worship her as one of the First Circle, with dances in fire-blackened antlers dipped in blood. Their stag-horn symbol dates from the days when hunter-gatherers believed she brought misfortune to hunters, such as being gored by a stag (SCAG, 2014).
- **Clergy:** Few dare to take her as a patron. Her rare clerics have been deeply affected by great misfortunes, and they either warn others of life's unfairness or inflict it (SCAG, 2014). The wiki says her priests play on morbid fears by acting mysteriously, and that they ask for their spells with sacrifices of burning brandy or wine (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Orders:** Wiki-sourced. The Doommasters are the best known order of specialty priests, feared for the pleasure they take in inflicting misfortune. The Black Fingers are a secret order of evil male clerics, thieves and fighters who serve as her assassins (both *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). The Wormlucks, or Priests of Bad Fortune, wear bright red robes over armor and obvious white wigs to honor her hair. They were created to spread her influence and to counter the church of Tymora, and calamities follow them wherever they go (*Warriors and Priests of the Realms*).
- **Temples and shrines:** Temples to her are virtually unknown (FRHoF). Rural folk set up a post with antlers at the site of a roadside accident or murder, and in cities the pious draw black antlers with charcoal on a nearby wall and leave them until the weather scours them away. These marks warn others of places of ill fortune (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Formal shrines:** They stand where people often hope to ward off misfortune. A shrine is a red post or stone with blackened antlers fixed to it, or a red triangular plaque with antlers, with a stone or bronze bowl for tossed coins and burnt offerings. The Red Wizards of Thay put such shrines outside their ritual chambers to guard against unfortunate mistakes (SCAG, 2014).
- **Holy days:** The wiki gives two, Midsummer and Shieldmeet, both celebrated with revelry and with food and drink (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). The published *Dragon Heist* places her festival at the Reign of Misrule on Marpenoth 10, a date the wiki does not list. The campaign calendar names Midsummer, Shieldmeet and the Reign of Misrule and ties none of them to her.
- **Messengers and omens:** When she speaks to a mortal directly, her voice whispers from broken mirrors. Otherwise she sends a quasit disguised as a black cat, or a great stag with black antlers dripping blood (FRHoF). The wiki adds a twelve-foot head with white hair and a maniacal expression, and a grotesque shadow with faint maniacal laughter. Cockroaches and rats are her animals, and her colors are black, mauve and purple (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*).
- **Chosen:** Her Chosen are conduits of misfortune. They move through the world steering people and events toward disaster, plague and the escape of demons (FRHoF).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Beshaba.
- **Servants:** Wiki-sourced. Alu-fiends in silver breastplates marked with stag horns serve her (*Tymora's Luck*, novel). Giant black stags with red eyes live in her realm and can gore an elephant (*Tymora's Luck*, novel).
- **How she appears:** Wiki-sourced. She is a tall, slim, beautiful maiden with a wild mane of white hair and corpse-pale skin flushed blue and violet at the cheeks and throat. Her yellow-rimmed eyes blaze with madness, and she wears a tight black gown and a tiara of black pearls (*Tymora's Luck*, novel).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced.
  - She is Tymora's twin and sworn enemy (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - She was Tempus's lover (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e) and turned down Talos, among other suitors (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Many male gods find her attractive, and some were devoured by her lust (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Tymora shares her gifts freely with worshipers and allies, and Beshaba hoards her divine power in a reserve (*Tymora's Luck*, novel).
- **Origin and rise:** FRHoF and the wiki agree on the outline and differ on the cause.
  - The Netherese revered a single god of luck, Tyche, who ruled good and bad fortune together. According to myth Tyche became corrupted and died, and her essence appeared as two sisters, Beshaba and Tymora, who share her portfolio (FRHoF).
  - The wiki puts the split in the Dawn Cataclysm, a disaster tied to Lathander's schemes. Tyche was infected by the taint of the dark god Moander, and her friend Selûne forced her to divide. The two halves sprang out fully formed and began to fight at once (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - In Selûne's telling, Beshaba took everything Moander had tainted and came out as the goddess of ill fortune, lovely to see and spiteful at heart. Other gods pulled the sisters apart, and Beshaba fled to the dark planes (*Tymora's Luck*, novel).
- **In the campaign:** Beshaba is present in 1492 DR, but no file shows her acting.

> **[GM]**

> #### Using Beshaba as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as the bad luck everyone talks around. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is Lady Doom, the sister of Tymora and the goddess of misfortune, and that you invoke her when a wagon wheel breaks and ask her to spare you when you cross a rickety bridge. They add that you welcome her at weddings so she does not take offense, that you never pray to both sisters, and that you fold your thumbs in and hold up your fingers like horns to turn her eye aside. A regular may swear "Beshaba's bad breath!" when things go wrong (wiki, *Pool of Radiance: Ruins of Myth Drannor*, a video game). Most people fear her and few admit to serving her.
> - **Waterdeep:** Beshaba has no temple or shrine in the city. The only thing the sources place there is a festival.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Beshaba row. It lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as the temple of "All deities", which includes her by its own wording (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30924`). No campaign file mentions a shrine, a priest or a worshiper of hers.
>   - The Reign of Misrule falls on Marpenoth 10 (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31522`). The text says "This day honors Beshaba, goddess of misfortune," and "People of the city are expected to break trust, belie oaths, and disobey the normal order-as long as no laws are actually broken and no rift is made that can't be later bridged" (`:31526`).
>   - The campaign calendar lists the Reign among the days after the Flamerule 11 window, which matter only if play runs past it (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:111`). It names the day and not the goddess.
>   - Charcoal antlers on a wall are a general custom of cities (FRHoF). No file places one in Waterdeep, and the wiki has no Waterdeep text for her.

## Overview

Beshaba is the chaotic evil goddess of misfortune, whom Waterdhavians know as Lady Doom and as the sister and rival of Tymora. People call on her name when a wheel breaks or a bridge sways, and in cities they draw her black antlers on walls to mark unlucky places. Waterdeep has no temple of hers, but the Reign of Misrule on Marpenoth 10 honors her with a day of pranks and broken trust. Most folk fear her and few worship her, though it is thought wise to welcome her at weddings and other formal gatherings so that she takes no offense.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **The Reign of Misrule:** The published text lists what the city does. Nobles serve meals to their servants, children take control of schools, priests give worship to their god's foes, and anyone may take part in a guild's trade. Pranks are played by and on many people. Sundown ends the festival, and most folk spend the night cleaning and reordering things for the next day.
> - **Those who refuse:** Many visitors decline to join in, but refusing often brings misfortune and does not avoid it. For fear of catching the cynics' bad luck, citizens avoid talking to or dealing with anyone known to have sat it out, until Gods' Day on Marpenoth 15 (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31526`).
> - **Use at the table:** The festival is outside the campaign's main window, which ends on Flamerule 11. Use it only if play runs into Marpenoth. No file gives it an event, an NPC or a consequence.

**Tone:** Dry and wary. Beshaba is a goddess people treat the way they treat bad weather, so describe the small habits they use to turn her aside before what she does, and keep her at a distance. The campaign writes no scene in which she appears.
