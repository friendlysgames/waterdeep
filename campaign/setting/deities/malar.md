# Malar

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Malar, the Beastlord and god of the hunt, for players who want a hunter, a barbarian-minded cleric or a devout character with a dark patron, and for a GM who needs to explain Howldown.
> - **Where it is used:** Malar never appears in person, and no campaign file names him. The page states what the published *Dragon Heist* text places in the city and what *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* places beneath it, so that no later page invents more.
> - **Featured in:** No quest. His name appears only on other Deities pages, as a power that **Chauntea**, **Deneir**, **Helm** and **Ilmater** oppose.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Malar is secret.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Malar, and says plainly that the city has no temple to him and honors him on one winter day only.

*The Beastlord*

- **Title:** God of the Hunt (FRHoF; SCAG and PHB, 2014, print "God of the hunt")
- **Other Names:** The Bloodgod, the Wild Hunter (FRHoF); the Black-Blooded One (SCAG, 2014). The wiki adds the Great Beast, the Beast of Beasts and Master of the Hunt.
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian, and one of the Gods of Fury (wiki)
- **Areas of Concern:** Hunting, predators, bloodlust, savage nature, lycanthropes (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Nature (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Clawed paw (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Carceri (Colothys) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Barbarians, hunters, lycanthropes (FRHoF)

> Hunt without mercy, and let the strongest survive.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Malar is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki lists him as a lesser deity in its 2e and 3e rows, as an exarch in its 4e row and as a deity in its 5e rows. The 4e entry is explained in the history below.
- **The Gods of Fury:** He belongs to a band of destructive powers led by Talos, which the wiki fills out with Auril and Umberlee (*On Hallowed Ground*). Its 5e row lists him as serving Talos (*Heroes of Faerûn*). Talos works with him only grudgingly (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e), and the wiki's Auril page says she and Malar hate each other.
- **What he is:** Malar is the thrill a predator feels while chasing its next meal, and the bloody, sadistic side of nature. He is the god of those who delight in the hunt, do not shy from bloodshed and savor the fear of their prey. Paintings and murals show him as a feline monster (FRHoF).
- **What the faith offers:** It offers no mercy. People believe he cannot be propitiated, so he receives prayers only from those engaged in a hunt. They ask for the help of his hunting skill, or they take on his fearsome mantle to ward off other predators (SCAG, 2014).
- **How the faithful pray:** Berserkers scream prayers to him as they rage, and hunters whisper one before closing in for the kill. Lycanthropes see themselves in his uncontrollable savagery. Followers wear or carry trinkets from the fearsome creatures they have slain, such as teeth, pelts, wings and claws (FRHoF).
- **Who follows:** Many lycanthropes call him their divine father, as do some other intelligent predators. He has devotees among savage druids and rangers, and many barbarians take him as a patron for his ferocity and cruelty (SCAG, 2014). The wiki's 5e row lists hunters, lycanthropes, barbarians, druids and rangers. It also records the People of the Black Blood, lycanthrope tribes that venerated him, at 3,000 or more in 1372 DR, in groups of fifty to a hundred scattered across the forests of Faerûn (*Lords of Darkness*, 3e).
- **Priests:** They believe in the survival of the fittest, and none may enter the priesthood without first showing prowess in hunting and combat (FRHoF). They use claw bracers, gauntlets with stylized claws jutting from the fists, as ceremonial weapons (SCAG, 2014).
- **Organization:** The wiki says the church has no formal hierarchy and revolves around the hunt. The most dominant figure in any group is the huntmaster, who sets the timing, prey and place of ceremonial hunts. A challenge to the huntmaster can come at any time, and most are settled by combat to the death (*Forgotten Realms Adventures*, 2e).
- **The Hunts:** His followers sometimes form bands called Hunts. A Hunt is forbidden to slay the young or the pregnant, and otherwise it follows his dogma that a bloody, drawn-out kill honors him (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Malarites oppose the druidic idea of Balance, and their priests hunt druids. Druid circles and the Harpers have worked to find and dismantle their strongholds (*Forgotten Realms Adventures*, 2e).
- **Temples:** He has no temples in Faerûn's major cities, though a village on the edge of the wilderness might house one (FRHoF). The wiki adds that civilization generally frowns on his followers (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Holy days:** In the tenday before Highharvestide his priests hunt tirelessly for game, and on Highharvestide they invite the community to a great feast called the Feast of the Stags (FRHoF). The wiki says everyone may attend, and that the priests promise some widows, orphans and elderly or infirm citizens meat for the winter (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Waterdeep marks a different day, Howldown, which the Using box describes.
- **The High Hunt:** His priests keep this ritual secret. They choose someone to become their prey for a day and a night, and if the prey survives they gain a favor from the priests. Death is the far likelier outcome (FRHoF). The wiki describes the prey as a captured humanoid who wins freedom by surviving (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Messengers:** When he needs to make his will known on the Material Plane, he sends a ferocious predator drenched in blood, such as a displacer beast, an owlbear or a peryton. His intentions rumble and snarl in the minds of those who delight in savage violence (FRHoF).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Malar. FRHoF says his Chosen are gifted trackers, sometimes blessed with lycanthropy, who can smell their quarry from miles away.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced; the wiki's relations paragraph cites no edition.
  - He has a long-standing alliance with Lolth against the Seldarine, and he is allied with dark powers such as Bane and Loviatar.
  - He fights the powers of peace, civilization and nature, and he hates Nobanion most, who beat him in a battle during the Time of Troubles.
  - The Chauntea, Deneir, Helm and Ilmater pages each list him among their opponents.
  - In the late fifteenth century DR, Umberlee turned to Talos and Malar against Auril, and with their help drove the Frostmaiden back to the Sea of Moving Ice (*Icewind Dale: Rime of the Frostmaiden*, 5e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He is a minor god who is not unique to Toril, a divine scavenger who forages for worshipers among perhaps a hundred predatory races on as many worlds (*Evermeet: Island of Elves*, novel).
  - After the Spellplague he was much reduced, and it was said his own beasts had hunted him down and slain him. He could then reach his followers only in dreams and nightmares (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). The 4e sources make him an exarch serving Silvanus in the Deep Wilds.
  - In the late 1470s DR his angelic servant Argon Bael revived him by returning his spirit and mind to the body of a cat-shifter named Kip, with the approval of Chauntea. After a day of slaughter on the isle of Moray, Malar shed the last of Kip's memories and took his true form again (*The Rose of Sarifal*, novel).
  - After the Second Sundering he is once again a full deity and one of the gods of the First Circle (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e, as the wiki cites it).
- **In the campaign:** Malar is alive and present in 1492 DR. No file treats him as dead or absent. The Spellplague death and the 4e exarch role are history, and Draft Notes records them.

> #### Using Malar as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as a god to be left alone. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Beastlord, the god of the hunt and of the cruel side of nature, shown as a great clawed cat. They say nobody prays to him for mercy because he gives none, that hunters and barbarians and the odd werecreature worship him, and that his cult is not welcome inside cities. They know Howldown as the day the City Guard goes out in his honor. The High Hunt stays out of the ordinary view, because FRHoF says his priests keep it secret. How the city feels about the god himself, beyond Howldown, is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Malar has no temple or shrine in the city. The one thing Waterdeep does in his name is Howldown.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Malar row (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30922-30996`), and FRHoF says he has no temples in Faerûn's major cities. The table's "All deities" row, Holyhands House in the North Ward (`:30923-30925`), is the only listing that could cover him, and no source says a Malarite is welcome there.
>   - Howldown falls on Nightal 11 (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31573-31578`). "In honor of Malar," members of the City Guard leave the city in groups to hunt the known threats to farmers and travelers, among them brigands, wolves, owlbears, ogres and trolls. The hunts last no longer than a tenday. The City Watch runs its own hunt for malefactors inside the walls during the same days, and the text warns anyone unsure of their standing with the law to stay away for a tenday afterward. The city's children play at the killing of predators, with mock hunts of adults dressed up as monsters.
>   - Nightal is a winter month, so Howldown falls long after the Flamerule 11 deadline in **Vault of Dragons** (derived). The campaign calendar lists no Howldown (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:33`, `:51`), and no campaign file places a priest, shrine or worshiper of Malar in the city.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Nature (SCAG, 2014; the 2014 Player's Handbook repeats it). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Nature Domain, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is War Domain (derived). The fit is loose, since War covers fighting and his faith centers on the stalking and the kill. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because his priests must show prowess in hunting and combat before they are admitted. A cleric who worships him openly in Waterdeep finds no temple and a city that frowns on his cult (wiki, 2e and 3e).
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Vengeance (derived). Its Vow of Enmity singles out one quarry, as a hunt does. The fit is loose, since the sources list no paladins among his followers and describe no oath tied to him.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The published adventures supply two things, both seeds for later work.
>   - Howldown, described above, is the only Waterdeep custom in the sources that names him.
>   - In *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, Level 5 is Wyllowwood. A large complex of ornate stone buildings south of the archdruid Wyllow's tower once belonged to a cult of Malar. Wyllow let the cultists hunt in her forest, they abused the privilege, and she and her green dragon ally destroyed them (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:13252`). The skeletons of sixty of them lie in alcoves in the cavern wall (`:15514`). In Malar's Tabernacle, area 20, a bloodstained altar summons a hostile chimera when fresh blood is spilled on it, and whoever drops the chimera gains inspiration as a minor blessing from Malar (`:16019-16041`).
>   - The Emerald Enclave rank event **Master of the Wild** already lists Wyllow and Wyllowwood among its Mad Mage seeds and says the Enclave has no contact with her (`campaign/quests/faction-events/emerald-enclave/r50-master-of-the-wild/ev-01-master-of-the-wild.md:377`). It does not mention the cult. Undermountain is future work, so treat all of this as seeds.

## Overview

Malar, the Beastlord, is the god of the hunt and of the cruel side of nature, and his worshipers are hunters, barbarians and lycanthropes. People believe no prayer or offering will turn him from a kill, so few ask him for anything except help on a hunt. The cities of Faerûn do not host his temples, and Waterdeep has none. Each year on Howldown, Nightal 11, the City Guard marches out in his honor to clear the roads of brigands and monsters.

**Tone:** Grave and plain, with no gore. Malar is the red side of nature, so describe the stalking, the patience before the kill and the trophies his followers carry. Keep him at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he acts.
