# Tharizdun

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**

> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the patron entry for Tharizdun, the imprisoned Chained God and a Great Old One patron for warlock characters. He is not a god of the Faerûnian pantheon, so this page replaces the cleric and paladin guidance of a deity page with warlock guidance and one line on cleric use.
> - **Where it is used:** Tharizdun never appears in the campaign. He is a choice for a player building a warlock, and no other Deities page names him.
> - **Featured in:** no quest. Neither adventure source names him. The nearest material is the *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* shrines of Ghaunadaur, whose title the Elder Eye resembles his own title, the Elder Elemental Eye.
> - **Spoiler limit:** this page holds no hidden fact that changes play, so it has no Secret box. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier and never mentions the Cassalanters. Everything above it may be read by the GM only.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what little an ordinary Waterdhavian knows about Tharizdun, gives a player what a Great Old One patron relationship with him can mean, says plainly that every source has him imprisoned, and states that the campaign has tied nothing to him so that no later page invents more.

*The Chained God*

- **Title:** The Chained God (wiki; *Dungeon Master's Guide* 4e, *Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e, and the revised 5e *Player's Handbook* and *Dungeon Master's Guide*). The 2014 *Player's Handbook* calls him the God of eternal darkness (5etools, PHB p.295).
- **Other Names:** The Elder Elemental Eye (wiki; *Dungeon Master's Guide* 4e and *Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e). The Eater of Worlds (5etools, XDMG p.148; wiki, revised 5e *Dungeon Master's Guide*).
- **Pantheon:** None. He is an imprisoned elder power and a patron, not a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. 5etools files him under the Greyhawk pantheon (PHB p.295; XDMG p.148), and the wiki files him under the Oerthly pantheon, which is the same setting's gods.
- **Areas of Concern:** Darkness, destruction, madness, the end of the world (derived from the wiki's portfolio lines: eternal darkness in 5e; darkness, elemental power, entropy, insanity, ruin and vengeance in 3e; annihilation and madness in 4e)
- **Domains:** Trickery (5etools, PHB p.295, 2014). The wiki's 3e row lists eleven more domains, and they are not a 2024 list.
- **Symbol:** A dark spiral or an inverted ziggurat (5etools, PHB p.295, 2014; wiki 5e). The revised 5e *Dungeon Master's Guide* gives a spiral rune (5etools, XDMG p.148).
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Evil (5etools, PHB p.295, 2014; wiki 5e and 4e). The wiki's 3e and 2e rows say Neutral Evil, and a *Dragon* 359 passage (edition not given) says his evil goes beyond law and chaos.
- **Plane:** Imprisoned in a demiplane (5etools, XDMG p.148; wiki, revised 5e). The wiki's 3e row says he has no home plane and is sealed in a prison plane, and its 4e row gives the Abyss as his dominion.
- **Typical Followers:** Nihilistic cultists (5etools, XDMG p.148; wiki, revised 5e). The wiki adds fanatic nihilists and evil elemental beings (*Complete Divine* 3e) and says only mad people worshiped him (*Dungeon Master's Guide* 4e).

> Free the Chained God, and let the world come to its end. (derived)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Tharizdun is an elder evil and a prisoner, and the sources show him only through his cults and their rites. The wiki ranks him a lesser deity and elder evil in revised 5e, a deity in 5e, and an intermediate deity in 3e and 2e. It lists no holy days in any edition.
- **Imprisoned in every source:** Each account that places him has him locked away. The 4e books say the other gods imprisoned him after he planted the seed that grew into the Abyss (*Monster Manual* 4e; *The Elder Elemental Eye*). The revised 5e *Dungeon Master's Guide* puts him in a demiplane (XDMG p.148), and the 3e row calls it a prison plane. No source frees him by 1492 DR, so the campaign has him present as a prisoner.
- **His voice:** After ages of imprisonment and growing madness he cannot communicate in any meaningful way. When he appears to his followers he speaks only in a shrieking babble that mortals cannot understand, so his true commands are unknown (*Dungeon Master's Guide* 4e, via the wiki).
- **What the cults teach:** They hold that his power must be channeled to help him break his bonds, that his lost relics and shrines must be found, and that the world must be brought to its end in anticipation of his return (*Dungeon Master's Guide* 4e, via the wiki). An inscription in one of his black pyramids names four aims: put out light, decay perfection, dissolve order and fragment minds (*Dragon* 294, 3e).
- **The Cult of Elemental Evil:** A rite quoted from the revised 5e *Monster Manual* has a cultist call Tharizdun the power of the Elder Elemental Eye and master of all destructive forces, and name himself Champion of Elemental Evil (via the wiki).
- **What he offers:** No source describes a gift, spell or reward that he gives his followers. [research]
- **Clergy, holy days, omens, messengers, temples:** None in the wiki or the campaign. [research] The black pyramids above are the only structures the wiki names for him.
- **Cults in the Realms:** Wiki-sourced, with the book named where the wiki gives one.
  - In 1340 DR a cult formed in western Chessenta. It hired bandits, consorted with demons and caused much havoc before an alliance destroyed it. Some credit the goddess **Mystra** with stopping him from gaining a permanent foothold on Toril (*Sword of the Gods*, edition not given).
  - In 1479 DR the Firestorm Cabal found a burnt piece of the plague demon Murmur's corpse in Akanûl. Murmur possessed a Cabal member named Leheren and founded a secret order within the Cabal to free Tharizdun and release him in Faerûn (*Sword of the Gods*, edition not given).
  - At about the same time the last of the Sabrak clan of dwarves found the Abyssal Plague in a temple of **Ghaunadaur** in the mountains south of Easting. They planned to turn its victims into plague demons and raise an army for the Elder Elemental Eye (*The Elder Elemental Eye* and *Dungeon* #214, editions not given).
  - In the late 15th century DR several cults of the Elder Elemental Eye arose in the Dessarin Valley and tried to summon the Princes of Elemental Evil (*Princes of the Apocalypse* 5e).
- **Relations with other powers:** Wiki-sourced. The wiki credits Mystra with keeping him off Toril, as above. In the 4e account Pelor and Ioun, two other gods, went with him to the Living Gate (*Player's Handbook 3* and *Psionic Power*, 4e). It gives him no other ally or rival on this journal's roster.
- **Origin:** Wiki-sourced, 4e books, and the only account the wiki gives.
  - He was a long-forgotten interloper god from Oerth who wanted power that the other gods did not have (*Demonomicon* 4e; *Monster Manual* 4e).
  - With Pelor and Ioun he found the Living Gate, which separates the Far Realm from the Astral Sea, and the three looked through it and learned terrible secrets (*Player's Handbook 3* and *Psionic Power*, 4e).
  - During the Dawn War he returned, killed the gate's guardian and opened it, which released Far Realm energy into the multiverse. He took a seed of chaos from a dead universe, the obyriths' Shard of Pure Evil, and it corrupted him into madness (*Player's Handbook 3* and *Demonomicon*, 4e).
  - The obyriths wanted the seed planted in the Astral Sea. He planted it instead in the Elemental Chaos, where it grew into the Abyss, and the other gods put aside their quarrels long enough to imprison him (*Demonomicon* and *Monster Manual*, 4e).

## Overview

Tharizdun is the Chained God, a power of darkness and madness that the other gods are said to have imprisoned long ago. Scholars also call him the Elder Elemental Eye and the Eater of Worlds. His followers are nihilistic cultists who want to free him and bring the world to its end. Sages tell of cults in Chessenta, Akanûl and the Dessarin Valley, and some credit Mystra with keeping him from a foothold on Toril.

**Tone:** Bleak and impersonal. Tharizdun is a prisoner who cannot be bargained with in words, so when a player asks about him, describe the chains and the cults that work to break them before describing what he wants.
