> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Shar, the god of darkness and loss, and the reference for her faith and her appearances in the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*.
> - **Where it is used:** Shar appears in no campaign file and has no temple in Waterdeep. The page serves character building and the future Undermountain pass.
> - **Featured in:** No quest. In the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* she is the power behind the cult of Vanrakdoom, which plots against the Waterdeep temples of Lathander and Selûne.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Shar is secret in the campaign, and the Mad Mage material sits in the Using box as seeds.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Shar, and states what the published adventure ties to her so that no later page invents more.

*The Lady of Loss*

- **Title:** God of Darkness and Loss (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Mistress of the Night, the Dark Lady, Our Lady of Loss (FRHoF); the Lady of Loss, the Nightbringer (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Darkness, night, loss, forgetfulness, secrets (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text, which list none)
- **Domains:** Death, Trickery (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Black disk encircled with a purple border (SCAG, 2014; the PHB, 2014 entry gives the border no color)
- **Alignment:** Neutral Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Hades (Niflheim) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Anyone suffering pain or loss (FRHoF)

> Give your pain and your secrets to the dark.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Shar is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and the dark twin of Selûne (FRHoF). The wiki calls her a greater deity in its 1e to 4e sources and a deity in its 5e row.
- **What the faith offers:** She represents pain that is hidden but not forgotten. People in distress pray to her to forget their pain or grow numb to a loss (FRHoF). The wiki says the relief is numbness and never healing, and that her priests teach the faithful to nurse spite and regret (wiki; its sources include *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Core tenets:** The altar inscriptions in the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* give four, carved in a secret language. The faithful act in the dark and never wait for it to pass, quench the light of the moon or hide from it, keep secrets from everyone but the faithful, and hold that only a fool believes in hope (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:50785-50788`).
- **Who follows:** Miners and others who venture into dark places pray to her for protection, and so do people in melancholy or despair who want to forget something or recover something lost (FRHoF). The wiki adds the blind, nihilists, goblinoids that shun light, and thieves and killers who work at night (wiki; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e, and *Cyclopedia of the Realms*, 1e).
- **Priests and Chosen:** Her priests often nurture their own deep wounds or dark secrets, which they believe makes them best suited to console others who suffer the same. A Chosen of Shar forgets who they once were and knows only what she has made them into (FRHoF).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Shar.
- **Outlawed worship:** The tragedies and losses her followers have caused have led realms to outlaw her worship, and her priests have gone into secrecy. The bans sharpen the priests' resentment of authority and make the faithful a rallying point for revenge against whoever rules (FRHoF). The shadovar of Anauroch are an entire society dedicated to her. In the 15th century the wiki has them making her worship the state religion of their empire (wiki; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **The Order of the Dark Moon (FRHoF):**
  - The order studies darkness, deception, stealth, assassination and espionage. It welcomes monks, rogues, sorcerers, grim rangers and anyone who wants to cast their grief into forgetfulness.
  - It never takes applicants. It watches a candidate's reputation and deeds, then approaches them with relief from their anguish or a way to indulge their darker impulses.
  - An initiate is a Wanderer. Lessons woven with enchantment magic make a Wanderer doubt their memories and identity, and replace the broken pieces with a personality that suits Shar's service. A Wanderer can still reclaim bits of who they were.
  - A Wanderer who proves their loyalty is anointed a Dark Sibling in a midnight ceremony. Dark Siblings learn subtle strikes, deception and ambush, receive a free Comfortable lifestyle at any holding of the faith, and get help with disguises and forged or stolen credentials.
  - The order works against Selûne's militant orders and priests, and it finds allies in the cult of Myrkul.
- **How she appears:** As a lithe dancer with skin like a black night set with stars, or as a mysterious woman in swirling black robes. Rarely she appears as a black sphere limned in purple flames, like a strange solar eclipse (FRHoF).
- **Messengers:** She visits mortals in dreams, or sends a Death Tyrant or a Shadow as an emissary (FRHoF).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** Rising of the Dark, held on the Feast of the Moon (wiki, 2e and 3e; the 4e and 5e rows are blank). The campaign calendar places the Feast of the Moon between Uktar 30 and Nightal 1.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book tagged where the wiki gives one.
  - Selûne is her sister and eternal enemy. Their war began before the other Faerûnian gods existed and is fought through followers, servants and in person. Shar prefers to strike at what her sister loves, such as her faithful, over attacking her directly (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - The creation of the Shadow Weave made her the enemy of Mystra (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). She also opposes Lathander, Helm and Amaunator, at least while Amaunator stood in the Netherese pantheon (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e, and *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e, among its sources).
  - Her only regular ally is Talona, and the alliance was recent as of 1370 DR (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). She resents Mask for holding the shadows and thieves she thinks should be hers (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - She and Selûne coalesced together out of the primordial essence and were first one being, the Two-Faced Goddess. Together they made Toril and the other worlds, then split when Chauntea asked them for warmth. In the war that followed, Selûne tore out a part of her own essence and hurled it at Shar, and the two essences together formed Mystryl, the first goddess of magic (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - During the Time of Troubles in 1358 DR she killed the god Ibrandul in a deep chasm on a lost level of Undermountain. She took his portfolios of caverns, dungeons and the Underdark, and she kept his church as a front for undermining Selûne's (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *The Ruins of Undermountain*).
  - In the wiki's account of 1385 DR, Cyric and Shar killed Mystra and brought on the Spellplague (*The Grand History of the Realms*; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). Shar had meant to take the Weave for herself and lost her own Shadow Weave in the collapse. She then fused the Plane of Shadow with the leftover death energy of the Negative Energy Plane and made the Shadowfell (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - In 1486 and 1487 DR her Shadovar agents attacked Cormyr and drained the mythals of Candlekeep and Myth Drannor, in a bid to seize the Weave before the Tablets of Fate were renewed. Mystra manifested to stop her, and Shar lost Thultanthar, her hold on Sembia and most of the Tanthuls (*The Herald*, 2014; *Fire in the Blood*, 2015).
- **In the campaign:** Shar is present in 1492 DR, as every power is. The wiki's accounts of the Spellplague and the Second Sundering are history and change nothing in play.

> #### Using Shar as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as a grief-goddess that people are wary of. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is the Lady of Loss, the goddess of night and forgetting and the dark twin of Selûne, whose moon temple the city knows. They add that she eases grief by numbing it and that the numbness has a price. Priests of Selûne, Mystra and Lathander warn the grieving away from her (wiki; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Miners and night workers may still murmur a prayer to her for protection. Older noble families remember Lord Vanrak Moonstar, who turned to her and fled into Undermountain (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:472`). Whether anyone outside House Moonstar tells that story is [research].
> - **Waterdeep:** The sources place no temple, shrine or festival of Shar in the city.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists no temple of hers. It lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple for all deities, and no source says her faithful use it.
>   - *Dragon Heist* names her once, in the Selûne's Hallowing entry for the full moon of Uktar. The Wand of the Four Moons, kept at the House of the Moon, is said to be the mace Selûne wielded in her first battle against Shar and in their fight during the Time of Troubles (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31558`).
>   - In the wiki's account of 1358 DR, Shar came to Waterdeep in Selûne's guise, was welcomed at the House of the Moon and lived in the high priestess's quarters until Selûne's avatar and her friends exposed her (the comic storyline the wiki cites). This is history and not a campaign scene.
>   - Whether Waterdeep outlaws her worship is unsourced. [research]
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Death and Trickery (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Death subclass, so Trickery Domain is the fit (derived), and it is a close one because the faith deals in secrets and deception. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), since her clergy work through rites and hidden lore more than open fighting. The faith is outlawed in many realms (FRHoF), so a Sharran cleric in Waterdeep has no temple to turn to and no sourced local law to rely on.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Vengeance, a loose fit (derived). SCAG describes Shar as keeping vengeances nurtured away from the light, which is the nearest match among Devotion, Glory, the Ancients and Vengeance. FRHoF lists no paladins among her followers, and her tenets sit badly with the other three oaths.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* does, in the seeds below. Undermountain is future work, so nothing here is designed.
>   - Lord Vanrak Moonstar, a Waterdhavian noble, turned to Shar after blaming Selûne's temple for his father's death. He became a death knight in Vanrakdoom, renounced her in 1436 DR and destroyed himself with a sun blade (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:48599`, `48607`, `48613`).
>   - The vampire cleric Keresta Delvingstone leads Shar's cult in Vanrakdoom, which consists mainly of vampire spawn with a few living cultists. She plans to send the group to destroy the Spires of the Morning, the temple of Lathander in Waterdeep, and has her sights on the House of the Moon, the temple of Selûne, which she blames for Vanrak's ruin (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:48631`, `48712`).
>   - Keresta's map of Waterdeep marks the Spires of the Morning and the House of the Moon with daggers, and names the clerics and paladins of Lathander and Selûne she plans to assassinate (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:50537`). The **Lathander** page words the same seed.
>   - Lady Wylynd Moonstar asks Helion to convince adventurers to find the shadow dragon Umbraxakar in Vanrakdoom, once the bronze dragon Glyster, and break Shar's hold on him. The reward is the friendship of House Moonstar (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:472-473`).
>   - Destroying Keresta ends the cult. Several months later the Lords of Waterdeep grant the party the deed to a run-down estate in the city worth 15,000 gp (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:50852`).
>   - Midna Tauberth, a human priest of Shar, is one of the Fine Fellows of Daggerford. She has taken refuge in her lair on Level 2 and carries holy symbols of Shar and Waukeen (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:4051`, `6387`).

## Overview

Shar is the god of darkness and loss, also known as the Lady of Loss and the Mistress of the Night. She is the dark twin of Selûne, whose temple stands in the Sea Ward, and the two have fought since before the other gods. People who grieve pray to her to forget their pain, and miners and others who work in the dark pray to her for protection. Many realms have outlawed her worship, her priests keep to the shadows, and no temple of hers is known in Waterdeep.

**Tone:** Sorrowful and patient. Shar offers relief, so describe the comfort she promises before the hold it keeps. Keep her at a distance, since no campaign scene brings her or her cult into Waterdeep.
