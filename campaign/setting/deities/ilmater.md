*The Crying God*

- **Title:** God of Endurance (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Crying God, He Who Endures, the Rack-Broken Lord (FRHoF); the Broken God, the Lord on the Rack, the One Who Endures and Our Martyred Father, with Ilmatari as the word for his faithful; Ayuruk among the Iulutiun and Itishikopak among the Angulutiun of the Great Glacier (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Endurance, suffering, martyrdom, perseverance, and the relief of the sick, the poor and the oppressed (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Endurance, suffering, martyrdom, perseverance" and the FRHoF text describes the relief work; no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Life, Twilight (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Hands bound at the wrist with red cord (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Bytopia (Shurrock) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Monks, the oppressed, the poor (FRHoF)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Ilmater is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and the second power to join the Triad, after Tyr and before Torm. The wiki rates him a lesser power in 1e, an intermediate power in 2e and an intermediate deity in 3e. In those three editions he serves Tyr, who was his superior until the Spellplague.
- **What the faith asks:** His followers give succor when they can and use force to end torture and the suffering inflicted on others. They put others ahead of themselves, share all they have, and value the spiritual nature of life over the welfare of the body (FRHoF).
- **What the faith offers:** He gives comfort to anyone in pain, wronged or in great need, and he speaks to mortals in moments of despair and agony, where his voice soothes their hurt and fear. A martyr receives a final rest and reward in his afterlife (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Who follows:** Monks, the oppressed and the poor (FRHoF). The wiki adds the sick, the lame, serfs and slaves, and says his worship is popular among the poor of big cities (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Older lists add merchants, thieves and a few guards (*Cyclopedia of the Realms*, 1e). Anyone of any alignment may worship him, though his followers and above all his clergy are mostly lawful and good (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Clergy and orders:** The wiki names clerics, paladins and monks, organized as the Church of Ilmater with many affiliated knightly and monastic orders (*Cyclopedia of the Realms*; *Faiths & Pantheons*). His priests take in the sick, the starving and the injured, and his temples give away most of what they receive to offset the suffering of the world (FRHoF).
- **Hair shirts:** A priest on a mission of aid wears a hair shirt, a vest of coarse fur. Harming such a priest at work is taboo, even on a battlefield, and warlike groups avoid attacking a peaceful priest who tends their fallen as well. Most folk respect the faith's work and help it where they can (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **The Broken Ones:** This monastic order guards temples and caravans (FRHoF).
  - Monks are its foremost members, and fighters, particularly Eldritch Knights, and paladins who swear the Oath of Devotion also join. Some members are ascetics who withdraw from worldly affairs, and others know they must be active in the world to protect it.
  - A new member begins as a supplicant who defends a temple, then takes tasks in the wider world, and the most trusted hunt down, punish or retrieve those who inflict suffering. Induction requires enduring great personal suffering for others, then a period of fasting and carefully inflicted pain, so many members carry scars and old breaks.
  - Members receive a free Comfortable lifestyle at Ilmater's temples and monasteries and can obtain *Potions of Healing* and similar items. The order opposes anyone who harms the weak and innocent, especially followers of **Loviatar**, and finds lasting allies in the faithful of **Lathander**.
  - No source places the Broken Ones in Waterdeep.
- **Chosen:** The FRHoF says his Chosen eventually become martyrs, because they are driven to step between victims and the source of their suffering. His power lets them intervene in brutally perilous situations, and it carries them closer to the embrace of the Crying God. No file names a Chosen of Ilmater in Waterdeep.
- **How he appears:** As a short, burly, balding man in only a breechcloth, with a plain, kind face. His body shows the marks of the rack, with open cuts, scars and burns, broken joints and smashed hands that still work, and the wounds never heal, however much his avatar mends (wiki, citing *Cyclopedia of the Realms*, *Faiths & Avatars* and *Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **His temper:** He is even-tempered and slow to anger. Cruelty to children and young creatures enrages him most, and then he sets aside his nonviolence and uses his full force to end it (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Faiths & Pantheons*). He is held to be the most forgiving of beings, and the wiki says he might forgive **Loviatar** if she repented (*Champions of Valor*).
- **Messengers and signs:** He sometimes sends a shield guardian to protect a mortal in need. He shows favor by growing daisies and white roses or by sending peaceful animals (FRHoF). The wiki adds devas, planetars, solars, hollyphants and the spirits of slain martyrs as his agents, white donkeys, doves, field mice and sparrows as animal signs, and the colors gray and red as a minor sign of favor (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Deity Do's and Don'ts*).
- **Holy days:** No source gives one, and the wiki's holy-day fields are blank. No file ties a city festival to him. [research]
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (2e and 3e sources).
  - He is the closest ally of **Tyr**, whom he often travels with, helping the Just God learn to live without sight. He also has an excellent relationship with **Torm**. The three together are the Triad, a lawful good alliance stronger than its members apart (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - **Lathander** is another ally, and so is Ibrandul, the god of caves and darkness.
  - He opposes powers that enjoy causing pain, above all **Loviatar**, who hates him because he protects her victims. His other foes are **Talona**, **Bane**, **Bhaal**, Garagos, **Malar**, **Myrkul**, **Shar** and **Talos**.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He was an older god, obscure in the third century before Dale Reckoning, who rose to prominence during Tyr's Procession of Justice in the Vilhon Reach. Tyr's deeds and sacrifices caught his attention, and he allied himself with Tyr in -243 DR. The Procession ended in -238 DR, the two kept working together, and Torm joined them many years later as Tyr's war leader (*Faiths & Pantheons*; *The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - His first holy symbol was a blood-stained rack. A new one, white hands crossed and bound at the wrist with a blood-red cord, was in common use by 1356 DR and became almost the only symbol after the Godswar of 1358 DR. The gentler symbol raised his popularity across Faerûn (wiki, citing 1e to 3e sources).
  - An old text from the island of Ixinos, written in the 300s DR, tells how his avatar wrestled the cur of Gehenna, a minion of Bhaal, carried it into the sea and died with it. Only his iron helm was found on the beach, and it became a minor relic of his faith (*Volo's Guide to All Things Magical*, 5e).
  - In 1384 DR the Triad broke apart, and Ilmater left the House of the Triad and, at Sune's invitation, moved his realm of Martyrdom to Brightwater (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e). By 1479 DR he had returned to Torm's side and reestablished the realm in Celestia (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - During the Second Sundering, in the late 1400s DR, a Chosen of Ilmater began a passive slave revolt in Calimshan against the genie overlords and then disappeared. The overlords fell, but only by bloody means, and many humans still hope the Chosen will return to finish the work without violence (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e).
- **Elsewhere in the Realms:** The people of Damara venerate him and his champion, St. Sollars (*Cyclopedia of the Realms*; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 2e). In Calimshan he is one of the most worshiped gods, especially among the lower classes and slaves (*Empires of the Shining Sea*).
- **In the campaign:** Ilmater is alive and present in 1492 DR. His Hospice is a working temple in the North Ward, and no file treats him as absent. The wiki's 3e and 4e homes for his realm are history, and the Plane field follows the FRHoF.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Using Ilmater as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god who sits with the dying and the poor. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says Ilmater is the Crying God, who takes pain onto himself so that others need not. They add that his priests wear hair shirts of coarse fur, feed the starving and nurse the sick, and that nobody harms a priest of Ilmater at work, not even a soldier in a battle. They say that his temples give away most of what they receive, and that a martyr is said to win rest in his afterlife. Waterdhavians know the Hospice of St. Laupsenn in the North Ward as his temple. No source records how Waterdhavians in particular talk about him, so this voicing draws on the FRHoF and SCAG descriptions. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Ilmater has one temple in the city, the Hospice of St. Laupsenn in the North Ward.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Ilmater, Hospice of St. Laupsenn, North Ward" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30943-30945`). The adventure text for Kalain's residence calls it "a temple of Ilmater, god of suffering" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:13267`).
>   - No campaign file names or describes the Hospice. The sources do not say who built it, who runs it, how large it is, or who St. Laupsenn was. [research]
>   - The same table lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple of all deities. Trollskull Manor, the party's tavern, also stands in the North Ward (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:16`).
>   - In the Dragon Heist text one priest works at the Hospice, Vhaspar Holmdreg, a lawful good Illuskan human in his seventies who is half blind with cataracts. He rents a room in the west wing of Kalain's residence and brings her food twice a week, and firewood three times a week in winter. He is the only person she treats politely. The campaign's Kalain's Tower event does not use him, so he is a seed for a GM who wants a face at the Hospice.
>   - The Order of the Gauntlet's temple benefits name the temples of Tyr, Helm and Torm, and the Hospice is not among them (`campaign/quests/faction-events/order-of-the-gauntlet/00-first-meeting/ev-01-first-meeting.md:203`).
>   - No other shrine or temple of Ilmater exists in the campaign files, and no city festival is tied to him.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Life and Twilight (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Twilight Domain, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is Life Domain (derived). It matches his first domain, and his priests spend their days healing and feeding the suffering. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because his clergy minister to the sick and teach that the spirit matters more than the body. A cleric who joins the Broken Ones to guard temples and caravans would take Protector instead (derived).
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion. The FRHoF says paladins who swear it join the Broken Ones, so the source gives the fit directly, and the wiki's 2e and 3e class lists include paladins as well.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** One staff candidate and a naming overlap, with no quest behind either. The Trollskull Manor staff table lists Brother Aldric as "Thin, calm, Ilmater robes, unhurried stillness", a member of the "Order of Ilmater (between monasteries; this is his sabbatical, not an assignment)" (`campaign/guides/trollskull-manor/03-staff-and-hiring.md:67`). The Order of the Gauntlet's files speak of "the Triad", and the GM Notes below set out how that name sits beside Ilmater.

## Overview

Ilmater is the Crying God, a lawful good power of endurance, suffering and martyrdom who offers comfort to the sick, the poor and the unjustly treated. His priests take in the injured and the starving and wear hair shirts of coarse fur, and even warlike groups avoid attacking a peaceful priest who tends their fallen. His symbol is a pair of hands bound at the wrist with red cord. In Waterdeep his temple is the Hospice of St. Laupsenn in the North Ward.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Brother Aldric:** A Trollskull Manor cook candidate, neutral good and human, who asks 8 gp a tenday and leaves after exactly 30 days whatever the party wants. He says "I cook for my brothers at the monastery. I am between postings and find cooking for strangers clarifying," and then offers healing prayers. He casts *Cure Wounds* (2d8+4) once a day and treats feeding people as an act of worship (`campaign/guides/trollskull-manor/03-staff-and-hiring.md:67`). The Alexandrian appendix adds that he has cooked at his monastery for eight years, wears the cord of Ilmater at his waist, and that Order of the Gauntlet characters find him respected across the devout orders (`sources/Appendix_D_-_Running_the_Tavern.md:371-376`). He is not a Broken One in any file, and he is not tied to the Hospice.
> - **Vhaspar Holmdreg:** The Dragon Heist priest of the Hospice is a different man from Vhaspar Coldwater, the retired Watch sharpshooter in the Lords' Alliance mission **The Archer Above**. That mission's design notes changed the archer's surname because "Vhaspar Holmdreg is already a Dragon Heist name" (`campaign/quests/faction-events/lords-alliance/m04-the-archer-above/design-notes.md:9`), so keep the two men apart at the table.
> - **The Triad in the Order's files:** The Order of the Gauntlet's temples are the Halls of Justice, the Temple of Helm in the Castle Ward and the Chapel of the Steadfast Shield in the Dock Ward, and one rank event calls their priests "The Triad's Spells" (`campaign/quests/faction-events/order-of-the-gauntlet/r10-whitehawk/ev-01-whitehawk.md:148-150`). The Faerûnian Triad is Tyr, Torm and Ilmater. The Order's files never mention an Ilmater temple, so the Hospice gives a member no healing or spells under the Order's benefits.

**Tone:** Gentle and plain. Ilmater is the god of endurance, so describe the comfort his priests give before the pain they share, and keep the pain off the page. Keep him in the present tense and at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he speaks.
