**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Loviatar, and states that the campaign has tied nothing to her so that no later page invents a temple, a cell or a cultist in the city.

*The Maiden of Pain*

- **Title:** God of Pain (FRHoF). The 2014 Player's Handbook and SCAG print "Goddess of pain", and the wiki and every other source use "she".
- **Other Names:** The Scourge Mistress, The Willing Whip (FRHoF); Patroness of Torturers (wiki, *Faiths & Avatars* 2e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Pain, torture, cruelty, secret cults (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki portfolio lines; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Death (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Nine-tailed barbed scourge (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Gehenna (Mungoth) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Those suffering from pain or betrayal (FRHoF). The wiki's 5e row adds masochists, sadists and torturers.

> Pain is holy, so endure it and give it without mercy.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Loviatar is a goddess of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki files her as a deity in its 5e row, an intermediate deity in 4e, a lesser deity in 3e and 2e, and a demigoddess in 1e. In its 4e source she is both queen and servant to the greater god **Bane** (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **What the faith teaches:** Her followers hold that all pain is holy, from crude brutality to the grief of the heartbroken and the betrayed, and that pain is proof of her attention. Being able to inflict it and to endure it is a path to power (FRHoF). They think a cold, cruel manner is the ideal because it copies hers, and they prize beauty, refinement and skill at manipulation for the same reason. The wiki adds that the best skill a follower can have is understanding a victim well enough to hurt them most (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Her nature:** She is a cold and calculating despot who enjoys her work, and for her pain is the goal in itself. That quality endears her to Bane (FRHoF). The wiki says she is almost immune to emotion and only laughs at attempts to hurt her. Her one weakness is her assumption that people are selfish, so truly selfless behavior can unravel her plots (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Who follows:** Temples are rare and the faithful are numerous. She is a preferred deity of people who inflict pain as a matter of course, including torturers and others who need to break the will of their victims (FRHoF).
- **Cells and secrecy:** Her followers form cells of secret adherents. Each cell is led by someone who takes pleasure in administering pain and dominating others, and is supported by submissive followers (FRHoF). They rarely gather in numbers outside populous cities and never worship openly. When a small cadre keeps to itself, few citizens take notice, but the cults sometimes kidnap and keep prisoners, which draws the attention of the authorities (FRHoF).
- **Open worship:** SCAG (2014) says some cults run secret slavery rings, and that open worship and clearly dedicated temples are rarely seen except in lands where slavery is accepted. The wiki says the faith was the state religion of Dambrath (*Shining South*, edition not stated).
- **Rites and prayer:** Followers often whip themselves in the morning when they pray for spells. They mark each season with the Rite of Pain and Purity, an ordeal endured under the whips of higher-ranking priests, and hold a smaller rite every twelve days (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Clergy:** Novices are called Kneeling Ones, and confirmed priests rise through eleven titles from Taystren to Truescar. The last two are given to those who have led a temple or distinguished themselves (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Specialty priests called pains travel as envoys, agents or inquisitors, and ordinary clerics hold them in high regard (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Orders:** The wiki says the faith has no knightly orders. Its one named order is the Disciples of the White Rod, a monastic order of monks near Calimport who follow the Way of Transcendence (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. FRHoF calls her Chosen paragons of inflicting and receiving pain, who work their way into the fabric of society and then tear through it. No file names a Chosen of Loviatar.
- **Messengers:** She takes the form of a slender woman, like a whip in her movement. She speaks to followers during long sessions of enduring or inflicting pain, in the delirium of agony or ecstasy. Her emissary is usually a chain devil, an incubus or a succubus (FRHoF).
- **How she appears:** As a slim, beautiful human maiden with platinum-blonde hair to her thighs, dressed in black leather with spikes or in white silk with silver chains. She speaks softly and her black blood is thick as syrup (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Omens and holy days:** No source gives an omen or a calendar holy day. [research]
- **Divine realm:** A palace of ice on Mungoth, the third layer of Gehenna, defended by enchantments her parents Tuonetar and Tuoni cast against the realm's bursts of heat (*Manual of the Planes*, 1e). The wiki's 3e row gives the Barrens of Doom and Despair as her home plane.
- **Temples elsewhere:** The wiki lists the House of Nine Blessings in Calimport, the House of Scarlet Hooks in Calaunt, the Lady's Hand Monastery in Ladypeak, the Resting Place of the Whip in Melvaunt and the Tower of Pain Exalted in Zhentil Keep. It also lists a House of Pain in Undermountain, citing *City of Splendors: Waterdeep*, p. 41.
- **Relations with Bane and Shar:** She served **Shar** for a time. When Bane returned she served him again as his consort (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). FRHoF says her love of pain for its own sake endears her to Bane.
- **Relations with Bhaal, Talona and Malar:** Before the Time of Troubles she was a servant of **Bhaal** and the rival of her fellow servant **Talona**. In -33 DR Talona killed her sister, the demigoddess Kiputytto, and Loviatar set out to torment Talona in revenge. She believes Talona will one day serve her. At the same time she was affiliated with **Malar** (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Netheril: Empire of Magic*).
- **Relations with Ilmater:** Her portfolio conflicts most with **Ilmater**'s, because she despises those who help others. Her followers were encouraged to wipe his followers from the Realms (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). The wiki says Ilmater might forgive her if she repented (*Champions of Valor*).
- **Relations with Eldath and Lliira:** She hated **Eldath** and **Lliira** because they offered rewards with no suffering to earn them (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). The Order of the Scarlet Mummers was founded to avenge a High Revelmistress of Lliira whom a local cult of Loviatar murdered in Selgaunt, and it fights her followers (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced. The wiki gives her parents as Tuonetar and Tuoni (*Manual of the Planes*, 1e) and names no founding event for her faith. Before the Time of Troubles she served Bhaal (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). The wiki has no Spellplague or Second Sundering text for her.
- **In the campaign:** Loviatar is alive and present in 1492 DR. The wiki writes of her in the past tense throughout but records no death, and no campaign file treats her as dead or absent.

> #### Using Loviatar as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as a goddess people prefer not to discuss. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is the Maiden of Pain, the goddess of agony, whose symbol is a barbed scourge with nine tails. They say torturers and the cruel favor her, that her temples are rare, and that her followers meet in secret cells that sometimes keep prisoners. Priests of Ilmater and Lliira add that their faiths oppose hers (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). How Waterdhavians feel about her beyond this, and which of her names they use, is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Loviatar has no temple or shrine in the city in the campaign sources.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Loviatar row. Its "All deities" row, Holyhands House in the North Ward, covers her by its own wording (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30922-30926`).
>   - The wiki lists a House of Pain in Undermountain and gives no ward (`City of Splendors: Waterdeep`, p. 41). No campaign or Dragon Heist line mentions it.
>   - No campaign file places a priest, a Chosen or a cell of hers in the city, and no festival is tied to her. A cell in Waterdeep fits the FRHoF description of secret adherents in a populous city, and the campaign has not written one.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Death (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Death domain, and none of Life, Light, Trickery or War fits well. Trickery Domain is the nearest (derived), because her faith works through hidden cells and manipulation and her Chosen insinuate themselves into society. The fit is loose. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), which gives martial weapon training, and the scourge is the faith's weapon. A Loviatan cleric in Waterdeep belongs to a hidden cell and has no temple to turn to.
> - **A paladin PC:** None of the four oaths fits (derived). FRHoF lists no paladins among her followers, and the wiki says her faith has no knightly orders. Oath of Vengeance is the loosest fit (derived). A paladin who opposes the faith fits Vengeance or Devotion better, as the Order of the Scarlet Mummers does for Lliira.
> - **A warlock PC:** Fiend (derived). The sources describe no pact, and the fit is loose. Her emissary is usually a chain devil, an incubus or a succubus (FRHoF), so a Fiend patron speaking through her servants is the nearest reading.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* names her worshipers once, and Undermountain is future work, so treat it as a seed. The Tower of the Seven Woes entry (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:62705`) reads: "Carved into the column is a seven-story prison composed of windowless torture chambers connected to one another by rough-hewn stairways. Worshipers of Loviatar used this tower to imprison and torture slaves. The walls throughout are lined with manacles; rusted shackles lie strewn amid torture racks, spiked cages, and other contraptions that have fallen into disrepair." The tower is a prison her worshipers used, not a temple, and the entry does not name the House of Pain.

## Overview

Loviatar is the goddess of pain, called the Maiden of Pain, and her symbol is a barbed scourge with nine tails. Her faithful hold that all suffering is holy, and torturers and others who break the will of their victims favor her. Temples to her are rare, and her followers meet in secret cells that sometimes kidnap and keep prisoners. No temple of hers is known in Waterdeep.

**Tone:** Cold and plain. Loviatar is a goddess of cruelty, so describe what her faith believes and does in a few direct sentences, and never dwell on the suffering itself. Keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she acts.
