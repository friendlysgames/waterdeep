# Ghaunadaur

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the patron entry for Ghaunadaur, called That Which Lurks, an Underdark power touched by the Far Realm and a Great Old One patron for warlock characters. The sources also give it a cult, a cleric domain and a seat in the Dark Seldarine, so the Using box keeps one line for clerics. Because it is not a god of the Faerûnian pantheon, the page puts warlock guidance first.
> - **Where it is used:** Ghaunadaur never appears in the campaign files. It is a choice for a player building a warlock, and the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* gives it a statue and a temple on Level 10 and a worshiper cult on Level 19.
> - **Featured in:** no quest. Undermountain is future work, so the Level 10 and Level 19 material sits in the GM Notes as seeds.
> - **Spoiler limit:** this page holds no hidden fact that changes play, so it has no Secret box. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Everything above it may be read by the GM only. Bregan D'aerthe has rejected Lolth, and that does not make any member a follower of Ghaunadaur.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what little an ordinary Waterdhavian knows about That Which Lurks, gives a player what a Great Old One patron relationship with it costs, and states that no campaign file uses it, so that no later page invents a temple, a cult or a Bregan D'aerthe worshiper.

*That Which Lurks*

- **Title:** That Which Lurks (MTF; 5etools)
- **Other Names:** The Elder Eye and the Ancient One (wiki; *Demihuman Deities* 2e, *Faiths & Pantheons* 3e). The Lord of Slime (wiki; the video game *Baldur's Gate II: Shadows of Amn*). The wiki also lists Ghaunadar, Gormauth Souldrinker, Juiblex and the Elder Elemental God as aspects (*Demihuman Deities* 2e).
- **Pantheon:** Drow, the Dark Seldarine (MTF). It is not part of the Faerûnian pantheon.
- **Areas of Concern:** Oozes, slimes, outcasts, all things subterranean, drow who lack status (derived from the wiki's portfolio rows and the MTF text; neither lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** War (MTF, 2014 rules). The wiki's older rows differ: Destruction, Earth and Madness in 4e, and Cavern, Chaos, Drow, Evil, Hatred and Slime in 3e.
- **Symbol:** A purple eye with a black sclera (MTF). The wiki's 3e row describes a purplish eye on purple, violet and black circles.
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Evil (MTF, 2014 rules)
- **Plane:** [research] for the current edition, since the 5etools entry gives none and the wiki's 5e row is blank. The wiki's older rows name the Paraelemental Plane of Ooze (*Demihuman Deities* 2e), the Demonweb Pits (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e) and the Dismal Caverns (4e).
- **Typical Followers:** Drow, oozes and jellies (wiki; 5e row). The wiki's 3e row adds aboleths, fighters, outcasts and ropers (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e).

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Ghaunadaur is the god of oozes, slimes and outcasts and a member of the Dark Seldarine (MTF; wiki). The wiki rates the power a lesser deity in its 2e and 3e rows, a greater deity in 4e, and gives no 5e rank. It says Ghaunadaur is believed to be touched by the Far Realm (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide* 4e).
- **Its nature:** It is called That Which Lurks because uttering its true name risks drawing its attention. Its real form, if it has one, is unknown, and it is shown as an ooze-like creature with many tentacles or as a purple pupil ringed in black. The liquid shape stands for its unpredictability (MTF).
- **What it offers:** It sometimes rewards its followers with supernatural powers or wealth, and it is equally likely to curse them with hideous torments and afflictions. MTF gives two examples. A subterranean hunter who whispers its name may stumble on a forgotten treasure trove, while a devoted priestess who offers long prayers and valuable sacrifices may be eaten by a gelatinous cube. A small sacrifice and a prayer may go unnoticed, bring punishment or earn a great reward.
- **Why it draws followers:** The randomness attracts drow who lack status and are desperate to gain it (MTF). The wiki adds that false worshipers were sometimes given permanent magical boons and could just as easily be devoured without a second thought (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e).
- **How it behaves:** The wiki says it enjoys watching large monsters hunt and devour their prey. It speaks by telepathy and keeps its messages blunt and simple, and old records mention a gibbering, bestial language in its court (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e).
- **Who worships it:** Worship is widespread in the Underdark and is not limited to drow. Even mindless oozes and jellies sometimes behave in ways that look like reverence (MTF).
- **Enemies among the drow:** Followers of Lolth often oppose its cultists and drive them into hiding or open conflict (MTF). Some priestesses and scholars say the enmity began when it betrayed Lolth soon after she betrayed Corellon, and that both gods punished it for the double betrayal by casting it down as a skinless, boneless mass. Other stories call it an incredibly ancient and ineffable being, one of the so-called Great Old Ones, and MTF says both claims may have merit.
- **Cults and clergy:** The wiki says its church is a set of cults scattered across the Underdark, each dominated by one person, with a more organized clergy in only a few drow and aboleth cities. A cleric must serve it completely and keep its altars supplied with sacrifices, and the clerics who bring willing victims rank highest and earn magic power and items. It encourages clerics to learn acids, poisons and alchemist's fire, and its temples and worshipers wear purple, green, black and metallic colors (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e).
- **Infighting and servants:** It does not care what bonds hold its clergy together, and clerics who murder one another to rise do not displease it. The wiki also says slime lords are its favorite servants, and the stronger ones can change shape to spy and infiltrate for it (*Ascendancy of the Last*, as the wiki cites it; *Player's Guide to Faerûn*).
- **Rites:** A cleric prays for spells once a day, at a time the local cult picks, and each prayer comes with a sacrifice. Living offerings are preferred, bones and food burned in oil are accepted, and a worshiper with nothing to give must pray with one unprotected hand in an open flame (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e). Its prayers are as shapeless as it is, and a cleric who speaks gibberish is not suspected of blasphemy (*Ascendancy of the Last*, as the wiki cites it).
- **The Gathering:** Cultists raid to seize sentient victims for the altar. The victims are drugged and held under compulsion, then pushed to walk up to the altar, where the leading cleric prays, turns into slime, and the slime envelops and consumes the victim (*Ascendancy of the Last*, as the wiki cites it).
- **Temples and signs:** Its shrines and temples are foul, with fetid smells, mists and slime trickling down the walls into pools, strongest near the halls where sacrifices are made (*Ascendancy of the Last*, as the wiki cites it). Its older sign is a Y shape, which may appear as a mark of favor or be found in ancient places devoted to it. The wiki lists purple mist, a golden eye and tentacles among its manifestations (the web enhancement *Deity Do's and Don'ts*, edition not given).
- **Holy days and messengers:** No holy day in any source, and the wiki's holy-day rows are blank for every edition. [research] The wiki's minions row is blank, and the only servant it names on the Material Plane is the drow oozemaster Kurolaboath Notepirroth (the web article *Perilous Gateways*, edition not given).
- **Relations with other powers:** Wiki-sourced.
  - It was thought to have no allies (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e), although the archomental Bwimb and the god Moander counted as allies (*Demihuman Deities* 2e).
  - Its foes are nearly every god of the Underdark, the whole Seldarine, **Malar** and Gargauth (*Demihuman Deities* 2e).
  - **Lolth** says she neither commands nor compels it (*Ascendancy of the Last*, as the wiki cites it). The Lolth page records that she feared it could sway the drow and that it did not care enough about drow matters to attract many.
  - **Eilistraee** hated its influence on the drow and fought it often (*Demihuman Deities* 2e). It holds a grudge against her for defeating its avatar and trapping it near the Promenade of the Dark Maiden (*The Seven Sisters*, edition not given). The Eilistraee page records the wiki's claim that this protected Waterdeep.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact. MTF's legend of the double betrayal is under Enemies among the drow.
  - Lolth calls it old even before Ao's time (*Ascendancy of the Last*). It is rumored to have emerged from the primordial ooze. In that first age the largest slimes and crawling creatures worshiped it, and many of them had an alien intelligence. It struck most of them mad in a rage over an unknown wrong and took their minds, and the worshipers it destroyed took its power down with them (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e).
  - Only in later ages did evil beings turn to it as an alternative to the established gods (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e). Some drow of Ilythiir worshiped it from the First Flowering through the Crown Wars (*Lost Empires of Faerûn* 3e; *The Grand History of the Realms* 4e). It became a member of the drow pantheon because of drow who were disaffected with Lolth (*Faiths & Pantheons* 3e).
  - In 1379 DR, tired of Lolth, it left the Demonweb Pits and made a new realm in the Deep Caverns (*The Grand History of the Realms* 4e). The wiki says it broke from the Dark Seldarine after the War of the Spider Queen (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide* 4e) and rejoined after the Second Sundering (MTF).
- **In the campaign:** Ghaunadaur is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. The wiki's account of the break and the return is history, and the Dark Seldarine membership in MTF is the state in force.

MTF says some stories name it one of the Great Old Ones, the wiki calls it touched by the Far Realm, and the wiki files it under Great Old One patrons. No source gives Ghaunadaur-specific features. It speaks by telepathy in blunt, simple messages, which suits a Great Old One's mind-touching magic (derived). What it wants is sacrifice, above all from victims who offer themselves willingly. What a pact costs is certainty. A reward and a curse are equally likely, a faithful priestess may be eaten while a whisperer finds treasure, and it does not mind if its clerics murder one another to rise.

War Domain is the matching 2024 subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War (derived), and the fit is close, with Protector as the Divine Order for a martial cleric (derived). The wiki's 3e and 4e domains fit none of the four. A cleric of it finds no temple and no clergy in Waterdeep. No paladin connection in the sources.

## Overview

Ghaunadaur, called That Which Lurks, is the chaotic evil power of oozes, slimes and outcasts, and its worship is widespread in the Underdark. Its true name is rarely spoken, because uttering it is said to draw its attention, and no temple of it stands in Waterdeep. It may reward a worshiper with power or wealth, and it is equally likely to curse the same worshiper with torments and afflictions. Some tales call it one of the Great Old Ones, and the faithful of Lolth drive its cults into hiding.

> #### GM Notes
> - **Undermountain seeds (published, future work):** The lines below are from `sources/adventure-wdmm.json`. None of them ties Ghaunadaur to a Dragon Heist or campaign character.
>   - **Level 10, Muiral's Gauntlet, area 9a, The Elder Eye:** The area sits in the God-Watched Gates, rooms that "house shrines dedicated to gods of the drow pantheon" (`:29968`). A 20-foot-diameter hemisphere of magical darkness hides a 12-foot statue that reshapes itself every minute, from a pillar of ooze with eyeless faces to a wave of slime covered with fang-filled mouths (`:29975-29976`). A creature that beholds it must succeed on a DC 14 Wisdom saving throw or gain short-term madness, and a DC 15 Intelligence (Religion) check identifies the statue as Ghaunadaur, "god of oozes, slimes, and all things subterranean" (`:29976-29977`). The locked double door to the south opens for a creature suffering any madness (`:29984`).
>   - **Level 10, area 17a, Temple of Ghaunadaur:** The text calls Ghaunadaur "a god of subterranean horrors" who "is respected and feared by many Underdark races, including drow" (`:30408`). Green slime covers the walls and hides a secret door, and the altar is a hollow glass block with three psychic gray oozes sealed inside (`:30413-30419`). The temple is one of several in area 17 that are "dedicated to the Dark Seldarine, a pantheon of drow gods under Lolth" (`:30401`).
>   - **Level 10, area 25a, Hall of the Dark Seldarine:** Ghaunadaur is the fourth of eight fountain statues, between Kiaransalee and Zinzerena (`:30762`).
>   - **Level 19, Caverns of Ooze:** The text says the Caverns of Ooze "have, at various times, served as a temple and sanctuary for worshipers of Ghaunadaur". Halaster rewards the zealots by turning them into oozes that keep most of their memories and intelligence, and the oozes believe the god has blessed them. Halaster does nothing to correct them (`:50909`).
>   - **Level 19, area 4, The Weeping Eye:** A 10-foot orb of ooze floats over a pool and weeps ooze into it (`:51852`). Ghaunadaur's worshipers think it is part of the god and call it the Weeping Eye. The text says Halaster found the orb and brought it there to flood the caverns and mislead them (`:50910`).
>   - **Level 19, areas 5 and 12, and the wandering encounters:** Three intelligent black puddings that were drow worshipers wait in the pit of area 5 (`:51892`) and on the ceiling of area 12 (`:52141`). Area 12 holds a shrine, a purple disk with a black glaring eye set in an altar, and behind it a 9-foot statue of an amorphous pillar with a dozen clawed human arms (`:52142`). If the altar is opened the statue animates and attacks, and its claws destroy nonmagical armor (`:52144-52145`). The wandering encounters are six duergar worshipers fused into one gray ooze and two drow worshipers turned into black puddings (`:50926`, `:50961`).

**Tone:** Unsettling and plain. Ghaunadaur is an unpredictable power, so describe the offer and the chance of reward before the chance of ruin, and keep it at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which it acts.
