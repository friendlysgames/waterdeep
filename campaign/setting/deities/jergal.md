**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Jergal, and states plainly that the campaign has tied nothing to him, so that no later page invents a temple, a priest or a Chosen.

*The Final Scribe*

- **Title:** Scribe of the Dead (SCAG, 2014; FRHoF has no Jergal entry)
- **Other Names:** The Pitiless One, The Bleak Seneschal (SCAG, 2014); Lord of the End of Everything, the Forgotten One, Scribe of the Doomed, and Nakasr among the successor states of Netheril (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** The records of the dead, the names of the dead, tombs and proper burial, the work of undertakers (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Records of the dead" in its 5e row and "Fatalism, guardian of tombs, proper burial" in its 3e row; no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Knowledge, Death (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A skull biting a scroll (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** The Fugue Plane (wiki, 3e and 4e)
- **Typical Followers:** Undertakers and caretakers of gravesites (SCAG, 2014); undertakers (wiki, 5e); monks, necromancers and paladins (wiki, 3e)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Jergal is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon who serves Kelemvor (SCAG, 2014). The wiki calls him a demigod in 3e and an exarch of Kelemvor in 4e, and in 2e a demipower who had once been a greater deity. Its 5e row gives no rank.
- **What he keeps:** The SCAG calls him an uncaring custodian of the dead. He is thought to record the passing of the living and to help Kelemvor see that souls are bound to their proper afterlives (SCAG, 2014). The wiki adds fatalism, the guarding of tombs and proper burial to his portfolio (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **How he is honored:** Few people acknowledge him directly. He is mentioned at funerals, and some people follow the custom of writing the name of the dead on a sheet of parchment and placing it in the corpse's mouth. The custom is common where a grave or tomb does not carry the person's name (SCAG, 2014).
- **Who follows:** Few people favor him, and most of those are concerned with the dispensation of the dead in some way. His priests serve communities as undertakers and caretakers of gravesites (SCAG, 2014).
- **Temples and records:** He has no temples of his own, apart from abandoned places devoted to his older and darker incarnation. His priests are welcome in the temples of Kelemvor, Deneir and Myrkul. His faithful send their annual recordings of mortality to holy sites where records of that kind are kept (SCAG, 2014).
- **Omen:** His favored manifestation is the sound of a closing book, which carries a chilling feeling of finality (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e, wiki). No messenger is sourced. [research]
- **Holy days:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Jergal.
- **How he appears:** He is frequently shown as a wizened, mummy-like member of an ancient alien race that resembles a cross between a humanoid and a praying mantis. He wears white gloves and a gray cloak and holds a quill and a scroll of unreadable script. He can also take the form of an old man with a white beard (*Powers & Pantheons* and *Netheril: Empire of Magic*, 2e, wiki).
- **Nature and relations:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - He is loyal to the office of death, but he could subtly undermine a holder of that office who neglected its duties.
  - He served Myrkul, then Cyric, then Kelemvor. He works well with Kelemvor and kept his scorn for Cyric.
  - He spent much of his effort against Velsharoon's attempts to prolong life into undeath.
  - The Kelemvor page records him as the god who keeps the records of the final disposition of the dead.
- **Origin and rise:** The SCAG gives the legend and the wiki gives the rest, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - In the time of Netheril he was worshiped as the god of death, murder and strife. He grew bored with his power, and three powerful mortals came to the lands of the dead meaning to destroy him and take it. Jergal calmly gave up his throne of bones and let each of them take a part of his divinity. Bane took strife, Myrkul the rulership of the dead and Bhaal murder, and Jergal became a scribe of the dead (SCAG, 2014).
  - The SCAG tells the handover as the Legend of Knucklebones. The three mortals fought over who should rule, so Jergal offered a game of skill, first skull bowling and then knucklebones. Malar arrived to claim a share and was left only the beasts. Bane won first choice, Myrkul second and Bhaal third, and Jergal smiled at being relieved of the office (SCAG, 2014).
  - The wiki calls the three mortals the Dark Three. It says he was a greater deity older than many greater gods of Faerûn and that he became Myrkul's seneschal, a post he kept after Myrkul died and Cyric and then Kelemvor took the office (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - After the Second Sundering he was reported in a gate-town of Sylvania, inviting visitors to a game of skull bocce at an undead club called the Afterparty (*Planescape: Adventures in the Multiverse*, 5e).
  - In 1492 DR his avatar Withers woke in a temple on the Sword Coast that had lain abandoned for over a century. He aided a party of adventurers for a small fee, and afterward he dismissed Bane, Bhaal and Myrkul as vermin who would trouble the Realms no more (*Baldur's Gate III*, video game).
- **In the campaign:** Jergal is present in 1492 DR as a scribe of the dead who serves Kelemvor. The wiki's Withers account places his avatar on the Sword Coast, not in Waterdeep, and no campaign file uses it.

> #### Using Jergal as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as a half-forgotten clerk of death. A sage or a priest says he is the old god of the dead who now only writes down who has died, that Kelemvor judges while Jergal keeps the book, and that he is cold about the work. A regular at the bar has most likely never heard of him. Undertakers and gravekeepers know him best, and some of them name him at a funeral. Whether Waterdeep's own burial trade follows the parchment custom is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Jergal has no temple and no shrine in the campaign files or the published adventure.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30910-30997`) has no Jergal row, and a search of the adventure text for his name finds nothing. The word "Seneschal" at line 31832 is a city office, Seneschal of Castle Waterdeep, and has no link to him.
>   - The SCAG says his priests are welcome in the temples of Kelemvor, Deneir and Myrkul. The campaign files place none of those temples in Waterdeep, so a priest of Jergal in the city has no temple to work from. Kelemvor is honored in the City of the Dead, and no file says who keeps its graves.
>   - The wiki has no Waterdeep text for him. Its one temple, abandoned for a century, lies on the Sword Coast.
> - **A cleric PC:** Knowledge Domain (derived; Heroes of Faerûn). Knowledge is one of his two domains (SCAG, 2014), and he is the god of the records of the dead. Grave Domain (derived; Ravenloft: The Horrors Within) is the alternative for a cleric who tends tombs and burial more than the book. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because his clergy are record keepers and gravekeepers who rely on learning.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived; Player's Handbook, 2024), a loose fit. The wiki's 3e row lists paladins among his worshipers, and Devotion suits a knight sworn to duty, order and honest record against anyone who would hide or falsify a death. The SCAG describes him as uncaring, so the oath would draw more from his loyalty to the office than from any warmth.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power.

## Overview

Jergal is the Final Scribe, the lawful neutral god who keeps the records of the dead. Legend says he was once the lord of death, murder and strife, and that he gave those offices away out of weariness. Now he writes down each death and helps Kelemvor see souls to their proper afterlives. Few people worship him and most of them are undertakers and keepers of graves, so Waterdeep has no temple of his.

**Tone:** Dry and still. Jergal is a god who has stopped wanting anything, so describe his records and his patience before his coldness, and keep him at a distance. He is felt as the sound of a closing book, and the campaign writes no scene in which he speaks.
