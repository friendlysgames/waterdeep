**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Waukeen, and lists the festivals, dates and adventure characters the sources tie to her so that no later page invents more.

*The Coinmaiden*

- **Title:** God of Trade (FRHoF). The 2014 Player's Handbook and SCAG print "Goddess of trade", and every source uses "she".
- **Other Names:** The Coinmaiden, Our Lady of Gold, the Merchant's Friend (FRHoF); the Golden Lady (*For Duty & Deity*), Liberty's Maiden (*Cyclopedia of the Realms*) and the Lady of Trade (*The Sapphire Crescent*) (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Trade, wealth and money, markets and new ventures, contracts and bargains (derived from the FRHoF text and the wiki portfolio "Trade, money, wealth"; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Knowledge, Trickery (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** An upright coin with Waukeen's profile facing left (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** The Outlands (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Merchants, the rich, traders (FRHoF)

> Keep wealth moving so that everyone has a chance to earn.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Waukeen is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki's 5e row, which cites FRHoF, lists her simply as a deity, and it calls her a lesser deity in its 1e, 3e and 4e rows. Its 2e sources rank her as an intermediate deity presumed dead and as a lesser deity who is missing, which matches the account of her imprisonment below.
- **What she cares about:** She is interested in anything that increases trade and the flow of money, whether new trade routes, new inventions or the whim of changing fashion (FRHoF). She keeps cordial relations with the other gods except Mask, her only real enemy, because larceny is bad for business (FRHoF). SCAG (2014) calls her the goddess of wealth and trade "on both sides of the law".
- **The faith's ethos:** Her followers create opportunity for all and see competition for wealth as the best method of progress, so they are at odds with guilds and others who form monopolies (FRHoF). The wiki's 3e book adds that her church sometimes lets the ends justify the means (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **The tithe:** Followers commonly set aside a tenth of their profits. The money does not go to a temple. It helps a struggling business, finances a new endeavor or, if all else fails, pays for frivolous fun (FRHoF).
- **Who follows:** Her ardent worshipers include shopkeepers, members of trade organizations, wealthy merchants, caravan guides, itinerant peddlers, moneychangers and smugglers (FRHoF). Greed motivates those who take her as a patron, but she frowns on misers and smiles on the industrious and the profligate, so priests who bear her symbol are welcome in towns and cities. The wiki adds investors, moneylenders, fences and black marketeers (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **Temples and clergy:** Her temples resemble guildhalls and serve as meeting places for trade associations (FRHoF). The wiki places the ornate ones in centers of commerce from Tashalar up through Amn, around the Sea of Fallen Stars (Chessenta, Cormyr, Sembia, the Vast and Impiltur) and over to Thesk, and it names no Waterdeep site (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). Her clerics are called Goldeyes, and the faithful as a whole are the Waukeenar. Goldeyes travel the trade routes to aid merchants and caravans, or work in the temples as hosts for wealthy donors, moneylenders, currency exchangers, warehousers, covert fences and, above all, security guards (*Faiths & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Avatars*).
- **How she appears:** As a splendidly dressed woman with long golden hair, often with two golden lions beside her (FRHoF).
- **Messengers and omens:** She may show herself as sprouting golden daffodils, or as a stack of gold coins that writhes and slithers like a snake. When she sends an emissary it is an awakened golden cat, lion or eagle, or a sphinx of lore (FRHoF). The wiki adds a coin lying face up at a crossroads to show the right path, gold nuggets or citrine as a sign of favor and fool's gold as a possible sign of disfavor, and a pair of golden eyes watching from a curtain of darkness (*Faiths & Avatars*).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Waukeen. FRHoF says her Chosen understand contracts and agreements so well that they can rival powerful devils at unwinding convoluted terms, and that they step in when the wealth of nations is at stake.
- **Holy days:** *Dragon Heist* and the campaign calendar give her two festivals.
  - Waukeentide runs from Tarsakh 1 to 10 and gathers older holidays into a holiday season. Caravance (Tarsakh 1) is a gift-giving day that marks the first caravans of the season, Goldenight (Tarsakh 5) has shops open all night, Guildsmeet (Tarsakh 7) is a guild gala from dusk to dawn, and Leiruin (Tarsakh 10) is the day guilds pay their dues and renew their charters with the Lords of Waterdeep.
  - Sornyn runs from Flamerule 3 to 5. It is a festival of Waukeen and Lathander for planning business, making treaties and receiving envoys, and the saying for the season is "My enemy is like family to me."
  - The wiki's 2e table adds eleven more holy days: Cold Counting (Hammer 15), Great Weave (Alturiak 20), Highcoin (Ches 30), Spheres (Tarsakh 10), Sammardach (Mirtul 12), Brightbuckle (Kythorn 21), Huldark (Eleasis 17), Spryndalstar (Eleint 7), Marthoon (Marpenoth 1), Tehennteahan (Uktar 10) and Orbar (Nightal 24 and 25). Two fall inside the campaign window, Highcoin on the last day of Fleetswake and Spheres on the same day as Leiruin (derived from the festival calendar). No campaign file uses either. The wiki's 5e row gives no holy days.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (2e and 3e sources).
  - Her closest ally is **Lliira**, who held Waukeen's power and portfolio in trust during the Time of Troubles. When Lliira answered the prayers of Waukeen's followers during the long absence, many of them assumed their goddess was dead and changed allegiance. Waukeen came back jealous and upset about the faithful who did not return, and the friendship soured slightly (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Running the Realms*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - She was one of the first gods to support **Gond** and his inventions when most of the others doubted them. **Shaundakul** is an ally because his care for long-range traders and caravans overlaps with hers (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - Her one real enemy among the Faerûnian gods is **Mask**. She also nurses an unending hatred and a wish for revenge against the demon lord **Graz'zt**, who broke a deal with her and held her captive (*Faiths & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Avatars*; *Player's Guide to Faerûn*).
  - In the 3e books her realm shares Brightwater with quarters held by Lliira, **Tymora**, **Sune** and Sharess (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*; *Player's Guide to Faerûn*).
- **Leiruin and Leira (*Dragon Heist*):** In times long past, Waukeen caught **Leira**, the goddess of illusions and deception, trying to cheat her in a deal. She buried Leira under a mountain of molten gold as punishment, and Leiruin commemorates it.
- **Her realm:** FRHoF gives the Outlands. The wiki places her in the Marketplace Eternal, a bazaar that covers many square miles. It lies in the Outlands in the 1e and 2e books, in Brightwater in 3e, and around **Amaunator**'s Palace of the Four Suns on the Eternal Sun after the Spellplague in 4e.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book named on each fact.
  - She joined the Faerûnian pantheon late, sometime after Tyr's arrival in -247 DR, and her popularity grew with the expansion of trade and the merchant class. Her church became wealthy and admired for its generosity, risk-taking and business sense (*Cyclopedia of the Realms*; *The Grand History of the Realms*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - When Lord Ao cast the gods out as mortal avatars in the Time of Troubles (1358 DR), she met Lliira and the two tried to bribe **Helm** to let them up the Celestial Stairway at Shadowdale. Helm refused, so Waukeen set out to smuggle herself home through the Astral Plane and the Abyss, with Lliira holding her divinity until she returned (*Faiths & Avatars*; *For Duty & Deity*).
  - Graz'zt agreed to give her passage and then broke the deal and imprisoned her in the Abyss. After the Godswar the Waukeenar kept only first- and second-level spells, and by 1365 DR Lliira had stepped in as regent for the portfolio. Many of the faithful took that to mean Waukeen was dead (*Forgotten Realms Adventures*; *Running the Realms*; *Faiths & Avatars*).
  - A band of adventurers rescued her from the Abyss (*For Duty & Deity*). She announced her return on Nightal 24, 1370 DR, and the date became another festival of her church.
  - The church spent years recovering from its losses, a period the wiki calls the Interdeium of Waukeen. The Harlot's Coin Heresy, spread by the League of Six-Fingered Gentlemen inside the hierarchy, taught that she had sold much of her divinity to Graz'zt and was now a figurehead (*Dragon* 355).
- **In the campaign:** Waukeen is alive and present in 1492 DR. The imprisonment, the regency and the heresy are history, and no file treats her as missing.

> **[GM]**

> #### Using Waukeen as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as the patron of the market. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is the Coinmaiden, the goddess of trade and wealth, and that shopkeepers, caravan guides, moneychangers and a good many smugglers pray to her. They add that she smiles on the hard-working and the free spender and frowns on misers, that her followers give a tenth of their profits to a struggling business instead of the temple plate, and that she once buried Leira under a mountain of molten gold for cheating her in a deal. Every Tarsakh the city keeps Waukeentide in her honor with gifts, all-night sales and guild galas. How Waterdhavians feel about her stand against monopolies, in a city whose eighteen guilds control every significant trade, is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Waukeen has no temple or shrine in the city in any source, and she has two festivals.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Waukeen row. The same table lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple for all deities, which includes her.
>   - The Castle Ward gazetteer names no temple of hers, and the wiki's temple sentence names no Waterdeep site. Whether any shrine or Goldeye exists in the city is [research].
>   - No source places her in the Trades Ward, where most of the guild halls stand, and *Dragon Heist* links her to the guilds only through the festival.
>   - Waukeentide (Tarsakh 1 to 10) is the city's festival for her. On Caravance, parents hide gifts in the house and tell their children that Old Carvas left them, a mythical peddler who arrived with the first caravan to reach Waterdeep. On Goldenight, celebrants decorate themselves with gold dust and wear coins as jewelry. The Guildsmeet gala runs from dusk to dawn and overruns the Market, the Cynosure and the Field of Triumph.
>   - Sornyn (Flamerule 3 to 5) is shared with Lathander. It falls in the ten days between Founders' Day and Flamerule 11.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Knowledge and Trickery (SCAG, 2014). Trickery Domain is the closer 2024 subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War (derived), because it matches one of her two domains and the smugglers and fences among her followers. The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Knowledge subclass. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), since the wiki has Goldeyes guarding caravans and temples. A cleric of hers in Waterdeep finds no temple to join, and the sources place no Goldeye in the city.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived), as a loose fit. The oath of honest dealing suits a follower who keeps every bargain, but FRHoF lists no paladins among her followers, and her church lets the ends justify the means (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources. The Harlot's Coin Heresy says she sold much of her divinity to **Graz'zt**, who is a patron on this roster, but the wiki calls it a heresy and never presents Waukeen as a patron.
> - **Campaign ties:** Waukeentide, Sornyn and three published characters.
>   - Waukeentide gives the Cassalanters the public galas that make up the entry points of **Cassalanter Villa**. The details are in the GM Notes below.
>   - Sornyn falls between Founders' Day and the Cassalanter twins' ninth birthday, and the festival calendar uses it as cover for envoys and strangers.
>   - In *Dragon Heist*, Obaya Uday, a neutral good Chultan human priest of Waukeen, is one of the Yawning Portal's Familiar Faces. She has come from Port Nyanzaru to sponsor Undermountain expeditions for her employer, the merchant prince Wakanga O'tamu. *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* has her pay in platinum for unwanted magic items.
>   - In the same book, Halleth Garke, a half-elf cleric of Waukeen, is a revenant pacing in the pit on Level 1, area 37. Midna Tauberth, a priest of Shar, wears his stolen gold-coin symbol of Waukeen on Level 2, area 11. Undermountain is future work, so treat all three as seeds.
>   - No campaign NPC worships her. The moneylender Istrid Horn worships Vergadain, and the banker Victoro Cassalanter is publicly a worshiper of Lathander.

## Overview

Waukeen, the Coinmaiden, is the goddess of trade and wealth, and merchants, caravan guides, moneychangers and smugglers all pray to her. Her faithful hold that open competition for wealth helps a society prosper, and they oppose guilds that form monopolies. Followers give a tenth of their profits to struggling businesses and new ventures instead of to a temple. Waterdeep honors her each Tarsakh with Waukeentide, a tenday of gift-giving, all-night sales and guild galas.

> #### GM Notes
> - **Waukeentide and Cassalanter Villa:** The Cassalanters host three events in the tenday, and the Cassalanter Villa structure document uses all three as windows. Caravance (Tarsakh 1) is the holiday on which the family gives most of its staff the night off. Goldenight (Tarsakh 5) is a gala for 40 to 60 guests. The Waukeentide Charity Ball (Tarsakh 7) is a fundraiser for Field Ward reconstruction and the main social entry point. The festival calendar lists the same three dates as the villa's entry points. Staffing, rosters and the checks are in **Cassalanter Villa**, not here.
> - **Date overlap (derived):** The structure document calls the Tarsakh 7 event the Charity Ball, and *Dragon Heist* gives Tarsakh 7 to Guildsmeet. No file says the ball is the Guildsmeet gala, so treat them as two events on one night.
> - **Cover:** Goldenight and Guildsmeet put crowds in the Cynosure and the Field of Triumph, and any surveillance of the villa during Waukeentide has cover (festival calendar).
> - **No Chosen, no intervention:** No file has Waukeen appear or speak, and none names a Chosen of hers. Her reach in the story is the festival calendar.

**Tone:** Brisk and good-humored. Waukeen is a goddess of open markets, so describe what trade offers before what it costs, and keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she speaks.
