# Helm

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Helm, god of watchfulness, and the reference for the faith practiced at the Temple of Helm in the Castle Ward.
> - **Where it is used:** Helm never appears in person. His temple is one of the three temples the Order of the Gauntlet treats as friendly, and it supplies healing, a hospice and briefings to Order members.
> - **Featured in:** **Trollskull Alley** (the Order's recruitment table) and the Order of the Gauntlet events: **Order of the Gauntlet First Meeting**, **Spined Devils in Twelvedog Court**, **The Tithe**, **Marcheon**, **Whitehawk** and **Righteous Hand**.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Helm is secret, and the temple's pattern lists never name the Cassalanters.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Helm, and lists the temple, the Order of the Gauntlet benefits and the events the campaign has tied to him so that no later page invents more.

*The Watcher*

- **Title:** God of Watchfulness (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** He of the Unsleeping Eyes, the Vigilant (FRHoF); the Vigilant One (SCAG, 2014); the Great Guard, God of Guardians (wiki, 2e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Vigilance, protection, guardianship, keeping one's word (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Protection, vigilance, watchfulness" and no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Life, Light, Twilight (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A staring eye on an upright left gauntlet (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Mechanus (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Explorers, fighters, guards, paladins (FRHoF)

> Stay vigilant, and keep your word.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Helm is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki ranks him as a lesser or intermediate deity in its 1e to 3e rows and as a plain deity in its 5e rows.
- **What he stands for:** Helm is a god of vigilance and protection, the guardian, watcher and sentinel. He embodies watchfulness without regard for good or evil. In legends he is honorable and keeps his word to a fault (FRHoF).
- **What the faith teaches:** His priests tell followers to be vigilant, aware and ready for their enemies, and they hold that patience, clear thought and careful planning always beat rushed action. The faithful try to be alert, clearheaded and true to their word. None of this makes them pleasant company, and many people think them inflexible and merciless (FRHoF).
- **Who follows:** Anyone who makes a living protecting someone or something favors him, including bodyguards, members of the city watch and the guards of a treasury vault (SCAG, 2014). The wiki's 3e row adds mercenaries.
- **Everwatch Knights:** This mercenary knighthood is formed by Helm's faithful. It sells his vigilance for a fee, and the income supports the wider faith. Clerics, fighters and paladins fit among the knights, and so do bards, rangers and rogues. Recruits are called seekers and learn from many knights in turn. A seeker must have a reputation for upholding contracts and must defend themself in an armed encounter with enemies of Helm, then hold a vigil at a post for a day with only water to drink. The knights keep a loose rank structure, train in stealth and infiltration, and can supply a Knight or a Spy to defend a charge or gather information (FRHoF). No file places the Everwatch Knights in Waterdeep.
- **Other orders:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Avatars* and *Faiths & Pantheons*, 2e). The paladin order called the Vigilant Eyes of the God, the Companions of the One True Vision (shock troops, in some disgrace after the Maztican campaign) and the Watchers over the Fallen (battlefield healers). The hierarchy was strict and military, and no one had held the post of Supreme Watcher since 992 DR.
- **Chosen:** Helm's Chosen are called Sentinels. They come from any walk of life, and he picks those who stand against overwhelming peril for the sake of others (FRHoF). No campaign file names a Chosen of Helm.
- **How he appears:** As a towering figure in full plate armor with a concealing visor. When he speaks to mortals he arrives as a massive armored figure or sends a planetar with his word (FRHoF). The wiki gives his height as fourteen to twenty feet (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy day:** The wiki names Shieldmeet, the leap day after Midsummer, when his faithful held the Ceremony of Honor to Helm (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; the 3e row agrees). The FRHoF and 5e rows are blank. The campaign calendar treats 1492 DR as a Shieldmeet year (derived), and no file ties a city observance to Helm.
- **Realm:** The wiki puts his realm, Everwatch, in the House of the Triad in its 3e row. It is a watchtower that circles the four mountains there and is manned by a guardian in mithral armor who reads the alignment and purpose of every visitor. FRHoF gives his plane as Mechanus, and so does the wiki's 2e row.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 2e), with the Everwatch note from FRHoF.
  - Torm is his one full ally, though the two clergies are bitter rivals, and the Everwatch Knights keep a close alliance with several Torm-worshiping organizations.
  - He opposes powers whose plots threaten the people and stability of Faerûn, especially Bane, Cyric, Mask and Shar. He is also at odds with Garagos, Malar and Talos over their uncontrolled violence and careless destruction.
  - Lathander's Dawn Cataclysm killed Murdane, a lesser god of pragmatism who was Helm's lover, and Helm has held it against the Morninglord since.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He is a very old god. In 1358 DR, during the Time of Troubles, Lord Ao left him his full powers and set him to guard the Celestial Stairway, so that no god could return to the heavens without the stolen Tablets of Fate (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; FRHoF and SCAG agree on the guard duty).
  - When Mystra tried to force her way past him that Midsummer, he destroyed her in an explosion above Castle Kilgrave, north of Arabel (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Gods and mortals alike held him in great contempt afterward, though his following stayed strong in the south.
  - After the Time of Troubles his worship faltered. It suffered further when his priests helped conquer Maztica (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - In 1384 DR he carried messages between Tyr and Tymora while the two courted. A misunderstanding led Tyr to accuse him of stealing Tymora's heart, and Tyr killed him in a duel. Cyric is suspected of arranging it (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e; FRHoF), and *Dragon* 381 (4e) states that he did.
  - His worship never faded. His followers believed the Watcher could not be vanquished, and his return after the Second Sundering proved them right (FRHoF). The wiki dates his reappearance to Nightal 1486 DR, before his Chosen Kleef Kenric (*The Sentinel*, novel).
- **In the campaign:** Helm is alive and present in 1492 DR. His temple is a working temple, his healers treat the poor, and no file treats him as dead or absent. The duel is history (see Draft Notes).

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Using Helm as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god of the guard post. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Watcher, a giant in full plate with his face hidden behind a visor, whose eyes never close. They add that the City Watch, bodyguards and vault guards favor him, that his faithful keep their word to the letter, and that they are hard people who will not bend. The old story they tell is that he held the stairway to the heavens when the gods walked the world and would not let them past. A Watch veteran adds that the Temple of Helm sees to the Watch's poor and their old soldiers. Nobody in this conversation says anything about the Everwatch Knights or a Chosen.
> - **Waterdeep:** Helm has one temple in the campaign, the Temple of Helm in the Castle Ward.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Helm, Helm's Hall, Southern Ward". The campaign's Order of the Gauntlet files put the Temple of Helm in the Castle Ward, so this page follows the campaign file. The page treats "Helm's Hall" as the published name for the same temple, though no file says so (see Draft Notes).
>   - Its healers treat the Watch's poor for free. It runs a hospice wing for Watch veterans and their families, and the hospice takes donations for its healing work.
>   - It is one of the three temples that give Order of the Gauntlet members healing, a bed and briefings. A member names Savra to its door-warden to be seen. No file names a priest or a door-warden of the Temple of Helm.
>   - Gods' Day on Marpenoth 15 marks the end of the Godswar in 1358 DR. That night the Griffon Cavalry lights a great bonfire on the peak of Mount Waterdeep for the gods who appeared in the city, and Helm is one of those named. The holiday is also a semiofficial "Be Kind to the Guard and Watch Day".
>   - No other shrine or temple of Helm exists in the campaign files.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Life, Light and Twilight (SCAG, 2014). Life Domain is the closest 2024 subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War (derived). It matches the protective, healing work of his temple, and Light is a close second. Twilight has no 2024 Player's Handbook subclass, and its watchful, protective theme sits nearest to Life. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because his clergy stand guard in armor and defend those in their charge.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived). A paladin of Helm is honorable, true to their word and ready for their enemies, and FRHoF lists paladins among his typical followers. The fit is close but not exact. Helm is neutral on good and evil, so a Devotion paladin of Helm leans on the oath's honor and duty and gives less weight to its compassion.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** The Order of the Gauntlet and its temple benefits. The published *Waterdeep: Dragon Heist* says that "Any non-evil character can join the Order of the Gauntlet in Waterdeep. Clerics, monks, and paladins are especially welcome, particularly if they worship Helm, Torm, or Tyr." The Trollskull Alley table says only that "worship of Helm, Torm or Tyr only decides whom Savra addresses first". The details are in the GM Notes below.

## Overview

Helm is the lawful neutral god of watchfulness and protection. Guards, bodyguards and the City Watch favor him, and Waterdhavians picture him as a towering figure in full plate with his face hidden behind a visor. His priests teach patience, careful planning and keeping one's word, and many people find the faithful inflexible and merciless. The Temple of Helm in the Castle Ward treats the Watch's poor for free.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Recruitment:** Savra Belabranta speaks first to a candidate with a Cleric or Paladin class or an Acolyte, Guard or Soldier background. If none qualifies, she speaks first to a worshiper of Helm, Torm or Tyr, and after that she picks the candidate with the highest Wisdom score. Nobody is tested for class, alignment or faith at the door.
> - **Chevall benefit:** Savra vouches for a sworn member at any temple of Tyr, Helm or Torm in Waterdeep. Each temple casts *Cure Wounds* on the member once a day and gives a bed for up to three nights in a row. A member can walk between the three temples and take three castings in one day. The healing and the bed are free, and any stronger casting costs the usual temple rate.
> - **Marcheon briefings:** At Renown 3 the member can ask the door-warden of the Temple of Helm for a briefing, and a priest sees the member the same day. A temple gives one pattern a tenday, and a priest gives patterns and never names. The Temple of Helm's four patterns are watchers standing at doors the Watch never posted, two Field Ward watch-houses shuttered after dark for a tenday, a Trades Ward doorway that someone pays a beggar to watch, and guards hired by the night, in coin, for a warehouse that never needed them. None of the lists names the Cassalanters.
> - **Spined Devils in Twelvedog Court:** In the aftermath, a resident who was hurt is carried to the Temple of Helm.
> - **The Tithe:** The Temple of Helm is one of four causes a member can pay. Brother Hallin Ostrel, the Order's clerk, takes the coin for it, and Savra ranks none of the causes.
> - **Righteous Hand:** Isaldric Veyne invests the member, and the oath names "Tyr, Helm and Torm" as witnesses. A Righteous Hand is lodged free at any temple of Tyr, Helm or Torm for three nights a visit, with up to four companions. Outside Waterdeep, a town honors the writ when it has a temple of one of the three.
> - **Undermountain:** The published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* has no Helm content apart from items named for a helm. Undermountain is future work.

**Tone:** Steady and a little severe. Helm is a god of duty, so describe the post a worshiper keeps before the price of keeping it, and keep him at a distance. The campaign writes no scene in which he appears.
