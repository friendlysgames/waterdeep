# Tymora

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Tymora, the goddess of good fortune, and the reference for the faith practiced at the Tower of Luck.
> - **Where it is used:** Tymora never appears in person. Her only tie to the campaign is Meloon Wardragon, a Force Grey fighter who serves her and whose luck runs out when an intellect devourer takes him over.
> - **Featured in:** **Trollskull Alley** (Meloon), and the Meloon events listed on his Notable Figures page. The Players' Guide names her among the visible houses of worship.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier and never mentions Meloon's occupation. The faith itself holds no secret.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Tymora, and states which temple, festival and NPC the campaign has tied to her so that no later page invents more.

*Lady Luck*

- **Title:** God of Good Fortune (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** Our Smiling Lady (FRHoF); Tyche's Fair-Haired Daughter (SCAG, 2014); Shalamora, the halfling name for her (wiki, 2e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Good fortune, luck, risk-taking, gambling, adventurers (derived). The wiki portfolio is good fortune, skill, victory and adventurers, and no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Trickery (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Face-up coin (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Arborea (Arvandor) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Gamblers, Harpers, merchants, rogues (FRHoF)

> Fortune favors the bold.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Tymora is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and the sister of **Beshaba**. The wiki calls her a god in its 4e and 5e rows and an intermediate deity in its 2e and 3e rows.
- **What she loves:** She is the patron of game players and gamblers. She loves those who gamble with skill and daring, and she watches over everyone who takes a risk to better their fortunes (FRHoF).
- **What the faith asks:** Her followers believe the good in their lives comes from both good luck and the bravery to seek it out (FRHoF). Her priests teach that a person is their own master and must be brave enough to accept good and bad fortune alike (wiki, 2e). A follower who keeps leaning on the goddess directly is looked on with disfavor, because that shows a lack of trust in her luck (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Who follows:** Gamblers, Harpers, merchants and rogues (FRHoF). FRHoF adds that she draws all sorts, from the dashing noble and the risk-taking merchant to the daydreaming field hand and the scheming ne'er-do-well. The wiki's 5e row lists gamblers, game-players and adventurers, and its 2e and 3e rows add that halflings, and halfling adventurers in particular, favor her.
- **Tiefling visions:** By 1489 DR some tiefling followers claimed visions of her in their own image, a dark and devilish lady of fortune. They took it to mean that the heart and soul matter more to the gods than appearance or bloodline (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 2014, as the wiki reports it).
- **Prayer and divination:** Folk pray to her before any endeavor in which a little good luck would help. A common way to read the future is to toss a coin to a stranger and ask if it is heads. If it is, the coin stays with the stranger as payment for her favor, and if it is not, the stranger may keep the coin and its bad luck or hand it back (FRHoF).
- **Praying to both sisters:** People do not pray to Tymora when bad luck may be coming. They pray to Beshaba to be spared, and praying to both is thought to anger both goddesses (SCAG, 2014).
- **Offerings:** A common offering prayer runs "A copper to the Lady returns tenfold in gold," and a priest answers "Dare much, and the Lady keep you" (wiki, *Midnight's Mask*).
- **Temples and clergy:** FRHoF says priests and temples are scarce, since her faith does not stress intermediaries, and quotes the saying "Let the lucky man and the Smiling Lady suss it out." Shrines in gambling parlors are not unusual, and such a place may attract a priest and become a temple in practice (FRHoF). The wiki's 3e sources have her shrines as widespread as adventurers' need for healing, which made the temples wealthy.
- **Priesthood:** Her temples are independent, with little overall hierarchy, and each reflects the tastes of its high priest or priestess. The clergy carry a disk of Tymora on a small chain and dress in blue and silver. They are duty-bound to aid the daring with healing and minor magic items (wiki, 2e). In Cormyr her priests are called Luckbringers (*Dungeon* 198).
- **Chosen:** Her Chosen are infused with extraordinary luck, and some never realize they are Chosen and put their luck down to chance. She picks those who dive boldly into action and risk (FRHoF).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. *Dragon Heist* calls Meloon Wardragon the champion of Tymora and says his friends find him extraordinarily lucky, but no file calls him a Chosen.
- **How she appears:** As a tall, graceful young woman with flowing platinum-blond or silver hair (FRHoF). Halflings picture her as a female halfling rogue (wiki, 2e). The bronze statue at the Tower of Luck shows a laughing young girl.
- **Messengers and manifestations:** She may send a faerie dragon as an emissary (FRHoF). The wiki adds a silver pegasus, a silver bird or a silver glow (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Signs of her favor in Waterdeep:** *Dragon Heist* gives two. Landing a coin in the outstretched hand of the statue at the Tower of Luck is a sure sign of her favor. On Nightal 20, glimpsing your star through cloud is thought to be her blessing. No source gives an omen outside those two. [research]
- **Holy days:** The wiki gives Midsummer and Starfall (2e, 3e and 5e rows; the 4e row is blank). The campaign calendar has Midsummer as a festival day between Flamerule 30 and Eleasis 1, and it does not name Starfall or tie either day to her. The date of Starfall is [research].
- **The Fateful Coin:** Old tales say that when a child is born, Tymora flips a coin made from the remains of Tyche, and Beshaba calls it in the air. If Beshaba is right the child is cursed with misfortune for life, and if she is wrong Lady Luck smiles on the child. A rare few see the coin land on its edge, and they can forge their own fates (wiki, *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (2e and 3e sources, with the 1384 DR account from 4e).
  - Beshaba is her twin and fiercest foe, and the two have fought since they were born from Tyche's husk. She is also hostile to Bane and to Moander.
  - Selûne is her oldest friend, from this life and from the one before, when Tymora was Tyche. Lathander, Lliira, Sune, Shaundakul, Azuth and Garl Glittergold are among her allies, as are Brandobaris, Erevan Ilesere and Finder Wyvernspur.
  - She holds no malice toward Helm and Tyr, though she plays pranks on the staid gods.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - The Netherese revered Tyche, a god of luck who ruled good and bad fortune together. Tyche perished, and Tymora and Beshaba manifested soon after, each commanding half of her portfolio (FRHoF).
  - In the Dawn Cataclysm, Tyche picked a rose that was a trap set by Moander, and his corruption began to rot her from within. Selûne loosed a bolt of purifying light to save her, and Tyche split into Tymora and Beshaba (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; SCAG, 2014).
  - In 1358 DR, during the Time of Troubles, her avatar appeared in Suzail and went on to the Lady's House in Arabel. Her presence there is thought to have spared Cormyr the worst of the chaos (*Forgotten Realms Adventures*; *Dungeon* 198).
  - In 1384 DR Sune urged her and Tyr to court, to restore balance after Siamorphe left the House of the Triad. Tyr came to believe, some say through Cyric's scheming, that Helm was courting her behind his back. He challenged Helm and slew him in a duel. Tymora left her realm in Brightwater, and Selûne took her in at the Gates of the Moon (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e). Helm returned after the Second Sundering, and his page tells the same story.
- **Realm:** FRHoF gives her plane as Arborea (Arvandor). The wiki puts her realm in Brightwater before 1384 DR, in the casino palace called the Hall of Chance, and in the Gates of the Moon afterward (3e and 4e).
- **In the campaign:** Tymora is alive and present in 1492 DR, and her temple in the city is a working temple. No file treats the 1384 DR quarrel as anything but history.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Using Tymora as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as the friendly goddess of the gamble. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is Lady Luck, who smiles on anyone bold enough to take a chance. They add that you toss a copper to a beggar before a risky job, and that a coin landed in the hand of the statue at the Tower of Luck is a good sign. The sage knows she and Beshaba came from one goddess of luck, and warns that you do not pray to both. In Waterdeep she is the temple "popular with adventurers and criminals alike" (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:36`), and *Dragon Heist* closes its warning about Three Daggers Alley in the Dock Ward with "don't test Tymora's favor." Nobody in this conversation mentions Meloon.
> - **Waterdeep:** Tymora has one temple in the city, the Tower of Luck in the Sea Ward.
>   - Every placement found: the Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Tymora, Tower of Luck, Castle Ward" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30983-30985`). The Dragon Heist Sea Ward text describes the Tower of Luck inside the Sea Ward section (`:30882`) and puts the Shrines to Nature "just a block away" (`:30884`), which the table also places in the Sea Ward. `campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:36` lists it among the Sea Ward's major temples, and `campaign/gazetteer/waterdeep/castle-ward.md:165` and `:240` place it in the Sea Ward. The retired `campaign/structure/ch2-city-of-splendors.md:56` agrees.
>   - This page follows the Sea Ward. The campaign files win over the table, and the published Sea Ward text agrees with them. The conflict is in Draft Notes for the hub.
>   - The temple is a complex. The "tower" is a many-pillared atrium roofed over with glass. Under the roof, a bronze statue of a small, laughing Tymora leaps from the top of a fountain. Visitors come around the fountain on a walkway and toss a coin to her, and landing it in her outstretched hand is a sure sign of her favor.
>   - The Players' Guide tells new characters that "Tyr and Tymora both keep visible, active houses of worship" (`campaign/guides/players-guide/character-creation.md:44`, in the Acolyte background row).
>   - No file names a priest of the Tower of Luck. No other shrine or temple of Tymora exists in the campaign files, and FRHoF says shrines in gambling parlors are not unusual, so whether a Waterdeep gambling house keeps one is [research].
>   - The campaign calendar names no festival of hers. *Dragon Heist* mentions her in its Nightal 20 entry, Simril, as the source of the blessing in a star seen through cloud, though the campaign calendar does not name Simril. [research]
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Trickery (SCAG, 2014). Trickery Domain is the 2024 subclass (derived), a direct fit by name. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because her priests work small blessings of luck and aid, and the faith has no tradition of armored guardians. Protector suits a cleric who follows her priests' duty to heal the daring in the field.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Glory (derived). Daring deeds and heroic risk match her creed, but the fit is loose. FRHoF does not list paladins among her followers, and no 2024 oath concerns luck.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** Meloon Wardragon is the only one. *Dragon Heist* says he "serves the goddess Tymora and loves a good fight", and his page says the real Meloon wants to "honor Tymora through brave action". The details are in the GM Notes below. No faction or quest names her, and the Players' Guide line is the only other campaign mention.
> - **Never reveal:** That an intellect devourer from Xanathar's Guild has occupied Meloon Wardragon. A player who asks about Tymora gets the Overview, and the party's own evidence is the only thing that adds to it.

## Overview

Tymora is the chaotic good goddess of good fortune, called Lady Luck and pictured as a tall, graceful young woman with silver hair. Gamblers, rogues, merchants and adventurers pray to her before any venture where a little luck would help. Her temple in Waterdeep is the Tower of Luck in the Sea Ward, a glass-roofed atrium where visitors toss a coin to a bronze statue of her above a fountain. Her creed is that fortune favors the bold, and she is sister to Beshaba, the goddess of misfortune.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Meloon Wardragon:** A human fighter and Force Grey member who serves Tymora. His friends, among them Renaer Neverember and Vajra Safahr, call him honest, optimistic and extraordinarily lucky. *Dragon Heist* calls him the "champion of Tymora" and spends most of his page on how his luck ran out.
> - **The occupation:** An intellect devourer from Xanathar's Guild takes his body and reports to the Guild through a courier. In the campaign it takes him after the Field of Triumph, about three tendays before the first day of the watch. The party can force it out in **The Trouble with Meloon**, and the real Meloon then remembers the occupation as a long dream.
> - **At the table:** The voice notes list a toast to Tymora before every drink among Meloon's habits, and "Tymora's luck!" as his signature phrase. The Meloon page gives the faith no other role. Meloon's events are listed on his Notable Figures page.

**Tone:** Warm, quick and a little rueful. Tymora is a goddess of the daring, so describe the chance she offers before the loss that follows a bad throw, and let her worshipers carry the cheer. Keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she appears.
