> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Sune, the goddess of love and beauty, and the reference for the Temple of Beauty and the Grand Revel.
> - **Where it is used:** Sune never appears in person, and no campaign NPC worships her. She comes up through the Temple of Beauty in the Sea Ward and the Grand Revel on Alturiak 14, which falls before the campaign opens on Ches 1.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Sune, and states which temple and festival the sources tie to her so that no later page invents a priest, a shrine or a worshiper.

*Lady Firehair*

- **Title:** God of Love and Beauty (FRHoF). SCAG and the 2014 Player's Handbook print "Goddess of love and beauty", and every source uses "she".
- **Other Names:** The Lady of Love, the Princess of Passion (FRHoF); Firehair (wiki, citing 1e to 3e books); the Goddess of Beauty and Love, the Beautiful One (wiki, citing the novel *Crucible*). Her followers are Sunites (wiki).
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Beauty, love, passion, romance, the arts, the pleasures of the senses (derived from the FRHoF text and the wiki's portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Life, Light (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Face of a beautiful red-haired woman (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Arborea (Arvandor) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Artists, hedonists, lovers (FRHoF)

> Follow your heart to what is beautiful, and guard it from those who would tear it down.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Sune is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. FRHoF gives her no rank, and the wiki calls her a greater deity in its 2e, 3e and 4e rows.
- **What she embodies:** She is the goddess of beauty in every form, from sights and sounds to tastes, scents and the touch of silk. Her worshipers seek these pleasures because the experience of pleasure is itself an act of worship (FRHoF). Love and passion are part of her portfolio, and the wiki adds that she also governs the darker side of love, such as obsession and tragedy, which her faith keeps out of public view so that the church stays welcoming (a 2014 forum answer by Ed Greenwood, cited by the wiki).
- **What the faith holds:** Beauty is more than skin deep. It comes from the core of one's being and shows one's true face to the world. Sunites believe in romance, in true love winning over all, and in following the heart to its destination, and fated matches, impossible loves and beauty beyond the physical are all hers (FRHoF).
- **Reputation:** Her followers are known as hedonists. They answer that her priests make beauty, by creating art, by acting as patrons for promising talents, and by investing in merchants who bring satin and fine wine to places that have never seen them (SCAG, 2014).
- **Clergy:** Her priests count loveliness among their greatest callings, and they train in comportment, fashion and cosmetics (FRHoF). SCAG (2014) adds that many take pride in presenting themselves as stunning examples of either gender.
- **Temples and shrines:** A temple serves as a public bath and a place of relaxation, with a mirrored and well-lit salon where visitors can improve their appearance and see and be seen. Where a temple is far away, small roofed shrines hold a mirror and a stock of free perfumes and cosmetics (FRHoF). SCAG (2014) says temples are common in human lands and that a big city often has such a shrine near a street corner.
- **The Sodality of the Ruby Rose:** A knightly order dedicated to Sune. Anyone who wants to protect beauty and oppose those who would tear it down finds acceptance in it (FRHoF).
  - The Sodality guards her temples and holy sites and accompanies her priests and anyone questing on her behalf.
  - A Hopeful who has protected a meaningful aspect of beauty from something that would destroy it keeps vigil for one night in a temple of Sune. If she grants a vision or another boon, the Hopeful becomes a Sodalis, also called a Companion.
  - A Companion receives an enameled red rose brooch, and members of special distinction wear brooches with ruby petals. Companions live at a Comfortable lifestyle for free in the holdings of Sune's faithful and can requisition a Mage or a Priest of Sune.
  - The order occasionally clashes with the faithful of Talona, Talos, Tempus and Umberlee (FRHoF). The wiki names the order the Sisters and Brothers of the Ruby Rose and says its members are bards, fighters and paladins (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Sune. FRHoF says her Chosen exemplify some aspect of beauty, whether physical loveliness or art, music and writing.
- **How she appears:** As a woman of unearthly beauty. Whatever features she takes on, her hair is always impossibly long and fiery red (FRHoF).
- **Messengers:** She speaks in dreams and visions. Otherwise she sends a couatl or a deva to act as a muse on her behalf (FRHoF).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** The wiki lists Greengrass and Midsummer night (3e and 2e) and leaves the 5e row blank. *Dragon Heist* gives one festival.
  - The Grand Revel falls on Alturiak 14 and is led by the clergy of Sune, Sharess and Lliira. It is a day of dancing, music and sweet treats, from chocolate to red firemint candies. Some of the dancing is wanton and performed for show, and large ring dances in the street for all ages are also popular.
  - The dancing ends at dusk, and bards and minstrels then perform at "love feasts" for families. Couples, and those who want to become couples, slip away to kiss, exchange promises and trade small tokens, often rings blessed by clergy with prayers of faithfulness.
  - Alturiak 14 falls before the campaign opens on Ches 1 (derived from the festival calendar). Greengrass falls between Tarsakh 30 and Mirtul 1, inside the campaign's span, and Midsummer falls after Flamerule 30, past its end on Flamerule 11. No file ties Sune to either.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (2e, 3e and 4e sources).
  - Her allies are **Milil**, **Lathander** and **Selûne**. **Lliira** and Sharess serve her (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Selûne once served her too before going her own way, and the two remain friendly (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - She aided **Mystra** against **Shar** and the Shadow Weave, and Shar counts her an enemy because she saved Sharess from being subsumed (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - She has no true enemies, since it is hard to stay angry at her for long. Her foes are the Gods of Fury, whose functions destroy beautiful things, and she limits the damage from the rages of their leader **Talos** (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). **Umberlee** envies her beauty (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). She has no love for **Talona** or **Tempus**, though Tempus thinks her too flighty to be worth the dislike (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Myths link her romantically with nearly every god except Talos, Umberlee, **Malar**, **Auril**, Tempus and Talona (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). **Torm** won her fancy through his actions in the Time of Troubles, and Torm and **Amaunator** were both smitten with her in turn (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). The wiki calls her distant and flirtatious in such matters.
- **Realm:** FRHoF gives Arborea (Arvandor). The wiki puts her in Brightwater in Arborea, where her quarter is the Heartfire Quarter, a district of small rooms and hidden courtyards (2e and 3e). In 4e her dominion is the Gates of the Moon.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact. The wiki gives no account of how she became a goddess. [research] Her history is told mostly in myths of her romances (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - In the Time of Troubles she rescued Sharess from death at Shar's hands and restored her to the state she had before Shar's influence (*Powers & Pantheons*).
  - In the 3e books she shares Brightwater with **Tymora** and **Lliira**, and later with Sharess and **Waukeen** (*Player's Guide to Faerûn*).
  - In 1384 DR Siamorphe quarreled with **Tyr** and left the House of the Triad to make her realm with Sune in Brightwater. Tyr sent **Helm** there as his messenger, and Sune proposed a marriage between Tyr and Tymora as a way to restore balance. Tyr agreed and began courting Tymora (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e).
  - Rumor said Helm loved Tymora, and Tyr challenged him to a duel and killed him. Tymora left Brightwater, and **Ilmater** left the Triad to join Sune there at her invitation (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e). Helm returned after the Second Sundering, and his page tells the same story.
  - In 1385 DR, after the apparent death of Mystra, she and Tyr, with Lathander's help, imprisoned **Cyric** for a thousand years (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - During the Spellplague her plane of Brightwater was destroyed, and Selûne invited her and her exarchs into the Gates of the Moon. The wiki's realm section says instead that Sune moved Brightwater to Selûne's realm (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). In the same period many lesser powers of love, Hanali Celanil among them, were revealed to be aspects of Sune.
- **In the campaign:** Sune is alive and present in 1492 DR. Her Temple of Beauty is a working temple, and no file treats her as absent or as subordinate to another power. The 4e destruction of Brightwater and the imprisonment of Cyric are history, and no scene uses them (see Draft Notes).

> #### Using Sune as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as the lady of the mirror and the bath. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is the goddess of love and beauty, shown as a woman with long red hair, and that her temple in the Sea Ward lets anyone bathe and primp before a night out. They add that her followers are called hedonists but that her priests make art and back young talents, and that beauty runs deeper than skin. Every Alturiak 14 her clergy lead the Grand Revel, with ring dances in the streets and love feasts at dusk. How Waterdhavians feel about her clergy outside the festival is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Sune has one temple in the city, the Temple of Beauty in the Sea Ward, and one festival.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Sune, Temple of Beauty, Sea Ward". The Sea Ward text calls it the place to refresh yourself on the road or to primp before an important meeting or a night out. Its marbled public baths and mirrored salons are open from before dawn to after dusk, and neither they nor the advice and aid of the temple's many pleasant attendants cost a fee, though donations are encouraged (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30883`).
>   - No campaign file names the temple, and there is no Sea Ward gazetteer page. The temple's high priest and clergy are unnamed in every source. [research]
>   - The Grand Revel (Alturiak 14) is led by the clergy of Sune, Sharess and Lliira, which implies that her clergy are in the city. It falls before the campaign opens. This page follows *Dragon Heist* for the date and the clergy. FRHoF puts a Grand Revel at Midsummer under Milil, and the **Milil** page says which it follows.
>   - A street-corner shrine with a mirror and free cosmetics is the general rule in SCAG (2014), and no source places one in Waterdeep. Whether the party can find one is [research].
>   - Holyhands House in the North Ward is a temple for all deities, which includes her. The wiki has no Waterdeep text for her.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Life and Light (SCAG, 2014). Life Domain (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is a close fit, because her faith cherishes living beauty and her priests foster it as artists and patrons. Light Domain (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the alternative, since it is her other 2014 domain. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because the Sodality of the Ruby Rose exists to guard beauty from those who would destroy it. A cleric of hers has the Temple of Beauty to join, though no source names its priests.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of the Ancients (derived; Player's Handbook 2024), a close fit. The oath's theme is to preserve life and light in the world, which matches a knightly order that exists to guard beauty from those who would destroy it. The wiki says the Ruby Rose includes paladins. FRHoF lists none among her typical followers.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources. Her messengers, the couatl and the deva, are celestials, so the Celestial patron (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the nearest fit for a player who wants one, though no source presents her as a patron.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. *Dragon Heist* gives the Temple of Beauty and the Grand Revel, and neither appears in a campaign file.

## Overview

Sune, called Lady Firehair, is the goddess of love and beauty, and she is pictured as a woman with impossibly long red hair. Her followers say beauty comes from within, and they honor her through romance, art and the pleasures of the senses. Her Temple of Beauty in the Sea Ward keeps public baths and mirrored salons, and visitors use them free of charge. Her clergy also lead the Grand Revel on Alturiak 14, a day of dancing and sweets that ends in love feasts.

**Tone:** Warm and a little vain, in the brisk reference register. Sune is the goddess of beauty and love, so describe what the party can enjoy at her temple before what her faith asks, and keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she speaks.
