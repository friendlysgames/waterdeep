> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Bane, the god of tyranny, and the reference for the one Banite site in the campaign, the tower called Yellowspire in the Castle Ward.
> - **Where it is used:** Bane never appears in person. His faith in play is Amath Seccent and her four acolytes, who keep Yellowspire as a temple and a relay point for Manshoon's cell of the Zhentarim.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Bane, and records what the campaign has tied to him so that no later page invents more. His only Waterdeep temple belongs to a villain's cell, so this page keeps that site and its secrets together.

*The Black Hand*

- **Title:** God of Tyranny (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Black Hand, the Lord of Darkness (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014); the Black Lord, Lord Bane, the Dark One (wiki; *Cyclopedia of the Realms* and *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Tyranny, ambition, control, conquest (derived). The wiki's 5e portfolio is ambition, control and tyranny, and no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** War, Order (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** An upright black hand, thumb and fingers together (SCAG, 2014). The 2014 Player's Handbook and the wiki specify the right hand, and the handprints in the campaign are right hands.
- **Alignment:** Lawful Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Acheron, on the layer of Avalas (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Conquerors, fighters, monks, tyrants (FRHoF)

> The strong have the duty to rule the weak.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Bane is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and one of the Dead Three, with Bhaal and Myrkul. FRHoF says he was the first of the three to return and now holds a more prominent position than he did before his death. The wiki ranks him a greater deity in its 1e, 3e and 4e rows and a quasi-deity in its 5e row from *Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*.
- **What the faith teaches:** The strong have the duty to rule the weak. A tyrant who can seize power must do so, because the tyrant and the people under the tyrant both benefit. When a ruler grows decadent, corrupt or frail, a stronger and more suitable ruler will rise (FRHoF).
- **What the faith offers:** Bane stands for ambition and control, and people who have the first and lack the second pray to him for strength. He favors drive and courage, and he aids those who want to conquer, carve kingdoms out of the wilderness or bring order to the lawless. FRHoF says most of his worshipers do not act out of malice.
- **The record of the faith:** His faithful have been hailed as saviors for slaughtering raiders, toppling corrupt rulers and rescuing armies on the brink of defeat. In other places the same worship has created or supported cruel dictatorships and mercantile monopolies, and has subjugated whole populations (FRHoF).
- **Who follows:** Conquerors, fighters, monks and tyrants (FRHoF). The wiki adds blackguards and wizards (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Clergy:** Priests wear a black-enameled gauntlet on one fist (FRHoF). The church keeps a strict hierarchy from its most powerful worshipers to its weakest, and every member owes their blessings to the Black Lord. Priests pray for their spells at midnight and pledge eternal loyalty, and the penalty for failure or disloyalty is death (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
- **Customs and holy days:** Banite customs are spartan. The faithful keep no holidays for him, and the wiki's 2e and 3e rows list no holy days while its 5e row is blank. They show gratitude through service and through the ritual torture and sacrifice of sentient beings who offend him (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
- **Knights of the Black Gauntlet:** This military order of crusaders is based in Mintar on the shores of the Lake of Steam and ruled by the Lord Knight Imperceptor of Bane. Recruits join as soldiers whatever their class or background, and they rise by obedience, leadership and the loyalty or fear they inspire. A knight commands a contingent of soldiers, and many wear a black gauntlet on one hand. Members get a free Comfortable lifestyle at any temple of Bane or outpost of the order, and the order finds allies in the cults of Bhaal and Myrkul and among worshipers of Loviatar (FRHoF). No campaign file places the order in Waterdeep.
- **Chosen:** His Chosen become warlords or whispering advisers who tempt rulers to greater cruelty (FRHoF). The wiki names Fzoul Chembryl as his Chosen and the head of his reformed church after 1372 DR (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). Chosen in the campaign: none. The campaign history names Fzoul only as the Banite priest who joined Manshoon in 1263 DR.
- **How he appears:** Bane is marked by a jet-black right hand, which he hides under a gauntlet or glove when he wants to conceal himself. He comes to mortals in dreams as a shadowy armored figure wearing a black, jeweled gauntlet. His emissary is usually a horned devil, or a possessed knight with pitch-black eyes who remembers nothing of the visit afterward (FRHoF). The wiki adds a fiendish raven named Koravis (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e) and the manifestations of green or red flaming eyes in darkness, a black taloned hand with an icy touch, and possession (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Realm:** FRHoF gives his plane as Acheron, on the layer of Avalas. The wiki places his realm, the Black Bastion, on Avalas (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). In the 4e cosmology his plane is Banehold (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e), which 3e sources called the Barrens of Doom and Despair (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Seats of the faith:** Wiki-sourced. As of 1479 DR the church had its headquarters in the House of the Black Lord in Mulmaster, and it was the only church allowed in Thay (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). Other temples are the Lyceum of the Black Lord in Phlan, the Dark God Reformed in Voonlar and the Temple of Bane in Daggerdale. The wiki also lists a Black Altar and a Dark Shrine in Zhentil Keep, which the campaign history says the Shadovar razed in 1383 DR. FRHoF adds that cults of Bane are particularly active in Baldur's Gate, where noble patriar families are drawn by his promises of power.
- **Allies:** Wiki-sourced. His alliance with Myrkul reached back to their mortal days and lasted until both died in the Time of Troubles. He also had working relations with Loviatar, Talona and Mask (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e), and after his return in 1372 DR he rebuilt those alliances mainly by reminding them to fear him (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Malar sometimes worked beside him (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e), and the wiki says Asmodeus keeps a pact with him.
- **Servants:** Wiki-sourced. In his first life as a god his servants included Bhaal and his own son Iyachtu Xvim (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). In his second tenure they included Abbathor, Maglubiyet, Hruggek and Tiamat (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **Tiamat and Azharul:** Wiki-sourced from Tiamat's page. Tiamat served Bane reluctantly. After the Spellplague dissolved her realm, the wounded body of Azharul, her vessel, fell into his hands, and he learned what it was and enslaved it. She served him loyally despite his harassment and passed his secrets to Asmodeus. Within the decade she pulled Azharul out of Banehold and took much of his divine energy with it (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e; Ed Greenwood's 2015 Candlekeep answer).
- **Enemies:** Wiki-sourced. Since his return his greatest foes are Cyric, who drew many of his worshipers away, and the Triad, above all Torm, who killed him in the Time of Troubles. He was also enemies with Amaunator and Oghma, and in life with Helm and Midnight (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). The pages for Torm, Tyr, Helm, Oghma, Siamorphe, Tymora, Chauntea, Ilmater and Lathander each list him among the powers they oppose, and Mystra's page records that he captured her avatar.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Bane was once a human adventurer. Nothing records when he lived. He became known when he allied with two other mortals, Bhaal and Myrkul, and the three agreed to conquer the world and then the heavens (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - In -357 DR the three led an army drawn from three human nations, gnolls and other peoples of the Moonsea North. They killed the primordial Maram of the Great Spear at the Monument of the Ancients and divided its essence among them (*Dungeon* #170).
  - Jergal, the god of death, had grown tired of his crown and let them succeed. He tricked the three into turning on one another, then offered to divide his portfolio by a game of knucklebones. Bane won the sphere of strife (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - In 1358 DR Bane and Myrkul tried to steal the Tablets of Fate from Ao. Ao banished every god to walk Toril, which began the Time of Troubles (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). Waterdeep's own account in *Waterdeep: Dragon Heist* names Bane and Myrkul as the thieves, later joined by Bhaal.
  - Torm killed Bane on Eleasis 13, 1358 DR, near Tantras. Most of his power passed to the mortal Cyric, and a smaller share went to Xvim. At least a score of nations held festivals of thanks when the news spread (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Xvim was a cocoon for Bane's essence. At Midwinter in 1372 DR he burst open into Bane, destroying the younger god. Xvim's faithful, led by Fzoul Chembryl, converted to Bane overnight, and so did many of Cyric's (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - After his return Bane ended the infighting he had once encouraged and rebuilt the church under Fzoul (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - The Spellplague of 1385 DR removed two of his chief rivals, Mystra and Cyric, and in the century that followed he brought the goblinoid gods Maglubiyet and Hruggek to heel (*Dragon* #362; *The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - After the Second Sundering the wiki says he remained as a quasi-deity with Myrkul and Bhaal. He could still influence events across Faerûn but was essentially trapped in a mortal form (*Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Bane is a living power in 1492 DR. Amath Seccent serves him as a priest, and no file treats him as dead, absent or bound to a mortal body. The Sundering note is history and a conflict that this page does not use (see Draft Notes). The 4e god named Bane is a different god of war from the Nentir Vale (see Draft Notes).

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Using Bane as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the tyrant god of the Moonsea. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the god of tyranny, and that he and Myrkul stole the Tablets of Fate and brought on the Time of Troubles. They add that his priests wear a black gauntlet on one fist, that his church is strongest around the Moonsea, and that his faithful have been hailed as saviors in one land and cursed as tyrants in the next. A Gods' Day regular points out that the bonfire on Mount Waterdeep honors Myrkul, Cyric, Kelemvor, Mystra, Helm and Ao, and leaves him out. Nobody in this conversation links him to a tower in the Castle Ward.
> - **Waterdeep:** Bane has no public temple, no festival and no city shrine. The one site the campaign ties to him is Yellowspire, and it belongs to Manshoon's cell.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no row for Bane, the Castle Ward religion list in the gazetteer names none, and the wiki's list of his temples has no Waterdeep entry.
>   - Yellowspire is a tower of pale yellow brick in the Castle Ward. Amath Seccent, a priest of Bane, keeps it as a temple. The published adventure says she is in the midst of converting it (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:9741`), and the campaign's mission files already call it a temple of Bane. Her people call it the House of Tyranny, and the neighbors take it for a private home.
>   - Black prints of right hands, fingers together, cover every interior surface. The upper level was a wizard's laboratory, and now holds a candle-lit shrine, Amath's writing desk and a row of cots.
>   - Amath and her four acolytes pray on the upper level from dusk for about an hour. Candlelight fills the arrow slits and low chanting carries into the lane.
>   - A teleportation circle in the cellar leads to Kolat Towers. The GM Notes below hold the details.
>   - No other shrine or temple of Bane exists in the campaign files.
> - **A cleric PC:** Order Domain (derived; Tasha's Cauldron of Everything). Order is one of his two domains (SCAG, 2014), and it suits a church of strict hierarchy that teaches the strong to rule. War Domain (Player's Handbook, 2024) is his other domain and the alternative for a cleric of his soldiers and knights. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because his clergy are soldiers and knights who lead troops in armor. Bane is lawful evil, and no source places a public cleric of his in Waterdeep.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Conquest (derived; Xanathar's Guide to Everything). It is sworn by paladins who seek glory in battle and the subjugation of their enemies, which matches a faith that tells the strong to rule the weak and draws conquerors and fighters. FRHoF lists no paladins among his followers, but the Knights of the Black Gauntlet take recruits of any class. Oath of the Crown (derived; Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide) is the alternative for a knight who serves the order's hierarchy.
> - **A warlock PC:** Fiend Patron (derived; Player's Handbook, 2024). FRHoF says his emissary is usually a horned devil, and the wiki says Asmodeus keeps a pact with him. No source says Bane grants warlock pacts, so the fit is loose.
> - **Campaign ties:** Amath Seccent, her four acolytes and Yellowspire, and through them Manshoon's cell of the Zhentarim. The details are in the GM Notes below. The campaign history names Fzoul Chembryl as the Banite priest who joined Manshoon in 1263 DR, and the Asmodeus page records the wiki's pact between the two powers.
> - **Never reveal:** That Yellowspire is a Banite tower, that its cellar circle leads to Kolat Towers, or that Amath is allied with Manshoon. Until the party marks **Manshoon Named**, even the Doom Raiders say "Floxin's cell" or "the other cell" and call Kolat Towers "the towers in the Trades Ward". Before the party reaches the tower, a player who asks about Bane gets the Overview.

## Overview

Bane, the Black Hand, is the lawful evil god of tyranny. Waterdhavians remember him as one of the gods who stole the Tablets of Fate and brought on the Time of Troubles. His faith holds that the strong must rule the weak, and his priests wear a black gauntlet on one fist. Waterdeep has no public temple of Bane, and the Gods' Day bonfire does not name him.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Yellowspire and the Zhentarim split:** Amath is allied with Manshoon's cell, the splinter that peeled off the Doom Raiders' network. The Doom Raiders are the other Zhentarim cell and are at war with it. In **The Yellowspire Job** Davil Starsong sends a Doom Raiders member into the tower to steal a ledger of pass-amulet carriers. Davil tells the member that nobody above him has asked for the job and nobody above him will hear of it.
> - **What the tower is for:** Tashlyn Yafeera tells the Doom Raiders member that the Banites keep the place as a temple and a post office. For Manshoon's cell it is also a fallback position, and the cellar circle gives a route into Kolat Towers that skips the pass-amulet requirement. A pressure plate in the circle chamber alerts Kolat Towers. **Faction Outposts** runs the tower as a mini-heist, **Silencing Skeemo** ends at its door, and **Kolat Towers** can be entered through its circle.
> - **Amath Seccent (Lawful Evil, Human, she/her):** She is a priest of Bane in common clothes, composed and correct. She speaks in courteous, complete sentences, never raises her voice, and treats weakness with controlled contempt. She receives guests as though she were hosting a tea, and the handprints are the only thing in the tower she mentions with warmth. She treats her faith as a settled alignment with Bane, and it predates and outlasts any arrangement with the Zhentarim. If asked, she calls the tower the House of Tyranny and says the neighbors think it a private home, which she prefers.
> - **Her household:** Four acolytes serve her, and all five wear fur-lined robes inside and dress as ordinary neighbors outside. Three black flying snakes nest in the rafters. Agorn Fuoco (Neutral Evil, Human, he/him) visits in the evenings for company and is Amath's partner. He flees at the first sign of a fight.
> - **The hours:** From dawn to dusk and from the end of the prayer hour to midnight, Amath works at her desk upstairs. At the prayer hour all five are on the upper level. From midnight to dawn, Amath and all but one acolyte sleep on the cots upstairs.
> - **The turning rite:** In the published adventure the five hold a ritual to break the will of a captive broadsheet publisher, Shan Chien, so that he serves Bane. Amath uses it to turn publishers as part of Manshoon's plan to control the flow of information in the city (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:9749`). **The Yellowspire Job** does not use the rite or Shan Chien.
> - **Left to the quests:** The roster by party size, the approaches, the circle and the pressure plate are written in **The Yellowspire Job** and **Faction Outposts**, and this page adds nothing to them.

**Tone:** Grave and even. Bane is a god of order and ambition, so describe what he offers a person who feels they lack control before what his rule costs. Keep him at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he appears, and at Yellowspire let Amath's courtesy carry the menace.
