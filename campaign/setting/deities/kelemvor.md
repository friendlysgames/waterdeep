# Kelemvor

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Kelemvor, the god of the dead, who judges souls on the Fugue Plane, and the reference for the faith practiced in the City of the Dead.
> - **Where it is used:** Kelemvor never appears in person. His faith reaches the campaign through Sir Ambrose Everdawn, the knight who patrols the City of the Dead after dark and guards the way to the Brandath crypts.
> - **Featured in:** **Ten Nights in the City of the Dead** (an Emerald Enclave mission) and **Vault of Dragons** (the approach through the cemetery, still an unconverted structure doc). The Fugue Plane judgment is also the power that the priests of Asmodeus pitch against.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. The cause of the missing bones in **Ten Nights in the City of the Dead** sits in the GM Notes.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Kelemvor, and states exactly what the campaign has tied to him so that no later page invents a temple, a priest or a Chosen.

*The Judge of the Damned*

- **Title:** God of the Dead (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Lord of the Dead (FRHoF); the Judge (Sir Ambrose Everdawn); Kelemvor Lyonsbane, his mortal name, the Great Guide and Master of the Crystal Spire (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Death, the dead, the passage of souls to their afterlives, funeral rites, the prevention of untimely deaths and the destruction of undead (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Death, the dead" and no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Death, Grave (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Upright skeletal arm holding balanced scales (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Astral Plane (Fugue Plane) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Funeral workers, the dying (FRHoF)

> Treat death as a natural part of life, and prevent the untimely kind.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Kelemvor is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki ranks him a greater deity in its 2e, 3e, 4e and 5e rows.
- **What the faith teaches:** The FRHoF calls him "the just, fair, and comforting god of death" and says that "Death isn't a thing to be feared in Kelemvor's eyes, but a natural part of life that must be accepted in its time." His priests teach that those who revere the gods according to the rites of their own religion have done their service and will be offered the afterlife they seek (SCAG, 2014).
- **The Fugue Plane:** The FRHoF places him on the Astral Plane (Fugue Plane) and says that "When death occurs, Kelemvor takes a soul by the hand and leads it to the proper afterlife." Souls wait on the Fugue Plane for judgment, and the Asmodeus page records that the unjudged may wait centuries for Kelemvor to rule on them. That wait is the opening for the priests of Asmodeus, who offer a waiting soul comfort from a devil or a rank in the infernal host (FRHoF, Asmodeus entry).
- **How he judges:** The wiki says that faithful souls are claimed by their own deities, while the faithless and the false are judged before him. He is more lenient with those who were virtuous and honorable in life and harsher with the cowardly and the capricious (*Crucible: The Trial of Cyric the Mad*, novel).
- **His realm:** He rules from the Crystal Spire on the Fugue Plane. He rebuilt it from the Bone Castle, the citadel where Jergal, Myrkul and Cyric each ruled, and made it transparent so that death would no longer be a frightening mystery (*Crucible*, novel).
- **What the faith asks:** His followers must forestall or prevent untimely deaths whenever possible and eliminate the scourge of the undead (FRHoF). The wiki quotes his charge to novitiates: fear death, evade it or call it evil, and die with dignity, neither raging nor seeking undeath, and honor the dead because their strivings brought the Realms to where they are (*Faiths & Avatars*).
- **Clergy:** Priests provide peaceful transitions into death, sort the affairs of the dying, counsel bereaved mourners and officiate funeral rites. The most elite are called doomguides (FRHoF). They also hold funerals for people who cannot afford the lavish ceremonies of their own faith (SCAG, 2014). The wiki adds that his servants usually wear somber gray vestments and carry bastard swords (*Unclean*).
- **Orders outside the priesthood:** The FRHoF names three, and no file places any of them in Waterdeep.
  - The Vital Breath works to stop the spread of plagues and magical contagions.
  - The Judge's Vigil works to prevent murder.
  - The Knights of the Eternal Order search for and destroy powerful undead. Aspirants serve a knight as a squire, must destroy a dangerous undead to be knighted, and then swear an oath and receive black armor or a silver-trimmed black tabard marked with his symbol. A knight receives a free Comfortable lifestyle at any temple of Kelemvor and can call on a priest during a quest for the order. They ally with followers of Helm, Lathander and Mystra, and their chief foe is Cyric and his followers (FRHoF).
- **Enemies of the faith:** His followers are at odds with necromancers and priests of Myrkul. They routinely destroy writings on creating undead, which offends the faithful of Oghma and Deneir, and they seek the end of even good undead (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Chosen:** FRHoF says his Chosen embody the natural course of death, are optimistic and jubilant, and can resurrect the victims of improper deaths.
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Kelemvor.
- **Messengers:** He speaks to his worshipers in dreams, appearing as a silver-haired man in black hooded robes and a silver funeral mask, or he speaks through the body of a person who recently died (FRHoF).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** Shieldmeet and the Feast of the Moon (wiki, 2e and 4e; the 3e and 5e rows are blank). In 1492 DR Shieldmeet follows Flamerule 30, and the Feast of the Moon falls between Uktar 30 and Nightal 1, so both come after the campaign window.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced.
  - His greatest enemy is Cyric. He also opposes Velsharoon, the god of necromancers, and Talona, for the deaths her diseases cause (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - He is an ally of Lathander, who also opposes undeath (*Faiths & Pantheons*). The Mystra page records that the FRHoF counts him among her traditional allies.
  - Jergal, the first god of death, serves him by keeping records of the final disposition of the dead (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - As mortals, he and Midnight were lovers, and the relationship is unlikely to have continued after she became Mystra (*Faiths & Pantheons*). The *Crucible* novel ends it over the soul of Adon, her priest.
- **Origin and rise:** The adventure and the wiki disagree on how he became a god, so both accounts are given.
  - *Dragon Heist* (Waterdeep text): "It was on the slopes of Mount Waterdeep that Ao was last seen, when he granted godhood to the human heroes Kelemvor, Midnight (who became Mystra), and Cyric." The Time of Troubles began in 1358 DR, and the text places this at its end.
  - Wiki (*Prince of Lies*, novel): he was a mercenary with a paladin's heart under rude manners, Kelemvor Lyonsbane, burdened by a family curse. Bane removed the curse during the Time of Troubles in 1358 DR, and with Midnight and Adon he recovered the Tablets of Fate and brought them to Waterdeep (*Shadowdale*).
  - Wiki (*Shadowdale* and *Prince of Lies*, novels): Cyric killed him atop Blackstaff Tower with the sword Godsbane, which was Mask in disguise. His soul sat in the sword for ten years, until it escaped, led a revolt of the denizens of the City of the Dead, and defeated Cyric. The dead acclaimed him the new god of death, and the wiki dates his apotheosis to 1368 DR.
  - Wiki (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Faiths & Pantheons*): he is the most recent god of the dead, following Jergal, Myrkul and Cyric. He promoted the view that death is natural and should not be feared, unlike his predecessors.
  - Wiki (*Crucible*, novel): after his rise he set aside his human look for a darkly robed figure with silver hair and a silver death mask, and he re-evaluated every soul in the City of the Dead under new criteria.
- **In the campaign:** Kelemvor is present in 1492 DR. The wiki gives no death or abdication for him, and no file treats him as dead or absent.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Using Kelemvor as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the gentle judge at the end of every road. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Lord of the Dead and the Judge of the Damned, a god who takes each soul by the hand and leads it to the afterlife it has earned. They add that his priests do not want anyone to fear death, that his faithful will bury a pauper and say the rites for those who cannot pay, and that they hunt the undead and destroy books on raising them. A sage will tell you the city's own account, that Ao made him a god on the slopes of Mount Waterdeep at the end of the Time of Troubles, and that pilgrims still pay homage to him in the City of the Dead. Sir Ambrose Everdawn calls him "the Judge".
> - **Waterdeep:** Kelemvor has no temple and no shrine in the campaign files. The City of the Dead is where the city honors him.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30910-30997`) has no Kelemvor row. The Waterdeep text says that Waterdeep "has since attracted a steady stream of pilgrims who worship Midnight at Mystra's temple and pay homage to Kelemvor in the City of the Dead" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30484`).
>   - The City of the Dead is a public park dotted with mausoleums. It closes at night, with two City Guard soldiers at each entry gate, and hundreds of driftglobes gather there at dusk and leave at dawn for a reason no one knows (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:7765-7766`). The adventure gives no ward for it, and `campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:20` lists it as a walled necropolis apart from the wards.
>   - Sir Ambrose Everdawn, a knight of Kelemvor, patrols it from sunset to sunrise and chases off grave robbers (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:7767`). No file names a priest, a funeral house or a temple of Kelemvor in the city.
>   - On Gods' Day, Marpenoth 15, the Griffon Cavalry lights a bonfire on the peak of Mount Waterdeep "honoring the fallen and the risen gods Myrkul, Cyric, Kelemvor, Mystra, Helm, and Ao who appeared here" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31536`). The festival falls after the campaign window (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:111`).
>   - The campaign files give no temple of Kelemvor, so a Knight of the Eternal Order has nowhere in Waterdeep to claim the free lifestyle the FRHoF describes.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Death and Grave (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has neither, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is Life Domain (derived). The fit is loose. Grave's healing and protection against untimely death sit nearest to Life, and Light is a second fit for a cleric who hunts undead. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because his priests and knights stand between the dying or the dead and whatever threatens them.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of the Ancients (derived). SCAG calls undead "an abomination of the natural order", and the oath's duty to preserve life and the natural order matches that. The fit is loose. A paladin who hunts undead as a Knight of the Eternal Order leans toward Oath of Vengeance (derived), which suits the hunt but not his comforting side.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** Sir Ambrose Everdawn, **Ten Nights in the City of the Dead** and the **Vault of Dragons** approach, covered in the GM Notes below. The Notable Figures page says: "A silver gauntlet pin on his collar marks him as a knight of Kelemvor. He speaks in formal, complete sentences, never uses a contraction, and calls Kelemvor 'the Judge'." The mission's Overview says: "Bones are going missing from the City of the Dead, and a Kelemvorite knight needs the north patrolled for ten nights." The published adventure also hands the party a hook: "Sir Ambrose Everdawn, a grizzled old champion of Kelemvor, has offered to help the City Guard catch a necromancer who's stealing bones from the City of the Dead and animating them as skeletons." (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:3739`). The Asmodeus page names Kelemvor as the judge his priests pitch against. No campaign file puts the two in a scene.

## Overview

Kelemvor is the lawful neutral god of the dead, and Waterdhavians call him the Judge of the Damned. He takes each soul by the hand and leads it to its proper afterlife. His priests teach that death is a natural part of life and nothing to fear, and his followers bury the dying with care, fight untimely deaths and destroy the undead. Waterdeep has no temple of his, but pilgrims pay homage to him in the City of the Dead, where one of his knights patrols after dark.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Sir Ambrose Everdawn:** A Tethyrian Human knight of Kelemvor, about sixty, who has walked the City of the Dead for fifteen years. He uses the Knight stat block, is Lawful Neutral in the campaign files, never uses a contraction and does not swear on the grounds of the dead. He knows every family plot and the burial customs of the noble houses, and he does not offer what he knows of the Brandath crypts unasked. He draws his blade only in defense of the dead or the living, in that order.
> - **Ten Nights in the City of the Dead:** An Emerald Enclave mission for a member at Renown 3 and 3rd level, which awards no Milestone Points. Ambrose asks the Enclave for help because he can watch only half the grounds. He takes the south while the party walks the north for ten nights, and skeletons walk in on nights 4, 6 and 8. He stays out of those fights unless the party calls him. At the tenth dawn he names the Brandath crypts, unasked if the party reported the pattern and otherwise when asked and given a reason.
> - **What is actually true:** No necromancer is raising the dead. The Vault of Dragons lies under the Brandath grove and something in it presses against its wards, so the dead nearest the grove walk toward it. Ambrose knows only that the wrongness centers there, and nothing in the mission opens the Brandath doors.
> - **Vault of Dragons:** If a member marked **Brandath Crypts Visited**, Ambrose recognizes the party and waves it through. The unconverted structure doc (`campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:83`) decides the encounter in advance: a party that enters after dark meets him, and a plausible cover story (a Brandath-blood companion, a City Watch escort from Jalester's Mission 6, a writ from Laeral) gets a warning to be careful and be quick. A party that enters by day does not meet him, because his patrol is nocturnal.
> - **The god himself:** Kelemvor never appears, speaks or intervenes in any quest. The campaign has no Kelemvorite temple, priest, Chosen or Knight of the Eternal Order, and the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* has no Kelemvor content, so Undermountain adds nothing here.

**Tone:** Calm and unhurried. Kelemvor is a god of endings who is just and kind about it, so describe the comfort he offers before the judgment he gives, and keep him at a distance. He speaks only in dreams and through the recently dead, and the campaign writes no scene in which he does.
