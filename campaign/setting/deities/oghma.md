**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Oghma, and states what the campaign has tied to him and to the Font of Knowledge so that no later page invents more.

*The Lord of Knowledge*

- **Title:** God of Knowledge (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Binder of What Is Known, the Lord of Knowledge (FRHoF); the Binder (SCAG, 2014); the Wise God and Patron of Bards (wiki, 2e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Knowledge, ideas, inspiration, invention, literacy, records and libraries (derived). SCAG calls him the god of "inspiration, invention, and knowledge" and the wiki portfolio is "Knowledge, thought, ideas"; neither lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Knowledge (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A blank scroll (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** The Outlands (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Archivists, cartographers, sages (FRHoF)

> Spread knowledge and literacy as widely as you can.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Oghma is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki calls him a greater deity in its 1e through 4e sources and the most powerful god of knowledge in Faerûn, and says he has no superior (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). In the same accounts he leads the Deities of Knowledge and Invention (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **What the faith asks:** His faithful spread knowledge and literacy as widely as they can, because they hold that minds ought never to be shackled by ignorance, and they oppose those who foster deceit, trickery and ignorance (SCAG, 2014). His priests believe knowledge is the greatest tool of the mortal mind and oppose anyone who tries to keep people ignorant (FRHoF).
- **The prayer:** The faithful repeat an aphorism as a prayer. It begins "An idea has no heft but it can move mountains" and ends "Before anything else can exist, the idea must exist" (SCAG, 2014).
- **Who follows:** Archivists, cartographers and sages (FRHoF). SCAG adds wizards, artists, bards, clerks, inventors and scribes. The wiki says his clerics may be of any alignment, which is unusual for a neutral god (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Everyday prayer:** Students pray to him to fill their minds, and teachers pray that he will guide their instruction. People across Faerûn invoke his name while trying to remember something, and they curse him when a memory stays just out of reach (FRHoF).
- **Temples:** His temples collect and distribute books, scrolls and lore, and most of them double as public libraries. Those temples hold a daily evening service called the Covenant, in which clergy and laypeople read works of wisdom, songs and new knowledge aloud. They support themselves by selling maps, Spell Scrolls and other work of scribes, and they partner with priests of **Deneir** (Deities), who has few temples of his own (FRHoF).
- **Clergy:** The clergy include cloistered sages and archivists who analyze, file and copy the temple's archives, and traveling scholars who seek out new learning (FRHoF).
- **Organization:** Wiki and SCAG sources.
  - The faith was once one of the few in Faerûn with an established orthodoxy and a complete network of temples. Schisms during the Time of Troubles shattered that network, and the faith now runs as individual temples or small networks of allied temples (SCAG, 2014).
  - The wiki gives the cause. The Grand Patriarch Cullen Kordamant vanished from Procampur in 1358 DR, and the church split over whether he had ascended and who should succeed him. The Orthodox Church of Oghma in Procampur kept him as the only true Patriarch, while the Oghmanyte Church in Sembia and the Pursuers of Pure Knowledge in Mintar named new Patriarchs of their own. The split was still unresolved more than a century later (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Oghma. FRHoF says his Chosen have supernatural memories that let them memorize vast quantities of lore, and that in places where a library has burned they serve as a community's living archive.
- **Messengers:** To deal with mortals he sends an owl, and on rare occasions he speaks directly into the mind of someone deep in thought (FRHoF). The wiki adds that he manifests as a blue-green radiance with musical chords or as a blinding white light, and that he sends golems and metallic dragons among his servants (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** Midsummer and Shieldmeet (wiki, 3e; the 5e row is blank). Midsummer falls between Flamerule 30 and Eleasis 1, and in 1492 DR both days come after Flamerule 30. No file ties a city festival to him.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He consults **Deneir**, **Milil** and **Gond** (Deities) before he acts against a threat to knowledge, and he mulls over complex plots rather than intervening at once (FRHoF). The wiki makes the three his agents in the Deities of Knowledge and Invention, with Milil as the god of music, Deneir of writing and images, and Gond of crafts (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Deneir is called the Scribe of Oghma and his right hand, and Milil is his left hand, also called the One True Hand. SCAG (5e) gives both titles, in its Deneir and Milil entries.
  - Gond is the third hand and gives ideas physical form. Oghma admires his creativity but disapproves of how he places innovation above introspection and pushes technology too far, and the firearms made in Lantan in the 1360s DR strained their friendship (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - His allies include **Mystra** and her servant **Azuth** (Deities), **Lathander** (Deities) and **Lliira** (Deities) (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - His most common foes are **Mask**, **Cyric**, **Talos** and **Bane**, whom he sees as the greatest threats to knowledge because each tries to lock it away, corrupt it or destroy it. He also opposed **Leira**, the former goddess of lies, and kept her revolutionary ideas confined to her small cult (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - His faithful tell that soon after the world was made, a traveler from a faraway realm found a land of vague concepts waiting for form. He gave each one a name that bound it to a physical shape, and this is said to be the origin of the title the Binder. Some also say he gave written language to the world. Sages debate these tales, and none dispute that his faith is older than recorded history (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - He is an interloper from the Celtic pantheon (*Cyclopedia of the Realms*, 1e), who gained power on Toril around the rise of Netheril (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
  - His traditional role was to judge each new idea and invention and decide whether it should be released to the world or stay with its maker. In that role he favored caution and the status quo (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - After the Time of Troubles the wiki quotes him as saying that knowledge must be free to travel where it is desired (*Prince of Lies*, novel). In its 4e account he could no longer stand the suppression of any original thought after the Spellplague, and Milil became his exarch (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **In the campaign:** Oghma is alive and present in 1492 DR. The Font of Knowledge is a working temple, and no file treats him as dead or absent. The wiki's accounts of the Time of Troubles and the Spellplague are history and carry no weight in play.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Using Oghma as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god of everything written down. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says Oghma is the Lord of Knowledge, the god of ideas, invention and learning, shown by a blank scroll, and that scribes, sages, mapmakers and bards pray to him. They add that you ask him for help when a fact won't come back to you and curse him when it still won't, and that his priests at the Font of Knowledge teach reading to anyone for nothing. The Dragon Heist city text claims "no city in the world is as literate as Waterdeep," and a Waterdhavian can repeat that. No source records how Waterdhavians in particular talk about him, so this voicing draws on the FRHoF, SCAG and the Dragon Heist text. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Oghma has one temple in the city, the Font of Knowledge in the Castle Ward, and it is also the city's largest public library.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Oghma, Font of Knowledge, Castle Ward" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30962-30966`). The Castle Ward text says "The Font of Knowledge is a temple to Oghma, yes, but also the city's largest public library," and adds that visitors read titles from every age under the watchful eyes of the temple's priests (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31015`).
>   - The Castle Ward gazetteer says the same in three places. Services lists "The Font of Knowledge, the city's largest public library, with free reading lessons" (`campaign/gazetteer/waterdeep/castle-ward.md:27`). Culture says "Oghma's priests at the Font of Knowledge teach reading free" (`:162`) and names "the Font of Knowledge for Oghma, with the library" among the ward's temples (`:164`).
>   - The Dragon Heist city text says "Oghma's priests from the Font of Knowledge offer free instruction in reading to all who desire it," in a city with "over thirty publishers of broadsheets in addition to chapbook printers and book publishers" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31162`).
>   - The wiki calls Oghma a major faith in Waterdeep (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep*, 2e). The Undermountain book captions one picture "The exterior of the Font of Knowledge (temple of Oghma) in the Castle Ward" (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:61941`).
>   - The same table lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple of all deities, which includes Oghma.
>   - No campaign file describes the building, its size, its hours, its high priest or its staff, and the gazetteer has no landmark entry for it. FRHoF says most temples of Oghma hold the Covenant each evening, and no file says the Font does. Which network of the faith the Font belongs to, if any, is unsourced. [research]
>   - No other shrine or temple of Oghma exists in the campaign files, and no city festival is tied to him.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Knowledge (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Knowledge Domain, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is Light Domain (derived), and the fit is loose. Illumination and clear sight sit nearest to a god of learning. The wiki's 3e and 4e domain lists include Trickery, but SCAG says his followers oppose deceit and trickery, so this page does not choose it (derived). The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because his clergy are archivists, scribes and scholars, and the extra cantrip and the bonus to Arcana and Religion checks suit them.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived), a loose fit. No source lists paladins among his followers, and the wiki's class lists for him name bards, clerics, fighters and wizards. Devotion suits a knight sworn to honest learning and against deceit.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** A priest of Oghma in Renaer's circle, an archivist at the Font, and the Font itself. The details are in the GM Notes below. No quest or event has Oghma act, and in the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* the Font appears only in a picture caption.

## Overview

Oghma is the Lord of Knowledge, a neutral god of ideas, invention and learning whose symbol is a blank scroll. His followers include sages, scribes, cartographers, bards and archivists, and most of his temples double as public libraries. His Waterdeep temple, the Font of Knowledge in the Castle Ward, is also the city's largest public library. Its priests teach reading to anyone who asks, free of charge.

> #### GM Notes
> - **Eiruk Weskur:** A Chondathan human priest of Oghma, neutral good, and a member of Renaer Neverember's Black Tears. His page lists him as a background figure with no scripted appearance, and the Renaer's Friends table in **Tavern Time** (entry 15) can bring him to the tavern. His page says he wants "to serve Oghma by gathering and sharing knowledge" and believes "knowledge shared is knowledge doubled." In a fight he would "heal first, fight second", and his page adds that his faith "teaches that knowledge includes knowing when to swing a mace."
> - **Zalara Moonwhisper and the Font's archive:** In **A Friend's House** (Harpers Mission 4), Zalara (Lawful Neutral, Elf, she/her), an archivist at the Font of Knowledge, is one of the twelve guests at Remallia Haventree's salon. She carries a folded copy of a 1244 DR entry that "names the vault as an extraordinary reserve under the Lords' disposition" and describes a ceremonial lock, and she asks the party to read the whole entry in the Font's archive at ten the next morning.
> - **The archive visit is unwritten:** No event runs the visit. The mission's design notes say the 1244 DR record "has no anchor in the Vault of Dragons structure doc" (`campaign/quests/faction-events/harpers/m04-a-friends-house/design-notes.md:23`), so this page adds nothing about what the full entry says. Zalara has no faction in her file, and the file does not say whether she worships Oghma. The record is a seed for **Vault of Dragons**, and the Font is where the GM would stage it.

**Tone:** Warm, patient and a little bookish. Oghma is the god of learning, so describe the reading, copying and teaching his priests do before anything about the ideas he judges. Keep him in the present tense and at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he speaks.
