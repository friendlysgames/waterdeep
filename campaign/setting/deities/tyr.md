> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Tyr, the god of justice, and the reference for the faith practiced at the Halls of Justice.
> - **Where it is used:** Tyr never appears in person. His faith runs through the Order of the Gauntlet, whose Waterdeep chapter works out of his temple, and through Hlam and Lady Gondafrey.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Tyr, and lists the temples, orders and NPCs the campaign has tied to him so that no later page invents more.

*The Even-Handed*

- **Title:** God of Justice (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** Grimjaws, the Maimed God, Wounded Tyr, the Lord of Justice (FRHoF); the Just God (Father Corwen Thale); the Blind Overlord, and Anachtyr in Calimshan (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Justice, law, honesty and oaths, the punishment of the guilty (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Justice" and the FRHoF credo names lawfulness and honesty; no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** War, Order (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Balanced scales resting on a warhammer (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Mount Celestia (Lunia) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Judges, law enforcers, lawyers, paladins (FRHoF)

> Seek the truth, keep your word, and let the law fall evenly on all.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Tyr is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and the head of the Triad, with Ilmater and Torm. The wiki calls him a greater god, and in its 2e and 3e sources the only lawful good one among the greater gods of Faerûn.
- **What the faith asks:** His followers devote themselves to achieving justice, righting wrongs and delivering vengeance. The devotion is about discovering the truth and punishing the guilty, and it cares little for equality or fairness (FRHoF). His credo of lawfulness and honesty is demanding, and his priests tell the faithful not to judge too harshly those who fall short of it.
- **What the faith offers:** The church is highly organized and strongest in the more civilized lands. It never refuses service or aid to a faithful follower in distress (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). Followers are expected to show fairness, wisdom and kindness to the innocent, and they never enforce an unjust law (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **Who follows:** Judges, law enforcers, lawyers and paladins (FRHoF). The wiki adds magistrates, the oppressed and police (*Faiths & Pantheons*), and its 5e row lists judges and knights.
- **Clergy and orders:** Many orders of knighthood are devoted to him, among them the Knights of Holy Judgment and the Knights of the Merciful Sword. Knights, judges, priests and other worshipers wear thin strips of diaphanous cloth over their eyes as a symbol of their faith (FRHoF).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Tyr. The FRHoF says his Chosen suffer for their pursuit of justice and that his power strengthens them to keep his strict code.
- **How he appears:** As a noble warrior without his right hand, which he lost to Kezef the Chaos Hound in an act of bravery and sacrifice. His eyes are wrapped in cloth, and the blindness shows that justice applies evenly to all (FRHoF).
- **Messengers:** Weary of mortal foolishness, he seldom appears or speaks directly. He sends an awakened mastiff as an emissary, or the sound of a war gong or a choir (FRHoF). The wiki adds a floating warhammer and names the choir as male voices (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Deity Do's and Don'ts*).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** The 1st, 13th and 22nd of every month (wiki, 3e and 2e; the 5e row is blank). No file ties a city festival to him.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (2e and 3e sources).
  - Torm served as his war commander and is called the "good right hand of Tyr" (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e).
  - He is close to Lathander, and he opposes Bane, Bhaal, Cyric, Mask, Talona and Talos.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He is an interloper, the same power as the Norse god of the same name. Across the Outer Planes he is the One-Handed, and in the Realms he is worshiped only as a god of justice, never of war (*Cyclopedia of the Realms*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - His first major mark on Toril was the Procession of Justice in -247 DR. He came through a portal near the future Alaghôn in Turmish and led ten scores of archons against the chaotic and evil forces in the ruins of Jhaamdath. Ilmater allied himself with Tyr in -243 DR, and by the end of the campaign in -238 DR Tyr had destroyed Valigan Thirdborn, a god of anarchy. Torm joined them some years later to complete the Triad (*Faiths & Pantheons*; *The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - He lost his right hand to Kezef while a coalition of gods chained the Chaos Hound. Kezef insisted that Tyr put his right hand in its mouth if the contest was to be fair, and Tyr agreed (*Polyhedron* 118; *Powers & Pantheons*).
  - Lord Ao put out his eyes at the start of the Time of Troubles, for failing to notice the theft of the Tablets of Fate and for questioning Ao's justice (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e; *Faiths & Avatars*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - In 1384 DR he quarreled with Siamorphe over a battle between Tethyrian and Calishite forces. She left the House of the Triad to make her realm with Sune in Brightwater, and Sune proposed a marriage between Tyr and Tymora as a way to restore balance (*The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - Tyr had sent Helm to Brightwater as his messenger. Rumor, which *The Grand History of the Realms* has Cyric suspected of starting and *Dragon* 381 states he arranged (both 4e), said Helm loved Tymora, so Tyr challenged him to a duel and killed him. Tymora left Brightwater to be with Tyr out of duty, and Ilmater left the Triad to join Sune there (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e). Helm returned after the Second Sundering, and his page tells the same story.
  - After he and Sune, with Lathander's help, imprisoned Cyric for a thousand years, he gave up his godhood and passed all his power to Torm, having lost faith in his own ability to lead and judge. His portfolio of justice went to Bahamut, who later served Torm (*The Crystal Mountain*; *Dragon* 381, 4e).
  - He died between 1385 DR and 1479 DR fighting a demonic invasion of the upper realms (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). He returned to life in the Second Sundering, in the 1480s DR (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Tyr is alive and present in 1492 DR. His temple is a working temple, his priests act in his name, and no file treats him as dead or absent. The 4e death is a conflict that this page does not use (see Draft Notes).

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Using Tyr as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god behind the city's law. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the blind, one-handed god of justice, shown as scales balanced on a warhammer, and that Waterdeep is a city of laws molded by his spirit of justice. They add that a cause that is just will be heard fairly, and that a wrong takes time to weigh on his scales before his hammer falls with the whole city behind it. At the Halls of Justice, Father Corwen Thale calls him the Just God and Isaldric Veyne the Even-Handed, and Savra Belabranta of the Order of the Gauntlet calls him the Maimed God.
> - **Waterdeep:** Tyr has one temple in the city, the Halls of Justice in the Castle Ward, west of the Market.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Tyr, Halls of Justice, Castle Ward". The Castle Ward gazetteer describes it as the temple of Tyr and the courts, and a petitioner can hire an advocate there to file a petition for fifty gold.
>   - The main hall holds sixty people. The Order of the Gauntlet keeps a clerk's office in the east annex, with an armory and a narrow chapel where a wooden statue of Tyr stands with its right hand carved away at the wrist. Oaths are sworn in an antechamber off the main nave, open every day from the second bell of morning to dusk.
>   - Father Corwen Thale receives the oaths. Isaldric Veyne is the high priest of Tyr in Waterdeep, Brother Haldric Wence is the door-warden, and Sister Ilvane Haste sits as the priest of the Halls' bench.
>   - Hlam leads the Order of the Even-Handed, a small monastic group devoted to Tyr, from a cave halfway up Mount Waterdeep, which lies wholly inside the Castle Ward (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31007`). Students learn the Way of the Sacred Fists, which combines cleric magic and monk training.
>   - The Chapel of the Steadfast Shield in the Dock Ward belongs to Torm, and the Temple of Helm in the Castle Ward belongs to Helm. The Order of the Gauntlet treats all three as friendly, but neither is a temple of Tyr.
>   - No other shrine or temple of Tyr exists in the campaign files, and no city festival is tied to him.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are War and Order (SCAG, 2014). Order Domain (derived; Tasha's Cauldron of Everything) is the best fit, since it is the second domain he is listed with and a god of law and oaths is what its features are built around (Voice of Authority, Order's Demand, Embodiment of the Law). War Domain (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the alternative for a cleric who fights beside the Order's knights. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because his clergy and knights guard the Halls and fight evil in the open.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived; Player's Handbook 2024). Its theme is justice and order, which matches the FRHoF credo of lawfulness and honesty, and the FRHoF lists paladins among his typical followers. Oath of Vengeance (Player's Handbook 2024) is the alternative for a paladin who answers the faith's call to punish the guilty.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** The Order of the Gauntlet, Hlam, Lady Gondafrey and Sister Valdra. The details are in the GM Notes below. The Trollskull Alley table says only that "worship of Helm, Torm or Tyr only decides whom Savra addresses first." In the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, the ghost of Fidelio, a human paladin of Tyr, haunts the Obstacle Course, and the deva Aryx, whom Tyr sent to talk sense into the fallen planetar Fazrian, is trapped in a shield. Undermountain is future work, so treat both as seeds.

## Overview

Tyr is the god of justice, a lawful good power whom Waterdhavians picture as a blind warrior with one hand missing and a pair of scales resting on a warhammer. The city holds that its laws are his, and the Halls of Justice in the Castle Ward serves as both his temple and a seat of the courts. Judges, lawyers and the knights who wear thin strips of cloth over their eyes are the people most likely to speak for him. Visitors are told that a just cause will be heard fairly in Waterdeep and a guilty one will be punished, and the locals credit Tyr for both.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **The Order of the Gauntlet:** Savra Belabranta heads the Waterdeep chapter from a clerk's office in the east annex of the Halls of Justice, with a rotation of about a dozen Knights and Priests. The priests of the Triad help the Order without belonging to it. A member swears the oath at the Halls before Father Corwen Thale, a gray-haired priest of Tyr with a missing left hand, who has presided over the Order's oaths for twenty years and says "the Just God". At Renown 50, usually during **Dungeon of the Mad Mage**, Isaldric Veyne, who says "the Even-Handed", invests the member as Righteous Hand in the main hall.
> - **The Order's use of the Halls:** Brother Haldric Wence, the door-warden, keeps messages and arranges *Speak with Dead* on bodies the Watch holds. At Renown 1 a member gets *Cure Wounds* once a day and a bed for up to three nights at any temple of Tyr, Helm or Torm. In **Spined Devils in Twelvedog Court**, Sister Ilvane Haste reads the *Zone of Truth* scroll at the Halls. In **The Writ and the Weight**, a devil comes to the Halls for the exhibit, and Father Corwen keys the hallowed vestry. In **Savra's Past**, Savra takes the party to the chapel with the statue of Tyr. In **Vault of Dragons**, a party that marked **Order Recognized** has been formally recognized by the Halls of Justice as Order associates. Saeth Cromley, a retired City Watch sergeant, drinks in the sergeants' room most afternoons.
> - **Sister Valdra:** A half-orc cleric of Tyr and an unofficial Order operative whom Savra does not know. She has built a case against the Cassalanters on three months of her own time and a servant's story the Watch dismissed. She cannot say what the family hides, only that it is too generous and too perfect, so what she holds is suspicion and nothing more. No Order event uses her.
> - **Hlam and Force Grey Mission 1:** In **Consulting Hlam**, Vajra Safahr tells a Force Grey member by *Sending* to ask Hlam what threats he has perceived. She says "Hlam serves Tyr, the Even-Handed, and sees farther than my agents do." Hlam is a Calishite human monk, a staunch ally of the Order of the Gauntlet, and he hears lies before they are finished. He also appears in **Trollskull Alley** and **Vault of Dragons**.
> - **Lady Gondafrey and Kolat Towers:** Duhlark Kolat trapped a gargoyle imbued with the personality of a human knight of Tyr in a cell at Kolat Towers. In the campaign she was a City Watch captain who tried to breach the towers in 1379 DR, refused to swear Duhlark's oath of silence and has been a prisoner for 113 years. She wants to go on doing her duty as a knight of Tyr from inside the gargoyle's body. Her alignment shifts with the time of day, and the rule is set out in **Kolat Towers**.

**Tone:** Grave and even. Tyr is a god of law, so describe what he asks before what he punishes, and let the calm of his priests carry the weight. Keep him in the present tense and at a distance, since he seldom speaks and the campaign writes no scene in which he does.
