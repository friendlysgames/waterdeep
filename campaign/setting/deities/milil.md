*The Lord of Song*

- **Title:** God of Poetry and Song (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Lord of All Songs, The First Poet, The Bard of Oghma (FRHoF); the Lord of Song, the One True Hand of All-Wise Oghma (SCAG, 2014); the Guardian of Singers and Troubadours, One Who Watches While Music Is Alive (wiki, *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Poetry, eloquence, song, creativity, inspiration, performance (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki's portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Light (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Five-stringed harp made of leaves (SCAG, 2014). The SCAG text calls the leaves silvery.
- **Alignment:** Neutral Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Elysium (Amoria) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Bards, orators, poets, singers (FRHoF)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Milil is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. FRHoF gives no rank, and the wiki calls him a lesser deity in its 2e and 3e rows, a demigod in 1e, and an exarch of Oghma in 4e.
- **Oghma's left hand:** FRHoF says that, like Deneir, Milil defers to Oghma's will and is sometimes thought of as Oghma's left hand. It lists The Bard of Oghma among his names. SCAG (2014) says the same, that he is sometimes thought of as in service to Oghma, as the god's left hand or the One True Hand. SCAG adds that the phrase does not belittle Deneir, the right hand, because left-handedness is more often tied to great artistic ability and to the belief that the greatest art comes from accepting truth.
- **What he embodies:** Poetry, eloquence and song, and also creativity and inspiration. He stands for the entire song more than for the lyrics or the music alone, and for the finished thought, the result of the process that carries an idea from conception to realization (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **The ideal performer:** He is poised and confident, winningly charismatic, and a source of inspiration to his listeners. SCAG says he has total recall of anything he hears while music plays, and the greatest skill at improvisation.
- **Who prays to him:** Bards, troubadours and other entertainers venerate him. Anyone about to entertain or speak before a crowd might pray to him for a good performance, and so might anyone seeking inspiration for a creative task (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **His followers:** They need not be entertainers, only people who appreciate good music, and they tend to be more hedonistic than the followers of other gods (FRHoF). The wiki adds that the church is organized and draws attractive, sensual, musical people who also enjoy good food and wine. It is mostly clerics, with bards, mystics and spellsingers among them, and its worshipers are mainly human, elf and half-elf bards (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Tuneservants:** His most daring followers travel with adventuring parties and compose ballads about the group's heroics. Fanciful gold netting is their mark, draped over the shoulders or used to hold up the hair (FRHoF).
- **Holy sites and clergy:** Followers dedicate holy sites in performance venues and music schools, from vast concert halls to small choral chambers, and every site needs excellent acoustics. His priests are patrons of the arts, performers and tutors, and they teach the arts of performance at his shrines and temples (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Orders:** The wiki names the Harmonious Order, a group of personable and swaggering paladins who encouraged bards to join them and write ballads about their deeds in his name (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Holy days:** Each year at Midsummer his priests hold the Grand Revel. Clergy and followers dance, feast, act out parodies and sing wickedly satirical songs. Those in power are the revel's biggest targets, so tyrannical rulers outlaw it, and his priests then hold small private ceremonies (FRHoF). The wiki's holy-day rows are blank. In 1492 DR Midsummer falls between Flamerule 30 and Eleasis 1, after the campaign window that closes on Flamerule 11.
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Milil. FRHoF says his Chosen have angelic voices that carry far beyond those of most mortals, and that their passionate words can crack the hardest of hearts in song or in speech.
- **How he appears:** SCAG says his icons show a handsome male, sometimes human, sometimes elf, and in places with many half-elves, such as Aglarond, a half-elf. He may look young or old, but he always carries his five-stringed harp.
- **Messengers:** Songbirds are his heralds. He shows himself to mortals as a beautiful, haunting voice echoing through open spaces, or in visions that come to entertainers during a performance (FRHoF).
- **Omen:** No source gives an omen. The wiki gives one sign of his approval of a song, poem or play, a golden-lined image of two dancing hollyphants that appears in the air or on parchment as glowing yellow ink (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Temperament:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). He is a consummate performer who adores being the center of attention and is easily bored without it. He is also extremely flirtatious, with mortals and gods alike.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - He serves **Oghma** with **Deneir** and **Gond**. He has little in common with Gond, and the two have a slightly strained relationship.
  - He keeps very good relations with **Mystra**, **Sune** and **Lliira** and with the gods of the elven pantheon, the Seldarine. The **Lliira** page names him her best friend.
  - He thought Finder Wyvernspur showed some promise, and Finder did not return the opinion.
  - **Cyric** became his enemy after Milil wrote a mocking ballad about the god's insanity.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - In the Time of Troubles he appeared in Athkatla and founded a singing circle at Arbalest's House, a temple devoted to his own glory. He charged the Patriarch of Song with enlarging it and with recruiting more of the faithful (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - In the Era of Upheaval he was simply an exarch of Oghma (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - The wiki says that soon after the mocking ballad he was ejected from the pantheon and banished to the Fugue Plane, and that musical prayers went out of fashion among bards as a result. A being called Withers later pulled him out, and as payment Milil must "honor the worthy" whenever Withers asks (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Baldur's Gate III*, video game).
- **In the campaign:** Milil is alive and present in 1492 DR. The exarch rank, the banishment and the rescue are the wiki's history and carry no weight in play.

> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Light (SCAG, 2014). ## Overview

Milil is the Lord of Song, a neutral good god of poetry, eloquence and song whose symbol is a five-stringed harp made of leaves. Bards, troubadours and public speakers pray to him for a good performance, and anyone looking for inspiration may do the same. His priests perform and teach in concert halls and music schools, where the room has to sound right. Songbirds are called his heralds, and he is said to appear to mortals as a beautiful voice echoing through open spaces.

> #### GM Notes
> - **Lysa of the White Lute:** A neutral good half-elf and the second House Bard candidate in the Trollskull Manor staff table. She carries her lute case like something fragile and says, "I haven't performed professionally. I want to be transparent about that." She has played twelve years at guild functions and the Temple of Milil, and she wants to find out whether she is good in front of people who do not already like her. She asks 6 gp a tenday, rising to 8 gp if she succeeds, and her father's Carpenters' Guild connections are occasionally useful.
> - **How she plays in the tavern:** In her first tenday she makes a DC 13 Performance check. On a success she adds +1 Fame and +2 Revenue, and on a failure she costs -1 Fame and needs encouragement (DC 12 Persuasion or sincere support) to return. From her second tenday she adds +1 Fame and +3 Revenue. The Fame track gives +1 for passing her first performance and +1 a tenday from the second tenday onward (`campaign/guides/trollskull-manor/04-fame.md:65`).
> - **What the temple is:** The file says only that she has played there. It does not make her a priest or a worshiper of Milil, and it gives the temple no location, so a scene should add nothing about the building.
> - **Not tied:** No other character, faction or event in the campaign files mentions Milil.

**Tone:** Warm and a little vain, in the brisk reference register. Milil is a god of performance, so describe what his faithful make and share before what they mock, and keep him at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he appears.
