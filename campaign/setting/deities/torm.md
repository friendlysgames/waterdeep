> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Torm, the god of courage and self-sacrifice, and the reference for the Torm chapel that the Order of the Gauntlet uses in the Dock Ward.
> - **Where it is used:** Torm never appears in person. His faith reaches the campaign through the Order of the Gauntlet, which treats worshipers of Helm, Torm and Tyr as its natural recruits, and through the Chapel of the Steadfast Shield, which heals Order members and gives them briefings.
> - **Featured in:** **Trollskull Alley** (**The Factions Come Calling**), the Order of the Gauntlet's First Meeting, and the **Marcheon** and **Righteous Hand** rank events. *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* supplies Torm seeds for later.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Torm, and lists exactly what the campaign has placed in the city for him so that no later page invents more.

*The Loyal Fury*

- **Title:** God of Courage and Self-Sacrifice (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Loyal Fury, the Hand of Righteousness, Torm the True (FRHoF); the True (SCAG, 2014); the True God, the God of Duty, the Brave (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Courage, duty, loyalty, self-sacrifice, vigilance against corruption (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Duty, Loyalty, Obedience, Courage, Self-sacrifice", and the 5e line is "Courage, self-sacrifice, duty, loyalty". No entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** War (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** White right gauntlet (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Mount Celestia (Mercuria) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Guardians, knights, paladins (FRHoF)

> Do your duty, and stand between the weak and evil whatever it costs you.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Torm is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and one of the three gods of the Triad, with Tyr and Ilmater. The wiki calls him a lesser deity in its 2e and 3e sources and a greater deity in 4e, and says he began as a mortal hero and became a demigod (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **What the faith asks:** His followers believe salvation is found through service, and that brave acts of service please him. They keep the peace, oppose unjust laws and stay alert against corruption (FRHoF). Every failure of duty diminishes Torm and every success adds to his luster, and his priests teach that a minor fall is far less shameful than refusing to rise to his standard (SCAG, 2014).
- **Skill at arms is optional:** Many followers are warriors, but FRHoF gives the example of a council member who worships Torm and argues for what is just even at risk to their position.
- **Striking at rot:** Followers are expected to strike quickly and hard at any sign of rot in the hearts of mortals. As the sword arm of justice they are expected to bring quick deaths to betrayers (SCAG, 2014).
- **The 4e code:** The wiki lists three demands. A follower respects and enforces the law and honors tradition, shows truthfulness, loyalty and steadfast devotion to a rightful cause, and does not flinch in the execution of duty whatever the consequence (*Forgotten Realms Player's Guide*, 4e).
- **The Hand Resolute:** The gauntlet is made for the right hand because Torm is the good right hand of Tyr, and it stands for Tyr's sword hand. Torm is shown with the gauntlet raised and the palm forward. The faithful call this the Hand Resolute and take it as a reminder to pause and judge whether their intentions uphold Torm's ideals before they act. Temples, civic buildings and the homes of the faithful carry the image (SCAG, 2014).
- **Who follows:** Guardians, knights and paladins (FRHoF). SCAG adds that he welcomes anyone who seeks the best in themselves and others, and that most human paladins have him as their patron. The wiki calls his worshipers Tormtar, mostly human, with growing numbers of dwarves and elves after the Time of Troubles (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Temples:** Followers build fortress-temples on defensible heights, with drilling grounds, stables and austere quarters for residents and visiting knights. The white granite walls carry the coats of arms of fallen heroes, and lion statues and armored figures fill the halls (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Clergy and allies:** His priesthood is friendly with the priesthoods of Tyr and Ilmater, and it is also allied with the Red Knight and Lathander (FRHoF).
- **The Order of the Golden Lion:** This knightly order is based around Tantras, the city with the greatest concentration of Torm worshipers in Faerûn. Its knights each devote themselves to righting one of three Debts. Persecution means aiding good religions, Dereliction means rooting out cults of Bane and routing the Zhentarim, and Destruction means reporting and repairing dead and wild magic (FRHoF). Members wear golden pauldrons carved as lion heads, and a candidate must first complete a holy quest for a temple of Torm. The wiki adds that the pontiff Barriltar Bhandraddon ruled from the Temple of Torm's Coming in Tantras, with no edition tagged. This order is a different body from the Order of the Gauntlet.
- **Other orders:** The wiki names the Eye of Justice, based in Westgate, which many Tormites in the 14th century DR considered heretical because it worshiped a "Threefold God" combining Torm, Tyr and Helm. It also names the Girded Healers, who worship Torm and Ilmater and heal those touched by war or the Spellplague (*Dragon* 381, 4e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Torm. FRHoF says his Chosen dedicate themselves to fighting evil, especially the forces of Bane.
- **Messengers and omens:** In dreams he appears as an enormous flying metal gauntlet circled by arcs of lightning. To make his will known he may send a golden lion or, in extreme cases, a deva in gleaming armor (FRHoF). The wiki lists images of a gauntlet, shield or sword burned into fabric among his manifestations (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*). No source gives a separate omen.
- **Holy days:** The wiki lists the Divine Death on Eleasis 13 and the True Resurrection on Marpenoth 15 (2e, 3e and 4e sources), and its infobox adds Shieldmeet without an edition. In the city's calendar Marpenoth 15 is Gods' Day, and in 1492 DR Shieldmeet follows Flamerule 30. All three fall after the campaign window, and no file ties a city festival to Torm.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (2e, 3e and 4e sources).
  - He reveres Tyr as a spiritual father (*Dragon* 381, 4e), and he is a staunch ally of Ilmater (*Faiths & Pantheons*). The common folk call him the weakest of the three, but he holds the Triad together and tempers Tyr's zeal for justice with mercy and humility (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - He is good friends with Lathander and Amaunator, and Amaunator earns his great respect (*Dragon* 381; FRHoF). He and the Red Knight are fond of one another, and whether as friends or lovers they keep to themselves (*Powers & Pantheons*; FRHoF).
  - Bahamut is his comrade and served him as a knight serves a lord. The two have fought side by side against evil gods and devils (*Dragon* 381, 4e; *Whisper of Venom*).
  - He had a friendship with Helm, though the two priesthoods were cold to one another (*Faiths & Pantheons*; *Dragon* 381).
  - His chief enemies are Bane and Shar. He has a special hatred for Cyric because of the harm Cyric did to Tyr (*Dragon* 381, 4e). FRHoF says the animosity with Bane still seethes after the two gods destroyed each other in the Time of Troubles.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He was a mortal hero at the fall of Netheril, and scholars believe he came from Chalsembyr in the Border Kingdoms. In life he was the most faithful of warriors and obeyed his king whatever the danger (*Faiths & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Avatars*).
  - Before -238 DR he joined Tyr as his war leader during the Procession of Justice, and with Ilmater they became the Triad (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - In the Time of Troubles, in 1358 DR, his faithful helped keep one of the Tablets of Fate safe. His avatar destroyed the avatar of Bane outside Tantras on Eleasis 13. Torm lacked the power to win, so he absorbed the willing souls of his worshipers in Tantras, which killed thousands. He spared children aged fourteen and under, who became the Martyr's Progeny (*Faiths & Pantheons*; *The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - Torm died in that battle. Lord Ao resurrected him, and Tyr raised him to lesser deity (*Faiths & Avatars*; *Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - He found that the priests of Tantras had persecuted believers of other gods, and he ordered the Penance of Duty as atonement. The Order of the Golden Lion keeps it still (FRHoF; *Faiths & Avatars*).
  - In 1384 DR, in the wiki's 4e account, Tyr gave up his godhood and passed all his divine power to Torm, and told his followers to offer their allegiance to Torm (*Dragon* 381; *The Crystal Mountain*).
  - In 1385 DR, after the Spellplague, demons led by the balor lord Axithar invaded the House of the Triad. Bahamut sent his legions to help, and Tyr died in the fighting. Torm became the chief deity of the Triad, promoted Bahamut to the place Bahamut had held under Tyr, and the three gods moved to Celestia with Ilmater rejoining them (*The Crystal Mountain*; *Dragon* 381; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - Tyr returned to life in the Second Sundering, in the 1480s DR, and Torm seems to have gone back to being his right hand as the god of courage and self-sacrifice (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Torm is alive and present in 1492 DR, with Tyr reigning as the head of the Triad. The Order of the Gauntlet's files treat the Halls of Justice, the Temple of Helm and the Torm chapel as three working temples. The 4e abdication of Tyr is a conflict that this page does not use (see Draft Notes).

> **[GM]**

> #### Using Torm as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god of sworn duty. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says Torm is the god of courage and self-sacrifice, the patron of paladins and knights, and the good right hand of Tyr. They know his sign as a white gauntlet raised with the palm forward, and they say his followers keep their duty whatever it costs and rise again after they fail. Priests add that he is kind to the weak and the faithful and stern with evil and injustice. Anyone who wants a priest of his is sent to the Chapel of the Steadfast Shield in the Dock Ward, because the city has nothing grander of his. At the Order's investiture the words are "Tyr, Helm and Torm are witness to it."
> - **Waterdeep:** Torm has one chapel in the city, the Chapel of the Steadfast Shield in the Dock Ward.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Torm row. The Order of the Gauntlet files are the only source that places him in the city.
>   - The First Meeting names the three temples that treat an Order member as a friend: "the Halls of Justice, which is the temple of Tyr, the Temple of Helm in the Castle Ward, and the Chapel of the Steadfast Shield, a Torm chapel in the Dock Ward." The Marcheon event repeats the Dock Ward placement and heads the chapel's list "Chapel of the Steadfast Shield (Torm, Dock Ward)".
>   - The files call it a chapel and give it no size, staff, description or street. No priest, almoner or door-warden of the chapel is named. The Halls of Justice has Brother Haldric Wence as door-warden, and the Torm chapel has no such figure.
>   - No other shrine or temple of Torm exists in the campaign files, and no city festival is tied to him.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is War (SCAG, 2014), and the closest 2024 subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is War Domain (derived). It is a close fit, since he is a god of the front line and his clergy are knights and armored guardians. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because his faithful stand guard over others and fight in armor.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion. FRHoF says he is a patron god of many paladins and embodies the tenets of that oath, so the fit is sourced and not derived. A paladin who follows the Order of the Golden Lion would take one of its three Debts, and FRHoF lists cults of Bane and the Zhentarim among the targets of the Debt of Dereliction. The Zhentarim are one of the campaign's four villain factions, but no campaign file uses the Golden Lion.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** The Order of the Gauntlet is the whole of it. Dragon Heist says that "Clerics, monks, and paladins are especially welcome, particularly if they worship Helm, Torm, or Tyr." In the Trollskull Alley table, worship of Helm, Torm or Tyr "only decides whom Savra addresses first", and the First Meeting adds that "Nobody is tested for class, alignment or faith at the door." The chapel supplies healing, a bed and briefings to members, and the Order's final rank event notes that "The white gauntlet is Torm's symbol, and Torm is one of the Triad the Order serves." Undermountain holds more of his story in the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, which is future work, so treat it as seeds (see GM Notes).

## Overview

Torm is the lawful good god of courage and self-sacrifice, and his symbol is a white gauntlet for the right hand. Paladins and knights revere him as the good right hand of Tyr, and his faithful keep their oaths, oppose unjust laws and stand on the front line against evil. His priests are friendly with those of Tyr and Ilmater, and the three gods are known together as the Triad. His temples are usually fortresses on high ground, so Waterdhavians who need a priest of his look to the Chapel of the Steadfast Shield in the Dock Ward.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **The Order's pull:** Savra Belabranta speaks first to a candidate with a Cleric or Paladin class or an Acolyte, Guard or Soldier background. If none qualifies she speaks first to a worshipper of Helm, Torm or Tyr. Faith decides nothing else about recruitment, and a Torm worshiper gets no other advantage and faces no test.
> - **The chapel and the Chevall rank:** At Renown 1 the member may name Savra at the front desk of any temple of Tyr, Helm or Torm. A priest casts *Cure Wounds* on the member once a day and gives a bed for the night, up to three nights in a row, free of charge. A member can have three castings in one day by walking between the three temples. A priest casts nothing stronger without payment.
> - **Marcheon briefings:** At Renown 3 a member may ask one of the three temples for a briefing each tenday. The priest sees the member the same day in a side room and speaks for about a quarter of an hour. The chapel's list has four patterns and no names. The first is sworn men who left a post without a word and have not been found. The second is vows that were paid for, with one man swearing an oath on another man's behalf. The third is a charity whose books do not close, which a Torm almoner asked about and was refused. The fourth is known fiendish signs in general, animals that will not enter a house and a sleeper who talks in a language he does not know. The priest gives the fourth only to a member who has been through **Spined Devils in Twelvedog Court**, and before then the chapel repeats the first three.
> - **The almoner's charity:** No file says whose charity it is. The Order's lists name nobody, and none of them names the Cassalanters, so the chapel does not name them either. A priest never gives a name or opens a sealed record, because Tyr's law forbids it, and a member who presses, bribes or threatens a priest loses briefings for a tenday.
> - **Righteous Hand:** The investiture is at the Halls of Justice, and the words include "Tyr, Helm and Torm are witness to it." The writ is honored in Waterdeep, Neverwinter, Baldur's Gate and Daggerford, and refused in Luskan. Elsewhere on the Sword Coast a town honors it when it has a temple of Tyr, Helm or Torm. Any such temple lodges the member and up to four companions for three nights a visit, free of charge.
> - **Seeds from Dungeon of the Mad Mage:** These are one-line facts for later. The Order has no part in the published adventure, so use these and no others.
>   - Level 21: Fazrian, a planetar once in Torm's service, is corrupted. A character it judges innocent can ask it to judge itself with a **DC 26 Charisma (Persuasion)** check. If it dies, Torm bestows a *blessing of protection* on each character, permanent for a Torm worshiper and lasting 10 days for anyone else. A ghostly scene on the same level shows Brythia Mulspeer, a cleric of Torm whom Fazrian killed, with a white gauntlet on her breastplate.
>   - Level 22: Dezmyr and Zalthar Shadowdusk, former paladins of Torm, are now death knights. A shrine of Torm with a white gauntlet fresco stands in Shadowdusk Hold, and an unfinished marble statue of Torm stands elsewhere on the level.

**Tone:** Steady and earnest. Torm asks for service before he asks for glory, so describe the duty and the cost of keeping it before the gauntlet. Keep him in the present tense and at a distance, since he sends dreams, lions and devas and the campaign writes no scene in which he speaks. Keep the Dock Ward chapel modest, because the files give it a name and a list of briefings and nothing else.
