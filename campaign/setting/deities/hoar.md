*The Doombringer*

- **Title:** God of revenge and retribution (SCAG, 2014)
- **Other Names:** Poet of Justice, and Assuran in the lands along the Inner Sea (SCAG, 2014); Lord of Three Thunders, Hurler of Thunders, God of Revenge (wiki, citing *Old Empires* and *Cloak of Shadows*)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Revenge, retribution, poetic justice, the punishment that follows a broken law (derived from the SCAG text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** War (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A coin with a two-faced head (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** The Doomcourt in Mechanus (wiki, 2e); Banehold (wiki, 3e and 4e). SCAG gives no plane and no 5e source names one. [research]
- **Typical Followers:** Seekers of vengeance, bounty hunters, jailers, headsmen (wiki, 5e row)

> Repay every wrong in kind, and take no excuse on the wrongdoer's behalf.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Hoar is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon (SCAG, 2014). The wiki's 5e row calls him a god, its 3e row a demigod and its 2e row a demipower, and its 4e row makes him an exarch who served Bane.
- **One power, two names:** Hoar and Assuran are the same god under different names in different lands. Around the Inner Sea he is known as Assuran (SCAG, 2014), and the wiki places that name in Unther and Chessenta, while in the North he is the god of poetic justice (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e). Some pirates of the Sea of Fallen Stars who survived the unfair struggles of life came to worship Assuran (*Pirates of the Fallen Stars*).
- **How he is invoked:** Few people worship him on a regular basis, and his name is spoken by those who want vengeance. When a guilty person falls to fate, such as a murderer who escapes prosecution and is then killed by accident, the hand of Hoar gets the credit (SCAG, 2014).
- **Prayers:** Folk say his name when they want revenge, especially when they cannot take it themselves, and the cause may be a petty slight or a real injustice. The prayer can be spoken aloud or written down, and people believe a more permanent prayer is more likely to be granted. Some etch their prayers in lead and bury it, and others hide them inside diaries (SCAG, 2014).
- **His sign:** Three rolls of thunder in succession are taken as a sign from Hoar that an act of vengeance has been carried out. Many human societies ring a bell or a gong three times when a court passes judgment on a crime or an execution takes place (SCAG, 2014).
- **Who reveres him:** Bounty hunters and people on crusades of vengeance are the few who truly revere him, and fewer still would call themselves his priests (SCAG, 2014). The wiki adds that some assassins pray to him before an operation, and that the most devout try to kill or capture their marks in an ironic style that echoes his poetic justice (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e). Half-orcs are among the non-humans who often turn to him (*Races of Faerûn*, 3e).
- **Law and punishment:** Most people consider Tyr the arbiter of laws and Hoar the god who delivers the punishment that follows breaking them. A judge might favor Tyr, while a jailer or a headsman is more likely to pray to Hoar (SCAG, 2014).
- **The creed:** The wiki quotes a character in a video game who sums up the faith as like repaid with like, so that violence answers violence, evil answers evil and good answers good. In that creed, allowances for the condemned are false excuses that pervert justice (*Baldur's Gate II*, a video game).
- **His temper:** The wiki describes a god prone to violence and mood swings, with a taste for bitter humor and a morbid fascination with doomed mortals. He wants misfortune to fall on those who deserve it (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
- **Clergy and worship:** His worshipers rarely hold sermons or organized worship (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e). The wiki names no orders or ranks of priests.
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Hoar.
- **Temples and shrines:** SCAG says temples and shrines are almost nonexistent except for ancient sites in Chessenta and Unther (SCAG, 2014). The wiki says that by the late 15th century DR smaller shrines had spread north, with one in Baldur's Gate (*Murder in Baldur's Gate*, 5e) and a well-kept one on Dragonisle (*Pirates of the Fallen Stars*). It also names an amphitheater temple in Mourktar where the accused and accusers fought as gladiators, the Hidden Hand of Fate in the Arch Wood, a temple in Bezantur that is the main place of his worship in Thay, and the Thunderous Hand of Vengeance in Akanax (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Spellbound Campaign Guide*).
- **Holy days:** The wiki gives two festivals and no others (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e). The Penultimate Thunder falls on Eleint 11 and marks his victory over Ramman with lavish feasts. The Impending Doom falls on Marpenoth 11 and honors future justice with a full day of oaths, exhausting acts of purification and the music of rumbling drums. The wiki's 5e holy-day field is blank.
- **Manifestations:** His favorite sign is three deep rolls of thunder that echo across the sky, often at the moment a guilty person is punished. He also appears as a ghostly grinning face that only the guilty party sees as it delivers an ironic punishment. Red tear-shaped gems show his good will, and pulverized Laeral's tears show his fury (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
- **Servants:** The wiki lists aerial servants, invisible stalkers, haunts, revenants, maruts and several other creatures as his servants (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced.
  - His bitter rivals were the Untheric gods Anhur and Ramman, who both banished him from the region at one point. Centuries later his avatar killed Ramman (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Beshaba and Bhaal were among his few allies (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). In other pantheons he was allied with the drow goddess Kiaransalee and with Shevarash, the elven god of hatred for the drow (*Demihuman Deities*).
  - His bond with Tymora was complicated. He sometimes flattered and charmed her to win better luck, and at other times he treated her as an enemy (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
  - Tyr and Shar both tried to influence him. Shar wanted him to become her servant of bitter vengeance, and Tyr urged him to embrace the humor and irony of life and deliver satisfying, poetic justice. Hoar refused them both and worked with Beshaba to spread misfortune among those who did not honor them (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - SCAG says Hoar joined the Faerûnian pantheon when his worship spread beyond the lands that first revered him. The wiki gives a second cause. After Ramman drove him out of Unther, Ao let him gain power in Faerûn in return for giving Mystra influence in Unther (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Many centuries before the Godswar he fought Ramman, the war god of the Untheric pantheon. The battle ruined him, and his devotees were pushed out of Unther. His cult stayed active in Unther, Chessenta, Akanax and Mourktar, though Assuran was no longer part of the Untheric pantheon (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - In the Time of Troubles of 1358 DR he took the body of King Hippartes of Akanax as his avatar. As the king he led Akanax's mercenary companies against Cimbar, Soorenar and Luthcheq, and then marched on Unther and killed Ramman by turning Ramman's own lightning against him. Anhur received Ramman's portfolio from the dying avatar and drove Hoar's armies out (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - After the Time of Troubles he withdrew from the Realms. By 1368 DR his thunder had not been heard for ten years, and some thought him dead, but in fact he had been reduced to a demipower and was plotting revenge on Anhur (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - In the wiki's 4e account, Bane recruited him as an exarch after Tyr died, and he took on a crueler manner (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). After the Second Sundering he returned to his neutral outlook as a counterpoint to Tyr. Tyr arbitrates, and Hoar punishes those who break the law (wiki, citing SCAG).
- **In the campaign:** Hoar is alive and present in 1492 DR. No file treats him as dead, absent or bound to Bane, so this page gives the exarchy as history. No file uses him in play (see Draft Notes).

## Overview

Hoar is the god of revenge and retribution, called the Doombringer, and his symbol is a coin with a two-faced head. Around the Inner Sea the same god is known as Assuran. Few people worship him on a regular basis, but folk speak his name when they want revenge, and a guilty person's bad end is credited to his hand. Three rolls of thunder are said to mean that a vengeance has been done. Most people hold that Tyr judges the law and Hoar delivers the punishment, and no temple of his is known in Waterdeep.

**Tone:** Dry and grim, with respect for the grievance behind the prayer. Hoar is a god people mutter about when the courts have failed them, so describe the folk custom of lead tablets, diaries and three strokes of a bell before the power, and keep him at a distance. The campaign writes no scene in which he appears.
