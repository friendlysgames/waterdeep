# Maglubiyet

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Maglubiyet, the god of war who rules the goblinoid pantheon, for a GM who needs a power behind a goblin, hobgoblin or bugbear and for a player building a goblinoid cleric.
> - **Where it is used:** Maglubiyet never appears in person, and no campaign file names him. Goblins and bugbears work for Xanathar's Guild, and the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* ties him to the hobgoblins and goblins of Undermountain.
> - **Featured in:** no quest uses him as a god. The nearest goblinoid content is in **Finding Floon** (the goblins of the Xanathar Sewer Hideout) and in **Three Nights**, a Bregan D'aerthe mission with Guild bugbears.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Maglubiyet is secret.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Maglubiyet, and states that no source places a temple of his in the city and no campaign file names him, so that no later page invents a goblinoid cult in Waterdeep.

*The Conquering God*

- **Title:** Goblinoid god of war (PHB, 2014)
- **Other Names:** The Mighty One, the Lord of Depths and Darkness (wiki; *Deities & Demigods*, 1e); Fiery-Eyes, the High Chieftain, the Battle Lord (wiki; *Defenders of the Faith*, 3e); the Great One, the Conquering God (wiki; *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e); the Great Scourge (wiki; *Mordenkainen's Fiendish Folio*, no edition given)
- **Pantheon:** Goblinoid, and its head (wiki; *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e). The PHB, 2014, files him under "Nonhuman".
- **Areas of Concern:** War, conquest, the rule of the goblinoid peoples, and the discipline of their legions (derived from the wiki's portfolios, War in the 5e row and goblinoids, leadership and rulership in the 3e row; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** War, Order (PHB, 2014). SCAG and MTF have no entry for him.
- **Symbol:** Bloody axe (PHB, 2014). The wiki's 5e row gives a bloody headsman's axe.
- **Alignment:** Lawful Evil (PHB, 2014; the wiki's 2e and 5e rows agree)
- **Plane:** Acheron, in the realm of Clangor on the layer Avalas (wiki; the 2e and 3e rows name Clangor and Avalas, and the 5e row gives Acheron). The wiki's 1e row gives the Nine Hells.
- **Typical Followers:** Goblins, hobgoblins and bugbears (wiki; *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e)

> Win glory in his host, and you will earn a place in his Army of Immortals.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Maglubiyet is the chief god of the goblinoids and rules every other god of their pantheon. The wiki rates him a greater deity in its 1e, 2e, 3e and 5e rows and an exarch of Bane in its 4e row. The pantheon is smaller and more divided than the orcs', and most local goblin pantheons hold only minor gods and demigods (*Monster Mythology*, 2e).
- **Character:** He is an iron-fisted tyrant who is also capricious and prone to changing his mind. He is paranoid about rivals, tolerates no challenge to his power, and is known for treachery (*Monster Mythology*, 2e; *On Hallowed Ground*, 2e). Like Gruumsh he drives his people to war, but he wants the glory of war and carnage for their own sake, where Gruumsh wants territory.
- **How he appears:** As a battle-scarred goblinoid nine to eleven feet tall, with black skin, red eyes that shed flame, fangs and talons (*Deities & Demigods*, 1e; *Monster Mythology*, 2e). He fights with a huge jagged axe. He sends avatars rarely, because he fears leaving his base unguarded, and does so mostly when enough dwarves or gnomes are away from home to be worth the risk (*Monster Mythology*, 2e).
- **What the faith teaches:** Goblinoids are told in their youth that Maglubiyet and his servants are the only true gods, and that the world will suffer chaos and despair until every false god is conquered. They hate enemy clerics. They may accept a convert or spare the followers of a harmless god, but in the end no false god may stand (*Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
- **The Army of Immortals:** When different goblinoid peoples meet, the followers read it as his call to raise a host. Each member then gets the chance to prove himself in battle and win a place in the god's Army of Immortals after death. Bugbears read a host that falls into infighting as a sign that he has stopped watching, and they turn on their hobgoblin leaders, take a few heads and leave (*Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
- **The three peoples:** Wiki-sourced (*Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
  - Hobgoblins revere him and learned discipline from him, which made them the natural leaders among goblinoids. Most do not fear death, because it gives them a place in his army.
  - Goblins serve him out of fear. He taught them to dread his cruelty and showed them how easily their race is dominated, and they take that frustration out on others.
  - Bugbears do not worship him directly. They honor him by worshiping their own gods, Hruggek and Grankhul, who are his subordinates, and they demand loot, drink or holy severed heads before they help his cause.
- **Priests and shamans:** His priests and shamans are very important to him, because they hold great sway over the goblin tribes and are his tools on the Material Plane. He talks to them fairly often, sends visions to shamans and sometimes to clan chieftains, and may speak through a shaman in a trance (*Monster Mythology*, 2e; *On Hallowed Ground*, 2e). The priests teach that sacrifices give him life energy for his war against the orcs' treacherous spirits.
- **Rites:** Each month, usually on a night of the new moon, captives are beheaded with his holy weapon, the battleaxe, and the shamans offer their hearts. The best victims are captured dwarves and gnomes, then other demihumans and humans, then other goblinoids, and even junior members of the cult when no others are at hand (wiki; sources of 1e to 3e).
- **Temples:** The holy symbol is the headsman's axe, and the block under it is fed with the blood of conquered foes and neglectful goblinoids. A rack nearby holds the weapons of the other goblinoid gods in order of rank, with his axe at the top. The bugbear gods are honored instead with severed heads hung around the block (*Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
- **Omens:** Blood seeping from the edge of an axe, or odd behavior in the wolves and worgs the goblins ride, is read as his sign (*Monster Mythology*, 2e). Goblin acolytes also watch for a roll of thunder or a bird of prey landing nearby (*Monster Manual 2*, 4e).
- **Messengers:** No source names one. [research]
- **Holy days:** The wiki's holy-day fields are blank for every edition. The monthly rite falls on a new moon night, but no source names a festival.
- **Realm:** Clangor is a cold iron cube on Avalas, the first layer of Acheron, where war never ends and the dead of the battlefield revive at the blood-red sunset. He rules from a throne of flaming iron in a steel cavern at the foot of a waterfall on the River Lorfang. His temple-city and capital fortress is Grashmog, where clerics advise generals and train the elite of the army (*Manual of the Planes*, 3e; *Planes of Law: Acheron*, 2e). **Bane**'s realm lies on the same layer.
- **His pantheon:** Wiki-sourced (*Monster Mythology*, 2e; *On Hallowed Ground*, 2e; *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
  - He crushes any god of his pantheon who grows too strong. The goblin god Khurgorbaeyag serves him as overseer of the goblins and plots in secret to depose him.
  - Nomog-Geaya, the hobgoblin god, is the finest commander among the gods and second to Maglubiyet in personal power. Bargrivyek mediates between goblins and hobgoblins.
  - Hruggek and Grankhul, the bugbear gods, stay largely independent under his control. A trickster god of the old goblin pantheon survived his conquest as a splintered spirit that possesses goblins, who call them nilbogs.
- **Gruumsh and the orcs:** Maglubiyet and Gruumsh have fought for longer than anyone can reckon, and each commits most of his troops to the war. Gruumsh treats it as a chance for his people to prove themselves. Maglubiyet uses it to bring another pantheon under his rule, and he frames it to his followers as a sacred war against treacherous orc spirits (*Monster Mythology*, 2e; *Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
- **Dwarves, gnomes and the other peoples' gods:** He drives his followers to destroy the dwarves and gnomes, who are their main competition, and urges them to breed in great numbers to overrun them (*Races of Faerûn*, 3e). Many demihuman gods oppose the goblinoid pantheon. **Moradin** is noted to loathe Maglubiyet in particular (*Demihuman Deities*, 2e). The page for **Corellon Larethian** lists the goblinoid pantheon among his enemies.
- **Devils:** He lends the devils of the Nine Hells subtle, precise aid in the Blood War, keeps a few baatezu advisors and does not much care who wins (*On Hallowed Ground*, 2e; *Hellbound: The Blood War*, 2e). Hobgoblins who leave his pantheon may join the cults of **Zariel** (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes*, 5e, as the Zariel page records).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Despite what some goblinoid myths say, he did not create the goblinoids and is not their ancestor. They first appeared in the Feywild, where early goblins served in the court of the **Queen of Air and Darkness** and early bugbears lived in hidden, shadowed places (*Mordenkainen Presents: Monsters of the Multiverse*, 5e).
  - He saw the destructive streak in each people and set out to harness it. He conquered their gods, united them under his rule and moved a population from the Feywild to his realm in Acheron. From there they spread across the multiverse, and many reached the Material Plane (*Mordenkainen Presents: Monsters of the Multiverse*, 5e).
  - He once bargained with the General of Gehenna for yugoloth soldiers and then broke the deal. In revenge the General created the barghests, which feed on goblinoid souls (*Volo's Guide to Monsters*, 5e).
  - When **Tiamat** grew impatient with the Dragonfall War, she was believed to have struck a deal with him for goblinoid armies to scour the metallic dragons from the worlds of the Material Plane one at a time (*Dragon* 359, no edition given).
  - In the 4e era **Bane** rose and drew him into his command, reducing him to an exarch (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). The wiki says Bane lost that power after the Second Sundering and that Maglubiyet returned to his greater status (*Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*, 5e).
  - His hold on his peoples may be loosening. Many bugbears have slipped away from his influence, and many goblins pursue their own destinies, though they remember little of the Feywild (*Mordenkainen Presents: Monsters of the Multiverse*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Maglubiyet is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. The 4e exarch period is history, and the page keeps him as the head of his own pantheon (see Draft Notes).

> #### Using Maglubiyet as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as a distant and brutal war god known by reputation. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says Maglubiyet is the goblinoid god of war, that his symbol is a bloody axe, and that goblins, hobgoblins and bugbears march in his name. They add that hobgoblins serve him with soldierly discipline and goblins out of fear. How much a Waterdhavian really knows of him, and which of his names the city uses, is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Maglubiyet has no temple, shrine, priest or festival in the city in any source.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no goblinoid row (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30921-30996`). Its "All deities" row, Holyhands House in the North Ward (`:30923-30925`), is the only listing that could cover him, and no source says goblinoids use it.
>   - Undermountain lies below the city and outside city jurisdiction (`campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md:21`). The published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* puts his worship there, on Levels 3 and 14, and those scenes are in the GM Notes below.

> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are War and Order (PHB, 2014). War Domain (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the best fit, because he is a god of war and the sources give his clergy the work of advising generals and driving the tribes to war. Order Domain (derived; Tasha's Cauldron of Everything) is the alternative, for a cleric who commands the host, since he is a lawful evil tyrant and the hobgoblins learned their discipline from him. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), since his clergy advise generals and train the army's elite at Grashmog.
>   - The sources describe his clergy as goblin and hobgoblin priests and shamans, so a cleric of his faith is most at home as a goblin or hobgoblin character (derived). He is lawful evil, and his faith demands sacrifice, so the table should agree on an evil character before play.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Conquest (derived; Xanathar's Guide to Everything), a good fit. No source lists paladins among his followers. The oath calls to paladins who seek glory in battle and the subjugation of their enemies, which matches a tyrant who wants every false god conquered. Oath of Glory (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the alternative, since its stress on deeds and renown matches a faith in which a warrior earns his place in the Army of Immortals by proving himself in battle.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The nearest campaign content is the goblins and bugbears of Xanathar's Guild, described in the GM Notes below, and no file says any of them worships him. ## Overview

Maglubiyet is the lawful evil god of war and the ruler of the goblinoid gods. Goblins, hobgoblins and bugbears follow him, and his symbol is a bloody axe. His followers are taught that every other god is false and must be conquered, and that a warrior who proves himself in battle will join the god's Army of Immortals after death. Hobgoblins revere him, goblins serve him out of fear, and bugbears honor him mostly through their own gods. Waterdeep has no temple of his.

In **Three Nights**, the Guild's bugbears come for Ott Steeltoes on the first night (`campaign/quests/faction-events/bregan-daerthe/m03-three-nights/overview.md:31`). > - **Level 1, Dungeon Level (seed):** Nine bugbears, five of them controlled by intellect devourers, run the Guild's watch posts with goblin help (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:795`). The posts are Worg's Eye in area 23 (two bugbears and fifteen goblins), Grick Snack in area 28 (two bugbears and six goblins) and Big Ears in area 39 (three bugbears, nineteen goblins and two ettins) (`:3205`, `:3471`, `:3852`). The bugbears plan to kidnap an Undertakers leader and carry that person to Skullport for an intellect devourer (`:796`). The text names no god.
> - **Level 2, Arcane Chambers (seed):** The Rustbone tribe of goblins runs a bazaar in an old throne room, area 1, and opposes the Guild. Its boss, Yek, wears a circlet that turned him into a human, and his goblins resent taking orders from one (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:4009-4017`). The text names no god.
> - **Level 3, Sargauth Level (seed):** Goblinoids and drow vie for the ruins of Stromkuhldur. Azrok, the blind hobgoblin warlord of the Legion of Azrok, hides that his dagger of blindsight gives him sight, and other goblinoids believe Maglubiyet blessed him (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:7410`, `:7426`). Many of his goblins and bugbears have fled to Skullport to join the Guild (`:7430`). In area 21s, Azrok's War Chest, a 7-inch statuette of Maglubiyet carved from blood-red stone stands on the altar of what was once a dwarven shrine. It is not valuable (`:11196-11203`).
> - **Level 14, Arcturiadoom (seed):** Doomcrown, a hobgoblin and Azrok's son, is a devout follower of Maglubiyet and wants fire giants to rule the giant ordning (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:7427`, `:37720`). In area 9c a hobgoblin captain leads twenty hobgoblins at attention in a liturgy to the dread god (`:39503`).
> - **Skullport:** The Skullport section names no shrine to him. The only goblinoid text there is a line on werebats of goblin stock (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:64380`).
> - **The Vault of Dragons fresco:** Room V3 of the vault carries a 20-foot fresco of dwarves battling goblins, which charms viewers who fail a save (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:14570`; `campaign/structure/arc-j-vault-of-dragons.md:146`). No source ties it to Maglubiyet. The vault's location is a quest secret, so keep it out of any answer about him.
> - **The Zariel link:** The Zariel page records that hobgoblins who leave his pantheon join her cults (`campaign/setting/deities/zariel.md:38`).

**Tone:** Harsh and martial. Maglubiyet is a tyrant who rules by fear, so describe what his followers are taught and what the host demands of them before the god himself, and keep him at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he acts.
