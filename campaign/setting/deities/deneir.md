*The First Scribe*

- **Title:** God of Writing (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Lord of All Glyphs and Images, The Scribe of Oghma (FRHoF); the Prattler, a derogatory name (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Writing, reading, literacy, glyphs, illustration that records the truth, and the keeping of records (derived). The wiki portfolio is "Literature, literacy" for 5e and adds cartography, glyphs, images and scribes for 2e and 3e; no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Arcana, Knowledge (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A lit candle above an open eye (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Neutral Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Elysium (Amoria) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Sages, scribes, students, teachers (FRHoF)

> Record the truth accurately and teach everyone to read it.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Deneir is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and the servant of Oghma. The FRHoF calls him Oghma's scribe and sometimes his right hand. The wiki ranks him a lesser deity in its 2e, 3e and 5e rows and a demigod in its 1e row.
- **What the faith asks:** His followers hold that information not recorded and saved for later use is information lost (SCAG, 2014). They treat literacy as one of the god's most important gifts, and his priests teach people to read and write free of charge (FRHoF). Priests also take an oath of charity that obliges them to write letters and transcribe information for anyone who asks (SCAG, 2014).
- **Who follows:** Sages, scribes, students and teachers (FRHoF). Illustrators who want to convey concrete information or realism favor him over gods of creativity such as Lathander or Milil, and they acknowledge him before they begin and after they finish an illuminated manuscript, a woven wall hanging that tells a story, or any other work meant to record things as they are (FRHoF). The wiki's 2e row adds cartographers, illuminators and the Harpers.
- **Everyday prayer:** Anyone who writes a letter or records information may pray to Deneir to avoid mistakes (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). SCAG adds that the faithful believe he is hidden within the lines, shapes and passages of every written work, so they preserve works and also read them.
- **Clergy:** His priests tend to be individualists, united by their faith but unconcerned with hierarchy and protocol. They seldom establish temples of their own. They work out of temples of Oghma, schoolhouses and libraries, or visit people in their homes (FRHoF). SCAG says his blessings of divine magic fall more often on people who lose themselves in written works than on people who see themselves as part of a temple or order.
- **The Tome of Universal Harmony:** This two-thousand-page book is the seminal text of the faith. His followers have read it at least once, and many carry it everywhere. Copies are nonmagical, but his Chosen can use them to channel powerful magic from Deneir himself (FRHoF). SCAG says that contemplating the Tome is the most effective way to deserve his blessings.
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Deneir.
- **Messengers:** To reach mortals he uses books. Words on a page rearrange themselves into a message, a quill writes on a blank page by itself, or the crease between two open pages splits into a whispering, papery mouth (FRHoF). The wiki adds a book surrounded by glowing light, a glowing humanoid hand, letters of fire and candles (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** Shieldmeet (wiki, 2e; the 5e row is blank). Shieldmeet is the leap day that follows Midsummer, and 1492 DR is a Shieldmeet year by the calendar page's arithmetic. No file ties a city festival to him.
- **In legend:** The FRHoF says he is typically portrayed as scatterbrained and out of touch with mortal affairs. The wiki's 2e source describes an old, balding sage with a huge flaring beard who always carries a quill and parchment, trails off in the middle of sentences and drifts into long digressions, though every story he tells carries some vital piece of information (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Deneir, Oghma, Milil and Gond make up the Deities of Knowledge and Invention. Like Milil he acts as an intermediary for Oghma, carrying new information to him and to those he favors (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Oghma values his devotion to study and truth, though he suspects his servant is chasing half-imagined shadows (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - His scholarly outlook and magical interests made him popular among the gods of magic, including Mystra, Azuth and particularly Savras (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Lathander and Lliira are also among his allies, and Lliira enjoys making him uncomfortable (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - He counts as enemies those who hide or distort knowledge, such as Cyric, Mask and Shar, and those who destroy it, such as the Gods of Fury, notably Malar and Talos. These powers fill the normally collected scribe with rage (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - His purpose is to record. He searches for the Metatext, a single perfect work of writing reflected in snippets of every written work, which would unlock the secrets of the multiverse when read. Oghma embodies the creative spark, and Deneir records humanity's epiphanies (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - He works to see that everything known and true is accurately recorded in the Great Library, and he spends much of his time filing, indexing and maintaining what has been written and drawn (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - During the Spellplague he transcribed the Metatext into the remnants of the Weave and wrote himself into it, passing out of known existence (*The Ghost King*, novel; the wiki's 4e row calls him a dead power).
  - By 1489 DR he had been revived and was worshiped by many (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Deneir is alive and present in 1492 DR, and no file treats him as dead or absent. The Spellplague account is history and a conflict between wiki sources, listed in Draft Notes.

> #### Using Deneir as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god of everyone who writes things down. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says Deneir is the First Scribe, the god of writing and of pictures that tell the truth, shown by a lit candle above an open eye. They say a clerk or a letter-writer murmurs a prayer to him to avoid mistakes, and that a painter does the same before starting an illuminated manuscript. They add that his priests will teach you to read for nothing and will write your letter if you ask, and that the stories about him make him sound forgetful and a little out of date. The Dragon Heist city text says "no city in the world is as literate as Waterdeep" and credits Oghma's priests with the free reading lessons, so a Waterdhavian is more likely to name Oghma than Deneir when talking about learning to read. No source records how Waterdhavians talk about Deneir, so this voicing draws on the FRHoF, SCAG and the Dragon Heist text. [research]

## Overview

Deneir is the god of writing, known as the First Scribe, a neutral good power whose symbol is a lit candle above an open eye. Sages, scribes, students, teachers and illustrators honor him, and anyone writing a letter or keeping a record may pray to him to avoid mistakes. His priests teach reading for nothing and write letters for people who ask. They seldom keep temples of their own and work out of the temples of Oghma, schoolhouses and libraries. Waterdeep's list of major temples does not name a temple of his.

**Tone:** Patient, bookish and a little absent-minded. Deneir is the god of the written record, so describe the reading lessons, the copying and the careful prayers before a page is begun, and keep the legends of his forgetfulness light. Keep him in the present tense and at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he speaks.
