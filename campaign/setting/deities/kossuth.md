# Kossuth

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Kossuth, the Firelord and god of elemental fire, for players who want a Light cleric, a fire-priest or a devout character with an elemental power, and for a GM who needs a name behind a forge's heat, a cremation or a Thayan wizard's faith.
> - **Where it is used:** Kossuth never appears in person, and no campaign file or published adventure names him. The page records that Waterdeep gives him no temple, and that the nearest ties are the Red Wizards of Thay in the campaign files and a handful of fire-themed seeds in Undermountain.
> - **Featured in:** No quest. His name appears only on other Deities pages, as the fire power beside **Akadi**, **Grumbar** and **Istishia** and as the enemy of Istishia. The Red Wizards whom the wiki ties to his worship appear in **The Red Wizard's Price** and **Kolat Towers**.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Kossuth is secret.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Kossuth, and says plainly that no source gives him a temple, a festival or a worshiper in Waterdeep.

*The Firelord*

- **Title:** God of Fire (SCAG, 2014; the 5etools data have no FRHoF entry for him)
- **Other Names:** The Lord of Flames, the Tyrant-King, the Tyrant Among Fire (wiki). His followers and things of his faith are called Kossuthan (wiki, 2e).
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian (SCAG, 2014). The wiki also lists him among the Zakharan pantheon's cold gods of the elements.
- **Areas of Concern:** Fire, purification through fire, renewal and destruction by flame, and the cremation of the dead (derived from the wiki portfolios, which read "Fire" in 5e and "elemental fire, purification through fire" in 3e, and from the wiki's funeral note; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Light (SCAG, 2014). The wiki's 3e list is Destruction, Fire, Renewal, Suffering and Wrath.
- **Symbol:** Flame (SCAG, 2014). The wiki's 3e row gives a twining red flame, and its 2e row gives a springing flame or a flaming orb.
- **Alignment:** Neutral (SCAG, 2014). The wiki gives neutral in 1e through 3e and unaligned in 4e.
- **Plane:** The Elemental Plane of Fire (wiki, 3e), in the Crimson Pillar (wiki, 2e). The wiki's 4e row gives his realm as the Undying Pyre.
- **Typical Followers:** Druids, elemental archons of fire, fire creatures and Thayans (wiki, 3e)

> Let fire purify what it touches, and let no other element rule.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Kossuth is a power of the Faerûnian pantheon and one of the four elemental powers, with Akadi, Grumbar and Istishia (derived from the wiki's count of four elemental deities on its Akadi page and from the SCAG titles). The wiki ranks him a greater deity in its 2e and 3e rows, a lesser power in 1e and a deity in 5e. In 4e he is a primordial (see Draft Notes).
- **What he is:** He is an elemental being who has ruled fire since the earliest moments of the multiverse. Whether the Kossuth of the late fifteenth century is that same primordial or one of a line of successors with the same name is a matter of conjecture. He rarely meddles in the mortal world and spends most of his time in the intrigues of the Inner Planes (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **What the faith teaches:** Its doctrine is elemental supremacy, which all but guarantees conflict with the other elemental lords (wiki, as the paragraph cites *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **The church:** It is hierarchical and mostly impersonal, and its aims tend to revolve around acquiring land, power and wealth. Its followers and clerics are often lawful, without regard for good or evil (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
- **Who follows:** The wiki's 3e row lists druids, elemental archons of fire, fire creatures and Thayans. Many Red Wizards of Thay worship him (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Arcana Unleashed: Deadfall*), and the Flaming Brazier of Eltabbar was supposedly the largest temple of Kossuth in all the Realms (*Unclean*). The Akadi and Grumbar pages of this journal record, from the wiki, that every elemental power except Kossuth has only a small following (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Priesthoods:** The wiki names four, the Burning Braziers, the Black Flame Zealots, the Brothers and Sisters of the Pure Flame and the Order of the Salamander (*Unclean*). No source describes them further.
- **Monastic orders:** Three monasteries follow him, and each stresses a different aspect of the god (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*). The Disciples of the Phoenix are the good branch, who rigidly order their members' studies and behavior and focus on purification. The Disciples of the Salamander are the evil branch, who see fire as necessary for renewal, and some of their members hold a view of destruction that borders on Talos's. The Brothers and Sisters of the Purifying Flame are the neutral branch, who balance purification against destruction and act as go-betweens for the other two. His monks can become clerics of the Firelord without giving up their monk training.
- **Funerals:** The bodies of worshipers are cremated after death (*Unclean*).
- **Regional worship:** In Zakhara he is one of the cold gods of the elements, seen as uncaring toward human affairs and opposed to the land's culture of Enlightenment. Few Zakharans will worship a cold god to gain power (*Adventurer's Guide to Zakhara*).
- **How he appears:** He manifests as a red-hot column of flame more than 60 feet high and 20 feet across. His voice carries for hundreds of yards in every direction as a hissing whisper mixed with crackling and popping (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Divine realm:** The Crimson Pillar is a globe of primordial fire ten miles wide that floats over the Sea of Fire on the Elemental Plane of Fire. Its blue-white flames give off a heat that makes the rest of the plane seem chilly (wiki, 2e row, citing *The Inner Planes*).
- **Messengers and omens:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** No source gives a date. The wiki's holy-day fields are blank. [research]
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Kossuth, and the wiki names none.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He is vehemently opposed to Istishia and his clergy (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - He watches the upstart archomental Imix, who is always trying to undermine him, though Kossuth remains the true master of fire. The wiki names the Grand Sultan of the Efreet as the only being on the Plane of Fire with even a slightly comparable level of power (*The Inner Planes*).
  - He interacts very little with the other powers of Faerûn. Moradin and Flandal Steelskin honor him for the heat of the forge, and he barely responds (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - The return of Bane pleased him. The two seem to agree on the importance of a strong religious hierarchy, and both are intolerant of the ephemeral and unpredictable nature of chaos (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - In -150 DR powerful Raumatharan wizards summoned him from his elemental home and told him to destroy a besieging army from Narfell. He did so, then turned on the wizards and burned their city for their arrogance. The fire consumed much of the northern coast of the Alamber Sea and burned for more than a decade after he withdrew from Toril (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - In 801 DR he appeared again to help some of his faithful destroy a colossal octopus (*Icewind Dale: Heart of Winter*, video game).
  - His faithful credit him with limiting the Salamander War in Thay in 1357 DR, and they say it would have been a greater debacle without him (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - He was not among the deities sighted in Faerûn when Ao exiled the gods in the Time of Troubles of 1358 DR (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - The 4e *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide* presents him as a primordial, a being whose power rivaled that of a true deity, and SCAG (5e) lists him as a god again.
- **In the campaign:** Kossuth is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. SCAG still lists him as a god, and the wiki's 4e account of a primordial is history. Draft Notes records the conflict.

> #### Using Kossuth as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as a distant power that most people never think about. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Firelord, the god of fire, shown by a flame, and that he is one of the four elemental powers. They add that his church is a stiff hierarchy that cares more for land, power and wealth than for its worshipers, that many of his followers are Red Wizards of Thay, and that his dead are burned. Priests of other gods say he and Istishia are bitter enemies. How Waterdeep feels about his worship, and which of his names the city uses, is unsourced. [research]

> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Light (SCAG, 2014), and the wiki's 3e list adds Destruction, Fire, Renewal, Suffering and Wrath. The matching subclass is Light Domain (derived; Player's Handbook, 2024), an exact fit by name whose always-prepared spells include burning hands, scorching ray, fireball, wall of fire and flame strike. ## Overview

Kossuth, the Firelord, is the neutral god of fire and of purification through fire. Sages count him among the four elemental powers and describe a stiff, impersonal church that seeks land, power and wealth. Many of his followers are Red Wizards of Thay, and his dead are cremated. He is a bitter enemy of Istishia, and Waterdeep has no temple of his.

**Tone:** Plain and a little remote. Kossuth is an elemental power who rarely meddles in the mortal world, so describe the heat, the smoke and the ash before the god, and keep him at a distance. The campaign writes no scene in which he acts.
