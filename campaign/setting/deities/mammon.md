**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what little an ordinary Waterdhavian knows about Mammon, gives a player what a Fiend patron relationship with him costs, and states that the campaign has tied nothing to him so that no later page invents more.

*The King of Greed*

- **Title:** Lord of the Third, Archduke of Minauros (wiki; *Fiendish Codex II* 3e and *Dungeon Master's Guide* 5e)
- **Other Names:** Minauros, the Serpent, Lord of Avarice, Lord of Lust, King of Covetousness, Viscount of Minauros (wiki; 3e, 4e and 5e sources as tagged on the page)
- **Pantheon:** None. He is an archdevil and a patron, not a god of the Faerûnian pantheon.
- **Areas of Concern:** Greed, wealth, the soul trade, bargains made in gold (derived from the wiki's accounts of his work and his cults; the wiki lists none)
- **Domains:** Not applicable to 2024 cleric subclasses. The wiki's 3e row lists Diabolic, Evil, Greed and Trickery.
- **Symbol:** An open pair of scaled red hands above a diamond-shaped black gem, or a medallion-sized coin stamped with his serpent form (3e, via the wiki)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Evil (3e, via the wiki; its 4e row says Evil and its 5e field is blank)
- **Challenge:** [research] for 5e, since the wiki's 5e field is blank. The wiki gives 25 for 3e.
- **Plane:** Minauros, the third layer of the Nine Hells (wiki; 5e)
- **Typical Followers:** Dragons and duergar (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e, via the wiki). The wiki's 3e row adds beholders, evil dragons and mind flayers.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Mammon is an archdevil and one of the Lords of the Nine, though the wiki calls him a lesser archdevil in Hell's politics, short of allies and trusted by nobody. It says he may be the richest being in existence (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e). The wiki gives him no calendar of holy days.
- **His nature:** He is a miser whose only loyalty is to coin, two-faced in body and in manner (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e; *Guide to Hell* 3e). He measures everything by monetary value and makes deals only for profit. He is also hungry for power and so paranoid that he keeps a supply of worthless devils on hand to fire at a moment's notice (*Fiendish Codex II* 3e; *Dungeon Master's Guide* 5e). He grovels to Asmodeus and looks down on his inferiors. His speech winds through unclear requests, even when he is giving orders (*Dragon* 223, edition not given).
- **What he sells:** He is one of the few archdevils who will bargain in gold instead of souls. He makes loans in exchange for items and services, and he hires mercenaries against demonic hordes in return for the invaders' loot. His soul collectors wait on the banks of the River Styx where it crosses Minauros, and they log the lemures they haul away by contract and current law. Surplus petitioners are sold for money (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e). He and his greater devils created the soul coins that circulate through the Nine Hells (*Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus* 5e).
- **The soul trade:** His harvest of souls is the most bountiful of any archdevil's, and he pays for it with raw wealth (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e). His agents use a guidebook, *The Accounting and Valuation of All Things*, to price a soul against other goods, and they are often startled by how little a soul costs (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e).
- **How his agents work:** He is patron of the greedy and the lustful, and he draws in materialists who enjoy denying wealth to others as much as having it. He sends devils to mortal lands to wreck economies, where they talk the rich out of charity, sabotage industries and bankrupt prosperous nations, so that the desperate millions end up in Minauros (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e; *Fiendish Codex II* 3e).
- **What cultists get:** His power lets followers steal and deceive with near-supernatural skill. They can grasp unattended items from several feet away and bend the idea of value in other minds. A cultist invited to his court may leave with wealth beyond a mortal's imagination (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e; *Fiendish Codex II* 3e).
- **Initiation and clergy:** A neophyte must betray a close friend for personal gain and perform a ritual that is demeaning and disgusting (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e). His clerics are called covetors and wear gold-trimmed red robes and golden ornaments. Rogues and bards who care more about money than murder sometimes serve him as disciples (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e).
- **Who follows:** Duergar are the 5e worshipers (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e). The wiki's cult text adds goblinoids, beholders, and mind flayers, who supply him with a team of information gatherers, and it calls the more sociable evil dragons his favorite followers (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e; *Fiendish Codex II* 3e).
- **Where cults sit:** His cultists gain their money through dirty dealing, coercion and extortion, and the wiki says they are found at the peak of high society and in merchant and trade guilds (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e; *Fiendish Codex II* 3e).
- **How followers behave:** Like their patron, they switch between attitudes to manage other people. They lie, cheat and steal as needed, betray allies to get ahead, and keep moving from town to town before their reputation catches up (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e; *Fiendish Codex II* 3e).
- **Temples:** They are gaudy to the point of absurdity, with altars, sacrificial daggers and walls made of gold and set with gems. Cultists use them for back-alley deals and commercial pacts, and the wards, traps and guardians are usually non-humanoid monsters that serve as cultists (*Fiendish Codex II* 3e; *Book of Vile Darkness* 3e).
- **Holy days, omens, messengers:** None in the wiki or the campaign. [research] The aspects under the next bullet are the only emissaries the wiki describes.
- **Aspects:** The wiki says his aspects are common in Faerûn, often in the guise of yuan-ti. They dislike sunny weather and keep to cities, from behind the walls of Zhentil Keep to the streets of Sembia and the alleys of Waterdeep. They own nothing except the gear for their task, and everything else they acquire goes to Mammon (*Fiendish Codex II Fiendish Aspects*, web article, 2007).
- **His realm:** Minauros is a mire under constant rain, lit by yellow-green swamp gas, and its capital, the Sinking City, is slowly dropping into the muck. His palace Galbuach is a gem-studded golden hall, and it sinks with the rest (*Manual of the Planes* 4e; *Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e; *Dragon* 75, edition not given). He spends almost nothing on upkeep and uses his treasury mainly to keep the layer's soul market running (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e). The wiki says his hoard is vast and that nothing in it ever leaves his vaults (*Dragon* 91, edition not given).
- **Servants:** Barbazu haul carts and trade with other layers, gelugons lead his armies, and osyluths and hamatulas make up most of the rank and file (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e; *Fiendish Codex II* 3e). His agents copy his greed and take risks to guard their gold (*Dragon* 223, edition not given).
- **His court:** He neglects Minauros and leaves it to his seneschal Focalor, who despises him and wants to undermine him. His chief vassal, the general Bael, plots to seize the layer, and the other dukes scheme against him as well (*Dragon* 75, edition not given; *Dragon* 360, edition not given).
- **His daughter:** Baelzra, whom he had with the archdevil Sagirsa, runs an illegal soul-harvesting operation on Avernus that brings him a share of the profit. He still dotes on her despite her exile (*The Plane Above* 4e; *Fiendish Codex II* 3e).
- **History of his fall:** Wiki-sourced, and the account is short. After the Reckoning he was the first to retreat and submit to Asmodeus, and he was disgraced and punished despite the groveling. His relationship with Glasya ended, and he changed his name to Minauros (*Guide to Hell* 3e; *A Paladin in Hell*, edition not given). Asmodeus then changed his natural form, perhaps because Mammon asked him to as proof that he was a new devil who would not betray him (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e; *Guide to Hell* 3e). The sources disagree on the form he received (see Draft Notes). Some mortals mistake him for Baalzebul, since both were given forms they hated as punishment (*Fiendish Codex II* 3e; *Faces of Evil: The Fiends*, edition not given).
- **Relations with other archdevils and powers:** Wiki-sourced, with the book named on each fact.
  - Asmodeus is the master he fawns on. Mammon began groveling the moment Asmodeus won the Reckoning, and he betrayed his own partners without a second thought, which earned him the scorn of every other archdevil (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e).
  - Dispater was once his ally, and Mephistopheles with him, but the wiki says he likely never trusted the Iron Duke (*Monster Manual II*, 1st edition).
  - Glasya was his consort. One account says Asmodeus forced her on him as a punishment, and another says she came to Minauros willingly, and the relationship ended after the Reckoning (*Dungeon* 197 and *Guide to Hell*, editions not given; *Book of Vile Darkness* and *Fiendish Codex II*, 3e).
  - Tiamat despises him for luring away her dragon worshipers with an appeal to their greed (*Fiendish Codex II* 3e).
- **In the campaign:** Mammon rules Minauros in 1492 DR, as every power in this journal is present in that year. The wiki writes of him in the past tense, and the Reckoning is history.

A sage can repeat the Overview, and a merchant or a moneylender is the likeliest to have heard the name, as the devil who buys souls with gold (derived from his followers and his trade). > - **Waterdeep:** None. The Major Temples of Waterdeep table in *Dragon Heist* lists no temple of Mammon, and no campaign file places a shrine or cult in the city. The wiki's one Waterdeep item is that his aspects lurk in the alleys of Waterdeep. No campaign scene uses it, and this page does not place an aspect anywhere (see Draft Notes).
> - **A warlock PC:** The Fiend patron in the 2024 *Player's Handbook* fits him (derived). The wiki does not file him under Fiend patrons and gives no Mammon-specific features. [research] What he wants from a bargain is souls and gold, and he is the archdevil to approach for a deal paid in coin instead of a soul (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e). The price is steep either way. The wiki's quotation box says closing the deal without offering a soul takes the wealth of a dozen kingdoms. He is also duplicitous, and he seduces his victims with silver-tongued promises before he watches their faces as he betrays them (*Fiendish Codex II* 3e). The wiki gives no worked example of a warlock with a Mammon pact.
> - **A cleric or paladin PC:** The wiki gives him covetor clerics, and its 3e domains include Trickery. Trickery Domain is the nearest 2024 subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War (derived), though the wiki's other domains (Diabolic, Evil, Greed) fit none of the four. No source gives a Divine Order, a paladin follower or an oath, and no source places a cleric of his in Waterdeep.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The Asmodeus page names him once, in its list of archdevil relations ("Mammon fawns"), and the Tiamat page names him as the archdevil she resented for converting some evil dragons. Nothing in the Vault of Dragons material, Neverember's hoard or the moneylender Istrid Horn, who worships Vergadain, refers to him.
> - **Never reveal:** That the Cassalanters' wealth comes from a pact, and with whom (see the Asmodeus page). No file connects Mammon to the family, so a player who hears "archdevil of greed" and asks whether a rich family follows him gets the Overview and nothing more. Do not invent a Mammon cult in Waterdeep to answer the question.

## Overview

Mammon is the archdevil who rules Minauros, the third layer of the Nine Hells, and sages call him the King of Greed. He is said to be among the richest beings in existence, and unlike most devils he will bargain in gold as well as in souls. Scholars describe him as a miser and a flatterer who grovels before Asmodeus and trusts no one else. His servants are said to talk the rich out of charity and to wreck whole economies, so that the ruined end up in his layer.

**Tone:** Oily and eager. Mammon is a banker who flatters those above him and bullies those below, so when a player asks about him, describe what he will pay and what he wants in return before describing what he does to the people who accept.
