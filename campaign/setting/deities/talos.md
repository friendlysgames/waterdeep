# Talos

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Talos, the Storm Lord and god of storms and destruction, for players who want a raider, a storm-minded cleric or a devout character with a feared patron, and for a GM who needs a power to stand behind bad weather, brigands or a wandering doomsayer.
> - **Where it is used:** Talos never appears in person, and no campaign file names him. The published *Dragon Heist* does not name him either. The page records the one *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* line that names his Underdark alias, so that no later page invents more.
> - **Featured in:** No quest. His name appears only on other Deities pages, as the leader of the Gods of Fury with **Malar**, and as a power that **Chauntea**, **Deneir**, **Gond**, **Helm**, **Ilmater**, **Lathander**, **Mielikki**, **Oghma**, **Silvanus** and **Tyr** oppose.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Talos is secret.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Talos, and says plainly that no source gives him a temple, a festival or a worshiper in Waterdeep.

*The Storm Lord*

- **Title:** God of Storms (FRHoF; SCAG and PHB, 2014, print "God of storms")
- **Other Names:** The Destroyer, the Raging One (FRHoF); the Stormlord (SCAG, 2014, spelled as one word); the Stormstar, the Wildfire (wiki). Regional names: Bhaelros in Calimshan (FRHoF), Kozah in ancient Netheril and among the Bedine, and Malyk in the Underdark (wiki).
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian, and leader of the Gods of Fury (FRHoF)
- **Areas of Concern:** Storms, destruction, forest fires, earthquakes, tornadoes, raiding and banditry (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Tempest (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Three lightning bolts radiating from a point (SCAG, 2014; the PHB, 2014 entry says "a central point")
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Pandemonium (Pandesmos) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Barbarians, druids, raiders (FRHoF)

> Take what you can while you can, because the storm may strike at any moment.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Talos is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and the leader of the Gods of Fury. The wiki ranks him a greater deity in its 2e and 3e rows and a deity in its 5e rows, and its 4e rows are blank.
- **The Gods of Fury:** FRHoF says he rules over Auril, Malar and Umberlee, though it is said Malar would kill him if he could. The wiki adds that he gathered them by force and keeps them under tight control (*On Hallowed Ground*). The wiki's Umberlee page also cites SCAG (5e) for the First Circle, a loose group of nature gods that includes Talos, Auril, Malar, Umberlee, Chauntea, Eldath, Mielikki and Silvanus.
- **What he is:** Talos is the dark side of nature, the uncaring and destructive force that may strike at any time (SCAG, 2014). FRHoF says his acts seem petty and vengeful, driven by anger and by a need to posture among the other gods. The wiki describes a short-tempered bully who holds a grudge for centuries and who exists to prove that nothing can be built that he cannot destroy (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *On Hallowed Ground*).
- **What the faith asks:** His followers are taught that life is chaos and that each person should seize what they can, when they can, because nobody knows when Talos will strike and send them on to the afterlife (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). They love destruction and call storms down on towns in his name. Many become brigands who wander from place to place demanding loot, and some destroy for the sake of destroying (FRHoF).
- **How the world treats him:** Day-to-day worship is outlawed in most of Faerûn, though people make desperate offerings to save themselves from natural disasters (FRHoF). He is a popular god whose name is invoked more out of fear than reverence (SCAG, 2014).
- **Who follows:** Ravagers, raiders, looters and brigands (SCAG, 2014), along with the barbarians and druids that FRHoF lists. The wiki's 3e row adds fighters, half-orcs and people who fear the destructive power of nature.
- **Clergy:** His priests have few temples and travel like a wayward storm. They wear ragged robes and cloaks shot through with jagged lines of gold and silver, and many wear an eye patch in deference to the god (FRHoF). SCAG says most are traveling doomsayers who warn of disasters to come and accept charity in exchange for blessings of protection, and that many of the faithful wear a black eyepatch even when both eyes are sound.
- **How the clergy works:** The wiki says the clergy has no formal hierarchy and enforces obedience through strength. Priests extort sailors and farmers with the threat of storms, chase wealth and luxury, and win converts through fear or the lure of raw power. Many train as barbarians, sorcerers or wizards (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). The same sources dress the clerics in black robes marked with teardrops and jagged lines of gold and silver, and the high clergy in blue-white robes streaked with crimson.
- **Pilgrimage:** Priests and devout worshipers travel to the Gulf of Storms, a canyon in eastern Amn. Iron ore in its walls draws electrical charges, and the lightning feeds storms that race down the valley across Amn and Tethyr (FRHoF).
- **Holy sites:** Most are secret because of the church's reputation. A public church is often a castle or fortified stronghold on an earthquake fault or in the path of a storm or lava flow, and Talos keeps it unharmed (wiki, *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Rites:** Clerics celebrate with ceremonies that summon lightning and storms. In Calling Down the Thunder an intelligent being is sacrificed to a lightning strike. In the Fury the cleric prays, makes berserk attacks to wreck as much as possible in a short time, and prays again (wiki, *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Orders:** The wiki says orders tied to him are shrouded in rumor and names two speculated groups. The Lords of the Tempest are wizards who combine elemental magic, and the Circle of Rust and the Worm is a cabal of insane sages who want to destroy the world. No file places either in Waterdeep.
- **Divine realm:** The Towers of Ruin, also called the Screaming Towers, lie in Pandesmos and are always ringed by a howling whirlwind (wiki, *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Holy days:** No source gives a date. The wiki's holy-day fields are blank. [research]
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Talos. FRHoF says his Chosen cast spells such as *Storm of Vengeance*, *Earthquake* and *Meteor Swarm* against cities and villages that do not bend to him.
- **How he appears:** Religious art shows a broad-shouldered, bearded man with an eye patch (FRHoF). SCAG makes him young, with a single good eye and the other covered by a dark patch. The wiki adds half-plate over black leather and an empty socket filled with whirling stars (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). In Calimshan he has a genie-like look (FRHoF), and the wiki describes a turbaned genie rising out of a sandstorm.
- **His staffs:** He is said to carry three staffs made from the first tree cut down, the first silver smelted and the first iron forged. With them he raises destructive winds, brings terrible storms and splits the land. The three bolts of his symbol stand for the staffs, and he is thought to hurl them down as lightning when he vents his wrath (SCAG, 2014).
- **Messengers and omens:** The Raging One manifests as bellowing laughter in the heart of strong winds or as red lightning strikes in a storm (FRHoF). The wiki's manifestation field adds a pair of fist-sized storm clouds with lightning and thunder. No source gives a formal omen.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Auril is allegedly close and cordial with him, because her outlook matches his, yet he tries to erode her power and take her portfolio. She calls on Umberlee for help more often than on him, since he claims the glory and the worship afterward (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - His bond with Umberlee is flirtatious and competitive. He has been taking over her portfolio, and she would kill him to become the only god of destruction. Until then she distracts him with romance, and her priests kill anyone who credits him with the storms of the sea and shore (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - Malar is the worst of his relationships. They cooperate only grudgingly (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - He courted Beshaba, perhaps to add misfortune to his control of destruction, and she turned him down (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Akadi can be asked to move the wind or bring gentle rain, but she can neither make nor end true storms, which belong to Talos, or to Umberlee at sea (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e, as the wiki's Akadi page cites it).
  - He hated gods of learning, building and nature, and anyone who tried to change the weather for long (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Chauntea opposed deities of destruction, above all Talos and the Gods of Fury. Silvanus hated him because Talos ignored the Balance, and Silvanus's priests call such gods the Unbalanced. Mielikki and Eldath also opposed him (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Oghma and Deneir opposed him for destroying knowledge, and Deneir, who is usually calm, burned with rage against the Gods of Fury. Gond's one true foe is Talos, who threatens his inventions and his dominion over destructive devices (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Tyr held a particular enmity for him, Helm had to fight his destructive impulses at every turn, and Savras disliked his pointless death and destruction (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
  - Valkur was the sworn enemy of the Gods of Fury, above all Umberlee, because he protects sailors from what they unleash (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
  - Sune disliked him for destroying beautiful things (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). Lathander blamed him and a few other evil gods for most of Toril's ills, and Lathander, Sune and Mystra, along with Chauntea, worked to limit the effect of his rages (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *On Hallowed Ground*). Mystra did not get along with him (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He sprang up from the wreckage of the first battle between Selûne and Shar, near the dawn of the world (FRHoF; *Netheril: Empire of Magic*). The people of ancient Netheril worshiped him as Kozah and called him the Destroyer and the Raging One. The Bedine of Anauroch, who descend from Netherese, still called him Kozah in the fourteenth century DR (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Calimshan and Airspur in Chessenta worshiped him as Bhaelros (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Old Empires*). In the Underdark a small following knew him as Malyk, a god of wild magic. As Malyk he tried to fold wild magic into his portfolio, until Mystra challenged him openly and most wild magic zones were neutralized (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e).
  - In the months after the Time of Troubles in 1358 DR, his clergy proclaimed that violent death fell within his portfolio. It was unclear whether Cyric could answer the claim (*Forgotten Realms Adventures*, 2e).
  - After the Spellplague of 1385 DR he was thought to be severely weakened (*The Rose of Sarifal*, novel). In an event called the Stormstar Requiem he was said to have revealed himself as the orcish god Gruumsh, which got him expelled from the Gods of Fury (*Dragon* 367, 4e era). Many came to believe he was an aspect of Gruumsh (*Dragon* 378, 4e era).
  - He was still feared through the late fifteenth century DR alongside Bane and Asmodeus, though he could do little more than visit his faithful in dreams and nightmares (*The Rose of Sarifal*, novel).
  - The wiki says he returned to the pantheon after the Second Sundering. The Second Sundering ended in 1487 DR and undid the merger by ruling, so Talos and Gruumsh are separate gods again. In the late 1480s DR he sided with Umberlee in her fight with Auril and forced Auril to retreat (*Icewind Dale: Rime of the Frostmaiden*, 5e). The Talos wiki says she retreated to Icewind Dale, and the **Auril** (Deities) page follows the Auril wiki's Sea of Moving Ice.
  - After the Battle of Leilon in the late fifteenth century DR he was furious with Fheralai Stormsworn for failing him, and his avatar appeared to punish her Cult of Talos (*Divine Contention*, organized play).
- **In the campaign:** Talos is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. FRHoF describes him in the present tense as the ruler of the Gods of Fury. He and Gruumsh are separate gods. The Spellplague weakening, the Stormstar Requiem and the Gruumsh identity are history, and Draft Notes records them.

## Overview

Talos, the Storm Lord, is the chaotic evil god of storms, forest fires, earthquakes and tornadoes, and Waterdhavians picture him as a bearded man with one eye hidden under a patch. He leads the Gods of Fury, who are Auril, Malar and Umberlee. Raiders, looters and brigands follow him, and most other people say his name out of fear and make desperate offerings only when a disaster threatens. Worship of him is outlawed in most of Faerûn, and Waterdeep has no temple of his.

**Tone:** Grave and plain, with no spectacle. Talos is the uncaring side of nature, so describe the weather and what it leaves behind before the god, and let fear rather than devotion carry the page. Keep him at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he acts.
