**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Silvanus, and states exactly what the sources place in the city so that no later page invents more.

*The Oak Father*

- **Title:** God of Wild Nature (FRHoF; SCAG and PHB p.294 print the same). The PHB p.298 entry, from the Celtic pantheon, prints "God of nature and forests".
- **Other Names:** The Old Oak, Old Father Tree (FRHoF). The wiki adds the Forest Father and Treefather (3e), the God of Wild Nature (*Crucible*) and the Green Father (*Prayers from the Faithful*).
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian (FRHoF). The wiki and the PHB p.298 entry also list him in the Celtic pantheon, where he began (see Origin and rise).
- **Areas of Concern:** Wild nature, forests, mountains and plains, the cycle of growth and decay, druids (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki portfolio "Wild nature"; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Nature (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Oak leaf (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** The Outlands (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Barbarians, druids, rangers, wood elves (FRHoF)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Silvanus is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki calls him a greater god in its 1e, 2e and 3e rows and a plain deity in its 5e rows.
- **What he governs:** FRHoF ties him to forests, mountains and plains, although in truth he stands for all of nature, deserts as well as forests and sharks as much as deer. In legends he commands the other nature deities and hands out rewards and punishments to them as he sees fit. Folk in the North, who face the dangers of forests, mountains and plains, think of him as a grim and severe father who metes out flood and drought, fire and ice, and life and death in the wilderness (SCAG, 2014).
- **Temperament:** Accounts of his personality vary widely. Many see a vengeful god who looks on the people of Faerûn with the aloof disgust of a shopkeeper facing an infestation of rats. His worshipers hold that he despises no one and only wants nature's balance kept, since the wilderness is brutally fair about life and death and so is the Oak Father (FRHoF).
- **How the wiki describes him:** He cares greatly about the balance of growth and decay, water and drought, and fire and ice. He is emotionally distant about it, which led some to call him heartless although he valued all life (3e and 4e sources; *Dragon* 412). He pays little attention to the schemes of mortals and other gods, and he holds a special hatred for anyone who uses fire carelessly or to destroy (*On Hallowed Ground*; *Dragon* 412; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **What the faith teaches:** Wild nature is the true state of the world, so its glory must be preserved, and its beauty is only part of the reason. Nature refreshes and revitalizes the mortal soul and gives breath to the world. His faithful oppose settlements spreading into wild places and treat the excessive use of natural resources as wasteful and blasphemous (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **How his priests think:** They try to see the whole situation and the macrocosm instead of one person's or one nation's idea of what is best. SCAG gives the example of a farming community lost to goblin raids, which is a tragedy for some and a chance for the wilderness to grow back and make the land fertile again (SCAG, 2014).
- **Who follows:** Barbarians, druids, rangers and wood elves (FRHoF). Travelers in wild lands, explorers and the residents of rural communities far from a local lord or a great city often honor him (SCAG, 2014). The wiki adds hermits and wilderness dwellers (*Forgotten Realms Player's Guide*, 4e) and a few halfling druids who prefer him to their own pantheon's nature god (*Unearthed Arcana* and the *Cyclopedia of the Realms*, 1e).
- **Clergy:** Druids make up most of his following (FRHoF). The wiki says clerics also serve him, with druids more prominent because his focus on wild nature leaves little room for balance with civilization, and that their numbers vary by region (1e and 3e sources).
- **What the clergy do:** The wiki has them protect the wilderness from encroachment, which means stopping the felling of trees and keeping farms and ranches to land already cleared. They also fight disease, plant new growth and oppose the servants of Malar (*Forgotten Realms Player's Guide*, 4e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms*).
- **Shrines and temples:** The oak leaf is his symbol. Villagers may dedicate a grove of oaks nearby as a shrine to him, and where oaks do not grow, an oak leaf etched into the bark of another kind of tree marks a sacred site. Official temples are few and far between and are likely to be hidden deep in a forest instead of in a busy city (FRHoF).
- **Worship by region:** The wiki places his worship in east and south Tethyr, especially the Forest of Tethir, and in the Dalelands, the Moonshae Isles, the Chondalwood of the Vilhon Reach and the Yuirwood in Aglarond (*Empires of the Sands*; *A Grand Tour of the Realms*, 2e; *Dragon* 412). It names no worship in Waterdeep.
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Silvanus, and the wiki names none. FRHoF says he tasks his Chosen with presiding over a forest, a desert, a lake or another natural region. Some form a bond with their domain like a dryad's with its sanctuary, and feel the pain when it suffers or comes under attack.
- **Messengers:** To speak directly to his followers he sends a dryad, a treant or a twig blight (FRHoF). He can also bring the natural world to sentient life whenever he wishes, so that streams find their own courses and trees uproot to walk (FRHoF).
- **Omens:** The wiki lists an oak leaf blown out of nowhere, and an eerie green glow that quenches fire and comes with the sound of faint whistling wind and running or dripping water (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Holy days:** The wiki's 4e row lists Greengrass, Midsummer and Highharvestide. Its 3e row lists Greengrass, Midsummer night, Highharvestide and the Night the Forest Walks. The FRHoF and 5e rows give none. Greengrass falls between Tarsakh 30 and Mirtul 1, inside the campaign calendar (see Timekeeping and the Festival Calendar), but no file ties a Waterdeep observance to him.
- **How he appears:** Wiki-sourced (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - As the Old Father he is a bearded human face with brown, gray and fissured flesh like old wood, floating among trees or growing from the trunk of a very old one.
  - As the Young Strider he is a young, long-limbed man in armor made of leaves, with a wooden mallet. He most often takes this form when a sacred grove is threatened or a circle of druids is attacked at worship.
  - As the Horned Hunter he is a silent man with burning white eyes, stag antlers and shaggy, bark-like fur. He appears among the trees, gestures or nods to point out something, and disappears.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named where the wiki gives one.
  - He is allied with and served by the other non-evil gods of animals and nature, among them **Eldath**, **Mielikki** and Shiallia (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Cyclopedia of the Realms*, 1e; *Running the Realms*, 2e). Eldath saw him as a father figure and found his robustness intimidating, and Mielikki made the *banish blight* spell partly as a gift to him (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - His clergy often work with the clergy of Mielikki and Eldath. In one account those two clergies are told to support, protect and, if the need arises, obey the clergy of Silvanus (*Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms*).
  - **Chauntea** and **Lathander** are also his allies, and he sometimes worked with them against the Gods of Fury (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Cyclopedia of the Realms*, 1e). The wiki adds Ki, the nature goddess of the Untheric pantheon (*On Hallowed Ground*).
  - Moander, **Malar**, **Talona** and **Talos** are his enemies (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). He actively opposed the last three, because their love of destruction upsets the balance of nature (3e).
  - The 4e books make Malar his exarch. After the Spellplague, Malar became subject to his rule as a resident of the Deep Wilds, and Silvanus did not rein him in (Richard Baker, 2008, cited by the wiki).
  - A legend says he fathered Mielikki with the elven goddess Hanali Celanil. The wiki says it grew out of the honorific "Daughter of Silvanus", which Mielikki's worshipers gave her because of her close alliance with him (*Demihuman Deities*; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced (*On Hallowed Ground*; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Powers & Pantheons*).
  - He began as a god of the Celtic pantheon. Around the rise of Netheril a wave of planar immigrants came to Toril from another world, and one group with a culture tied to nature, the sea and the warrior history of its greater god brought the Celtic gods with them.
  - As Celtic gods commonly did when their pantheon settled on a world, he joined one of the local pantheons. The wiki says he was being pushed out of power in his own.
- **Realm:** FRHoF gives the Outlands as his plane. The wiki's 3e row puts his home in the House of Nature, its 1e row in the Concordant Opposition, and its 4e row makes the Deep Wilds his dominion.
- **In the campaign:** Silvanus is alive and present in 1492 DR. The wiki records no death, abdication or merger for him, and its 4e rows (unaligned, the Deep Wilds, Life, Storm and Wilderness domains) belong to another edition's frame and are not used here.

> #### Using Silvanus as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the stern father of the wild places, far from the city. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Oak Father, the god of wild nature, whom druids, rangers and country folk honor with an oak leaf. They add that he is no gentle god, since the wilderness he rules hands out flood and drought and life and death with an even hand. They know the Sea Ward keeps gardens for him and Mielikki behind an iron fence, where only the ward's own residents may walk. No source says how Waterdhavians feel about him beyond that. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Silvanus has one shrine site in the city, the Shrines of Nature in the Sea Ward, which he shares with **Mielikki**.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Mielikki, Silvanus, Shrines of Nature, Sea Ward" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30953-30955`). The Sea Ward text calls them "resplendent gardens dedicated to nature gods like Mielikki and Silvanus", a block from the Tower of Luck (`:30884`). The park is closed to all except residents of the Sea Ward, and passers-by can glimpse the shrines, statues and fountains through the iron fence that surrounds it.
>   - The table writes "Shrines of Nature" and the Sea Ward text writes "Shrines to Nature". This page follows the table's name.
>   - The table also lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple for all deities, which includes Silvanus (`:30923-30925`).
>   - No campaign file names a keeper, a priest, a festival or a hook at the Shrines of Nature, and none says whether a resident may bring guests inside. [research] No campaign file sends the party inside, so the iron fence is as far as play goes.
>   - Phaulkonmere in the Southern Ward is Mielikki's, and no source ties it to Silvanus.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domain is Nature (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Nature Domain, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is Life Domain (derived). It is a loose fit, chosen because his creed says wild nature refreshes the mortal soul, his clergy fight disease and the wiki's 4e row lists Life among his domains. War Domain (derived) is the second choice for a cleric who defends a sacred grove. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), because the wiki gives his clergy the work of guarding the wilderness from encroachment. A player who wants the closest match can play a druid instead, since FRHoF says druids make up most of his following (derived).
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of the Ancients (derived), the oath of protecting life and the wild places. The fit is loose, because FRHoF lists no paladins among his typical followers and the oath rests on his creed alone.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The Emerald Enclave files name Mielikki alone, through Jeryth Phaulkon, Chosen of Mielikki, who is the patron of Phaulkonmere. No Enclave page, Notable Figure or faction event names Silvanus or any other power of the wild.

## Overview

Silvanus, the Oak Father, is the god of wild nature. Druids, rangers and wood elves follow him and ask that wild places be left to run their own course. Travelers and country folk honor him with an oak leaf, and his official temples are few and usually lie deep in forests. In Waterdeep he shares the Sea Ward's Shrines of Nature with Mielikki, and only the ward's residents may enter. A passerby can see the statues and fountains through the iron fence.

**Tone:** Grave and unhurried. Silvanus is a stern father, so describe the fairness of the wild before its harshness, and keep him far from the city and in the present tense. The campaign writes no scene in which he acts.
