# Gond

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Gond, the god of craft and invention, and the reference for the faith practiced at the House of Inspired Hands in the Sea Ward.
> - **Where it is used:** Gond never appears in person. His faith runs through the House of Inspired Hands, its senior cleric Valetta and the temple's nimblewright, Nim.
> - **Featured in:** **Fireball!** (the House of Inspired Hands event), **Sea Maidens Faire** (the brass model in the House's main hall) and the Lords' Alliance rank event **Stingblade**. The Day of Wonders, his festival, falls after the campaign window.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Gond is secret, but the nimblewrights sold across the city and the model in the main hall belong to Jarlaxle's affairs, and they sit in the GM Notes and the Never reveal bullet.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Gond, and lists the temple, the people and the festival the campaign has tied to him so that no later page invents more.

*The Wonderbringer*

- **Title:** God of Craft (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Holy Maker of All Things, the Inspiration Divine (FRHoF); the Lord of All Smiths, the Gearsmith, the Meddler, Zionil in the Shining Lands, and Nebelun among gnomes (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Craft, invention, artifice, construction and smithwork (derived). The wiki portfolio for the 2e and 3e rows is "Artifice, construction, craft, smithwork"; no entry lists Areas of Concern.
- **Domains:** Forge, Knowledge (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Toothed cog with four spokes (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** The Outlands (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Artificers, crafters, engineers, inventors (FRHoF)

> Build what is new, and make it useful to others.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Gond is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki rates him a lesser power in its 1e row and an intermediate deity in its 2e and 3e rows, while its 5e row calls him a deity and its 4e row gives no rank.
- **What the faith teaches:** Anyone who is making something may pray to Gond to guide the work, and folk know that he smiles most brightly on new inventions that others find useful (SCAG, 2014). His followers are credited with much of Faerûn's industrial and magical innovation (FRHoF).
- **What the faith allows:** The wiki describes a god who pushes invention to a dangerous degree and cares little for the complications that follow. He accepts commissions from any patron, however unscrupulous, who pays well and lets him build something new (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). He does not oppose magic, which he treats as one more way to build (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). He is content for his priests to grow rich (*Ed Greenwood Presents Elminster's Forgotten Realms*), and some read wealth as proof of their worthiness (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Who follows:** Most followers practice a time-honored craft, as smiths, engineers, architects, weavers, leatherworkers and jewelers. The faith also has a well-earned reputation as a haven for crackpot inventors and visionaries (SCAG, 2014). The wiki's 5e row adds gnomes and its 3e row adds the Lantanese.
- **Temples and priests:** Temples of Gond double as workshops and laboratories. Blacksmiths, woodworkers and engineers count themselves among his followers, but inventors carry his spirit of innovation and are the most likely to become priests (FRHoF). In large cities the Gondar build great workshops and inventors' labs, where resident scribes record the journals that wandering priests hand over (SCAG, 2014).
- **Wandering priests:** They travel the North in saffron vestments, belts of large linked metal medallions and enormous sunhats, and they serve distant villages as tinker, carpenter and civil engineer. Every priest keeps a journal of ideas and inventions. When a priest invents something, the priest makes two copies and keeps one in a temple. The other is smashed or burned as an offering to Gond (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Gond bells:** The faith has its own money. A Gond bell is a small brass bell worth 10 gp, and its look varies slightly with the temple that cast it. Priests enjoy finding bells from faraway temples, since they show how fast ideas travel (FRHoF).
- **Gatherers of Gond:** This clandestine order secures the materials and intelligence that new inventions need. Gatherers spy on builders who do not belong to the faith and bring back their trade secrets for the faith to improve on, and they sabotage nobody. They work with dwarf miners to find metals such as adamantine, and with svirfneblin guides to find zurkhwood bark. They also explore ancient ruins to draw schematics and seek out magic items to study. A candidate joins by presenting an original invention and stepping through the heatless fire of a *Continual Flame* spell in a "forging" ceremony (FRHoF), though no file places the Gatherers in Waterdeep.
- **Lockkeepers:** These Gatherers are skilled in tracking and coercion. The priests keep a few ideas proprietary, most of all how to make smokepowder, and the Lockkeepers hunt down anyone who breaches that secret. They avoid outright assassination and use blackmail or spells such as *Modify Memory* instead (FRHoF).
- **Chosen:** Chosen of Gond have a knack for problem-solving. They can tell how an object works at a glance and can design clockwork trinkets nobody has seen before (FRHoF). No campaign file names a Chosen of Gond.
- **Messengers and signs:** Gond comes in dreams to give inventors the hint of inspiration they need, and on dire occasions the flames of a forge twist into his face and speak in his voice (FRHoF). The wiki adds that he sends golems, lightning mephits, baku, einheriar, maruts, and steel or crystal dragons as guides (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). His manifestations are a hammer wrapped in smoke, or a black eye in smoke with the sound of hammering (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*), and no source gives a separate omen.
- **Holy days:** No source lists one, and although the Day of Wonders on Marpenoth 3 is the Gondar festival in Waterdeep, no file calls it a holy day.
- **Realm:** The wiki names his realm Wonderhome. FRHoF and the wiki's 2e and 5e rows put his home in the Outlands, and its 3e and 4e rows put his home plane in the House of Knowledge.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Along with Milil, Deneir and Oghma, he is one of the Deities of Knowledge and Invention (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). He is on fairly good terms with Oghma, though the two clash when Gond pushes new inventions into the world before anyone weighs the consequences. His relationship with Milil is strained, and the wiki's 2e and 4e rows list him as serving Oghma.
  - He has a friendly rivalry with Chauntea and amiable relations with Lathander, Waukeen and Tempus (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). FRHoF says the Gatherers partner with followers of Lathander, Oghma, Tempus and Waukeen, since the inventions Gond's priests make often relate to creativity, knowledge, profit or war.
  - Talos is his one major foe among the Faerûnian gods, because the urge to destroy devices of creation threatens Gond's work (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). FRHoF says the Gatherers oppose Talos for the same reason.
  - Among the other pantheons he is a friend to Moradin, to the dwarf gods Dugmaren Brightmantle, Dumathoin and Vergadain, to Cyrrollalee of the halfling pantheon and to Flandal Steelskin, the gnome god of smithing (*Demihuman Deities*).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Worship of Gond as Zionil began among the Adama in -256 DR (*The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - Lantan was the main center of his worship until the late 14th century DR (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). During the Time of Troubles in 1358 DR his avatar washed up on Lantan in the form of a gnome. The Lantanese recognized him, and he rewarded their devotion with the secret of smokepowder (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). FRHoF tells the same story and says that since then he has communicated more subtly.
  - The Lantanese brought his worship to the Sword Coast through Waterdeep, and the inventions of his clergy greatly helped the rise of Waterdhavian trade (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep*, 3e). SCAG (2014) puts the center of his worship on the Sword Coast in Baldur's Gate, where the High House of Wonders and the Hall of Wonders stand.
  - In 1368 DR the newly risen Cyric commissioned armor from Gond for his inquisitors, who used it to begin the Second Banedeath (*Prince of Lies*; *The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - Gnomes know him as Nebelun the Meddler. A few gnome groups hold that Nebelun is a separate god, and the wiki says they are right, since Nebelun is active only on other crystal spheres (*Demihuman Deities*; *Monster Mythology*).
- **In the campaign:** Gond is alive and present in 1492 DR. His temple is a working temple and no file treats him as absent.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Using Gond as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the patron of tinkers and showmen. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Wonderbringer, the god of craft and invention, and that smiths, engineers and inventors pray to him to guide their work. They add that the House of Inspired Hands in the Sea Ward is more workshop than temple, that its priests will tell a visitor that worship is work, and that inventions too risky for the building get tried out in the alley by the south door while the neighbors watch. The favorite story about the Day of Wonders is the flying chair of Marchell, which rose beautifully and could not come down until the Griffon Cavalry rescued its rider. A skeptic adds that the faith is a haven for crackpot inventors.
> - **Waterdeep:** Gond has one temple in the city, the House of Inspired Hands in the Sea Ward, on the corner of Seawatch Street and Shark Street.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Gond, House of Inspired Hands, Sea Ward", and the campaign files agree. A Lantanese engineering text in **Fireball!** names it "Waterdeep's only active temple of Gond" and the foremost authority on Lantanese mechanical constructs in the city. The Players' Guide tells gnome characters that it doubles as a guild of inventors and tinkerers.
>   - The book says the House "looks like a cross between a temple and a workshop", with the toothed cog displayed prominently. Its Sea Ward text says the city's innovative minds invent and experiment there, trying to build everything from flying machines to stronger door hinges, and that "worship is work" for anyone at the temple. It adds that a visitor should not expect a museum of marvels like the one in Baldur's Gate.
>   - The temple is open and busy in daylight and closes from sunset to sunrise, when the acolytes retire to their quarters to work on pet projects. Inventions too dangerous to run inside are tested in Gondwatch Lane, the alley at the southern entrance, where locals stand and watch while food vendors work the crowd.
>   - Valetta, the senior cleric, works by day with two junior priests and three lay initiates, and Nim, the temple's nimblewright, lives in the attic. No guards are posted, since the House is a public temple. The temple holds an annual invention competition called Gond's Artifice, and the winning entries stand on display in the main hall.
>   - The temple sponsors the Day of Wonders parade on Marpenoth 3, in which the Gondar reveal their inventions, from new cabinet hinges to constructs that walk or roll, and failures are common. In the published adventure the parade gathers at the House before it marches through the city. The campaign calendar places the day after Flamerule 11, so it has no live function during play.
>   - No other shrine or temple of Gond exists in the campaign files. The Major Temples table's "All deities" row, Holyhands House in the North Ward, is not attributed to him.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Forge and Knowledge (SCAG, 2014). Neither is in the 2024 Player's Handbook, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is War Domain (derived), through Forge alone. Forge centers on weapons, armor and smithwork, FRHoF says Gond's inventions often relate to war, and his Gatherers work with the followers of Tempus. The fit is loose, because Knowledge has no counterpart among the four, and Light, whose spells carry the forge's fire, is a weaker second. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because his clergy are inventors and tinkers rather than guards, and the cantrip *Mending* suits a priest who repairs things. Valetta is the model of a cleric of Gond in Waterdeep.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived). No source lists paladins among his followers, so the fit is weak. A paladin of Gond guards the workshops and the people who work in them, and leans on the oath's Duty tenet and on Valetta's stated aim of constructs that serve rather than harm.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** **Fireball!**, Valetta and Nim, the Lords' Alliance and the Sea Maidens Faire. The details are in the GM Notes below. In the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, area 17b, the Desecrated Temple, holds the one statue of six still standing in its alcoves, coated in black pudding. A character who passes a DC 15 Intelligence (Religion) check once the pudding sloughs off sees that it shows Gond, and a worshiper of Gond passes automatically. Undermountain is future work, so treat the statue as a seed.
> - **Never reveal:** That the nimblewrights sold across Waterdeep are Jarlaxle's, that each one feeds a crystal ball aboard the *Scarlet Marpenoth*, that Captain Zardoz Zord is Jarlaxle, or that the model in the main hall shows his ship. No file says the temple knows any of this, and the party's own evidence is the only thing that adds to it.

## Overview

Gond is the Wonderbringer, the neutral god of craft and invention, and smiths, engineers and inventors pray to him for guidance. His temple in Waterdeep is the House of Inspired Hands in the Sea Ward, as much workshop as place of prayer. Each year on Marpenoth 3 the Gondar show their newest inventions in the Day of Wonders parade, and a few of them break down in the street. Folk say Gond smiles most brightly on new inventions that others find useful.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Fireball!:** The event **The House of Inspired Hands** sends the party to the temple once they know a nimblewright set off the blast. Three paths lead there. Rishaal's Lantanese engineering text names the House, any faction contact can identify a nimblewright from witness descriptions, and Barnibus Blastwind's Watchful Order contacts can identify the construct within 48 hours of the blast. The Harpers are fastest, since Remallia Haventree knows the high priest personally. A note from Vajra Safahr to a Force Grey member the next morning says the blast left a signature the Watchful Order describes as a nimblewright detonation, "registered Lantanese manufacture, auditable through the House of Inspired Hands".
> - **At the House:** Valetta meets the party at the entrance and takes them to Nim, who built the runaway nimblewright about three months before the fireball to ease its loneliness. The runaway fled about a month before, with its behavioral lattice incomplete. Nim hands over a detector it built to find the runaway. Valetta offers 500 gp for proof that the runaway is destroyed, plus one invention from the temple's collection. The location journal **House of Inspired Hands** holds the rooms, the sparrow trap and the attic, and this page repeats none of it.
> - **Valetta:** A bronze dragonborn priest of Gond (neutral), and the temple's senior cleric. She wants to keep the House a center of innovation, to destroy the rogue nimblewright that threatens Gond's reputation, and to build constructs that serve rather than harm. She treats the bounty as a civic duty and a theological obligation, and she values constructs as Gond's children.
> - **Stingblade:** In the Lords' Alliance rank event **Stingblade**, Valetta is one of the eight people the Alliance can introduce to a member, through **Jalester Silvermane**. She receives the member at the House and, within 3 days, examines one device or mechanism or repairs one. She works on no magic items.
> - **Sea Maidens Faire:** A brass model of the *Scarlet Marpenoth*, labeled "Launched 1489 DR" and unsigned by its donor, stands in the House's main hall. A party that studied it recognizes the hull when it finds the real vessel under the *Eyecatcher*. The History checks that tie the model to Bregan D'aerthe are in the location journal.
> - **The Day of Wonders and Jarlaxle:** In the published adventure, Jarlaxle visits the House as Zardoz Zord the day before the parade to plan it with the acolytes, and he leads the parade as grand marshal for the Faire. In the campaign the day falls after the campaign window. The nimblewright ledger records the first sale, NW-01, on Marpenoth 3, 1491 DR (the year is derived), which is the same date.

**Tone:** Practical and a little cheerful. Gond is a god of workshops, so describe what his followers are building before what it costs, and let his priests be more interested in the thing on the bench than in the visitor. Keep him at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he speaks.
