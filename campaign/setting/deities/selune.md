*The Moonmaiden*

- **Title:** God of the Moon (FRHoF). SCAG (2014) and the 2014 Player's Handbook print "Goddess of the moon".
- **Other Names:** The Moonmaiden, the Night White Lady, Our Lady of Silver (FRHoF). The wiki adds She Who Guides, Selûne Moonmother, and the regional names Bright Nydra in the Farsea Marshes, Elah among the Bedine and Lucha in the Shining Lands.
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** The moon, stars, navigation, motherhood, the lost and the questing, and benevolent lycanthropes (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Knowledge, Life, Twilight (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A pair of eyes surrounded by seven stars (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Ysgard (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Lycanthropes, sailors, spellcasters (FRHoF)

> Light the way for the lost, and welcome anyone who turns to the moon.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Selûne is a goddess of the Faerûnian pantheon and one of the most ancient of Faerûn's deities (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). The wiki's rank for her changes by edition: lesser deity in 1e, intermediate in 2e and 3e, greater in 4e, and plain "deity" in its 5e row.
- **How she appears:** As a woman of varying ages who sometimes passes slowly through the phases of aging as the moon passes through its cycle, and in any form she gives off the silvery-blue glow of the brightest full moon (FRHoF). Most Faerûnian humans take the moon to be the goddess gazing down on the world, and the trailing lights behind it to be her tears (SCAG, 2014).
- **Temperament:** SCAG (2014) calls her a calm power. The wiki's 2e and 3e sources say her moods follow the moon, so she can be joyous and vivacious, motherly, remote with old sadness, or fierce and cold to her enemies. They also say she is generous, asks little of her followers, and always answers her clergy.
- **What the faith offers:** Compassion and guidance. The words given to novices say that all on whom her light falls are welcome, that life waxes and wanes like the moon, and that she will be the true guide of anyone who turns to it (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e, via the wiki). Couples ask her for children when the time is right, and women look to her for courage and strength (wiki, 2e and 3e).
- **Who follows:** Navigators, sailors, people who work at night, people who seek protection in the dark, the lost and the questing (FRHoF). SCAG (2014) adds that human women venerate her most often, and that those who work honestly at night pray to her. The wiki adds mavericks, outcasts and adventurers, female spellcasters born under a full moon, and, by the late fifteenth century DR, non-wicked tieflings (SCAG, 5e). The Gur of the Western Heartlands call themselves her children (*Races of Faerûn*, 3e; SCAG, 5e).
- **Lycanthropes:** Lycanthropes are said to be blessed by Selûne (FRHoF). The wiki says she watches over good and neutral lycanthropes, and that the old euphemism for lycanthropy is "Selûne's Kiss" (*Heroes' Lorebook*). *Dragon Heist* adds that her priestesses make potions from the tears of her holy mace that can cure lycanthropy.
- **Rites:** Her rites include trances and meditation, and milk is a symbol of motherhood that appears in many of them. Those who favor her set a bowl of milk outside on each night of the full moon (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). Most goodly folk pay her homage during the full moon (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e, via the wiki).
- **Temples:** Her temples include a series of windows that show the moon in the night sky at different positions and phases through the year (FRHoF). The wiki adds that moonstones are sacred to her and that some folk believe silver is her hardened tears (*Volo's Guide to All Things Magical*, 5e).
- **Holy days:** The one holy day any published adventure gives is Selûne's Hallowing, held on the night in Uktar when the moon is fullest (*Dragon Heist*). The wiki's 2e row lists the Conjuring of the Second Moon, on Shieldmeet, and the Mystery of the Night, which varies. Its 5e row is blank.
- **Messengers and omens:** She sends an owl or other night bird, a moonlight-hued planetar, or a benevolent lycanthrope, most commonly a werebear (FRHoF). The wiki names her planetar servants the Shards and adds lesser servants called slivers, the colors blue and silver, and the appearance of moonstones as signs of her favor (2e and 3e sources).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Selûne. FRHoF says her Chosen step into danger to light the way through darkness, and that a select few can create moonfire, a substance that grants magical power to objects or people.
- **The Swords of the Lady (FRHoF):** An order of knights devoted to Selûne, known for an unexpectedly jovial, joking nature and for unpredictable tactics. The order welcomes those who contemplate the moon and seeks out arcane spellcasters born under a full moon. Recruits are called Slivers and are encouraged to improvise, use their surroundings and rely on surprise. A Sliver who proves worthy keeps a vigil under a waxing half-moon and then receives a tabard, cloak and cloth mask of white fabric stitched with silver. Swords learn some divine or arcane magic, can borrow silvered weapons or buy them at half price, and in a crisis can call on a friendly lycanthrope. The order stands against the children of Shar, against the followers of Mask, and against Umberlee for her carelessness with sailors at night. It counts the followers of Lathander, Mystra and Sune as allies.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced (2e and 3e sources).
  - Her eternal enemy is her sister **Shar**, the goddess of the night. Their war began before the other Faerûnian gods existed and is fought in the sky, across the planes and through their followers. FRHoF calls the battle at the beginning of time the principal legend about Selûne.
  - Her other great foes are **Mask**, for the wickedness he does in the shadows her moonlight casts, **Umberlee**, over the fate of ships at sea, and Moander, the rotting god.
  - Her allies are the other powers of the moon, beauty, fortune, joy, light, magic and weather. They include **Mystra**, her greatest ally against Shar since 1358 DR, **Eilistraee** and **Lliira**, who share her love of dancing under the moon, **Sune**, **Lathander**, **Shaundakul**, **Valkur**, **Chauntea**, **Eldath**, **Tymora** and **Corellon**. Sehanine Moonbow, the elven goddess of the moon, works closely with her.
  - Those who follow the Dark Moon heresy hold that Selûne and Shar are two faces of one goddess (*Power of Faerûn*, 3e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - She coalesced from the primordial essence of Realmspace alongside her twin sister Shar. The two were identical opposites, silver-haired and raven-haired, and for a time saw themselves as one being, the Two-Faced Goddess (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Together they made Abeir-Toril and the other worlds and formed Chauntea. When Chauntea asked for warmth, the sisters divided over whether the worlds should have more life, and their war began (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e; *Magic of Faerûn*, 3e). Selûne tore out part of her own essence and hurled it at Shar, and the two made Mystryl, the first goddess of magic, who mediated a truce (same sources).
  - The Netherese worshiped her. A temple called the Abbey of the Moon was founded in -3847 DR (*The Grand History of the Realms*), and in -339 DR she is said to have carried the city of Opus into the Gates of the Moon when Netheril fell (*Lost Empires of Faerûn*, 3e).
  - During the Dawn Cataclysm she tried to purify the goddess Tyche of Moander's corruption, and Tyche split into **Tymora** and **Beshaba** (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; SCAG, 5e).
  - Her might waned for a time, and she served **Sune** for several centuries. After the Godswar of 1358 DR the two parted on good terms and Selûne was free again (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - For a long time she lived in Waterdeep as a mortal innkeeper named Luna, who ran the inn Selûne's Smile and kept the Wand of the Four Moons at the House of the Moon. In the Time of Troubles in 1358 DR, Shar took the form of a false Selûne and held Luna prisoner there, until Luna regained her power and drove Shar off over the streets of the city (*Advanced Dungeons & Dragons* comics; *City of Splendors* boxed set, 2e).
  - After the Spellplague of 1385 DR her priesthood made pilgrimages across Faerûn to bring hope, and she became a popular goddess (*Dungeon* 193, 4e).
  - By 1489 DR some tiefling followers claimed visions of her as a pale horned goddess of the moon. Mainstream churches found this disturbing, and the tieflings argued that the gods care more for the heart than for the appearance (SCAG, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Selûne is alive and present in 1492 DR. The Tower of the Moon is a working temple, and no file treats her as absent.

> #### Using Selûne as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as the lady of the harbor night. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is the Moonmaiden, goddess of the moon and of everyone who steers by it, and that the faithful set out a bowl of milk for her on full-moon nights. They add that her priestesses collect droplets from her holy mace, which they call her tears, and make potions from them that heal and cure lycanthropy. They also say she is the twin sister of Shar, the goddess of the night, and that the two have fought since before the other gods. Her temple is the one with the tallest tower in the Sea Ward. The campaign's temple list calls it "widely attended", and no source says more about how the city feels about her. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Selûne has one named temple in the city, the Tower of the Moon in the Sea Ward.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists "Selûne, Tower of the Moon, Sea Ward". `campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md` lists her among the Sea Ward's major temples as "widely attended", and the Castle Ward gazetteer repeats that the Tower of the Moon is a Sea Ward temple. This page follows the campaign file and uses the name Tower of the Moon.
>   - The Sea Ward text of the same book calls the temple the House of the Moon, and so do the Selûne's Hallowing entry and *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*. The sources treat them as one building (derived). The text says it has the tallest tower of any temple in the city, rising some seventy-five feet above the street, and that priests of Selûne bask in the light of the moon at its top in all seasons.
>   - Selûne's Hallowing falls on whatever night in Uktar the moon is fullest. A parade of worshipers leaves the temple at moonrise and walks down to the harbor, where the high priestess wields the Wand of the Four Moons in a ceremony that blesses all navigators.
>   - The Wand is a holy relic said to be the mace Selûne carried in her first battle against Shar, and again in a fight with her sister during the Time of Troubles. *Dragon Heist* says it appeared in Waterdeep after the Godswar and has been the focus of many divine signs since. A visitor can see it in the temple at other times of the year, but only from a well-guarded distance.
>   - Droplets said to be Selûne's tears sometimes form on the Wand. The priestesses collect them for potions that heal, cure lycanthropy and serve as holy water.
>   - The table also lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple for all deities, which includes Selûne.
>   - Uktar lies outside the campaign calendar, which runs from Ches 1 to Flamerule 11, so the Hallowing does not fall in play (derived). No campaign file ties the Feast of the Moon, a festival day between Uktar 30 and Nightal 1, or the Twin Parades on Ches 21 to her.
>   - No campaign file names a priestess of the temple, and the high priestess has no name in the sources. No other shrine or temple of Selûne exists in the campaign files. [research]
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Knowledge, Life and Twilight (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Knowledge or Twilight Domain, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is Life Domain (derived), because Life is on her list and her tears heal. Light Domain (derived) is a good second fit for a cleric who leans on moonlight, since the Twilight Domain is built on soft light and protection in the dark. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because her rites are trances, meditation and potions. A cleric who serves the Swords of the Lady would take Protector instead (derived).
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of the Ancients (derived). The oath of light, life and joy suits a chaotic good goddess whose knights are jovial and improvise. Oath of Devotion (derived) is the second fit for a Sword of the Lady who swears to protect the lost. FRHoF lists no paladins among her typical followers, so both fits rest on the Swords of the Lady.
> - **A warlock PC:** The Celestial patron is a loose fit (derived). No source names a warlock of Selûne. The sources do give her moonlight-hued planetar emissaries, and the wiki names her planetar servants the Shards.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The only campaign lines that name her are the Sea Ward temple list in `campaign/setting/waterdeep-lore.md` and the Castle Ward gazetteer's note on the Tower of the Moon. In the published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, the Shar cult in Vanrakdoom plans to destroy her temple. The details are in the GM Notes below.

## Overview

Selûne, the Moonmaiden, is the goddess of the moon, navigation and motherhood, and sailors, travelers and night workers pray to her. She is the twin and oldest rival of Shar, the goddess of the night. Her temple in Waterdeep is the Tower of the Moon in the Sea Ward, also called the House of the Moon, and its tower is the tallest of any temple in the city. On the fullest night of Uktar her faithful parade to the harbor, where the high priestess blesses every navigator with the Wand of the Four Moons.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **Vanrakdoom and the Shar cult:** In *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, the vampire cleric Keresta Delvingstone leads Shar's cult in Vanrakdoom and plans to send the group to destroy the Spires of the Morning, which her map marks together with Selûne's temple. She also means to destroy the House of the Moon, which she blames for the destruction of Vanrak Moonstar. A map of Waterdeep in her chamber has daggers in both temples and the names of clerics and paladins of Lathander and Selûne whom she plans to assassinate. Undermountain is future work, so treat all of it as a seed. See the **Lathander** page, which words the Spires of the Morning half of the plan.
> - **Vanrak Moonstar:** Lord Vanrak broke with Selûne's temple after his father's death in the 1100s DR and turned to Shar. In 1182 DR his followers burned a temple of Selûne called the High House of Stars, killing most of its occupants. In 1436 DR he renounced Shar and begged Selûne's forgiveness before destroying his own undead form. A moonlight that flickers across the dragon in one Vanrakdoom scene is a blessing from Selûne that the characters cannot confirm.
> - **No Chosen, no intervention:** No file has Selûne appear or speak, and none names a Chosen of hers. The wiki's account of Luna and the Time of Troubles stays out of play.

**Tone:** Calm and a little melancholy. Selûne is a goddess of guidance, so describe what the moon offers a traveler before describing her war with Shar, and keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she acts.
