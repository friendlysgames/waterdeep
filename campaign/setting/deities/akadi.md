*The Lady of Air*

- **Title:** Goddess of Air (SCAG, 2014; the 5etools data have no FRHoF entry for her)
- **Other Names:** The Lady of the Winds, the Queen of Air (wiki, 2e). Teylas, the Lord of the Sky and Lord of Storms, among the nomads of the Hordelands (wiki, 2e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian (SCAG, 2014). The wiki also lists her among the Zakharan pantheon's cold gods of the elements.
- **Areas of Concern:** Air, wind, speed, flight and flying creatures, movement and travel (derived from the wiki portfolios, which read "Air" in 5e and "elemental air, flying creatures, movement, speed" in 3e; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Tempest (SCAG, 2014). The wiki's 3e list is Air, Illusion, Travel and Trickery.
- **Symbol:** Cloud (SCAG, 2014). The wiki's 2e and 3e rows give a white cloud on a blue background.
- **Alignment:** Neutral (SCAG, 2014). The wiki gives true neutral in 3e and unaligned in 4e.
- **Plane:** The Elemental Plane of Air (wiki, 3e). The wiki's 2e row gives her realm as the Great Funnel, and its 4e row gives Sky Home in the Elemental Chaos.
- **Typical Followers:** Animal breeders, rangers, rogues, sailors and air elemental archons (wiki, 3e)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Akadi is a goddess of the Faerûnian pantheon and one of the four elemental powers, with Grumbar (earth), Istishia (water) and Kossuth (fire), as the SCAG titles name them. The wiki ranks her a greater deity in its 2e and 3e rows, a lesser power in 1e and a deity in 5e. In 4e she is a primordial (see Draft Notes).
- **What she is:** She is the element of air and the goddess of elemental air, speed and flying creatures (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). She is both the whispering wind and the turbulent tempest, sweet and moist at times and bitter and cold at others, and her form changes with the seasons (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **What the faith asks:** Akadi is a being of freedom and travel, and she told her followers to move as much as they can from place to place and from activity to activity (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). Her followers travel across Faerûn, listen to the howl of the winds, spread word of her glory and act on whatever interests them at the moment (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Life experiments:** Some clerics throw themselves into a goal of their own, useful or personal. Examples include breeding sleeker hawks or faster running hounds, working out how orcs cope with powerful windstorms, and designing artificial wings for cats (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **How she treats mortals:** Her reactions are hard to read, because she behaves almost alien toward mortals who are not native to the Plane of Elemental Air. Like the other elemental lords she is relatively uncaring toward her followers on Toril, though she shows some small affection, mostly when large offerings are made (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **What she requires:** She expects a fitting sacrifice before she recognizes her followers. One example is burning incense that the winds carry to her (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **What she grants:** Appeals to her can change the wind, producing a strong current of air, a good flying current or a bout of gentle rain. She will not make or end a true storm, because storms belong to Talos, or to Umberlee at sea (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Size of the faith:** Her following is very small, like that of every elemental god except Kossuth (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Clergy:** Her clerics pray for their spells before dawn, so that they can chant their incantations on the morning winds. They divide into two philosophies. The Whisper discreetly brings about change, and the Roar openly preaches her dogma. The priests are otherwise alike, and they are often arranged into small sects under one philosophy or the other (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Orders:** The Knights of the Wind's Four Quarters are a crusader order that carries out quests for church elders and fights the followers of Grumbar. The Companions of the Summer Wind are good mystics, the Disciples of Spring's Breeze are neutral mystics, and the Alliance of Midwinter's Teeth are evil mystics (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). The Azure Guard is a dozen female air genasi fighters who serve as her proxies on the Elemental Plane of Air (*The Inner Planes*). No file places any of these orders in Waterdeep.
- **Holy day:** Midsummer is the only holy day of the order, and the church keeps few others (wiki, 2e and 3e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Worshipers gather in the Shaar at the ruins of Blaskaltar, where her first shrine was built. Wind and rain have since worn the shrine away. They chant the names of the heroes of the faith and add the names of those who died in the past year. In the campaign calendar Midsummer falls between Flamerule 30 and Eleasis 1, after the Flamerule 11 end of the campaign window.
- **Messengers and omens:** Her manifestations are a rising wind and whispers in the breeze (wiki, citing *Deity Do's and Don'ts*). Flying creatures are her animals, and the wiki lists air elementals, ice serpents and air genasi among her monsters. No source gives a formal omen. [research]
- **How she appears:** Religious art shows a giant, blue, translucent woman with huge feathery wings that trail off into the clouds. Some lands revere her in male and female aspects alike, and her faithful see no difference between them. Her avatar is usually a column of swirling wind about 160 feet tall, and her voice can range from a slight breeze to a squall heard for miles (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Regional worship:** In Zakhara she is one of the cold gods of the elements, seen as uncaring toward human affairs, and few Zakharans will worship a cold god to gain power (*Adventurer's Guide to Zakhara*). Among the nomads of the Hordelands she is Teylas, the God of Heaven of the steppe. He watches the herdsmen and sends rain and blessings against evil spirits, and he sends savage storms when angered (*The Horde*). The elven goddess Aerdrie Faenya was reported to be an aspect of her (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Akadi, and the wiki names none.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Quorlinn and Syranita are among her allies. Yan-C-Bin, the archomental of evil air, wanted to challenge her power, though she had no superiors among aerial beings (*The Inner Planes*).
  - Grumbar, the elemental god of earth and a fellow greater power, is her enemy. He opposes her flimsy and ever-changing ways, and her Knights of the Wind's Four Quarters fight his followers (*The Inner Planes*; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - The Shaundakul page lists her as one of his allies (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e, as that page cites them). Her own wiki page does not name him.
  - Talos and Umberlee hold the true storms she cannot make or end. After the Spellplague, Auril claimed dominion over wind and storms and came into direct conflict with Akadi (*Dragon* 367, 4e era, as the wiki's Auril page cites it).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact. The wiki gives no account of how she came to be.
  - She is one of the four elemental deities, and history and the passage of time have left her unchanged (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - After the Spellplague of 1385 DR she was reported to be a primordial, a member of an elemental race that once competed with the gods before they were driven away to Abeir. She was one of the few primordials who never warred with the gods, so she stayed in power on Toril (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - During the Second Sundering she left Toril for Abeir (*The Sentinel*, 2014). She still seems to have a presence in Faerûn (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Akadi is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. SCAG still lists her as a goddess, and the wiki's 4e primordial and Abeir accounts are history. Draft Notes records the conflicts.

## Overview

Akadi is the neutral goddess of air, wind and flying creatures, and Waterdhavians who know her call her the Lady of Air. Sages count her among the four elemental powers and picture her as a giant blue woman with feathery wings that fade into the clouds. She is thought to care little for mortals unless the offering is large, and her few followers travel from place to place spreading her word. She can be asked for a good wind or a gentle rain but not for a storm, and Waterdeep has no temple of hers.

> #### GM Notes
> - **The Howling Hatred:** It was an elemental air cult that worked in Waterdeep until about three years ago. It served Yan-C-Bin, the Prince of Evil Air, and preached that walls, contracts and gold kept the world from his wind. It broke in a single tenday of arrests, and it is not active during the campaign (`campaign/quests/faction-events/order-of-the-gauntlet/s02-savras-past/ev-01-savras-past.md:112-116`). *Dragon Heist* calls it "an evil elemental cult" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:3950`).
> - **Why it is not Akadi's cult:** Neither Dragon Heist nor the campaign files tie the cult to Akadi. The wiki names Yan-C-Bin as an archomental who wanted to challenge her power, so the cult serves a rival of hers. Voice a surviving cultist or an arrested follower as someone who worshiped the Prince of Evil Air, and keep Akadi's own priests out of it.
> - **Savra Belabranta:** A Human knight of the Order of the Gauntlet, she spent three years in the Howling Hatred and left about three years ago. She says so to anyone who asks sincerely, and she tells what she did in it only to a member she has come to trust (`campaign/setting/notable-figures/order-of-the-gauntlet/01-savra-belabranta.md:22`).
> - **The cult's money:** The tower where the cult met for its last two years could not have been paid for by member offerings, and no Watch record names the source (`ev-01-savras-past.md:114-115`). Mirt suspects Cassalanter money and has no proof. Nothing here changes that, and no Akadi scene should confirm it.

**Tone:** Plain and a little remote. Akadi is a power of motion and distance that pays mortals little attention, so describe the wind, the sky and the people who travel under them before the goddess, and keep her far off. The campaign writes no scene in which she acts.
