**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Talona, and states what the sources place in the city (nothing) so that no later page invents a poison cult or a plague shrine in her name.

*The Lady of Poison*

- **Title:** God of Poison and Disease (FRHoF). The page uses FRHoF's title because FRHoF is newer than SCAG, whose table reads "Goddess of poison and disease".
- **Other Names:** The Lady of Poison, the Mistress of Disease, the Plague Crone (FRHoF); the Plague-crone (SCAG, 2014); the Mother of All Plagues (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Poison, disease, plague, blight, brackish wells, failing crops (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Death (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Three teardrops in a triangle (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Carceri (Cathrys) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Those suffering from plague (FRHoF)

> Placate the Lady of Poison with blood and tears, or she will bring the sickness back.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Talona is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon (FRHoF). The wiki ranks her a demigoddess in 1e and a lesser deity in 2e and 3e, and its 5e rows list her as a deity.
- **What she wants:** FRHoF calls her one of Faerûn's most feared deities, blamed for everything from common illness to crop failure and from brackish wells to plague. She craves propitiation from mortals and pursues it by any means necessary. SCAG adds that she is among the most often beseeched of the gods, and that many prayers beg her for protection from illness and poison.
- **Her temper:** She is petulant more than conniving, and reactive more than active (FRHoF). Sages compare her to a greedy, petulant child who swings between a hunger for attention and the aloofness of a discarded lover, and she has no personal taste for violence (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **The cycle of worship:** When she lashes out with plagues, blights and magical contagions, victims pray to her for respite and placate her for a time. Once the contagion ends, the prayers dwindle, and the neglected god lashes out again (FRHoF). The wiki records that veneration peaked in plague years, among them 75 DR with the destruction of Asram, 902 DR in the Rotting War, 1150, 1252 and 1253 DR, and 1323 DR after the Great Plague of the Inner Sea (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **How she appears:** Temple iconography shows a withered crone holding a cup or vase that contains every variety of disease and poison (SCAG, 2014). The wiki describes a gaunt human woman ten feet tall, with long unkempt hair, elongated fingers and a scarred, tattooed face. Her avatars look as if they were once beautiful and have been ravaged by age, disease and starvation, though her dancing is said to be alluring (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Rites of placation:** The faithful let fall three drops of blood or three tears. The drops go into a well that has gone bad, onto the handkerchief of someone beset by coughing, into a fire made by burning a withered crop, or into the mouth of a plague sufferer (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Her symbol in use:** It is common practice to mark a container of poison with three droplets in a triangle. During epidemics, folk paint the same image on the homes of the infected (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Temples and cults:** She has almost no temples and few cults, although she receives many prayers, and she resents the neglect (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). A cult or a shrine may arise in an area after a pestilence, when some of the survivors decide to revere her or become priests (SCAG, 2014). The only temple the sources name is the House of Night's Embrace in Tashluta, which the wiki calls her largest church in Faerûn (*Serpent Kingdoms*, as the wiki cites it).
- **Priests:** They rarely enter her service willingly. Some are forced into the role by their communities, and others have nothing else to live for, among them the sole survivors of harrowing contagions and people who lost loved ones to her whims. These priests dedicate their lives to keeping the same fate from others. A priest keeps a small shrine rather than a temple, and people lavish offerings on it in hard times and neglect it in health (FRHoF).
- **The Talontar:** The wiki calls her priests the Talontar and her lay worshipers Talonites. The Talontar wore ragged gray-green robes that they washed and never mended, and senior members scarred or tattooed their bodies. They sold poisons, antidotes and medicines, built up an immunity to disease and poison through inoculation and magic, and treated the sick or buried the dead. Paranoid merchants and rulers hired them to test food for poison (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **The fanatics:** Her most devoted followers brew poisons in her name and summon magical contagions to punish people for neglecting her (FRHoF).
- **The Plague Rats:** An elite order of assassins, thieves and wererats that the wiki places throughout the North and the Western Heartlands (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). No campaign file names the order.
- **Who follows:** Those suffering from plague (FRHoF). The wiki's 3e row gives assassins, druids, healers, rogues and the diseased and ill, and its 2e row gives wererats. Its 5e row gives survivors of pestilence, taken from SCAG, and FRHoF's line replaces it.
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Talona. FRHoF says her Chosen, drawn from the fanatics, are gifted with immunity to poisons and magical maladies.
- **Messengers:** When she wants to demand propitiation, she sends a vulture, a giant rat or the corpse of a creature infested by insects. The messenger is wrapped in a sickly yellow-brown glow (FRHoF).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** No source gives one. [research] No file ties a city festival to her.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced.
  - Before the Time of Troubles she served Bhaal, and she feuded with her fellow servant Loviatar, who mocked her for her looks, the size of her portfolio and her few followers. She sometimes helped good-aligned adventurers whose aims would damage Loviatar's name (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Around 1369 DR she formed an alliance with Shar (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - She despised Chauntea, Mielikki, Kelemvor and Tyr (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). The Silvanus and Ilmater pages also list her among their foes.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book named on each fact.
  - In -33 DR a demipower named Kiputytto, the sister of Loviatar, challenged her for her portfolio. The fight spread plagues across the nation of Asram, and its people gave Talona devotional power in the hope that she would ease them. Talona won and soon afterward murdered Kiputytto, and later texts treated Kiputytto as an alias of Talona (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *Netheril: Empire of Magic*).
- **In the campaign:** Talona is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. No file treats her as dead or absent, and the wiki records no death for her.

> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as a goddess people pray to out of fear. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says she is the Lady of Poison, the god of disease and poison, blamed for plague, bad wells and failing crops. They add that people beg her for mercy when sickness comes and forget her when it passes, that she is shown as a withered crone with a cup of every poison, and that three teardrops in a triangle mean her, whether painted on a sick man's door or on a flask of poison. > - **A cleric PC:** Death Domain (derived; Dungeon Master's Guide). Death is her one SCAG domain (the 2014 Player's Handbook repeats it), and the domain's Touch of Death and Inescapable Destruction suit a god of plague and poison. Life Domain (derived) is the alternative for a priest in the FRHoF mold, who tends the sick to keep her fate from others. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because the faith runs on small rites and shrines. This is an evil character, and a cleric of Talona in Waterdeep has no temple to turn to.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oathbreaker (derived; Dungeon Master's Guide) is the only oath that can serve a chaotic evil goddess, and the fit is loose. Neither FRHoF nor SCAG lists paladins among her followers. A paladin sworn to fight blight and disease stands against her instead, and Oath of the Ancients is the closest oath for that (derived).
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. No campaign file ties a poisoner, an assassin or an apothecary to her, so the page does not either.

## Overview

Talona is the Lady of Poison, the god of disease and poison, whom the people of Faerûn blame for plague, brackish wells and failing crops. She has almost no temples, but many pray to her when sickness comes, and the prayers fade as soon as it passes. Folk mark the homes of the infected and flasks of poison with her symbol, three teardrops in a triangle. Waterdeep has no temple of hers.

**Tone:** Grave and plain, with no gore. Talona is a goddess people placate out of fear, so describe the sickness and the small rites of the frightened before the poison. Keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she acts.
