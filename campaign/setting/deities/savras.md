> - **Where it is used:** Savras never appears in person, and no quest, faction or Notable Figure uses him. No file places a temple or a shrine of his in Waterdeep. He is named only in the Azuth, Mystra and Deneir deity pages, as part of the history of the gods of magic.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Savras, gives a diviner or cleric player what the faith asks, and states that the campaign has tied nothing to him so that no later page invents more.

*The All-Seeing*

- **Title:** God of Divination and Fate (SCAG, 2014; PHB, 2014)
- **Other Names:** The Third Eye, Divination's Lord (SCAG, 2014); He of the Third Eye, the Lord of Divination (wiki, citing *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Divination, fate, truth, diviners and truth-speakers (derived from the portfolio fields on the wiki page; neither SCAG nor the PHB lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Arcana, Knowledge (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A crystal ball containing many kinds of eyes (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Arcadia, in the realm of Buxenus (wiki, 2e), or Dweomerheart (wiki, 3e). The wiki names his realm as the Eye in both editions and gives no 5e plane. [research]
- **Typical Followers:** Investigators, diviners, judges (wiki, 5e row). SCAG, 2014 adds "others who have a need to uncover the truth".

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Savras is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon in the wiki's 5e row. The wiki's 3e entry makes him a demigod, and its 2e entry a demipower who had been a lesser deity before.
- **What the faith is:** He is a god of divination and fortunetelling. Few people worship him, but many pray to him when they perform small rituals of foresight. A young man or woman may gaze into a mirror and say a rhyming chant that calls on Savras, hoping to learn the name of a future spouse (SCAG, 2014).
- **Temples and shrines:** SCAG says he has no currently active temples in Faerûn. His shrines are few and far between, tucked away in the corners of libraries and scriptoria (SCAG, 2014). The wiki says priests still worshiped him in temples across Chult in 1490 DR (*Tomb of Annihilation*, 5e). This page follows SCAG (see Draft Notes).
- **Who pays homage:** Investigators, diviners, judges and others who need to uncover the truth honor him regularly. They can sometimes be recognized by the elaborate staffs they carry in his name. The devout decorate their staffs with great care, each hoping Savras will find it a welcoming place to stop for a time (SCAG, 2014).
- **Where he is loved:** The wiki says he was worshiped in both the north and the south as a god of diviners and truth-speakers (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). He was especially beloved in the nations of the Chultan Peninsula, among them Tashalar, Samarach and Nimbral, where scrying and spying were national pastimes born of fear of the yuan-ti (*Neverwinter Nights 2: Storm of Zehir*, a video game). The Gur peoples worshiped him, which may explain how the faith survived his long imprisonment (*Races of Faerûn*, 3e).
- **Clergy:** His clergy was still sparse in the late 14th century DR, because he had only just been released from his prison (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). His only famous servant was Alaundo of Candlekeep, the one true prophet of the Realms (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
- **The Benign Order of the Third Eye:** A secret order of archivists formed in 1371 DR, with the highest concentration of archivists of any group in the Realms. Savras granted each member a vision that told them to collect magic and lore for a coming battle with Cyric. The order became known for robbing graves in its search (*Class Chronicles*, a web article, 3e).
- **How he is pictured:** He is a man with a crystalline face that anyone can see through. His statements are clear and concise, and they are rarely what the listener hoped to hear. His symbol is a monstrous eye or a crystal ball full of eyes (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Tomb of Annihilation*, 5e). His emotionless manner hides deep concern for the fate of Faerûn, which he has little power to change (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **What people believe he does:** Some hold that he guides the forces of destiny, and others that he knows all history and cannot affect any of it (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e). Sages have also wondered whether he foresaw his own defeat by Azuth and planned it as a feint in a long game. He refuses to speak of it (a quotation the wiki gives from *Foresight Forsaken*, citing *Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
- **His duty:** The wiki says his main duty was to watch the casting of epic magic and to curb its use among non-mortals and extraplanar beings, especially when it was done heedlessly (a 2023 Greenwood's Grotto post).
- **Messengers, omens and holy days:** No source gives any. The wiki's holy day fields are blank in every edition. [research]
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced.
  - He served Azuth along with Velsharoon, and so served Mystra indirectly. He resented serving Azuth, but the two were slowly and cautiously working out a division of portfolios and a friendlier relationship (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Mystra sponsored him as well. The two gods fought for her favor for many years, until Azuth defeated Savras (the Mystra page; *Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
  - His scholarly interests made Deneir popular with the gods of magic, Savras among them (the Deneir page; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - He was once a mortal wizard of Halruaa (*Magic of Faerûn*, 3e).
  - As a demigod he was the god of spellcasters. Azuth contended for his place and the two fought a duel, which Azuth won (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). He imprisoned Savras in the Scepter of Savras, and the Azuth page adds that Azuth made the staff himself and rose to godhood with Mystra's help (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
  - Savras lost his place as god of spellcasters. He remained a prisoner until the Time of Troubles in 1358 DR, when Azuth released him in exchange for a pledge of fealty. He then became the deity of the school of divination only (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). On the Azuth page, Azuth loses the Scepter, Syluné Silverhand returns it, and guilt moves him to free Savras.
  - In 1385 DR Cyric entered Dweomerheart with Shar's aid and murdered Mystra. The plane broke apart, which hurled Azuth and Velsharoon into the Astral Plane and killed Savras outright (*The Grand History of the Realms*, 4e).
  - He returned to godhood by the 1480s DR (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e; *Player's Handbook*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Savras is alive in 1492 DR and holds his place among the gods of magic. No file shows him as dead or absent, so the page treats the 4e death as history.

> #### Using Savras as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the god of the mirror and the library corner. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the All-Seeing, the god of divination and fate, shown as a crystal ball full of eyes. They add that few people worship him but many call on his name in small rituals of foresight, and that investigators, diviners and judges carry decorated staffs in his honor. A sage may tell the old legend that Azuth trapped him in a staff for ages and let him go only when he swore fealty, and may add that some wonder whether he foresaw his own defeat. Nobody in this conversation knows of a temple of his in the city.
> - **Waterdeep:** The sources place no temple or shrine of Savras in the city.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table (`sources/adventure-wdh.json`, caption at line 30910) has no Savras row. It lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as the temple of "All deities" (line 30924), which includes him by its own wording.

>   - SCAG says his shrines sit in the corners of libraries and scriptoria. No file names a library or scriptorium in Waterdeep with such a shrine, so a PC who sets up one is adding to the city and not following a source.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Arcana and Knowledge (SCAG, 2014; the 2014 Player's Handbook lists Knowledge only). The 2024 Player's Handbook has neither, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is Light Domain (derived). The fit is loose, because Light is the subclass built around sight and revelation and none of the four covers divination. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because his worshipers study omens and hidden truths and the order's bonus to Arcana and Religion checks suits them. A cleric of Savras has no temple to report to, only library shrines and private rites.
> - **A wizard PC:** Not a template bullet, but SCAG names diviners among his regular worshipers. A Diviner wizard (derived) can carry a decorated staff in his honor and treat it as a place for him to stop for a time.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived). The fit is loose, because no source names a paladin of Savras. Judges are among his followers, and Devotion's insistence on honesty suits a god of truth.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. Three deity pages name him as history, and none is a scene. The Mystra page says she sponsored him and that Azuth defeated him (`campaign/setting/deities/mystra.md:48`). ## Overview

Savras is the god of divination and fate, a lawful neutral power whom the faithful call the All-Seeing. Few people worship him openly, but many call on him in small rituals of foresight, such as a rhyming chant said into a mirror to learn the name of a future spouse. He has no active temples, only shrines tucked into the corners of libraries and scriptoria, and investigators, diviners and judges carry decorated staffs in his honor. Tales say that the wizard god Azuth once trapped him in a staff and freed him only after he swore fealty.

**Tone:** Quiet and exact. Savras is a god of looking, so describe the mirror, the chant and the decorated staff before the power, and keep him at a distance. The campaign writes no scene in which he appears.
