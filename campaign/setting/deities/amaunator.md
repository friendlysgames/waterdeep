# Amaunator

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Amaunator, the sun god and keeper of law and oaths, and the reference for the old Netherese faith that Lathander's and Siamorphe's pages touch.
> - **Where it is used:** Amaunator never appears in person, and no campaign file or *Dragon Heist* line names him. This page exists so a cleric or paladin player can choose him, and so the GM can answer the question of how he relates to Lathander and Siamorphe.
> - **Featured in:** No quest, faction or NPC. The **Lathander** and **Siamorphe** pages mention him as history.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. Nothing about Amaunator is secret, and the Overview is the common-knowledge tier.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Amaunator, and sets out how the sources relate him to Lathander and Siamorphe so that the three pages agree.

*The Keeper of the Eternal Sun*

- **Title:** God of the Sun (FRHoF)
- **Other Names:** The Light of Law, the Yellow God (FRHoF). The wiki adds Keeper of the Yellow Sun, Keeper of the Golden Sun, Keeper of Law and the Eternal Sun.
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian (FRHoF); Netherese in the wiki's older books
- **Areas of Concern:** Law, oaths and contracts, order and bureaucracy, the sun (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki portfolios; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Life, Light (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Golden sun (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Mechanus (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Farmers, lawmakers, travelers (FRHoF)

> Keep your word, and keep the law.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Amaunator is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon in FRHoF and SCAG, and neither entry gives a rank. The wiki calls him a greater deity in ancient Netheril and again in 4e, with a long slide through lesser deity to dead power in between (2e and 3e).
- **What the faith asks:** His followers keep their promises, honor contracts and uphold the rule of law. He is a stern and unforgiving god who cares that events proceed according to the celestial order. SCAG likens his temper to Silvanus, though he has no concern for the balance of life.
- **The letter of the law:** The wiki's 2e sources have him preach obedience to the law to the letter, since laws are the foundation of any society. His followers learn the law so they can avoid punishment, and they obey their superiors while keeping tabs on their rulings so that precedents accumulate (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). The 4e *Forgotten Realms Player's Guide* adds that he wants his followers to honor tradition, meet their commitments and arrive on time.
- **Death and rebirth:** His priests teach that he has died and been reborn many times. Like the sun, he may pass into darkness, and his gaze will fall on the world again (SCAG, 2014). His followers believe the same (FRHoF).
- **Oaths under the sun:** Farmers and travelers pray to him for sun or rain, and so does anyone who wants a favorable change in the weather. The most common worship is swearing oaths, signing contracts and declaring laws under the sun. People closing a deal or issuing an edict often wait for a passing cloud to clear the sun before they finish (SCAG, 2014; FRHoF).
- **Clergy:** His priests help communities set up bureaucracies and lawful order. They witness contracts and signed agreements and stamp them with the golden sun to show they are valid (SCAG, 2014; FRHoF).
- **Temples:** They have courtyards and skylights to let in the sun. Underground or under constant cloud, a temple mounts a torch carrying a *Continual Flame* spell over its doorway. Each temple keeps extensive records of the laws and legal proceedings of its community, and most open them to the public. Where corruption has reached a temple, access to the records costs a hefty sum (FRHoF).
- **Who follows:** Farmers, lawmakers and travelers (FRHoF). The wiki adds merchants, nomads and paladins (4e), and politicians (3e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Amaunator. FRHoF says his Chosen master magic that dispels darkness and unveils hidden truths, and that some enforce the law across the land while others become magistrates in the highest courts of Faerûn.
- **Messengers:** An envoy of his is unmistakable. A couatl, or a bird that looks ordinary but has eyes like small suns, descends from the sky in a shaft of bright light. He also sends dreams in which the night is suddenly driven out by full daylight (FRHoF).
- **Manifestations (wiki, 2e):** A woman in an opalescent gown that carries a balance of golden scales. Whoever meets her reads his standing from the scale. If it tips left, the person faces seven years of poverty, debt and servitude. If it tips right, good fortune follows. If it is level, the person is invited to join his clergy. Thirteen giant hyenas also serve him. They kill an offender who deserves it, or someone who cannot avenge a wrong themselves, and tear the body apart across an acre (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Omen:** No source gives one apart from the manifestations above. [research]
- **Holy days:** The third full moon of the year and the summer solstice (wiki, 2e). The 3e, 4e and 5e holy-day fields are blank. No file ties a city festival to him. The nearest festival in the city's calendar is Midsummer (derived), which falls after Flamerule 30 and outside the campaign window.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the edition named on each fact.
  - **Lathander:** They are separate gods in 1492 DR. FRHoF says Amaunator's followers quarrel with Lathander's over whether the Morninglord is an aspect of Amaunator. That is a belief the two faiths argue over, and it does not change the fact that the gods are distinct. Lathander stands for the optimistic light of dawn and Amaunator for the sun in every aspect, warm and encouraging at times and harsh at others. The history of the claim is under Origin and rise.
  - **Siamorphe and Waukeen (4e):** After he returned, he lived on the astral dominion of the Eternal Sun with Waukeen, and Siamorphe served him as his exarch (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e). The same book says he found Sune attractive.
  - **Torm:** They are good friends, and Torm holds him in great respect (*Dragon* 381, 4e; FRHoF).
  - **Enemies:** Bane is his enemy (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 3e). Shar opposed him as well, at least while he stood in the Netherese pantheon (2e and 3e sources).
  - **Mystryl:** In the Netherese pantheon the goddess of magic and the god of law kept to their own spheres, and he never acted on his claim to time for fear of her displeasure (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - **Ancient Netheril:** He was the sun god of the Netherese, a greater deity revered by many rulers, soldiers and powerful wizards. He judged disputes over the Pantheon Contour, the agreement among the Netherese gods (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). A misplaced mark in a legal paper let him call himself god of time, but he never acted on it (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - **The fall of Netheril:** Netheril fell in -339 DR (see **The Long Shadow**). The wiki says Amaunator did nothing to stop it, because a divine contract gave magic to Mystryl and he had no right to interfere (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). His Netherese worshipers abandoned him over it, and only the lower and middle classes stayed (*Lost Empires of Faerûn*, 3e).
  - **The long decline:** His worshipers dwindled for about a thousand years, and he fell from greater deity to lesser deity to dead power. His realm, the Keep of the Eternal Sun on Mechanus, ceased to exist, and his corpse drifted into the Astral Plane. The wiki dates the end of the slide to about 661 DR (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e; *The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - **The faith that survived:** Netherese survivors told the Bedine stories of the Yellow God that twisted over the years into At'ar the Merciless, a goddess with no clerics (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e). Clerics of the dead god could still gain spells by learning the art of a servant of the fallen (*Lost Empires of Faerûn*, 3e). Two heresies in Lathander's church held that Amaunator would return, the Three-Faced Sun heresy and the Risen Sun heresy (*Power of Faerûn*, 3e).
  - **The Risen Sun heresy:** It taught that Lathander was Amaunator reincarnated. In the 14th century DR its radicals believed Lathander was about to reveal himself as Amaunator. In 1374 DR a fire genasi named Daelegoth Orndeir drew a crowd to Elversult with a spell of his own making that he passed off as a sign, and many flocked from Lathander's church to the new faith (*Power of Faerûn*, 3e).
  - **After the Spellplague (4e):** In the 4e account, Lathander was revealed to be Amaunator in 1385 DR, just after the Spellplague (*The Crystal Mountain*). Amaunator became an established greater deity and the keeper of time among the gods (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*). By 1479 DR he was worshiped both as Lathander and by his own name, and he encouraged people to see Lathander as his youthful aspect (*Forgotten Realms Player's Guide*).
  - **After the Second Sundering (settled by ruling):** The Second Sundering ended in 1487 DR and undid every Spellplague-era merger, so Lathander and Amaunator are separate gods again. The line in the *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide* that the two were worshiped as separate beings by 1491 DR records that this had already happened. The wiki's note that it is unknown whether they were truly separate does not apply.
- **In the campaign:** Amaunator is present in 1492 DR as a god in his own right, and he and Lathander are separate gods. This matches the **Lathander** page, which keeps the aspect story as 4e history, and the **Siamorphe** page, which keeps her as a demigod and gives her service to him as a 4e account. The wiki's deaths and returns, the 1385 DR revelation and the Eternal Sun are history and carry no weight in play.

> #### Using Amaunator as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the witness to every oath. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the sun god and the god of law, that farmers and travelers pray to him for good weather, and that a promise sworn under the sun is a promise to him. They add that a merchant closing a deal will wait for a cloud to pass before signing, that his priests stamp contracts with a golden sun, and that he is stern and forgives little. A priest or sage adds that his faith is old, that the Netherese worshiped him, and that his priests teach he has died and risen many times. Some say the Morninglord is only another face of him, which the followers of both dispute. How often a Waterdhavian actually names him is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** No source places a temple or shrine of Amaunator in the city, and no festival is tied to him.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples of Waterdeep table has no Amaunator row (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30910`).
>   - The same table lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as a temple for all deities, which covers him by its own wording (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30923-30925`). No source says his faithful use it.
>   - No campaign file or adventure line names him, so a cleric of his has no temple to join in the city. Whether the priests at the Spires of the Morning, Lathander's temple in the Castle Ward, take any view on the claim that Lathander is Amaunator's aspect is [research].
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Life and Light (SCAG, 2014). Order Domain (derived; Tasha's Cauldron of Everything) is the best fit, because his portfolio is law, oaths and contracts, his priests witness agreements and keep a community's records, and the domain's features include Voice of Authority and Embodiment of the Law. Light Domain (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the alternative for a cleric who leans on the sun, since he is the sun god and his symbol is a golden sun. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), since his clergy witness contracts, keep records and study the law, and his Chosen master magic that unveils hidden truths.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of the Crown (derived; Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide). The oath is sworn to civilization, to a sovereign or to the service of a deity of law, and the 2e faith has his followers learn the law, obey their superiors and keep tabs on their rulings. Oath of Devotion (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the alternative for a paladin who simply keeps promises and deals honestly, and the wiki lists paladins among his 4e worshipers.
> - **A warlock PC:** Vestige Patron (derived; Arcana Unleashed). A Vestige warlock's pact draws on a dying god once worshiped by countless followers, and the sources give Amaunator that history: a fall to dead power with his corpse drifting in the Astral Plane, clerics of the dead god who could still gain spells, and priests who teach he has died and risen many times. He is a living god in 1492 DR, so the fit is a backstory choice for the player. No source names a warlock of Amaunator. The Celestial patron (derived; Player's Handbook 2024) is the alternative for a warlock who serves the living god, since the sources give him couatl messengers, a golden-sun symbol and Chosen who wield magic against darkness.

## Overview

Amaunator is the god of the sun and of law, known as the Keeper of the Eternal Sun and the Light of Law. Farmers and travelers pray to him for sun or rain, and people across the Sword Coast swear oaths and sign contracts beneath his light. His priests witness agreements and stamp them with a golden sun, and his temples keep the law of their communities on public record. He is an old god, a power of fallen Netheril who is said to have died and been reborn many times. His followers and Lathander's argue over whether the Morninglord is another face of him. The two are separate gods, and Waterdeep has no temple of his own.

**Tone:** Stern and orderly. Amaunator is a god of promises, so describe what he asks of a person who gives their word before describing what he does to one who breaks it. Keep him in the present tense and at a distance, and let the Lathander question stay a quarrel between two faiths, spoken of as a belief and never as a fact about the gods.
