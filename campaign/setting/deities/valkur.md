> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Valkur, the Captain of the Waves and the god of sailors, and the reference for what a Waterdhavian can say about him.
> - **Where it is used:** Almost nowhere in the campaign. The festival calendar lists him third among the sea gods honored at Fleetswake, and no other campaign file, Notable Figure or published adventure line names him. The Dragon Heist Major Temples table gives him no temple. The page exists for character building, so a player can pick him as a sailor's, cleric's or paladin's patron.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what a sailor, a sage or a regular at the bar says about Valkur, and states plainly that the campaign places no temple or priest of his in the city so that no later page invents more.

*The Captain of the Waves*

- **Title:** Northlander God of Sailors (SCAG, 2014). No FRHoF entry exists for him.
- **Other Names:** The Captain of the Waves, the Mighty (wiki, citing *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e, and the *Cyclopedia of the Realms*, 1e). His followers are called Valkurytes (wiki, citing the *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Sailors, ships, favorable winds, naval combat (derived from the wiki's 2e and 3e portfolio lines; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Tempest, War (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A cloud and three lightning bolts (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Good (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Ysgard (wiki, 2e) or Warrior's Rest, as part of Tempus's realm (wiki, 3e and 4e). His own realm is Safe Harbor (wiki, 2e and 3e). FRHoF gives no plane for him.
- **Typical Followers:** Sailors, and Northlanders above all (SCAG, 2014, via the wiki's 5e row). The wiki's 3e row lists fighters, rogues and sailors.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Valkur is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon, and the wiki calls him a minor one. Its rank for him changes with the edition: a demipower in 2e (*Powers & Pantheons*), a demigod in 3e, an exarch of Tempus in 4e (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*), and a hero-god and lesser deity in its 5e row.
- **What the faith asks:** His doctrine tells followers to defy the odds and dare the impossible, both to spread humanity's reach and for the thrill of it. It treats life aboard a ship, fighting the elements themselves, as the most invigorating life there is. It encourages independence, and a follower owes loyalty first to the crew, second to the ship and third to Valkur (wiki, citing *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **What the faith offers:** Many gods receive a sailor's prayers about one part of the work, and Valkur is the one asked for good tidings for the ship, everyone aboard and the voyage as a whole (wiki, citing a forum post by Ed Greenwood). His friendship with Selûne gave his faithful a minor blessing of better navigation (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e). His name is spoken to bless the spirits of dead sailors and lost ships (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
- **Temperament:** He is jolly, never holds a grudge, and is bold and lucky. He is also fickle, and nothing guarantees that he will answer a call for aid, although he is loyal to his crew and counts the protection of sailors as his chief duty. He became more responsive to his faithful after the Time of Troubles (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Warriors and Priests of the Realms*).
- **Who follows:** Sailors, and everyone who earns a living from the sea, from rich merchants to fisherfolk (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e). Inland folk know little of him. The wiki's 3e sources add that water genasi and air genasi clerics sometimes choose him (*Races of Faerûn*).
- **Northlanders:** In the past Northlanders honored him only now and then, because his protection could not be relied on (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 2e; *The Savage Frontier*). By the fifteenth century DR he is their one god, revered above all others as the embodiment of strength, courage, ferocity, guile and sailing skill, and nobody does well to question that devotion (SCAG, 2014, via the wiki).
- **Where he is worshiped:** The wiki's 3e sources say the humans and half-elves of Aglarond venerate him, that he is favored in Impiltur, Altumbel, Tharsult and Wizard's Reach, that sea traders in Amn appease him, and that he matters where Illuskans and Tethyrians live together. None of these lists names Waterdeep.
- **Clergy:** The clergy is loosely organized. Many of its clerics serve as captains or sailors aboard ships, and they usually meet only by chance, when they serve the same ship or reach the same port. They pray for their spells around dawn, as the first breeze stirs (wiki, citing *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
- **Holy days:** The church keeps one, the Shattering, in spring on a date that varies. It celebrates the breaking up of the ice floes in the north, which opens the sailing season (wiki, citing *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). The campaign's festival calendar gives him a place at Fleetswake instead, on Ches 21 to 30, and no file puts the Shattering in the city's calendar.
- **Omens and messengers:** He often appears as an aurora, and sailors say he visits becalmed ships as a ghostly wind in which his face can faintly be seen. Sometimes he appears as a ghostly ship on the horizon that always sails toward the nearest port and never seems closer or farther away. Dolphins, whales (narwhals especially) and seabirds serve him. A silver albatross or gull drifting on the wind shows his favor, and a night-black raven shows his displeasure (wiki, citing *Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No source in the bundle names a Chosen of Valkur.
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - **Selûne** is his friend, and he looks to her for guidance across the seas (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e). **Deep Sashelas** of the Seldarine has lent him aid so that he can better counter Umberlee's growing influence (*Demihuman Deities*, 3e). **Istishia** has ties to him, although the elemental god of water cares little about sailors or Umberlee's storms (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - **The Red Knight**, and Tempus through her, tried to persuade him to take a greater interest in naval conflict (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Her expectations sometimes exceeded his commitment to anything but protecting sailors (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e), yet she still counts him her best ally in naval conflict despite his unreliability (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - **Tempus** is casually friendly with him, since Valkur is at least somewhat martial (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). In the wiki's 4e account Valkur serves Tempus as an exarch (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*).
  - He is the sworn foe of the Gods of Fury, because he shields ships from the destruction their leader **Talos** sends and sailors call on him for favorable winds against it (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting*, 2e; *Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - His true nemesis is **Umberlee**, whose cruel whims he defends sailors against. The wiki says she might yet try to destroy him for challenging her dominion, but as far as anyone can tell he has defied her without serious retribution (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - His worship began in the North of Faerûn centuries before the thirteenth century DR. He seems tied to a legendary captain from Mintarn, a mortal sailor and possibly Valkur himself, who challenged Umberlee and beat her against all odds (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). The wiki's categories list him as a formerly mortal human.
  - The video game *Icewind Dale: Heart of Winter* makes him a friend of the mage Gullwyn, who gave him the Dagger of Safe Harbor after retiring from the sea. That is a game source and ranks below the books.
  - From the North his faith spread quickly to Tethyr and Amn, where it became centered, and then steadily southward (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; *Lands of Intrigue: Amn*, 3e).
  - The wiki says his activity during the Time of Troubles is unclear. Afterward he worked on his unresponsiveness, which had led northern sailors to placate the Gods of Fury instead of trusting him. In the fifteenth century DR his focus has settled on the North, and he is counted a god of Northlander sailors (*Powers & Pantheons*, 2e; SCAG, 2014, via the wiki).
- **In the campaign:** Valkur is alive and present in 1492 DR as a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The 4e exarchy is wiki history that this page does not use as his present rank (see Draft Notes).

> #### Using Valkur as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the sailors' friend, who is not always at home. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the Captain of the Waves, the god of sailors, ships and fair winds, and that his sign is a cloud with three lightning bolts. They add that sailors ask him to see a voyage through from the first tide to the last, that he is a cheerful sort who bears no grudges, and that you cannot count on him to answer. Old hands say he comes to becalmed ships as a ghostly wind, and that a silver gull on the wind means he is pleased. Northlander sailors hold him above every other god, and an insult to him is a bad idea in their company. How the rest of Waterdeep feels about him is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** The sources place no temple, shrine, priest or standing observance of Valkur in the city, and the campaign gives him one festival mention.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Valkur row. Its only generic row is "All deities", which names Holyhands House in the North Ward and no power (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30922-30926`). The wiki has no text that mentions Waterdeep.
>   - The campaign calendar lists Fleetswake, Ches 21 to 30, as a festival that "honors the sea, maritime trade and the sea gods, with Umberlee first, then Deep Sashelas and Valkur" (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:82`). It starts on the day of the Twin Parades (`:63`), so the festival falls inside the campaign window.
>   - The Dragon Heist entry for Fleetswake names only Umberlee and "the gods of the sea" (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31390-31395`). The winners deliver their trophies to the priests of Umberlee at the Queenspire, her temple on the beach by the east entrance to the Great Harbor, and on the last day the City Guard collect offerings for Umberlee. No source gives Valkur a collection, a priest or a ceremony.
>   - Whether a dockside shrine to him stands anywhere in the city is [research].
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Tempest and War (SCAG, 2014). The 2024 Player's Handbook has no Tempest Domain, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is War Domain (derived), a good fit because War is one of his two domains. The Divine Order that fits is Protector (derived), for a cleric who guards a crew. A cleric would find no temple of his in the city and no priest to teach the faith, so the wiki's clergy of captains and sailors is the model.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Glory (derived), a loose fit. His doctrine of daring the impossible matches the oath's heroic bent. No source lists paladins among his followers.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** The festival calendar is the only campaign file that names him. It lists him third among the sea gods at Fleetswake, and its note for the festival, "Cover for Sea Maidens Faire reconnaissance", ties the festival to the Faire and not to Valkur. No quest, faction or Notable Figure uses this power.

## Overview

Valkur is the Captain of the Waves, a chaotic good god of sailors, ships and favorable winds, and his symbol is a cloud with three lightning bolts. Sailors ask him for a safe voyage, though he is a cheerful and fickle protector who may not answer. Northlanders hold him above every other god. Waterdeep has no temple of his, and his name is heard mostly at Fleetswake, the sea festival of Ches 21 to 30, where he is honored after Umberlee and Deep Sashelas.

**Tone:** Brisk and salty, in the plain reference register. Valkur is a god of sailors, so describe the voyage and the weather before the faith, and let his followers sound hearty and a little superstitious. Keep him in the present tense and at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which he acts, and keep Waterdeep out of his story apart from Fleetswake.
