*The High One*

- **Title:** God of Wizardry (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014)
- **Other Names:** Lord of Spells, the Shining Hand of Sorcery (FRHoF); the Lord of Spellcraft, the First Magister (SCAG, 2014); Patron of Wizards, Patron of Mages, Hand of Sorcery (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian
- **Areas of Concern:** Wizardry, spellcraft, the preservation of spells and the magebond among spellcasters (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text; neither entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Arcana, Knowledge (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** A left hand pointing upward, outlined in fire (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Lawful Neutral (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Arcadia (Buxenus) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Arcane spellcasters (FRHoF)

> Give magic its form, and keep wizards' quarrels out of the street.

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Azuth is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon, renowned as the right hand of Mystra and the First Magister (FRHoF). The wiki ranks him a lesser deity and says he served Mystra (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **His portfolio:** Mystra is the power of magic in all its forms, and Azuth is the lord of spellcasting, the Art of giving shape to the raw power of the Weave. His Aphorism says "Where Mystra is force, Azuth grants form" (FRHoF). SCAG describes him as the god of a wizard's long hours of study, exacting standards of movement and speech, and cramped, ink-stained fingers (SCAG, 2014).
- **What the faith asks:** His priests teach that the Art must be wooed with adoration and reverence, like a lifelong love, because the myths show the First Magister deeply in love with Mystra. They claim that spellcasters who meditate on the Aphorism reach new understanding of the Art (FRHoF).
- **Good and evil:** Azuth cares less about good and evil than Mystra does. He tolerates evil spellcasters as long as they do not deceive or spread chaos (FRHoF).
- **The magebond:** His priests spread and enforce a social covenant among the spellcasters of Faerûn. It says that personal disputes between spellcasters should not attract the attention of mundane society, and it urges them to settle such matters among themselves, preferably in peace (FRHoF).
- **Who follows:** Arcane spellcasters (FRHoF). Few people pay homage to him aside from wizards, for whom he embodies everything they hold dear. Wizards invoke him when they scribe scrolls, inscribe magic circles, memorize spells and cast them, usually by silently pointing the left index finger at the sky, a gesture many make without noticing (SCAG, 2014).
- **Clergy:** His priests are called the magistrati. They host magic fairs, collect rare spell scrolls so that a spell's inventor does not take it to the grave, give spellbooks to people with talent who cannot afford to study, and sometimes teach at schools of magic (FRHoF). Clerics of Azuth are extremely rare (SCAG, 2014).
- **The wiki's picture of the clergy:** The magistrati include wizards, monks and clerics. They prefer temples in large cities, ally with powerful magical guilds and act as a kind of magical administration that eases communication between spellcasters and enforces the magebond (*Faiths & Pantheons*). Laypeople often see the church as archaic, pedantic in its debates and blind to everyday problems, and the clergy's moral neutrality earns it an unfavorable name (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
- **Orders:** His Chosen are called the Favored, and they push the limits of magical research, creating new ways to interact with the Weave (FRHoF). The wiki adds two orders (*Faiths & Pantheons*). The Loomwarden are a stern sect that distrusts sorcerers and the chaotic side of Mystra's power, and they hold sway in Halarahh and Bezantur. The Spellsavants are a tolerant order that urges all arcane practitioners to cooperate. No file places any of them in Waterdeep.
- **Temples and shrines:** Azuth has few dedicated temples, but every temple of Mystra holds at least one shrine to him or a chapel dedicated to him (FRHoF). Even in Halruaa only a handful of holy places are his, and a wizard more often keeps a personal shrine at home (SCAG, 2014).
- **How shrines show him:** A hooded and bearded figure with the left hand held high and the finger pointing up, or sometimes only the hand. The finger often serves as a candleholder or as the point of origin for a *Light* spell (SCAG, 2014).
- **Messengers:** He speaks through a *Magic Mouth* with a gray beard and mustache, or sends a gray animal such as a cat or an owl. In extreme cases he sends a sphinx (FRHoF). The wiki adds gray dogs and mice, and lists an upright glowing hand with a silver aura on the forefinger and an electric blue radiance among his manifestations (wiki infobox; *Deity Do's and Don'ts*).
- **Omen:** No source gives one. [research]
- **Holy days:** The wiki's 2e row lists the ascension of a new magister and occasional Wild Nights. The 5e row is blank. No file ties a city festival to him.
- **The Old Staff:** He wields the Old Staff, an artifact of incalculable arcane power marked with sigils and topped by a shape-changing crystal (FRHoF).
- **Manner:** The wiki calls him paternal and sober but not humorless, with a dry, sardonic wit so well delivered that listeners cannot tell when he is joking (*Faiths & Avatars*). He is usually pictured as a white-haired, bearded, vigorous old man in gray silk robes, and he never wears a hat (*Faiths & Avatars*).
- **Relations:** The FRHoF says Azuth is friendly with Oghma, who shares his neutral outlook. The rest is wiki-sourced.
  - The wiki lists Deneir, Oghma and Leira as his allies (*Faiths & Avatars*).
  - Velsharoon and Savras served him. Savras was his prisoner for many years, which left their relationship complicated (*Faiths & Pantheons*).
  - He served Mystra as her Chosen, her subordinate and her lover, and acted as her adviser (*Powers & Pantheons*; *Faiths & Pantheons*). He was paternal toward the later Mystra, Midnight, and guided her through her first difficult years (*Faiths & Avatars*).
  - The Mystra page in this journal names him among her traditional allies.
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact.
  - Little is known of his mortal life. He studied several lost empires for forgotten secrets and became the first Magister, which Mystra granted him as her most promising pupil (*Powers & Pantheons*). He later felt unworthy of the title, and Mystra made him one of her Chosen (*Magic of Faerûn*). His apotheosis came before 147 DR (*The Grand History of the Realms*).
  - During or shortly after the Dawn Cataclysm he tried to steal part of the demigod Savras's power. The battles ended in a duel that shattered a mountain and left a deep lake behind. Azuth won, imprisoned Savras in a staff he had made, the Scepter of Savras, and ascended with Mystra's help (*Powers & Pantheons*).
  - He lost the Scepter, and Syluné Silverhand found it and returned it. He then felt guilty and freed Savras after the Time of Troubles, in exchange for a pledge of fealty (*Powers & Pantheons*; *Volo's Guide to All Things Magical*).
- **The Spellplague and Asmodeus:** Wiki-sourced, with the edition or book on each fact. This account agrees with the Asmodeus page, which gives the same dates.
  - The 4e *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide* has Azuth dead in 1385 DR, and the wiki marks the date "allegedly". Many people believed the archdevil Asmodeus killed him and consumed his divine essence to become a god (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e).
  - The wiki says the destruction of Dweomerheart sent the dying Azuth into Nessus. In fact Asmodeus fused with him, and the two gods have coexisted in one body since (*Ashes of the Tyrant*).
  - Asmodeus overpowered him most of the time and used the divine power while Azuth lay dormant (*The Devil You Know*). Azuth sometimes took control, and devils in the Nine Hells thought Asmodeus had gone mad (*Ashes of the Tyrant*).
  - By 1486 DR Azuth had regained most of his strength and chose Ilstan Nyaril as his Chosen (*Fire in the Blood*). The two gods then struggled for control of the body, which threatened the hierarchy of the Nine Hells (*Ashes of the Tyrant*).
  - Ilstan and Farideh, a Chosen of Asmodeus, devised a plan to separate them. Asmodeus agreed to let Azuth go, and in return the Untherite god Enlil let Asmodeus consume the divine spark of Nanna-Sin, another Untherite god, whom the plan restored as a non-god immortal (*The Devil You Know*).
  - The ritual took place in Djerad Thymar, during the First Tymanther-Unther War, on Hammer 10, 1487 DR. Ilstan gave his life, and Azuth became an individual god again (*The Devil You Know*).
  - After the Second Sundering Azuth returned to the Faerûnian pantheon (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e; *Player's Handbook*, 2014).
- **In the campaign:** Azuth is alive and separate from Asmodeus in 1492 DR. No file shows him as dead, absent or sharing a body with another god, so the page treats the 1385 DR death and the fusion as history.

> #### Using Azuth as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice him as the patron of the study room. A sage, a priest or a regular at the bar says he is the High One, the god of wizards, shown as a hooded, bearded figure with a raised left hand. They add that wizards point a finger at the sky when they scribe a scroll or begin a spell, and that Azuth is Mystra's right hand. A Waterdhavian who knows the Trades Ward tells how a spell battle once tore the Weave in the Court of the White Bull, and how Azuth had to appear and stitch it together. Laypeople find his priests fussy, and a sage may add the old story that the archdevil Asmodeus devoured him in the Spellplague and that he came back after the Sundering (wiki; *Forgotten Realms Campaign Guide*, 4e; *Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e). Nobody in this conversation knows of any priest or shrine of his in the city.
> - **Waterdeep:** The sources place no temple or shrine of Azuth in the city.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Azuth row. It lists Holyhands House in the North Ward as the temple of "All deities", which includes him by its own wording. No campaign file mentions a shrine of Azuth anywhere in Waterdeep.
>   - The one place in the city that names him is the Court of the White Bull in the Trades Ward, a plaza and not a temple (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:31038`). The text says "Azuth, god of wizards, was forced to appear and set things right" after a spell battle between the archmage Thongalar the Mighty and the evil mage Shile Rauretilar, and "a wrinkle in the fabric remains."
>   - The same text says magic used in the Court sometimes goes awry and that "the use of magic items and spells is forbidden in the area." It names no shrine, priest or visitor.
>   - The House of Wonder in the Sea Ward is a temple of Mystra, so the FRHoF rule that every temple of Mystra holds a shrine or chapel of Azuth would put one there (derived). No file says the House has one.
> - **A cleric PC:** The domains are Arcana and Knowledge (SCAG, 2014; the 2014 Player's Handbook lists them in the other order). The 2024 Player's Handbook has neither, so the closest subclass among Life, Light, Trickery and War is Light Domain (derived). The fit is loose. Light brings radiance and evocation, and SCAG notes that his shrines use the raised finger as a candleholder or the source of a *Light* spell. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because his clergy study magic and the Arcana bonus suits a cleric of the Art. Clerics of Azuth are extremely rare, so a PC cleric would stand out in any temple.
> - **A wizard PC:** Not a template bullet, but the faith is made for wizards. A wizard can point the left finger at the sky when scribing a scroll, drawing a circle or preparing spells, as SCAG describes. A wizard can also keep a home shrine and follow the magebond, which asks spellcasters to keep their private disputes away from mundane society (FRHoF).
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Devotion (derived). The fit is loose, because the FRHoF lists only arcane spellcasters as his followers and no source names a paladin of Azuth. Devotion matches his insistence that tolerated spellcasters may not deceive or spread chaos.
> - **A warlock PC:** No warlock connection in the sources.
> - **Campaign ties:** No quest, faction or NPC uses Azuth. Three mentions exist, and none of them is a scene.
>   - The published adventure names him at the Court of the White Bull (see Waterdeep above).
>   - The Asmodeus page says Asmodeus "consumed the god Azuth after the Spellplague of 1385 DR and became a god" and that "Azuth was separated from him on Hammer 10, 1487 DR" (`campaign/setting/deities/asmodeus.md:46-47`).
>   - The Mystra page calls Azuth "the first Magister, a brash young wizard whom she sponsored to godhood as a demipower and the Patron of Wizards" (`campaign/setting/deities/mystra.md:47`).
>   - No Notable Figures page names Azuth or ties a wizard to him. The files tie Laeral Silverhand to Mystra as her daughter, and the Blackstaff's office to her only in the wiki, so do not give either of them a faith in Azuth.

## Overview

Azuth is the god of wizardry, a lawful neutral power whom the faithful call the High One and who is held to be the right hand of Mystra. Wizards invoke him when they scribe scrolls and cast spells, and many point a finger at the sky as they do. Few temples are dedicated to him, though every temple of Mystra is said to hold a shrine or chapel in his name. Waterdhavians tell that he once appeared in the Court of the White Bull in the Trades Ward to mend the Weave after a spell battle, and a wrinkle in it remains.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - **The Court of the White Bull:** The published text calls the plaza a grazing area that became the site of the White Bull Tavern. The tavern vanished in the battle between Thongalar and Shile Rauretilar, and Shile and his apprentices died. The campaign adds nothing to this, so the plaza is a place of lore where magic is forbidden and sometimes goes awry. The text says Azuth "is said to have" stitched the Weave together, so no source confirms he was ever there.
> - **The Asmodeus link:** The fusion and the separation in 1487 DR are wiki history, and the Asmodeus page records the same dates. Nothing in the campaign has Azuth speak, appear or take part in any scene. The link gives Azuth no part in the Cassalanters' pact, and no file ties him to the family, so do not use it to hint at the infernalism the family hides.

**Tone:** Dry and learned. Azuth is the god of study, so describe the long hours and the careful gestures before the power, and keep him at a distance. The campaign writes no scene in which he appears.
