> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the patron entry for Demogorgon, the two-headed demon lord who calls himself the Prince of Demons and a Fiend patron for warlock characters. He is not a god of the Faerûnian pantheon and the 5etools deity list has no entry for him, so this page replaces the cleric and paladin guidance of a deity page with warlock guidance and takes every field from the wiki, tagged.
> - **Where it is used:** Demogorgon never appears in the campaign. He is a choice for a player building a warlock, and no quest, faction or Notable Figure names him.
> - **Featured in:** no quest. Neither *Dragon Heist* nor *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* names him. The nearest published material is Halaster's demon-binding on Undermountain Level 20 and the kuo-toa of Level 4, and neither mentions him (see Campaign ties).
> - **Spoiler limit:** this page holds no hidden fact that changes play, so it has no Secret box. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier and never mentions the Cassalanters. Everything above it may be read by the GM only.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what little an ordinary Waterdhavian knows about Demogorgon, gives a player what a Fiend patron relationship with him can offer and cost, and states that the campaign has tied nothing to him so that no later page invents more.

*The Prince of Demons*

- **Title:** Prince of Demons (*Monster Manual* 1e, via the wiki)
- **Other Names:** The Deep Father, called Leemooggoogoon by the kuo-toa (*Out of the Abyss* 5e). The Sibilant Beast, called Ahmon-Ibor by troglodytes (*Fiendish Codex I* 3e). Siosivash to the yuan-ti (*Fiendish Codex I* 3e). Master of the Spiraling Depths (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e). Master of the Mysterious and King of the Deviants (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e). Some followers of Helm call him the Imprisoned One (*Baldur's Gate II: Throne of Bhaal*, video game).
- **Pantheon:** None. He is a demon lord and a patron, not a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki also rates him a lesser deity in 2e (*Monster Mythology*) and a primordial in 4e (*Monster Manual 2*).
- **Areas of Concern:** Madness, destruction, domination, the draining of energy, demonkind (derived from the wiki's description and 3e portfolio; the wiki lists no Areas of Concern as such)
- **Domains:** None in 5etools. The wiki lists Chaos, Corruption, Demonic and Evil (*Fiendish Codex I*, 3e), none of them a 2014 or 2024 cleric domain.
- **Symbol:** A forked tail, often wrapped around a skull or a sword (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e, via the wiki)
- **Alignment:** Chaotic Evil (wiki 5e entry)
- **Challenge:** 26 (wiki 5e entry; the 3e entry gives 23, with a note that *Book of Vile Darkness* gives 30)
- **Plane:** The Abyss, in the realm called the Gaping Maw, which the wiki numbers as the 88th layer (3e) and names for 5e.
- **Typical Followers:** Ixitxachitl and kraken in 2e (*Monster Mythology*), and also troglodytes, kuo-toa and other humanoids, particularly evil humans (the wiki, citing *Monster Manual 2* 4e)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Demogorgon is a demon lord, and his own title of Prince of Demons was won by power and influence rather than granted. Many demons challenged it and none could defeat him, and other demon lords fear him (*Monster Manual 2* 4e). The wiki gives him no calendar of holy days in any edition.
- **What he is:** The wiki calls him an embodiment of madness and destruction that wants to drag everything down into the depths of the Abyss (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e). Even the ixitxachitl, his chosen race, dared not speak his name aloud (*Fiendish Codex I* 3e).
- **Two heads:** Aameul is the left head and Hethradiah the right (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e; *Out of the Abyss* 5e). Aameul is charismatic and calculating, relishes deception and wants to break free of the other. Hethradiah is impulsive and feral, relishes destruction and does not want to be separated (*Monster Manual 2* 4e; *Book of Vile Darkness* 3e). Each head thinks the other inferior, and the wiki says this quarrel is the main thing that kept him from conquering the rest of the Abyss (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e).
- **Pacts and boons:** He is powerful enough to offer patronage pacts to warlocks, who usually keep quasits as familiars (*Player's Handbook* 5e; *Volo's Guide to Monsters* 5e). His most devout followers receive boons such as an aspect of his dual minds, which gives resistance to mind-affecting effects, and signature spells that mimic his own abilities, among them *charm person*, *enlarge/reduce* and *vampiric touch* (*Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes* 5e).
- **Worship and clergy:** His cult is small beside those of true deities and much larger than those of most fiends. The ixitxachitl draw power from the Abyss itself to become better spellcasters, and the vital energy drained by vampiric ixitxachitl passes directly to Demogorgon by an unknown Abyssal means (*Monster Mythology* 2e). The cult prospers in times of chaos and does great harm wherever it appears, and its temples are split in half, one side for Aameul and one for Hethradiah (*Monster Manual 2* 4e).
- **Aspects:** Mortals cannot summon Demogorgon himself, but the most pious can sacrifice to summon a temporary aspect that is smaller and weaker than he is. An aspect is always unbalanced and takes after one head more than the other (*Monster Manual 2* 4e; *Fiendish Aspects II*, web enhancement).
- **The kuo-toa cult:** In the late 15th century DR a faction of the kuo-toa of Sloobludop started a cult after a vision of the demon lord in the Darklake (*Out of the Abyss* 5e).
- **Holy days, omens, messengers, temples:** None in the wiki or the campaign. [research] The wiki's only temple sentence describes the split design above and places no temple in any city.
- **His realm:** The Gaping Maw is a briny sea broken by sharp rocky promontories. His palace, Abysm, is two twin towers shaped like coiled serpents, linked by a bridge near the top, with most of the fortress underwater. Aboleths, kraken and ixitxachitl live in the reefs below and worship him from there (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e; *Stormwrack* 3e). His bog fortress, Ungorth Reddik, houses his armies of hezrous, aboleths and skum, and his capital is Lemoriax on the layer's one large landmass (*Book of Vile Darkness* 3e; *Fiendish Codex I* 3e).
- **Servants and creations:** The balor Belaphoss considered himself the most powerful of his servants (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide* 5e). The wiki credits him with creating chimeras and merrow (*Monster Manual* 5e), ettins (*Monster Manual 2* 4e) and retrievers (*Fiendish Codex I* 3e), and with turning the first death knight, St. Kargoth (*Dragon* 79, edition not given).
- **History of his origin:** Wiki-sourced, and the sources disagree.
  - One account makes him the first tanar'ri, born from the combined fear of mortal souls after the Queen of Chaos defeated the obyrith Obox-ob. He was cast aside as a deformity with boneless arms and twin heads, and he lay hidden while the Queen shaped the other tanar'ri (*Dragon* 357, edition not given).
  - Another account makes him a primordial and the first of his kind to enter the Abyss. A blow from Amoth split his single head in two, and he defeated the stone primordial Storralk and made the ettins from Storralk's blood (*Demonomicon* 4e; *Monster Manual 2* 4e).
  - In both accounts Obox-ob has vowed to murder him and reclaim the title (*Fiendish Codex I* 3e; *Dragon* 357).
  - After the Queen of Chaos fell, Graz'zt and Orcus competed for the title of Prince of Demons. Demogorgon, who had grown stronger in exile, struck throughout the Abyss and slew a dozen demon lords by draining their life forces, and even Graz'zt and Orcus knelt to him (*Demonomicon* 4e; *Dragon* 357).
- **History in the Realms:** Wiki-sourced.
  - Over a thousand years before the Era of Upheaval he was bound in Watcher's Keep in Tethyr. Helm tasked an order of his knights with renewing the seal, and in 1369 DR the knights asked the adventurer Abdel Adrian to perform the ritual in their place. The wiki says only that he later returned to the Abyss (*Baldur's Gate II: Throne of Bhaal*, video game; *Archmage*).
  - On Nightal 15, 1486 DR, the archmage Gromph Baenre weakened the faerzress barriers of Menzoberranzan and inadvertently summoned him to the Prime Material Plane. He wrecked the tower of Sorcere as he left the cavern. Gromph acted on a plan that the wiki says Lolth conveyed to him in a psychic message (*Archmage*, 2015 hardcover).
  - In 1487 DR Drizzt Do'Urden defeated him in Menzoberranzan by acting as a conduit for a barrage of magical energy held by the barrier of an illithid hive-mind (*Maestro*).
- **Relations with other powers:** Wiki-sourced.
  - The hatred between Demogorgon and Orcus was legendary, and he was a dedicated foe of Graz'zt (*Monster Manual 2* 4e; *Monster Manual* 1e). He tried and failed to conquer the realm of Fraz-Urb'luu (*Dragon* 357).
  - He hated the sahuagin god Sekolah and urged his followers to kill sahuagin (*Monster Mythology* 2e). One myth says he was once Sekolah's vassal, forced to obey through the power of an artifact (*Monster Mythology* 2e).
  - He is allied with the obyrith lord Dagon, who supplies the intellect while Demogorgon supplies the brute strength (*Fiendish Codex I* 3e; *Monster Manual 2* 4e). Zuggtmoy visits the Gaping Maw to trade fungi and talk, and he is allied with Ilsidahur, lord of the bar-lgura (*Dragon* 357).
  - The succubi Shami-Amourae and Malcanthet were his lovers, and both manipulated him for their own ends (*Dungeon* 148; *Dragon* 357). The wiki names the demon lord Mandrillagon as his brother (*Dance of Demons*).
  - Some followers of Helm call him the Imprisoned One (*Baldur's Gate II: Throne of Bhaal*, video game). The wiki lists no relation between him and any other power on this page's roster.

## Overview

Demogorgon is the two-headed demon lord who calls himself the Prince of Demons, and he rules the Gaping Maw, a flooded layer of the Abyss. Scholars say he wants to drag everything down into madness and the dark water. His worshipers are mostly creatures of the deep and the Underdark, such as ixitxachitl, kuo-toa and troglodytes, with a scattering of evil humans. Waterdeep has no temple of his, and the few mortals who bargain with him are warlocks.

**Tone:** Dizzying and divided. Demogorgon is two minds in one body, so when a player asks about him, describe the quarrel between his heads and the sea he rules before describing what he offers a warlock.
