> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this page is:** the deity entry for Auril, the Frostmaiden and goddess of winter, for players who want a cold-climate cleric or a devout character with a feared patron, and for a GM who needs a power to stand behind a hard winter.
> - **Where it is used:** Auril never appears in person, and no campaign file names her. The page records the one *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* line that names her, and the one Waterdeep holiday the wiki ties to her, so that no later page invents more.
> - **Featured in:** No quest. Her name appears only on other Deities pages, as one of the Gods of Fury with **Talos**, **Malar** and **Umberlee**.
> - **Spoiler limit:** everything above the `## Overview` may be read by the GM only. The Overview is the common-knowledge tier. Nothing about Auril is secret.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM voice what an ordinary Waterdhavian says about Auril, and says plainly that no source gives her a temple, a shrine or a worshiper in Waterdeep.

*The Frostmaiden*

- **Title:** God of Winter (FRHoF; SCAG and PHB, 2014, print "Goddess of winter"). Every source writes of her as "she".
- **Other Names:** The Cold Crone, Icedawn (FRHoF); Lady Frostkiss (SCAG, 2014); the Cold Goddess, the Queen of Cold and Frost, Lady Icekiss, the Brittle Maiden, the Queen of Frozen Tears, Winter's Womb, Sovereign of Summers Lost, General of Winter's War, the Frost Sprite Queen, Storm-bringer, and Saukuruk among the Iulutiun of the Great Glacier (wiki)
- **Pantheon:** Faerûnian, and one of the Gods of Fury under Talos (FRHoF)
- **Areas of Concern:** Winter, cold, ice, deep winters, the hoarding of beauty (derived from the FRHoF and SCAG text and the wiki portfolio; no entry lists Areas of Concern)
- **Domains:** Nature, Tempest (SCAG, 2014)
- **Symbol:** Six-pointed snowflake (SCAG, 2014)
- **Alignment:** Neutral Evil (SCAG, 2014)
- **Plane:** Pandemonium (Pandesmos) (FRHoF)
- **Typical Followers:** Druids, inhabitants of cold climates (FRHoF)

## Influence and Followers

- **Rank:** Auril is a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The wiki ranks her a lesser deity in its 2e, 3e and 5e rows, a deity in its 4e row and a demipower in its 1e row. Its 2e row adds that she was formerly an archfey. FRHoF says she serves Talos.
- **What she is:** FRHoF calls her the merciless god of cold indifference, who embodies winter's cruelty and whose ice grips all things and preserves them against time. She hoards beauty in every form, from art objects and flowers to skilled artisans, and freezes it in magical ice for her own pleasure (FRHoF). The wiki describes an arrogant and vain power who is incapable of mercy and who traps offenders in blizzards, drives them mad with visions of warmth and then freezes them (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Frostburn*; *Icewind Dale: Rime of the Frostmaiden*, 5e).
- **What the faith asks:** Folk propitiate her with offerings and prayers for mercy. Worshipers stock food, fuel and other comforts and sacrifice them to her for deliverance from a harsh winter. In times of deep desperation, thinking beings may be left in the deadly cold as a sacrifice (FRHoF). Few favor her except those who make their living from winter or truly love the season (SCAG, 2014; FRHoF).
- **The clergy's charge:** The wiki quotes the charge she gives her priests: cover the lands with ice, quench fire, let in the wind and the cold, hide the sun, spare an arctic creature only in great need and make all Faerûn fear her (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
- **Clergy:** Her priests tend to be people who would be outcasts if not for their station. They avoid personal relationships, stay aloof when not serving, and practice celibacy (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014). They warn local residents to prepare for winter and to stock extra provisions so there is something to offer her.
- **Rites in Luskan:** Her temple there is the Winter Palace, a roofless array of pillars and arches of white stone. Visitors gather to watch supplicants put on garments packed with ice and run between six white pillars called the Kisses of Auril, which stand around the city. Each runner climbs a pillar and touches the rusty iron plate at the top. In winter the runs resemble frantic footraces, with frostbite and falls from the slippery pillars as the risks (FRHoF; SCAG, 2014).
- **Wet parades:** SCAG says patrons from the nearby taverns cheer the runners and bet on their stamina. Those who finish are thought to have made the winter easier, and they rarely pay for food or ale all winter long (SCAG, 2014).
- **Chosen in the campaign:** None. No file names a Chosen of Auril. FRHoF says her Chosen are spies or assassins who root out the agents of her enemies, and that the most prominent is the Harper agent Artus Cimber.
- **Messengers:** If she speaks to a mortal, her voice whispers like icy needles on a cold wind, or she sends a yeti or an air elemental to speak for her (FRHoF). The wiki adds frost giants and winter wolves that carry her commands to her cults (*Planes of Chaos*, 2e), and frostwind viragos and winter hags as her handmaidens (*Monster Manual V*; *Dragon* 367, 4e era).
- **Omens:** The wiki lists three manifestations. They are an icy breath with a cold, ruthless chuckle, a blue-white aura that leaves a trail of frost, and a blank-eyed face of frost with hair that gives off cold (*Deity Do's and Don'ts*; *Monster Manual V*). No source gives a formal omen.
- **Holy days:** The wiki gives three (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Midwinter, from Hammer 30 to Alturiak 1, is the most holy night of the year for her clergy and a festival of ice-dancing that lasts all night. Druids also pay her respect on that night (*Legacy of the Crystal Shard*, 5e).
  - The Coming Storm and the Last Storm are informal rituals in which priests gather and call howling ice storms down on a region to mark the start and end of winter.
  - Auril's Blesstide, or Auril's Day, was held in Waterdeep on the first day of new frost, to appease her in hope of a mild winter (*City of Splendors: Waterdeep*, edition not tagged by the wiki).
- **Where she is worshiped:** FRHoF says worship is strongest in regions with deep winters, notably Icewind Dale during the Everlasting Rime. The wiki says she was commonly worshiped and feared across the North, including the Great Glacier, the Icerim Mountains and Vaasa (*Player's Guide to Faerûn*, 3e), and that Illuskan invaders carried her worship to Tethyr (*Races of Faerûn*, 3e). In the Moonsea her followers were shunned, and in winter they recruited openly in Zhentil Keep from the destitute and homeless, who were offered salvation in place of the risk of freezing in an alley (*Mysteries of the Moonsea*; *Ruins of Zhentil Keep*). Members of the Arcane Brotherhood were among her worshipers (*Lords of Darkness*, 3e).
- **Other temples and shrines:** The wiki lists shrines in Bezantur, Calaunt, Iriaebor, Nashkel, Phlan, Tasseldale and Zhentil Keep, and temples in Glister, south of Nashkel, in Calimport, in Luskan and in Icewind Dale. Waterdeep is on neither list.
- **Druids:** The wiki says druids count her among the First Circle of nature deities and honor her despite her harsher nature (*Player's Handbook*, 5e).
- **Auril's Breath:** Sailors of the northern Trackless Sea islands, among them Gundarlun, Ruathym and Tuern, call the bitterly cold northwest winds by that name (*The Savage Frontier*).
- **Relations:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named where the wiki gives one.
  - Talos is allegedly close and cordial with her and still tries to take her following. She prefers not to rely on him, because he answers her calls and then directs the glory to himself (*Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - She and Malar hate each other. Umberlee was the one of the Gods of Fury she cooperated with, and Umberlee's page on the wiki calls Auril her closest ally (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e; *Faiths & Avatars*, 2e).
  - Chauntea was her mortal enemy (*Cyclopedia of the Realms*; *Player's Guide to Faerûn*, 3e). Sune opposed her for destroying so much that was beautiful (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e).
  - Uthgar despised her because the Elk tribe left him for her faith (*Faiths & Pantheons*, 3e). Rellavar Danuvien, a minor elven god of cold, tried to thwart her plans whenever he could (*Dragon* 236).
  - She once had a relationship with Thrym, god of the frost giants, and bore him the empyrean Nalkara (*Waterdeep: Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, 5e).
  - Some fey of the Shiverpine Forest in the Deep Wilds took the Frost Sprite Queen to be the same power as the Queen of Air and Darkness (*Dragon* 367, 4e era). The wiki adds that after the Sundering the Queen was seen to be only impersonating Auril to keep followers on Toril (an Ed Greenwood post, as the wiki cites it).
- **Origin and rise:** Wiki-sourced, with the book or edition named on each fact. The wiki gives no account of how she became a god.
  - After the Spellplague she drew power from the faiths of Ulutiu, Aerdrie Faenya and Gruumsh or Talos, and she claimed wind and storms as well as cold. That put her in direct conflict with Akadi (*Dragon* 367, 4e era). The same source says she served Silvanus after the Stormstar Requiem, where FRHoF has her serving Talos.
  - In 1450 DR she intervened in Calimshan's Second Era of Skyfire, froze the Skyfire Wastes and sent her followers against both Calim's genies and Memnon's efreeti (*Dragon* 367, 4e era).
  - In 1485 DR her Chosen Hedrun Arnsfirth began a war in Icewind Dale (*Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide*, 5e).
  - In the late fifteenth century DR she froze Umberlee's chaotic tides. Umberlee ended their alliance and turned to Talos and Malar, and with their help drove Auril back to the Sea of Moving Ice. From an island called Solstice she raised the Everlasting Rime in 1487 DR, a winter that covered the region and reached Icewind Dale for two years (*Icewind Dale: Rime of the Frostmaiden*, 5e).
  - Sustaining the Rime weakened her to a mortal form. The wiki says she cannot be destroyed for good, because so long as she keeps earthly worshipers she can be reborn at the next winter solstice (*Icewind Dale: Rime of the Frostmaiden*, 5e).
- **In the campaign:** Auril is alive and present in 1492 DR, as every power is. FRHoF describes her in the present tense as a god of the Faerûnian pantheon. The Spellplague accounts are history, and no file says what became of her after the Rime. Draft Notes records both.

> #### Using Auril as a Deity
> - **The ordinary Waterdhavian's view:** Voice her as a power people bargain with and never love. Dragon Heist says Waterdhavian winters are harsh, with snow piled around the city, ice filling the harbor and the gates sealed (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:607`), so a sage, a priest or a regular at the bar knows what she is blamed for. They say she is the Frostmaiden, the goddess of winter, shown as a six-pointed snowflake. They add that few care for her except those who live by the winter or truly love it, that everyone else lays in stores against the cold and leaves her an offering, and that druids honor her among the nature powers. They know Luskan's Winter Palace and its ice-packed runners from the road north. Old-timers may mention Auril's Blesstide, the first day of new frost, when the city once sought a mild winter from her (wiki). How the city feels about her worship is unsourced. [research]
> - **Waterdeep:** Auril has no temple, shrine or priest in the city in any source.
>   - The Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no Auril row (`sources/adventure-wdh.json:30921-30996`). The "All deities" row, Holyhands House in the North Ward (`:30923-30925`), is the only listing that could cover her, and no source says her faithful use it.
>   - The wiki's temple and shrine lists leave Waterdeep out. The nearest temple is the Winter Palace in Luskan (FRHoF).
>   - Dragon Heist names Midwinter among the annual holidays (`:632`) and describes a Deadwinter Day feast that the party may host in Trollskull Alley (`:30383`). Neither names Auril. The campaign calendar lists Midwinter between Hammer 30 and Alturiak 1 (`campaign/setting/history/09-timekeeping-and-the-festival-calendar.md:40`) and gives it no tie to her. The wiki's holy night for her clergy falls on the same day.
>   - The campaign starts on Ches 1, so Midwinter has passed by then, and first frost comes with the cold months, so Auril's Blesstide falls outside the campaign window too (both derived). No campaign file places a priest, shrine or worshiper of Auril in the city.
> - **A cleric PC:** Tempest Domain (derived; Player's Handbook, 2014). Both of her domains in SCAG, 2014 are Nature and Tempest, and no 2024 book has reprinted either, so both are available as printed. Tempest wins because her priests call howling ice storms down on a region in the Coming Storm and the Last Storm. Nature Domain (Player's Handbook, 2014) is the alternative for a cleric of the druids and cold-country folk who honor her. The Divine Order that fits is Thaumaturge (derived), because her priests are aloof, celibate rite-keepers who warn the community and take the offerings. A cleric who worships her openly in Waterdeep finds no temple and no clergy to answer to.
> - **A paladin PC:** Oath of Conquest (derived; Xanathar's Guide to Everything). The wiki quotes her charge to her priests as covering the lands with ice and making all Faerûn fear her, and it calls her the General of Winter's War. That is conquest by fear, which is what the oath is built around. The fit is loose. No source lists paladins among her followers or ties an oath to her, and her neutral evil alignment suits no oath well. Oath of Vengeance (Player's Handbook, 2024) is the alternative, since her Chosen hunt the agents of her enemies.
> - **A warlock PC:** Archfey Patron (derived; Player's Handbook, 2024). The wiki's 2e row says she was formerly an archfey, and some fey of the Deep Wilds took her for the Queen of Air and Darkness (*Dragon* 367, 4e era). The fit is loose, because no source describes a pact with Auril herself. A warlock who wants the Queen as the patron can use **the Queen of Air and Darkness** (Deities).
> - **Campaign ties:** No campaign file uses this power. The published *Dungeon of the Mad Mage* names her once, and both Undermountain items below are seeds for later work.
>   - On Level 23, in area 33, Halaster summons Nalkara the Empyrean from a circle of runes to test intruders. The book calls her "the neutral evil daughter of Auril, the god of winter, and Thrym, the god of the frost giants" (`sources/adventure-wdmm.json:62028`, page 301).

## Overview

Auril, the Frostmaiden, is the goddess of winter and cold, and Waterdhavians picture her with a six-pointed snowflake for a symbol. She is merciless, and her followers are druids and people of the cold lands, who leave her offerings of food and fuel and pray for a mild winter. Her priests warn the folk around them to stock up before the cold comes. Her best-known temple is the Winter Palace in Luskan, and Waterdeep has no temple of hers.

**Tone:** Grave and plain, with no spectacle. Auril is the cold that does not care, so describe the frost and what it preserves before the goddess, and let the careful stocking of food and fuel carry the page. Keep her at a distance, since the campaign writes no scene in which she acts.
