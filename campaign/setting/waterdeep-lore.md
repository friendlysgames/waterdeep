# Waterdeep: Lore

> Structuring draft.

### Lore of Waterdeep

**Purpose:** Gives the GM enough of the city to make it feel inhabited — dense enough for Waterdeep to have friction, history, and personality before the first session.

**Content:**

*The Wards*
- **Castle Ward** (city center, government, Yawning Portal): seat of the Masked Lords; City Watch headquarters; Durnan's Yawning Portal is the campaign's literal and symbolic entry point
- **Sea Ward** (wealthy northwest): noble estates, major temples, the Field of Triumph; Cassalanter Villa is here
- **North Ward** (established families, quieter money): quieter money, old names, established households; Trollskull Manor (the party's home base) is here — operating mechanics in the **Trollskull Manor** guide
- **Trades Ward** (commerce, guilds, day-and-night activity): most guild halls; faction front businesses cluster here; Kolat Towers (Manshoon's fortress) occupies a fortified block here
- **Southern Ward** (working class, immigrants, industry): where new arrivals settle first
- **Dock Ward** (port, crime, contraband): the most dangerous ward; Xanathar's Guild's primary surface territory; Istrid Horn's moneylending operation works out of a warehouse here; the Sea Maidens Faire's carnival fleet moors here from Ches 21 for Fleetswake
- **City of the Dead** (walled necropolis): the Brandath Mausoleum (vault entrance) is here, reached by the approach called the Brandath Crypts; patrolled but not well-staffed
- **Undermountain** (below the city): the Yawning Portal well drops to Level 1; Skullport is three levels down; outside city jurisdiction

*Governance*
- **Open Lord Laeral Silverhand**: one of the Seven Sisters, former Chosen of Mystra; politically formidable; knows more about the Grand Game than she lets on; would prefer to recover the vault quietly if it ever surfaces
- **Masked Lords**: anonymous governing council; their identities are the city's most closely guarded secret; individual Lords may have faction affiliations the PCs never learn
- **The Code Legal**: posted publicly, enforced by the Watch; notable for what it doesn't cover (most guild business) as much as what it does
- **Magisters**: city judges; Barnibus Blastwind handles sensitive investigations
- **The City Watch**: 1,200 guards across three shifts; understaffed, overworked; not corrupt by default but susceptible to political pressure

*Guilds*
- 18 recognized guilds control every significant trade; operating without guild membership invites fines and Watch attention
- Relevant guilds: the Dungsweepers' (connected to the Dock Ward underworld), the League of Skinners and Tanners (Zhentarim material transit cover), the Jewelers' (money laundering vector), the Carpenters' (Cassalanter villa maintenance)
- Most major guilds have at least one faction operative embedded in leadership

*Religion*
- Major temples in Sea Ward: Tymora (Luck; popular with adventurers and criminals alike), Selûne (Moon; widely attended)
- Halls of Justice, the temple of Tyr, in the Castle Ward (Justice; City Watch captain is a worshiper)
- House of Inspired Hands: a Gondar workshop-temple, not a standard place of worship; **Fireball!** connects here
- Hidden: the Cassalanters maintain an illegal temple to Asmodeus beneath their villa; the Code Legal offense alone is capital

*Festival Calendar (GM reference)*
| Date | Festival | Campaign function |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| Ches 21–30 | Fleetswake | Maritime festival; the Faire's carnival fleet arrives in the harbor on Ches 21; cover for Dock Ward investigation |
| Tarsakh 1–10 | Waukeentide | Commerce festival; Cassalanters host public galas; cover for Sea Ward investigation |
| Flamerule 1 | Founders' Day | HARD DEADLINE — Cassalanter sacrifice; city celebrates publicly |
| Marpenoth 3 | Day of Wonders | Gondar invention festival; nimblewright demonstrations; adjustable for **Fireball!** pacing |

*The Dragonward and Walking Statues*
- The dragonward: Ahghairon's founding spell; prevents dragons from entering the city without the Open Lord's permission
- Eight Walking Statues: 90-foot animated stone constructs, standing at eight points around the city, all dormant except the Griffon, which woke in 1479 DR; a civic reference point, not a player resource

**Tone:** Dense, specific, practical. Every fact should imply a consequence. The dragonward matters because Jarlaxle moves by sea. Guild membership matters because Trollskull Manor needs permits. The Watch matters because faction violence eventually reaches it.
