# Timekeeping and the Festival Calendar

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this is:** The reference page for how Waterdeep counts days and which dates the campaign runs on. It holds the Calendar of Harptos basics, the campaign calendar from Ches 1 to Flamerule 11, the festival table with its campaign functions, and the festival customs the GM can use as cover.
> - **Where it is used:** The GM opens it at session zero to fix the start date, and again whenever the party asks what day it is, what is being celebrated, or how long remains before a deadline. It is the single home of Founders' Day.
> - **Featured in:** **Finding Floon** (Ches 1), **Trollskull Alley** (Ches 20 and 21), **Fireball!** (Ches 22), **Gralhund Villa** (Ches 24), **Faction Outposts**, **Cassalanter Villa**, **Sea Maidens Faire** (Tarsakh 20), **Vault of Dragons**.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM answer any date question without leaving the page, and shows which holidays give the party cover, which put pressure on it, and which fall outside the campaign window.

**Keywords:** Calendar of Harptos, tenday, Fleetswake, Waukeentide, Founders' Day, Day of Wonders, Shieldmeet, Dale Reckoning

> - The year has twelve months of thirty days, each in three tendays, and five festival days that belong to no month, which makes 365 days. A leap day, Shieldmeet, follows Midsummer every fourth year.
> - The campaign opens on Ches 1, 1492 DR and runs to Flamerule 11, about four and a half months. Four dates inside it are structure, not flavor: Fleetswake, Waukeentide, the Tarsakh 20 sailing and Founders' Day.

### The Calendar of Harptos

- **The tenday.** A month is three tendays of ten days. Tendays have no formal day names, so a date is given by month and day number ("Flamerule 4"). Waterdhavians use "tenday" where other worlds say "week".
- **Festival days.** Five days fall between months and belong to neither. They are Midwinter, Greengrass, Midsummer, Highharvestide and the Feast of the Moon.
- **Shieldmeet.** Once every four years a leap day follows Midsummer and comes before Eleasis 1. Fairs, bazaars and tournaments are held, nobles hear petitions in open court, and trade pacts and alliances are made or renewed.
- **Edition notes.** The month epithets below come from the third-edition Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting (pp. 76-77), as the Forgotten Realms Wiki reports them. Later editions dropped the leading "the" from the Ches and Tarsakh epithets. The published adventure spells the fourth month "Tarsahk" in places. The campaign spells it Tarsakh.
- **Dating.** Years are Dale Reckoning. The published adventure names no year name for 1492 DR; a wiki-only year name sits in Draft Notes and is not used.

- **Tarsakh 20** closes the Sea Maidens Faire heist and alliance paths. Jarlaxle's ships leave on schedule and no delay is possible.
- **Flamerule 1** is the sacrifice window. The party must stop the feast to prevent the poisoning, and arriving after the dinner ends changes nothing.
- **Flamerule 11** comes ten days later. A party that destroyed the contract during **Cassalanter Villa** does not face it. A party that did not has the ten days after Founders' Day to find another resolution.
- **Founders' Day versus the birthday.** The feast is the deadline the party must beat. The birthday is the date on which the contract comes due. Do not tell the players either meaning. Calendar awareness is a player resource, and the connection to the Cassalanters is a discovery they earn.

### Founders' Day

- **Date and meaning.** Flamerule 1 commemorates the birth of the city.
- **Customs.** The Field of Triumph hosts illusory displays that chronicle Waterdeep's history, and the City Guard gives martial exhibitions. Festhalls hold costume contests for the best recreation of a historical figure. Several mages veil Castle Waterdeep as Nimoar's ancient log fortress from midday to sunset. The veiling was once banned as frivolous and has been reinstated.
- **The feast.** Ammalia Cassalanter invites one hundred of Waterdeep's poorest to a charity dinner on the villa's lawn pavilions through her Field Ward charity. The guests are served ninety-nine cups of wine with midnight tears, and they die within 24 hours, scattered across the city. In the campaign the feast begins at noon.
- **Why that date.** The civic holiday makes an invitation to the poor unremarkable, Ammalia's charity makes the feast plausible, and ten days before the birthday leaves time to verify that the contract's terms are met.
- **The forfeit.** The contract falls due on Flamerule 11, the twins' ninth birthday and ten days after Founders' Day. The soul half of the buy-out fails if the feast is stopped, whatever happens with the gold. If the feast proceeds, ninety-nine people are dead whether or not the party later destroys the contract.
- **The ten days between.** Sornyn (Flamerule 3 to 5) and Lliira's Night (Flamerule 7) fall inside them. See the hooks below.
- **At the table.** The Trollskull Manor guide gives the tavern's revenue and event rules for the day.

### Festival Hooks

- **Fey Day, Ches 19.** Masked balls run all evening, so a masked guest at any noble house draws no remark. The Grand Opening of Trollskull Manor follows the next day.
- **Fair Seas Festival, Ches 29 and 30.** Guards collect offerings for Umberlee from tavern to tavern. The chests are dumped in the harbor at sunset on Ches 30, into Umberlee's Cache, which merfolk guardians watch under orders to kill anyone who disturbs it.
- **Waukeentide.** Goldenight and Guildsmeet put crowds in the Cynosure and the Field of Triumph, and the Cassalanters host their own events. Any surveillance of the villa during these days has cover.
- **Dragondown, Kythorn 20.** Dragon effigies burn across the city on a date near the Aurinax thread.
- **Sornyn and Lliira's Night, Flamerule 3 to 7.** Envoys and strangers are expected in the city and smokepowder fireworks boom until dawn on Flamerule 7, so unfamiliar faces and loud noises draw no notice in the ten days between the feast and the birthday.
- **Shieldmeet, 1492 DR.** The extra day falls after Flamerule 30. Nobles hold open court and renew agreements, which fits the Mad Mage bridge if the party is still in the city.

**Tone:** Practical and brisk, in the Ember reference register. Give every date its use, and state the deadline rule once, flatly, so the GM never has to reconcile two readings.

## Overview

Waterdeep keeps the Calendar of Harptos, in which twelve months of thirty days, each divided into three tendays, are joined by five festival days that belong to no month and by a leap day, Shieldmeet, every fourth year. The city fills its calendar with public holidays. Fleetswake honors the sea in the last tenday of Ches, Waukeentide fills the first tenday of Tarsakh with trade and galas, and on Founders' Day, the first of Flamerule, the Field of Triumph stages illusory histories of the city while Castle Waterdeep is veiled as the log fortress of Nimoar. Guilds, temples and the Lords of Waterdeep all use the holidays to meet, renew charters and make agreements.
