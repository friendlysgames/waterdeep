# Ahghairon's Gifts

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - **What this is:** The Era page for the founding of Waterdeep's government, the oldest layer of the campaign's backstory. It is the first of eight eras, before **The Long Shadow**.
> - **Where it is used:** The GM reads it before answering any question about why the city has an Open Lord and Masked Lords, what the dragonward is, who lies in the tower in the Palace courtyard, or why Ahghairon's name turns up in so many places.
> - **Featured in:** **Gralhund Villa** (Istrid Horn's meeting at Ahghairon's Statue, and Yalah's bid for a Masked Lord's seat), **Kolat Towers** (the hold Manshoon's cell has over two Masked Lords), **Vault of Dragons** (the dragonstaff of Ahghairon), **An Audience with the Open Lord** and **Lioncrown** (Laeral on the Masked Lords).
> - **Spoiler level:** Everything above the Overview is GM-only. The Overview is the public history that any Waterdhavian learns as a child and mentions no vault, no Stone and no blackmail.

**Purpose:** Lets the GM explain where Waterdeep's laws, rulers and protections came from, and gives the campaign's closing question, whether the system holds, a place to start.

**Keywords:** Ahghairon, Raurlor, Open Lord, Masked Lords, throne, dragonward, Code Legal, magisters, Griffon Cavalry, guilds, City Watch, Ahghairon's Day

**Span:** 1032 DR to 1256 DR, about two and a quarter centuries. The published adventure dates the start to 1032 DR, when Ahghairon defied the warlord Raurlor, and says only that he ruled "over two centuries." The end year, 1256 DR, is Ahghairon's death in the Forgotten Realms Wiki and in no campaign file. The era ends about 236 years before the campaign opens on Ches 1, 1492 DR, and the 460 years from 1032 DR to the campaign are arithmetic that no source states.

**Other Names:** The Lords' Rule Begins. This is the title of the Age Two chapter of *A History of Waterdeep*, the in-world book that the published adventure cites for the events of 1032 DR. "Ahghairon's Gifts" is the campaign's own name for the era. `[research]`

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Lore Summary
> - Ahghairon was a wizard who rose to fame in the troll wars. In 1032 DR he defied Warlord Raurlor, who wanted a northern empire, and ended the warlord's rule.
> - From Raurlor's throne he set up the system Waterdeep still uses. One lord sits in public as the Open Lord, and a council of near-equal lords rules beside him, hidden even from one another.
> - He helped found the city's magisters, the Griffon Cavalry and the guilds, and the Forgotten Realms Wiki adds the wards, the Watch and the dragonward to that list.
> - He ruled for more than two centuries, until the magic that sustained him failed. He lies in his tower in the Palace courtyard behind an invisible force cage.
> - The campaign reads all of this as one design: Ahghairon built a government meant to survive bad rulers, and the four villain factions are the test of it. This closing idea is campaign framing and is not stated by any source. `[research]`

### The Founder

- Waterdeep was settled long before Ahghairon. The elves of Illefarn held the site as the city of Aelinthaldaar, and the dwarves of Clan Melairkyn mined mithral under Mount Waterdeep.
- Halaster Blackcloak built his tower at the foot of the mountain and ruled the lands around it until he vanished into the depths below, as the Melairkyn had.
- Later warlords claimed the harbor. Nimoar raised a wooden stockade around the settlement, which was then called Nimoar's Hold, the Town of Waters Deep.
- Ahghairon first made his name as a young man, when his magic turned the war against the trolls that had been clawing at the town. He lived on in his prime for decades, either from a store of potions of longevity or from learning to make them.
- The campaign calls Ahghairon the true founder of Waterdeep: the founder of its first system of rule, though not of its first settlement. The old outline states this, and the published adventure does not, because the adventure describes earlier settlements.
- Founders' Day commemorates the birth of the city, and mages veil Castle Waterdeep that day as Nimoar's log fortress. Ahghairon has a separate holiday, Ahghairon's Day, on Eleasis 1. The dates and rituals belong to **Timekeeping and the Festival Calendar**.

### The Warlord and the Throne

- In 1032 DR, in his 112th winter, Ahghairon argued before all the people against Warlord Raurlor. Raurlor wanted to turn Waterdeep's wealth and arms into a northern empire.
- Raurlor ordered Ahghairon chained. Ahghairon turned aside everyone who tried to touch him, and when Raurlor struck at him with his own sword, Ahghairon rose out of reach and changed the blade into a serpent. The serpent killed Raurlor in front of his followers.
- Ahghairon then gathered the leaders of the armies and the powerful families. He had the flames in the empty warlord's throne roar until no one could sit there, and when the gathering met in the audience chamber he sat down on it. The fire died and left both the throne and the wizard unharmed.
- That is the same throne the Open Lord uses today. It stands in the audience chamber of the Palace of Waterdeep.

### The Open Lord and the Masked Lords

- From the throne Ahghairon decreed that he would sit openly as lord while a council of other lords of nearly equal power ruled with him. He did not rule alone.
- The identities of the other lords were hidden even from each other, so that none of them could be approached with a bribe or a threat. These are the Masked Lords.
- The Open Lord is the only member of the government the public can name. The Commander of the Watch reports to the Open Lord.
- The Forgotten Realms Wiki gives the Open Lord reserved powers: choosing and vetting new Lords, naming the next Open Lord, and fixing how many Lords sit. No campaign file or published-adventure line states them. `[research]`
- The old outline described the Open Lord as designed to be removed if they fail the city. No source says so. The only removal on record is Dagult Neverember, whom the other Lords voted out, and that belongs to **The Open Lord's Fall**.
- A magister's ruling can be overturned by any Masked Lord, though the published adventure notes that one is rarely present when a petitioner needs it.

### The Institutions

- The published adventure says Ahghairon was instrumental in founding the black-robed magisters, the Griffon Cavalry and the guilds. The wiki dates the Griffon Cavalry to the siege of 1235 DR, when he flew food into the city on griffons, and the guilds to 1248 DR, when he ordered them as a defense against growing crime.
- The wiki says the city's ward system began in 1035 DR with four wards: Castle, Trades, Dock and the Southern Ward, which it calls the Temple Ward. `[research]`
- The wiki says Ahghairon restructured Raurlor's army and navy into the City Watch and the City Guard. The published adventure keeps both services and credits him with neither. `[research]`
- Waterdeep's law is a body of custom and precedent whose main text is the Code Legal. Copies are kept at the Palace, and magisters have broad discretion in sentencing. No source says who wrote it or when. `[research]`
- The old outline called the dragonward, the Code Legal and the City Watch protections built by someone who had seen what tyranny does. The only supported link is that Raurlor planned an empire and Ahghairon stopped him. The rest is campaign framing. `[research]`

### The Dragonward

- The dragonward is a permanent and undispellable effect that covers the city. It begins beneath Ahghairon's Tower and keeps dragons from entering Waterdeep without the Open Lord's permission.
- The wiki places the ward in 1071 DR, after the adventurer Ranressa Shiard came back to Mount Waterdeep riding the copper wyrm Galadaeros, and Ahghairon laid a mythal over the city in response.
- The ward does not cover the harbor or Undermountain. The campaign's old note says the dragonward matters because Jarlaxle moves by sea.
- The dragonstaff of Ahghairon is the one known object that lets a dragon ignore the ward. Aurinax holds it.

### The Tomb and the Test

- Ahghairon ruled wisely for over two centuries, until the magic that kept him alive failed. The wiki puts his death in 1256 DR.
- He lies entombed in his tower in the Palace courtyard. An invisible force cage surrounds it, and additional wards lie inside the cage. The bones of the last person who tried to defy them still hang in the air outside.
- The Griffon, the eighth walking statue, rose from the Ethereal Plane in 1479 DR to defend the tower and later settled near Peaktop Aerie.
- Waterdhavians honor him on Ahghairon's Day. The Lords toast him and the Watchful Order, guildmasters toast the Lords in his name, commoners leave violets, and the Open Lord tours the taverns. His marble statue stands in the City of the Dead.
- The system failed once, after his death. The wiki records the Guildwars of 1256 DR to 1262 DR and the Lords Magister who followed, until Baeron and Shilarn restored the Lords in 1273 DR.
- The campaign's closing idea for this era is that Ahghairon built Waterdeep to survive bad rulers, and the Grand Game is the test of whether he was right. `[research]`

## Overview

Ahghairon was the wizard who founded Waterdeep's government and served as its first Open Lord. In 1032 DR he defied the warlord Raurlor before the whole city, turned the warlord's own sword into a serpent that killed him, and took the warlord's throne, which the Open Lord still uses. He ruled openly beside a council of Masked Lords whose names were hidden even from one another, and he helped establish the city's magisters, its Griffon Cavalry and its guilds. Ahghairon ruled for more than two centuries and now lies in his tower in the Palace courtyard, behind an invisible barrier, and each year on Ahghairon's Day the people of Waterdeep leave violets around it.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - The Open Lord still sits on Raurlor's burned throne. The Masked Lords' anonymity protects them from bribes offered by strangers, and it gives no protection against blackmail by anyone who has learned their names.
> - Manshoon's cell holds Masked Lords Jelenn Urmbrusk and Corylus Thann through blackmail. House Gralhund joined the Zhentarim in order to learn the identities and weaknesses of the Masked Lords, and Yalah Gralhund wants a seat for herself. See **Manshoon**, **Jelenn Urmbrusk** and **Yalah Gralhund**.
> - The Masked Lords are hidden from each other, but the Open Lord is the one who vets and names them, so the Open Lord can know them all. That is how Laeral can say in **Lioncrown** that she knows them. The reserved powers are wiki-only, so this reading is a campaign ruling. `[research]`
> - Mirt is the least careful of the Masked Lords about hiding who he is, and he wears the Lord's ensemble only to official meetings with the others.
> - The dragonward is a tradable object. The dragonstaff of Ahghairon passed from the archmage Maaril to Aurinax before the Spellplague, and Jarlaxle wants it as leverage over Waterdeep. See **Aurinax** and **Jarlaxle Baenre**.
> - Ahghairon's tomb is the dragonward's source, and the Griffon statue rose in 1479 DR to defend the tower against intrusion.
> - Halaster's Hold was sealed by Ahghairon in 1007 DR, according to the wiki, and Laeral calls Halaster the oldest danger under the city. Undermountain is outside the dragonward.

**Tone:** Grave and admiring, in the Ember history register. Ahghairon is a founder worth honoring and a design worth testing. Treat both as facts and give the GM no verdict. The Overview stays on what an ordinary Waterdhavian knows, and the GM Notes name the weak points.
