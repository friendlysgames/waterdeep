# Arc H — Sea Maidens Faire

*An adventure for 4th- to 6th-level characters.*

---

## Arc Opener

Jarlaxle Baenre arrived in Waterdeep during the last tenday of Ches, when the Fleetswake festival filled the harbor with color and noise and the city looked the other way at almost anything. Three ships: the Heartbreaker and Hellraiser, galleons painted crimson and gold that docked on the Mistshore pier, their hulls flanking a festive walkway strung with lanterns; and the Eyecatcher, the flagship, anchored a quarter-mile out in Deepwater Harbor where it glittered in the morning light and refused to be docked like ordinary ships. Between the Heartbreaker and the Hellraiser, the Sea Maidens Faire drew crowds — fire-eaters, acrobats, fortune-tellers, caged beasts, nimblewrights performing as carnival attractions. It was an excellent show. It was designed to be.

Attached to the Eyecatcher's keel, hidden beneath twenty feet of harbor water, the Scarlet Marpenoth had been running silent since they arrived. The submarine was gnome-engineered, cramped, and extraordinarily well-maintained — crewed by three gnome engineers who treated it with the devotion of master artificers and the paranoia of men who knew exactly what the vessel contained. Jarlaxle used it as his command center, his vault, and his escape plan. He slept in the submarine. The Eye of the Stone of Golorr sat in a velvet-lined compartment built into the base of the crystal ball pedestal that controlled his nimblewright surveillance network across Waterdeep. He had placed it there deliberately — close to the thing he used most, in a compartment invisible to anyone who didn't know to look. Jarlaxle was patient. The Eye would keep.

The nimblewright network was Jarlaxle's primary intelligence asset in Waterdeep. Nine nimblewrights sold to nine wealthy households at 25,000 gold pieces each — a steal, and deliberately so. Each nimblewright contained a paired clairvoyance crystal feeding recorded surveillance to the master crystal ball in U4. The network gave him eyes inside the homes of nobles, guild leaders, and civic officials. He knew about affairs and debts and crimes and alliances that their participants assumed were secret. He intended to use this knowledge to build the political case for Luskan's admission to the Lords' Alliance — the campaign that was, underneath every disguise, the reason he had come to Waterdeep at all. Five hundred thousand stolen dragons, recovered from the Vault of Dragons and deployed as a diplomatic investment, would help. The network would help. Having the Stone of Golorr in cooperative hands would help most of all.

The Eye had arrived through Bregan D'aerthe intelligence work — the same network that had placed Nar'l Xibrindas inside Xanathar's Guild eleven years ago. Jarlaxle understood the Eye's significance better than any other faction in the Grand Game, which is why he was not rushing. He would watch. He would learn who else was moving on the Stone. If the party pursuing the Stone proved competent, he would engage them on his own terms. If they did not, he would take the Eye himself when the moment was right.

What he had not accounted for was Zelifarn.

The young bronze dragon had taken up residence near an old elvish wreck in Deepwater Harbor three weeks after the Sea Maidens Faire arrived. Zelifarn was twenty-two years old, enthusiastic about ships, curious about everything, and growing suspicious that the Eyecatcher had a second hull. His mother Asphosis had been killed four years earlier by agents whose employers Zelifarn had not yet identified. Her crystal ball — a family heirloom, a masterwork scrying device — had disappeared with her hoard. Zelifarn had no way to know that the crystal ball now sat in U4 controlling nine clairvoyance crystals across Waterdeep. He only knew that something about the Eyecatcher's underside was wrong, and that it scraped against the grief he had been carrying for four years without a name.

## Scene 1: The Lair Intelligence Briefing

**Purpose.** Translate Arc E outpost intelligence and prior Jarlaxle interactions into actionable fleet knowledge. Each faction contact provides ship-specific intelligence calibrated to what the party already discovered. Same format as Arc F Scene 1 and Arc G Scene 1: a montage of faction consultations, each adding a piece to the picture.

**Content.**

*Running This Scene.* Each contact below is available only to party members of that faction. A party with no Harper has no channel to Mirt; a party with no Force Grey member has no Vajra briefing. The scene's intelligence yield depends entirely on which factions the party joined during Arcs B and C — a party that spread its memberships widely arrives knowing a great deal; a party that clustered into one or two factions arrives with significant blind spots. This is by design.

Unlike Arc F (where the briefing confirms a known dungeon) or Arc G (where the briefing describes a villa that looks normal from outside), this briefing reveals that the target is itself a deception. Every contact is pointing at the same fleet, but none of them fully understand what it is. The party should leave this scene feeling that they are casing something that does not want to be cased — a carnival that is also a military installation, operated by people who have not been found out by any of them.

*Mirt (Harpers — renown 1+).* Mirt knows the Sea Maidens Faire is not what it appears. Harper agents have observed that crew members on the Eyecatcher avoid direct sunlight and that the carnival's operating hours do not match a legitimate entertainment business. If BD outposts were hit in Arc E, Mirt confirms the drow connection: "The carnival is a front. The crew are drow in disguise. The question is what the front is covering." He does not know about the Scarlet Marpenoth unless the party reported Zelifarn's observation through Force Grey. If they did, his assessment sharpens: "An underwater vessel attached to a carnival ship in Waterdeep's harbor. That is not entertainment infrastructure." Mirt can provide harbor patrol schedules and identify which Watch officers have been paid to look away from the Faire's private docking arrangements. At renown 10+, Mirt can activate a Harper informant embedded in the Faire's carnival staff for one specific intelligence request — crew shift rotation, Jarlaxle's schedule for a particular date, or the location of a keyed area not visible from the pier (DC 13 Charisma check; failure means the informant is unavailable for two weeks).

*Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance — renown 1+).* The Sea Maidens Faire uses private docking arrangements that circumvent standard Watch inspection protocols — Jalester flagged this irregularity in Arc C. He can provide the Faire's official docking permits, which list crew numbers that do not match observed headcounts. If BD outposts were hit, Jalester can arrange harbor patrols to be "elsewhere" during a specific window. He also notes that the Council of Musicians filed an objection to the Faire as a public entertainment, resolved through the Jesters' Guild — the Faire has legal protection that makes Watch intervention politically difficult. If the alliance path is available (party has faction contacts willing to sponsor Luskan's Alliance bid), Jalester is the most relevant contact: any political commitment regarding Luskan runs through the Alliance council, and he will want to be briefed before the meeting with Jarlaxle. At renown 10+, Jalester can arrange a formal harbor inspection by Watch authority — a 90-minute window when three Faire crew members must answer questions aboard the Heartbreaker, reducing the Eyecatcher's available response roster. Requires 24 hours' notice; usable once.

*Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave — renown 1+).* Unusual aquatic behavior patterns near the Eyecatcher's anchorage — fish avoid the area beneath the hull, water temperature fluctuations consistent with mechanical discharge from the submarine's engines. Melannor cannot identify the cause but confirms that something unnatural is operating beneath that ship. If the party has met Zelifarn through Force Grey Mission 2, Melannor can coordinate with the dragon — Zelifarn's underwater observations complement Melannor's surface readings, and Melannor will advocate for including Zelifarn as an ally rather than a curiosity. At renown 10+, Jeryth Phaulkon casts water breathing on the entire party before the harbor approach — 24 hours, no concentration required — making the underwater transit to the Eyecatcher's hull viable regardless of Athletics proficiency.

*Savra Belabranta (Order of the Gauntlet — renown 1+).* The Gauntlet has tracked Soluun Xibrindas's murders — elven and half-elven victims found dead in the Dock Ward after Seven Masks Theater performances. Savra identifies the pattern: the killer strikes on performance nights and the bodies are found near the harbor. She does not know Soluun is a drow or that he is connected to the Faire, but the geographic correlation is clear. If the party connects Soluun to the Eyecatcher, Savra frames boarding the ship as a law enforcement obligation: "There is a killer aboard that vessel. The Watch cannot touch it because of the docking permits. We can." She provides a Gauntlet contact in the Dock Ward willing to serve as a distraction or lookout. At renown 10+, Savra's contact conducts a formal crew interview regarding the elf murders — three to five Eyecatcher crew members are occupied for 90 minutes at the Dock Ward watchpost, reducing the ship's response roster during that window.

*Vajra Safahr (Force Grey — renown 1+).* Arcane assessment of the fleet. Vajra detects transmutation and illusion magic saturating the Eyecatcher — consistent with sustained disguise effects on multiple humanoids. She identifies the nimblewright crystal ball's signature as a persistent clairvoyance network: "Someone on that ship is watching nine locations in this city simultaneously through paired crystals." If the party completed Force Grey Mission 2 (Zelifarn and the Harbor), Vajra has already filed the submarine observation in her threat assessment. She now provides the critical operational detail: the Scarlet Marpenoth detaches from the Eyecatcher's hull via a keel collar mechanism. If Jarlaxle decides to flee, the submarine separates and submerges independently. "If your target can leave underwater, you need a plan for that." At renown 10+, Vajra stations a Blackstaff mage in a harbor skiff with a communication bead matched to one in the party's possession; once per contact, the mage casts silence or fog cloud at range, provided the party is within 200 feet of the harbor entrance.

*Tashlyn Yafeera or Davil Starsong (Doom Raiders — renown 1+).* The Doom Raiders have had dealings with Jarlaxle's operation — he approached them early in the campaign about intelligence sharing, which they declined. Tashlyn or Davil can provide the Faire's supply schedule: food deliveries from the Butchers' Guild on Ches 22, 26, and Tarsahk 2, 6; water cask deliveries from the Watermen's Guild every three days; exotic animal feed from a private supplier in the Field Ward. Each delivery requires a crew of 4–6 and takes 45 minutes. Delivery crews have unescorted access to the cargo holds. The Doom Raiders also note that the Faire's revenue does not match its operating costs: "They're spending more than they're earning. The carnival is subsidized by something else." At renown 10+, Yagra Stonefist accompanies the supply delivery infiltration — she knows enough Guild cant to bluff past a cargo inspection and can carry concealed equipment for the party without triggering search protocols.

*Jarlaxle (Bregan D'aerthe — renown 1+).* Available only if a PC is a BD member. Jarlaxle contacts them directly through the existing relationship. His pitch is straightforward: he knows the party has the Stone of Golorr, and possibly an Eye or two already restored. He wants the hoard — not for himself, but for Luskan. "Five hundred thousand dragons buys a seat on the Lords' Alliance council that Luskan cannot earn by reputation alone. Let me tell you why that matters, and what I can offer in return." He lays out Luskan's Alliance bid plainly. He does not threaten. He frames cooperation as mutual benefit. How much he reveals beyond the pitch depends on trust: at Operative rank (Renown 10+), Jarlaxle deals plainly and may offer Eye #3 outright as a show of good faith. At Soldier or Initiate rank, he wraps the same agenda in the Zardoz Betrayal Pitch — a competence and loyalty test before he shows his full hand (see Scene 3). If no PC is a BD member, Jarlaxle is not available as a briefing contact.

**Tone.** Convergent intelligence with a note of uncertainty. Unlike Arc F (where the target is a known dungeon) or Arc G (where the target looks normal from outside), this briefing reveals that the target is a deception — and the party must decide how deep the deception runs before they can plan against it. Every contact is pointing at the same fleet, but none of them fully understand what it is.

## Scene 2: Casing the Target

**Purpose.** Steps 1–3 of the heist framework applied to the Sea Maidens Faire. The caper genre means casing happens through attendance at the carnival, harbor observation, and social interaction with the crew — not through tunnel scouting or attending formal events.

**Content.**

*Identifying the Score.* Eye #3 is aboard the Scarlet Marpenoth, in Jarlaxle's stateroom (U4), sitting in a velvet-lined compartment built into the base of the crystal ball pedestal. The Stone's impression (if 1+ Eyes restored) sharpens to: "a ship in the harbor, the Scarlet Marpenoth, a bald elf who has been patient for eleven years." Arc E outpost documents — Fenerus Stormcastle's decoded messages confirming intelligence flows to the Sea Maidens Faire, Malcolm Brizzenbright's ghost testimony that "Rongquan Mystere" met with drow who mentioned the Faire — converge on the fleet. Arc F documents — the Report on Seven Masks Theater from X35 — provide a third confirmation path.

*Three Entry Methods.* Each is seeded by prior arcs. All three are simultaneously available. A party may use more than one.

**Carnival Cover (via Arc C Scene 4).** The pier carnival between Heartbreaker and Hellraiser is open to the public during operating hours. The party walks in as carnival-goers, mingles with crowds, observes crew patterns, and identifies the gangway to the Eyecatcher's harbor skiff. Advantage: completely legitimate presence during carnival hours; no checks required to enter the pier area; ability to case the docked ships' exteriors and observe crew rotations over multiple visits. Disadvantage: the Eyecatcher is anchored a quarter-mile out — the party must still cross open water to reach it. Carnival cover ends at closing time. Nimblewrights on the pier may recognize party members who triggered the nimblewright detector in Arc C.

**Harbor Approach (via Force Grey Mission 2 / Zelifarn).** If the party befriended Zelifarn in the Force Grey mission, the dragon provides underwater transport to the Eyecatcher's hull. Zelifarn knows the Scarlet Marpenoth's docking position, its detachment schedule ("it comes out at night — the hatch opens from below"), and the hull's external access points. Advantage: bypasses the pier entirely; arrives at the Eyecatcher underwater; Zelifarn can create diversions and provide an underwater extraction route. Disadvantage: the party arrives wet and must waterproof equipment; Zelifarn is conspicuous near the Eyecatcher; the approach is only viable at night or in poor visibility.

**Supply Delivery Infiltration (via Doom Raiders / guild contacts).** Guild delivery schedules from the Doom Raiders provide windows when cargo is being loaded onto the docked ships. The party disguises as delivery crew — Butchers' Guild, Watermen's Guild, or the exotic animal feed supplier. Advantage: access to cargo holds of the Heartbreaker and Hellraiser without boarding checks; delivery carts provide concealment for equipment; 45-minute window of unescorted access. Disadvantage: does not reach the Eyecatcher directly (deliveries go to the docked ships only; Eyecatcher supplies are ferried by skiff); cover story fails if the real delivery crew arrives simultaneously.

*The Fleet Layout.*

**Pier Carnival (Heartbreaker + Hellraiser, docked).** Public area during operating hours. Carnival stalls, performers, and crowds fill the pier between the two galleons. Nimblewrights serve as carnival attractions — mechanical dancers, fortune-telling automata. Below-deck areas are restricted. The pier is the staging area: the party transitions from public space to restricted space here.

**Eyecatcher (anchored in deep harbor).** The flagship. A quarter-mile from the pier, reachable by harbor skiff (departing hourly from the pier, 2 sp), swimming (DC 12 Athletics, cold water, awkward current), or Zelifarn's underwater transport. Boarding from a skiff requires a credible reason (DC 14 Deception) or an invitation from Zord. Boarding clandestinely at night: DC 14 Stealth to reach the hull, DC 16 to board unseen. The Eyecatcher contains Jarlaxle's public quarters (J30), crew quarters, the nimblewright storage (J18), and — critically — the trapdoor in J30 that accesses the Scarlet Marpenoth beneath.

**Scarlet Marpenoth (submarine, attached beneath Eyecatcher).** Two access points: the trapdoor in J30 (Zardoz Zord's cabin), which requires reaching the Eyecatcher's aft deck and entering the locked cabin; and the external hull access, reachable underwater from the Eyecatcher's keel — a hatch visible from beneath (DC 14 Perception from underwater, or Zelifarn's guidance). The submarine is the score's location. U4 (Jarlaxle's stateroom) holds the crystal ball on its pedestal, with Eye #3 in the compartment at the pedestal's base.

*Festival and Event Windows.*

**Carnival Parades (5 dates: Ches 10, 21, 25, Tarsahk 1, 5).** During parades, the Faire's performers march through Waterdeep's streets. The pier is nearly empty. The docked ships retain skeleton crews. The Eyecatcher's crew is reduced to watch personnel only. Best pure-heist window: fewest adversaries, maximum time before absence is noticed. Duration: 3–4 hours per parade.

**Shipwright's Ball (Ches 25).** Jarlaxle attends the Ball in disguise — as J.B. Nevercott or Rongquan Mystere. The Eyecatcher is reduced to skeleton crew with no captain aboard. Social cover for surveillance: half the party can attend the Ball while the other half boards the Eyecatcher. Duration: full evening (6 hours). This is the best diplomatic window — Jarlaxle is accessible in a social setting where business is expected.

**Tarsahk 20 — Sea Maidens Faire Departure.** The hard deadline. If the party has not acted by this date, the ships leave Waterdeep. Arc H becomes a pursuit-at-sea scenario or a negotiation from extreme weakness. At Lockdown tier (both BD outposts hit plus spillover), the departure accelerates to Tarsahk 15.

*Crew Observation.* The Eyecatcher's defining casing challenge. The crew are drow in human disguise — sustained transmutation effects, hats of disguise, careful discipline about behavior in sunlight. A party briefed by Mirt (crew avoids sunlight) or Vajra (illusion magic saturating the ship) can identify the disguises with a DC 15 Intelligence (Arcana) check or a DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check if they know what to look for. Dispel magic on any crew member reveals a drow. This knowledge changes the stakes: the party is not robbing a carnival; they are robbing a drow intelligence operation.

*Nimblewrights as Sentries.* The Eyecatcher carries deactivated nimblewrights in J18 (8 units) and active nimblewrights as valets in J10 and J30. Active nimblewrights respond to intruders with combat programming. The party's nimblewright detector from Arc C overwhelms near the fleet — useful for confirming the connection but not for locating specific units.

**Tone.** Reconnaissance with a carnival veneer. The party is casing a target that looks like fun — bright colors, music, crowds, exotic spectacles. Beneath the surface, the same methodical intelligence-gathering as Arc F and Arc G, but dressed in greasepaint. The contrast between the carnival atmosphere and the covert military operation underneath is the scene's defining texture.

## Scene 3: Preparation

**Purpose.** Step 4 of the heist framework. The party finalizes their plan, acquires tools, navigates the moral dimension (Zelifarn's crystal ball), and encounters the three resolution paths. This scene also presents the Zardoz Betrayal Pitch if conditions are met.

**Content.**

*The Three Resolution Paths.* Arc H's defining structural choice. The party may pursue any path, in any order. The text presents all three without advocacy.

**Path 1: Heist Against Jarlaxle (standard).** The party treats Jarlaxle as an adversary. They infiltrate the ships, navigate to the Scarlet Marpenoth, and steal Eye #3 from U4. This is the default path and the one most of the scene structure supports. The heist plays out in Scenes 4 and 5.

**Path 2: Alliance with Jarlaxle (diplomatic).** The party negotiates directly. Jarlaxle's primary goal is Luskan's admission to the Lords' Alliance, not Eye #3. If the party can credibly promise political support through faction contacts — Mirt influences Harper votes on the Alliance council, Vajra has Laeral Silverhand's ear, Jalester represents the Alliance directly — Jarlaxle will trade Eye #3. His ask: "Luskan deserves a seat at the table. I need someone at the council who can make that argument." The political cost is real: Luskan has a terrible reputation (piracy, drow influence, historical slavery), and sponsoring its admission strains every faction relationship the party has built. This path bypasses Scenes 4 and 5 entirely. A DC 16 Charisma (Persuasion) check with advantage if the party presents concrete commitments from two or more faction contacts. Jarlaxle's Insight is +11; he detects empty promises instantly.

**Path 3: Jarlaxle as Patron (BD-member PCs).** If a PC joined Bregan D'aerthe during Arc B or Arc C, Jarlaxle may give them the Eye as a reward for proven service (Operative rank, Renown 10+), or use the Zardoz Betrayal Pitch as a loyalty and competence test (Initiate or Soldier rank, Renown 1–9). This path is conditional on an active BD membership. It bypasses the heist but carries its own stakes: the PC's factional loyalty is being tested, and the report back to Jarlaxle determines whether BD treats the party as assets or liabilities going forward.

*The Zardoz Betrayal Pitch (conditional).* This fires if: a PC has BD membership at Initiate or Soldier rank (Renown 1–9) and has not yet identified Jarlaxle as the heist target, OR Jarlaxle has independent reason to be interested in the party from prior interactions — Arc C's Sea Maidens Faire visit, the nimblewright investigation, faction activity he has observed through the surveillance network. Jarlaxle creates contact through an intermediary (J.B. Nevercott approaching them at a tavern, a note via Fenerus Stormcastle's network, an Eyecatcher dinner invitation from "Captain Zord"). If no PC is a BD member and the party has had no meaningful prior contact with the Faire, the Pitch does not fire — Jarlaxle has no channel and no occasion.

Jarlaxle (as J.B. Nevercott) hires the party to rob "Captain Zardoz Zord." He provides a cover story: Zord has stolen a valuable artifact from Nevercott's employer; the party is to board the Eyecatcher, locate the artifact in the captain's quarters, and deliver it to Nevercott. He provides floor plans (accurate), crew schedules (accurate), and a fee of 500 gp, paid in advance. Everything he provides is real. The job is real. The deception is in who benefits.

Three purposes: testing the party's operational competence (can they actually pull off a ship heist?); testing their loyalty (will they report back honestly about what they found, or keep the Eye?); demonstrating that Jarlaxle is always three steps ahead (when the party reports back, he drops the Nevercott disguise and explains). If the party completed the mission and reported honestly, Jarlaxle is impressed and moves directly to the alliance path. If they completed it and kept the Eye, Jarlaxle is disappointed but adjusts — he respects competence even when it is directed against him. If they saw through the deception mid-mission and confronted him, Jarlaxle is delighted: "You're smarter than most. Good."

The Pitch adds a layer of dramatic irony: the party may be executing a plan provided by their own target, with the target watching the entire operation through the nimblewright crystal ball network.

*Potential Allies.*

**Zelifarn (young bronze dragon).** If the party befriended Zelifarn in Force Grey Mission 2, he is the arc's most significant ally. Zelifarn provides: underwater transport to the Eyecatcher's hull; knowledge of the Scarlet Marpenoth's external hatch location and detachment schedule; diversionary capability (surfacing near other ships, creating waves, capsizing harbor skiffs); and an underwater extraction route if the heist goes wrong.

Zelifarn also introduces the arc's moral dimension. The crystal ball in U4 — the Nimblewright Crystal Ball that controls Jarlaxle's surveillance network — belonged to Zelifarn's mother Asphosis. Jarlaxle's agents killed Asphosis and took it. Zelifarn does not know this when the party first engages him for Arc H assistance. He discovers it only if the party brings him to U4 or tells him what they found there. His reaction is grief, then cold fury, then a request: "That was hers. Bring it to me." The party must decide: the crystal ball controls nine paired clairvoyance crystals across Waterdeep. Returning it to Zelifarn destroys the surveillance network and honors a grieving son's claim. Keeping it gives the party a city-wide intelligence tool. The decision is not mechanically consequential for Arc H's completion — the Eye is separate from the crystal ball — but it is morally consequential and shapes the party's relationship with Zelifarn for the remainder of the campaign.

**Faction Contacts as Diversion Providers.** Arc H has no friendly NPCs embedded in the target. The party's allies are external: Jalester can arrange a harbor inspection that draws crew to the pier; Savra can stage a Gauntlet investigation of the Soluun murders requiring crew interviews; Mirt can arrange a Harper agent to visit Zord with a business proposition that occupies his attention for 1–2 hours. Each diversion creates a window but carries risk — if the cover is blown, the faction contact is exposed.

*Variable Difficulty.* The fleet's defensive posture depends on the BD escalation tier from Arc E.

| Tier | State | Effect on Ships |
|------|-------|----------------|
| Unaware | No BD outposts hit | Standard roster. Carnival operating normally. Crew relaxed. Disguises not reinforced. |
| Suspicious | Seven Masks Theater hit | Patrols increased on all ships. Jarlaxle aware someone is investigating BD. Carnival hours shortened. Drow operatives more visible on the Eyecatcher deck. |
| Alert | Both BD outposts hit | Ships on high alert. 4 of 8 nimblewrights from J18 now active and patrolling. Jarlaxle may have relocated Eye #3 to his person. External submarine hatch sealed and trapped (DC 16 Thieves' Tools to disarm; 4d6 lightning damage on failure). |
| Lockdown | Spillover + both outposts | Carnival shut down. Ships preparing early departure (Tarsahk 15). Jarlaxle relocated to the Scarlet Marpenoth permanently. Full combat roster. All 8 nimblewrights from J18 active. Pier access restricted to crew only. |

**Tone.** Planning that shifts based on which path the party chooses. The heist path feels like Ocean's Eleven — assembling allies, timing windows, identifying weaknesses. The alliance path feels like a diplomatic thriller — political favors, credibility checks, the weight of promises. The patron path feels like a spy film — double-crosses, loyalty tests, the awareness that the employer may also be the target.

## Scene 4: The Operation

**Purpose.** The heist itself. Step 5 of the framework. Full keyed-area treatment for gameplay-significant locations. Multi-location structure: pier → Eyecatcher → Scarlet Marpenoth.

**Content.**

*Running This Scene.* Arc H's operation uses a three-beat improvisation structure rather than a staged alarm cascade. Something Goes Wrong → The Complication Escalates → Jarlaxle Confrontation or Clean Escape. This is deliberate: the caper genre does not use tightening pressure so much as rolling improvisation. When something goes wrong aboard the Eyecatcher, the party has seconds to respond — not minutes. The response creates the next complication.

Jarlaxle's location by operation window: during carnival parades, he is marching in the parade (not aboard the Eyecatcher); during the Shipwright's Ball, he is ashore attending the Ball; during standard operating hours, he is in J32 (the sauna, his preferred location when aboard); during Zord's business errands in the city, he is away for 2–4 hours. The DM does not roll for Jarlaxle's position — the operation window determines it.

*Eyecatcher — Critical Path Areas.*

**J1 (Main Deck).** The primary boarding point. 1 mate and 3 sailors (all drow in disguise). Harbor skiff docking point on the starboard side. During parades: 1 sailor only. During the Shipwright's Ball: 1 mate, no sailors. A DC 14 Deception or Stealth check to move past the deck watch into the ship's interior without challenge.

**J3 (Mates' Cabin).** 2 mates off-duty. Contains personal effects and a duty roster mapping crew locations by watch period — the party can use this to predict guard positions for the next 4 hours. DC 13 Investigation to find the roster among personal effects.

**J5 (Gallery / Kitchen).** Cook (commoner, non-combatant). The galley is a low-security transit area. The cook will not raise an alarm unless directly threatened, but will mention "visitors" to the next crew member he sees. He accepts 10 gp to "take a break."

**J10 (Captain's Cabin — Upper Level).** The operational commander's office. 1 drow mage (in human disguise, serving as the Eyecatcher's captain — not Jarlaxle) + 1 nimblewright valet. The captain knows the trapdoor in J30 leads to the Scarlet Marpenoth but does not have the key. The nimblewright responds to intruders with combat programming. During the Shipwright's Ball: captain absent (attending with Jarlaxle), nimblewright present.

**J17 (Upper Cargo Hold).** 4 giant spiders painted as carnival decorations — they look like papier-mâché props until they move. A DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check distinguishes real spiders from genuine props. The spiders attack intruders who enter the hold without speaking the drow command word ("vaeluss"). Thorough casing (multiple carnival visits, DC 16 Perception from the pier) reveals that crew members speak a word before entering the hold.

**J18 (Storage).** 8 deactivated nimblewrights. These activate on a command word from the captain or Jarlaxle. At Unaware tier: all 8 dormant. At Suspicious tier: 2 active, patrolling the main deck. At Alert tier: 4 active, patrolling corridors. At Lockdown tier: all 8 active.

**J29 (Guest Cabin).** Margo Verida (bard) + Khafeyta Murzan (swashbuckler). Jarlaxle's companions — skilled combatants and genuine friends. They can be charmed, deceived, or negotiated with: Margo responds to flattery about her music (DC 13 Persuasion), Khafeyta responds to a good fight story (DC 14 Persuasion). Neither will betray Jarlaxle, but both can be distracted.

**J30 (Zardoz Zord's Cabin).** Jarlaxle's private quarters aboard the Eyecatcher. 1 nimblewright valet. Contains: the Nimblewright Sales Ledger (complete list of nine buyers — cross-reference Arc C Scene 4 for the partial ledger the party may already hold); the Report on the Cultists of Asmodeus (identifies the Asmodean Shrine on Aveen Street and the ancient worship site below the Sea Ward — Three Clue Rule path to Arc G, if not yet completed); Jarlaxle's Report on the Grand Game (references all four factions' positions including Manshoon's Kolat Towers — Three Clue Rule path to Arc I). The trapdoor to the Scarlet Marpenoth is beneath a rug in the aft corner — DC 16 Perception to locate, DC 18 Thieves' Tools to unlock or Jarlaxle's key.

**J31 (Training Area).** 5 attack mannequins — training dummies that animate when someone not carrying a BD identification token enters the room. They fight as animated armor. A BD identification token (recoverable from any defeated drow crew member, or given to BD-affiliated PCs by Jarlaxle) suppresses the animation.

**J32 (Jarlaxle's Sauna).** A steam room with exotic wood paneling and a mechanical ventilation system. This is Jarlaxle's location during standard operating hours. If the party encounters him here, he is unarmed and unarmored but wearing his hat of disguise (Zardoz Zord appearance). He is not defenseless — his bracer of flying daggers and ring of spell storing are always worn. He is relaxed, and an encounter here has a different texture than a confrontation on deck. He will talk first. He will talk for a long time if the conversation is interesting.

*Scarlet Marpenoth — Critical Path Areas.*

**U3 (Soluun's Stateroom).** Soluun Xibrindas is present during standard operating hours and at night; absent during parade windows (he uses the crowds for stalking). Contains: a Letter from Nar'l Xibrindas confirming the BD mole in Xanathar's organization and mentioning the Seven Masks Theater purchase. Soluun is the most dangerous combatant aboard the submarine — volatile, cruel, and fanatically loyal to Jarlaxle. He attacks intruders without hesitation and without the non-lethal restraint other BD operatives show. If the party contains an elf or half-elf, Soluun fixates on them.

**U4 (Jarlaxle's Stateroom).** The score. A crystal ball sits on a pedestal in the center of the stateroom — the Nimblewright Crystal Ball, connected to nine paired clairvoyance crystals across Waterdeep. Eye #3 is in a velvet-lined compartment built into the pedestal's base: DC 14 Investigation to locate the compartment; DC 13 Thieves' Tools to extract the Eye without disturbing the crystal ball's alignment. Disturbing the crystal ball without extracting the Eye first triggers a silent alarm to J10 — the captain dispatches the nimblewright valet and alerts Jarlaxle if reachable. The crystal ball is separate from the Eye — it can be taken, left, or given to Zelifarn as a separate decision.

**U5 (Fel'rekt's Stateroom).** Fel'rekt Lafeen is present unless ashore with Krebbyg (they move together). Less dangerous than Soluun — Fel'rekt will not kill unless cornered. He can be negotiated with (DC 14 Persuasion) if the party demonstrates they are not there to harm BD personnel. He values Krebbyg's safety above his own.

**U6 (Krebbyg's Stateroom).** Krebbyg Masq'il'yr is present unless ashore with Fel'rekt (they move together). Reckless, follows Fel'rekt's lead. If Fel'rekt has been neutralized or agreed to stand down, Krebbyg follows suit. If Fel'rekt is in danger, Krebbyg fights without thinking.

**U7b (Command Center).** 3 gnome engineers + 2 mates. The submarine's operational heart. The gnomes are non-combatants (commoner statistics) who will not fight but will attempt to submerge the submarine if they detect a hostile boarding. Submerging takes 3 minutes from the command center — the party has that window to prevent it or escape. The mates (drow in disguise) fight to protect the gnomes.

*Adversary Rosters.*

**Standard Roster (Unaware/Suspicious tier).** As listed above, distributed across multiple decks. The party should never face more than 6–8 combatants at once unless a full alarm triggers.

**Parade Roster.** Significantly reduced. J1: 1 sailor. J3–J9: empty. J10: nimblewright only. J17: spiders (always present). J18: nimblewrights per tier. J29: empty (Margo and Khafeyta march in the parade). J30: nimblewright valet only. J31: mannequins (always present). Submarine: skeleton crew — U7b (1 gnome + 1 mate), all staterooms empty.

*The Three-Beat Improvisation Structure.* Arc H does not use Arc F's four-stage alarm cascade or Arc G's four-stage social exposure system. Instead:

**Beat 1 — Something Goes Wrong.** The plan hits its first complication. A crew member spots something, a nimblewright activates, a locked door resists, a patrol arrives early. The party must improvise. This is not a failure state — it is the genre expectation. The complication should feel like an obstacle to be overcome creatively, not a punishment for poor planning.

**Beat 2 — The Complication Escalates.** The initial improvisation creates a secondary problem. The distracted crew member told someone; the defeated nimblewright's absence is noticed; the bypassed lock was on a schedule. The escalation narrows options without closing them. Crew begin moving toward the disturbance. If Jarlaxle is aboard, he becomes aware that something is happening.

**Beat 3 — Jarlaxle Confrontation or Clean Escape.** Either the party extracts before Jarlaxle intervenes (clean escape), or Jarlaxle confronts them (see Scene 5). There is no state between these two outcomes in the caper genre.

**Tone.** The operation should feel like a heist film — tight timing, unexpected problems, creative solutions, the constant awareness that the clock is ticking. The multi-location structure (pier → Eyecatcher → submarine) creates natural act breaks. Each transition raises the stakes.

## Scene 5: Complications and Escape

**Purpose.** What goes wrong, what goes sideways, and how the party gets out — managed alongside the Manshoon parallel strike and the Jarlaxle confrontation.

**Content.**

*The Manshoon Parallel Strike.* Manshoon knows Eye #3 is aboard the Eyecatcher. His intelligence sources — scrying via simulacrum, double agents in multiple factions, Zhentarim informants in the Dock Ward — have confirmed the target. He dispatches a strike team regardless of what path the party has chosen.

**The Strike Team.** Urstul Floxin (if alive from Arc D; if dead, replaced by Vevette Blackwater) + 1 lieutenant + 4 thugs. They approach by rowboat from the harbor's south side, boarding the Eyecatcher via grappling hooks on the port hull. They carry pass-amulets to Kolat Towers for emergency extraction.

**Trigger Conditions.** The Manshoon strike always fires. On the heist path, the strike begins 30 minutes after the party boards the Eyecatcher. On the alliance or patron path, the strike hits while the party is aboard negotiating or receiving the Eye — Jarlaxle's crew repels the Zhentarim, but the attack gives Jarlaxle a reason to ask the party for payback: "They came to my ship. In my harbor. I want to know where they came from, and I want it to stop." This becomes a direct hook into Arc I — Jarlaxle sends the party after Manshoon at Kolat Towers, providing whatever BD intelligence the network has on the location. The Zhent attack transforms the alliance and patron paths from clean resolutions into obligations with teeth.

**Interaction Matrix.**

| PC State | Zhentarim Action | Result |
|----------|-----------------|--------|
| PCs undetected, moving through Eyecatcher | Zhentarim board port side, trigger alarm on J1 deck watch | Alarm draws crew AWAY from PCs toward the Zhentarim; pressure relief for the party but chaos increases |
| PCs detected, improvising | Zhentarim arrive into existing chaos | Three-way: PCs vs. BD crew vs. Zhentarim. BD crew splits response. Party can exploit the confusion |
| PCs in Scarlet Marpenoth | Zhentarim fight through Eyecatcher, reach J30, discover trapdoor | Race to U4 — Zhentarim are 5–10 minutes behind the party in the submarine |
| PCs have Eye, escaping | Zhentarim encounter PCs during extraction | Zhentarim attempt to take the Eye from the party; BD crew may treat both groups as hostile or focus on the more visible threat |
| PCs encounter Zhentarim directly | Combat or negotiation | Zhentarim will trade information (Kolat Towers intelligence, Manshoon's operations) for the Eye. They will not accept anything less. If defeated, their pass-amulets to Kolat Towers are recoverable — Three Clue Rule path to Arc I |
| PCs allied or patron path (not heisting) | Zhentarim board and attack BD crew | Jarlaxle's crew fights back; party can assist or stand clear. Jarlaxle uses the attack as leverage: "Now you owe me — find where they came from." Pass-amulets recovered from defeated Zhentarim + Jarlaxle's BD intelligence on Manshoon = strongest Arc I entry path available |

*Facing Jarlaxle.* If Jarlaxle confronts the party — because the alarm reached him, because the Zhentarim strike drew him out, or because he was in J30 or J32 when the party arrived:

Jarlaxle does not attack first. He talks. He assesses. He asks why they are here and what they want. His first instinct is always to negotiate. If the plan was good and the improvisation creative — if the party got further than he expected — Jarlaxle is genuinely impressed. He may offer the Eye in exchange for a favor, information, or the political alliance from Path 2.

If the party was sloppy, Jarlaxle is disappointed but not hostile. He offers a deal: leave the Eye, take whatever else they found, owe him nothing — or take the Eye and owe him a favor. He frames every outcome as a transaction.

If the party was running the Zardoz Betrayal Pitch and reports honestly, Jarlaxle drops the disguise, reveals himself, and offers the Eye freely. The test is passed.

If combat occurs, Jarlaxle uses hit-and-run tactics: smoke bombs (20-foot-radius darkness), levitation, illusions (mirror image, major image of himself running the wrong direction), and his bracer of flying daggers. He fights to escape, not to kill. If reduced to 30 HP, he disengages and retreats to the Scarlet Marpenoth. The submarine is his exit plan — if he reaches U7b, the gnomes begin submerging. The party has 3 minutes to escape or descend with the submarine, which resurfaces 2 miles offshore 30 minutes later. Soluun fights to kill; Fel'rekt and Krebbyg fight to disable and cover Jarlaxle's retreat.

*Escape Routes.*

**Harbor skiff (fastest if deck is clear).** The skiff used to board. Rowing to shore takes 10 minutes from the Eyecatcher. If the deck watch is alerted, DC 14 Athletics to reach the skiff under fire, DC 12 Athletics to row against the current.

**Zelifarn underwater extraction (if allied).** Zelifarn surfaces alongside the Eyecatcher's hull. The party leaps or climbs down to the waterline. Zelifarn can carry 4 Medium creatures while swimming. He dives to 40 feet and surfaces near the Mud Flats. Fastest, safest, most dramatic extraction option in the arc.

**Stolen rowboat.** The Eyecatcher carries 2 rowboats. DC 13 Dexterity check to lower one without fouling the davits. 10 minutes to shore. The deck crew may pursue in the second rowboat.

**Submarine surfacing (if the party controls U7b).** If the party neutralized the submarine crew and has a character with proficiency in water vehicles — or a gnome engineer willing to cooperate (DC 16 Persuasion) — the Scarlet Marpenoth can detach and surface independently. This is the most audacious extraction. It removes the submarine from Jarlaxle's control, which is a significant campaign consequence.

**Tone.** The complications should feel like a heist going sideways — not catastrophic, but requiring rapid adaptation. Jarlaxle's confrontation should feel like a chess match, not a battle. The Manshoon strike adds genuine danger — the Zhentarim are not charming; they are violent, and their presence transforms the caper into something more dangerous. The contrast between Jarlaxle's wit and the Zhentarim's brutality is the scene's defining dynamic.

## Scene 6: Aftermath

**Purpose.** Resolve the heist. Deliver Eye #3. Trigger the Stone of Golorr's knowledge expansion calibrated to Eye count. Assess faction state changes. All-seven-faction debriefs, outcome-branched.

**Content.**

*Eye #3 Recovery.* Eye #3 is extracted from the velvet-lined compartment at the base of the U4 pedestal — a smoky-gray gemstone matching the Stone of Golorr's material. Inserting it into the Stone follows the same physical simplicity as prior Eyes.

*Stone of Golorr Upgrade — Three Scenarios.*

**If this is Eye #1 (neither Arc F nor Arc G completed).** The Stone awakens for the first time. A voice — neither masculine nor feminine, faintly resonant with something older than the gemstone itself — speaks for the first time: "Two remain." The fragmentary impressions clarify into two distinct images: a beholder's unblinking eye in deep darkness, carved stone wet from underground springs; and a Sea Ward villa with a sun symbol marking a hidden door, the Cassalanter house mark pressed into the stone above a spiral stair. The Stone does not reveal vault location, command phrase, or the nature of its own consciousness. It says "Two remain," and then it is quiet.

**If this is Eye #2 (one prior lair arc completed — either Arc F or Arc G).** The Stone's voice deepens, and the quality of its attention changes. It is more present. It says "One remains," and then it does something it has not done before — it describes what it knows. The final Eye's location resolves fully:

- *If Arc F was completed first:* "A villa in the Sea Ward. A temple beneath it, stone floor engraved with the Cassalanter house mark. The children's laughter has a hollow quality — it comes from a room that has been sealed for three years."
- *If Arc G was completed first:* "A beholder's eye, unblinking in darkness. The lair beneath Skullport. A paranoid mind that trusts nothing and no one — but trusts the fishbowl."

The Vault's existence is acknowledged: the Stone confirms it knows where the Vault is, and that three Eyes will unlock the full knowledge. The Stone's agenda deepens: "I am remembering what I was." An aboleth consciousness bleeds through more strongly — not hostile, not helpful, simply ancient and attentive.

**If this is Eye #3 (both Arc F and Arc G already completed).** The Stone goes still. Then it speaks with sudden, precise clarity — the fragmentary impressions are gone. The vault's location resolves in complete architectural detail: the Brandath Crypts in the City of the Dead, a specific mausoleum, a specific passage, a specific door. The command phrase is spoken once, clearly, without repetition. The guardian's nature is described with the clinical precision of a being that has watched a gold dragon stand alone in the dark with five hundred thousand gold coins for four years: Aurinax, bound by a geas, loyal, patient, extraordinarily dangerous.

The aboleth consciousness is fully present. Not a whisper, not an impression, but a mind. Illuun, dreaming on Undermountain Level 4, is closer to the surface of the Stone's personality than it has ever been. The Stone does not explain Illuun. It simply stops pretending to be only a stone.

This is the campaign's pivotal revelation. The DM should give this moment the weight it deserves.

*Faction State Changes.*

**Bregan D'aerthe.** Set the "BD operational?" flag. Five states:

- *Allied (diplomatic resolution):* Jarlaxle traded the Eye for a political commitment. BD is now a campaign ally providing intelligence, operational support, and Jarlaxle's considerable personal charm. The cost: the party owes Luskan's Alliance bid genuine support. Jarlaxle will collect. The alliance does not make BD harmless — it makes them a partner with their own agenda.
- *Impressed (heist succeeded, Jarlaxle confronted and negotiated):* Jarlaxle lost the Eye but gained respect for the party. He is not hostile — he is intrigued. BD continues to operate but treats the party as worthy opponents rather than targets. Jarlaxle may approach them for Arc I or Arc J cooperation.
- *Robbed (heist succeeded, clean escape):* Jarlaxle lost the Eye without knowing who took it (possible at Unaware tier). BD launches an intelligence operation to identify the thieves. Jarlaxle is embarrassed, which makes him more decisive when he discovers the truth.
- *Hostile (combat, casualties among BD lieutenants):* If the party killed Soluun, Fel'rekt, or Krebbyg, Jarlaxle's response escalates from professional to personal. He reaches for leverage before force, but he reaches harder. BD response teams become more aggressive in future encounters.
- *Patron (BD member received the Eye):* Jarlaxle's investment in the PC is confirmed. BD renown increases. The PC is now a trusted operative, which means more missions, more intelligence, and more expectations.

**Manshoon's Zhentarim.** If the Zhentarim strike team was defeated, Manshoon knows — the pass-amulets went dark. He adjusts Arc I preparations. If the strike team caused chaos but failed to get the Eye, Manshoon redirects to Kolat Towers defense. If the strike team recovered the Eye (party failure case), Arc I becomes a recovery operation as well as an assault.

**Xanathar Guild.** If Arc F already completed, no change. If not, Xanathar's awareness depends on whether Guild patrols in the Dock Ward observed harbor activity.

**Cassalanters.** If Arc G already completed, no change. If not, imp surveillance from the Asmodean Shrine may have observed harbor activity.

*All-Seven-Faction Debriefs — Outcome-Branched.*

**If Eye recovered (heist succeeded or diplomatic resolution):**

- *Mirt (Harpers):* "You robbed a drow intelligence operation. In Waterdeep's harbor. And you're telling me about it over breakfast." Impressed. Wants the nimblewright ledger if the party recovered it — it maps BD's surveillance network across the nobility. If the party returned the crystal ball to Zelifarn, Mirt approves: "Good. No one should have that much invisible reach in this city."
- *Jalester (Lords' Alliance):* Wants to know about the submarine. A covert underwater vessel in Waterdeep's harbor is a national security concern — he will brief Laeral Silverhand. If the alliance path was taken and Luskan's Alliance bid is on the table, Jalester is conflicted — professionally obligated to consider it, personally skeptical.
- *Melannor (Emerald Enclave):* "Is the dragon all right?" If Zelifarn was involved and unharmed, Melannor is satisfied. If Zelifarn was harmed, Melannor is quietly furious.
- *Savra (Order of the Gauntlet):* "Did you find the elf-killer?" If the party encountered Soluun and dealt with him, Savra considers the arc a success regardless of the Eye. If Soluun escaped, Savra asks the party to prioritize his capture.
- *Vajra (Force Grey):* Fascinated by the submarine's engineering and the nimblewright crystal ball network. Wants technical details. If the party recovered the crystal ball, Vajra offers to study it for a tenday — she returns it, but the analysis lets her start mapping the nine surveillance locations. If the party gave it to Zelifarn, Vajra respects the choice but is visibly disappointed.
- *Tashlyn or Davil (Doom Raiders):* "What happened to the Zhentarim team?" Names and faces from the strike team help them map Manshoon's remaining force structure. The pass-amulets to Kolat Towers are of particular interest: "Those get you inside his base. That is extremely valuable intelligence."
- *Jarlaxle (if allied or impressed):* If the party negotiated the alliance: "I trust you'll remember our arrangement. Luskan is patient. I am less so." If the party robbed him cleanly: silence for three days, then a note on Seven Masks Theater stationery — "Well played." If the party fought him: a more cautious approach through intermediaries, testing whether hostility has become permanent or was situational.

**If Eye not recovered (heist failed):**

All contacts shift to problem-solving mode. Where is the Eye now? Is Jarlaxle leaving Waterdeep? Can the alliance path be attempted retroactively? The tone is operational reassessment, not recrimination.

*Calendar Update.* Arc H consumes 1–2 tendays: several days of intelligence and casing (Scenes 1–3), 1–3 days for the operation and escape (Scenes 4–5), 2–4 days of recovery and debrief (Scene 6). Update the campaign calendar. If the departure deadline is approaching, the DM communicates urgency through environmental details: carnival stalls being packed, the Heartbreaker's sails being inspected, harbor supply deliveries ceased.

*Transition.* The party holds the Stone with 1–3 Eyes. The remaining lair arcs are available in any order. The Report on Cultists of Asmodeus (J30) points toward Arc G if not yet completed. The Letter from Nar'l (U3) confirms the BD/Xanathar connection, providing context for Arc F. Jarlaxle's Report on the Grand Game (J30) and the Zhentarim pass-amulets (if recovered from Scene 5) both point toward Arc I. If this was Eye #3 — the Full Awakening — Arc J launches immediately.

**Tone.** Decompression weighted with consequence. The caper's resolution carries a different emotional register than Arc F's revelatory awakening or Arc G's moral reckoning. Arc H's aftermath is about relationships — with Jarlaxle (enemy, rival, or ally?), with Zelifarn (the crystal ball decision), with Manshoon's Zhentarim (confirmed threat, now owed payback). The party has engaged with the most charming villain in the Grand Game. What that engagement produced depends entirely on how they played it.

# Design Notes: Sea Maidens Faire

## Genre Identity

Arc H must feel like a caper, not a dungeon infiltration (Arc F) or a social infiltration into a dungeon (Arc G). The distinction is mechanical, not just aesthetic.

Arc F rewards careful routing and surveillance suborning. Arc G rewards maintaining cover and reading social cues. Arc H rewards improvisation — the plan will go wrong, and the party's ability to adapt in the moment determines success. The three-beat improvisation structure (Something Goes Wrong → Escalation → Confrontation or Clean Escape) replaces Arc F's four-stage alarm cascade and Arc G's four-stage social exposure system. The caper genre does not use staged escalation because the drama comes from the moment of improvisation, not from a slowly tightening noose.

The DM should telegraph this genre from the briefing onward. When faction contacts describe Jarlaxle, they should convey that this is not a paranoid beholder in a sealed lair or desperate parents in a gilded villa. This is a man who enjoys being robbed if the thieves are interesting enough. That single characterization reframes the entire arc.

## The Three Resolution Paths

Arc H is the only lair arc that offers three fundamentally different approaches to obtaining the Eye. This is because Jarlaxle is the only villain who would willingly trade it for something he values more. Xanathar does not negotiate — he eats envoys. The Cassalanters are too desperate — the children's deadline consumes their attention. Manshoon holds no Eye. Jarlaxle is the one Grand Game villain who treats every interaction as a transaction, and the Eye is currency he is willing to spend if the price is right.

The heist path is the default and receives the most structural support. The alliance path exists because it reflects Jarlaxle's actual priorities — Luskan's Alliance bid matters more to him than one component of a treasure-hunting artifact. The patron path exists because the campaign supports BD membership from Arc B onward and must reward that investment with a meaningful payoff.

All three paths lead to the same aftermath structure, but the faction state changes and debrief reactions differ significantly. The text presents all three without advocacy.

## Jarlaxle as Charming Antagonist

The Jarlaxle confrontation is the design principle that most distinguishes Arc H: it plays as a chess match, not a fight.

Xanathar confronted is a lethal puzzle — eye rays, lair actions, raw destructive power. The Cassalanters confronted are a moral crisis — desperate parents, infernal contracts, children between the party and the door. Jarlaxle confronted is a conversation. He is faster, better informed, better equipped, and more experienced than the party. He also does not want to fight them. He wants to understand them, evaluate them, and determine whether they are more useful as allies than as enemies.

Writing Jarlaxle's confrontation correctly means the DM must play him as someone who is genuinely delighted to discover that the party is competent. His respect, his offers, and his real-time recalculation of whether alliance or opposition serves him — all of it is genuine. The party can influence that calculus through what they say and how they say it.

Combat with Jarlaxle is a narrative failure state: the caper's improvisational energy collapsed into violence, and both sides lost something. The text makes this clear without forbidding it. Some parties will fight Jarlaxle. The consequences should reflect that this was a missed opportunity, not a tactical error.

## The Zardoz Betrayal Pitch as Optional Layer

The Zardoz Betrayal Pitch is the single most Jarlaxle element in the arc — a con within a con, a test disguised as a job, a moment where the party discovers that they have been working for the person they are trying to rob. It is conditional because it requires either a BD channel (a PC at Initiate or Soldier rank) or Jarlaxle's independent interest in the party from prior interactions — and because it should feel like a natural development from the party's history, not a forced encounter.

When it fires, the Pitch adds dramatic irony: the party is executing a plan provided by their target, with the target watching through the crystal ball network. Everything Jarlaxle provided is accurate — the floor plans, the crew schedules, the fee. The details are accurate. The frame is the con.

The reveal — Jarlaxle dropping the Nevercott disguise and explaining — should be played for its full dramatic weight. This is one of the campaign's signature NPC moments: the mastermind showing his hand because he has already won, and winning gracefully is more important to him than winning invisibly.

## Manshoon Parallel Strike as Rival Actor

Every lair arc needs a parallel heist actor: Arc F has Jarlaxle's simultaneous heist, Arc G has Esvele Rosznar, Arc H has Manshoon's Zhentarim strike team. The parallel actor creates three-way pressure, provides a pressure-relief valve, and connects the current arc to future arcs.

Manshoon is the correct choice for Arc H because: he has the intelligence resources (scrying, double agents) to know the Eye's location; his Zhentarim are the most violent faction, creating genuine danger contrast against Jarlaxle's charm; the strike team's pass-amulets to Kolat Towers provide a Three Clue Rule path to Arc I; and on the alliance or patron path, the Zhent attack gives Jarlaxle a personal reason to send the party after Manshoon — transforming a clean diplomatic resolution into one that carries a new obligation and provides the strongest possible Arc I entry.

The strike always fires regardless of which resolution path the party chose. Manshoon's intelligence is independent of the party's relationship with Jarlaxle. This is a deliberate departure from Arc G's Esvele matrix, where the parallel actor could be avoided entirely — Manshoon is more aggressive and more consequential.

## Zelifarn's Crystal Ball as Moral Thread

Every lair arc has a moral dimension: Arc F has the smokepowder choice, Arc G has the children's fate and the four resolution paths. Arc H's moral dimension is smaller in scope but personal — the Nimblewright Crystal Ball in U4 belonged to Zelifarn's mother, killed by Jarlaxle's agents.

The choice is whether to return the crystal ball to Zelifarn (honoring a grieving dragon's claim, destroying Jarlaxle's surveillance network) or keep it (gaining a city-wide intelligence tool). The Eye is separate — the party can take the Eye regardless. This makes the crystal ball decision purely moral, not mechanically necessary.

Zelifarn's reaction to learning the truth should be played for genuine emotion. He is a young dragon — enthusiastic, curious, not yet cynical. Learning that the interesting ship with the funny name was operated by the people who killed his mother is a formative moment. How the party handles his grief shapes whether he becomes a reliable campaign ally or a volatile wild card in Undermountain.

## The Fleet as Multi-Location Target

Arc H's physical structure is unique among lair arcs: four sub-locations (pier carnival, Heartbreaker and Hellraiser, Eyecatcher, Scarlet Marpenoth) spread across open water. This creates navigation challenges that do not exist in dungeon or villa environments and serves the caper genre by creating natural act breaks. Moving from the pier to the Eyecatcher raises the stakes. Moving from the Eyecatcher to the Scarlet Marpenoth raises them again. Each transition is a commitment — once in the submarine, exit options narrow.

The fleet structure also gives the departure deadline physical reality. The ships can leave. If the party boards during a parade window and the parade ends early, the pier fills with returning crew. If the party is in the submarine when Jarlaxle decides to flee, the submarine detaches and submerges — taking the party with it.

## Stone of Golorr Full Awakening

If Arc H delivers the third Eye, this is the campaign's pivotal revelation. The Stone reveals everything: vault location, command phrase, Aurinax's nature. This moment must be given its full weight regardless of which arc delivers it — the text frames the Full Awakening identically whether the third Eye came from Xanathar's fishbowl, the Asmodeus statue, or Jarlaxle's crystal ball pedestal.

The Full Awakening also brings the aboleth consciousness fully forward. The Stone stops pretending to be an object. It is Illuun's instrument — an ancient, cold, patient intelligence that has been observing the party through every attunement since Arc E. The Mad Mage seed planted in the 1-Eye awakening germinates here. The party is not carrying a treasure map. They are carrying a mind.

## Variable Difficulty and Departure Deadline

The four-tier difficulty table translates Arc E's BD outpost consequences into Arc H's fleet environment. At Unaware, the Faire operates normally — the party walks into a carnival. At Lockdown, the carnival is shut down and the ships are preparing to leave early — the party assaults a military vessel.

The departure deadline (Tarsahk 20, or Tarsahk 15 at Lockdown) is unique to Arc H. Neither Arc F nor Arc G has a hard deadline tied to the target physically leaving the campaign's geography. The departure deadline creates urgency of a different kind than Arc G's Founders' Day: not "something bad happens if you wait" but "the opportunity disappears." The fleet sails. The Eye goes with it.

## Three Clue Rule Paths Out of Arc H

Arc H provides Three Clue Rule paths to three other arcs.

**To Arc G (if not yet completed).** The Report on the Cultists of Asmodeus (J30) identifies the Asmodean Shrine on Aveen Street and references an ancient worship site below the Sea Ward, converging on Cassalanter Villa. This is one of three independent paths to Arc G.

**To Arc F (if not yet completed).** The Letter from Nar'l Xibrindas (U3) confirms the BD mole in Xanathar's organization and mentions the Seven Masks Theater purchase. This connects to the Xanathar Guild intelligence network and provides context for approaching Arc F.

**To Arc I (Kolat Towers).** Two independent paths from Arc H: Jarlaxle's Report on the Grand Game (J30) references Manshoon's operations and Kolat Towers; and the Zhentarim pass-amulets recovered from the Manshoon strike team (Scene 5) provide physical access to Kolat Towers. These join the existing paths from Arc F (Samara's testimony from X7, Nihiloor's files from X23) and Arc G (Cassalanter Report on the Grand Game from C6 or C22). The Three Clue Rule is satisfied to Arc I through any combination of prior arcs.
