# Xanathar's Lair

*An adventure for 4th- to 6th-level characters.*

---

## Quest Opener

Three months before the Trollskull fireball, Manshoon dispatched two Zhentarim lieutenants to Skullport carrying a gift: Eye #1 of the Stone of Golorr, recovered from a drow courier killed on the Trade Way. The Eye was offered as a peace overture — proof that Manshoon's splinter faction could be a valuable partner in the Grand Game. Xanathar received the envoys in his throne room, listened to their proposal, and ate them. The Eye passed to Ott Steeltoes, who placed it in Sylgar's fishbowl because it had a pretty color and Xanathar liked Sylgar's bowl to look interesting.

The lair beneath Skullport has been sealed for weeks. Since the fireball disrupted the Street of Silver trade routes, Xanathar has withdrawn into paranoia — convinced that one of the other Grand Game factions has mapped the lair's entrances. Ahmaergo runs external operations while Xanathar broods in the Sanctum, running his eye rays across the empty room and interrogating Guild members whose faces he does not immediately recognize. The gladiatorial games continue because the arena revenue continues, and because stopping them would signal weakness. But Xanathar does not attend from his throne as he once did. He watches from invisibility, alone.

Nihiloor has used this paranoia as cover. The illithid's three unauthorized projects — running in parallel for years — are now approaching critical phases. The Infiltration: intellect devourers inserted into four positions in Waterdeep's Watch and civic administration, with two additional placements pending. The Experiments: twelve captives from the gladiatorial arena modified into psionic relay organisms, intended to form the backbone of a city-wide intelligence network that would answer only to Nihiloor. The Puppet: four months ago, Nihiloor successfully applied Dominate Monster to Xanathar through a sustained psionic relay device in X26. The Xanathar who has been running the Guild since then — the Xanathar who killed Manshoon's envoys, who sent response teams against the party's allies in **Faction Outposts**, who holds the Eye in a fishbowl because he trusts Ott's aesthetic judgment — has been Nihiloor's instrument. The beholder does not know this. The domination does not feel like domination; it feels like his own decisions.

The panopticus surveillance network — five ghostly beholder eyestalks wired through enchanted crystal to a monitoring station at X16 — was Nihiloor's gift to Xanathar three years ago. The beholder believes the network serves his paranoia. Nihiloor built a structural back door into the control array from the beginning.

Jarlaxle has known about Nar'l Xibrindas's double-agent role for eleven years. Through Nar'l's monthly reports, the Bregan D'aerthe have assembled a precise picture of the lair's layout — including the secret passage at X14 that bypasses the panopticus entirely and opens forty feet from the Sanctum door. Jarlaxle has been waiting for an opportunity to retrieve the Stone and its Eye. He has been in no hurry. He has always been certain the opportunity would come.

## Scene 1: The Lair Intelligence Briefing

**Purpose.** The party holds the Stone of Golorr and knows an Eye is somewhere in Xanathar's lair; that much was established in **Faction Outposts**. This scene converts that knowledge into operational intelligence. Seven faction contacts provide lair-specific information, each from a different angle on the same target. By the scene's end, the party has at least one confirmed entry method, two potential inside contacts, and enough threat assessment to plan an approach.

**Content.**

*Running This Scene.* Each contact below is available only to party members of that faction. A party with no Harper has no channel to Mirt; a party with no Force Grey member has no Vajra briefing. The scene's intelligence yield depends entirely on which factions the party joined during **Trollskull Alley** and **Fireball!** — a party that spread its memberships widely arrives knowing a great deal; a party that clustered into one or two factions arrives with significant blind spots. This is by design.

This briefing is narrower than **Faction Outposts**' faction consultation, which covered the entire faction landscape. Every contact here is pointing at a single location. Run it as a montage of consultations: brief, specific, each one adding a piece to the picture.

*Mirt (Harpers — renown 1+).* Thorvin Twinbeard is inside the lair — a Harper asset embedded in the lair's engineering staff for six years, Mirt's most carefully kept secret. Mirt provides Thorvin's location (X13, the workshop) and a recognition code: ask him what he thinks of the new pulley design. His response — "It'll hold if the load is distributed right" — confirms the channel. If Xanathar outposts were hit in **Faction Outposts**, Mirt warns that Thorvin's cover may be compromised: a gazer has been assigned to watch him, and Thorvin knows it. At renown 10+, Mirt arranges a Harper field agent — use the spy stat block — to stage a timed surface distraction above the guildsign entrance: a staged brawl that occupies Guild topside watchers for 20 minutes at a prearranged signal.

*Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance — renown 1+).* Watch patrol schedules for the sewer approaches to Skullport: three viable windows per day in the Dock Ward drainage network. If Manshoon outposts were also hit in **Faction Outposts** (cross-faction spillover raising the Watch's general alert posture), the gaps narrow. Jalester can identify a 90-minute window on the third watch that still exists even at elevated patrol density. At renown 10+, Jalester provides a letter of introduction to the Dock Ward senior Watch officer, who will formally log any disturbance report the party files — giving their sewer approach a legal cover story and keeping Watch patrols clear of the guildsign entrance for two hours.

*Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave — renown 1+).* Environmental displacement in the sewer tunnels below Skullport: unusual fungi growth patterns where a large excavation disturbs underground airflow, water flow disrupted by tunnels cut against the natural gradient, specific junctions where small animals avoid and don't return. This is scouting intelligence that points toward sewer access routes even for parties who haven't found the guildsign trail — Melannor's information can substitute for the Orb Confectioners documents as a path to X1. At renown 10+, Jeryth Phaulkon casts water breathing on the entire party before the sewer approach — 24 hours, no concentration required, no components.

*Savra Belabranta (Order of the Gauntlet — renown 1+).* A Gauntlet informant who recognized Samara from the gladiatorial circuit confirmed her transfer to the lair's cell block six weeks ago. Savra frames this as a moral obligation: the party is going into that lair; they should bring people out. She does not frame it as a tactical bonus, though it is one — Samara has months of interior observation. Savra asks the party to bring out whoever they find alive. She does not ask about the Eye. At renown 10+, Savra provides three potions of healing from the Order's armory and positions herself at the guildsign entrance as extraction backup — ready to draw off a pursuing patrol if the party signals retreat.

*Vajra Safahr (Force Grey — renown 1+).* Arcane assessment of the lair's surveillance system. Vajra identifies the magical signature: beholder-derived divination, sustained through a central crystalline array, consistent with a monitoring installation rather than a passive ward. She cannot map the specific sensor placements without being inside — but she tells the party what to look for (a faint electrical quality to the air, shadows that fall wrong) and points them toward finding "the center of the web." With this briefing, panopticus suborning checks are made with advantage. At renown 10+, Vajra casts one preparatory spell on the party before entry — nondetection, see invisibility, or water breathing — and deploys a junior Blackstaff mage (use the mage stat block) to monitor the guildsign entrance; if the party sends a signal spell within 8 hours, the mage activates a Force Grey emergency response.

*Tashlyn Yafeera or Davil Starsong (Doom Raiders — renown 1+).* Contact depends on quest order: Tashlyn if **DR Mission 4 — Silencing Skeemo** is not yet complete (Davil still in custody), Davil if it has been (he's back at the Yawning Portal). Either way, the intelligence is the same — secondhand accounts from Guild agents who defected to the Doom Raiders over the past two years. The Noska–Ahmaergo rivalry has reached near-blows twice in the past month; during an active tournament, Noska is arena-side and Ahmaergo is in his quarters reviewing fight records, neither monitoring the other's territory. That window is real and exploitable. At renown 10+, Yagra Stonefist is available as outside backup — she can hold the guildsign entrance open from the exterior, delay a pursuing Guild patrol by 5 minutes, or cover the party's extraction from the sewer exit. Once per operation.

*Jarlaxle (Bregan D'aerthe — renown 1+).* Available only if a PC is a BD member. Jarlaxle offers two things: the location of the secret entrance at X1 (reached via guildsign from the sewer network), and Nar'l Xibrindas's name and position inside the lair — framed as proof of trust. At renown 1–2, he describes Nar'l as "a useful contact who values his own continued good health." At renown 3+, he confirms Nar'l's BD allegiance directly. He does not mention his own heist plan. Everything he says is accurate. Everything he says is shaped.

**Tone.** Tactical and convergent. Every contact is pointing in the same direction. The party should leave this scene feeling that the lair is knowable — not safe, but legible. The drama is in the pattern forming, not in any single piece of intelligence.

## Scene 2: Casing the Target

**Purpose.** Steps 1 through 3 of the heist framework: Identify the Score, Gather Information, Onsite Surveillance. The party transitions from intelligence briefing to direct reconnaissance of the lair. This scene establishes the three entry methods, maps the panopticus challenge, and forces the party to decide how to handle Xanathar's location before the operation begins.

**Content.**

*Identifying the Score.* The Stone of Golorr's impression — a beholder's eye, unblinking in darkness, carved stone wet from underground springs, a long way down — combined with **Faction Outposts** outpost documents confirms the Eye's location. Xanathar's lair beneath Skullport. The party needs to determine how to enter, how to navigate the surveillance network, where the Eye is held inside, and where Xanathar will be during the operation.

*Three Entry Methods.*

**The Guildsign Trail (via Orb Confectioners correspondence).** N'arl Xibrindas's letter from the **Faction Outposts** sewer hideout contains guildsign directions to Area X1 — a false wall in the sewers below Skullport that opens into a rough-hewn passage. Following the guildsign is a series of DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) checks, rising to DC 17 in the final approach. Three cumulative failures before reaching X1 loses the trail. Advantage: X1 delivers the party into the lair's quieter western sectors, away from the arena and main thoroughfares. The first panopticus sensor is at X2, forty feet in — the party has a window to detect it before passing through. Disadvantage: X1 opens into a monitored approach corridor. At Alert tier, the entrance is additionally trapped (DC 15 Perception to notice, DC 13 Dexterity saving throw or 14 (4d6) piercing damage).

**Gladiatorial Transport (via Terasse Estate).** Discovered through the Terasse Estate mini-heist in **Faction Outposts**. The trap door at Terasse leads to an underground dock where guests are blindfolded and transported by boat to Area X4, arriving as spectators during the gladiatorial games. Advantage: legitimate spectator cover. The crowd and noise provide concealment; DC checks to remain unnoticed in spectator areas drop by 3 during active fights. The party can use tournament sessions to observe NPC movement patterns and confirm Xanathar's location. Disadvantage: the party arrives unarmed. Weapons and obviously magical items are confiscated at X4. The party must smuggle equipment (DC 16 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) per item) or recover it from Area X9, the equipment cache. If the party's escalation tier is Alert or higher, the tournament may be suspended — this entry method loses its crowd cover and the party arrives into a searched, guarded X4.

**Ott Steeltoes as Guide (if rescued from the Interrogation House).** Ott knows the lair's maintenance corridors — service tunnels the Guild's rank-and-file barely know exist. He guides the party through sewer access to a service entrance at X15, providing guard schedule gaps and specific patrol timings. Advantage: the guided approach bypasses both X2 and X4 checkpoints entirely; the party enters through infrastructure, not a guarded threshold. Disadvantage: Ott is terrified. Under pressure, make a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw for Ott at the start of each combat — on a failure, he freezes, stammers loud enough to draw attention, or attempts to flee. If captured, he tells the Guild everything within minutes.

At least one of these three paths is available to any party regardless of **Faction Outposts** outpost results; Melannor's environmental intelligence from Scene 1 can substitute for the guildsign trail if the Orb Confectioners documents weren't recovered.

*The Panopticus.* The lair's central defensive challenge. Ghostly beholder eyestalks — preserved remnants of long-dead beholder-kin, maintained in crystal housings — occupy Areas X2, X6, X18, X23, and X32. Each transmits visual information to the Panopticus Dwarves in X16, who signal guards on any unusual activity.

Detection: characters with a Passive Perception score of 14 or higher notice an unnatural quality in rooms with sensors — a faint electrical crackle, shadows that fall slightly wrong. A DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana) check confirms active divination magic. A character who has identified one sensor and then casts detect magic can triangulate X16's location from two separate detection points. Vajra's briefing from Scene 1 grants advantage on both the detection check and the later suborning check.

Suborning: reaching X16 and altering two specific runes in the control array takes 3 minutes and requires a DC 16 Intelligence (Arcana) check. On a success, the live feed is replaced with a recorded loop of the last ten minutes — the dwarves see nothing new. On a failure by 4 or less, the feed glitches for 30 seconds; a DC 15 Deception check convinces the dwarves it was a routine fluctuation. On a failure by 5 or more, the alarm triggers immediately.

Without suborning the panopticus, the party must reroute entirely around monitored areas. X18 and X19 both have sensors; reaching the Sanctum through the main corridors without suborning or bypassing X18 means moving through active scrying coverage.

*Xanathar's Location.* Xanathar is in X19 (the Sanctum) with Sylgar. He has barely left for weeks. The Eye is there. So is he.

This means the party cannot simply walk into X19 and take the Eye — not unless they create a window. The dream nullifier in X20 exists for this purpose: destroying it sends Xanathar into the corridor to find Ahmaergo, giving the party approximately 4 minutes. A party that found X20 through reconnaissance or Thorvin's guidance has a clean extraction opportunity. A party that didn't must extract the Eye while a paranoid beholder is in the room.

*Safe Zone: Area X28.* The Guild Recreation Hall has no panopticus sensor. Nihiloor built this blind spot intentionally; rank-and-file Guild members speak more freely without surveillance, and their unguarded conversations are useful intelligence. The party can use X28 as a staging area once they reach it: rest, plan, treat wounds, or establish a rendezvous. Guild members here are occupied with card games and arguments and will not scrutinize strangers with a plausible story.

**Tone.** Methodical tension. The party is studying a place that is studying them back. This scene should feel like planning under observation — every piece of intelligence narrows the decision space without eliminating risk. By the scene's end, the party knows the challenge and knows their options. They do not yet know whether their options will work.

## Scene 3: Preparation

**Purpose.** Step 4 of the heist framework. The party finalizes the operational plan, makes contact with inside allies, acquires any remaining tools, and commits to an approach for the panopticus, the extraction, and the optional smokepowder choice. The decisions made here shape Scene 4 directly.

**Content.**

*Inside Ally: Thorvin Twinbeard (X13).* Thorvin is a dwarf engineer who has maintained the lair's mechanical infrastructure for nine years and served as a Harper informant for six. He is watched by a gazer at all times — a tiny beholder-kin that floats near the workshop ceiling and observes everything. He knows it is there. He communicates in short, technical-seeming statements that encode information in their structure.

Thorvin provides: a hand-drawn schematic of every mechanical trap in the service corridors; the location of the smokepowder kegs Nar'l cached in X36; identification of five structural weak points that, hit simultaneously, collapse the lair's central chambers within one hour; and the guard schedule for X18 and X19 during non-tournament periods.

If the gazer is destroyed (13 HP, AC 13), Thorvin speaks openly for the first time. His relief is immediate and genuine. He confirms what the party may have begun to suspect: Nihiloor's domain (X23–X27) contains evidence that would change everything the party thinks they know about who has been running the Xanathar Guild.

Thorvin will not evacuate himself. He has a family in the Trades Ward, and he intends to survive this. He will delay alarm responses to mechanical failures by "taking unusually long to diagnose the problem." A DC 12 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) check creates a failure he can credibly explain.

*Inside Ally: Nar'l Xibrindas (X35).* The only drow in the lair, which Xanathar considers proof of his own cosmopolitan generosity. Nar'l's grell bodyguard carries a kill-on-disloyalty order from Xanathar, an instruction the grell will carry out without hesitation. Nar'l cannot dismiss it. He can only avoid triggering it.

Without BD allegiance revealed: Nar'l is coolly professional. He offers mild operational assistance framed as Guild hospitality, pointing the party toward X28 as a rest point, confirming which guards accept bribes and at what rate. He reveals nothing about himself.

With BD allegiance revealed: Nar'l reassesses, then cooperates warily. He can suppress the alarm signal for a specific area for up to ten minutes (logged as a maintenance calibration), redirect a guard patrol once per session, and confirm Xanathar's current location. He will not act against the grell. He will sacrifice the party if doing so is the only way to preserve his cover.

If a party member is BD: Nar'l recognizes them as colleagues and cooperates fully — no hesitation, no wary reassessment. He shares the complete guard rotation, confirms Xanathar's location in X19 and the dream nullifier's purpose, and actively manages alarms rather than offering a single suppression. He also reveals the existence of X14 (the secret passage to X19) if the party has not already found it. The grell constraint still applies: he will not act against it regardless of orders, because the grell is Xanathar's mechanism, not Jarlaxle's. He will evacuate immediately if the smokepowder option is chosen. One additional consequence of BD membership: the Jarlaxle simultaneous heist does not fire. Jarlaxle knew the party was BD, trusted them to handle it, and is waiting for Nar'l's report rather than sending a competing team.

*The Smokepowder Option.* Nar'l smuggled eight smokepowder kegs into a concealed cache in Area X36 over four months. Thorvin's structural schematic identifies five load-bearing junctions that, struck simultaneously, trigger a controlled collapse of the lair's central chambers within one hour.

Execution requires: one keg per target junction (a Bag of Holding carries one; the party needs to coordinate transport of five), clockwork detonators assembled from Thorvin's components (DC 15 Dexterity, 20 minutes, Thorvin's tools required), and a single pull-cord mechanism triggerable from up to 100 feet away.

Consequences: Xanathar is destroyed if present in the lair, or escapes through the Undermountain connection at X4 if the DM wants to preserve the thread. The Guild collapses into factional fighting between Ahmaergo and surviving lieutenants. Nar'l and Thorvin must be evacuated or they die. Thorvin will not leave the lair's surviving workers; Nar'l will leave without hesitation. Nihiloor was not in his domain at the time and escapes to Undermountain Level 17 with his three projects intact.

Permanent consequences: the Guild is gone as an intelligence resource. The **Vault of Dragons** approach loses access to Guild knowledge and contacts. The Undermountain connection from X4 is sealed. The party trades a dangerous faction they can understand for an unconstrained Nihiloor operating below the city with no leash.
*The Dream Nullifier (X20).* A copper bowl the size of a serving dish, mounted in a maintenance alcove between X19 and X21. A Passive Perception score of 15 or higher notices it while passing through the corridor. A DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana) check identifies its function: it prevents Xanathar from dreaming of other beholders, waking him before the dream takes hold.

Destroying it (antimagic field or dispel magic against DC 15) triggers behavioral change within 3 minutes: intrusive dream-images of other beholders crowd Xanathar's perception. He leaves X19 to find Ahmaergo, demanding explanation, seeking confirmation that the lair is still secure. He moves toward X18 or X12. X19 is empty. The party has approximately 4 minutes.

*Variable Difficulty: Escalation Tier.*

| Tier | Entry | Panopticus | Guards | Tournament |
|------|-------|------------|--------|------------|
| Unaware | Standard | Standard (DC 16) | 20-minute patrol intervals | Runs on schedule |
| Suspicious | X1 unwatched; X4 checked | DC checks +2 | Extra pairs at X2, X4 | Runs; Xanathar remains in X19 |
| Alert | X1 trapped | DC checks +2 | Posts doubled; Ahmaergo patrols X12–X18 | Suspended |
| Lockdown | X1 barricaded and trapped; X4 crossbow-point | DC checks +4 | Full response team in X6 at all times | Suspended; X4 transport severed |

**Tone.** Planning and commitment. The party is making decisions that foreclose others. By this scene's end, they have an inside contact or they don't, a plan for the panopticus or they don't, and a position on the smokepowder option. These are real choices with real costs attached to each path not taken.

## Scene 4: The Operation

**Purpose.** The heist itself. Step 5 of the framework. The party enters, navigates the lair, and extracts Eye #1. This scene provides keyed-area treatment for the fifteen gameplay-significant areas of the lair's thirty-six total, with two adversary rosters and a defined alarm cascade.

**Content.**

*Keyed Areas: The Critical Path.*

**X1 — The Secret Entrance.** A false wall in the sewers, opened by pressing three specific stones in sequence. The guildsign trail leads here. Without the trail: DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation) or Passive Perception 15 to notice the seam. At Alert tier: a wire trap across the passage floor 10 feet inside (DC 15 Perception, DC 13 Dexterity saving throw or 14 (4d6) piercing damage). At Lockdown: the wall is additionally barricaded from inside — 8 HP of wooden bracing, AC 13, deals 14 (4d6) bludgeoning damage to any creature adjacent when it breaks.

**X2 — Entrance Hall.** A vaulted stone chamber with beholder motifs carved into every pillar. Two Guild Soldiers stand watch at the far end. The panopticus sensor is embedded in the ceiling above the entrance, angled to capture anyone passing through. Passive Perception 14 notices the crystal housing. At Suspicious tier: visitors must show a Guild pass (leaden disk, beholder eye stamp). At Alert and above: no visitors admitted without escort.

**X4 — Skullport Connection.** The lair's cargo dock, with two flat-bottomed boats and a raised platform where gladiatorial transport arrivals are processed. The intensity of the search scales with escalation tier: Unaware means a cursory check; Suspicious adds a weapon search; Alert and Lockdown mean every item in every bag examined. A DC 13 Passive Perception notices the secondary maintenance tunnel running beyond the lair's floor plan — the connection to Undermountain Level 1, the emergency descent route.

**X6 — The Arena.** A sand-floored oval with spectator benches on three sides. During tournament play: 30–60 spectators, noise and crowd providing passive concealment. The panopticus sensor covers the arena floor and spectator tiers but not the maintenance access points behind the seating. The concealed door to X33 is in the north seating section — DC 17 Perception to notice the gap, DC 15 to confirm it's a door. Guard positioning during fights is arena-focused; the service corridors along the back wall are unmonitored.

**X7 — Cell Block.** Eight cells cut into rock, three occupied. Samara (gladiatorial combatant, six weeks in captivity) knows: Xanathar holds "a magic stone and a marble with a colored center"; the marble is in "the fish tank in the big room with the purple carpet"; and Manshoon's organization is based in a tower in the North Ward (overheard from another prisoner since sent to the arena). The tower information is one of three clue paths to **Kolat Towers**. Two Guild Soldiers watch the cell gate; a third is asleep in a chair. Cell locks are DC 13 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools). If all three prisoners are freed, the two Guild members in custody offer to start a fight at X2 — buying the party 5 minutes before being subdued.

**X12 — Ahmaergo's Quarters.** Organized with obsessive precision: fight records, tournament schedules, arena financial ledgers, a battered sparring sword from Baldur's Gate. On the desk: *Briefing for Xanathar Concerning the Grand Game*. The document covers the current state of the Stone competition — intelligence on the Cassalanters, the Bregan D'aerthe (incomplete), and a physical description of the party accurate enough to confirm they've been watched. Ahmaergo knows who the party is.

**X13 — Thorvin's Workshop.** Cluttered with mechanical components and active engineering projects. Thorvin works here during daylight hours. A gazer floats near the ceiling and watches him constantly. Thorvin communicates via encoded technical-sounding statements — "the third pulley from the north has a load problem" means the third guard station north of X1 is on elevated alert. The party extracts intelligence by asking about specific "mechanical issues." If the gazer is destroyed, Thorvin speaks openly, confirms Nihiloor's projects, and tells the party that the entity running the Guild is not the one making its decisions.

**X16 — Panopticus Chamber.** A circular room with a central crystal array connecting five crystal housings to the lair's sensors by preserved magical filaments. Three dwarves monitor the feeds. The room smells of ozone and something faintly organic. The control runes are visible on the primary housing (DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana) to identify); altering two specific runes (DC 16, 3 minutes) replaces the live feed with a recorded loop. The three dwarves are non-combatants — they surrender immediately if confronted and know which areas have active sensors, what each feed currently shows, and when the last alarm test was run. They do not know about Nihiloor's back door into the system.

**X18 — Audience Chamber.** The lair's largest upper-level room. A high-vaulted chamber with a raised dais; the panopticus sensor here is the widest-field in the lair, covering the entire chamber floor and both side corridors. During an active audience session: 15–25 Guild members waiting for their turn. The door to X19 is behind the dais — two guards flank it during empty periods; four guards flank it during an active session.

**X19 — Xanathar's Sanctum.** A circular room with purple carpet. Personal relics on shelves: small figures, collected baubles, trinkets from significant moments in Xanathar's life. At the center, Sylgar's bowl — a large crystal sphere filled with salt water, algae, decorative rocks, and Sylgar the goldfish. At the bottom of the sphere, among the rocks, glinting faintly through the water's refractions: Eye #1. A small smoky-gray gemstone Ott Steeltoes placed there months ago because it looked like it belonged.

Xanathar is here. The party must extract the Eye with a paranoid beholder in the room — unless they have already used X20.

Extraction without disturbing Sylgar: the sphere has a narrow top opening. DC 12 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) to remove a stone from the bottom without startling the fish. On a failure, Sylgar thrashes; the water splashes audibly. Xanathar snaps to attention: "What happened to Sylgar? What did you do to Sylgar?" If the fish is harmed — even accidentally — Xanathar becomes Hostile regardless of any prior rapport and does not negotiate. He begins with eye rays.

**X20 — Dream Nullifier.** A copper bowl in a maintenance alcove between X19 and X21. Passive Perception 15 to notice while passing. Destroying it: antimagic field or dispel magic (DC 15). Effect: within 3 minutes, Xanathar experiences intrusive beholder-images and leaves X19 in agitation to find Ahmaergo — demanding explanation, seeking confirmation the lair is secure. He moves toward X18 or X12. X19 is empty. The party has approximately 4 minutes.

**X28 — Guild Recreation Hall.** No scrying sensor. Card games, arm wrestling, complaints about Xanathar. Off-duty Guild members here will not scrutinize strangers with a plausible story. The party can rest up to 1 hour, plan next steps, or wait out a patrol sweep.

**X33 — Crypt of Xanathar's Past.** Through the secret door from X6 (DC 17 Perception), a narrow passage leads to a chamber containing the preserved remains and personal effects of every previous beholder who held the title "Xanathar" — predecessors stretching back centuries. Nar'l Xibrindas is the only current Guild member who knows this room exists. The room contains: evidence that "Xanathar" is a title claimed through murder, not inherited; the original Guild charter signed by the first holder; and locked coffer containing the personal journals of the last three Xanathars, including early dealings with Manshoon's predecessors. The revelation that Xanathar is a title implies the current one could be replaced, and that someone may already be planning to do so.

**X35 — Nar'l's Office.** Sparse and organized by a mind that trusts nothing to memory when paper will do. The desk holds Guild operational reports and a coded communication log in Jarlaxle's cipher (DC 16 Intelligence to decode without the key). In a false-bottomed drawer (DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)): the *Report on the Seven Masks Theater* — a summary of Jarlaxle's operational activity through the theater's ownership chain, its connection to the Sea Maidens Faire, and a partial decoding of Jarlaxle's Grand Game position. Nar'l has kept this from Xanathar for eleven months. Finding it is one of three clue paths to **Sea Maidens Faire**.

**X36 — Smokepowder Cache.** A sealed maintenance alcove marked on Thorvin's schematic as "decommissioned drainage junction." Eight smokepowder kegs wrapped in Guild-color cloth. DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation) without Thorvin's schematic; DC 11 with it. The clockwork detonators Thorvin provides take 20 minutes to assemble (DC 15 Dexterity, Thorvin's tools required) and trigger simultaneously via pull-cord from up to 100 feet.

*Nihiloor's Domain (Areas X23–X27).* The secondary objective zone. Rank-and-file avoid this area by standing practice — guards assigned here request reassignment within weeks, citing nightmares and intrusive thoughts. The dread is real: Nihiloor has allowed it to accumulate because it discourages investigation.

- **X23 — Competitive Intelligence Files.** Nihiloor's research on all four villain factions, assembled as preparation for an eventual play for true control. The Manshoon section is the most detailed: intercepted Zhentarim communications, field reports on Kolat Towers' external security, a partial lieutenant roster, and a ground-level map of Kolat Towers. This is one of three clue paths to **Kolat Towers**.
- **X24 — The Infiltration.** Placement records for four intellect devourers inserted into Watch and civic administration positions, with two additional placements pending. Each record: host name, position, insertion date, behavioral integration notes.
- **X25 — The Experiments.** Research notes and twelve living specimens at various modification stages. The notes describe a process for creating psionic relay organisms. Reading them fully requires a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw or the reader gains one level of Exhaustion from the psychological impact.
- **X26 — The Puppet.** A psionic relay device, currently active, maintaining a Dominate Monster effect on a psychic signature that matches Xanathar's. Detailed notes track behavioral corrections applied over four months: which tactical decisions were changed, which Guild members were elevated or removed at Nihiloor's direction, and the long-term strategic repositioning of the Guild toward Nihiloor's actual goals. The beholder who rules the Xanathar Guild does not rule the Xanathar Guild. Every Xanathar decision the party has encountered was Nihiloor's decision.

*Adversary Roster — Standard Configuration.*

Xanathar's location is pre-set per Scene 2. His combat behavior is governed by the two-phase boss stat block in the **Bestiary** (not yet drafted): Phase 1 is the paranoid tyrant at full power; Phase 2 (below half HP) is the cornered megalomaniac — more dangerous in raw eye ray output, but less tactically focused. The ring of invisibility escape and flight toward X19 are designed into the Phase 2 transition. The party can predict this if they gathered intelligence; the **Xanathar** page in Notable Figures confirms Xanathar runs when genuinely frightened.

Ahmaergo occupies X12 during non-tournament periods and patrols the X12–X18 corridor during tournaments. He never retreats while Xanathar is alive in the lair; if Xanathar falls, Ahmaergo fights to the death regardless. Nar'l is in X35 unless pre-arranged otherwise, with grell bodyguard. He covertly assists within cover constraints but will not enter open combat for the party. Nihiloor occupies X26 unless drawn by a specific threat, uses Dominate Monster on the most dangerous combatant, retreats toward X4 at half HP. Noska Ur'gray is in X8 during non-tournament periods, in X6 arena-side during games; responds to alarms with rust monster companion. Ott Steeltoes flees at first sign of violence.

Area-assigned: Guild Soldiers in pairs at X2, X4, X18 (doubled at Alert tier); Xanathar's Guards at X7, door detail at X18, and X19 during Xanathar's absence; Panopticus Dwarves (3) in X16, non-combatants.

*Adversary Roster — Tournament Configuration.*

30–60 civilian spectators in X2, X6, X17, and X18. Xanathar processes from X18 through X17 to X6 every 2–4 hours. Guard contingent at X6 shifts to crowd management rather than intrusion detection during active fights. Ahmaergo is in X12 reviewing records. Noska is arena-side. During the 15-minute interval between fights (arena reset, spectator movement), the panopticus sensors in X6 and X18 are trained on the arena floor and seating — not the service corridors behind the seating. This is the optimal movement window for parties using the gladiatorial transport entry.

*The Alarm Cascade.*

**Stage 1 — Local Alert.** Trigger: panopticus detects unauthorized movement (panopticus not suborned), a guard is attacked, or a Guild member shouts alarm. Response: X16 signals nearest guard station; two Guild Soldiers dispatched to the triggered area. Response time: 1d4+1 minutes.

**Stage 2 — Sector Alert.** Trigger: Stage 1 response finds armed intruders, or a second alarm fires before the first resolves. Response: Noska and rust monster respond to the sector; all doors in the affected area lock (DC 15 Thieves' Tools); Xanathar is notified and moves to X18 if not already there. Response time: 2d4 minutes from Stage 1.

**Stage 3 — Full Alert.** Trigger: a named NPC (Ahmaergo, Nar'l, Noska) confirms combat with intruders. Response: all off-duty Guild members in X28 and X8 mobilize; Ahmaergo leads a team toward the reported position; Xanathar retreats to X19 with four Guild Soldiers. Response time: 1d6 minutes from Stage 2.

**Stage 4 — Xanathar Engaged.** Trigger: Xanathar is directly threatened or Sylgar is disturbed. Response: Xanathar deploys eye rays without tactical prioritization; Ahmaergo abandons his team position and moves directly to X19; the lair's entire combat-capable population converges. The only exits are through them, past them, or under cover of the Jarlaxle distraction.

**Tone.** Operational tension in real time. The party's Scene 2 and 3 choices determine which version of Scene 4 they experience: the quiet infiltration or the running fight. Both paths reach the Eye. They feel completely different.

## Scene 5: Complications and Escape

**Purpose.** The heist's pressure release. What goes wrong, how it escalates, and how the party gets out — managed alongside Jarlaxle's simultaneous heist, which may be helping, competing, or both simultaneously.

**Content.**

*Jarlaxle's Simultaneous Heist.*

This event fires if all three conditions are met: (1) Jarlaxle is active in the Grand Game (conditional on **Fireball!** Sea Maidens Faire investigation), (2) the party has not allied with Jarlaxle or joined Bregan D'aerthe, and (3) the party has not yet recovered Eye #3 in **Sea Maidens Faire**.

Jarlaxle's team — himself, one BD lieutenant, and a response team — uses whichever entry method the party did not. His specific advantage: Nar'l's reports over eleven years gave him knowledge of the secret passage at X14, which bypasses the panopticus entirely and opens forty feet from the Sanctum door.

| Party State | Jarlaxle's Action | Result |
|-------------|------------------|--------|
| Undetected, moving toward X19 | Launches a distraction — a loud "accident" at X4 drawing guards and giving X16 two contradictory feeds | Party benefits from the confusion; may encounter BD operatives in corridors and mistake them for enemies |
| Detected, cascade active | Exploits the chaos the party created to rush X14 → X19 | Race to the Eye; whoever arrives at X19 first claims it |
| Allied with Nar'l | Nar'l tips Jarlaxle; BD team avoids X19 for 20 minutes | Party may discover this coordination from Nar'l's behavior |
| Party in X19 when Jarlaxle arrives | Three-way: PCs, Jarlaxle, Xanathar (present unless X20 was used) | Jarlaxle immediately negotiates — he'd rather split benefit than fight. His offer: the Eye for a favor to be named later. He does not negotiate down from this |
| Party has the Eye, escaping | Jarlaxle withdraws his team | Clean exit. But Jarlaxle knows what they took, and **Sea Maidens Faire**'s stakes are now shaped by this |

The simultaneous heist is a pressure-release valve. If the party's heist collapses at Stage 3 or 4, Jarlaxle's distraction pulls heat and opens a window. If the heist fails entirely and Jarlaxle claims the Eye, the campaign does not end. It redirects. **Sea Maidens Faire** becomes a recovery operation with the Eye in Jarlaxle's possession. The party enters **Sea Maidens Faire** from behind, under pressure, with less leverage. This is a consequence.

*The Cascade in Progress.*

Once Stage 3 fires, the party has approximately 4 minutes before Ahmaergo's team reaches their reported location. Use this window to complete the extraction and reach an exit, destroy the dream nullifier to create a secondary distraction, coordinate with Jarlaxle's team if encountered, or detonate the smokepowder option (if prepared in Scene 3). The cascade timing is exact and pre-specified; the DM does not need to improvise escalation decisions.

*Escape Routes.*

**Route 1 — Entry Return.** Fastest if the party is undetected or at Stage 1. Follow the entry route back out. Each cascade stage adds one patrol encounter (DC 14 Stealth to pass undetected) per route segment.

**Route 2 — Skullport Tunnels via X4.** Longer but routes around the lair's internal guard network entirely. At Alert tier and above, X4 itself is guarded by a full response team; the party must defeat or bypass them before accessing the tunnels.

**Route 3 — Undermountain Level 1.** Emergency use only. The maintenance tunnel through X4 connects to Undermountain Level 1 approximately 600 feet below the lair. The party is safe from Xanathar, but now in Undermountain, which has its own conditions. This route is a designed fail-forward path: the campaign continues into the Undermountain premise prematurely, which is a consequence the party should understand before choosing it.

**Tone.** Chaos, managed. Everything the party planned for is tested simultaneously. Jarlaxle may be helping, competing, or both. The cascade is advancing. The Eye may or may not be in hand. The scene is designed to feel overwhelming but not hopeless; every exit remains available if the party moves decisively.

## Scene 6: Aftermath

**Purpose.** Resolve the heist. Insert the Eye. The Stone of Golorr wakes for the first time. Faction states shift. The party debriefs, recovers, and pivots toward the next target.

**Content.**

*Eye #1 Recovery.* The Eye is a smoky-gray gemstone the size of a large marble. Beside the Stone of Golorr, it is obviously the same material, like a piece broken from the Stone's surface and polished separately. It seats into one of three oval sockets on the Stone's face. The fit is exact. There is a resonant tone, felt more than heard, and then the Stone changes.

*Stone of Golorr Upgrade — Three Scenarios.*

**If this is the first Eye restored (neither **Cassalanter Villa** nor **Sea Maidens Faire** completed).** This is the scene's centerpiece. The DM should pause the action here. The party has been carrying an object. They are about to discover they've been carrying a consciousness.

The attuned character hears a voice for the first time. It is not warm. It is old and precise, like water that has been underground for a long time. It does not say hello. It says what it means and stops.

**Confirmation.** "Two remain." No elaboration. No empathy. The Stone states the fact.

**Sharpened Impressions.** The fragmentary images from **Faction Outposts**, Scene 1 clarify. The "red light through colored glass, incense smoke, children's laughter" resolves: the Stone shows a villa in the Sea Ward — a large private estate with a temple beneath it, stone floor engraved with a sigil the party may recognize as the Cassalanter house mark. The children's laughter has a hollow quality now. The "salt air and creaking wood, dark-skinned elf" resolves: a ship in Waterdeep harbor, flagged with a theater company's banner. A bald elf stands at the prow, watching the city with the patience of someone who has been waiting a long time and expects to wait longer. Parties who hit the Sea Maidens Faire or Cassalanter outposts in **Faction Outposts** will connect these images immediately. Parties who didn't have enough to investigate.

**The Stone's Agenda.** Unprompted, the Stone adds one more thing: "I want to be whole." The phrasing is flat, not pleading. A DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana) check suggests the Stone is sentient but alien — it does not experience wants the way a person does. It experiences an incomplete state and the compulsion toward a complete one. Its cooperation with the party is overlap of interest, not loyalty. When the interests diverge, the Stone will not help.

**What It Does Not Say.** The vault's location, the command phrase, and the precise position of either remaining Eye (that requires 2 Eyes). The Stone communicates more than it did. It does not communicate everything.

**Mad Mage Seed.** For one moment — brief, involuntary — the attuned character experiences the Stone's communication as something vaster than a Stone. Not just old. Something that once dreamed, much further below, in the dark under the dark. The impression passes in seconds. The Stone says nothing about it. The DM should not explain it. It is a seed, and it belongs to Undermountain.

**If this is the second Eye restored (one of **Cassalanter Villa** or **Sea Maidens Faire** completed).** The Stone's voice deepens. It becomes more communicative — almost conversational, still alien and cold. New disclosures:

- "One remains."
- The remaining Eye's location resolves fully. Which impression sharpens depends on which arc the party completed first. If **Cassalanter Villa** was first (already raided), the final impression is: "A ship in the harbor. The Scarlet Marpenoth. A bald elf who has been patient for eleven years." If **Sea Maidens Faire** was first (Jarlaxle's Eye already recovered), the final impression is: "A villa in the Sea Ward. A temple beneath it, stone floor engraved with the Cassalanter house mark. The children's laughter has a hollow quality." The Stone identifies each Eye by proximity-sense, not by name.
- The Vault's existence acknowledged: the Stone confirms it knows where the Vault is and that three Eyes will unlock the full knowledge. It does not reveal the location or command phrase.
- The Stone's agenda deepens: "I am remembering what I was." The aboleth consciousness bleeds through more strongly — references to deep water, dreaming minds, geological patience. The Mad Mage seed intensifies.
- What it does NOT reveal: the vault location, the command phrase, and Aurinax's nature. These require all three Eyes.

**If this is the third Eye restored (both **Cassalanter Villa** and **Sea Maidens Faire** already completed).** The Stone goes still, then speaks with sudden, precise clarity. The fragmentary impressions are gone. The vault's location, the command phrase, and the nature of its guardian resolve in full. Run the 3-Eye disclosure scene from **Vault of Dragons** at this point. Quest order does not change the awakening, only its timing.

*Faction State Changes.*

**Xanathar Guild:** Set the "Xanathar operational?" flag to one of three states:

- *YES — Xanathar alive, Eye taken:* He doesn't know immediately what was stolen. He notices Sylgar's bowl was disturbed within hours. When he identifies what is missing — days of interrogation later — his response will be disproportionate. The party is now personally known to Xanathar. This intensifies **Vault of Dragons**'s difficulty.
- *WOUNDED — Xanathar alive, fought and fled:* As above, plus Ahmaergo takes over lair security. Xanathar is less effective in **Vault of Dragons** but more enraged.
- *GONE — Lair demolished or Xanathar killed:* Guild collapses into Ahmaergo-led factional fighting. Response teams disbanded. Nihiloor escapes to Undermountain Level 17. The party permanently loses Guild intelligence as a resource. **Vault of Dragons**'s approach routes through former Guild contacts who are now independent actors.

**Manshoon's Zhentarim Splinter.** Monitoring via agents watching the lair's entrances. If Xanathar is weakened or eliminated, Manshoon moves within days to absorb abandoned Guild territory. His alert tier increases if the party was observed.

**Cassalanters.** Imp surveillance detects unusual activity around Skullport entry points. If the relationship with the party is adversarial, Victoro's network flags the event. The Cassalanters don't know what was retrieved, but they know something changed.

**Bregan D'aerthe.** If Jarlaxle's simultaneous heist fired: he knows the outcome. He will not contact the party immediately; he processes before communicating. Either way, **Sea Maidens Faire**'s stakes are now shaped by this quest's result.

*All-Seven-Faction Debriefs.* Key questions each contact raises:

- **Mirt:** "Is Thorvin alive?" Genuine concern. Then: "What did you find in the beholder's private rooms?" He suspects Nihiloor. He does not say so directly.
- **Jalester:** "How did the Guild respond? Are their patrol routes disrupted?" Lords' Alliance interest: Guild operational status affects city security assessments.
- **Melannor:** "Did anything unusual happen to the underground water systems near the collapse point?" He noticed something in the environmental readings. He doesn't have context.
- **Savra:** "Were prisoners recovered?" Warm and direct. If Samara was freed: genuine relief. If not: quiet grief, followed by asking whether they were alive when last seen.
- **Vajra:** "Tell me about the panopticus. All of it." She already suspects what Nihiloor might be doing. She needs specific evidence before bringing it to the Open Lord.
- **Tashlyn or Davil:** "Is Xanathar still operational?" Direct and tactical — the Doom Raiders' expansion into former Guild territory depends on the answer. "And Ahmaergo?"
- **Jarlaxle (if contacted):** Characteristically oblique. "I hear there was excitement near Skullport last night." If pressed on whether he was there: "I am always where I'm needed." He never confirms. He never denies.

*Founders' Day Clock.* This quest consumes 1–2 tendays total: several days of intelligence briefing and casing (Scenes 1–3), 1–3 days for the operation and escape (Scenes 4–5), and 2–4 days of recovery and debrief (Scene 6). Update the campaign calendar. The DM communicates the advancing deadline through specific environmental details: festival preparations in the Market Ward are accelerating; a Cassalanter document recovered from the lair carries a date; a faction contact mentions the Open Lord's Founders' Day schedule is filling.

*Transition.* The party holds the Stone of Golorr and Eye #1. The Stone can talk. It wants to be whole. Two Eyes remain; the sharpened impressions point toward a Sea Ward villa and a ship in the harbor. **Cassalanter Villa**, **Sea Maidens Faire**, and **Kolat Towers** are available in any order. The party's next choice is informed by the Stone's impressions, the faction debrief intelligence, and whatever documents they recovered from the lair.

**Tone.** Decompression, then revelation. The heist is over. The Stone speaks for the first time — cold, precise, and alien. The party's relationship to what they're carrying has changed. It is not an object. It is a transaction. The remaining targets are coming into focus. The deadline is closer. The campaign is accelerating.

# Design Notes: Xanathar's Lair

## Genre Identity

This quest must feel like infiltration rather than dungeon crawl, and the distinction matters mechanically, not just aesthetically. A dungeon crawl rewards fighting through encounters in sequence; an infiltration rewards planning, patience, and exploitation of specific weaknesses. If the party treats this quest as a dungeon crawl — enter, fight whatever they find, proceed to the next room — the alarm cascade is the consequence. It doesn't kill them immediately, but it creates pressure that compounds with every Stage: more guards, faster response times, fewer options. The party that fights its way to X19 is running a race against escalation rather than executing a plan.

The panopticus is the mechanism that enforces this distinction. Suborning X16 before moving into the lair's core converts the surveillance network from a threat into a solved problem. Failing to suborn it limits routing so severely that reaching X19 through the main corridors requires passing through active scrying coverage — at that point, detection is a matter of when, not whether. The heist framework penalizes the dungeon-crawl approach with compounding difficulty while rewarding the infiltration approach with compounding advantage. This is the correct calibration.

## The Panopticus as Player-Facing Mechanic

The panopticus has been written as a system the players interact with, not a background feature the DM manages. A common approach in published adventures makes surveillance systems invisible to players and uses them as a DM tool for applying pressure — enemies appear from unexpected directions, reinforcements arrive faster than expected. This creates frustration rather than tension. Players feel the consequences of a system they could not interact with, which reads as unfairness rather than challenge.

In this quest, the panopticus is a puzzle with visible pieces. The party can learn that sensors exist (through passive detection or faction briefings), learn which areas are monitored, understand the detection consequence, find the control room, and execute a solution. This converts the surveillance from an imposed threat into a problem the players can solve. The dramatic tension comes not from whether they'll be detected by something they don't know about, but from whether their plan for the thing they do know about will work.

## Eye #1 in the Fishbowl

Eye #1 has been intentionally placed in Sylgar's fishbowl rather than a vault, safe, or guarded chamber. The reason is characterization.

Xanathar does not know the Eye's value. Ott Steeltoes thought it was a pretty stone and placed it where Xanathar likes his fish's habitat to look interesting. It has been sitting there for months, protected less by paranoid security than by the fact that nobody knows it's there. This placement tells the party more about Xanathar's nature in one beat than any amount of descriptive prose: a being of tremendous power who keeps a world-altering artifact as an inadvertent pet decoration.

Xanathar is in X19 when the party arrives. This is not conditional on tier or timing — it is the design. The question is not whether Xanathar is there but whether the party has found X20.

A party that found the dream nullifier through Thorvin's guidance or onsite reconnaissance has a tool: destroy it, wait three minutes, follow Xanathar out of the room, and extract the Eye with four minutes of silence. A party that didn't find X20 must extract the Eye while a paranoid beholder watches them. Both paths reach the same moment — the Sleight of Hand check against the fishbowl — but the first is deliberate and timed, and the second is improvised and desperate. This is the correct two-track design for a heist arc: preparation should matter, and the party that prepared should feel it.

Making the rule that harming Sylgar turns Xanathar immediately and irreversibly Hostile is correct: it reflects that Xanathar values Sylgar above guild loyalty, above tactical self-interest, above anything else in the lair. Violating that should carry the most severe possible consequence.

## The Stone Speaks

The 1-Eye upgrade has been written as a scene, not a mechanical footnote. The Alexandrian baseline is telepathic communication; the remix adds a specific voice quality, a defined personality, a disclosed agenda, and a calibrated information release. The Stone's personality derives from its nature: a compressed aboleth consciousness, experienced in geological time, operating through a medium of incomplete information. It does not experience the world the way a person does. It communicates what it has calculated is necessary. When communication is not necessary, it is silent.

Making the Stone's awakening a moment — not just an item description update — changes the campaign's emotional texture from this point forward. The party is no longer carrying a MacGuffin. They are in a transaction with something that has its own interests, its own logic, and an agenda that currently overlaps with theirs. When the interests diverge, the Stone will communicate exactly what it is prepared to say and nothing more. This creates a different relationship with the artifact than "useful tool the party found." It creates something closer to an uneasy alliance with a party member who is ancient, cold, and patient in ways the party is not equipped to measure.

The Mad Mage seed planted here — the brief impression of something vast and dreaming, far below — is a load-bearing element for Undermountain. The party won't have context for it until Undermountain Level 17. It should land as texture here: something the attuned character experienced and may or may not mention, not a revelation the DM explains. Leave it unresolved.

## Jarlaxle's Simultaneous Heist

The simultaneous heist exists for three reasons, and all three are required to justify its design complexity.

First, dramatic tension: the party should not be the only competent actor in Waterdeep. Jarlaxle has better lair intelligence than the party; he has had a spy embedded for eleven years. The heist's outcome is not guaranteed to favor the party simply because they are the protagonists.

Second, failure-forward design: a heist that can fail completely needs a designed resolution that keeps the campaign moving. Jarlaxle claiming the Eye redirects the story to **Sea Maidens Faire** as a recovery operation rather than ending it. The party enters **Sea Maidens Faire** from behind, with less leverage, under pressure. This is a consequence.

Third, **Sea Maidens Faire** setup: whether Jarlaxle got the Eye or didn't, **Sea Maidens Faire**'s stakes are directly shaped by this quest's outcome. A party that beat Jarlaxle to the Eye negotiates from strength. A party that didn't negotiates from weakness. **Sea Maidens Faire** plays differently depending on which way this went, which means this quest's outcome creates meaningful forward variation.

The three trigger conditions have been carefully set to ensure the heist fires only when it creates the right dramatic conditions. If the party has allied with Jarlaxle or is BD members themselves, running his team against them is wrong. If **Sea Maidens Faire** is already complete, the Eye situation is resolved and the heist is moot.

## The Smokepowder Choice

The smokepowder option is presented without advocacy. The text does not celebrate it or discourage it. It states the costs alongside the benefits and lets the party decide.

The case for it is real: a villain faction eliminated, a dangerous location made safe, a complicated enemy relationship resolved with finality. The case against it is also real: permanent loss of Guild intelligence, Nihiloor freed from his puppet's leash and operating unconstrained from Undermountain, Nar'l and Thorvin killed unless the party explicitly evacuates them, the Undermountain connection severed before the campaign descends. The party that destroys the lair is trading a faction they understand — paranoid, predictable, with known structures and exploitable NPCs — for an unknown quantity in Nihiloor, who is smarter than Xanathar, does not have Xanathar's attachment to a goldfish, and has been running the Guild in secret for months without anyone noticing.

This is a real trade.

## Nihiloor's Three Projects

Nihiloor's three projects have been written as discoverable, not required. The party completes the heist by reaching X19 and extracting the Eye — nothing in X23–X27 is needed for that. What the secondary objective zone offers is the campaign's deepest active conspiracy, available to parties thorough enough to explore a dangerous area that even the Guild's guards avoid.

The Puppet revelation is the most significant: Xanathar has been Nihiloor's instrument for four months. Every aggressive, paranoid, apparently irrational decision attributed to the beholder in **Faction Outposts** and this quest was Nihiloor's decision. This changes the party's understanding retroactively. The mastermind they've been treating as the main threat was itself controlled by something worse. The real threat from the Xanathar Guild faction was never Xanathar.

The Manshoon intelligence in X23 is a designed cross-quest clue. Three independent clue paths lead to **Kolat Towers**: Samara's testimony in X7 (a tower in the North Ward), Nihiloor's intelligence files in X23 (partial Kolat Towers layout and lieutenant roster), and a third path seeded in **Cassalanter Villa**. The Three Clue Rule is satisfied across quests, not within a single location. This is the correct application of the rule in a campaign where quests run in variable order.

## Xanathar's Boss Stat Block

Xanathar uses a custom two-phase boss stat block rather than the 2024 Monster Manual beholder entry. The two-phase structure reflects his psychological collapse under direct threat: Phase 1 is the paranoid tyrant at full power; Phase 2 (below half HP) is the cornered megalomaniac — more dangerous in raw output, less tactically controlled. The ring of invisibility escape and flight toward X19 are designed into the Phase 2 transition, not left to DM improvisation. A party that gathered intelligence knows to expect this; the **Xanathar** page in Notable Figures confirms Xanathar runs when genuinely frightened.

The purpose of the two-phase design is to give Xanathar a combat identity that reflects character rather than just capability. A beholder is already terrifying by the numbers. The specific flavor of Xanathar's fight — the specific eye rays he prioritizes, the moment he breaks and runs, what he screams when he runs — comes from who he is. That specificity distinguishes this fight from any other beholder encounter the party might have.

*The stat block itself will be built in the **Bestiary** (not yet drafted) using `/boss-design`.*

## Variable Difficulty and Escalation State

The four-tier difficulty table translates **Faction Outposts**' outpost consequences into this quest's environment without requiring DM judgment. The reason this must be table-specified is that it rewards player decisions in **Faction Outposts** with concrete, legible consequences here. If hitting outposts changes nothing about the lair, the party learns that outpost actions don't matter. If hitting outposts makes the lair harder in specifically defined ways they can discover through reconnaissance, the party learns that their actions have weight. The tier table makes this legible, repeatable, and consistent regardless of which DM runs it.

## Faction Hooks

**Harpers.** Mirt wants Nihiloor dead — three Harper assets have been compromised by the mind flayer's intellect devourers, and the identities of those assets are not something he will disclose. He offers active tactical support for the lair operation contingent on the party committing, before they enter, to eliminating Nihiloor permanently rather than merely extracting the Eye. Accepting this condition shapes the party's approach to X23–X27: bypassing Nihiloor's domain is no longer the low-risk play. See the Quest Hooks section of the **Harpers** Factions guide page.

**Emerald Enclave.** Before the party enters Xanathar's lair, Jeryth Phaulkon provides an enchanted herb sprig that functions as a mild psychic shield. The holder gains advantage on the first saving throw made against an implanted intellect devourer, usable once. Jeryth asks that it be carried into the lair specifically — the enchantment is keyed to the environment's psychic signature and does not function elsewhere. See the Quest Hooks section of the **Emerald Enclave** Factions guide page.

**Order of the Gauntlet.** Savra Belabranta considers the Xanathar Guild's use of intellect devourers a specific theological offense — aberrant intelligence overwriting natural souls. She offers to accompany the party into Xanathar's lair as her Renown 3 companion benefit (once per quest), but only if the party commits to eliminating Nihiloor permanently rather than bypassing him. If that commitment cannot be made, she does not join. The offer is genuine; so is the condition. See the Quest Hooks section of the **Order of the Gauntlet** Factions guide page.

**Force Grey.** Force Grey Mission 4 ("The Spawning Pool") is not a preparatory mission for this quest — it is this quest's operation, run as part of the same lair raid. The Spawning Pool is inside Xanathar's lair; the mission and the quest cover the same event from complementary vantages. If Mission 4 was completed before this quest, the Spawning Pool encounter is already resolved when the party enters. If this quest fires first, Mission 4's objectives are embedded in Scene 4's operation and resolve there. The DM does not need to stage both as separate events. See the Quest Hooks section of the **Force Grey** Factions guide page.
