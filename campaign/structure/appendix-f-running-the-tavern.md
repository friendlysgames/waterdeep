# Appendix F — Managing Trollskull Manor

Trollskull Tavern is the party's home in Waterdeep — rebuilt with their own coin, their own labor, and the uneasy blessing of a poltergeist bartender who has been waiting for new owners since 1479 DR. This appendix gives that investment mechanical and narrative weight.

**Source:** `sources/Appendix_D_-_Running_the_Tavern.md` — the full polished content, including all narrative callouts, staff candidate profiles, and faction visit scenes. This structure draft organizes and cross-references that content; the artifact renders it.

---

## Part 1: Operating Costs

Three expense categories recur every tenday. Guild fees and staff wages together form the **base 60 gp/tenday** figure established during Act I. Supply costs sit on top and scale with Fame.

**Unpaid Debt rule:** Every 1 gp of unpaid debt subtracts 1 from the next Revenue Roll.

---

### Category 1: Guild Fees (Fixed — 10 gp/tenday)

Non-negotiable in ordinary business. Missing a payment escalates: formal notice → supply disruption → license review.

| Guild | Obligation | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Fellowship of Innkeepers | Operating license | 3 gp |
| Launderers' Guild | Linen service | 2 gp |
| Dungsweepers' Guild | Street cleanup | 1 gp |
| Loyal Order of Street Laborers | Exterior upkeep | 1 gp |
| Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors | Fire-suppression ward | 2 gp |
| Stablemasters' and Farriers' Guild | Courtyard access | 1 gp |
| **Total** | | **10 gp/tenday** |

**Faction reductions (active standing required — missions running, not just enrolled):**
- **Harpers (Renown 3+):** Mirt arranges −1 gp/tenday on the Launderers' line. Binds linen supply into Harper-adjacent networks.
- **Lords' Alliance:** Jalester's civic designation reduces Fellowship line from 3 gp to 1 gp (−2 gp/tenday). Any Watch citation resets this.
- **Emerald Enclave:** Enclave druids replace the Watchful Order ward, eliminating that 2 gp/tenday fee. Monthly visits from a druid who will have opinions about the oak tree in the courtyard.
- **Doom Raiders:** Istrid Horn's bulk agreements reduce supply costs (Category 3), not fees directly.

Maximum reduction through faction leverage: **5 gp/tenday** (floor of 5 gp/tenday). Stacking all three requires simultaneous standing with Harpers, Lords' Alliance, and Emerald Enclave.

#### Lif the Poltergeist Bartender

If appeased during Act I, Lif tends bar for free. Communicates by gesture, chalk slate, and atmospheric emphasis. His signature drink — **The Half-Elf** (two-layer spirit-and-ale, layers never mix) — adds +2 to the Revenue Roll any tenday he is tending bar and it is on the menu.

If not appeased, Lif does not prevent the party from hiring a living bartender. See bartender candidates below.

---

#### Staff Candidates

When a position is open, candidates arrive within a day. The DM chooses which appear based on campaign needs, or rolls 1d4 among options 1–4 (option 5 always appears last if the position is still open after three days).

**Running the Interviews:** The hiring process is a low-stakes social encounter that teaches players Waterdeep is full of people with agendas. Each candidate has something they're hiding or something they want. PCs who ask the right questions — or succeed on a **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight) check** during the conversation — catch the tells. Those who hire on first impressions deserve what they get.

Not every candidate needs a full scene. Present the appearance, let the candidate speak a line or two, and watch who the players fixate on. Develop the ones they ask follow-up questions about; decline the rest with a handshake. **DC 13 Insight:** reveals the candidate's hidden quality or agenda. **DC 20:** reveals the specific secret. **Failure:** candidate reads as broadly trustworthy or broadly untrustworthy at the DM's discretion — not specifically.

### Category 3: Supply Costs (Variable; not included in 60 gp base)

Raw materials the tavern sells. Scale with volume, quality, and Fame tier. Paid against revenue, not as flat overhead.

**Cash flow rule:** Supply costs must be available at the *start* of each tenday. If unavailable: tavern operates at reduced capacity, −15 to Revenue Roll that tenday.

**Premium stock option:** Spend 20% above baseline for that Fame tier → +5 Revenue that tenday. Do this for 3 consecutive tendays → +1 Fame.

| Fame Tier | Fame Score | Est. Nightly Covers | Supply Cost/Tenday |
|---|---|---|---|
| Condemned | −50 to −20 | 3–8 | 8–15 gp |
| Struggling | −19 to −1 | 8–18 | 15–30 gp |
| Known | 0–30 | 18–35 | 30–50 gp |
| Respected | 31–80 | 30–40 | 48–65 gp |
| Fashionable | 81–150 | 35–40 | 62–80 gp |
| Renowned | 151–350 | 35–40 (+overflow) | 75–95 gp |
| Institution | 351+ | Fully booked nightly | 90–110 gp |

**DM guidance:** Don't track supply costs to the gold piece every session. Use them as a narrative lever — two tendays away adventuring, the cook ran supplies low, 40 gp owed to the Vintners' Guild. The number matters most when the room stops filling from faction pressure.

### Faction Renovation Assistance (Act I)

The 1,250 gp renovation cost is not small for a 2nd-level party. What each faction offers:

- **Harpers (Mirt):** Personal loan up to 500 gp, no stated interest — repayable as a favor when useful. Alternatively, supplier discounts of 15–20% via Harper-adjacent guild networks (binds supply chain, no personal debt to Mirt).
- **Lords' Alliance (Jalester):** Civic grant up to 200 gp from Laeral's discretionary fund. Ongoing compliance oversight — Jalester checks in.

## Part 2: The Reputation Track (Fame)

Fame represents the tavern's standing in Waterdeep's social geography. Numerical score, tracked tenday by tenday. Range: **−50** (condemned) to **351+** (institution).

Fame modifies Revenue Rolls and shapes NPC behavior. It can't be spent or traded.

**Design note:** The original WDH system (1 gp promotion spend = +1 to business roll) is replaced by this track. Famous taverns attract notable patrons; notable patrons cause memorable events; memorable events build more fame. Staff choices, faction incidents, and what happens in the common room have mechanical teeth.

---

### Starting Fame

When the Grand Opening occurs, roll 1d6 and add bonuses earned through Act I preparation:

| Preparation | Bonus |
|---|---|
| Lif appeased and tending bar | +2 |
| Promotion spending (flyers, word-of-mouth via NPCs) | +1 per PC-tenday spent (max +3) |
| Notable NPC personally invited and attended | +1 per NPC (max +3) |
| Live music at Grand Opening | +1 |
| Special menu item at Grand Opening | +1 |
| Public disruption or violent incident during opening | −3 |
| Lif caused a violent scene (unappeased) | −2 |

### Fame Thresholds

A well-staffed tavern (Lif + Maren + Mattrim + Pell + Hadra's first 3 tendays) accumulates 5–6 Fame/tenday from staff contributions alone. The early tiers reflect natural momentum; the higher tiers require active management.

**Calibration:** At 5 Fame/tenday average with no special investment, the journey from 0 to Institution takes ~70 tendays. With active play (events, faction meetings, notable patron moments), 50–60. Either way, it spans most of the campaign. Passive staff gains will plateau the tavern somewhere in Fashionable; reaching Renowned and Institution requires the party to treat the tavern as a social project.

## Part 3: Revenue and Costs

### The Revenue Roll

At the end of every tenday: **roll d100 + 10 + Fame modifier + Promotion modifier − Unpaid Debt modifier**.

- **Promotion modifier:** +1 per 1 gp spent on promotion (flyers, city criers, drinks-on-the-house nights), max +20.
- **Unpaid Debt modifier:** −1 per 1 gp outstanding.
- **Faction Pressure Penalty:** When a faction actively disrupts the tavern's neighbourhood, −10 to next 1d3 rolls.

### Revenue Table

| d100 + Modifiers | Gross Revenue | Flavour |
|:---:|---|---|
| 1–20 | 2d6 × 5 gp (avg 35 gp) | Dire stretch. Doors were open; almost no one came in. |
| 21–30 | 3d6 × 5 gp (avg 52 gp) | Poor takings. Handful of regulars, thin nights. |
| 31–40 | 4d6 × 5 gp (avg 70 gp) | Below average. Trading, but not filling. |
| 41–60 | 6d6 × 5 gp (avg 105 gp) | Average. Common room fills most evenings; regulars spend normally. |
| 61–80 | 8d6 × 5 gp (avg 140 gp) | Solid tenday. Word is spreading; new faces; till comes in ahead. |
| 81–90 | 10d8 × 5 gp (avg 225 gp) | Strong. Room fills early and stays full. Premium stock moves. |
| 91–110 | 10d10 × 5 gp (avg 275 gp) | Excellent. People make a point of coming here instead of anywhere else. |
| 111+ | 10d12 × 5 gp (avg 325 gp) | Exceptional. Gain +1 Fame. Something clicked. These don't happen often. |

### Net Profit Calculation

Subtract in this order from gross revenue:

1. **Fixed Costs (60 gp/tenday):** Guild fees + staff wages. Adjust for faction leverage.
2. **Variable Supply Costs:** Per Fame tier table (Part 1). Note if premium stock was purchased.
3. **Incidental Losses:**
   - Orryn Tetch skimming: −2 gp/tenday until caught
   - Pell Ashford breakage: −1d6 gp/tenday
   - Brawl with no bouncer: −2d6 × 5 gp furniture damage
   - Staff dismissed mid-tenday: full tenday wage still owed
4. **Loan Repayments:** Off the bottom line, before the party takes anything.

**Net Profit = Gross − Fixed − Supply − Incidentals**

Negative result → Unpaid Debt, subtracts from next Revenue Roll.

**Context:** At Known tier (~40 gp supply, ~100 gp total costs), an average roll (avg 105 gp) yields ~5 gp net. A solid roll yields ~40 gp net. Startup costs of 1,250 gp won't recoup from average Known-tier rolls — the tavern is a home base and social engine through Arcs B–E, not a profit center. Consistent strong rolling at Respected tier recovers startup investment in ~15–20 tendays.

**DM guidance:** Don't run the Revenue Roll every session. When the party is deep in an investigation arc, run it in the background and report the result in a line. The roll matters most during the early game when money is tight, and during any period when faction pressure is actively disrupting the neighbourhood. After Fame 31+ (Respected), hand-wave the roll unless something dramatic happened that tenday.

## Part 4: Tavern Time

Use this four-step procedure for each significant night the characters spend at the tavern.

**Step 1 — Check for an Event.** Roll 1d6. On a 1, an Event occurs tonight. At Fame 81+ (Fashionable), Events occur on a 1–2.

**Step 2 — Determine Significant Patrons.** Roll 1d6 for the number of significant patrons present. Roll on the Patron Table (below) for each. Duplicate results mean that patron is more central to the evening than usual.

**Step 3 — Establish Topics and Agendas.** Look up Topics/Agendas for each patron. Choose one topic per patron, or select a single topic to drive the evening. Default to the first unplayed topic.

**Step 4 — Let It Run.** The party decides how to engage. Two strong interactions are worth more than five rushed ones. Look for collisions between patrons — that's where the interesting moments live.

- **Auto-resolved with the right staff:** A Bouncer on duty resolves brawls and arm-wrestling automatically. A Master Cook or experienced Cook handles kitchen Events. Lif handles most drunk-patron disturbances.

- Asks if the party left a message at the shop for Variel Duskwhisper — he received two messages for that name and isn't sure which is which
- Vincent Trench has been in the shop twice this tenday. Both times buying books on planar binding. Rishaal is not worried. He is mentioning it.

**Jarlaxle Baenre** *(Bregan D'aerthe, NE male drow; see Appendix A and Act III)*
*Always in disguise. Different disguise each visit. Always the best seat without seeming to choose it.*
Finds everything interesting; asks exactly one question per conversation he already knows the answer to; never orders the same thing twice.

**Running Jarlaxle at the bar:** He visits because the party interests him — they are his most unpredictable variable. Every visit should leave the party wondering what he wanted and whether they gave it to him. Play him as delighted, not menacing. The delight is worse. Drow PCs feel his attention differently — he has specific and complicated interest in drow operating independently on the surface.

- Compliments something specific about the tavern — Lif's pour depth, the spirit selection — then sits in comfortable silence as if he's said everything necessary
- Business proposition: "If you were going to move something — something that couldn't go through official channels — across the city without the Watch knowing, how would you do it?" Listens carefully. Does not say whether it's hypothetical.
- Drops one specific accurate detail about another faction's current operations, between other subjects, without explanation or ask
- Drops the disguise almost — a gesture wrong for the persona, a phrase in Elvish that doesn't fit. Watches to see if the party notices. Either way, he smiles.

## Part 7: Faction Response Teams at the Tavern

Response Teams are detailed fully in Appendix E (Villain Factions). This section covers how each team behaves specifically at the tavern — when they come, what they want, what the visit looks like at the bar rather than in an alley.

**Ground rule:** Response Teams do not attack the tavern unprovoked while customers are present. A public massacre in a licensed North Ward establishment invites City Guard, the Watchful Order, and potentially Vajra Safahr personally within the hour. What they do instead is visit. The manner of the visit tells the party exactly where they stand.

**Exception:** Noska Ur'gray's bugbear team. Noska is Xanathar's enforcer, not his diplomat. Bugbears do not read the social calendar.

**Direct attack conditions:** Attacks come at night, after hours, when the tavern is empty or nearly so — and only if the party has taken an Eye and it's been tracked to this address, or they've publicly humiliated a faction leader in a way demanding visible response.

---

### Xanathar Guild — Gorath's Duergar

*The Guild does not negotiate. It sends lessons.*

**Who comes:** Gorath (four duergar in unremarkable clothes). If Gorath is neutralized: Korgstrod Uxgulm's team (half-ogre + cloaked gazer). Noska Ur'gray does not visit — he arrives with intent.

**Triggers:**
- Party has discovered, looted, or interfered with a Xanathar outpost or operation
- A Guild plant as staff (Tobrin Ashvale, Grevik Nass) has been exposed
- Party has the Stone of Golorr or an Eye and the Guild knows it

**Early campaign (Acts I–II):** Four duergar, same time, sit at two tables covering door and stair. One looks at the party once with a filed-tooth smile. Leaves exact coin. No tip. No return that night. The Guild knows where you live.

**Active campaign (Acts II–IV, once the Guild marks the party as a threat):** Gorath sits at the bar with his back to the room, orders something good, and says: "You cost us something. We want it back. Or we want something worth the same. You have until the new moon." Then leaves. His team is outside.

**What the party can do:** Negotiate, return goods (if goods rather than information), or prepare for Korgstrod's team. No social check makes the Guild forget what they lost. Exchange or escalation.

**Fame impact:** Handled without public incident: 0. Visibly intimidated and regulars notice: −1. Handled calmly and team leaves without trouble: +1.

---

### Manshoon's Zhentarim — Vevette Blackwater

*Manshoon's people are the weakest response team. They're still capable of doing damage.*

**Who comes:** Vevette Blackwater (CE swashbuckler, fiery red hair with two platinum streaks) — alone or with two thugs in decent clothes. Charming, extracts information through conversation before trying anything else. Urstul Floxin only visits if specifically hunting the party for the Gralhund Villa incident.

**Triggers:**
- Party interfered with the Gralhund Villa operation or disrupted Manshoon's Stone acquisition
- Party identified as potential Eye sellers — Manshoon looks for people who might deal
- Party allied with the Doom Raiders; Manshoon wants to know how close that alliance is

**Early contact:** She was inside before the party noticed her. On her second drink. When she catches them looking, raises her glass. "Your bartender makes a very good case for this place." Then: "The people I work for are curious about something you may have found recently. I'm told you're the sort of people who appreciate a direct conversation." She waits.

**If Doom Raiders are known allies:** She places a card face-down on the table. "The people I work for are going to win. There's a number on that. If you find what they're looking for and don't deliver it to Starsong first, that number is yours. No faction." Stands and leaves before the card is turned over.

**What the party can do:** Engage, refuse, or pass the card to Davil (+1 Doom Raiders renown). Engaging without following through: both factions notice within two tendays, costs standing with both.

**Doom Raiders reaction:** If Doom Raiders PCs, Davil will ask politely whether they had any unusual visitors. He already knows. He wants to know if they'll tell him.

**Fame impact:** Vevette is well-dressed and quiet. No impact unless the party escalates.

---

### The Cassalanters — "Willifort Crowelle" (Doppelganger)

*The Cassalanters do not send teams. They send one person. The person is worse.*

**Who comes:** "Willifort Crowelle" — a doppelganger, the Cassalanters' most trusted lieutenant. Always arrives as a different identity: friendly rival tavern owner, minor city functionary checking compliance paperwork, Harper contact the party hasn't met. Never arrives as himself, because himself does not exist.

If the party is a serious Act III threat: Seffia Naelryke's team (cult fanatic + 2 spies + 1 veteran + 1 invisible imp) conducts a quiet search during a busy service — not a confrontation, a reconnaissance.

**Triggers:**
- Party has connected the Cassalanters to the Grand Game through investigation
- Party has been to the Brandath Mausoleum or shown interest in the Third Eye's location
- Party has publicly refused a Cassalanter social invitation or caused visible embarrassment
- A Cassalanter staff plant has been exposed (Rella Asp or Ivrala Sorn)

**"Willifort" as compliance officer:** Has a real Fellowship of Innkeepers badge (he had it made). Polite, thorough, two degrees more specific than required. How often deliveries? Regular overnight guests? Cellar access? At the door on the way out: "Do you know a man named Dalakhar? Small, gnomish build?" Watches the party's face.

**Seffia's team:** Invisible unless DC 15 passive Perception or DC 13 active check in the cellar or upstairs during service. The imp looks for Golorr artifacts. Does not steal; reports. DC 17 Detect Magic or active True Seeing detects the imp.

**Escalation:** Once the party is in direct competition for the Gold, Willifort shifts from information-gathering to interference: impersonating party members to damage faction relationships, or arranging for a Cassalanter-connected Watch lieutenant to take a new interest in the tavern's compliance record.

**Fame impact:** Willifort's visit: 0. Seffia's team detected during busy service: −1 (patrons notice something wrong, can't say what).

---

### Bregan D'aerthe — Fel'rekt Lafeen (or Another Lieutenant)

*Jarlaxle's people are the only faction that might enjoy the tavern.*

**Who comes:** Fel'rekt Lafeen most often in the early campaign (Acts II–III), with 2–4 drow in excellent surface disguise. They order interesting things, tip well, cause no trouble. Jarlaxle himself appears separately in a different disguise each time (see Patron Profiles), preceded by Fel'rekt scoping the room first.

**Triggers:**
- Party has interfered with Bregan D'aerthe's Eye operations or the Sea Maidens Faire
- Jarlaxle has decided the party is interesting enough to monitor closely
- A drow PC or BD-affiliated PC is in the party (significantly accelerates his interest)

**Reconnaissance visit:** Four people come in within minutes of each other. Well-dressed, surface-correct with slight hesitation. Learning the room. One orders a Calishite spirit by its proper name and watches Lif pour it with professional appreciation. They stay two hours. One leaves a tip in a coin the party doesn't recognize. It is worth more than expected.

**If a drow PC is in the party:** Fel'rekt does not disguise his heritage. Sits at the bar openly. Looks at the drow PC the way one professional looks at another who has found a good hiding spot. "Jarlaxle sends his regards, and wonders if you'd like to have dinner. Remalia's, Thursday. He'll know if you come."

**What the party can do:** Meet Jarlaxle (full scene — see Patron Profiles), refuse him (he finds refusal interesting), or tail Fel'rekt back to the Sea Maidens Faire (DC 16 Dexterity Stealth vs. Fel'rekt's +6 Perception).

**Fame impact:** BD visits handled without incident add +1 Fame after the first two occurrences — they tip extraordinarily and word gets around that the Trollskull attracts interesting people.

## Part 8: The Bigger Picture

### The Tavern and the Grand Game (Arcs C–J)

By the time the Grand Game reaches its active phase, the tavern's status matters to the factions:

- **Harpers** may use it as a casual dead-drop point (rotating books via Rishaal).
- **Doom Raiders** (if not hostile) may send a Response Team to drink and tip — showing normalcy. They know where you live. For now, that doesn't trouble them.
- **Xanathar Guild** has no specific interest in the tavern, but a Guild operative may follow a target inside. This is the Guild's version of not caring about the tavern.
- **Bregan D'aerthe**, through Jarlaxle, finds the tavern charming. He has a table reserved — the party doesn't know this yet. He begins appearing occasionally in a different disguise each time.

### The Tavern and Undermountain (Post-Act IV)

When the campaign transitions into *Dungeon of the Mad Mage*, Trollskull Tavern becomes the above-ground anchor — a reason to return to the surface, an income source between dungeon runs, a place where Undermountain rumors circulate. Durnan's Yawning Portal is the professional operation; the Trollskull is where the amateurs talk.

NPCs who disappeared in the dungeon may appear at the bar. Faction agents may request meetings there specifically because the dungeon entrance is close. The party has something to protect. That matters when Halaster Blackcloak starts paying attention.

**Halaster Easter Egg (Fame 15+):** Add to the Event Table as an additional result on a 20, or whenever it feels dramatically right:

> A stranger in a battered traveling cloak takes a corner table and nurses a single drink for three hours. They leave without incident. But Lif — who otherwise welcomes all paying customers with poltergeist efficiency — refused to serve them, retreating behind the bar and staying there. The stranger's seat, examined afterward, holds a single copper piece, tails-up. Etched into the surface, too small to read without magnification: *Interesting.*
