# Arc C: Fireball

---

## Chapter Purpose

Arc C is the campaign's tonal shift. It delivers Waterdeep's first genuine loss, converts the party's neighbors into the personal stakes that drive the investigation, and teaches intelligence synthesis as a skill: multiple witnesses, multiple evidence layers, a city-wide elimination search, and a target address at the end. Every investigative tool the party used in Arc A reappears here at higher difficulty. The investigation that follows the fireball is not optional — it is personal.

---

## 1. Core Concept

Dalakhar, a rock gnome spy working for exiled Lord Dagult Neverember, had been carrying the *Stone of Golorr* toward Trollskull Manor when four separate factions converged on the same alley at the same moment. Urstul Floxin's Zhentarim moved to intercept him from the north. The Gralhund nimblewright — a Lantanese construct sold by Jarlaxle Baenre's Sea Maidens Faire, armed with a *necklace of fireballs* by Lady Yalah Gralhund — watched from a rooftop, under orders to secure the Stone before any other faction could claim it. When Floxin appeared about to take Dalakhar alive, the nimblewright hurled a bead into the crowd. The fireball killed Dalakhar, two Zhentarim sellswords, nine bystanders, and the DM's chosen Arc B victim. Floxin survived — burned and furious — the Stone already gone. The nimblewright dropped from the roof, rifled Dalakhar's body, pocketed the Stone, and fled east across the rooftops toward Gralhund Villa. Floxin followed it back to the estate. Lady Gralhund disarmed him at swordpoint, confined him in a ground-floor room, and refused to yield the Stone. He killed his guards, escaped without it, and is now assembling a strike team for a forced return. That return is Arc D. The investigation that begins in Arc C must reach Gralhund Villa before the night of Ches 24th, when Floxin's team arrives and the situation becomes unrecoverable.

## 2. Sections

### Arc Opener

*An adventure for 3rd-level characters.*

The fireball detonated at midday on Ches 22nd. By the time the party reached the door, the alley held eleven bodies and a silence where music had been playing. One of the eleven was someone they knew. The investigation began in the street with a five-minute window before the City Watch cordoned the scene — evidence to collect, witnesses to find, a burned man in a merchant's coat limping north and leaving a trail that would eventually resolve into an address.

Three routes ran in parallel from the crime scene to the same conclusion. At the blast crater: a necklace of fireballs with a broken clasp, a gnome's sewer-caked boots, a construct-origin trace in the scorch pattern. The evidence pointed toward an advanced mechanical construct operating in the city. Witnesses painted its description from separate vantage points: Jezrynne on the cooperage roof, Martem at the rain barrel, Fala watching from the greenhouse. Renaer arrived within hours with a name for the dead gnome and a Harper contact who, within a day, placed the burned man walking into Gralhund Villa on Saerdoun Street.

The construct was a nimblewright, a rare Lantanese creation. Finding it meant finding its maker. The House of Inspired Hands, Gond's temple in the Sea Ward, kept its own: Nim, who had built a second three months earlier to ease its loneliness. The runaway had been gone a month. Nim had built a detector to find it, never used. Armed with it, the party swept the city: seven legitimate owners cleared across four wards, the device whirring at the harbor before it whirred harder at the North Ward. The Sea Maidens Faire complicated matters — its captain already knew everything they were looking for and was deciding how much to share.

By the time the detector spun hard on Saerdoun Street, the party had built their own intelligence picture: three paths converging on a single address, a city full of factions all reaching for the same object, and a window that was closing. The investigation ends at Gralhund Villa's locked gate. What happens when they go through it is Arc D.

### Scene 1: The Fireball

**Purpose:** Deliver the campaign's first genuine loss. Transition from Arc B's warmth to investigative urgency. Establish the crime scene as a structured information environment with three independent evidence layers leading to the same conclusion.

**Content:**

*The Explosion.* Midday detonation in Trollskull Alley. 20-foot-radius sphere, 8d6 fire damage, DC 15 Dexterity save for any character in the alley. Eleven bodies in the blast radius. The nimblewright threw the bead from a rooftop, dropped to the street, rifled Dalakhar's body, and fled east across the rooftops. Floxin survived to the north, badly burned.

*The Twelfth Body.* The DM's chosen Arc B victim is the twelfth. Dead, no ambiguity, no last-second survival. The DM's observation task from Arc B Scene 3 ends here. Cross-ref Arc B Milestone (fireball victim candidate table). Grief does not require months of backstory. It requires one specific thing made vivid.

*The Dead.* Eleven bodies beyond the victim: Dalakhar (male rock gnome, burned cloak, boots and cloak hem caked with dried gray sewer silt, clutching a dagger in a scorched fist, five 100 gp gems in a belt pouch); two cloaked male humans in leather armor, one with a winged snake tattooed on his right forearm — the Black Network brand; two female humans and one male half-elf in plain clothes (North Ward servants running early errands); four halflings (two women who were playing fiddle and flute, two men who had been dancing to the music); one elderly female human in a gray traveling cloak, taking her morning walk.

*The Investigation Window.* Five minutes before the first six City Guard soldiers arrive and cordon the alley. Twenty minutes before Barnibus Blastwind and Sergeant Saeth Cromley appear with twenty constables and formally take charge. After that, accessing the bodies requires DC 15 Stealth to avoid notice, or official Watch cooperation. Bodies are removed to the North Ward Watch Station by midmorning; *Gentle Repose* is cast on each. They remain available for *Speak with Dead* for five days.

*The Crime Scene Evidence.* Four layers accessible in the window before the Watch seals the scene:
- **Surface (no check):** Eleven bodies, blast crater, scorch ring, every ground-floor window within thirty feet shattered.
- **DC 12 Investigation:** Black Network tattoo on the dead Zhentarim; sewer silt on Dalakhar's boots; a rain barrel at the east end of the blast radius holds something metal — the *necklace of fireballs* (two beads remaining, broken clasp) dropped when the nimblewright's chain caught on a chimney as it fled east. Martem Trec has it.
- **DC 14 Perception:** Dalakhar's pockets turned out and emptied — someone searched the body after the blast, in a hurry.
- **DC 16 Arcana:** Construct-origin transmutation trace in the scorch pattern. The incendiary device was wielded by something nonmagical carrying a magic item, not a spellcaster. The signature is mechanically precise in a way that rules out all known humanoid and monster sources.

*The Watch Takes Over.* Barnibus Blastwind — a meticulous dwarf mage of the Watchful Order, ink-stained coat, soft-spoken, forensically precise — reaches three private conclusions he will not share voluntarily: the gnome was running from armed pursuers (at least three); all of them were moving toward the tavern at the end of the alley; the blast originated from above and to the east. DC 15 Persuasion to extract any of this. Sergeant Saeth Cromley directs constables to knock on every door in the alley. He has nothing to share and does not try.

*Speak with Dead.* Available after the Watch takes custody of the bodies. Three access methods: faction access at Renown 1+ in the Harpers, Lords' Alliance, Order of the Gauntlet, or Force Grey arranges a cleric through the appropriate temple; a temple donation of at least 25 gp per casting buys one casting with questions provided in advance; a character who knows *Speak with Dead* can attempt their own access with a DC 13 Persuasion check with Barnibus or Cromley. Dalakhar can answer up to five questions: he stole the *Stone of Golorr* from the lair of a beholder called Xanathar, deep in the dungeons below the city; he worked for the rightful Open Lord — meaning Neverember, not Silverhand; the Stone is the key to a hidden hoard of gold dragons sealed somewhere in the city; he heard of adventurers who rescued Neverember's son and decided his hands were safer than his own; Renaer Neverember vouched for them. The two dead Zhentarim (Bashekk Ortallis and Wern Malkrave) can each answer up to five questions: they worked for Urstul Floxin; they were assigned to intercept a gnome named Dalakhar; whatever Dalakhar carried would make them "as rich as kings"; they do not know where Floxin billeted the cell.

*Urstul Floxin.* Wounded, conscious, gives his real name when asked. Merchant cover story (DC 14 Insight to see through). He watched the "metal man" drop from the roof, rifle the gnome's body, and flee east with something clutched in its hand. His account is accurate — he wants the party to track it. He intends to follow. If left unattended, he slips away within the hour. If detained, the Watch releases him within 24 hours — no grounds to hold a witness.

*Three Independent Clue Paths to Revelation #1 (A Nimblewright Caused the Explosion):*

- **Witness Testimony.** Cross-referencing Jezrynne (cooperage roof, moved like a puppet on wires with no wires), Martem (rain barrel, a person made of metal like a clock that did not look back), and Fala (Floxin stooping over the gnome after the construct had already fled) produces a consistent construct description from three independent vantage points. A party that registered the nimblewright at the Twin Parades makes the identification without a check.
- **Crime Scene Evidence.** DC 16 Arcana on the scorch pattern, combined with the broken necklace found at the east end of the blast, establishes that the device was wielded by a nonmagical construct carrying a magic item — not a spellcaster, not a humanoid.
- **Floxin's Testimony.** "Metal man" description given freely. DC 13 Arcana or History identifies the type as a nimblewright — a rare Lantanese construct not previously documented operating in Waterdeep.

**Tone:** Visceral, then procedural. The explosion is chaos. The investigation that follows is controlled. The shift should be visible — the party moves from raw reaction to methodology the moment they start reading the scene.

### Scene 2: The Witnesses

**Purpose:** Deliver the expanded witness pool. Each witness provides a different evidence fragment; the party must synthesize testimony across multiple interviews. This is their first intelligence-analysis exercise — no single witness has the complete picture.

**Content:**

*The Witness Pool.* Seven witnesses with specific evidence fragments, accessible in any order. Most speak without needing a check; specific DCs apply where a witness is reluctant, traumatized, or concealing information.

- **Fala Lefaliir.** Was watering greenhouse plants on the second floor when the blast blew out the windows. Saw a cloaked man stoop over one of the bodies — the gnome — after the blast. He was badly burned, limping north toward the Bent Nail, looking back over his shoulder. Fala saw Floxin, not the nimblewright. The nimblewright had already taken the Stone and fled before Floxin reached the body. No check needed; Fala is shaken and willing.
- **Jezrynne Hornraven.** Noblewoman, had just left the Tiger's Eye detective office when the blast hit. Saw the nimblewright on the cooperage roof: it moved wrong, like a puppet on wires with no wires, had something in its hand, threw it down into the street, then ran east across the roof toward the Sea Ward. DC 13 Arcana to identify her description as consistent with a nimblewright. No check needed for the sighting itself.
- **Martem Trec.** Twelve years old. Hiding behind the rain barrel at the east end of the blast radius — not standing in the street with the adult witnesses. Finding him requires active searching or asking a neighbor where the children are. He watched the halfling musicians die. He has the *necklace of fireballs*; it fell into his rain barrel when the nimblewright's chain caught on a chimney as it fled east. He describes the construct without prompting if asked what he saw, quietly and precisely: "A person made of metal, like a clock, that ran across the roof and dropped the bead that killed them and didn't look back at all, not even once." DC 8 Persuasion (Advantage if the party shows genuine compassion rather than simply reaching for the item) to convince him to hand it over. On a failure, he pulls it back — not because he wants it, but because he is not ready to trust a stranger with something this dangerous.
- **Vincent Trench.** The Tiger's Eye detective agency, across the alley. Arrived after the explosion. DC 15 Insight: knows more than he's saying. DC 16 Persuasion: confirms he saw the construct from his window — "a construct of Lantanese manufacture, moving with purpose, heading for the harbor." He is a rakshasa. What he shares is accurate. What he withholds is that he recognized it immediately and already knows who builds them.
- **Rishaal the Page-Turner.** Book Wyrm's Treasure. No visual evidence. Within an hour of being asked, produces a Lantanese engineering text from his shop naming the House of Inspired Hands as Waterdeep's only active temple of Gond and the foremost authority on Lantanese mechanical constructs in the city.
- **Tally Fellbranch / Embric and Avi.** Supporting witnesses. Saw the flash and the smoke; Embric noticed a glint of silver moving east across the rooftops a moment before the blast. No single critical clue, but their accounts establish the timeline and confirm the direction of flight from a third vantage point.

*Renaer's Testimony.* Arrives within two hours of learning about the explosion. Dalakhar sent him a paper bird the morning of Ches 22nd: the Stone was in his hands, multiple factions pursuing him, he was coming to Trollskull Manor because Renaer had vouched for the party. Renaer knows the *Stone of Golorr* is "the key to everything" but not about the Eyes or the vault's location. He carries guilt for not acting on the message immediately.

If the party mentions Fala's account of the burned man fleeing north — Renaer reaches out to Harper contacts at once. Within one day, he returns: the burned man matches Urstul Floxin, Manshoon's chief field agent in Waterdeep; a North Ward resident saw Floxin — visibly burned and limping — enter Gralhund Villa on Saerdoun Street within hours of the blast. The Watch sent two constables who were told everything was fine. No warrant was issued.

*Three Independent Clue Paths to the Stone Connection:*

- **Renaer's Paper Bird.** Renaer arrives within two hours of learning about the explosion. Dalakhar's paper bird, sent that morning, states that the Stone of Golorr was in his hands and that he was on his way to Trollskull Manor. This path is the arc's proactive failsafe — it delivers the core facts regardless of prior investigation success.
- **Speak with Dead — Dalakhar's Body.** The bodies remain available for five days after the Watch takes custody. Dalakhar can answer up to five questions about the Stone directly: that he stole it from Xanathar's lair, that it is the key to a hidden vault of gold dragons somewhere beneath the city, and that Renaer Neverember vouched for the party. See Scene 1 for access methods.
- **Speak with Dead — The Two Dead Zhentarim.** Bashekk Ortallis and Wern Malkrave each provide up to five answers. They were assigned to intercept Dalakhar for Urstul Floxin, and confirm that whatever he carried would make the cell "as rich as kings." Their testimony establishes that the Guild was tracking Dalakhar specifically because he carried the Stone. Each body is an independent path; neither depends on the other.

**For Doom Raiders Characters. (New Content)** Davil Starsong is still free during Arc C — his arrest happens in the Watch's post-Gralhund crackdown, which follows Arc D. Davil identifies Floxin immediately as Manshoon's blade and suspects Floxin filed an anonymous tip naming the Doom Raiders as persons of interest in the fireball — damage control meant to draw Watch heat away from the Splinter cell. He wants proof of Floxin's direct involvement. *The Dilemma:* The bombing was Zhentarim — the other cell, the rival operation, a splinter this party never sanctioned. That distinction is real. Explaining it to Trollskull Alley neighbors is going to be very difficult.

*Player Faction Contact Reactions.* As the fireball investigation proceeds, each player faction contact responds in their own register. These exchanges can be delivered as flying snake messages, chance encounters, or requested meetings depending on the party's established communication patterns.

- **Harpers (Mirt, Renown 1+):** During the investigation — any point before the party reaches the Sea Maidens Faire — Mirt takes a Harper member aside for a private word. The Cassalanters funded the Howling Hatred cult three years ago; he quietly extracted their agents from Watch custody as a political courtesy. He is not certain whether their current interest in the Grand Game connects to any of this. He is sharing it because he is worried, which is different from being certain.
- **Lords' Alliance (Jalester, Renown 1+):** When the party's investigation reaches the Faire or the harbor district, Jalester mentions — without pressure to act — that the Sea Maidens Faire uses private docking arrangements that circumvent standard Watch inspection protocols. He's not pursuing it. He's noting it as an irregularity someone should eventually care about.
- **Emerald Enclave (Melannor, Renown 1+):** Jeryth Phaulkon perceived a sharp psychic disturbance somewhere in the Castle Ward the night of the fireball. She cannot pinpoint the source, but Melannor passes the report along: something was activated or awakened in that district around the time of the explosion. It may not be connected to the nimblewright. Melannor is not certain enough to say it is.
- **Order of the Gauntlet (Savra, Renown 1+):** Savra was tracking a supply delivery route that crossed Trollskull Alley the morning of the blast. She contacts the party directly, without pleasantries: she wants to know if anyone was collecting from the bodies or watching the scene before the constables arrived. She has a list of names she suspects. She will not share it until the party's account confirms or complicates any of them.
- **Force Grey (Vajra, Renown 1+):** The Blackstaff Tower sends a note the morning after. The explosion's magical residue carries a signature the Watchful Order describes as a nimblewright detonation — registered Lantanese manufacture, auditable through the House of Inspired Hands. Vajra wants the party's firsthand account of the blast site. She does not ask for conclusions. She asks for what they saw, specifically, in sequence.
- **Doom Raiders (Davil, Renown 1+):** Covered above. Davil also wants to know if anyone arrived at the blast site early — before the Watch, before the other witnesses — and behaved in a way that suggested foreknowledge. He has a suspicion he is not ready to name.
- **Bregan D'Aerthe (entry condition — Arc C investigation leads to Sea Maidens Faire):** Shortly after the party is visibly investigating — asking questions in the Alley, visiting the House of Inspired Hands, moving through the harbor district — a folded note on Seven Masks Theater stationery arrives by unmarked runner: two tickets to tonight's performance, no explanation, no name. Any drow party member receives it directly. If no drow are present, it is addressed to "the group from the Alley."

*Faction Response Teams Deploy.* All four villain factions move on the crime scene within hours. Xanathar Guild gazers post to watch the alley (DC 16 Perception to spot them); Manshoon's Zhent operatives run a parallel investigation through their own network; Bregan D'Aerthe observers activate conditionally, their level of engagement scaling with whether the party later reaches the Sea Maidens Faire; the Cassalanters send flowers and a warm handwritten condolence note — Ammalia's handwriting. Cross-ref Appendix E (faction response team rosters and behavior by arc).

**Tone:** Procedural, layered. Each interview adds a fragment. The party assembles the picture by talking to people, not by passing checks. Every witness who provides information is also a character with a reason for being in the alley that morning.

### Scene 3: The Nimblewright Hunt

**Purpose:** Convert the conclusion that a nimblewright caused the explosion into an actionable search. Introduce the House of Inspired Hands and arm the party with the nimblewright detector. Structure the city-wide search as an elimination montage that characterizes Waterdeep while closing in on the address.

**Content:**

*Three Independent Clue Paths to the House of Inspired Hands:*

- **Rishaal's Research.** The Lantanese engineering text Rishaal provides names the House of Inspired Hands directly. No additional check required.
- **Faction Intelligence.** Any faction contact can identify "nimblewright" from the witness descriptions and direct the party to the Gond temple. Harpers are fastest — Remallia Haventree knows the high priest personally.
- **Watch Cooperation.** Barnibus's Watchful Order contacts can identify the construct type from the Arcana trace and witness descriptions within 48 hours. Slower than the other two paths but available to parties without established faction contacts.

*The House of Inspired Hands.* Temple of Gond, corner of Seawatch Street and Shark Street, Sea Ward. The toothed cog of Gond appears on every surface — doorknobs, gutter spouts, collar clasps. The main hall holds prize-winning inventions on marble pedestals. Notable among them: a brass-plated model of a Lantanese submersible labeled *"The Scarlet Marpenoth — Launched 1489 DR."*

**Mechanical Sparrow Trap. (New Content)** As the party approaches within 60 feet of the entrance, a figure is visible on the rooftop parapet — slight, humanoid, still. It releases a mechanical sparrow. *Initiative count 10. +0 to hit, 1d3 piercing damage on a hit. AC 14, 1 hit point; can be intercepted with a readied action.* It shatters on impact regardless of result. The moment the sparrow is released, Nim disappears through a rooftop hatch and retreats to the attic, hoping to avoid the consequences of its miscalculation.

**Valetta. (New Content)** The temple's senior cleric — a tall bronze dragonborn with amber-yellow eyes, the machine oil smell of a woman who considers it a kind of perfume, deliberate compact movements, the toothed cog worn as a collar clasp. Patient, methodical, protective of the temple's reputation. She identifies Nim immediately from any physical description and escorts the party up the spiral staircase to the attic. Finds the attic door locked with a mechanism she does not recognize — new since the last time she was up here. Opens with DC 20 Thieves' Tools, a *Knock* spell, or DC 17 Persuasion through the door (Advantage with Valetta's firm encouragement).

*Nim's Attic.* Packed floor to rafter with half-finished contraptions — copper tubing, gear-trains on wire racks, a dozen projects in various stages. Nim is in the corner: the size of a small child, crouching, watching the party with expressionless brass eyes.

Nim understands Common but cannot speak. It communicates in **Common Sign Language.** Valetta has learned enough to translate imperfectly — she misses nuance and occasionally has to ask Nim to repeat. **Any character who selected Common Sign Language** communicates with Nim directly, without the intermediary. Nim responds with visible relief: it signs faster, more precisely, shares emotional texture (loneliness, pride in its creation, guilt about the runaway) it would not bother conveying through a partial translation. Give the player a moment. Nim has been waiting a long time to have a real conversation.

Through communication, the party learns: Nim built a second nimblewright three months ago to ease its loneliness. The creation panicked — incomplete behavioral lattice — and fled the temple grounds a month ago. Nim has a detector device it built to find it and never had the chance to use.

The detector is a 1-foot-long copper contraption with an umbrella-like metallic protrusion: *Wondrous Item, Uncommon (requires attunement). Magic Action to activate. While active, spins and clicks within 500 feet of any nimblewright other than its builder. Speed and volume increase with proximity; maximum intensity within 30 feet.*

**Valetta's Reward. (New Content)** 500 gp for proof of the escaped nimblewright's destruction, plus one invention of the party's choosing: adjustable stilts; a backpack parachute (reaction to deploy while falling, prevents all falling damage, requires 10 minutes to repack); a barking box (detects vibrations within 15 feet, activates for 8 hours per winding); or a matchless pipe.

*The Nine Nimblewright Owners.* Elimination sequence across four wards. Designed as a montage — most visits resolve in two or three exchanges. The DM plays out whichever visits advance plot or develop the world.

| # | Owner / Location | Ward | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | House of Inspired Hands / Nim | Sea Ward | Confirmed non-culprit; validates the detector |
| 2 | Bowgentra Summertaen | Castle Ward | Household servant; incapable of independent action |
| 3 | Lord Adarbrent | Sea Ward | Bodyguard; household logs confirm stationary on Ches 22nd |
| 4 | Lord Dezlentyr | North Ward | Personal attendant; a frail lord who relies on it daily |
| 5 | House of Wonder | Sea Ward | Temple guardian; high priest confirms its presence all morning |
| 6 | Mother Tamra's | Trades Ward | Porter and night watchman; proprietor protective but cooperative |
| 7 | City Armory | Castle Ward | Weapon drill demonstrator; operational log is maintained |
| 8 | **Gralhund Estate** | **North Ward** | Strongest signal outside the Faire. Guards refuse visitors. Signal unmistakable. |
| 9 | Faction member's nimblewright | Any ward | Brief uncomfortable moment; cleared quickly; incidentally surfaces that Jarlaxle sold nimblewrights across the city. |

*Two Signals Remain.* At the end of the sweep, two locations produce strong readings: the **Sea Maidens Faire** in the harbor and **Gralhund Estate** on Saerdoun Street. The decision of which to investigate first is the arc's fulcrum. A party that goes directly to Gralhund Villa skips Scene 4 and transitions to Arc D. A party that investigates the Faire first gains intelligence that changes how the Gralhund approach unfolds.

**Tone:** Investigative montage. Shoe leather and social competence rather than divination magic. The city-wide sweep characterizes Waterdeep as a place where different kinds of powerful people have legitimate uses for mechanical servants — and one of them does not.

### Scene 4: The Sea Maidens Faire

**Purpose:** Connect the nimblewright investigation to Jarlaxle's operation. Frame the Dock Ward visit as a deliberate investigative redirect — it feels like a stall but introduces Jarlaxle, establishes the nimblewright network, provides three independent paths to the Gralhund connection, and seeds the Arc H heist target.

**Content:**

*The Dock Ward Distraction.* When the party sweeps the Dock Ward, the detector whirs before they reach the North Ward — signal from the harbor. Three ships: the *Heartbreaker* and *Hellraiser* (docked galleons, painted crimson and gold, flying Faire pennants) and the *Eyecatcher* (anchored a quarter-mile out in Deepwater Harbor). Multiple nimblewright signals overwhelm the detector. Painted carnival stalls and bright canvas awnings fill the pier between the docked ships. The party will not find the Gralhund nimblewright here. Following a real lead to a productive-but-incomplete result is exactly the kind of investigative experience this arc is building. The payoff comes when the detector drags them north and starts spinning faster.

*Captain Zord's Office.* Captain Zardoz Zord keeps regular hours and is known to enjoy interesting visitors. A party that attended the Field of Triumph (Arc B Scene 5) recognizes him from the private box. His real name is Jarlaxle Baenre. He does not advertise this. He already knows about the fireball, the gnome, and the Stone. He is deciding how much to share and at what price.

*Three Independent Paths to the Gralhund Connection:*

- **Path 1: Ask Zord (Easy, Tips Off Jarlaxle).** Zord excuses himself briefly and returns with the Gralhund name, address, and the note that their nimblewright had "combat programming — unusual for a household model." He provides this freely. The consequence: the party has told Jarlaxle Baenre everything he needs to know. He deploys Bregan D'Aerthe agents to Gralhund Villa before the party arrives. Cross-ref Arc D (Jarlaxle's pre-positioning).
- **Path 2: Steal Jarlaxle's Records (Difficult, Preserves Secrecy).** Mini-heist aboard the *Eyecatcher* or the *Heartbreaker.* DC 16 Thieves' Tools (locked stateroom); DC 14 Stealth (nimblewright valet moving through the corridor); DC 15 Investigation (*Heartbreaker*) or DC 18 Investigation (*Eyecatcher*) to locate the ledger. Consequence: Jarlaxle discovers the theft within 24 hours but cannot identify who. He does not deploy to Gralhund Villa.
- **Path 3: The Crystal Ball (Hardest, Maximum Intelligence).** The *Scarlet Marpenoth* submersible — whose model stands in the House of Inspired Hands main hall — is docked beneath the *Eyecatcher*, connected by a keel collar. Discovering it requires DC 18 Perception from the docks at night, or a faction tip. Accessing it and operating the crystal ball provides recorded footage of the murder. An aspirational target for a 3rd-level party — establishes the *Marpenoth* as a high-value intelligence asset for Arc H.

*Ship Access Details.* Gangplanks technically open during daylight hours. DC 14 Stealth or DC 14 Persuasion/Deception to board the docked ships without challenge. Searching accessible areas: 30 minutes, nothing incriminating. Locked officer's quarters: DC 15 Investigation (*Heartbreaker*) for the partial ledger. *Eyecatcher* access: hire a harbor skiff (2 sp, departing hourly from the harbor steps), swim (DC 12 Athletics, cold water, awkward current), clandestine approach at night (DC 14 Stealth to reach the hull; DC 16 to board unseen), or a credible cover story with an invitation from Zord (DC 16 Deception without one).

*Partial vs. Complete Ledger. (New Content)* The **partial ledger** (recovered from the *Heartbreaker*'s locked officer's cabin) is a folio of waxed vellum pages. Nine rows total; seven excised cleanly with a blade. Two rows survive:

| NW-03 | CR-03 | Marpenoth 12 | [excised] | Yellowspire, Castle Ward | 25,000 gp |

The partial ledger confirms the network exists but does not name the Gralhunds. NW-02 — the Gralhund entry — is one of the seven rows cut out. The **complete ledger** (aboard the *Eyecatcher* or given directly by Jarlaxle to BD operatives) holds all nine entries:

**For Bregan D'Aerthe Characters. (New Content)** A BD character is escorted directly to Jarlaxle's private cabin. The persona drops — hat off, quieter smile, plain speech. He offers the complete ledger as a gift and a test. His ask: be in the room when the Stone conversation happens. **Renown: +1 for sharing intelligence back; +2 for withholding** — Jarlaxle is genuinely delighted to be outmaneuvered by his own people.

*The Voided Cassalanter Entry. (New Content)* The complete ledger records a Cassalanter commission — placed, programmed, voided before delivery. Notation: "Buyer withdrew — unspecified concerns re: detection." Victoro Cassalanter suspected the nimblewrights might contain surveillance devices. He was correct. The clairvoyance crystal reference numbers in the ledger are the key: each purchased nimblewright has a corresponding crystal recording what it witnesses. Victoro pulled his order when he worked this out. This pays off in Arc G when the party discovers what the Gralhund unit's crystal captured.

**Tone:** Layered, transactional. Every conversation is a negotiation. Information has a price. Every person who helps the party is also pursuing their own agenda. Jarlaxle's warmth and the party's frustration at the apparent stall are both genuine — neither cancels the other.

### Scene 5: The Cassalanter Dinner (New Content)

**Purpose:** The Cassalanters make their first move. The party has met Ammalia before — she visited Trollskull Manor in Arc B and is a known patron. This dinner is something different: Victoro is present, the invitation arrived the day after the fireball, and the conversation has an agenda. Ammalia's warmth is unchanged and genuine. Victoro's interest in the Stone is new, careful, and undisguised only to those paying close attention. The party's response — how much they share, whether they accept — becomes the Cassalanters' operational picture for Arc G.

**Content:**

*The Invitation.* Ammalia sends a personal note within a day of the fireball, handwritten on cream vellum in a clear, unhurried hand. She references the neighborhood tragedy, expresses concern for "those so near the incident on Ches 22nd," and invites the party to dinner at Cassalanter Villa the following evening. The letter mentions that Victoro is eager to meet them properly, which is the only signal that business will be discussed. There is no pressure: the invitation includes a footman's address for a reply.

*The Villa.* Cassalanter Villa sits in the Sea Ward, set back from the street behind an iron gate and a garden whose topiary has been maintained for two generations. The house staff is attentive, formal, and impeccably briefed. The dining room seats twelve; tonight it is set for six. The children are "not well enough for company" — a detail offered once and not returned to. The food is excellent. The wine is exceptional. Everything is exactly right, and a party paying attention will find the precision slightly exhausting.

*Ammalia at the Table.* She remembers every detail the party mentioned in Arc B — neighborhood politics, the state of Trollskull Manor, small personal observations — and asks after them with what reads as genuine interest. She is not performing warmth; she possesses it in excess and deploys it without calculation, which makes her harder to read than Victoro. A DC 14 Insight check reveals that she is watching to see how the party handles kindness, not suspicion: she is forming an opinion. The result of that opinion is Victoro's problem.

*Victoro's Pitch.* After the first course, Victoro raises the fireball. He expresses condolences. He mentions that the family had some acquaintance with Lord Neverember's later projects and that the object Dalakhar was carrying has "historical and financial significance beyond what most people appreciate." He does not name the Stone of Golorr. He offers to share what the Cassalanters have learned — contacts, research, documents — in exchange for a first conversation if the party recovers the object. His language is courteous, precise, and carries no threat. He does not ask for the Stone. He asks to be in the room.

*What the Cassalanters Know.* Victoro knows: Dalakhar worked for Neverember, the Stone is the key to a concealed vault, all three Eyes were separated from the Stone years ago, and the vault's physical entrance is in the Brandath family crypt. He does not share any of this at the dinner. His goal is assessment — he wants to know how much the party has found and whether they are worth cultivating.

*What the Cassalanters Do Not Know.* Nobody at this table knows the Cassalanters are infernalists — not the Harpers, not Force Grey, not the party. Ammalia and Victoro do not volunteer anything about the children, the pact, or the shrine. The debt is the conversation they are not having. A DC 16 Insight check on Victoro reads him as a man who made a decision he cannot unmake and has arranged his life carefully around it. This is accurate. It yields nothing.

*Party Response and Consequences.*
- **Sharing intelligence:** The Cassalanters learn where the investigation points. Victoro updates his operational picture. Entering Arc G, he knows what the party found at Gralhund Villa and what they did there. Cassalanter response teams are positioned accordingly.
- **Deflecting:** Victoro notes the restraint and interprets it as operational caution — a quality he respects. He does not press. Entering Arc G, he treats the party as capable but unaligned.
- **Accepting the arrangement:** Victoro follows up within two days with a sealed letter summarizing what the Cassalanters know — no vault specifics; he is not foolish. Entering Arc G, the party has a prior relationship and a document trail. The alliance track opens.
- **Refusing outright or treating the dinner as hostile:** Ammalia does not change expression. Victoro sees them out personally. Entering Arc G, the party is a complication rather than an asset.

**Tone:** Layered hospitality. Every comfort is real and every comfort is a negotiating position. Ammalia is the warmth; Victoro is the patience. The party is not in danger. They are being evaluated by people who are very good at it.

### Scene 6: Backtracking Dalakhar (Optional)

**Purpose:** Optional investigation branch rewarding thorough parties with Grand Game history, a Cassalanter connection through Kalain, and vault-opening clues that pay off in Arc J. Non-essential for reaching Gralhund Villa — gated behind proactive behavior, not hidden by design.

**Content:**

*Tracking Dalakhar.* DC 15 Charisma (Investigation) over a day's inquiry through inns and lodging houses in the Trades Ward establishes that a rock gnome matching Dalakhar's description stayed one night at the Inn of the Dripping Dagger on Snail Street, departing before breakfast on Ches 22nd.

*The Inn of the Dripping Dagger.* Proprietor: **Myl Dunpier**, a practical middle-aged woman with ink-stained fingers who takes no interest in her guests' business and considerable interest in being paid on time. She remembers Dalakhar clearly — a rock gnome, alone, paid in coin, asked for an upper-floor room with a window visible from the street, left before breakfast though he had prepaid through the evening. Four access methods to Dalakhar's room:
- Direct request: DC 14 Persuasion with a truthful explanation that the gnome is dead. She allows access if the party promises not to remove anything and she accompanies them.
- Watch authority: Renown 1+ in any faction, or a Watch badge obtained at the crime scene, grants automatic entry.
- Bribery: 5 gp buys access without conversation. 1 gp does not suffice.
- Stealth: DC 12 Perception to locate the room by smell (sewer silt, distinctive, in the upper hallway); DC 14 Thieves' Tools on the door lock.

*Dalakhar's Room.* Scrubbed clean before he left — professional habit. Sheets tucked tight, basin dried, wax drippings scraped away. DC 12 Investigation finds:
- Fine gray ash in the oil lamp's reservoir housing — correspondence burned here, cannot be reconstituted without magic.
- Scratch pattern under the writing table where something small was hidden and taken.
- Window latch tooling marks on the exterior side, roughly two days old — tested from outside.
- On the pillow, placed deliberately: a **disk of black stone**, flat, polished, one face painted with a stylized eye — ringed iris, vertical slit pupil. **Xanathar's death mark.** *We know where you sleep. We could have killed you.* Dalakhar never saw it; the agents were working from stale intelligence. DC 13 History or DC 14 Arcana to identify the symbol as Xanathar's personal sigil.

*Xanathar Response Team.* The room is under surveillance. If the party enters, the team approaches afterward and probes what they know. Intelligence shared scales with time elapsed:
- 1+ days after death: team is briefed on Dalakhar's death; knows Floxin was following him; aware the Guild now has eyes on Floxin; can inadvertently provide the Gralhund connection (track Floxin → Yellowspire safe house → Gralhund Villa).
- 3+ days after death: team has been pulled. Room empty.

*Faction Contact Responses to the Death Mark. (New Content)* Four distinct reactions, each providing different intelligence:
- **Mirt (Harpers, Renown 1+):** Identifies the mark without hesitation. Shares a rough map of observed Guild patrol patterns in the Trades Ward. Notes that Harper intelligence may be tainted — Manshoon has placed double agents in the network.
- **Vajra (Force Grey, Renown 1+):** Interested in what the mark implies about the Guild's operational sophistication. Has a partial dossier on Dalakhar — confirms his role, notes he infiltrated Xanathar's organization before going dark. DC 13 Persuasion to share it.
- **Jalester (Lords' Alliance, Renown 1+):** Reports to Laeral Silverhand. Returns within two hours: confirms Guild operating above street level, possible Watch leak. Offers 72-hour Watch ID papers. Notes that a North Ward household matching Gralhund Villa's description has been flagged as a possible Black Network safe house — a third path to the address.
- **Tashlyn Yafeera (Doom Raiders, Renown 1+):** Examines the mark with professional detachment. Confirms Guild death marks have been increasing. Questions who is feeding them intelligence — calculating whether the same leak that burned Dalakhar also burned Davil.

*Kalain's Letter.* Arrives four days after Dalakhar's death, addressed to him at the Inn of the Dripping Dagger. If the party left their names with Myl Dunpier and asked her to contact them if anything arrived for the gnome, she sends a street child to Trollskull Manor with it. Otherwise the letter sits uncollected behind the bar. Rewards the investigative habit of leaving contact information at every point of inquiry.

*Kalain's Tower.* A half-elf painter, once celebrated in Waterdavian circles, now alone in a dilapidated tower near the harbor. Former mistress of Lord Dagult Neverember. **Roleplaying:** Believes everyone who visits is secretly Dagult's assassin. Loses conversational threads mid-sentence and resurfaces on something different. Rubs her cheek with increasing agitation as she grows distressed. Calls animated figures from her smaller, older, peaceful paintings — sleeping cats, bowls of fruit, quiet landscapes — to fetch things or run small errands. The newer paintings are all monsters, all wearing the same face; creatures from these can be animated against intruders. DC 14 Persuasion to calm her on arrival. DC 12 Persuasion to talk her down if she accuses the party of killing Dalakhar on Dagult's orders.

What Kalain knows:
- Dalakhar worked for Neverember. She says so openly, and adds that she is glad he is dead — meaning Neverember. It takes a moment to establish which dead man she is talking about.
- Neverember was obsessed in their final months together with something he called a "Melairkyn ceremonial vault." She cannot now recall the specifics — the memory feels burned away, like a word she cannot retrieve. The *Stone of Golorr*'s influence, still active, suppresses the memory in everyone who has been near it.
- **Lord Victoro Cassalanter visited a few weeks ago, asking many of the same questions Dalakhar had asked years before — the vault, Neverember's last projects, whether she kept his papers.** She will not volunteer this. Asked directly whether anyone else has come asking about Neverember, she confirms it. He was polite. She found his politeness the most unsettling thing about the visit. This is the first concrete Cassalanter-to-vault link.

*Lord Dagult's Papers.* Lockbox beside the writing table (DC 13 Thieves' Tools). Among the correspondence:
- A crossed-out task list containing the entry: *"move the dragon to the Melairkyn ceremonial vault."*
- Correspondence with Hammond Kraddoc of the Vintners', Distillers', and Brewers' Guild confirming large bribes paid to cover a contaminated-liquor scandal in the Dock Ward.
- Notes on the Melairkyn dwarves and their cult of Dumathoin: a ceremonial vault "can be opened by standing before Dumathoin's doors and striking the scale of a dragon with a mithral hammer in the place where the sun's light should fall."
- An unsigned letter dated four years ago: *"The last of the three Eyes has been secured."* This confirms all three Eyes were in Neverember's hands four years ago — and that he deliberately separated them from the Stone. Pays off in Arc J.

**Tone:** Elegiac, secondary. Kalain's tower is a dead end inhabited by a broken person. Sadness and waste, not urgency. The information in the lockbox is some of the most valuable in the campaign; the experience of finding it should not feel like a victory.

# Design Notes: Fireball

## Investigation Architecture

***Three Phases, Not One Chain.*** The original WDH investigation runs as a sequential chain: find the nimblewright → go to the House of Inspired Hands → get the detector → find it at Gralhund Villa. One missed witness or one failed check can sever any link. The three-phase redesign distributes each critical revelation across independent paths — every conclusion has at least three routes — so no single failure blocks progress. The three phases (identify the construct, find all owners, determine which one is guilty) are not a required sequence; they are three distinct moments of synthesis that teach different investigative skills. Phase 1 is scene reading. Phase 2 is city-scale elimination. Phase 3 is convergence under faction pressure. Together they build the intelligence habits the heist arcs will demand.

***The Proactive Failsafe.*** If the party stalls after Scene 1 — no witnesses interviewed, no Arcana check attempted, no Watch contact made — Renaer arrives within two hours with Dalakhar's paper bird, delivering a name for the dead gnome and a direct pointer to the Stone. A passive party receives the basic facts; a thorough party earns the nuance. The failsafe keeps the investigation moving without removing the incentive to investigate.

## The Fireball Victim

***Arc B's Most Important Structural Output.*** Arc B's Milestone and Transition section contains the DM's observation task: identify, based purely on table investment across seven scenes, the neighbor the players bonded with. That person is among the dead in Scene 1 of Arc C. A fireball that kills eleven strangers is a plot event. A fireball that kills someone the party tried to warn, or hired, or saved once, or whose name came up in between-session speculation — that is the campaign's first genuine loss. It converts the investigation from a plot obligation into a personal imperative. The selection must be made before Arc C begins. This arc does not work if it is made on the fly.

***Why the DM Chooses, Not the Players.*** Voting converts tragedy into mechanics. The choice belongs to the DM because it is based on observation — who did the players ask follow-up questions about, who became real through accumulated small moments, whose name appeared in table speculation without prompting. No structural design element can manufacture that. Only attention and time can.

## Why the Nimblewright Steals the Stone

***The Change That Makes the Investigation Work.*** In the original WDH, Floxin is the perpetrator and is quickly identified; the investigation effectively ends at the crime scene. The Alexandrian change — the nimblewright as actor, Floxin as witness — converts a person-chase (find Floxin, done) into a systematic elimination (identify the construct type, find all owners, determine which one is guilty). The Stone already being at Gralhund Villa before the investigation begins means the party is racing against a closing window rather than chasing a mobile target. Urgency from the ticking clock; methodology from the elimination structure. Both are necessary.

***Floxin as Self-Serving Witness.*** His willingness to talk is not generosity. He wants the party to track the Stone so the Zhentarim can follow. His account is accurate because lying would be counterproductive — a false description would produce a false lead, and he needs the real one found. Parties that read his motive through Insight will have to decide what to do with a useful informant who is also a tail.

## Faction Visibility

***The First Arc Where All Four Villain Factions Pursue the Same Object.*** Arc A establishes the Xanathar Guild and Manshoon's Zhentarim as adversaries. Arc B introduces the Cassalanters as patrons and Bregan D'Aerthe as observers. Arc C is the first arc where all four factions reach for the same object at the same time in the same city. The design makes this concrete rather than atmospheric: Xanathar gazers in the alley, Zhent parallel investigation, BD conditional observers scaling with party choices at the Faire, Cassalanter condolence note and dinner invitation. These are not flavor. They are the Grand Game becoming visible for the first time — the moment the party understands they are not the only investigators in Waterdeep, and that the city is full of people who already know more than they do.

## Jarlaxle's Conditional Activation

***Investigation Quality Has Consequences.*** If the party reaches the Sea Maidens Faire, Jarlaxle enters the Grand Game as an active participant — he positions BD agents at Gralhund Villa, makes a direct approach, and begins treating the party as players worth engaging. If the party bypasses the Faire entirely and goes directly to the North Ward, he remains a background presence: aware, watching, not deployed. This is not a punishment for skipping Scene 4. It is a realistic consequence of investigative depth. The party that turns over more stones gives Jarlaxle more surface to react to.

***The Dock Ward as Deliberate Redirect.*** The Faire visit feels like a stall because it is one — the Gralhund nimblewright is not there. This is intentional. The frustration of following a real lead to a productive-but-incomplete result is exactly the kind of experience good investigative fiction creates. The payoff arrives the moment the party moves north: the detector's behavior changes on Saerdoun Street in a way that makes the Faire visit retroactively coherent. The intelligence gathered there — the ledger, the Cassalanter entry, the BD private meeting, the Jarlaxle identification — makes the detour worthwhile in ways the party cannot fully assess until later.

## The Cassalanter Approach

***The Cassalanters Make Their Move.*** Arc B establishes the Cassalanters as warm, generous patrons — Ammalia a known presence at Trollskull Manor. Arc C delivers the first approach with an agenda: the dinner invitation arrives the morning after the fireball, and Victoro is at the table. Ammalia's warmth is unchanged; her role is to make the party comfortable enough that Victoro's question feels collaborative rather than predatory. The dinner is a genuine attempt to recruit capable people before someone else does — and an intelligence-gathering operation. The party's response shapes what the Cassalanters know when Arc G begins. This scene solves a pacing problem in the original campaign where the Cassalanters appear in Arc B as patrons and vanish entirely until the Arc G heist, with no intervening contact to bridge that gap. The Arc C dinner makes the connection without revealing anything premature — no pact, no children, no Asmodeus.

## Backtracking Dalakhar as Reward for Thoroughness

***Non-Essential, Consequential.*** Scene 5 cannot block the party's path to Gralhund Villa — parties that skip it still reach Arc D. But parties that pursue it gain Kalain's account, the first concrete Cassalanter-to-vault link, the vault-opening clue, and the unsigned Eyes letter confirming Neverember's deliberate separation of Stone and Eyes. These are among the most consequential intelligence finds in the entire campaign, gated behind exactly the kind of proactive habits the investigation arcs are trying to build: exhaustive canvassing, leaving contact information, following up on letters addressed to dead men. The design rewards the investigative instinct without penalizing its absence.

***Kalain as Emotional Register.*** Kalain's tower is a site of elegiac loss — a person who was broken by someone who did not notice breaking her, surrounded by the evidence of a life that went wrong. The party should not feel they have won something by finding her. The lockbox contains some of the most valuable intelligence in the campaign; the conversation to reach it should feel like something they cannot fix. A scene where the information gain is high and the emotional experience is difficult is the right delivery method for this material.
