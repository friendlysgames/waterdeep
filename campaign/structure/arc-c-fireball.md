# Arc C: Fireball

---

## Chapter Purpose

Arc C converts the party from tavern owners into investigators. The fireball murders someone the party cares about, makes the investigation personal, and introduces the Grand Game's full scope — four villain factions pursuing the same artifact through the streets of a city the party has just learned to call home. The arc's structural job is to teach the party how intelligence-gathering works in Waterdeep before the heist arcs demand it at scale.

---

## 1. Core Concept

A gnome spy named Dalakhar stole the Stone of Golorr from Xanathar and ran for Trollskull Alley to deliver it to Renaer Neverember. He never arrived. The Gralhunds — a minor noble house secretly bankrolling Manshoon's Zhentarim — tracked Dalakhar with their nimblewright, a mechanical servant armed with a necklace of fireballs. The nimblewright detonated one bead in Trollskull Alley, killing Dalakhar, killing or wounding several bystanders, and taking the Stone from Dalakhar's body before retreating to Gralhund Villa.

The party's investigation follows three phases: identify what caused the explosion (a nimblewright), discover who owns nimblewrights in Waterdeep (nine current owners, traceable through the House of Inspired Hands), and determine which nimblewright did it (the Gralhunds', discoverable through Jarlaxle's records at the Sea Maidens Faire or the nimblewright detector's direct signal at Gralhund Villa). Along the way, the party encounters evidence that every major faction in the city is pursuing the same prize — and that the death in their alley was one move in a game that has been running for years.

---

## 2. Sections

### Arc Opener

*An adventure for 3rd-level characters.*

A fireball detonated in Trollskull Alley at midday. The blast killed a gnome no one recognized and someone the party recognized too well. Windows shattered across five storefronts. A mechanical figure was seen bending over the bodies, taking something, and sprinting south before the smoke cleared.

The City Watch arrived within minutes — Barnibus Blastwind, a senior investigator, and Saeth Cromley, his enforcer, who cordoned the scene and began taking statements. The witnesses told contradictory stories about what they saw, but three facts held: the explosion originated from a single point above the street, something mechanical caused it, and the gnome was the target.

The party's investigation led first to the House of Inspired Hands, where a priest of Gond named Valetta explained that the mechanical figure was a nimblewright — an advanced construct sold by a carnival merchant named Zardoz Zord to wealthy buyers across the city. Valetta's own nimblewright, Nim, provided a detector that could locate others of its kind. Armed with the device, the party tracked nimblewrights across Waterdeep's wards, eliminating owners one by one until the detector's signal led to one of two places: the Sea Maidens Faire in the harbor, or a noble estate in the North Ward.

The Sea Maidens Faire was Jarlaxle Baenre's operation — the drow mercenary lord who had been watching the party since Arc A. His nimblewrights were sold at a loss because each contained a clairvoyance crystal feeding intelligence back to a scrying device aboard his submarine. His records named every buyer, including the Gralhunds. The party could reach this information by asking Zord directly, stealing his records, or accessing the crystal ball itself — each path carrying different risks and different consequences for Jarlaxle's future involvement in the Grand Game. An optional side investigation led to the Inn of the Dripping Dagger, where Dalakhar had spent his last night alive, and from there to a painter named Kalain who once loved Lord Dagult Neverember and still held his papers.

The arc ended with a direction: Gralhund Villa, where the nimblewright had returned with the Stone of Golorr.

### Scene 1: The Fireball

**Purpose:** Deliver the campaign's first genuine loss. Transition the tone from Arc B's community-building warmth to Arc C's investigative urgency. Establish the crime scene as a structured information environment with multiple evidence layers.

**Content:**

*The Explosion.* Midday. The party is either in Trollskull Manor or within earshot of it when the fireball detonates in the alley outside. The blast originates from a single bead of a necklace of fireballs, thrown by the Gralhund nimblewright at Dalakhar as he approached the manor's front door. The 20-foot-radius sphere catches Dalakhar, the Zhentarim agents tailing him (all killed except Urstul Floxin, who barely survives), and anyone standing in the blast radius — including the DM's chosen fireball victim from Arc B's observation task.

The blast shatters the front windows of Trollskull Manor, Corellon's Crown, and the Bent Nail. Smoke fills the alley. Characters inside the manor take no damage but hear the detonation and feel the building shake. Characters in the alley within 40 feet of the blast must succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw or take 8d6 fire damage (half on a success).

*The Victim.* The party finds the body of the NPC chosen during Arc B — the person the DM identified as the player-bonded casualty. The victim is dead. No ambiguity, no last words, no dramatic rescue window. The body is among the wreckage alongside Dalakhar (a gnome none of them have met) and two burned Zhentarim corpses. A third Zhentarim agent, Urstul Floxin, lies wounded twenty feet from the blast center, conscious but unable to stand.

*The Immediate Aftermath.* Within seconds of the blast, neighbors respond. Fala Lefaliir (if alive) rushes from Corellon's Crown with healing supplies. Embric appears with a bucket of sand. Tally Fellbranch starts clearing debris from the street. The alley community the party built in Arc B is visible in crisis — helping, panicking, or frozen. The party has approximately ten minutes before the Watch arrives to either examine the scene freely, tend to the wounded, or pursue the fleeing nimblewright (which is already gone — see Three Clue Paths below).

*The Crime Scene.* The blast site contains the following evidence layers, each requiring different checks:

- **Surface evidence (no check):** Dalakhar's body, two Zhentarim corpses, Urstul Floxin wounded, blast pattern radiating from a single point above street level, shattered storefronts, the fireball victim's body.
- **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** The blast originated from an object, not a spell — no somatic or verbal components, and the residue matches alchemical fire rather than arcane evocation. A character proficient in Arcana identifies a fragment of a necklace of fireballs bead.
- **DC 14 Wisdom (Perception):** Dalakhar's pockets have been turned out. Whatever he carried is gone. His belt pouch is open and empty. Someone searched his body after the explosion but before anyone else reached the scene.
- **DC 16 Intelligence (Arcana):** The fireball's magical signature carries a faint secondary resonance — a transmutation trace consistent with a construct's stored-spell mechanism, not a humanoid caster. This corroborates witness accounts of a mechanical figure.

*The Watch Arrives.* Barnibus Blastwind and Saeth Cromley arrive with a squad of six Watch constables. Barnibus is a senior investigator of the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors — methodical, dry, and genuinely competent. Saeth is his muscle — a retired soldier who handles crowd control and physical evidence. The Watch cordons the scene, begins taking statements, and asks the party to remain available for questioning.

**Party reaction branches — the Watch:**

- *Party cooperates fully:* Barnibus shares information freely. He identifies Dalakhar as a known associate of Lord Dagult Neverember's intelligence network. He does not know what Dalakhar was carrying. He asks the party to report anything they learn — and means it. The Watch investigation runs parallel to the party's.
- *Party withholds information:* Barnibus notices. He does not confront them immediately but assigns Saeth to keep an eye on their movements. The Watch investigation proceeds without the party's input, slower and less effective. Barnibus is disappointed, not hostile.
- *Party claims jurisdiction (faction authority):* A party member enrolled in Force Grey, the Lords' Alliance, or the Harpers can invoke faction standing to request investigative access. Barnibus accepts Force Grey authority without argument (Vajra outranks him). Lords' Alliance or Harper standing requires a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check. Success grants the party crime-scene access and Barnibus's cooperation. Failure doesn't close the door — Barnibus is cautious, not obstructive.

*Urstul Floxin.* The wounded Zhentarim agent is conscious and coherent enough to talk. He gives his real name — Urstul Floxin — and claims to be a merchant who was passing through the alley when the explosion hit. A DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check reveals the merchant story is a lie. A DC 16 Intelligence (Investigation) check on his person reveals weapons and equipment inconsistent with a merchant — he is carrying a shortsword, three daggers, and a vial of assassin's blood poison.

If questioned about what he saw: Floxin describes a "metal man" — humanoid, fast, moving with mechanical precision — that ran toward the gnome, threw something, and then bent over the body after the explosion. He saw it take "a small object, a stone or maybe a gem" from the gnome's robes before running south. This testimony is genuine. Floxin watched the nimblewright steal the Stone of Golorr from Dalakhar, and he wants it back. His willingness to describe the nimblewright is self-serving — he wants the party to track it so he can follow them to it.

If the party leaves Floxin unattended, he slips away within the hour. He reports to Manshoon and begins his own operation to track the nimblewright back to Gralhund Villa. If the party detains him or hands him to the Watch, he gives nothing further and is released within 24 hours on insufficient evidence. Either way, the Zhentarim are now tracking the same lead.

*Three Independent Clue Paths to Revelation: "A Nimblewright Caused the Explosion"*

- **Witness Testimony.** Multiple witnesses describe a mechanical figure (see Scene 2 for the full witness list). Cross-referencing three or more accounts produces a consistent description: humanoid, fast, articulated joints, no visible face. A character who attended the Twin Parades in Arc B Scene 7 and succeeded on the DC 14 Perception check recognizes the description immediately.
- **Crime Scene Evidence.** The DC 16 Arcana check on the blast site reveals construct-origin magic. Combined with the necklace of fireballs fragment (DC 12 Investigation), this narrows the cause to a construct wielding a magical item — not a spellcaster.
- **Urstul Floxin's Testimony.** Floxin describes a "metal man" in specific enough terms that a character proficient in Arcana or History can identify the description as a nimblewright — a rare Lantanese construct. DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana or History).

**Tone:** Visceral, then procedural. The explosion is chaos. The investigation that follows is controlled. The shift from one to the other is the party deciding to act rather than react.

### Scene 2: The Witnesses

**Purpose:** Deliver the expanded witness pool. Each witness provides a different evidence fragment; no single interview completes the picture. The party must synthesize testimony across multiple sources — their first intelligence-analysis exercise.

**Content:**

*The Witness Pool.* The Alexandrian's expanded witness list includes every Trollskull Alley NPC and several bystanders. Each witness saw a different fragment of the event. The party can interview them in any order.

- **Fala Lefaliir (if alive).** Was tending the greenhouse counter when the explosion blew out the front window. Saw "something running" through the smoke immediately after the blast — fast, low to the ground, heading south. Did not see the explosion itself. Can confirm Dalakhar had been in the alley several times over the past few days, "looking for someone." Fala assumed he was looking for the party.
- **Jezrynne Hornraven.** A Waterdavian noble passing through the alley on errands. Saw the explosion from the north end of the street. Watched a cloaked figure throw something small that detonated midair. Did not see the figure's face or hands. Describes the figure as "moving wrong — too fast, too smooth, like a puppet on invisible strings." Will share her account with the Watch but is reluctant to testify publicly; her family has political entanglements she does not want scrutinized.
- **Martem Trec.** A teenager running an errand for his mother. Was across the street when the blast hit. Ducked behind a horse trough. Saw the aftermath: a figure bending over the gnome's body, rifling through pockets, then "running so fast it didn't look real." Describes the figure as "shiny, like armor, but the joints were wrong." Martem is shaken and eager to help.
- **Urstul Floxin.** See Scene 1. His testimony is the most detailed but also the most strategically motivated.
- **Tally Fellbranch (if alive).** Was inside the Bent Nail workshop. Heard the blast, came outside, saw smoke and bodies. Did not see the nimblewright. Can confirm that a gnome matching Dalakhar's description was seen near the alley entrance twice in the previous three days, asking passersby about "the adventurers who live here."
- **Embric and Avi.** Embric heard the blast from the forge. Avi saw a "flash of silver" moving south past the greenhouse before the smoke cleared — approximately three seconds after the explosion. Neither saw the throw or the figure's full body.
- **Vincent Trench.** Arrived at the scene after the explosion. Claims to have been "indisposed." A DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check reveals that Trench knows more than he's saying. He will not volunteer information, but if pressed with a DC 16 Charisma (Persuasion) check, he admits he saw the nimblewright from his second-floor window — "a construct of Lantanese manufacture, moving with purpose, heading for the harbor." He identified it immediately but chose not to involve himself. He is a rakshasa. He has seen nimblewrights before. He does not explain how.
- **Rishaal the Page-Turner.** Was reading in his shop. Heard the blast, came outside after the initial chaos subsided. Rishaal contributes no visual evidence but offers a research path: he has encountered references to nimblewrights in Lantanese engineering texts in his collection. If asked, he produces a slim volume within the hour that describes nimblewrights as "autonomous constructs of exceptional dexterity, crafted by Lantanese artificers and the clergy of Gond." The text names the House of Inspired Hands as Waterdeep's only known temple of Gond.

*Renaer's Testimony.* Renaer Neverember arrives at the scene within two hours — either summoned by the party or drawn by news of the explosion in his friend's neighborhood. His testimony is the most important because he knows something no witness can provide: why Dalakhar was here.

Dalakhar sent Renaer a paper bird the morning of the explosion. The message read: "I have the Stone. Your father's agents and worse are on my trail. I cannot reach the palace. I am coming to Trollskull Manor. Tell the adventurers. They are the only people in this city I have not been able to connect to any faction." Renaer read the message but could not act on it before the fireball struck.

Renaer knows that Dalakhar was his father's most trusted spy. He knows that the Stone of Golorr is connected to Lord Dagult Neverember's hidden treasure, but he does not know where the treasure is or what the Stone does exactly — only that his father considered it the key to everything. Renaer does not know about the Eyes.

If the party asks Renaer why he didn't warn them: he admits he didn't take the message seriously enough. Dalakhar had sent paper birds before, always claiming urgency, always about his father's paranoia. This time was real, and Renaer carries that.

*Faction Response Teams Deploy.* The fireball triggers immediate faction interest. Within 24 hours of the explosion, every villain faction deploys assets to monitor the investigation — and, where possible, to stay ahead of it:

- **Xanathar Guild:** Xanathar knows Dalakhar stole his Stone. He does not yet know who killed Dalakhar or where the Stone went. He dispatches gazers to surveil the crime scene and follow anyone who seems to be investigating. The party may spot gazers (DC 16 Wisdom (Perception)) hovering near rooflines in Trollskull Alley over the following days.
- **Manshoon's Zhentarim:** Urstul Floxin reports to Manshoon. Manshoon now knows a nimblewright killed Dalakhar and took the Stone. Zhentarim agents begin their own investigation, running parallel to the party's. The party may encounter Zhent operatives asking the same questions they are.
- **Bregan D'aerthe:** If Jarlaxle was tipped off during the nimblewright investigation (Scene 3), he already knows the Gralhund nimblewright is the culprit — his clairvoyance crystals recorded it. His agents observe but do not intervene until the party reaches the Sea Maidens Faire connection.
- **Cassalanters:** The Cassalanters learn of the fireball through their social network. They know the Stone is in play. Ammalia's response is indirect — she sends condolences to the party (flowers and a note expressing horror at the violence "in your beautiful neighborhood") and waits to see how the investigation develops. Cross-ref Scene 4 for the Cassalanter approach.

**Tone:** Procedural, layered. Each interview adds a fragment. The party assembles the picture by talking to people — the fundamental investigative skill the campaign will require for the next twenty sessions.

### Scene 3: The Nimblewright Hunt

**Purpose:** Convert the revelation "a nimblewright did it" into an actionable investigation. Introduce the House of Inspired Hands as an information source. Arm the party with the nimblewright detector. Structure the city-wide search as a sequence of elimination visits that build the world while narrowing the suspect list.

**Content:**

*The House of Inspired Hands.* The temple of Gond in the Sea Ward is a workshop as much as a place of worship. Clockwork devices fill every surface. The party's arrival — asking about nimblewrights — triggers two reactions.

First, **Valetta**, a dragonborn priest of Gond, explains what nimblewrights are: autonomous constructs of Lantanese manufacture, programmed by their creators, capable of independent decision-making within their operational parameters. Only a handful exist in Waterdeep. Valetta knows of one in the temple — Nim — and suspects others have been sold through private channels. She does not know who sold them or to whom.

Second, **Nim**, the temple's nimblewright, reacts to the party's questions with visible agitation. Nim is not the culprit — Nim has not left the temple in months — but Nim knows that other nimblewrights exist in the city and is afraid of being blamed. If the party treats Nim with patience rather than suspicion, Nim offers to build a nimblewright detector: a handheld device that vibrates and spins when within 500 feet of another nimblewright. Construction takes 24 hours. If the party is hostile to Nim, Valetta intervenes and the detector is still offered, but Nim does not trust the party and provides no further help.

*Three Independent Clue Paths to the House of Inspired Hands:*

- **Rishaal's Research.** Rishaal's Lantanese engineering text names the House of Inspired Hands as Waterdeep's only Gond temple. The party arrives with context.
- **Faction Intelligence.** A faction contact (Harper, Force Grey, Lords' Alliance, or Doom Raiders) identifies "nimblewright" from the party's description and directs them to the House of Inspired Hands. Harpers provide the fastest response (Remallia Haventree knows the temple's high priest personally).
- **City Watch Cooperation.** If the party is cooperating with Barnibus Blastwind, his Watchful Order contacts identify the construct type and recommend the Gond temple within 48 hours.

*The Nimblewright Detector.* Once built, the detector is a brass-and-crystal device the size of a compass. It spins and vibrates when within 500 feet of a nimblewright, spinning faster as the wielder approaches. It does not distinguish between nimblewrights — it detects all of them equally. The party must visit each known or suspected nimblewright location and either confirm or eliminate the owner's construct.

*The Nine Nimblewright Owners.* Jarlaxle sold nimblewrights to nine buyers across Waterdeep — each at a loss, because every nimblewright contains a hidden clairvoyance crystal feeding intelligence back to a crystal ball aboard the Scarlet Marpenoth. The party does not know this yet. What they know is that the detector identifies multiple signals across the city, and they need to visit each one.

The nine owners and their nimblewrights:

1. **The House of Inspired Hands (Sea Ward).** Nim. Already confirmed non-culprit if the party investigated the temple. The detector activates here, confirming the device works.
2. **Bowgentra Summertaen (Sea Ward).** An aging wizard who keeps her nimblewright as a household servant. The construct answers the door, serves tea, and is clearly incapable of independent action at the level required for the fireball attack. Bowgentra is offended by the suggestion.
3. **Lord Adarbrent (Sea Ward).** A minor noble who purchased the nimblewright as a bodyguard. The construct stands at attention in the foyer and has not left the estate in three months. Lord Adarbrent produces household logs documenting its position.
4. **Lord Dezlentyr (North Ward).** An elderly lord who keeps his nimblewright as a personal attendant. The construct assists with mobility and household tasks. Dezlentyr is frail and reliant on it; the nimblewright has not left his side.
5. **The House of Wonder (Sea Ward).** The temple of Mystra maintains a nimblewright as a temple guardian. The high priest confirms the construct has been on temple grounds continuously.
6. **Mother Tamra's House of Pleasure and Healing (Trades Ward).** A halfling matron runs a combination hospice and pleasure house. Her nimblewright serves as a porter and night watchman. Mother Tamra is protective and suspicious of the party's questions but cooperates when shown the Watch's authority or a faction badge.
7. **The City Armory (Castle Ward).** A nimblewright serves as a demonstrator for the Master of Arms. The construct runs weapon drills for visiting dignitaries. Its operational log is maintained by the Armory staff.
8. **The Gralhund Estate (North Ward).** The detector activates strongly near the estate's walls. The party cannot see the nimblewright from outside — it is inside the villa. If they approach the gate, they are told by guards that Lord and Lady Gralhund are not receiving visitors. The detector's signal is unmistakable. This is the strongest response outside the Sea Maidens Faire.
9. **A Faction Member's Nimblewright.** One nimblewright was sold to an agent within a player faction — the specific faction depends on which factions the party has joined. This creates a brief, uncomfortable moment when the detector activates near a trusted contact's location. The nimblewright turns out to be a gift from "a Luskan merchant" (Jarlaxle in disguise) and is used for mundane courier work. Cleared quickly but plants the Jarlaxle-sells-nimblewrights thread.

The elimination sequence is designed to be played as a montage across one to three in-game days. Not every visit requires a full scene — most can be resolved in two or three sentences. The DM should play out visits that advance the plot or characterize the city, and summarize the rest.

*Two Signals Remain.* After eliminating the non-suspects, two strong signals persist: the Sea Maidens Faire in the harbor and the Gralhund Estate in the North Ward. The party must decide which to investigate first — and how. This decision is the fulcrum of Arc C. Cross-ref Scene 4 for the Sea Maidens Faire path. A party that goes directly to Gralhund Villa transitions to Arc D.

**Tone:** Investigative montage. The mechanical process of elimination — visiting, checking, clearing — teaches the party that intelligence work in Waterdeep runs on shoe leather and social competence, not divination magic.

### Scene 4: The Sea Maidens Faire

**Purpose:** Connect the nimblewright investigation to Jarlaxle Baenre's operation. Provide three paths from "the Faire has nimblewrights" to "the Gralhunds own the one that killed Dalakhar." Each path carries different consequences for Jarlaxle's future posture toward the party.

**Content:**

*Arrival at the Faire.* The Sea Maidens Faire occupies three ships docked in Waterdeep Harbor: the Eyecatcher (carnival ship, open to the public), the Heartbreaker (crew quarters, restricted), and the Scarlet Marpenoth (submarine, hidden). The nimblewright detector goes haywire near the harbor — multiple signals from the ships. The Faire employs nimblewrights as performers, stagehands, and cargo handlers. The sheer number of signals makes it impossible to identify a specific culprit through the detector alone. The party needs records.

*Zardoz Zord.* The Faire's Illuskan captain — who the party met in Arc B Scene 5 at the Field of Triumph — greets them with the same theatrical warmth. If the party mentions nimblewrights, Zord's reaction depends on how they frame the question:

**Party asks about nimblewrights in general:** Zord explains freely that the Faire manufactures and sells nimblewrights as a commercial enterprise. He names the construction process, the programming limitations, and the general buyer profile — wealthy collectors who want autonomous servants. He does not volunteer the buyer list.

**Party asks who bought nimblewrights:** Zord pauses. He is willing to share the information, but not for free. He offers a trade: the buyer list in exchange for the party's account of why they are looking — specifically, what happened in Trollskull Alley and what they know about the Stone of Golorr. A DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) check reduces this to a general account without naming the Stone. Failure means Zord insists on the full story, including the Stone. This path is the easiest to the Gralhund connection but it tips Jarlaxle off to everything the party knows. Jarlaxle (as Zord) will use this intelligence to position Bregan D'aerthe agents at Gralhund Villa before the party arrives.

**Party demands the list:** Zord declines politely but firmly. He is a businessman; he protects client confidentiality. A DC 18 Charisma (Intimidation) check gets a partial list (he provides three names but not the Gralhunds — "some clients paid for discretion"). Failure produces nothing and earns the party Zord's professional displeasure.

*Three Paths to the Gralhund Connection:*

- **Path 1: Ask Zord (Easy, Tips Off Jarlaxle).** Zord provides the full buyer list, including "Lord and Lady Gralhund, North Ward." He notes that the Gralhund nimblewright was "a custom commission — combat programming, which is unusual for a household model." This is the fastest route to Arc D. Consequence: Jarlaxle deploys Bregan D'aerthe agents to Gralhund Villa. When the party arrives, Jarlaxle's team is already positioned in Artheyn Manor across the street. Cross-ref Arc D, Gralhund Villa observation teams.
- **Path 2: Steal Jarlaxle's Records (Difficult, Preserves Secrecy).** The party can attempt to steal the Nimblewright Ledger — Jarlaxle's complete record of every nimblewright sold, with purchaser names, delivery addresses, programming notes, and a voided Cassalanter entry. The Ledger is kept aboard the Eyecatcher in Zord's stateroom (locked, trapped, guarded by a nimblewright valet). This is a mini-heist — a preview of the full Arc H operation. DC 16 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) to pick the stateroom lock; DC 14 Dexterity (Stealth) to avoid the nimblewright valet; DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation) to locate the Ledger among Zord's extensive papers. Consequence: Jarlaxle discovers the theft within 24 hours but does not know who took it. He does not deploy to Gralhund Villa. The party arrives at the villa without Bregan D'aerthe already in position.
- **Path 3: Access the Crystal Ball (Hardest, Maximum Intelligence).** The Scarlet Marpenoth — Jarlaxle's submarine docked beneath the Eyecatcher — contains a Nimblewright Crystal Ball that receives clairvoyance feeds from every sold nimblewright's hidden crystal. The party would need to discover the submarine's existence (DC 18 Wisdom (Perception) from the harbor docks at night, or a tip from a faction contact), infiltrate it, and operate the crystal ball. This path provides not just the Gralhund connection but actual recorded footage of the nimblewright committing the murder. Consequence: This is extremely difficult for a 3rd-level party and is designed as an aspirational target — something they may attempt and fail, establishing the Scarlet Marpenoth as a high-value target for Arc H's full heist.

*The Voided Cassalanter Entry.* Regardless of which path the party uses, the Nimblewright Ledger (or Zord's verbal account if Path 1 is used) contains one anomalous entry: a Cassalanter commission that was placed, programmed, and then voided before delivery. The reason field reads "Buyer withdrew — unspecified concerns re: detection." This detail matters in Arc G, where the party discovers why the Cassalanters cancelled: Victoro realized the nimblewrights might contain surveillance devices. He was right.

*The Harbourmaster's Registry (Passive Clue).* A Harper-connected harbourmaster's registry, accessible through faction channels or a DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) check at the harbor offices, references a "Ledger of Commissioned Automata" filed by the Sea Maidens Faire upon docking. The registry does not contain the ledger's contents, but it confirms the ledger's existence — useful for parties pursuing Path 2 or 3 who need confirmation that the records exist before committing to a theft.

*The Cassalanter Approach.* Before the party reaches Gralhund Villa — ideally after they have identified the Gralhund connection but before they act on it — the Cassalanters make direct contact. Ammalia Cassalanter sends an invitation to dinner at the Cassalanter estate. The invitation is warm, personal, and makes no mention of the investigation, the Stone, or the Grand Game. It references the fireball: "We were horrified to learn of the violence in your neighborhood. Lord Cassalanter and I would be honored to offer our support, and perhaps our perspective, over dinner."

If the party accepts: the dinner is genuine. Ammalia and Victoro are gracious hosts. Over the course of the evening, Victoro steers the conversation toward Dalakhar, the Stone, and the party's investigation — not aggressively, but with the precision of a man who already knows most of the answers. He offers the party an alliance: share what they learn about the Stone's location, and the Cassalanters will provide financial support, political cover, and information of their own. He does not explain why the Cassalanters want the Stone. He does not mention the children, the pact, or Asmodeus. He is offering a deal that appears generous and reasonable.

If the party declines: no immediate consequence. The Cassalanters are patient. They will make contact again in Arc E.

**Tone:** Layered, transactional. Every conversation in this scene is a negotiation. The party is learning that information has a price and that every person who offers help is also pursuing their own objective.

### Scene 5: Backtracking Dalakhar

**Purpose:** Optional investigation branch that rewards thorough parties with additional context about the Grand Game's history and a connection to the Cassalanters through Kalain. This scene is non-essential for reaching Gralhund Villa — it deepens the world without gating progression.

**Content:**

*Tracking Dalakhar.* Once the party identifies Dalakhar as the fireball's target, they may attempt to trace his movements before the explosion. A DC 13 Charisma (Investigation) check at inns and boarding houses in the Trades Ward tracks him to the Inn of the Dripping Dagger, where he rented a room for one night.

*The Inn of the Dripping Dagger.* Dalakhar's room has been scrubbed clean in a professional fashion. A DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) check finds ash in the base of the room's oil lamp — a piece of correspondence was burned. The burned document was Renaer's response to Dalakhar's paper bird, confirming the Trollskull Manor meeting. On the pillow, in plain sight: a round disk of black stone painted with Xanathar's stylized beholder sigil. It is a death mark, left by Xanathar's agents who tracked Dalakhar to the inn.

*The Xanathar Response Team.* The room is under surveillance by a Xanathar response team. If the party enters, the team accosts them to learn what they know about Dalakhar. Cross-ref **Appendix E** (Xanathar response team roster and behavior).

Intelligence the response team possesses, scaled by time since the fireball:

- **More than 1 day since Dalakhar's death:** The response team has been briefed on the death and knows that "the boss knows a guy named Floxin — one of those Zhent bastards — was following that gnome; the boss has got eyes on Floxin now." This gives the party an alternative route to the Gralhunds: track Urstul Floxin. Floxin is currently operating out of Yellowspire (a Zhentarim outpost in the Castle Ward). Following him leads to Gralhund Villa.
- **More than 3 days since Dalakhar's death:** The response team is pulled from this location. The room is empty.

*Kalain's Letter.* Four days after Dalakhar's death, a letter arrives at the Inn of the Dripping Dagger addressed to him: "Dalakhar, I had to give considerable thought to your request. But you were always kind to me even when your demonic master was not. If you are still in need of my aid, you may claim whatever sanctuary I can offer. — Kalain of the Nine Waters."

The letter arrives late by design. Parties who left their names with the innkeeper, or who specifically asked to be contacted if new information surfaced, receive it as a proactive push — the inn sends word that a letter arrived for the dead man. This rewards thorough investigation behavior and makes the world feel functional beyond the party's immediate line of sight.

*Kalain's Tower.* Kalain lives in a dilapidated tower in the Sea Ward. She is a half-elf painter, once famous, now isolated by madness and Lord Dagult Neverember's systematic destruction of her reputation and career. She was Dagult's mistress; their affair ended when he chose Neverwinter over her. Her paintings are increasingly monstrous — she can animate them (creatures CR 3 or lower, lasting 1 minute).

Kalain is paranoid. She believes everyone is an assassin sent by Dagult. She loses conversational threads. She rubs her cheek with increasing agitation. A DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) check calms her enough for a coherent conversation. If told Dalakhar was killed, she blames the party for killing him on Neverember's orders and escalates toward violence unless quickly reassured.

What Kalain can provide:

- **Dalakhar's mission:** She knows Dalakhar was a spy for Lord Dagult. He sent a letter requesting sanctuary.
- **The vault:** During their final days together, Dagult was obsessed with a "Melairkyn ceremonial temple or religious vault." She cannot remember where it is — the Stone of Golorr's memory-suppressing effect prevents it.
- **Victoro Cassalanter:** If specifically asked, she mentions speaking with Lord Victoro Cassalanter about the vault "a few weeks ago." She does not volunteer this. This establishes a Kalain-to-Cassalanter link that the party can follow in either direction.

*Lord Dagult's Papers.* A document lockbox in Kalain's studio contains papers left behind by Lord Dagult:

- A list of tasks including "move the dragon to the Melairkyn ceremonial vault."
- Correspondence with Hammond Kraddoc documenting bribes to cover up a Dock Ward scandal.
- Notes on the Melairkyn vault: built by worshippers of Dumathoin, the Keeper of the Mountain's Secrets. The vault can be opened by "standing before Dumathoin's doors and striking the scale of a dragon with a mithral hammer in the place where the sun's light should fall."
- An unsigned letter from four years ago stating "the last of the three Eyes has been secured."

These papers are vault-opening clues — they pay off in Arc J. The party may not understand their significance now, but any player who takes notes will recognize them when the vault sequence arrives.

**Tone:** Elegiac, secondary. Kalain's tower is a dead end inhabited by a broken person. The emotional register is sadness and waste, not urgency. The scene rewards curiosity without punishing parties who skip it entirely.

*The Stone of Golorr — What the Party Knows.* By the end of Arc C, the party should understand the following:

- A gnome spy named Dalakhar stole an artifact called the Stone of Golorr from Xanathar.
- Dalakhar was killed by a nimblewright belonging to the Gralhund family.
- The nimblewright took the Stone from Dalakhar's body and returned to Gralhund Villa.
- The Stone is the key to Lord Dagult Neverember's hidden treasure — half a million gold dragons concealed somewhere beneath Waterdeep.
- Every major faction in the city is pursuing the Stone.

They may also know (depending on investigation thoroughness):

- The Stone has three "Eyes" that were separated from it as a security measure.
- The Cassalanters are willing to ally with the party — for reasons they have not disclosed.
- Jarlaxle Baenre operates the Sea Maidens Faire under the identity of Zardoz Zord.
- Manshoon's Zhentarim are tracking the same leads.
- The vault can be opened with specific ceremonial keys (from Dagult's papers).

*Mad Mage Seed.* When the party first examines the Stone of Golorr (which they do not yet possess — this seed activates in Arc D when they recover it), it whispers of "a dreaming mind below the city, older than the dwarves who built the vault, older than the dragon that guards it." This is the aboleth Illuun on Undermountain Level 4. The whisper is brief and unsettling. The party cannot act on it now.

*Transition to Arc D.* The investigation points to Gralhund Villa. What the party does next — approach the villa directly, surveil it, bring the Watch, or contact a faction for support — determines how Arc D opens. The transition is a decision point, not a scripted handoff.

**Tone:** Conclusive, directional. The investigation phase is over. The party knows where to go. What they do not know is that three other factions are converging on the same location.

### Design Notes: Fireball

## Investigation Architecture

***The Three-Phase Structure.*** The original Dragon Heist collapses the entire fireball investigation into a single linear chain: explosion → House of Inspired Hands → nimblewright detector → Gralhund Villa. The Alexandrian Remix expands this into three phases (identify the construct → find all owners → determine which one), and this structuring draft preserves that expansion because it solves three problems simultaneously.

First, the linear chain is fragile. If the party misses the House of Inspired Hands — because they don't think to ask about temples of Gond, or because they pursue a different lead first — the investigation stalls. The three-phase structure provides multiple entry points to each phase, ensuring no single missed connection halts progress.

Second, the expanded investigation teaches the party how intelligence-gathering works in Waterdeep. Arc C is the tutorial for the outpost and lair heists that follow. Visiting nine nimblewright owners across four wards — talking to nobles, priests, merchants, and military officials — builds the party's mental map of Waterdeep's power structures and social conventions. This knowledge pays off every time they need to infiltrate a faction outpost or gather pre-heist intelligence.

Third, the elimination sequence creates dramatic tension through narrowing. Nine signals become eight, become seven. Each cleared owner makes the remaining signals more significant. The two signals that persist — the Sea Maidens Faire and the Gralhund Estate — carry weight because the party has done the work to eliminate everything else.

***Why the Nimblewright Steals the Stone.*** In the original adventure, Urstul Floxin takes the Stone from Dalakhar's body. The Alexandrian Remix changes this to the nimblewright, and this draft follows that change for structural reasons. If Floxin takes the Stone, the investigation becomes "find Floxin" — a person-hunt that the Zhentarim can resolve offscreen by hiding him. If the nimblewright takes the Stone, the investigation becomes "find the nimblewright" — a construct-hunt that requires the party to build an entirely new investigative tool (the detector) and canvas the city. The construct-hunt is a better investigation because it is systematic, teachable, and leads to the broader discovery of Jarlaxle's nimblewright network.

The change also preserves Floxin as a witness rather than a fugitive. Floxin at the crime scene — wounded, talking, strategically sharing information — is more useful to the campaign than Floxin fleeing with the MacGuffin. He becomes a source the party can interrogate, a loose end they must manage, and a Zhentarim agent whose parallel investigation creates time pressure.

## The Fireball Victim

***Arc B's Payoff.*** The fireball victim is chosen by the DM based on Arc B observation — which NPC the players bonded with most naturally. This design note explains why the choice matters structurally, not just emotionally.

A fireball that kills strangers is a plot event. A fireball that kills someone the party cared about is a motivation engine. The investigation that follows runs on the party's desire for answers — and that desire must be personal, not abstract. "Who killed our friend" sustains twenty hours of investigation. "Who set off a bomb in our neighborhood" sustains three.

The victim is chosen late (after Arc B, not during session zero) because the DM cannot predict which NPC will land with a specific table. The candidate list in Arc B's Milestone section covers the highest-probability bonds, but the DM's observation during actual play overrides the list. If the players bonded with an NPC not on the list — a staff candidate, a faction contact, a Skullport trader — that NPC is the correct victim.

## Faction Visibility

***All Four Factions in Motion.*** Arc A introduced two factions through direct conflict (Xanathar Guild and Zhentarim). Arc B introduced all four through recruitment and social presence. Arc C is the first arc where all four villain factions are simultaneously pursuing the same objective — and the party can see them doing it. Gazers on rooftops. Zhentarim agents asking the same questions. Bregan D'aerthe observers shadowing the investigation. The Cassalanters extending a dinner invitation.

This visibility is the structural argument for why the party's situation is genuinely dangerous. It is not enough for the DM to tell the players that powerful factions are interested in the Stone. The players must see the factions' agents, encounter their evidence, and feel the pressure of competing investigations running in parallel. Arc C's faction response team deployments are not flavor — they are the campaign's central tension made visible for the first time.

## Jarlaxle's Entry into the Grand Game

***Conditional Activation.*** The plan file establishes that Jarlaxle enters the Grand Game actively only if the party's nimblewright investigation leads to the Sea Maidens Faire. If they bypass the Faire entirely — going straight from the nimblewright detector's signal to Gralhund Villa — Jarlaxle remains a background player. His agents observe but do not position at Gralhund Villa, and Arc H is a quieter affair.

This conditionality is preserved because it rewards thorough investigation. A party that traces the nimblewrights to their source discovers the full scope of Jarlaxle's operation — the clairvoyance crystals, the buyer ledger, the voided Cassalanter entry — and pulls the most dangerous intelligence operative in Waterdeep into active competition. A party that skips the Faire and goes straight to the villa gets the Stone (probably) but misses the Jarlaxle thread. Both paths work. The thorough path is richer.

## The Cassalanter Approach

***First Direct Villain Contact.*** The Cassalanters reaching out before Gralhund Villa is an Alexandrian recommendation that this draft adopts because it solves a pacing problem. Without the dinner invitation, the Cassalanters are invisible between Ammalia's Arc B Grand Opening appearance and their full reveal in Arc G — a gap of potentially ten or more sessions. The Arc C dinner keeps them in the party's awareness, establishes Victoro as a character (not just a name), and creates the first moment where the party must decide whether to accept help from people whose motives they do not understand.

The dinner is also designed to make the party complicit. If they share information with the Cassalanters, the Cassalanters use that information. The party's choice to trust or distrust Victoro's offer shapes the Cassalanters' intelligence advantage going into Act III. This is agency with consequences — exactly the structural principle the three pillars demand.

## The Optional Branch

***Backtracking Dalakhar as Reward for Thoroughness.*** Scene 5 exists for parties that investigate exhaustively. It provides vault-opening clues (Dagult's papers), a Cassalanter connection (Kalain's conversation with Victoro), and world depth (Kalain herself — a casualty of Dagult Neverember's personal destruction who has no direct role in the Grand Game but whose existence makes the setting feel lived-in).

The scene is deliberately non-essential. No critical-path clue is gated behind Kalain. The vault-opening information appears in revelation lists elsewhere (Stone of Golorr visions, Cassalanter study of the Grand Game, Gralhund papers). Parties that skip Scene 5 miss color, not progress. Parties that find it are rewarded with the satisfaction of connecting threads the campaign did not force them to find — the highest-value investigative experience the game can deliver.

The delayed letter is an important design element. Kalain's response arrives four days after Dalakhar's death — after the party has likely already visited the inn once. This pushes a clue to thorough parties who left contact information, rewarding the investigative behavior of asking innkeepers to follow up if anything changes. The world responds to player initiative. This is what makes an investigation feel alive rather than pre-scripted.

## 3. Key Decisions Before Writing

All eleven decisions settled from source evidence:

1. **The nimblewright steals the Stone, not Floxin.** Per Alexandrian continuity. Floxin becomes a crime-scene witness; the nimblewright becomes the investigation target. This restructures the entire arc around construct-tracking rather than person-hunting.

2. **Urstul Floxin survives and talks.** He gives his real name, lies about being a merchant, and accurately describes the nimblewright. His testimony is self-serving — he wants the party to find the nimblewright so the Zhentarim can follow them to it.

3. **Nine nimblewright owners.** Per the Alexandrian's expanded list. The elimination sequence doubles as a city-tour and intelligence-gathering tutorial.

4. **Three paths to the Gralhund connection.** Ask Zord (easy, tips off Jarlaxle), steal the Ledger (difficult, preserves secrecy), access the crystal ball (hardest, maximum intelligence). Each path has distinct consequences for Jarlaxle's posture and Bregan D'aerthe's deployment at Gralhund Villa.

5. **Jarlaxle's conditional activation.** If the party's investigation reaches the Sea Maidens Faire, Jarlaxle enters the Grand Game actively. If they bypass the Faire, he remains a background player. Per the plan file, Design Decision 3.

6. **The Cassalanter dinner.** Ammalia and Victoro host the party before Gralhund Villa. Victoro steers conversation toward the Stone and offers an alliance. No mention of the children, the pact, or Asmodeus. Per Alexandrian recommendation.

7. **Renaer knows about the Stone.** Dalakhar's paper bird told him. Renaer is a source of Stone-of-Golorr context — he knows his father considered it "the key to everything" — but does not know about the Eyes or the vault's location.

8. **The fireball victim is DM-chosen.** Per Arc B Milestone section. No additional candidates added; the Arc B list is sufficient.

9. **Backtracking Dalakhar is optional.** Non-essential for reaching Gralhund Villa. Provides vault clues, a Cassalanter connection through Kalain, and an Xanathar response team encounter. Gated behind proactive investigative behavior (leaving contact information at the inn).

10. **Clairvoyance crystals in nimblewrights.** Per the Alexandrian. Jarlaxle sold nimblewrights at a loss because each contains a hidden crystal feeding back to the Scarlet Marpenoth's crystal ball. The party may or may not discover this in Arc C; it becomes fully visible in Arc H.

11. **Mid-arc level-up.** Level 4 is earned within Arc C when the investigation reaches critical mass (three major revelations recovered). This matches the plan file's "Level 4 earned mid-arc" specification.
