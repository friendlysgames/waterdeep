# Faction Outposts

---

## Chapter Purpose

**Faction Outposts** changes the campaign's rhythm entirely. Where Acts I and II moved in a single investigative current — gathering evidence, following a thread, converging on a confrontation — **Faction Outposts** spreads that current into four separate streams. The dramatic question shifts from "where does the trail lead?" to "which lead do we follow first?" The party holds the Stone of Golorr. They know three Eyes are needed. They have glimpses of where those Eyes might be. Everything that follows is intelligence-gathering under time pressure, across four simultaneous enemy fronts.

This is the outpost phase: preparation for the chapters to come. The goal is to enter each lair heist knowing enough to have a plan, and to complicate that goal by raising the alert level of each faction whose intelligence is taken.

---

## 1. Core Concept

Ten outposts are distributed across Waterdeep's wards, maintained by four factions, each one a data point in the web of the Grand Game. The party cannot storm the lairs blind — Xanathar's sanctum, Cassalanter Villa's temple, the Sea Maidens Faire, and Kolat Towers are not targets until the party knows enough to attempt them. The outposts are the research: entering a Xanatharian front to find how the Guild accesses its own lair, or breaking into a cultist's apartment to find a map the Cassalanters spent months assembling.

Two outpost types govern how each entry plays:

**Mini-heist outposts** follow an abbreviated five-step framework: Gather Information, Case the Target, Acquire Supplies, The Operation, Getaway. These are the four targets warranting expanded treatment because each one gates a major lair approach or a critical mechanical resource. The party plans and executes; outcomes are not predetermined by the DM.

**Encounter-plus outposts** have keyed areas, a roster, multiple approach options, and planted documents, but no extended planning framework. They are intelligence waypoints — worth hitting, not worth staging a full operation around.

The ten outposts are not a gauntlet. They are a menu. The party's choices about which to hit, in which order, against which factions, are the gameplay of **Faction Outposts**. The DM's job is to keep all four threads visible and let the pressure mount.

## 2. Sections

### Quest Opener

*An adventure for 4th-level characters.*

When Dagult Neverember was removed from the Open Lord's chair eleven years ago, he did not surrender his deepest secret. The Vault of Dragons — five hundred thousand gold dragons cached below Waterdeep in a chamber sealed by the Stone of Golorr and commanded only by Neverember's spoken word — stayed hidden. He removed the Stone's eyes to blind it, then distributed all three Eyes to parties he calculated would keep them safe: one to a drow arms dealer whose silence could be purchased, one entrusted to agents whose infernal patrons guaranteed survival, and one brought deeper into the criminal networks beneath the city than any investigator would comfortably follow.

That calculation is no longer accurate. Neverember is in Neverwinter. The parties holding his Eyes have been in Waterdeep long enough to develop their own plans for whatever the Stone might reveal. Jarlaxle Baenre holds his Eye aboard the *Scarlet Marpenoth*, where it serves as leverage and curiosity in equal measure. The Cassalanter family, working through a network of cult agents and infernal diviners, located a second Eye buried in the crypt of a noblewoman who died without heirs, and extracted it. Xanathar holds the third in the deep sanctum of the lair beneath Skullport, where it arrived years ago as payment for silence and has remained in the dark ever since.

Manshoon wants none of the Eyes. He wants the Stone itself — specifically, the compressed aboleth memory within it, which encodes structural knowledge of the Weave that Mystra's servants have never catalogued. The vault is an instrument he would use to fund his actual goal. The Stone is the goal. He does not hold an Eye because he never intended to open the vault the way Neverember did — only to hold the Stone long enough to extract what he needs.

The Stone now sits with the party. The Watch crackdown following the Gralhund Villa incident has disrupted Manshoon's Brindul Alley operation, scattered the Zhentarim response teams, and landed Davil Starsong in Watch custody on circumstantial evidence. Xanathar's Guild is consolidating — pulling agents tighter, increasing surveillance. Jarlaxle watches the party with quiet interest and no urgency; he holds something they need, and he is in no hurry to discuss terms. The Cassalanters, undisturbed, continue their research. Below the city's peaceful surface, four factions know the Stone has surfaced. Three of them want it back.

### Scene 1: The Stone Speaks

**Purpose:** Transition from **Gralhund Villa** into **Faction Outposts**. The Stone of Golorr, now in the party's possession, reveals the three-Eye structure. The MacGuffin delivers its first substantial information, reframing the party's understanding of what they are holding and what it will take to use it.

**Content:**

*Attunement.* One character must attune to the Stone. The attunement requires no ritual — the Stone initiates contact the first time a character holds it with intent. As the attuning character focuses, the Stone reads their memories. It does not ask permission. The process takes one minute and leaves the character with a low, cold impression of something ancient paying close attention to them. See **Running the Villains** (GM Guide) for the Stone's full properties.

*The Three Eyes.* Once attuned, the Stone communicates in impressions rather than words. The message is clear regardless: three gemstone Eyes were removed from the Stone's face by Neverember when he chose to hide the vault from everyone, including the Stone. Without all three Eyes set in their proper sockets, the Stone cannot produce the vault's location or the command phrase to open it. It knows the Eyes exist. It knows they were separated. It has residual psychic impressions from the last beings that handled each one.

*Eye Impressions.* Three fragmentary visions, each pointing toward a faction without naming it:

A beholder's eye, unblinking in darkness. Carved stone wet from underground springs. The smell of stagnant water and something chemical. A long way down.

Red light through colored glass. Incense smoke rising to a vaulted ceiling. Above the stone corridors, somewhere: children's laughter.

Salt air and creaking wood. A dark-skinned elf holding a gemstone to a lantern, examining its facets. The sound of harbor water against a hull.

None of the visions identify who holds each Eye or where the holder can be found. They describe proximity to the Eye's last resting location — the feel of the spaces where each one has sat. A party with intelligence about Waterdeep's factions will recognize what the visions point toward. A party without that intelligence will need to gather it.

*Stone Knowledge Expansion (Forward Note — Implement in Heist Arcs).* The Alexandrian Remix establishes that the Stone's knowledge expands incrementally as each Eye is restored. With zero Eyes, it yields only the fragmentary impressions above. With one Eye, it reveals additional details. With two, more still. All three Eyes unlock the vault's precise location and command phrase. This expansion belongs in the lair heists (**Xanathar's Lair**, **Cassalanter Villa**, **Sea Maidens Faire**) where Eyes are actually recovered — not in this quest. This note exists so the mechanic is not overlooked when those arcs are drafted. Cross-ref `sources/8. The Eyes of the Stone.pdf` for the authoritative expansion table.

*The Fourth Faction.* The Stone does not produce a fourth vision, because Manshoon holds no Eye. If pressed — if the attuning character asks whether there are others — the Stone responds differently — an awareness rather than a vision. Something watches from a tower. It watches the Stone, not the vault. The impression ends abruptly; the Stone does not elaborate.

*Mad Mage Seed.* The attuning character's connection to the Stone changes something. Illuun's dreaming — the aboleth consciousness compressed within the Stone — leaks slightly more than before. Not enough to communicate. Enough to feel: a cold patience beneath the city, not geographic but geological, present in the stone itself rather than in any particular chamber. When the attuning character asks about this, the Stone changes the subject.

*Founders' Day Clock.* Attunement occurs approximately Ches 25th in the campaign calendar. Founders' Day — Flamerule 1st — is roughly three months away (approximately 97 days). The party does not know this deadline yet. The DM tracks it.

**Tone:** Intimate and strange. The Stone is a compressed aboleth consciousness performing contractual service — it cooperates because it is compelled to, not because it wants to help. Its communication is efficient and alien. The party should feel they are using something that was not designed with their comfort in mind, and that the thing is simultaneously more and less than they expected.

### Scene 2: What the Factions Say

**Purpose:** Genuine consultation round before the party commits to their first outpost target. Each contact delivers a tactically distinct, self-interested analysis calibrated to their faction's knowledge and agenda. Mirrors **Gralhund Villa**, Scene 1 in format; differs in register — this consultation is about operational commitment, not dramatic alignment.

**Content:**

*Mirt (Harpers — renown 1+).* The Harpers have been watching the Cassalanter family for years, and Mirt's summary reflects sustained, inconclusive surveillance. The Cassalanters give more to charity than houses twice their wealth, never miss a temple service, and have not made a single political enemy in a decade. "Nobody is that clean," Mirt says, without pleasure. He does not know what they are hiding. The Harpers have found no evidence of wrongdoing — only the complete absence of any wrongdoing at all, which in Waterdeep's political ecology is itself suspicious. He recommends starting with Xanathar outposts on the grounds that the Guild's paranoia makes early strikes more effective than late ones. His unstated reason: the Harpers have already exhausted their Xanathar intelligence, and the Cassalanter question is the one he cannot answer and cannot let go.

*Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance — renown 1+).* The Alliance has Watch patrol schedules for every ward, available to the party with LA credentials at renown 3 or higher. Jalester's advice is careful and nonspecific: reconnaissance before entry, Watch contact windows, the legal calculus of unauthorized access to private premises. He is building an intelligence file on all four factions and makes no secret of wanting the party to share what they find from all outposts, not just whichever they target. He does not volunteer that the Lords' Alliance has independent surveillance on Yellowspire; the party can earn that information by demonstrating commitment to the Manshoon thread.

*Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave — renown 1+).* Jeryth Phaulkon has been monitoring a disturbance in the Southern Ward for two weeks. The resonance is infernal in nature — a persistent presence that touches the ley lines near Coachlamp Lane in a way Jeryth describes as a wound that isn't healing. The Enclave knows the disturbance is infernal but has no lead on the source. "Jeryth felt it from Phaulkonmere. It is not natural and it is not stopping." Melannor is framing this as an environmental problem, not an investigation lead. Parties who trace the resonance to its source will find the Cassalanter chain's entry point. Melannor does not know this.

*Savra Belabranta (Order of the Gauntlet — renown 1+).* The Gauntlet's intelligence on Manshoon comes from prior mission fallout — the Splinter's intermediary network, the checkpoint strike near the Trade Ward, circumstantial connections to Brindul Alley. Savra has not verified the address, but she knows the Splinter keeps a Trade Ward safe house and that prisoners taken from previous encounters were being directed somewhere. Her concern is justice: she wants the torture operation exposed, and she wants it done properly — meaning with survivors willing to testify. She does not mention that the Gauntlet has its own reasons for wanting Manshoon's legal exposure rather than his elimination.

*Vajra Safahr (Force Grey — renown 1+).* Vajra wants BD's operational infrastructure — the theater, the message drops, the communication codes — before anyone approaches the Sea Maidens Faire directly. "The harbor is my problem. The shore is yours." She will not share what the Blackstaff's intelligence says about the Faire's defenses. What she wants is documented BD communication architecture, which she can use to build a containment plan for the Faire that does not require Force Grey to fire on civilian ships. She does not share the containment plan with the party.

*Tashlyn Yafeera (Doom Raiders — renown 1+).* Tashlyn has replaced Davil Starsong, who is in Watch custody on Gralhund-related charges. She is colder and more operational, and she establishes this in the first exchange: "Davil talked too much and trusted too easily. I won't make that mistake." She has been watching Yellowspire for two months and has schematics — structural layout, entry points, guard rotation estimates — which she will share when the party is ready to move, not before. Her immediate lead is tactical: every Manshoon agent carries a pass-amulet to Kolat Towers. Capturing one means one fewer entry point they can use. The subtext of everything Tashlyn says: she needs Manshoon neutralized because Manshoon neutralized is the precondition for Davil's release.

*Jarlaxle (Bregan D'aerthe — renown 1+).* Available only if a PC is a BD member (recruited during **Trollskull Alley**). Jarlaxle is always the primary Bregan D'aerthe contact, appearing in one of his surface identities — J.B. Nevercott or another persona appropriate to the channel established since **Trollskull Alley**. At renown 3 or higher, he has already briefed the Seven Masks Theater door staff; the party can meet him there freely. At renown 0, the theater is a location on a list, not a standing invitation. His approach this early in the quest is characteristically oblique. He does not offer tactical intelligence on other factions. What he offers is an acknowledgment: "You've been busy since Gralhund. I've been watching. I know what you're looking for, and I know where some of it is." The terms he proposes — actual terms, not favors — are information exchange, to be negotiated when both sides know what the other has. His unstated position: he wants to know what the Stone revealed before offering anything of equivalent value.

**Tone:** More transactional than **Gralhund Villa**, Scene 1. That consultation asked whether the party would act. This one asks where they will act first. Every contact has done homework. Every contact's analysis reflects their intelligence gaps as much as their intelligence. The party should feel the web of competing agendas tightening, and notice that all seven contacts are nudging them toward action — each for reasons that serve the faction offering the nudge.

### Scene 3: The Outpost Phase — How It Works

**Purpose:** DM-facing structural guide. This is not a scene the players experience. It provides the operational framework needed to run ten outposts in any order, across four factions, as a coherent arc rather than a sequence of disconnected encounters.

**Content:**

*Player-Driven Order.* The party chooses which faction to investigate first, which chain to complete first, and whether to pursue multiple chains in parallel. There is no prescribed sequence. The DM makes all four faction threads visible through Scene 2 and the Stone's impressions, then steps back. If the party pursues two chains simultaneously, each faction tracks its own escalation state independently, and spillover rules govern cross-faction effects.

*Outpost Tiers.* Two types:

**Encounter-plus** outposts have keyed areas, a roster, approach options, and planted documents, described under headings of *Approach*, *Key Areas*, *Intel Yield*, and *Escalation*. The party enters, navigates the space, acquires intelligence, and deals with the consequences.

**Mini-heist** outposts use an abbreviated five-step framework: *Gather Information*, *Case the Target*, *Acquire Supplies*, *The Operation*, and *Getaway*. Steps 1–3 describe what the party can learn before entering. Step 4 is the operation itself. Step 5 governs consequences and escalation. These targets warrant the expanded framework because each one gates a major resource.

*Finding Outposts Without Faction Help.* Every faction chain has at least one entry point discoverable without faction contacts and without prior outpost documents — through the party's own investigation. These are the street-level entry points:

**Xanathar Guild:** Grinda Garloth's operates openly in Mistshore as a black-market armorer. Anyone asking about Xanathar weapons or Guild suppliers in the Dock Ward gets pointed to her within a day. No credentials required.

**Manshoon's Zhentarim:** Parties who searched Urstul Floxin's effects at Gralhund Villa have documents pointing to Brindul Alley. Parties that did not can find the Interrogation House through Trade Ward canvassing — the safe house's traffic is unusual enough to notice if someone is looking (DC 14 Investigation over a day).

**Cassalanter outposts:** The infernal presence near Coachlamp Lane is detectable by any character with Divine Sense within 60 feet of the Converted Windmill, and confirmable with *detect evil and good*. Parties without such abilities can find the location through Coachlamp Lane neighborhood inquiry — neighbors report strange smells, unusual visitors at odd hours, a couple who never quite fit in (DC 13 Charisma, no faction required).

**Bregan D'aerthe:** The Seven Masks Theater is a functioning public theater in the Dock Ward. "Rongquan Mystere" is a name in theater district playbills. Any investigation into drow surface activity or Dock Ward entertainment leads here without a check.

*Revelation Lists.* Every outpost yields intelligence about its own faction's lair and at least one clue pointing toward a different faction. Captured documents are live intelligence, not flavor text. See **Running the Villains** (GM Guide) for the authoritative revelation list. This quest orchestrates those lists; it does not reproduce them.

*Escalation — Baked-In Consequences.* Each outpost entry specifies exactly what happens to that faction's alert state when the outpost is hit. No DM judgment between sessions is required. Each outpost's Getaway section names the exact tier the faction moves to, which response team deploys, and on what timeline. The four-tier system: **Unaware** (faction doesn't know the party exists as a threat), **Suspicious** (faction has noticed unexplained losses), **Alert** (faction knows the party is active, response teams deployed), **Lockdown** (faction in full security posture, lair defenses hardened). The DM reads the Getaway entry and applies it.

*Cross-Faction Spillover.* Xanathar and Manshoon are actively at war; each monitors the other's losses. Hitting a Xanathar outpost raises Manshoon's alert tier by 1, and vice versa — both factions assume the other is responsible before they conclude it was the party. Cassalanter imp surveillance of the Sea Maidens Faire creates a reciprocal intelligence channel; Cassalanter losses alert Jarlaxle that something is moving against infernal-adjacent operations in the city. See **Running the Villains** (GM Guide) for full inter-faction dynamics.

*Founders' Day Clock.* Every outpost entry includes a time estimate: how many in-game days the operation consumes. Encounter-plus operations run 1–3 days. Mini-heist operations run 3–7 days. Total duration: two to four tendays. The party learns about Founders' Day (Flamerule 1st) through Cassalanter intelligence and faction contacts, not through this section. The DM tracks the calendar and makes the approaching deadline legible through environmental detail — festival preparations appearing in the wards, Cassalanter documents referencing a family obligation in Flamerule, contacts mentioning the Open Lord's Founders' Day calendar.

**Tone:** Operational. This section is the DM's tool reference. The language is precise and the voice is direct.

### Scene 4: Xanathar Guild Outposts

**Purpose:** Present three Xanathar outposts as a linked intelligence chain, each one peeling back a layer of organized crime — a gentleman's clinic, an arms dealership, a confectioners' front over a duergar smuggling cellar. All three feed into **Xanathar's Lair**.

**Tone:** Criminal underworld. Each outpost peels back a layer of organized crime. The party traces a supply chain: weapons ordered through a respectable front, filled by a Dock Ward armorer, delivered to an underground dock that feeds into the lair itself.

---

#### 4A: Terasse Estate (Mini-Heist)

*Castle Ward, Elsambul's Lane.*

*Step 1 — Gather Information.* Three paths to Terasse Estate before the party enters it: the *Instructions for Gladiatorial Weaponry* found at Grinda Garloth's (names Terasse as a delivery point for exotic arms); the mechanical beholder letter at Orb Confectioners (names Terasse as the delivery address and dates it); or, if **Cassalanter Villa** has been completed first, a Cassalanter Villa report referencing the gladiatorial supply chain. Parties lacking these documents can attempt entry by bluffing past the door with the healing passphrase (DC 18 Deception if improvising) or by obtaining the passphrase from an interrogated Xanathar response team member.

*Step 2 — Case the Target.* A modest, unremarkable rowhouse. Two kinds of visitors come: those seeking healing (legitimate front), and those attending gladiatorial events in Xanathar's Lair. Two passphrases control entry: "I heard the red hand was hot" accesses the healing operation; "the bawdy star shines bright tonight" gets a guest past the front rooms. See the **Xanathar's Guild** Factions guide page for the full area layout.

*Step 3 — Acquire Supplies.* The healing passphrase is enough to enter. The gladiatorial passphrase reaches the trap door on the upper floor. Optional: wealthy patron disguises for parties attempting to blend in as potential gladiatorial spectators.

*Step 4 — The Operation.* Primary objectives: recover the *Instructions for Gladiatorial Weaponry* from Keln's desk (Area 6–7), which names Grinda Garloth as the supplier and Ahmaergo as the authorizing agent; recover the *Injury Reports* logbook (Area 5 Upper Landing), which contains an entry noting a wound caused by "incautious observation of the windmill on Coachlamp Lane" — a cross-faction clue pointing toward the Cassalanter outpost chain; recover the *Ill Man's Note* (Area 8–10 Sick Rooms), which contains the Orb Confectioners passphrase in verse. Optional: follow a guest being transported to the gladiatorial event. The trap door in the kitchen leads to an underground dock; guests are blindfolded before descent and transported by boat to Area X4 of Xanathar's Lair. This route is a genuine lair entry method, but arriving via gladiatorial transport means arriving unprepared, blindfolded, and unarmed. See the **Xanathar's Guild** Factions guide page for keyed area details.

*Step 5 — Getaway.* Keln Trigos (priest) and his guards raise the alarm when any objective area is breached: the trap door seals from below, the front door bars, and a flying snake courier carries word to the Lair. The alarm window is 1d4+2 rounds before lockdown. **Escalation: Xanathar moves to Suspicious (Tier 2).** If a second Xanathar outpost has already been hit, Xanathar moves directly to Alert (Tier 3) and Gorath's response team deploys to the last known party location within 2 days.

**Founders' Day Clock: 2–4 days.**

#### 4B: Grinda Garloth's (Encounter-Plus)

*Dock Ward, Mistshore.* — **Street-level entry point for the Xanathar chain.**

*Approach.* Grinda operates openly as a black-market armorer. Anyone asking about Xanathar weapons, Guild suppliers, or black-market arms in the Dock Ward gets pointed to Mistshore within a day (DC 12 Charisma in Dock Ward taverns or street contacts; no faction membership required). Three additional paths: the *Instructions for Gladiatorial Weaponry* from Terasse Estate names her; a captured Xanathar response team member's *Directive from Nar'l* names her as a supplier; Agorn Fuoco's *Report to Agorn Fuoco* (from any Zhentarim response team) confirms her location. Parties can approach as customers, investigators, or raiders. Grinda does not fight for the Guild — she fights for her business. She will negotiate if the party offers more immediate value than Xanathar does, and she will report the encounter afterward.

*Key Areas.* Area D2: 500 gp and the *Note Regarding Gladiatorial Weaponry* from Ahmaergo, confirming delivery to Terasse Estate and citing Xanathar's expectation of "exotic gutting." Area D3: mundane arms and armor including single-shot pistols; 1d4 pieces of magic arms or armor (DMG Table F); 1d4 miscellaneous magic items (DMG Table A or B). See the **Xanathar's Guild** Factions guide page for full area descriptions.

*Intel Yield.* The Ahmaergo correspondence confirms the Terasse connection and establishes Ahmaergo as a named Guild lieutenant. Grinda's knowledge of sewer patrol routes is available through negotiation or by locating her operational logbook — partial maps of Xanathar's Lair approach corridors. The magic item inventory is genuine loot.

**Escalation: Xanathar moves to Suspicious (Tier 2) if this outpost is raided; no tier change if the party engages as customers without taking documents.** Response teams do not deploy from a single customer interaction. **Founders' Day Clock: 1–2 days.**

#### 4C: Orb Confectioners / Xanathar Sewer Hideout (Encounter-Plus)

*Southern Ward, Rising Ride.*

An eye-themed confectioners' shop — smallcakes, candied nuts, ocular sugar sculptures — over a duergar operation run by Korgstrod Uxgulm.

*Approach.* Three paths to Orb Confectioners: a captured Xanathar response team member carries the passphrase or the location in their directive; Agorn Fuoco's *Report to Agorn Fuoco* names Orb Confectioners on Rising Ride; Terasse Estate's *Ill Man's Note* contains the passphrase in verse. The passphrase exchange: ask for "eight small eyes." The counter-staff responds: "How would you like those arranged?" Answer: "Around an ocular cake." Correct answers earn an escort to the cellar stairs. Use the Autumn Cellar Complex layout (DH p. 86). See the **Xanathar's Guild** organization page.

*Key Areas.* Area B7: *On the Delivery of a Mechanical Beholder* from Ahmaergo, setting a delivery deadline of Tarsakh 9th for a mechanical beholder to Terasse Estate — confirms the gladiatorial supply chain and ties Ahmaergo to Xanathar's engineering operations. Area B9b: a **mimic** disguised as the chest at the foot of Korgstrod's bed. A hidden coffer under the bed (DC 13 to find) holds 500 gp and *Correspondence with Nar'l Xibrindas*: Nar'l's letter references smokepowder and provides guildsign directions toward Area X1 of Xanathar's Lair. The guildsign trail requires DC 15 checks, rising to DC 17 near the lair itself; three failures before a success means the trail is lost. See the **Xanathar's Guild** organization page.

*Intel Yield.* Nar'l's correspondence is the primary entry path to Xanathar's Lair via the guildsign trail. The smokepowder reference seeds Nar'l's double-agent status — he is also in contact with Jarlaxle, a fact that pays off in **Xanathar's Lair** and **Sea Maidens Faire** when the BD-Xanathar connection surfaces.

**Escalation: Xanathar moves to Alert (Tier 3) if this outpost is cleared.** Korgstrod's response — invisibility, enlargement, and four duergar fighting to the death — signals this location is more important than the others. Response teams deploy within 1d4 days of the operation. **Founders' Day Clock: 1–3 days.**

### Scene 5: Manshoon's Zhentarim Outposts

**Purpose:** Two outposts that define Manshoon's character through contrast. The Interrogation House is ugly and deliberate — a place where precision curdles into cruelty. Yellowspire is quieter and stranger — a Banite tower hiding in plain sight in the Castle Ward. Together they deliver the campaign's first named reference to Manshoon and the only bypass route to Kolat Towers that avoids the force field.

**Tone:** Espionage thriller. Manshoon's operations are surgical. The Interrogation House is the exception — deliberately ugly, reminding the party that his patience has limits. Yellowspire is quieter, stranger, a Banite tower in plain sight.

---

#### 5A: Interrogation House (Encounter-Plus)

*Trade Ward, Brindul Alley.* — **Street-level entry point for the Manshoon chain** (for parties with **Gralhund Villa** documents).

*Approach.* Four paths to the Interrogation House: the *Directive to Urstul Floxin* from **Gralhund Villa** names Avareen Windrivver and Brindul Alley explicitly; Yellowspire's crumpled note from Avareen (for parties who hit Yellowspire first); questioning a captured Zhentarim response team member (prisoners taken by Zhent teams are brought here — they'll say so); or Trade Ward canvassing for parties lacking **Gralhund Villa** documents (DC 14 Investigation over a day; no faction membership required). The house appears unremarkable from the street. Use house L1 layout (DH p. 62).

*Key Areas.* Front Room: Avareen Windrivver (spy) and Zorbog Jyarkoth (thug) with three black flying snakes. Avareen carries a pass-amulet to Kolat Towers; her first instinct if captured is to destroy it. Backroom: a torture rack, an arcane circle, and Ott Steeltoes strapped to a chair.

*The Directive to Zorbog.* A flying snake arrives during or shortly after the party enters, bearing a sealed letter addressed to Zorbog. The letter is a *Directive to Zorbog* signed **Manshoon** — the first document in the campaign bearing his name as a signature. It orders the kidnap of Fenerus Stormcastle of Quill Alley, specifying delivery to the Interrogation House within four days. The party now knows: there is a named Manshoon. He is the Splinter's leader. He is targeting a specific civilian. And his directive's phrasing suggests awareness of the party's recent activities.

*Ott Steeltoes.* A gnome prisoner wearing a leather skullcap stitched with fake beholder eyestalks — an Xanatharian agent captured during the Guild war. Ott does not know what the Stone of Golorr is, but he knows Xanathar's Lair. If rescued and cultivated, he offers to introduce the party to the Guild or guide them personally to the lair's entrance. If freed without follow-through, he disappears back into Xanathar's network. See the **Ott Steeltoes** page in Notable Figures and the **Xanathar's Guild** organization page.

*Intel Yield.* Manshoon's name (first use in the campaign). The pass-amulet from Avareen (route to Kolat Towers). Ott Steeltoes as a potential **Xanathar's Lair** contact. The *Directive to Zorbog* naming Fenerus Stormcastle — a cross-faction thread pointing toward Bregan D'aerthe (Fenerus is a BD lamplighter; Manshoon has identified him as a soft target).

**Escalation: Manshoon moves to Alert (Tier 3).** Avareen's flying snakes carry the alarm faster than the party can move; if even one escapes, Manshoon knows within hours. Vevette Blackwater's team deploys within 1d4 days. If Xanathar cross-faction spillover applies, Manshoon moves directly to Lockdown (Tier 4). **Founders' Day Clock: 1–2 days.**

#### 5B: Yellowspire (Mini-Heist)

*Castle Ward.*

*Step 1 — Gather Information.* Five paths to Yellowspire: tracking Urstul Floxin from the Gralhund Villa aftermath (he returns to Yellowspire when his safe houses are compromised); questioning or threatening Agorn Fuoco (he gives up Amath Seccent's name and address without much resistance); the *Missive from Castle Ward* in Area G12 of Gralhund Villa (**Gralhund Villa** planted thread); Tashlyn's two months of Doom Raiders surveillance (she shares the schematics when the party indicates readiness); or the crumpled note from Avareen at the Interrogation House, which names Yellowspire as a communication address.

*Step 2 — Case the Target.* Use the Winter Old Tower layout (DH p. 79). Amath Seccent (priest) occupies the tower with four acolytes. Agorn Fuoco is present on a one-in-three chance. After **Gralhund Villa**, Chirada watches the tower from across the street on any visit — she is tracking the Stone's movements on behalf of remaining Gralhund interests, following up on the *Missive from Castle Ward*. DC 15 Perception to notice her. Cross-ref **Gralhund Villa**, Scene 6 Aftermath and Design Notes.

*Step 3 — Acquire Supplies.* No passphrase governs Yellowspire. The party needs a plan for Amath's wards and four acolytes who take orders from a committed Banite. Tashlyn's schematics, if obtained, identify the teleportation circle's location on the lower floor.

*Step 4 — The Operation.* Primary objective: locate and document the permanent teleportation circle to Kolat Towers. The circle is active. Stepping through it immediately means arriving at Kolat Towers unannounced and unprepared — a one-way trip that bypasses the force field, which is Yellowspire's entire value to Manshoon. Secondary: a crumpled note from Avareen, cross-referencing the Interrogation House. Tertiary: Agorn's pass-amulet to Kolat Towers, which he surrenders without hesitation in exchange for his life.

*The Chirada Complication.* If the Gralhunds remain active following **Gralhund Villa**, Chirada may mount her own investigation of the tower, complicating the party's operation. She is not an enemy by default — she is a competing investigator following the same evidence trail. The DM determines whether she arrives during the operation, after it, or is dealt with beforehand based on whether the party noticed her during reconnaissance.

*Step 5 — Getaway.* Amath fights with conviction; acolytes are less committed. Agorn flees. **Escalation: Manshoon moves to Suspicious (Tier 2) if the Interrogation House has not been hit; moves to Lockdown (Tier 4) if it has.** Agorn carries word to surviving Zhentarim contacts if he escapes. Response teams redeploy within 1d4 days.

**Founders' Day Clock: 3–5 days.**

### Scene 6: Cassalanter Outposts

**Purpose:** Two outposts structured as religious horror. Through their own investigation, the party discovers what no faction contact has managed to uncover: that the Cassalanters are connected to genuine infernal operations. The Asmodean Shrine is a place of worship, not a warehouse. The Converted Windmill is domestic horror — two people living normal lives around a blood pentagram and a map the Cassalanters have been assembling for months.

**Tone:** Religious horror. The Asmodean Shrine communicates authentic devotion, not crime. The Converted Windmill: two people living ordinary lives in a space with a blood pentagram and a dragon's vault map. Domestic normalcy against demonic devotion.

---

#### 6A: Asmodean Shrine (Encounter-Plus)

*North Ward, Aveen Street.*

*Approach.* The shrine's location is actively concealed; it requires at least one prior lead to find. Four paths: the Converted Windmill's meeting note ("Meeting Ches 17th, the shrine on Aveen Street") — circular only if the party finds the windmill first; the *Instructions from Lord Cassalanter* found inside the shrine on the apprentice wizard — circular if already inside; a BD report from the Sea Maidens Faire referencing imp activity in the North Ward, if **Sea Maidens Faire** has been completed first; or proactive Harper assistance — Mirt can connect the party with an Enclave contact who has been narrowing the Southern Ward infernal resonance to a North Ward secondary signature. There is no purely independent street-level entry; at least one prior outpost document or faction connection is required.

*Key Areas.* Eight areas; see the **Cassalanters** Factions guide page for full room descriptions. Critical areas for this quest:

Area 1 — Summoning Circle: two imps present, refreshed every nine days.

Area 2 — Styxian Shrine: anyone who lingers without succeeding on a DC 14 Wisdom saving throw loses eight hours of recent memory. The loss feels like waking from a dream — not a gap, but a seamless continuity that has been quietly rewritten. An apprentice wizard prays here; he carries *Instructions from Lord Cassalanter* directing imp surveillance of the Sea Maidens Faire.

Area 4 — Chapter House: an animate object statue guards the room (50 hp, AC 10, +6 to hit, 2d20+2 damage). On a shelf: *Arn's Book of Worship*, naming Arn Xalrondar and citing his address as "the Windmill upon Coachlamp Lane." This is the bridge between the two Cassalanter outposts.

Area 6 — Mirror of the Fiend: a tarnished floor mirror bearing an infernal sigil. DC 13 History identifies the sigil as a noble house marking; DC 16 identifies the house as Cassalanter. This is the arc's first solid evidence linking the Cassalanters to infernal operations — not a rumor, not suspicious charity, but a physical object bearing their family crest inside an Asmodean shrine.

Area 7 — Nine Hells Mural: a detailed depiction of Avernus. A DC 15 Arcana check recognizes architectural elements consistent with Asmodean temple design — foreshadowing what the party will encounter beneath the villa in **Cassalanter Villa**.

Area 8 — Hell Cyst: a pinprick of planar material from the Nine Hells, ancient and sealed, that functions as a focus for the shrine's rites. It cannot be traversed. It pulses with cold light when rituals are performed.

*Intel Yield.* Arn's Book of Worship names Arn and the Coachlamp Lane windmill — the bridge to the Converted Windmill. The Cassalanter mirror sigil provides the family identification. The *Instructions from Lord Cassalanter* confirms an active Cassalanter–BD surveillance operation. Cultists questioned under pressure name Cassalanter Villa and describe Victoro as the operation's director. A party that recovers all four pieces of intelligence now knows more about the Cassalanters than the Harpers have gathered in years.

**Escalation: Cassalanters move to Alert (Tier 3).** Imp survivors report to the family within hours. Seffia Naelryke's response team deploys within 2 days. If BD outposts have also been hit (cross-faction spillover), the Cassalanters flag the party as a threat to their Faire surveillance arrangement. See the **Cassalanters** Factions guide page (Response Teams section). **Founders' Day Clock: 1–2 days.**

#### 6B: Converted Windmill (Mini-Heist)

*Southern Ward, Coachlamp Lane.* — **Street-level entry point for the Cassalanter chain** (for parties with Divine Sense).

*Step 1 — Gather Information.* Five paths to the Converted Windmill: Seffia Naelryke's visiting card found on Vevette Blackwater from a Zhentarim response team (Seffia approached Vevette to open a communication channel; Vevette rebuffed her, suspecting a trap, but kept the card); Arn's Book of Worship at the Asmodean Shrine; the *Injury Reports* logbook at Terasse Estate ("the windmill on Coachlamp Lane"); a Cassalanter Villa report if **Cassalanter Villa** is completed first; or the infernal presence detected by Divine Sense within 60 feet of the building (DC 12 Wisdom for any character with Divine Sense; no faction required). Parties without Divine Sense can reach the windmill through Coachlamp Lane neighborhood inquiry — neighbors report strange smells, unusual visitors after midnight, a couple who never quite fit in (DC 13 Charisma; no faction required).

*Step 2 — Case the Target.* An apartment building that was once a working windmill. Seffia Naelryke and Arn Xalrondar live on the upper floor as ordinary tenants. An invisible imp watches from the windmill sails (DC 16 Perception, or *detect magic* automatically). Use the Summer Converted Windmill layout (DH p. 88). See the **Cassalanters** Factions guide page.

*Step 3 — Acquire Supplies.* No passphrase. The challenge is identifying which apartment belongs to Seffia and Arn — the building has other tenants — and entering without alerting the sentry imp. An imp that escapes invisible carries word to the Cassalanters within hours.

*Step 4 — The Operation.* Area W7 — Secure Apartment: beneath the bed, a note reading "Meeting Ches 17th, the shrine on Aveen Street." This links the two Cassalanter outposts for parties who haven't visited the shrine yet.

Area W8 — Room of Rituals: a blood pentagram drawn in salt and ash. A DC 15 Arcana check reveals it as a demonic divination circle. The working notes describe a search for "Neverember," "Golorr," and "that which has been lost to the waters of Lethe." A desk beside the pentagram holds the most important document in this quest: **a map of Waterdeep with the Brandath Crypt circled in red ink.** This map is the primary clue path to **Vault of Dragons**'s physical entrance. Seffia carries *A Letter from Victoro* confirming the Third Eye was found "in the crypt of Lady Brandath" — this letter requires either stealth or a confrontation to obtain.

*Step 5 — Getaway.* Seffia Naelryke (cult fanatic) and Arn Xalrondar (cult fanatic) with the sentry imp. Seffia fights with conviction — she is not a frightened acolyte but a dedicated operative who has worked years toward this goal. Arn follows her lead. The imp's ability to escape unseen is the primary complication; the party must neutralize it or accept the escalation consequence. **Escalation: Cassalanters move to Lockdown (Tier 4) if the shrine has already been hit; move to Alert (Tier 3) if this is the first Cassalanter outpost.** Response team deploys within 1d4 days. If the imp escapes, the Cassalanters know what was taken.

**Founders' Day Clock: 3–5 days.**

### Scene 7: Bregan D'aerthe Outposts

**Purpose:** Two outposts structured as noir intelligence work. Fenerus Stormcastle is a civilian in over his head. The Seven Masks Theater is glamorous on the surface, haunted in the walls, and hiding something operational in a dressing room. Malcolm Brizzenbright's ghost is the emotional counterweight to both: a man who wanted none of this and got all of it.

**Tone:** Noir espionage. Fenerus is a scared civilian. The theater is glamorous on the surface, haunted underneath, hiding something operational in the walls. Malcolm Brizzenbright is the emotional surprise — a ghost who wants his theater run honestly.

---

#### 7A: Fenerus Stormcastle's House (Encounter-Plus)

*Trade Ward, Quill Alley.*

*Approach.* Three paths to Fenerus: the Seven Masks Theater intelligence archive (a letter from "K" to "J" referencing Fenerus as a BD field contact and his address at Quill Alley); the *Directive to Zorbog* from the Interrogation House (Manshoon is targeting Fenerus — his address is in the directive); or a captured BD response team member's *Sealed Letter from Krebbyg*, naming Fenerus as a message recipient.

*The Kidnap Complication.* The *Directive to Zorbog* orders Fenerus captured within four days of issuance. This complication is live — it fires 1d6+2 days after the directive is issued, regardless of whether the party acts. Three possible states when the party arrives at Quill Alley:

If the party warned Fenerus: the house is barricaded, and he is frightened but cooperative. He shares everything he knows.

If the party did not warn him and arrives before the kidnap team: Zorbog's team may be in the alley or entering the building. A three-way encounter develops — party, Zhentarim kidnappers, and Fenerus in the middle.

If the party arrives after Fenerus has been taken: the house shows signs of struggle. The coded messages are undisturbed — Zorbog was after Fenerus, not intelligence. The trail leads back to the Interrogation House.

*Key Areas.* Per DH p. 63. The intelligence in the house is coded messages in Drow (DC 16 Intelligence to decode; advantage if the character reads Drow): a Gralhund Villa observation report signed by Fel'rekt Lafeen; theater business notes; a nimblewright delivery note addressed to Bowgentra Summertaen; a guild licensing note concerning the Sea Maidens Faire. Staking out the house and following Krebbyg Masq'il'yr (he visits every 1d3 days to drop or collect messages) leads directly to the Faire.

*Intel Yield.* Decoded messages provide multiple independent paths to the Sea Maidens Faire as the next target. Fenerus's testimony — if he is present and cooperative — confirms that messages travel to a contact at the Faire and that "Rongquan Mystere" is not the theater owner's real identity. The Gralhund observation report is historical intelligence confirming BD knew about the villa operation before it happened.

**Escalation: Bregan D'aerthe moves to Suspicious (Tier 2) if the party searches the house without BD renown; no tier change if they visit openly with Jarlaxle's introduction.** If Fenerus has been kidnapped and the party visits an empty house, no escalation triggers — the kidnap is not traceable to the party from the BD perspective. **Founders' Day Clock: 1–3 days (longer if staking out for Krebbyg).**

#### 7B: Seven Masks Theater (Mini-Heist)

*Dock Ward.* — **Street-level entry point for the BD chain.**

"Rongquan Mystere" is a name in Dock Ward playbills. Any investigation into drow surface operations, theater district contacts, or the Faire connection leads here without a check; the theater is listed in public directories.

*Step 1 — Gather Information.* Four paths to the theater as a BD outpost rather than a public venue: Fenerus's decoded messages (theater play titles serve as coded correspondence; one decoded message lists a stage address resolving to a dressing room number); a Xanathar's Lair report naming the Seven Masks Theater as a BD surface operation (available after **Xanathar's Lair**); Soluun Xibrindas's *Bloodstained Playbill* recovered from a BD response team, marked with a seat number pointing to the safehouse dressing room; or public theater investigation — noticing that one dressing room is never listed on the cast sheet.

*Step 2 — Case the Target.* Use the Autumn theater layout (DH p. 75). The theater runs performances on a regular schedule. One dressing room — never occupied by any listed cast member — is kept for BD use. With BD renown 3 or higher, the door staff have been briefed; the party enters freely. With renown 0, the party must find another approach: performance tickets and social navigation, an after-hours entry, or theater district contacts.

*Step 3 — Acquire Supplies.* At BD renown 3 or higher: nothing required. At renown 0: tickets for a performance (2 sp each) establish legitimate lobby access. After-hours entry requires DC 14 Dexterity (Stealth) past the stage door guard.

*Step 4 — The Operation.* Primary objective: the BD safehouse dressing room and its stored intelligence. Stage Manager's Office (P6): *A Letter from K to J* — Krebbyg writing to Jarlaxle — warning that Fenerus's address has become known to a third party and recommending a new message drop. This letter cross-references the Fenerus outpost and confirms the BD communication architecture.

Malcolm Brizzenbright, the theater's ghost, is non-hostile. He appears in the backstage corridors as a translucent figure in a director's coat, carrying a script that was never performed. He is not a haunting — he is an anxious witness. He has watched "Rongquan Mystere" transform his theater over the past two years and does not approve. A party that treats him with respect earns his testimony: "Rongquan" is a false identity, drow use the dressing room at irregular hours, and he overheard a conversation about "the Faire" and "cargo that must not be examined." See the **Malcolm Brizzenbright** page in Notable Figures.

*Soluun's Murders.* A pattern emerges if the party investigates the *Bloodstained Playbill* further: seat numbers corresponding to elves and half-elves who attended the theater and subsequently disappeared. Soluun Xibrindas is stalking victims from the audience after performances. The pattern is discoverable through theater records (DC 14 Investigation) or through asking Malcolm, who witnessed it. This is not required for the main intelligence pipeline — but it is leverage. Jarlaxle is aware of Soluun's behavior and has not acted on it. "He's useful" is his entire position on the matter.

*Step 5 — Getaway.* Two drow guards occupy the safehouse dressing room when it is actively in use (roughly one-in-three chance at any given time). Malcolm is non-hostile and does not interfere. If the drow guards escape, they carry word to the Faire within hours — Jarlaxle knows before the party reaches the door. **Escalation: Bregan D'aerthe moves to Alert (Tier 3) if the dressing room is searched without BD sanction.** Jarlaxle's response is measured — no immediate retaliation, but the next contact with him begins from a different position. BD renown penalties apply if the outpost is raided without established relationship. **Founders' Day Clock: 2–4 days.**

### Scene 8: Response Teams in the Field

**Purpose:** Guidance for running faction response teams as live encounters during the outpost phase. Response teams are consequences of the party's choices, not random encounters — and they are intelligence opportunities.

**Content:**

*When Response Teams Deploy.* Each faction deploys response teams when they reach Alert (Tier 3) or above. The exact timing is specified in each outpost's Getaway section. A faction at Suspicious (Tier 2) may dispatch a single investigative agent — surveillance, not a combat team. Alert and above means a full team in the field.

*Response Team Compositions.* See the relevant Factions guide pages for full compositions and carried documents. Every team leader carries faction-specific documents that function as intelligence opportunities: Zhentarim teams carry directives naming Brindul Alley or Kolat Towers; Xanathar teams carry operational correspondence from Nar'l or Ahmaergo; BD teams carry playbills or coded messages in Drow; Cassalanter teams carry instructions from Lord Cassalanter. The party should treat every response team encounter as a potential document recovery, not just a combat encounter.

*Cross-Faction Response Team Encounters.* Xanathar and Manshoon teams may encounter each other during this quest — both factions are moving through the same wards. Witnessing these confrontations without engaging either side generates intelligence about both factions simultaneously. BD teams prefer misdirection over lethal force; they are more likely to shadow the party than confront them directly. Cassalanter teams vary by the party's relationship with the family.

*The Cassalanter Dual Mode.* The Cassalanters respond differently depending on whether the party has accepted Ammalia's **Fireball!** dinner invitation and maintained a working relationship. Working with the Cassalanters: Seffia's team assists or at minimum does not interfere. Opposing or refusing them: imps maintain constant surveillance, Seffia's team competes at **Xanathar's Lair** operations, and Willifort Crowelle makes social approaches that escalate to assassination attempts if the party actively works against the family. See the **Cassalanters** Factions guide page (Response Teams section).

**Tone:** Tactical and consequential. Factions are thinking opponents reacting to the party's pattern of movement. The party should feel watched between operations, and realize that every action has intelligence implications beyond the immediate outpost target.

### Scene 9: Aftermath and Faction Debriefs

**Purpose:** Close the outpost phase. Compile intelligence, assess escalation states, deliver faction debriefs, and orient the party toward their first lair heist. The debriefs are the party's reward for thorough investigation — each contact responds specifically to what the party learned.

**Content:**

*DM Checkpoint.* Before the debrief scene, the DM reviews: which outposts were hit, which documents were recovered, which NPCs were rescued or cultivated, and each faction's current escalation tier. This assessment determines which lair is most accessible (the faction with fewer hit outposts will have lower lair defenses) and which contacts have the most to offer.

*Mirt (Harpers).* Full intelligence briefing. If Xanathar outposts were hit, Mirt asks specifically about Nihiloor references — the Harper file on the mind flayer is extensive, and any new document strengthens the case for Watch action. If Cassalanter outposts were hit and the party brings the Mirror of the Fiend sigil identification, the Cassalanter cult documents, or the cultists' testimony, Mirt's response is: relief, then urgency. He has been building suspicion for years without evidence. What the party has found in one outpost run is more concrete than everything he gathered in three years of surveillance. "I knew it couldn't just be charity." He does not have enough to move on the Cassalanters legally — he needs **Cassalanter Villa** for that — but the calculus on Founders' Day has changed.

*Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance).* Wants all outpost intelligence shared with the Watch, framed as actionable intelligence about criminal operations rather than unauthorized entry. Offers legal cover for all this quest's operations retroactively in exchange for Manshoon intelligence that will hold up in a Magisters' proceeding. He is building a legal case, not an arrest warrant; the distinction matters to him.

*Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave).* Asks whether the Coachlamp Lane infernal disturbance was addressed. If the Converted Windmill was cleared: Jeryth confirms the resonance has quieted. Melannor is satisfied and asks no further questions about what caused it. If the windmill was not cleared: Jeryth's garden is showing symptoms — herbs dying, birds leaving the grounds, a persistent wrongness spreading rather than shrinking. The Founders' Day pressure arrives through the natural world for Enclave-allied parties.

*Savra Belabranta (Order of the Gauntlet).* Asks about captives and methods. Were innocents harmed? Were prisoners rescued? If Ott Steeltoes was recovered from the Interrogation House, Savra registers it as a moral victory regardless of the prisoner's faction affiliation. She asks about him by name if she has any intelligence on the Interrogation House operation.

*Vajra Safahr (Force Grey).* Asks about arcane intelligence: teleportation circles, magical defenses, ward schematics. If the Yellowspire teleportation circle was documented, she immediately requests the sigil sequence. Vajra can verify the circle's destination (Kolat Towers) through divination and will confirm it within a day of receiving the sequence. She shares that the Blackstaff has been tracking Manshoon's arcane signature and can use the sigil sequence to triangulate his operational base. She does not share what she intends to do with this information.

*Tashlyn Yafeera (Doom Raiders).* Asks about Manshoon's operational capacity. If pass-amulets were captured, she wants to examine them — specifically to determine whether they contain identification components that could be used to track the holder's movements. If the Interrogation House was hit, she wants to know whether Davil's name appears in any recovered documents. Her concern is documented and unstated in equal measure: she is building a legal defense for Davil and needs evidence that Doom Raiders involvement in the Gralhund operation was circumstantial rather than directed.

*Jarlaxle (Bregan D'aerthe).* Always the primary BD contact, in disguise. If BD outposts were hit, he is calm — he expected this eventually, and he finds the party's methodology worth acknowledging. "I showed you the door. You picked the lock anyway. I respect that." If BD outposts were not hit and the party approaches him directly, he offers nothing unprompted but is available to discuss terms. The gap between what Jarlaxle says and what he knows is always present; the party should feel it.

**Tone:** Decompression. The operational phase is over. Intelligence has been gathered, costs have been paid, and the factions are differently positioned than they were in Scene 2. What the party chooses to share — and with whom — will shape what they receive in the lair heists that follow.

## 3. Key Decisions Before Writing

1. **One file.** `arc-e-faction-outposts.md`. Cross-faction mechanics, escalation consequences, and the structural guide must be visible in one place. Splitting by faction would scatter the interdependencies.

2. **Nine scenes.** Three framing scenes (1–3), four faction sections (4–7) treated as a menu rather than a sequence, one response teams scene (8), one aftermath scene (9). The faction scenes are non-sequential by design.

3. **Mini-heist format: abbreviated 5-step.** Gather Information, Case the Target, Acquire Supplies, The Operation, Getaway — applied to four outposts. Steps 1–3 compressed; Step 4 is the substantive section; Step 5 bakes in escalation.

4. **Encounter-plus format.** Approach, Key Areas, Intel Yield, then baked-in Escalation and Founders' Day Clock. No 5-step framework.

5. **Player faction hooks gap-filled.** Enclave: infernal resonance confirmed as infernal but source unknown (→ Cassalanters). Gauntlet: Splinter intelligence trail (→ Manshoon). Force Grey: BD operational infrastructure (→ theater and message architecture).

6. **Escalation baked-in.** Each outpost's Getaway section specifies the exact tier change, response team, and timeline. No DM judgment required between sessions.

7. **Manshoon reveal sequence.** Name only at Interrogation House (*Directive to Zorbog*). Identity at **Kolat Towers**. Motivation at **Vault of Dragons**.

8. **Fenerus kidnap timing.** Directive arrives live at the Interrogation House. Kidnap fires 1d6+2 days later. Three branching outcomes depending on whether the party warns Fenerus, arrives during the kidnap, or finds an empty house.

9. **Chain completion defined.** Two outposts of the same faction plus one piece of actionable lair intelligence. The first completed chain awards 1 Milestone Point (cumulative 10), not enough to reach level 5. Level 5 is reached on the first completed lair heist (**Xanathar's Lair**, **Cassalanter Villa**, **Sea Maidens Faire**, or **Kolat Towers**).

10. **Founders' Day Clock.** Per-outpost day estimates in every entry. Party learns about the deadline through play, not through this guide.

# Design Notes: Faction Outposts

## Modular Structure

***The Problem of Non-Linear Content in a Linear Format.*** Every quest through **Gralhund Villa** moved in a single investigative current — a question asked, an investigation run, a confrontation resolved. **Faction Outposts** cannot function this way. Ten outposts in any order, at the party's discretion, cannot be scripted into a sequence without defeating the point. The nine-scene architecture is the solution: three framing scenes that run in order (Stone attunement, faction consultation, operational briefing), four faction sections that function as a menu, and two closing scenes that apply after the menu has been used. The faction sections are labeled as scenes to maintain the document's structural vocabulary — each has a Purpose, Content, and Tone — but the DM should understand that "Scene 4" does not mean "fourth in sequence." It means "this is the Xanathar section."

***Two Tiers Prevent Two Failure Modes.*** If all ten outposts were mini-heists, the arc would stall for three to four sessions of planning-and-execution per outpost before the party reached any lair. If all ten were encounter-plus, the arc would lose the campaign's heist identity entirely. The tier split preserves both. Encounter-plus outposts provide intelligence efficiently and without extended planning friction. Mini-heist outposts apply the full framework to the four outposts warranting it: those that gate major resources (a lair entrance, a teleportation circle, the campaign's primary vault clue). Six encounter-plus and four mini-heist is the ratio that prevents fatigue while maintaining identity.

## Escalation Architecture

***Baking In vs. Tracking.*** The Alexandrian's framework uses a five-stage escalation system requiring the DM to assess and advance stage between sessions. This guide's standing rule is that the DM must make no creative decisions between sessions that the text could make for them. Each outpost's Getaway entry is the implementation of that rule: it names exactly what changes (faction tier, response team, timeline), eliminating the DM's role as interpreter. Without it, lair defenses feel static regardless of how many outposts were hit, and player choices become invisible in their consequences. The baked-in system makes choices legible without requiring the DM to track them.

***Cross-Faction Spillover and the Xanathar–Manshoon War.*** The spillover rules — hitting Xanathar raises Manshoon's tier and vice versa — have a specific purpose beyond realism. They ensure that a party pursuing only one faction's chain still generates meaningful pressure across multiple fronts. A party that focuses entirely on Xanathar outposts will encounter Manshoon response teams without having engaged Manshoon directly, surfacing the inter-faction war as lived experience rather than exposition.

## The Cassalanter Discovery

***The Factions Don't Know.*** The Cassalanters' infernal operations have been intentionally withheld from every faction contact — including Mirt, including the full Harper intelligence apparatus. Their cover is the best in Waterdeep: no enemies made, no traceable crimes committed, nothing but admiration from every quarter. Mirt's "nobody is that clean" is as close as external observation gets. The purpose of this choice: it makes the players the first people in Waterdeep to uncover the truth, through their own investigation, rather than receiving it as briefing material from a contact. The Mirror of the Fiend, the cultists' testimony, Victoro's signed letter at the Windmill — the party assembles the picture one piece at a time, the way investigators are supposed to. Discovery earned is worth more than discovery given.

***This Quest's Discovery vs. Cassalanter Villa's Revelation.*** This quest and **Cassalanter Villa** deliver different things. In this quest, thorough investigators learn that the Cassalanters maintain infernal cult operations, that Victoro directs them, and that there is a specific Eye hidden at Brandath Crypt. What they do not yet know: the pact's terms, the children's involvement, the Founders' Day deadline. **Cassalanter Villa** delivers the full horror. This quest is the evidence that makes **Cassalanter Villa** meaningful — "we suspected it, and it is worse than we suspected" lands harder than "we were briefed and it is bad."

## The Manshoon Reveal Sequence

***Name, Identity, Motivation.*** Manshoon's revelation has been staged across three quests: name at the Interrogation House, identity at **Kolat Towers**, motivation at **Vault of Dragons**. The decision to surface his name on a document that arrives live during the party's operation — rather than in a cutscene or NPC speech — is deliberate. The *Directive to Zorbog* bearing his signature is the party's first tangible evidence that the Splinter has a named leader. It lands differently there, in a house they just broke into, while a prisoner they may or may not rescue is strapped to a chair nearby, than it would in a faction briefing. The emotional environment matters.

## Live Complications

***The Fenerus Kidnap as Active Threat.*** The original source identifies the Fenerus kidnap directive but does not specify when it fires relative to the party's arc progression. This guide settles it: the directive arrives live at the Interrogation House, the kidnap fires 1d6+2 days later, and three outcomes branch from there. The branching is genuine — whether the party finds Fenerus safe, catches the kidnap in progress, or arrives at an empty house determines a materially different scene. The party that reads the directive and moves immediately finds Fenerus safe; the party that doesn't finds an empty house and a cold trail.

***Soluun's Murders as Leverage.*** This intelligence unlocks no door — it's an internal BD problem Jarlaxle is aware of and hasn't acted on. Including it creates a conversation the party can have with Jarlaxle about what he is actually willing to enforce within his own organization. "He's useful" reveals something about Jarlaxle that no amount of charming banter does.

## Three Clue Rule Verification

***Chain Entry Points.*** Every faction chain has at least three independent paths to its first outpost: one from prior quest documents (**Gralhund Villa** fallout, **Fireball!** Faire visit), one from within the faction's chain (cross-outpost documents), and one requiring no prior relationship or document (street-level entry). The last path — Grinda's reputation in Mistshore, Trade Ward canvassing for Brindul Alley, Divine Sense near Coachlamp Lane, theater district playbills — ensures that a party who has avoided faction contacts and found no cross-outpost documents still has a way in. Three Clue Rule requires three independent paths that cannot all fail simultaneously; it does not require three equally prominent paths.

***Lair Entrance Verification.*** Four lair entrances, all reachable from this quest's intelligence: Xanathar's Lair — Nar'l's guildsign trail, gladiatorial transport via Terasse, Ott Steeltoes as guide (three paths); Kolat Towers — Yellowspire teleportation circle, captured pass-amulet from Avareen, Agorn's amulet traded under duress (three paths); Cassalanter Villa — Ammalia's **Fireball!** dinner invitation, Cassalanter cultist testimony from this quest's outposts, Esvele Rosznar in **Cassalanter Villa** (three paths, the last requiring no completion of this quest at all); Sea Maidens Faire — Fenerus's decoded messages, Malcolm Brizzenbright's testimony, Xanathar's Lair report from **Xanathar's Lair** (three paths).

## Faction Hooks

**Harpers.** This quest's Harper hook is an ongoing operative benefit rather than a single scene beat. Once the party reaches Renown 30+, Mirt can direct embedded Harper informants to provide advance intelligence against Xanathar and Manshoon outposts — shift rotations, guard counts, unusual personnel — delivered before each operation rather than discovered during it. A separate informant inside the Sea Maidens Faire reports that Jarlaxle has been asking about the party by description; Mirt passes this to Harper PCs as a quiet heads-up in the days before **Sea Maidens Faire** begins. These benefits are not surfaced in Scene 2's consultation; they activate through maintained renown and are delivered as one informant report at the start of each outpost operation the party undertakes in Scenes 4–7, once a Harper PC has Renown 30+. See the Quest Hooks section of the **Harpers** Factions guide page.
