# Arc I — Kolat Towers

*An adventure for 4th- to 6th-level characters.*

---

## Arc Opener

Duhlark Kolat built the towers that bear his name during the late fourteenth century as an experiment in paranoid architecture. His brother Alcedor wanted a home; Duhlark wanted a fortress that could withstand siege, scrying, and betrayal from within. The result — two interconnected stone towers in the Trades Ward, linked by a bridge at the third floor and bound together by a force field that covered the entire structure from basement to roof — was more fortress than home. Alcedor moved in anyway. The force field admitted no one without a specially keyed amulet. Alcedor accepted this as the cost of living with his brother. He died in the towers in 1385 DR under circumstances that remain unclear. Duhlark survived him by nine years before being found dead in his study, apparently of old age, with the force field still running.

Lady Gondafrey, then a Captain of the Order of the Gauntlet, attempted to breach the towers in 1379 DR while investigating Duhlark's suspected dealings with the Xanathar Guild. The force field repelled her. The arcane lock on the front door defeated her thieves. She attempted a third entry through the bridge on the third floor and was captured. Duhlark offered her a choice: remain imprisoned, or swear never to speak of what she had seen. She refused the oath. She has been in the towers since 1379 DR — forty-seven years.

The Manshoon who now occupies Kolat Towers is a clone of the original Zhentarim founder — a body activated in Undermountain when the original Manshoon died, as the clone network was designed to do. Halaster Blackcloak, who tolerated the clone for a time out of professional curiosity, eventually severed Manshoon's left arm at the shoulder with a blade that prevented magical regrowth. The clone fled Undermountain carrying the staff of power he had smuggled in and arrived in Waterdeep to find the Kolat Towers abandoned, the force field still running on its original enchantment, and the amulet manufacture instructions in Duhlark's study. Manshoon needed a base. The towers were available.

He spent two years building the Splinter cell — the faction that bears his name but operates independently of the Zhentarim's surviving leadership. The structure is hub-and-spoke: Kolat Towers as the command center, with the Interrogation House in the North Ward and Yellowspire in the Castle Ward serving as field offices connected by teleportation circles. A pass-amulet system replaced the need for a trusted courier network; amulets could be revoked remotely, confiscating access without confrontation. The towers' extradimensional sanctum — a space behind the towers that is not physically behind the towers, accessible only via a teleportation ring keyed to a specific circle in K22 — houses Manshoon's closest lieutenants and his most sensitive operations.

The simulacrum in E7 is Manshoon's contingency against assassination. It was created seven years ago and has been ice-preserved since — a duplicate with half his hit points, none of his 6th-level or higher spell slots, and a different quality to its decision-making that those who know Manshoon well can identify after the fact. The simulacrum has never been activated outside of tests. Manshoon has never needed it. He is considering, for the first time in years, whether he might.

The blackmail operation is what funds the Splinter's political reach. Corylus Thann, a Masked Lord, was photographed in a situation that would end his career; he supplies Guild intelligence in exchange for the ledger's continued privacy. Jelenn Urmbrusk accumulated significant debt to a Splinter front organization; his votes in the Council of Guildmasters follow Manshoon's preferences without his ever being asked directly. The ledger documenting these arrangements, and three others, lives in an arcane-locked drawer in Manshoon's study in E13. The identity list in the same drawer names Mirt, Thardouk Starbuckler, and Dorgar Adarbrent as Masked Lords — intelligence Manshoon has been accumulating for reasons that are not yet clear.

## Scene 1: The Lair Intelligence Briefing

**Purpose.** Convert prior-arc intelligence — the name "Manshoon," the Kolat Towers location, the pass-amulet system, the Splinter network — into an operational picture of a fortified position. Unlike Arc F (a dungeon whose defenses the party can infiltrate around) or Arc G (a villa with social cover) or Arc H (a carnival that looks like something other than a military installation), this briefing reveals a target that is overtly what it is: a wizard's fortress behind an impenetrable force field. The party cannot sneak up on Kolat Towers. They can only prepare better or worse for the assault.

**Content.**

*Running This Scene.* Each contact below is available only to party members of that faction. A party with no Harper has no channel to Mirt; a party with no Force Grey member has no Vajra briefing. The scene's intelligence yield depends on which factions the party joined during Arcs B and C — a party that spread its memberships widely arrives knowing a great deal about the towers' defenses; a party that clustered into one or two factions arrives with real gaps. This is by design. The raid genre does not allow the party to compensate for intelligence gaps with improvisation as freely as the caper or social genres; blind spots in the briefing become operational liabilities in Scene 4.

Run this as a montage of consultations. Each contact adds one piece. The picture assembles over multiple meetings across several days.

*Mirt (Harpers — renown 1+).* Harper agents have been watching the Kolat Towers block since Arc E, tracking lieutenant movement. Mirt provides: exterior description and confirmed ward location (Trades Ward, not the North Ward — the misdirection in Samara's Arc F testimony was fragmentary field intelligence); pass-amulet delivery patterns (Manshoon's couriers approach from two directions, suggesting two off-site entry points); the observation that the force field extends twelve feet above the roof and six feet below street level, making aerial approaches impossible and undermining impossible without specialized tools; and a warning that Watch patrol coverage in the surrounding streets has been quietly reduced over the past eighteen months — someone with civic influence arranged it. At renown 10+, Mirt activates a 48-hour Harper surveillance rotation: a field agent (spy stat block) delivers a written report covering patrol patterns by time of day, delivery schedules, and one verified force-field gap cycle including the gap's precise location, duration, and rotation frequency.

*Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance — renown 1+).* Watch patrol schedules for the surrounding streets show three daily windows when foot traffic in the block drops to near zero — early morning before the Trades Ward merchants open, a midday lull when patrols break for meals, and a late-night window between the final patrol and dawn. Jalester has confirmed that Watch coverage around the towers is "informally diminished" — not on the patrol schedule, but in practice. He suspects Masked Lord influence, which he cannot investigate without evidence. If the party recovers the identity list from E13, Jalester will understand what happened and why. At renown 10+, Jalester arranges a 4-hour formal Watch reassignment to "festival preparations" — no Watch interference during that window.

*Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave — renown 1+).* Environmental damage in the immediate vicinity of the towers is distinctive. Dead birds at the base of the force field's perimeter — they flew into an invisible wall and broke their necks. Dead grass in a three-foot band around the same perimeter, killed by the field's constant low-level magical discharge. Insect populations entirely absent within twenty feet of the towers. Melannor identifies these as consistent with a maintained abjuration of considerable age and power, and notes that such effects always have a physical anchor inside the warded structure — a rune, a stone, a specific constructed object that cannot be destroyed from outside the field. "Find the anchor, destroy it, and the field collapses." He does not know where the anchor is. At renown 10+, Jeryth Phaulkon casts pass without trace on the entire party (+10 Stealth for 1 hour) for the approach to the towers, suppressing sound and scent on the street before entry.

*Savra Belabranta (Order of the Gauntlet — renown 1+).* Captain Gondafrey's service record. Rank at the time of her disappearance: Captain, Third Rank, Order of the Gauntlet. Specialization: arcane disruption and structural assault — she had more expertise in breaching warded locations than anyone else in the Waterdeep chapter. The Order has officially listed her as dead since 1397 DR but has never closed the investigation. Savra frames this as obligation, not sentiment: "She was taken while performing Order business. If she is alive in there — in any form — we do not leave her behind." The moral obligation is the contact's primary function. It gives the party a reason to make the harder call during Scene 4 rather than bypassing K13. At renown 10+, Savra positions herself and a Gauntlet knight (knight stat block) at the party's designated extraction point with two potions of healing and 25 HP of lay on hands remaining — practical support for a raid that does not end cleanly.

*Vajra Safahr (Force Grey — renown 1+).* Arcane assessment. The force field is sustained abjuration anchored to a physical rune — it cannot be dispelled from outside the field, and it cannot be bypassed with passwall, dimension door, or any conjuration-school transit spell. Dimension door targeting a point inside the field terminates at the field boundary; the caster arrives at the field's exterior surface, not inside. Vajra also detects conjuration magic consistent with an extradimensional space — "There is something behind the towers that is not behind the towers." The space is accessible only from inside. She cannot determine access method. She warns of a failsafe: a significant conjuration signature that reads as summoning-adjacent. Something large is being held. If it is released, it will not be controlled. At renown 10+, Vajra casts nondetection on one party member for 8 hours before the operation, and stations a Blackstaff mage in a nearby building monitoring the towers' exterior. A signal spell from inside within 6 hours prompts the mage to begin intermittent dispel magic attempts at the force-field anchor location — requiring the party to have identified and reported the anchor's location during Scene 2 surveillance.

*Tashlyn Yafeera or Davil Starsong (Doom Raiders — renown 1+).* The richest briefing. The Doom Raiders have been building toward Kolat Towers for longer than any other faction — six missions of accumulated intelligence over Arcs B through E. What they provide depends on which missions the party completed: the pass-amulet distribution ledger (Mission 5), a diagram showing the three force-field gap locations and their 6-hour rotation cycle drawn by Ziraj during his own surveillance (Mission 6), a list of 43 identified Splinter operatives by name and role, and a garrison estimate. If Missions 5 and 6 are not complete, Tashlyn still provides the 43 names and garrison estimate — that intelligence has been accumulating since Arc B. At renown 10+, Davil commits to full joint-operation planning: synchronized entry timing, objective assignment between the party and the DR team, designated rendezvous point at K22, and a contingency signal protocol for communication mid-raid. Below renown 10+, the Doom Raiders' operation launches regardless — it always does — but coordination is improvised rather than planned.

*Jarlaxle (Bregan D'aerthe — renown 1+).* Available only if a PC is a BD member. Jarlaxle's interest in Kolat Towers is personal. If Arc H's Manshoon parallel strike fired — and it always fires — Manshoon sent agents to attack Jarlaxle's ships in his own harbor. The experience left Jarlaxle more annoyed than alarmed, which for him is the more dangerous emotional state. "They came to my ship. In my harbor. I want to know where they came from, and I want it to stop." BD intelligence on Kolat Towers is ground-floor-only — the Bregan D'aerthe never penetrated past the second floor of the towers and have no information on the sanctum. What Jarlaxle provides: partial interior layout (K1 through K9), confirmation of the pass-amulet system by name and mechanism, confirmation that Sidra Romeir commands internal security, and the observation that Manshoon's agents carry amulets minted with a specific design that distinguishes them from amulets issued to the towers' service staff. If the party recovered pass-amulets from the defeated Zhentarim strike team during Arc H's Scene 5, Jarlaxle confirms they work — those amulets bypass the force field and unlock the front door. At renown 10+, Jarlaxle provides one additional piece: "The wizard in those towers lost an arm in Undermountain. Whatever he is — and I have suspicions — he came up from below." The Halaster connection is stated without elaboration. It is the first hint that Manshoon's identity is stranger than a Zhentarim faction leader.

**Tone.** Tactical and confrontational. The party is not being briefed on a heist opportunity — they are being briefed on an assault. The contacts' intelligence this scene has a harder, more operational quality than in prior arcs. Mirt is not charming. Vajra is not academic. Savra is not measured. Everyone who provides intelligence here is telling the party something that ends with "and you will have to fight your way through it."

## Scene 2: Casing the Target

**Purpose.** Steps 1–3 of the heist framework adapted for the raid genre. Kolat Towers does not offer the same casing texture as Arc F (a dungeon with accessible entry points to scout), Arc G (a villa with a public face), or Arc H (a carnival in which the party can wander freely). The force field makes exterior casing the only option. The party cannot see inside the towers before the operation begins. What they can learn is: entry timing, approach windows, and force-field gap mechanics. The Identifying the Score step is unusual in Arc I — no Eye, no Stone impression — which means the party's motivation must come from somewhere else entirely.

**Content.**

*Identifying the Score.* Kolat Towers holds no Eye. The Stone of Golorr offers no impression pointing to this location. The party's motivation is strategic: neutralize Manshoon before he ambushes them at the Vault, recover the blackmail ledger, seize intelligence that makes Arc J viable. This is the only lair arc where the party might legitimately ask "why are we doing this?" The answer comes from three convergent directions. First, every faction that provides intelligence about Manshoon eventually ends the same way — "and he will not stop." The ledger, the Vault approach intelligence, the political reach — leaving Manshoon operational means arriving at Arc J against an adversary who has had weeks to prepare. Second, Lady Gondafrey. Savra's briefing makes her existence and her location known; the party knows there is a prisoner in K13 who has been there for forty-seven years. Third, the intelligence haul itself has specific arc consequences that every contact will name if pressed. "The Blackstaff wants that spellbook. The Open Lord wants that ledger. Whatever Manshoon knows about the Vault, you want to know it first." The party should arrive at Scene 4 having decided what they are there to get.

*Three Clue Rule — Paths to Arc I.* All of the following were seeded in prior arcs:
- Samara's testimony (Arc F, X7): "a tower in the North Ward" — deliberate misdirection from fragmentary field intelligence. The ward is wrong but the target is right.
- Nihiloor's intelligence files (Arc F, X23): partial Kolat Towers layout and Splinter lieutenant roster.
- Cassalanter Report on the Grand Game (Arc G): references Manshoon and identifies the towers' location in the Trades Ward, correcting Samara's misdirection.
- Jarlaxle's Report on the Grand Game (Arc H, J30): references Manshoon's Splinter operations.
- Zhentarim pass-amulets (Arc H, Scene 5): recovered from the defeated strike team — physical entry tokens.
- Doom Raiders intelligence (Appendix D, Missions 5–6): pass-amulet ledger, force-field gap diagram.

Arc I is the convergence point for every faction's Manshoon intelligence. A party that spread across multiple arcs arrives here having assembled the picture from multiple angles. A party that skipped most of those arcs arrives having assembled less but still enough — Samara's testimony alone names the target.

*Three Entry Methods.* Each is seeded by prior arcs. All three are available simultaneously. A party may combine them — using Yellowspire for one team while another uses captured amulets — if they have the personnel.

**Yellowspire Teleportation Circle (via Arc E outpost).** The teleportation circle in the Yellowspire outpost connects directly to K22 at the top of the second tower, bypassing the force field entirely. The party arrives at elevation, inside the fortified structure, without a street approach. Advantage: complete force-field bypass; no approach window needed; entry point is at the top of the tower rather than ground level, which means the party moves downward through opposition rather than up against gravity. Disadvantage: K22 is guarded — a spectator and four flying snakes at Unaware/Suspicious tier, plus two additional thugs at Alert and above. The circle is one-way without a teleporter ring; the party cannot use K22 to exit unless they acquire a ring during Scene 3 or Scene 4. No retreat path via this method without additional preparation.

**Captured Pass-Amulets (via Avareen Windrivver, Agorn Fuoco, or Arc H Zhentarim strike team).** Amulets recovered from any of these sources allow the party to walk through the force field to K1 (the ground-floor entrance) without triggering it. The amulets also prevent the force field's passive alarm from registering unauthorized entry. Advantage: social window — Sidra will not attack immediately on sight at Unaware or Suspicious tier, creating 1–3 minutes of ambiguous contact before the operation's nature becomes clear. Disadvantage: the approach is on the street, visible from the towers' windows. At Alert tier, captured amulets require a DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana) check to recalibrate before use — a failure produces an audible rejection tone that the garrison hears. At Lockdown tier, captured amulets are invalid; only freshly issued amulets function.

**Rooftop Force-Field Gaps (via Doom Raiders Mission 6 or Mirt's Harper surveillance).** Three gaps in the force field rotate on a 6-hour cycle — each gap opens for 60 seconds, closes, and a different gap opens 2 hours later. Ziraj's diagram from Mission 6 marks the gaps' locations on the towers' exterior; without it, identifying a gap requires 24 continuous hours of observation (DC 16 Perception). The gaps are at upper-floor height (K9/K11 level), requiring a DC 14 Athletics check to climb to the entry point within the 60-second window. Advantage: bypasses the ground-level garrison entirely; the party arrives on the bridge or upper floors. Disadvantage: at Alert tier, the gap cycle shifts by 30 minutes from Ziraj's diagram — a party relying on the diagram without fresh confirmation will arrive at the gap 30 minutes early or late. At Lockdown tier, all gaps are sealed; this entry method is unavailable.

*Exterior Surveillance.* One day of observation from the surrounding streets yields: patrol patterns by time of day (guard rotations, entry/exit frequency), delivery schedules (none visible — the towers appear to be self-sufficient, consistent with Vajra's extradimensional space assessment), and rough garrison estimates from personnel visible at windows. Without Ziraj's diagram, identifying a force-field gap requires 24 continuous hours of observation and a DC 16 Wisdom (Perception) check. A party that arranges the Harper surveillance rotation through Mirt (renown 10+) receives all of the above in written form without needing to spend time on it themselves.

**Tone.** Compressed and decisive. The raid genre imposes its own time pressure before the operation even begins. Every hour the party spends observing the towers is an hour Manshoon has to prepare. The Founders' Day clock, the Vault approach intelligence sitting in E9 and E13, and Manshoon's active Arc J preparations all push against extended casing. The party should feel that the optimal casing window is shorter than they would like, and that waiting longer costs something.

## Scene 3: Preparation

**Purpose.** Step 4 of the heist framework. The party finalizes the assault plan, coordinates with the Doom Raiders if a PC is a DR member, acquires the tools that make the operation viable, and confronts the constraints imposed by their escalation tier. Scene 3 is shorter than in prior arcs — there is no ally recruitment requiring social investment, no moral decision that precedes the operation, no deception to construct. The raid genre does not build to the operation through social maneuvering. It builds through operational planning.

**Content.**

*Potential Allies.*

Lady Gondafrey is a secondary objective, not a recruited ally — the party will not know her current alignment state before entering K13. Savra's briefing establishes her existence and the moral case for her extraction. What she provides if freed depends on her alignment: a lawful-good Gondafrey is the most valuable ally in the towers, with 47 years of architectural knowledge and a deeply personal motivation to see Manshoon's operation destroyed; a chaotic-evil Gondafrey is an active threat. The DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check that detects her current state is available only after K13 is reached. The party should decide, before entering the towers, whether extracting Lady Gondafrey is a primary objective, a secondary objective, or a risk they are not willing to take.

Doom Raiders coordination at renown 10+: a planning session with Tashlyn Yafeera before the operation. The DR bring three veterans and Yagra Stonefist. The planning session assigns specific objectives, determines entry timing (the party's method and the DR's method are synchronized — they enter different points within 5 minutes of each other), designates K22 as the rendezvous point, and establishes two contingency signals. This is the most significant pre-operation coordination available; a party with renown 10+ in the Doom Raiders goes into Scene 4 knowing exactly what Tashlyn's team is doing and why.

*The Teleporter Ring System.* Reaching the sanctum from K22 requires a teleporter ring keyed to the K22 circle. Without a ring, the circle is inert. Sources for rings: Sidra Romeir (K2, carries two spares in addition to her own); Manafret Cherryport (K3, wears one on his right hand); any named lieutenant in the towers or sanctum. The party should identify how they plan to acquire a ring during Scene 3 — whether by defeating or bypassing a ring-holder in the towers. If the party enters via Yellowspire, they arrive at K22 but still need a ring to activate the circle in reverse for exit.

*Variable Difficulty — Escalation Tier.*

| Tier | Force Field | Garrison State | Manshoon & Simulacrum | Timer |
|------|-------------|----------------|----------------------|-------|
| Unaware | Standard; gaps on normal rotation | Standard roster; Sidra assumes visitors are guests until credentials fail | Manshoon studying in E13; simulacrum dormant in E7 | 30 min |
| Suspicious | Gaps shifted 15 min from diagram; amulets work | +2 thugs at K2; Sidra checks credentials before allowing passage | Manshoon studying in E13; simulacrum dormant in E7 | 25 min |
| Alert | Gaps shifted 30 min; amulets require DC 14 Arcana to recalibrate | Posts doubled; K22 adds 2 thugs to standard spectator/snake garrison; Manafret poisons all kitchen food | Manshoon in E12, staff in hand; simulacrum activated in E1 as decoy | 20 min |
| Lockdown | Gaps sealed; only fresh amulets work; Yellowspire circle still functions | Full garrison at battle stations; Sidra does not ask questions | Manshoon in E12, defensive spells prepared; simulacrum activated in E1, briefed on party capabilities via Skeemo's intelligence if he survived Arc E | 15 min |

**Tone.** Commitment under pressure. By the time Scene 3 ends, the party has made irreversible decisions — entry method, primary objectives, handling of secondary objectives like Lady Gondafrey, coordination with the Doom Raiders. Scene 3 is the last point at which changing the plan costs only time. Scene 4 does not allow replanning.

## Scene 4: The Operation

**Purpose.** The raid itself. Step 5 of the heist framework. Full operational content for both locations: Kolat Towers (K-series areas) and the extradimensional sanctum (E-series areas). Unlike the prior lair arcs — which offer genuine stealth windows throughout the operation — Kolat Towers compresses the stealthy phase to the approach and entry. Once combat begins in the towers, the reinforcement timer starts, and the primary strategic variable becomes speed.

**Content.**

*Running This Scene.* The critical path runs K1/K22 (entry) → K2/K3 (Sidra, Manafret, teleporter ring) → K22 (circle to sanctum) → E1/E2/E3 (sanctum foyer) → deeper sanctum → E12 or E13 (Manshoon). Everything else is optional. A party that clears every room fights a garrison that gets larger with every minute they spend. A party that moves with purpose reaches Manshoon before the timer reaches its hardest entries on the schedule. The DM communicates timer progression through environmental cues: flying snake couriers crossing between towers, shouted orders echoing from below, boots on stairs that were silent a minute ago.

Jarlaxle's position and the simulacrum's state are predecided by escalation tier. At Unaware and Suspicious, Manshoon is studying in E13 and the simulacrum is dormant in E7. At Alert and Lockdown, Manshoon is in E12 with the staff of power in hand and the simulacrum is activated and standing in E1. These positions do not change during Scene 4 unless the party's actions force Manshoon to move — and Manshoon moves toward a confrontation, not away from one.

*Kolat Towers — Critical Path Areas.*

**K1 — Ground-Floor Entrance.** The front door. Only relevant for pass-amulet entry. A Zhentarim thug behind a viewing port opens it if the amulet check passes, admits the visitors to the narrow entry hall, and moves to alert Sidra in K2. At Unaware tier, this happens without alarm. At Suspicious and Alert, the thug calls for Sidra before the door is fully open.

**K2 — Dining Room.** Sidra Romeir (mage) + 2 thugs. The towers' internal security commander. At Unaware tier, Sidra greets visitors who present amulets as potential legitimate contacts — there is a 1–3 minute social window before her suspicion overcomes her professionalism. She is not foolish; she notices inconsistencies in cover stories with a passive Insight of +6. At Suspicious and above, the social window does not apply — she is in a defensive posture when the door opens. Sidra carries two spare teleporter rings in addition to her own. Defeating or otherwise removing Sidra from the equation provides both ring access and removes the towers' primary internal command authority.

**K3 — Kitchen.** Manafret Cherryport (mage) + 1 kitchen worker (commoner, non-combatant). Manafret is one of the longest-serving Splinter members — formerly of the Acquisitions division, recruited because he can operate a teleportation circle with mechanical precision under fire. He wears a teleporter ring on his right hand and will not give it up voluntarily. At Alert and Lockdown tiers, he has poisoned all food in the kitchen with midnight tears (DC 17 Constitution saving throw, 31 (9d6) poison damage on a failed save, or half on success; onset 1 minute; DC 13 Perception or DC 12 Nature to detect before consumption). He is efficient and loyal but not suicidal — if reduced below 15 HP, he attempts to flee to E3 via the K22 circle.

**K4 — Musty Library.** 2 gargoyles standing motionless in the corners. They activate on any living creature entering without a pass-amulet or without Sidra present to vouch for them. A hidden spellbook behind a false shelf (DC 16 Investigation to locate, reduced to DC 13 if Lady Gondafrey advises from K13) contains "Flumph Mating Rituals" — a cover title for Duhlark Kolat's original working notes on the force field, including the anchor's construction specifications. This is the primary pre-K18 intelligence source on the force field's anchor mechanism.

**K9 — Bridge Between Towers.** 2 thugs on watch. The bridge connects the towers at the third floor — the only interior connection between the two structures above ground level. After the reinforcement timer triggers, the thugs erect a wooden barricade (AC 15, 18 HP) across the bridge's midpoint. Removing the barricade requires action, costs time, and has to happen twice — once for each direction.

**K13 — Holding Cell.** Lady Gondafrey. She has been in this room for 47 years. The alignment shift mechanic: her alignment resets at dawn each day, shifting on a d10 — but per standing campaign design rules, the DM does not roll. Her alignment for the operation is determined by when the party arrives relative to dawn: if the party enters the towers before dawn, she is at whatever state she was at last night's reset, which is chaotic-evil (Manshoon uses the alignment oscillation as a psychological tool — she is sometimes herself and sometimes not, and she cannot be certain which she is). If the party enters after dawn and within 4 hours of it, she is lawful-good. If the party enters more than 4 hours after dawn, she is drifting — treat as true neutral, cooperative but not fully capable of tactical coordination. The party's entry timing therefore determines which version of Lady Gondafrey they find. If she is lawful-good: she provides a complete verbal floor plan of both towers and the sanctum (she memorized the layout over decades), identifies the anchor in K18 by name ("the rune room — destroy the stone in the wall, the field falls"), and offers to fight alongside the party with whatever she can find. She is a skilled combatant at CR 5 equivalent (veteran stat block, with innate arcane disruption abilities from her Order training). If she is chaotic-evil: she attacks the party on sight.

**K15 — Summoning Chamber.** 3 apprentice wizard minions (22 HP each) in active ritual, binding a barlgura in the summoning circle. The apprentices are the minimum complement required to maintain the binding — disrupting any one of them (dropping them below half HP or breaking their concentration) releases the demon. Released, the barlgura attacks the nearest creature, friend or enemy, for 3 rounds, then rampages through the towers following noise. The trade-off: a released barlgura draws 2–4 Zhentarim defenders from other positions to contain it, creating a tactical window for the party — but it also triggers the reinforcement timer if not already running, and poses genuine injury risk. The barlgura ignores pass-amulets.

**K17 — Flesh Golem Access Corridor.** A pressure plate 10 feet before the K18 door releases a cloud of contact poison gas (DC 13 Constitution saving throw, 14 (4d6) poison damage on a failed save; the poisoned condition lasts 1 minute on failure). DC 14 Perception to notice the plate before stepping on it; DC 13 Thieves' Tools to disable it. The flesh golem in the corridor guards the approach to K18 and does not have pass-amulet discrimination — it attacks anything moving toward K18 that does not carry a specific command stone (in Manshoon's possession).

**K18 — Arcane Rune (Force Field Anchor).** A stone rune set into the east wall of a bare room — the physical anchor for the force field. The rune radiates strong abjuration. The moment it takes any damage (AC 10, 22 HP), a red slaad teleports into the room. The slaad is Manshoon's failsafe — it attacks the party until destroyed or dismissed. The slaad does not discriminate; it will attack Zhentarim defenders as readily as the party. Destroying the rune drops the force field permanently — which may not be desirable if the party still has defenders to avoid on the way out.

**K19 — Duhlark's Bedroom.** A flameskull patrols this room. A secret compartment beneath the floorboards (DC 14 Investigation) contains a wand of binding with 3 charges remaining — Duhlark's contingency item, missed by Manshoon's initial search because the flameskull's combat-mode activation damaged the floor when it first triggered on Manshoon's arrival.

**K22 — Teleportation Circle.** The access point to the sanctum. A spectator + 4 flying snakes guard the circle. The circle is inert without a teleporter ring held by the activating creature — the ring must be worn (not held) to function. At Alert and Lockdown tiers, 2 additional thugs have been posted at K22 by Sidra after the alarm began. The circle activates when a ring-wearer steps into the center and speaks the command word ("sanctum") — transport to E1 is instantaneous. Without a ring, there is no access to the sanctum from the towers.

*Extradimensional Sanctum — Critical Path Areas.*

**E1 — Arrival Point.** The sanctum's receiving room. Glass walls show purple mist beyond. At Unaware and Suspicious tiers, E1 is empty — the sanctum is at normal operating posture. At Alert and Lockdown tiers, the simulacrum stands here. It looks exactly like Manshoon — same height, same robes, same silver hair, the same artificial left arm. It does not speak first. It evaluates.

**E2 — Foyer.** 12 copper Masks of Manshoon mounted on the walls discharge 5 (1d10) lightning damage each when triggered by a Zhentarim coin pressure plate in the floor (DC 14 Perception to notice; DC 12 Thieves' Tools or DC 13 Arcana to disarm). The masks discharge simultaneously, targeting every creature in the room. This is the sanctum's entry alarm — the discharge also sends a magical pulse to E3, alerting Kaevja.

**E3 — Guard Station.** Kaevja Cynavern (mage), Sidra's counterpart in the sanctum. Kaevja carries a yellow elemental gem (earth elemental) and has the sending spell prepared at all times. If she is not eliminated silently before she acts, her first action is to cast sending to Manshoon — regardless of his location — and to Agorn Fuoco or Vevette Blackwater in E8. A successful sending accelerates the reinforcement timer by 5 minutes. Kaevja is the sanctum's force multiplier: her sending is more dangerous than her combat capability. The party should treat her as a priority target.

**E5 — Laboratory.** Urstul Floxin is here if he survived Arc D. He recognizes the party immediately (he has not forgotten them) and shouts an alarm as his first action — not combat, alarm. His shout reaches E3 and E8 without magic. At this point Urstul wants the party to suffer rather than to fight them; if the alarm has already reached the garrison, he will engage in combat. If the party can silence him before he can shout — or present him with an exit — he is not suicidal.

**E7 — Simulacrum Chamber.** Extremely cold; breath fogs at the door. An ice sarcophagus occupies the center of the room. At Unaware and Suspicious tiers, the simulacrum is here, dormant inside the sarcophagus — it activates automatically within 1 minute of Manshoon being incapacitated or killed. A party that reaches and defeats Manshoon in E13 without checking E7 may face a second Manshoon arriving behind them from this room without warning. At Alert and Lockdown tiers, the sarcophagus is empty — the simulacrum is already activated and standing in E1. The empty sarcophagus is readable: frost patterns inside show recent occupancy. A DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana) check identifies the sarcophagus as a simulacrum preservation vessel and concludes that the simulacrum has been recently removed, which means a duplicate is active somewhere in the sanctum.

**E8 — Reading Room.** Vevette Blackwater + Agorn Fuoco (if survived Arc E). Vevette fights; Agorn does not if the fight is going badly — he offers his ring (he carries one), Manshoon's daily routine, and the ledger's location in exchange for being allowed to leave. A green flame brazier in the center of the room: any creature saying "green flame" within 10 feet of the brazier while aware of its function triggers a fireball (8d6, DC 15 Dexterity saving throw). A DC 14 Investigation check on the brazier reveals the inscription. Manshoon told the lieutenants; Agorn will mention it if he is negotiating.

**E9 — Library and Astral Vault.** The library is accessible normally. The vault at the back of the room is a bloodlocked mechanism: DC 20 Thieves' Tools to bypass with disadvantage, or DC 17 Arcana to bypass via dispel magic or disguise self (making the magical bloodsensor read the user as Manshoon). Inside: 3 spare pass-amulets, 2 spare teleporter rings, and Manshoon's Report on the Grand Game. The Report is the critical Arc J intelligence — it describes the Brandath Crypts approach, identifies Aurinax as "a guardian of considerable patience and considerable power," includes a partially decoded Elvish command phrase, and names Manshoon's analysis of all four faction positions in the Grand Game. This document goes directly to Arc J preparation.

**E10 — Audience Chamber.** Havia Quickknife + Mookie Plush, two halfling adepts who serve as Manshoon's household fanatics. Both fight to death. They are the sanctum's most physically capable non-spellcasting combatants — trained in martial arts, faster than their size suggests, and entirely loyal. They are also the least tactically sophisticated opponents in the sanctum; they attack in a straight line and do not retreat.

**E12 — Manshoon's Quarters.** At Alert and Lockdown tiers, Manshoon is here when the party arrives — staff of power in hand, moving around the room with the controlled energy of someone who has been waiting for a fight. At Lockdown, he has cast mage armor, mind blank, and globe of invulnerability in the past hour. At Unaware and Suspicious, E12 is empty — Manshoon is in E13. A hidden staff compartment in the north wall (DC 16 Investigation) holds a spare arcane staff of ordinary make — not a staff of power, but a plausible decoy for a party who finds an empty room and decides to wait.

Manshoon talks first. This is not a courtesy — he is assessing. He wants to know who sent the party, whether they have the Stone, whether the Vault approach has been compromised, and whether any of them represent a faction he might negotiate with. If the party has something he wants, he offers a trade. If they do not, he offers mercy: "Leave. Tell no one. This is the only time I will make this offer." If they refuse: he uses the simulacrum as a shield if it is present and activated, wall of force to isolate the most dangerous party member, dominate person on the second most dangerous. He does not commit to lethal combat until cornered. The Retributive Strike Gambit: at 30 HP or below, Manshoon grips the staff of power and states, clearly, that he will break it. This is not a bluff — a broken staff of power in E12 releases a 30-foot-radius force explosion dealing 8 × charges remaining force damage with a DC 17 Dexterity saving throw for half. The party must find a way to disarm him (DC 16 Sleight of Hand, a targeted attack to knock the staff from his grip, or a sufficiently compelling negotiation that takes him below the 30 HP threshold of despair) or accept the consequences.

**E13 — Manshoon's Study.** At Unaware and Suspicious tiers, Manshoon is here — books open, the Stone of Golorr's history spread across the desk, notes in cramped Elvish describing the Vault's defenders and their known weaknesses. The party finds him at work. He reacts the same way he would in E12 — he talks first — but the setting is different: he is at a desk, not armed and waiting. He requires one action to reach the staff (on a weapon stand beside the desk) before the Retributive Strike Gambit becomes available. At Alert and Lockdown, E13 is empty. A helmed horror guards the spellbook shelf regardless of tier — it attacks any creature other than Manshoon who approaches the shelves. The blackmail ledger is in an arcane-locked drawer in the desk (DC 17 Thieves' Tools to bypass the lock; DC 15 Arcana to bypass via dispel magic or knock; knock works).

*Adversary Roster — Standard Configuration.* The full garrison is present in both towers and the sanctum. If Skeemo Weirdbottle survived Arc D or Arc E, he is in the sanctum (the DM should note whether Skeemo escaped during those arcs). Skeemo recognizes the party and immediately alerts the garrison to their capabilities — he knows which party members are the primary threats, which spell slots were visible during their prior encounter, and which tactics worked against them before. His presence at Alert or Lockdown tiers means Manshoon has been briefed.

*The Reinforcement Timer.* From the moment any alarm triggers — combat noise audible beyond one room, the E2 pressure plate, Kaevja's sending, Urstul's shout, or any NPC successfully casting sending or message — the timer begins. The DM tracks in 5-minute increments and communicates through environmental description rather than announcing numbers.

| Time | Event |
|------|-------|
| 0 min | Alarm triggers. Sidra activates internal response in K2. All NPCs shift to defensive positions. Kaevja in E3 casts sending (if not yet eliminated). |
| 5 min | 2 thugs arrive at K22 from the Interrogation House via the Yellowspire circle. Kaevja's sending reaches Manshoon and field agents if not silenced. |
| 10 min | 1 mage + 2 thugs arrive at K22. Sidra locks the teleporter ring system — rings already in use still function, but the ring pairing cannot be reversed from K22 without a DC 16 Arcana check by a proficient spellcaster. |
| 15 min | Manshoon (at Unaware/Suspicious, if not yet reached) moves from E13 to E12, retrieving the staff of power. The simulacrum (if dormant in E7) activates and moves to defend the sanctum. |
| 20 min | Vevette's response team arrives at K22 if Vevette is not already present in E8. Full garrison at combat readiness. |
| 25 min | If Manshoon was absent from the sanctum: his sending reply arrives. Simulacrum, if still operational, begins preparing final defensive position. |
| 30 min | External reinforcements — Agorn's team or replacement spy + 4 thugs — arrive at the perimeter with fresh pass-amulets. The party is fighting the entire remaining Splinter cell. |

**Silent Entry Bonus.** If the party reaches the sanctum (E1) without triggering any alarm in the towers, the timer does not begin until an alarm triggers inside the sanctum. The silent-entry window rewards advance planning and precise execution while ensuring that the sanctum itself is never alarm-free — E2's pressure plate, Kaevja in E3, and Urstul in E5 are all alarm triggers even without tower combat.

**Timer Variation by Escalation Tier.** At Unaware, the timer runs as above (30-minute expiration). At Suspicious, reduce to 25 minutes (Kaevja receives an earlier sending confirmation, Sidra's standing orders are more aggressive). At Alert, reduce to 20 minutes (pre-positioned reinforcements at K22, Kaevja already on sending alert). At Lockdown, reduce to 15 minutes (the garrison is already at battle stations; the first alarm confirms the attack rather than initiating a response).

**Tone.** Violent and compressed. This is the campaign's most combat-intensive lair arc by design — the raid genre tests combat capability and speed, not social navigation or spatial puzzle-solving. The party that moves with purpose encounters a manageable garrison. The party that clears every room first, or that pauses to assess after each engagement, arrives at Manshoon facing a garrison that has had time to reinforce.

## Scene 5: Complications and Escape

**Purpose.** What goes wrong, the Doom Raiders parallel operation, the Manshoon confrontation and identity reveal, and extraction. The complications in Arc I are not the rolling improvisation of Arc H's caper structure — they are consequences of the raid's violence accumulating on both sides.

**Content.**

*The Doom Raiders Parallel Operation.* Always fires. Davil Starsong and Tashlyn Yafeera have been building toward Kolat Towers for six missions — more deliberate preparation than any other parallel actor in the campaign. They are not waiting for the party's permission. Their operation launches on the same night the party raids, timed to maximize pressure on the garrison rather than to coordinate with the party's specific movements.

If a PC is a Doom Raiders member, Tashlyn operates as a field ally. She briefs the party before the raid (Scene 3 coordination), her team enters via a different entry method than the party, and during Scene 4 she responds to the party's tactical situation with genuine cooperation — relaying garrison positions via hand signals if they encounter each other, pulling opposition off the party's flank, and prioritizing objectives that overlap with the party's (Manshoon's operational records, pass-amulet system destruction, Vevette Blackwater captured alive). If no PC is a DR member, the party discovers the Doom Raiders mid-raid — an independent force hitting the same target on the same night, with no coordination and no standing obligation to cooperate.

| PC State | Doom Raiders' Action | Result |
|----------|---------------------|--------|
| PCs undetected, moving through towers | DR breach a different entry, triggering the reinforcement timer | Garrison splits; opposition facing PCs reduced, but timer now running |
| PCs detected, timer running | DR arrive into existing chaos | Three-way fight; Tashlyn coordinates if she encounters the party (DC 12 Charisma) |
| PCs in the sanctum | DR fight through towers toward K22 | DR may follow into the sanctum 10–15 minutes after party, arriving as reinforcements during Manshoon confrontation |
| PCs defeated Manshoon, securing objectives | DR arrive, claim Manshoon's operational records | Negotiation over spoils; DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) for full share |
| PCs encounter DR directly in towers | Tashlyn proposes immediate tactical alliance | Combined force; DR provide flanking and area control |
| PCs failed or retreating | DR continue independently | If DR succeed, Davil delivers records to party afterward — minus operational details he kept |

*Facing Manshoon.* Regardless of which room the party reaches him in, Manshoon talks first. The conversation is an assessment — he wants to know what the party knows about the Vault, whether they have the Stone, and whether there is anything they want badly enough to trade for. His opening is never a threat; it is a question. "What do you actually want?" If the answer reveals that they have come for the ledger, he does not offer to trade it — that leverage is too important. If the answer reveals they have come for the Report on the Grand Game, he may be willing to discuss an arrangement, because the party having accurate Vault intelligence does not hurt him if he reaches the Vault first. If the answer reveals they have come to neutralize him entirely: "Then at least you're honest." Combat.

Manshoon's combat priorities in order: the simulacrum as a shield (if present), wall of force to isolate the most dangerous party member, dominate person, and sustained concentration-based control rather than nova damage output. He is not trying to kill the party quickly — he is trying to exhaust them, because a fresh opponent makes better decisions than a desperate one. The Retributive Strike Gambit is real and available at 30 HP.

*The Manshoon Identity Reveal.* This is Arc I's dramatic centerpiece. Three independent evidence trails converge:

Documents in E9 (Manshoon's Report on the Grand Game, which refers to "my predecessor" and "the original network" in a way that implies historical continuity without explaining it) + a sigil on documents in E13 (DC 14 Intelligence (History) to identify as the historical Zhentarim founder's personal mark, in use 200 years before the current Manshoon was born) + Lady Gondafrey's testimony, if she was freed ("He told me he founded the Zhentarim centuries ago. I thought he was mad. Then I saw what was left of his arm — the joint where it ends, the way it ends — and I stopped thinking he was mad.") combine to establish:

- Manshoon is a clone of the original Zhentarim founder, one of multiple surviving clone bodies.
- The clone was activated in Undermountain and subsequently fled.
- Halaster Blackcloak amputated the left arm; the artificial replacement is a tell.
- The simulacrum mechanic: Manshoon can create ice-preserved duplicates with limited capabilities.
- Manshoon's political reach extends to Masked Lords — his blackmail operation is decades in the making.

No single piece is sufficient on its own. The party assembles the reveal from the documents they recover and the witnesses they find. A party that found Lady Gondafrey, read E13's documents, and identified the sigil has the full picture. A party that skipped K13 or didn't attempt E9's vault has a partial picture — enough to understand what Manshoon is, not enough to fully document it.

*Escape Routes.*

**Teleporter Ring Exit.** A ring activates K22's circle from inside E1. Transport is instantaneous to the top of the second tower. The party then moves through the towers (cleared or not) and exits via the force field gap or through the front door with pass-amulets. If the force field is down (K18 rune destroyed), any window or door becomes an exit.

**Yellowspire Circle.** A DC 15 Arcana check with a teleporter ring allows a skilled spellcaster to reverse the K22 circle's destination, redirecting it to the Yellowspire circle in the Castle Ward outpost. This bypasses the towers entirely, depositing the party in a location they control or can access safely. Requires a party member with Arcana proficiency and a working ring.

**Force Field Breach.** If the K18 rune is destroyed (the field is down), the party can exit through any exterior window or door. The red slaad may still be present inside; the garrison may still be active. The field's destruction is visible from the street — it collapses with a low concussive sound and a brief flash of blue light. Savra's extraction team (renown 10+) is positioned outside and provides immediate cover.

**Doom Raiders Extraction.** If the DR cleared the towers (possible if the DR arrived early and the party reached the sanctum), Tashlyn offers to escort the party through the now-controlled building to the street. The DR have a safehouse 7 minutes away in the Trades Ward.

**Tone.** The aftermath of violence. Arc I's escape is not slipping away — it is emerging from a warzone. The party exits carrying wounded, carrying recovered documents, carrying a functional understanding of something that should not exist. The caper genre's clean escape is not available here. They got out.

## Scene 6: Aftermath

**Purpose.** Resolve the raid. No Eye to insert, no Stone upgrade. Instead: deliver the intelligence haul, record the Manshoon faction state, stage the identity reveal through faction debriefs, and position the party for Arc J. Scene 6 is shorter than in prior arcs — the dramatic question of Arc I resolves on a strategic rather than an emotional register. The party has done something violent and decisive; the aftermath reflects that.

**Content.**

*The Intelligence Haul.* Four items with specific campaign consequences.

**The Blackmail Ledger (E13 desk).** Delivering it to Laeral Silverhand through Mirt or Vajra collapses Manshoon's political position — Corylus Thann and Jelenn Urmbrusk are exposed, their influence over Watch patrol assignments and Guildmaster votes evaporates, and two of Manshoon's most valuable civic tools are neutralized regardless of whether Manshoon himself survived. The identity list on the back pages names five Masked Lords total: Corylus Thann, Jelenn Urmbrusk, Mirt, Thardouk Starbuckler, and Dorgar Adarbrent. Mirt will want a word with the party privately before the ledger goes to Laeral, not to suppress the names but to explain them: "I volunteered. The others didn't."

**Manshoon's Spellbook (E13 shelves, guarded by helmed horror).** A 9th-level wizard's working spellbook — 42 spells across all schools, with marginalia indicating active research into planar travel and gate mechanics. Vajra offers 15,000 gp and a favor from the Blackstaff's office in exchange for the spellbook, primarily for what its marginalia reveals about Manshoon's research trajectory. The Doom Raiders offer 8,000 gp and priority intelligence access. Jarlaxle offers, characteristically, "a conversation about the future" rather than a price — he is interested in the planar travel research for reasons he does not specify.

**Manshoon's Report on the Grand Game (E9 astral vault).** The most directly actionable Arc J item. Describes the Brandath Crypts approach in architectural detail, identifies Aurinax by name and species, includes a partially decoded Elvish command phrase (the party has the partial — the complete phrase will require additional research or the Stone's Full Awakening), and analyzes all four Grand Game factions' positions as of the report's date. The analysis is accurate. Manshoon's intelligence on the vault is as complete as anyone's outside the original Masked Lords who created it.

**Lady Gondafrey's Freedom (K13, if freed).** Gondafrey is not merely a rescued prisoner — she is a living witness who can testify before the Masked Lords Council about Manshoon's political infiltration, the blackmail operation's scope, and the simulacrum's nature. Her 47-year captivity is itself evidence. Savra will take Gondafrey to the Order's care immediately and arrange the testimony. Gondafrey herself has one request: to be present when the ledger is delivered to Laeral.

*Faction State: "Manshoon Operational?"*

Three values; this flag is read directly in Arc J.

- **Destroyed.** Both the original Manshoon and the simulacrum were eliminated during the raid. The Splinter cell collapses within a tenday — Vevette (if alive) attempts to hold the surviving operatives together but lacks Manshoon's authority and his clone-network insurance. No Manshoon at the Vault. The party arrives at Arc J facing Aurinax without a third-party obstacle.
- **Simulacrum Only.** The original Manshoon was destroyed but the simulacrum survived and escaped (or was not found). The simulacrum has no 6th-level or higher spell slots and no staff of power. It arrives at the Vault with reduced capabilities and diminished tactical creativity — it can execute plans Manshoon taught it but cannot adapt to novel situations effectively.
- **Alive.** Manshoon survived (escaped, negotiated, or was never reached). He arrives at the Vault at full strength with surviving lieutenants and the staff of power. The hardest Arc J configuration. He will have had time to prepare.

*All-Seven-Faction Debriefs.*

**Mirt (Harpers).** Wants the ledger, and specifically wants to review the identity list before Laeral receives it. "I volunteered" is the context he provides — he became a Masked Lord knowing Manshoon's network existed and chose to be legible to it rather than to fight it from cover. He is not apologizing. If the Manshoon Identity Reveal is fully assembled, Mirt is quiet for a moment before responding: "Clone of the Founder. Of course. That's why he could never be bargained with — he has nowhere to retreat to. He's already been everywhere."

**Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance).** "Is Manshoon neutralized?" Direct and operational. If alive: "Then he will be at the Vault. We plan accordingly." If destroyed: brief satisfaction, then immediately to the next question — the identity list and its implications for Watch patrol integrity. He will want to know which Watch officers Manshoon's Masked Lord contacts influenced.

**Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave).** "Is the force field down?" If the K18 rune was destroyed, Melannor is relieved — four decades of environmental damage to the Trades Ward block. He sends a junior member to begin soil remediation. If the barlgura escaped during the raid and is unaccounted for, Melannor is alarmed; he begins tracking within the hour.

**Savra Belabranta (Order of the Gauntlet).** "Lady Gondafrey. Is she alive?" If freed: Savra's response is not relief — it is a snap back to professional mode, because she understands what this moment means for the Order. "Captain Gondafrey. You're on duty, Captain." If Gondafrey died during the raid, or was never reached because the party did not go to K13, Savra asks the party to go back. She frames it as a standing Order obligation. She is not wrong.

**Vajra Safahr (Force Grey).** "The spellbook. Tell me about the spellbook." Vajra's interest is less in the combat applications and more in the marginalia — Manshoon's planar travel research represents an intelligence gap Force Grey has been aware of for years. She also wants the construction specifications for the extradimensional sanctum: "A personal extradimensional space of that scale, anchored to a physical structure, is not a known construction technique. He developed it. I want to know how." If the party destroyed the sanctum without recovering the specifications, she is professionally disappointed.

**Tashlyn Yafeera or Davil Starsong (Doom Raiders).** "Is it done?" If the DR participated in the raid, Tashlyn's debrief is businesslike — which objectives were achieved, which gaps remain, what the operational records recovered tell them about surviving Splinter personnel. If Vevette Blackwater was captured: "Good. She has things to answer for. So does the rest of what's left of that network." The Doom Raiders' internal war with the Manshoon Splinter ends here, one way or another. If the Splinter was only partially dismantled, Davil begins planning follow-up operations without asking the party's involvement.

**Jarlaxle (if contacted in Scene 1).** If the Manshoon parallel strike fired in Arc H and the party helped or acknowledged it: "Now we're even. Or close enough." Jarlaxle wants to read the Report on the Grand Game — specifically Manshoon's analysis of BD's position in the Grand Game, which he suspects is more accurate than anyone outside BD has any right to be. If the party assembled the full Manshoon Identity Reveal: Jarlaxle confirms the one piece he withheld from his Scene 1 briefing. "I said I had suspicions. The arm was one. There is another thing about him — the way he holds the staff. No one born to a wand holds a staff like that. You hold it like something that has always been yours." He suspected. He wanted to see if the party could find the rest.

*Campaign Calendar.* Arc I consumes 1–3 tendays total: several days of intelligence gathering and preparation (Scenes 1–3), 1 day for the operation and immediate escape (Scenes 4–5), and 2–4 days of recovery and debriefs (Scene 6). The operation itself — Scenes 4 and 5 — is the shortest single-session window of any lair arc. Update the campaign calendar accordingly. The Founders' Day clock is relevant here only as context: Arc I consumes roughly the same calendar window as the Founders' Day events, which provides cover for Watch attention being elsewhere.

*Transition.* The party holds the Stone of Golorr with between 0 and 3 Eyes. If 3 Eyes are restored, Arc J launches — the Full Awakening has already occurred and the Vault's location is known; Arc I's intelligence confirms the approach route. If Eyes remain unrestored, other lair arcs are still available; the intelligence haul from Arc I (specifically Manshoon's Report on the Grand Game) enriches whichever arcs follow. The Report's partial command phrase is a direct Arc J hook regardless of Eye count — the party knows the phrase is partially decoded and that completing it requires either the Stone's Full Awakening or additional research.

**Tone.** Tactical decompression. Arc I's aftermath has a different texture than the prior lair arcs' endings: no revelatory awakening (F), no moral reckoning (G), no relationship reassessment (H). This is strategic positioning — the party has done a violent thing that was necessary, has recovered intelligence that changes the Arc J situation, and has set a faction state flag that will determine what they face at the Vault. Professional satisfaction or grim assessment, depending on how the operation went.

# Design Notes: Kolat Towers

## Genre Identity

Arc I is a raid — the fourth and final genre in the lair arc sequence. Arc F was dungeon infiltration (stealth and navigation). Arc G was social infiltration (identity and exposure management). Arc H was a caper (improvisation and rolling adaptation). Arc I is a raid: the party assaults a fortified position with overwhelming violence, and the primary strategic variable is speed. The reinforcement timer is not a complication added to the raid — it is the genre's defining mechanic. Every arc in the sequence tests different party capabilities. Arc I tests combat effectiveness and decision-making under time pressure.

## The Raid as Final Genre Evolution

Four arcs, four genres, because the party has four different capability sets that a single-genre sequence cannot test. The raid placement at the end of the sequence is deliberate: by Arc I, the party has demonstrated stealth capability (F), social intelligence (G), and improvisational flexibility (H). The raid asks whether they can leverage all of those in a context where they cannot afford to use any of them thoughtfully — speed collapses the time for planning, and the reinforcement timer punishes hesitation. A party that treats the raid like a dungeon crawl (clearing every room before advancing) loses to the timer. A party that treats it like a caper (improvising room by room without a plan) arrives at Manshoon underpowered. The raid rewards parties who have internalized the prior arcs' lessons and can execute without pausing to plan.

## The No-Eye Structural Deviation

Arc I is the only lair arc without an Eye recovery. The structural risk is that the scene feels lower-stakes than F, G, or H — that without the Stone's awakening, the climax lacks weight. The intelligence haul addresses this by giving each recovered item a specific, named Arc J consequence that the party understands before they enter the towers. The blackmail ledger is not "valuable treasure" — it is the political tool that neutralizes Manshoon's Masked Lord influence. The Report on the Grand Game is not "lore" — it contains the partial command phrase that opens the Vault. Every item in the haul has a downstream consequence that the party can predict, which makes recovering it feel as meaningful as restoring an Eye.

The Manshoon Identity Reveal replaces the Stone's awakening as the arc's dramatic centerpiece. Where the Stone awakening in prior arcs revealed something about the campaign's hidden architecture (Nihiloor's domination in F, the Cassalanters' debt in G, the aboleth consciousness in H), the Identity Reveal reveals something about the antagonist's nature that recontextualizes every Manshoon interaction the party has had since Arc E.

## The Simulacrum Ambiguity as Player-Facing Puzzle

The simulacrum's predecided positioning creates two structurally different encounter sequences depending on escalation tier. At Alert and Lockdown, the party encounters the simulacrum first in E1 — they may believe they have defeated Manshoon before they reach E12. At Unaware and Suspicious, the party reaches Manshoon first in E13 and may not discover the simulacrum in E7 until it activates behind them.

Three discoverable clues let the party identify which version they fought: the simulacrum cannot cast 6th-level or higher spells (DC 15 Arcana to notice the pattern after observing its spell selection); the simulacrum has roughly half the hit points of the original (a party that tracked damage dealt will notice the fight ended faster than expected); and Lady Gondafrey, if freed, knows that Manshoon "sometimes sends a quieter version of himself" and can describe the behavioral difference after the fact.

The ambiguity is a puzzle, not a gotcha. The clues are present and discoverable. A party that notices and investigates walks out of the sanctum knowing exactly what they fought. A party that does not notice and investigate may discover the answer in the Arc J confrontation instead — when they arrive at the Vault expecting a diminished Manshoon and encounter a full-strength one.

## Doom Raiders as Parallel Actor

The Doom Raiders have more setup investment than any other parallel actor in the campaign — six missions across Arcs B through E, each contributing a piece of the intelligence picture that culminates in the Kolat Towers operation. This investment gives their parallel action credibility that a sudden appearance would not have. They were not deployed for Arc I because Arc I is dramatic — they were deployed for Arc I because they have been building toward it since the beginning of the campaign and they have finally run out of reasons to wait.

The "always fires" decision ensures that the Doom Raiders' investment pays off regardless of the party's faction choices. If the party never engaged with the Doom Raiders, the DR still launch and the party encounters them as an independent force — which is itself a characterization of what the DR are. They do not need the party's permission to finish their own war.

## The Manshoon Identity Reveal

The three-arc reveal structure — Name in Arc E, Identity in Arc I, Motivation in Arc J — treats Manshoon differently than the other three villain faction leaders. Xanathar is immediately legible; Jarlaxle makes himself legible on his own terms; the Cassalanters are legible once the party reaches their temple. Manshoon is the one faction leader whose true nature requires assembly from distributed evidence, because his true nature is genuinely strange — a clone of a 200-year-old wizard, not just a Zhentarim leader — and because understanding it changes the party's strategic relationship with him in a way that understanding the other faction leaders does not.

A party that assembles the full reveal understands why Manshoon cannot negotiate the ledger away (he cannot expose the operation without exposing himself), why he fled Undermountain (Halaster is one of the few beings who could destroy him permanently), and why his Vault ambitions are less about the gold than about the political legitimacy 500,000 dragons would purchase. This is the Motivation that Arc J will confirm.

## The Retributive Strike as Moral Dimension

Each lair arc has a moment that asks the party to weigh a consequence against a tactical advantage: smokepowder demolition in F, the children's sacrifice in G, Zelifarn's crystal ball in H. Arc I's equivalent is the Retributive Strike Gambit. If the party is winning the fight against Manshoon, they must decide how to prevent him from breaking the staff — which means either disarming him while he's still dangerous enough to threaten the gambit, or accepting the explosion, or finding a third path (negotiation that takes him past his desperation threshold without combat reaching 30 HP). The gambit compresses Arc I's moral dimension into a single decision point because the raid genre does not leave space for extended moral deliberation.

## Variable Difficulty and Reinforcement Timer

The escalation tier system reaches its harshest expression in Arc I. Lockdown tier halves the timer (30 to 15 minutes), seals the force-field gaps, invalidates captured amulets, and pre-activates the simulacrum. A party that hit every Manshoon outpost in Arc E arrives at Kolat Towers with Lockdown already active — which means the fastest timer, the fewest entry options, and the best-prepared garrison. This is the correct consequence of aggressive Arc E play. The difficulty is not a punishment; it is a calibration. The party that did the most to expose and destabilize the Splinter network is also the party most capable of running the raid under the hardest conditions.
