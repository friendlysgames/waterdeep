## Chapter Purpose

The DM reference chapter. Read before the campaign begins — not during sessions. Provides the world context, faction histories, and villain intelligence the DM needs to run Waterdeep as a living city with a specific past rather than a set of keyed locations.

Sections 1–3 (Lore, History, Grand Game) are read once before play. The four villain sections are consulted throughout the campaign each time the DM needs to know how a villain would respond, escalate, or negotiate. The History section in particular is foundational: it covers the city's political backstory and the origin of every faction competing for the vault. Read it before anything else.

---

## 1. Core Concept

Waterdeep has always been governed by secrets — the Masked Lords, the hidden guild structure, the underworld that feeds on the legal economy's margins. The Grand Game is the city's nature, clarified by one unusually high-stakes prize.

## 2. Sections

### Lore of Waterdeep

**Purpose:** Gives the DM enough of the city to make it feel inhabited — dense enough for Waterdeep to have friction, history, and personality before the first session.

**Content:**

*The Wards*
- **Castle Ward** (city center, government, Yawning Portal): seat of the Masked Lords; City Watch headquarters; Durnan's Yawning Portal is the campaign's literal and symbolic entry point
- **Sea Ward** (wealthy northwest): noble estates, major temples, the Field of Triumph; Cassalanter Villa is here
- **North Ward** (established families, quieter money): quieter money, old names, established households; Trollskull Manor (the party's home base) is here — operating mechanics in Appendix F
- **Trades Ward** (commerce, guilds, day-and-night activity): most guild halls; faction front businesses cluster here; Kolat Towers (Manshoon's fortress) occupies a fortified block here
- **South Ward** (working class, immigrants, industry): Istrid Horn's moneylending operation; where new arrivals settle first
- **Dock Ward** (port, crime, contraband):** the most dangerous ward; Xanathar Guild's primary surface territory; Sea Maidens Faire moors here during Fleetswake
- **City of the Dead** (walled necropolis): the Brandath Crypts (vault entrance) are here; patrolled but not well-staffed
- **Undermountain** (below the city): the Yawning Portal well drops to Level 1; Skullport is three levels down; outside city jurisdiction

*Governance*
- **Open Lord Laeral Silverhand**: one of the Seven Sisters, former Chosen of Mystra; politically formidable; knows more about the Grand Game than she lets on; would prefer to recover the vault quietly if it ever surfaces
- **Masked Lords**: anonymous governing council; their identities are the city's most closely guarded secret; individual Lords may have faction affiliations the PCs never learn
- **The Code Legal**: posted publicly, enforced by the Watch; notable for what it doesn't cover (most guild business) as much as what it does
- **Magisters**: city judges; Barnibus Blastwind handles sensitive investigations
- **The City Watch**: 1,200 guards across three shifts; understaffed, overworked; not corrupt by default but susceptible to political pressure

*Guilds*
- 18 recognized guilds control every significant trade; operating without guild membership invites fines and Watch attention
- Relevant guilds: the Dungsweepers' (connected to the Dock Ward underworld), the League of Skinners and Tanners (Zhentarim material transit cover), the Jewelers' (money laundering vector), the Carpenters' (Cassalanter villa maintenance)
- Most major guilds have at least one faction operative embedded in leadership

*Religion*
- Major temples in Sea Ward: Tyr (Justice; City Watch captain is a worshiper), Tymora (Luck; popular with adventurers and criminals alike), Selûne (Moon; widely attended)
- House of Inspired Hands: a Gondar workshop-temple, not a standard place of worship; Arc C connects here
- Hidden: the Cassalanters maintain an illegal temple to Asmodeus beneath their villa; the Code Legal offense alone is capital

*Festival Calendar (DM reference)*
| Date | Festival | Campaign function |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| Ches 21–30 | Fleetswake | Maritime festival; Sea Maidens Faire arrives; cover for Dock Ward investigation |
| Tarsakh 1–10 | Waukeentide | Commerce festival; Cassalanters host public galas; cover for Sea Ward investigation |
| Flamerule 1 | Founders' Day | HARD DEADLINE — Cassalanter sacrifice; city celebrates publicly |
| Marpenoth 3 | Day of Wonders | Gondar invention festival; nimblewright demonstrations; adjustable for Arc C pacing |

*The Dragonward and Walking Statues*
- The dragonward: Ahghairon's founding spell; prevents dragons from entering the city without the Open Lord's permission
- Eight Walking Statues: 90-foot animated stone constructs, dormant at eight points around the city; last activated during the Spellplague; a civic reference point, not a player resource

**Tone:** Dense, specific, practical. Every fact should imply a consequence. The dragonward matters because Jarlaxle moves by sea. Guild membership matters because Trollskull Manor needs permits. The Watch matters because faction violence eventually reaches it.

### History of Waterdeep

**Purpose:** The complete narrative backstory of the campaign, told in chronological order. A DM who reads it should understand not just what happened, but why every villain is exactly where they are when the campaign begins. Every faction's present is a consequence of something that happened here.

**Structure:** Written as continuous narrative history organized by era, in the manner of CoS:R's History of Barovia — not a timeline table, not organized by faction. Events build on each other. Each era sets up the next. Faction histories are woven into the chronology as they become relevant, not treated as separate threads.

**Content:**

*Era 1 — Ahghairon's Gifts (early history of the city)*
- Ahghairon the Wizard as Waterdeep's true founder — the first system, even if not the first settlement
- The Open Lord: a visible, accountable executive, designed to be removed if they fail the city
- The Masked Lords: anonymous councilors whose identities are the city's most closely guarded secret; Ahghairon's answer to the problem of power is to make sure no one person can hold all of it
- The dragonward, the Code Legal, the City Watch — built not as conveniences but as protections; frame these as the work of someone who watched what tyranny actually looks like and designed against it specifically
- The closing note for this era: Ahghairon built Waterdeep to survive bad rulers. The campaign is the test of whether he was right.

*Era 2 — The Long Shadow (the Xanathar Guild's century)*
- The name "Xanathar" as a title that predates any individual beholder; the first holder established the criminal underworld's structure; subsequent Xanathars inherited the apparatus and the paranoia together
- The Guild's method of continuity: systematic elimination of anyone who knows more than Xanathar itself; what remains after a century of this is a surveillance network with no institutional memory beyond what the current beholder chooses to believe
- The current Xanathar's ascension: how it came to believe it is the original; why this belief is now structural to the organization's identity rather than correctable
- Skullport as the Guild's undercity foundation — the shadow city three levels below Waterdeep, outside city jurisdiction, processing everything the surface economy won't handle; the Xanathar Guild's relationship to Skullport is the relationship between a city and its sewers
- Close this era on the Guild's current state: an empire built on fear, run by a paranoid intelligence, served by people who survive precisely because they have no ambitions

*Era 3 — Bregan D'aerthe's Long Road (decades before the campaign)*
- Jarlaxle Baenre born illegitimate in Menzoberranzan's most powerful house; should not have survived infancy; what it says about him that he did
- The founding of Bregan D'aerthe ("those of the broken house"): a mercenary company built from drow males without house ties — no status, no future underground, nothing to lose; how Jarlaxle turned disposability into leverage
- The organization's rise: becoming the underdark's most reliable intelligence network and mercenary force across decades of operation; the decision to expand to the surface as a strategic rather than desperate move
- The Luskan operation: Jarlaxle as the hidden power behind the Ship Captains council for years; his goal throughout — Lords' Alliance membership for Luskan — explained here as the destination he has been moving toward the entire time
- This era ends with Jarlaxle's intelligence about Neverember's vault reaching him; his decision to move on Waterdeep; the Sea Maidens Faire established as the vehicle

*Era 4 — The Founding of Chains (the Zhentarim's origins and fracture)*
- Manshoon of Zhentil Keep as the Zhentarim's founder: a mercantile conspiracy built into a criminal network spanning the Sword Coast; ambition on an organizational scale
- The simulacrum problem as the organization's defining condition: Manshoon has died and been replaced by his own simulacra multiple times; the Zhentarim that now operates across the Sword Coast is an organization that has outlasted its founder's coherent leadership and retained only his methods
- The fracture: Davil Starsong's cell separates from Manshoon's organization on explicitly pragmatic grounds — visible violence and criminal overreach shorten lifespans; a moderate operation presenting a legitimate face is more profitable and survivable; this is the Doom Raiders, now operating in Waterdeep as "the Zhentarim" that most of Waterdeep knows
- Manshoon's separate Waterdeep operation: the original philosophy, arrived later, read Neverember's ouster as political opportunity; Kolat Towers established quietly; whether the man in the towers is the original Manshoon or a simulacrum is a question the text leaves open until Arc I
- This era's closing note: two organizations now use the same name in the same city and despise each other; neither acknowledges the other as legitimate; the players will eventually need to understand this distinction

*Era 5 — A Debt in Blood (the Cassalanter bargain, approximately twenty years before the campaign)*
- The Cassalanter name in Waterdeep's noble registry: three centuries; comfortable wealth before any intervention; the bargain was made from ambition, not desperation
- What the bargain was: three children's souls in exchange for wealth compressed from three generations into one, and political access that normally takes a family centuries to build; Victoro and Ammalia discussed it, understood what they were signing, and signed
- The proof of contract: souls claimed one at a time; Osvaldo, the eldest, was seventeen; he is now in his early thirties and is a chain devil living in the temple beneath his parents' villa; they visit him
- What the Cassalanters built in the years since: the charitable reputation, the political connections, the public warmth — none of it faked; they became what they presented themselves as while also accumulating what Asmodeus requires
- The recovery plan: 99 soul coins plus 500,000 Dragons to reclaim the claims on Terenzio and Elzerina; approximately 40 coins accumulated over eight years; the Founders' Day deadline is Ammalia's own calculation based on her assessment of the children's soul erosion — it does not appear in the contract; she believes she has until this Flamerule 1
- This era closes on what the Cassalanters look like at campaign start: impeccably positioned, running out of time, prepared to do something irreversible if nothing changes

*Era 6 — The Open Lord's Fall (Neverember's tenure, ending approximately three years before the campaign)*
- Who Neverember was: not a villain by origin; an ambitious politician who genuinely improved Waterdeep — harbor expansion, post-Spellplague rebuilding in the Dock Ward, Watch funding increases; the improvements were real
- How the embezzlement began: small diversions for infrastructure projects he couldn't get officially approved; grew into a reserve; the reserve became personal; by the time the Masked Lords investigated, he had convinced himself his contributions to the city entitled him to it
- The Stone of Golorr: an aboleth artifact Neverember had modified to encode the vault's location in a form only the three Eyes could unlock; he scattered the Eyes among trusted contacts before his ouster; the trust did not outlast his position
- His ouster: the Masked Lords voted him out; Laeral Silverhand appointed; Neverember retreated to Neverwinter and declared himself "Lord Protector" there; the Lords managed the public story; the private damage is ongoing
- Renaer: knew about the embezzlement in general terms, not the vault's location; has spent three years working to separate his name from his father's; the vault, if it surfaces, destroys that effort regardless of what Renaer does or didn't know

*Era 7 — A City Watching Itself (Laeral's Waterdeep, the present)*
- The current era: stable, reformist, watchful; Laeral Silverhand as the kind of leader Ahghairon designed the system to produce
- Her read on the vault: she suspects it exists; doesn't know where; would prefer quiet recovery over public confrontation — the story of how the vault exists at all damages the Lords' institutional credibility regardless of who tells it or how
- Her relationship to the Grand Game: watching and choosing not to intervene yet; her calculus is to let the factions exhaust themselves competing, then move when the gold is found and the winners are tired; she has contingencies

*Era 8 — The Players Arrive (the months before the campaign begins)*
- The convergence: all four factions move on Waterdeep in the same window — Manshoon's cell establishing Kolat Towers, Jarlaxle arriving with the Sea Maidens Faire, the Cassalanters accelerating their timeline, the Xanathar Guild responding to new pressure from competitors it hasn't yet fully identified
- Nar'l Xibrindas placed inside the Xanathar Guild by Jarlaxle before the campaign begins; intelligence about the Stone and the vault already flowing to Bregan D'aerthe
- Dalakhar: Neverember's agent sent to Waterdeep to recover the Stone of Golorr; the chain of events that puts the Stone in the Gralhunds' hands and the Eyes in motion; this thread leads directly to the fireball
- This era does not end neatly — it ends with the PCs walking into the Yawning Portal and everything already in motion

**Tone:** Continuous narrative history organized by era. Each era is a story with named people making identifiable decisions for understandable reasons. No timeline tables. No faction-organized subsections. Events cause later events — the history should feel like a river arriving at the campaign's present, not a set of parallel biographies.

### The Grand Game

**Purpose:** Gives the DM the in-world frame for the faction competition — not faction stats, but the texture of how power actually works in Waterdeep.

**Content:**

*What the Grand Game Is*
- The term used in the city's criminal and political underground for the ongoing competition between power blocs for influence, territory, and profit
- Not formally organized; no governing body; called the Grand Game because everyone who plays it understands they are playing it

*How Information Moves*
- **Front businesses**: Sea Maidens Faire (Bregan D'aerthe), Istrid Horn's moneylending (Doom Raiders), a Trades Ward importing company (Manshoon), a charitable hospital (Cassalanters)
- **Informants**: the Watch has informants in every ward; every faction has informants in the Watch; Jarlaxle has informants in every other faction; Skeemo Weirdbottle compiles all of it
- **Social events**: Sea Ward dinner parties are intelligence operations with better wine; the Cassalanters' charitable galas are recruitment screens
- **The Yawning Portal**: Durnan has heard everything; he shares selectively; every faction knows this and cultivates him anyway

*The City Watch as a Character*
- Genuinely trying, most of the time; aware that some cases are politically untouchable
- Most officers: not corrupt by rule, susceptible to pressure from above
- Barnibus Blastwind: the exception — thorough, meticulous, personally compromised, and more useful than any other Watch contact

*What Each Faction Knows at Campaign Start*

| Faction | What they know | What they have | What they're doing |
|---------|---------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Xanathar Guild | Stone was with Dalakhar (now Gralhund/PCs); Dalakhar worked for Renaer | One Eye | Pursuing the Stone; hunting Dalakhar's trail |
| Manshoon's Zhentarim | Stone was with Dalakhar; Gralhunds intercepted it | One Eye | Urstul Floxin redirected to Gralhund Villa |
| Cassalanters | Vault exists (Lords' Alliance contact); have one Eye | One Eye | Accumulating soul coins; not yet pursuing Stone actively |
| Bregan D'aerthe | Full intelligence via Nar'l Xibrindas inside Xanathar Guild | No Eye yet | Moving to acquire one; observing all other factions |
| Doom Raiders | Aware of Grand Game at high level; not primary competitors for vault | Nothing | Maintaining territory; watching |

**Tone:** Practical and in-world. The DM should finish this section with a working sense of how power moves in Waterdeep — the texture of it, not just the stats.

### Xanathar

**Purpose:** Gives the DM the tools to portray Xanathar as a specific, three-dimensional villain: a paranoid intelligence that has built a criminal empire around its own terror of vulnerability, wearing the trappings of a monster because the trappings are convenient.

**Personality Phases**

| Phase | Name | When active | The mode |
|-------|------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | The Paranoid Tyrant | Acts I–II | Calm control punctuated by administrative violence; the PCs are inside a system whose center they haven't identified |
| 2 | The Wounded God | Act III | Paranoia spiking to full-body vigilance; PCs specifically identified; everything implies conspiracy |
| 3 | The Cornered Monster | Arc F | Volcanic and occasionally lucid; all safeguards failed; the goldfish is the only consistent anchor |

*Phase triggers*
- Phase 1 → 2: PCs steal an Eye, raid a Guild location, or free a significant prisoner
- Phase 2 → 3: PCs enter the Lair, or multiple Guild operations fail in a short window

*Pop culture inspirations:* Jabba the Hutt (immovable center of an empire who is also the most childish person in the room); Inspector Javert (certainty as personality disorder); Tony Soprano (the businesslike violence of someone who stopped registering others as fully real)

**Relationships**

| NPC | Role | Nature of relationship |
|-----|------|----------------------|
| Ahmaergo | Majordomo | Most trusted lieutenant; the only one Xanathar registers as competent; Ahmaergo knows he is replaceable |
| Nar'l Xibrindas | Advisor | Trusted as a drow voice on Guild matters; actually a Bregan D'aerthe spy; discovery ends Nar'l immediately |
| Nihiloor | Mind flayer operative | Trusted intelligence gatherer; Xanathar grants it unusual autonomy; plant for Mad Mage integration |
| Manshoon | Rival | Active enemy; Xanathar has ordered Manshoon killed twice; both failed; this is accelerating the paranoia |
| Jarlaxle | Unknown | Not yet identified as a player; the beholder is paranoid about anything operating in its territory without its knowledge |
| The PCs | Threat | Irritants → active problem → something requiring Xanathar's personal attention (rarely good for anyone) |

**Goals**

1. **Acquire the Vault of Dragons** — wants the gold as proof of supremacy, not primarily for wealth; method: recover the Stone, use the three Eyes, open the vault; contingency: pursue whoever has it; connects to Arc F
2. **Eliminate Manshoon** — the splinter cell incursion is a personal insult; method: Guild response teams targeting Zhentarim operations; no contingency (this failure is part of what drives Phase 3)
3. **Maintain the Undercity** — Skullport supply lines fund the Guild; method: Ahmaergo manages this operationally; connects to Mad Mage integration

**Key design note:** Xanathar's goldfish Sylgar receives more consideration than any humanoid lieutenant. The DM should establish this early — it becomes a specific vulnerability and a specific dramatic moment if Sylgar is harmed during the lair heist.

### Manshoon (Zhentarim Splinter Cell)

**Purpose:** Distinguishes Manshoon clearly from the Doom Raiders and gives the DM the tools to play the campaign's most intellectually dangerous villain — the one who is always further ahead than anyone believes.

> **The Two Zhentarims — DM Note:** Manshoon's splinter cell and Davil Starsong's Doom Raiders are entirely separate organizations. They share a name and a history and nothing else. Never conflate them in NPC dialogue. See also: Ch. 1 Campaign Contract; Appendix C.

**Personality Phases**

| Phase | Name | When active | The mode |
|-------|------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | The Shadow | Acts I–II | Absent; only his lieutenants are visible; PCs fight "the Zhentarim" without knowing Manshoon is a distinct player |
| 2 | The Architect | Acts II–III | Revealed; negotiates from strength; charming, precise, and calm about consequences in a way that should unsettle |
| 3 | The Simulacrum Problem | Arc I | Cornered; the performance of calm drops; the simulacrum fights while the real Manshoon exits |

*Phase triggers*
- Phase 1 → 2: PCs identify Manshoon specifically by name or signature, requiring at least two separate intelligence threads to connect
- Phase 2 → 3: PCs penetrate Kolat Towers or destroy a significant portion of his operation

*Pop culture inspirations:* Tywin Lannister (the chess player who is also the most dangerous piece on the board); Anton Chigurh (the profound calm of someone who has settled their relationship with consequence); Hannibal Lecter (the politeness is real)

**Relationships**

| NPC | Role | Nature of relationship |
|-----|------|----------------------|
| Urstul Floxin | Field lieutenant | Most effective operative; trusted with the Stone recovery at Gralhund Villa; his failure there is the first crack |
| The Doom Raiders | Rival cell | Professional contempt; would absorb them if it were efficient; hasn't needed to yet |
| Xanathar | Rival | Useful; the Guild's visibility keeps the Watch occupied; when the beholder becomes a direct obstacle, Manshoon has a plan |
| Jarlaxle | Mutual wariness | The only Grand Game player Manshoon takes seriously; non-interference arrangement, fragile; Jarlaxle has better intelligence and both know it |
| The Cassalanters | Missed opportunity | Their infernal connections could have been leveraged; Manshoon chose independence over shared control |
| The PCs | Escalating problem | Early: noise; mid-campaign: a problem to solve; first solution is always a job offer, not an assassination |

**Goals**

1. **Acquire the Stone of Golorr** — the Stone is a transformed aboleth encoded with ancient Weave knowledge; Manshoon means to absorb the Weave and supplant Mystra; the vault's gold funds the process; method: Stone recovery → Weave absorption; has one Eye; contingency: destroy the vault rather than cede it; connects to Arcs D and I
2. **Control Waterdeep's Underworld** — eliminate Xanathar, absorb the Doom Raiders, marginalize Bregan D'aerthe; method: patient erosion through targeted disruption and strategic violence; the Dock Ward conflict in Acts I–II is this plan in early operation
3. **The Simulacrum Insurance** — the simulacrum is not a backup; it is the primary survival strategy; defeating Manshoon in combat may defeat only his copy; connects to Arc I design requirement

**Key design note:** The simulacrum reveal needs to be timed carefully. Too early, and it deflates the Kolat Towers raid. Too late, and it feels like a cheat. Target: players discover it during the raid itself, as a revelation that changes how they approach the final confrontation.

**Source references:** `adventure-wdh.json` Ch.8; Appendix B (Manshoon stat block); `1. The Villains.pdf`; `12. Lair Zhentarim Kolat Towers.pdf`

### The Cassalanters

**Purpose:** Gives the DM the tools to portray the campaign's most emotionally complex villains — people whose motives are comprehensible, whose actions are inexcusable, and who are both things at the same time.

**Personality Phases**

| Phase | Name | When active | The mode |
|-------|------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | The Generous Patrons | Acts I–II | Warmth, ease, genuine charity; the public face that is also genuinely true; warmth toward tieflings is specific and real |
| 2 | The Desperate Parents | Acts II–III | Mask off; they did it and they've lived with it; Ammalia holds steady, Victoro is starting to fracture |
| 3 | The Sacrificers | Arc G | Founders' Day decided; a terrible calm; they will take any genuine alternative the PCs offer, immediately and without negotiation |

*Phase triggers*
- Phase 1 → 2: PCs investigate the villa's lower levels, encounter infernal evidence, or learn the truth from a secondary source
- Phase 2 → 3: Founders' Day is three sessions away or less

*Pop culture inspirations:* Walter and Skyler White (Breaking Bad — the partnership of complicity where love was real and the decision is now made); Cersei Lannister (the parent whose love for children becomes the root of atrocity)

**Relationships**

| NPC | Role | Nature of relationship |
|-----|------|----------------------|
| Victoro and Ammalia (each other) | Co-conspirators | The marriage is real; decision made together, burden carried together; Ammalia makes the final calls; Victoro has stopped questioning this |
| Osvaldo | Eldest child (transformed) | Chain devil; the Cassalanters know where he is; they visit; the DM decides what these visits look like |
| Terenzio and Elzerina | Surviving children | Nine and seven years old; don't know about the bargain; should appear in Arc B before PCs investigate the villa |
| Asmodeus | Creditor | The terms are fixed; Asmodeus does not negotiate with mortals who have already signed |
| The Harpers | Watchers | Ammalia knows the Harpers monitor their charitable work; their genuine goodness has kept the surveillance clean for years |
| The PCs | Unplanned variable | Either a catastrophe (exposure) or a solution (alternative path); the Cassalanters will tell the truth if they believe the PCs can help |

**Goals**

1. **99 Soul Coins Before Founders' Day** — currently have approximately 40; need 59 more; method: criminal-adjacent acquisitions at arm's length; contingency: partial payment will not be accepted (they don't know this yet); connects to Arc G
2. **The 500,000 Dragons** — required to complete the Asmodean payment; have one Eye; not yet actively pursuing the Stone; method: political intelligence through Lords' Alliance contacts; connects to Arc G
3. **Keep the Temple Secret** — discovery is a capital Code Legal offense and destroys their plan before it can succeed; method: Willifort Crowelle (doppelganger butler) manages household security; connects to PCs' primary leverage point in any negotiation

**Key design note:** Terenzio and Elzerina must appear before Arc G. The DM should identify during Arc B when the children will first be visible — a festival, a neighborhood event, a Cassalanter charitable appearance. Once the PCs have met the children, the Founders' Day deadline becomes personal rather than abstract.

**Source references:** `adventure-wdh.json` Ch.6; `1. The Villains.pdf`; `10. Lair Cassalanter Villa.pdf`

### Jarlaxle Baenre

**Purpose:** Gives the DM the tools to play the campaign's most flexible villain — the one whose role (adversary, ally, or patron) is determined by player choices, and who is playable and interesting in all three versions.

> **Conditional Entry:** Jarlaxle enters the Grand Game as a named player only if the nimblewright investigation (Arc C) leads to the Sea Maidens Faire. If the PCs never follow that thread, Bregan D'aerthe remains a background faction. Personality phases below apply only if the conditional is met. See: Ch. 1 Faction Affiliations; Key Decisions, item 4 below.

**Personality Phases**

Phase 1 is universal. After the reveal, the track splits based on whether the PCs choose opposition or negotiation.

*Phase 1 (universal) — Zardoz Zord*

| When active | The mode |
|-------------|----------|
| Acts I–II (conditional) | The disguise; a plausible, theatrical Calishite eccentric who is too observant; PCs may like him before they know who he is; the performance holds but the intelligence behind it keeps slipping through |

*Trigger to Phase 2:* PCs penetrate the disguise, or Jarlaxle decides they've earned the reveal. Either way, "Zardoz Zord" ends here.

---

*Adversary Track — if the PCs choose to oppose or heist against him*

| Phase | Name | When active | The mode |
|-------|------|-------------|----------|
| 2A | The Unmasked | Arc H (adversary path) | True identity revealed without negotiation; Jarlaxle is gracious about it — he respects the attempt and doesn't rage; the deal is no longer on the table, but the disdain is minimal; he treats them like colleagues who chose the wrong firm |
| 3A | The Graceful Enemy | Arc H onward, adversary | He doesn't hunt them, but he doesn't help them either; Nar'l's intelligence is redirected to protect Bregan D'aerthe's position; he may counter-heist them (recover what they took), may simply outmaneuver them; the posture is professional, not vendetta |

*Trigger to Phase 3A:* PCs successfully steal the Eye, or the confrontation resolves without an alliance.

---

*Ally Track — if the PCs negotiate or join Bregan D'aerthe*

| Phase | Name | When active | The mode |
|-------|------|-------------|----------|
| 2B | Jarlaxle in the Game | Arc H (ally path) | True identity revealed as an invitation; negotiates as an equal; his offer (Eye for Lords' Alliance membership for Luskan) is genuine and he'll honor it; he's charming because it's easier than not being charming, not because he's performing |
| 3B | The Patron | Arc H onward, ally | Asset rather than adversary; provides intelligence, drow operatives, the Faire as a safe house; the political favor comes due at Arc J; he'll remind them once, gracefully, that what he wants hasn't changed |

*Trigger to Phase 3B:* PCs choose alliance, accept the deal, or formally join Bregan D'aerthe.

**Relationships**

**Goals**

1. **Lords' Alliance Membership for Luskan** — the real prize; Bregan D'aerthe's Luskan operations need political legitimacy; method: trade intelligence and an Eye to the PCs or directly to the Open Lord; contingency: if the avenue closes, Bregan D'aerthe continues without it — Jarlaxle loses a round, not the game; connects to Arc J political aftermath
2. **One Eye of the Stone** — as a bargaining chip, not for personal use; method: Nar'l's intelligence tells him where Eyes are; move to acquire before the other factions consolidate; contingency: negotiating it from the PCs is also acceptable and preferable for the relationship; connects to Arc H
3. **Waterdeep as Permanent Base** — the Sea Maidens Faire is seasonal; Jarlaxle wants Bregan D'aerthe with a permanent Dock Ward presence; method: be indispensable to whoever resolves the vault situation; this is the long play that outlasts Dragon Heist

**Key design note:** Jarlaxle is the only villain whose faction (Bregan D'aerthe) the PCs can join. This must be written directly into the campaign structure, not treated as an edge case. A party that joins Bregan D'aerthe inverts Arc H — the Faire becomes their base, and Jarlaxle sends them after another faction's Eye.

## 3. Key Decisions Before Writing

1. **Villain chapter as reference vs. character document:** The four villain sections here are structural frameworks. Full CoS:R-style prose character documents (extended personality phase guidance, full relationship paragraphs, complete goal write-ups) should be written as separate documents per villain and cross-referenced here. Decide before drafting whether those full documents are needed before Arc A, or can be written arc-by-arc.

2. **Neverember's framing in the History:** The History section must not cast Neverember as a simple villain. The vault's moral weight depends on the gold's ambiguity — stolen from the city by a man who convinced himself he deserved it, who also genuinely improved the city. A simplistic reading undercuts Arc J.

3. **When the children appear:** Terenzio and Elzerina Cassalanter must appear in the campaign before Arc G. The DM should identify during Arc B exactly when and where this introduction happens. Write it into the Arc B notes, not left as a vague "sometime before the villa." Once PCs have met the children, everything changes.

4. **Jarlaxle's conditional entry backup:** If the group is unlikely to follow the nimblewright investigation in Arc C, the DM should create a second clue path to the Sea Maidens Faire. The conditional should not become a missed arc because of one failed investigation thread. The Three Clue Rule applies here as it does everywhere.

5. **The simulacrum reveal timing:** Players need to learn Manshoon has a simulacrum during Arc I (the Kolat Towers raid), not before. Pre-reveal deflates the confrontation; post-reveal feels like a cheat. The discovery should happen during the fight itself.

6. **Xanathar's goldfish:** The DM should establish Sylgar early — Arc A or Arc B, through NPC gossip, a Watch report about a Guild response to a perceived insult involving a fish, anything that makes the detail feel like world texture before it becomes plot. By Arc F, it should be a known fact with dramatic weight.
