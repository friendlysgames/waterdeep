# Cassalanter Villa

*An adventure for 4th- to 6th-level characters.*

---

## Quest Opener

Three years before the Trollskull fireball, Victoro and Ammalia Cassalanter signed a contract with Asmodeus. The terms were specific: Osvaldo, their eldest, age fifteen — taken immediately, transformed into a chain devil, bound. The twins, Terenzio and Elzerina — souls forfeit on their ninth birthday, one tenday after Founders' Day. The buy-out clause: 999,999 gold pieces and the sacrifice of ninety-nine souls, delivered before the twins' birthday. The Cassalanters had traded their children for time they could not afford and power they could not refuse.

The desperation began with Dagult Neverember. His embezzlement from the city treasury gutted the Cassalanter banking operations — loans underwritten against civic funds that no longer existed. Victoro's investments collapsed in sequence. The family's reputation held because reputation lags reality, but the ledgers told a different story. Ammalia found the path through infernal research: a patron who could restore their fortune and guarantee their children's futures. The contract arrived through a pit fiend intermediary. They signed it together, in the temple beneath their own villa, on a night when Osvaldo was sleeping two floors above.

Osvaldo's transformation was immediate. The boy screamed for three hours before the change completed. Victoro sealed the attic with abjuration wards, soundproofed the floor, and has not entered the room since. Ammalia reads to her son nightly through the warded door — children's stories he loved before the contract, spoken to a chain devil who weeps black ichor and claws at the walls. Two imps in spider form watch the corridor. The household staff knows the attic is forbidden. Only Willifort Crowelle — the doppelganger butler who serves Asmodeus directly — knows what is behind the door.

Eye #2 came to the Cassalanters through infernal divination. A commune ritual performed in the temple — Asmodeus answering through a pit fiend proxy — identified the Eye's resting place: the crypt of Lady Alethea Brandath, Renaer Neverember's mother, interred in the Brandath family mausoleum beneath the City of the Dead. Victoro dispatched Seffia Naelryke's team to extract it. The Eye now rests behind the base of the Asmodeus statue in A7, the ceremonial hall of the temple beneath the villa — warded by infernal magic, guarded by the cult's bearded devils, secured in the one place the Cassalanters control absolutely.

The villa above is Waterdeep's most admired household. Ammalia's charity in the Field Ward feeds hundreds. Victoro's banking operations underwrite half the guilds in the city. The children attend social functions, well-dressed and well-mannered, accompanied by their caretaker Laiba Rosse. The butterfly garden hosts dueling nobles and garden parties. The ballroom floor is a Siamorphe mosaic, and the Cassalanters maintain an ancestral devotion to the goddess of nobility — publicly. Beneath that floor, down through the wine cellar and a secret door marked with a sun symbol, a spiral stair descends thirty feet to a temple where midnight-every-tenday gatherings see crimson-robed cultists in golden masks kneeling before a thirty-foot statue of Asmodeus carved from what was once the image of Siamorphe herself.

Founders' Day falls on the first of Flamerule. Ammalia has prepared ten vials of midnight tears poison, stored in her study beside the grimoire that contains the commune ritual. The plan: invite one hundred of Waterdeep's poorest to a charitable feast on the villa's lawn pavilions. The food is genuine. The poison is administered through the wine — ninety-nine cups, one untouched, satisfying the contract's "one shy of a hundred" requirement. The gold from the Vault of Dragons — all five hundred thousand pieces — provides the financial component. Together, gold and souls buy the twins' freedom before their birthday.

The household holds six souls who know what lies beneath. Willifort Crowelle, doppelganger, serves Asmodeus with genuine devotion — the family is secondary. Tissina Khyret, Ammalia's attendant, is a cult fanatic who joined out of loyalty to Ammalia personally — she does not know the contract's full terms. Laiba "Nana" Rosse, the twins' caretaker, is a cult fanatic who joined the cult to remain close to the children — she would betray the Cassalanters to save Terenzio and Elzerina. Jandar Chergoba, head chef, is a cult fanatic bound by debt — Victoro holds a financial obligation over him that converted into cult service. Seffia Naelryke commands the external operations team and knows the cult's military posture. Arn Xalrondar, her lieutenant, handles field security for the outposts and has never set foot in the temple.

## Scene 1: The Lair Intelligence Briefing

**Purpose.** Translate **Faction Outposts** outpost intelligence and prior Cassalanter interactions into operational knowledge of the villa. Each faction contact provides villa-specific intelligence calibrated to what the party discovered in earlier quests. Unlike **Xanathar's Lair**'s dungeon-focused briefing, this briefing is social and political — the target looks normal from the outside.

**Content.**

*Running This Scene.* Each contact below is available only to party members of that faction. A party with no Harper has no channel to Mirt; a party with no Force Grey member has no Vajra briefing. The scene's intelligence yield depends entirely on which factions the party joined during **Trollskull Alley** and **Fireball!**. This is by design.

The intelligence here is shallower than **Xanathar's Lair**'s — no faction has an embedded agent inside the villa, and the Cassalanters' social position makes direct surveillance difficult. What the contacts provide is context, suspicion, and operational windows.

*Mirt (Harpers — renown 1+).* Mirt has attended Cassalanter social events for years. He knows the villa's public layout from personal observation — the entrance hall, the ballroom, the garden, the dining rooms. He has always suspected hidden areas: "No noble family needs that much cellar space. I've been in that wine cellar — it's too small for a house that size. The stonework on the west wall doesn't match." If the Asmodean Shrine outpost was hit in **Faction Outposts**, Mirt can confirm the cult connection and warn that Cassalanter security will have tightened. He lacks hard evidence sufficient for the Masked Lords — he needs proof from inside the villa. He says this directly: "I can suspect all I like. The Lords need something I can put on a table." At renown 10+, Mirt arranges a Harper operative — use the spy stat block — to attend the charity ball as a genuine social guest, providing a real-time outside observer with a paper bird: one communication per hour to the party inside the villa.

*Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance — renown 1+).* The City Watch cannot touch the Cassalanters without evidence — they are too well-connected. Jalester provides the guest list for upcoming Cassalanter social events, including the Waukeentide charity ball: names, affiliations, and which guests have standing invitations. If the party has evidence from **Faction Outposts**, Jalester can arrange Watch patrols in the Sea Ward to be "assigned elsewhere" during a specific window. He frames this as plausible deniability: "I can give you a gap. What you do with it is between you and the city." At renown 10+, Jalester pulls Watch files on the villa's recent contractor logs and delivery records — a week of staff patterns that refines the guild blueprint entry method and confirms which service entrances each contractor firm uses.

*Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave — renown 1+).* The butterfly garden at the rear of the villa sustains exotic species that should not survive Waterdeep's climate — tropical specimens that require sustained magical heat and humidity. Melannor suspects magical maintenance but has not been able to inspect the garden directly. He does not know the butterflies share space with six imps in raven form. His assessment: "Something in that garden is not natural. I don't mean the butterflies — I mean whatever is keeping them alive." At renown 10+, Jeryth Phaulkon casts pass without trace on the party before they enter the villa grounds — all DCs to detect movement through the garden or at the A9 stream entrance increase by 10 for the first hour.

*Savra Belabranta (Order of the Gauntlet — renown 1+).* The Gauntlet has suspected Cassalanter piety for years. Savra reports a secondhand account from a servant who left Cassalanter employ: screaming from above the top floor, muffled but persistent, always at night. The servant assumed rats. Savra is not convinced. If the party has evidence of Asmodean worship from **Faction Outposts**, Savra frames the temple as a holy obligation — the Order exists to destroy fiendish influence. She provides a Siamorphe holy symbol, consecrated by the Order's chaplain, and explains its significance: "Siamorphe was the Cassalanters' ancestral patron. If anything of the old faith survives in that house, this will find it." The holy symbol is the key to pacifying Caladorn's ghost in A4. At renown 10+, Savra personally attends the charity ball — Gauntlet formal dress over armor — and can suppress suspicion about one incident during the Social Event adversary roster: guests expect a knight of the Order to respond to disturbances, and her presence buys the party one improvised intervention without immediate social consequence.

*Vajra Safahr (Force Grey — renown 1+).* Arcane assessment of the villa's defenses from external scrying. Vajra detects residual conjuration and abjuration signatures consistent with devil-binding and warding circles — concentrated beneath the villa's foundation, not within the living spaces. She identifies the helmed horror in C6 as a construct guardian: "Something guards that office. It is not alive, but it is very attentive." She warns against dispelling wards carelessly: "Abjuration circles contain things. If you break a circle without knowing what it contains, you may have two problems instead of one." This foreshadows Osvaldo in C24. At renown 10+, Vajra casts one preparatory spell on the party before entry — detect thoughts countermeasures (via mind blank, if the party has a member she can target) or nondetection — and deploys a Force Grey mage outside the villa grounds as arcane backup; a bright flash from any window signals the mage to enter through the garden and provide two combat rounds of support before extracting.

*Tashlyn Yafeera or Davil Starsong (Doom Raiders — renown 1+).* The Cassalanters hold banking leverage over half the guilds in Waterdeep. Tashlyn or Davil can provide intelligence on the household's operations: servant rotation schedules, delivery windows, and which guild contractors have regular access to the villa grounds. The Cellarers' and Plumbers' Guild has records of the villa's basement construction — partial blueprints available through a Doom Raider contact. These blueprints show the wine cellar (C28), the false vault (C27), and the original underground construction that predates the villa. A hatch between C22 and C23 appears on the old plans — it was not originally concealed. The blueprints do not show C29 (later construction) or the temple interior. At renown 10+, Yagra Stonefist drives a Black Network carriage and serves as lookout outside the villa during the operation — maintaining contact through a code system (a kerchief in a window) and ready to run an immediate extraction through the Sea Ward's back streets.

*Jarlaxle (Bregan D'aerthe — renown 1+).* Available only if a PC is a BD member. Jarlaxle knows the Cassalanters worship Asmodeus — he considers them "the most dangerous faction in the Grand Game because they have something none of the others have: a deadline." At renown 3+, he reveals the Founders' Day sacrifice plan in broad strokes: "They intend to buy their children's souls with other people's lives. One hundred invitations. Ninety-nine cups of wine. A calculation dressed as charity." He does not reveal that Bregan D'aerthe has its own Cassalanter intelligence operation running — imp counter-surveillance from the Sea Maidens Faire. He does not mention "Vessa."

**Tone.** Measured and ominous. The target looks normal. The intelligence is social and political — not architectural. The party is casing a household, not a dungeon. The walls are painted, the food is excellent, and the hosts are charming. The faction contacts can sense something wrong without being able to prove it.

## Scene 2: Casing the Target

**Purpose.** Steps 1 through 3 of the heist framework: Identify the Score, Gather Information, Onsite Surveillance. The social-infiltration genre means casing happens through attendance at events and discreet inquiry, not through tunnel scouting. This scene establishes three entry methods, maps the social surveillance challenge, and forces the party to decide their approach to a target that is watching them back.

**Content.**

*Identifying the Score.* Eye #2 is in the villa's hidden temple. The Stone's impression — if 1+ Eyes are restored — sharpens to "a noble villa in the Sea Ward, hidden chambers beneath painted floors, the Cassalanter house mark on a sigil carved in stone." **Faction Outposts** documents converge on the same target: Seffia's letter from the Converted Windmill confirming Eye #2 was found "in the crypt of Lady Brandath," the Windmill's Brandath Crypt map, the Asmodean Shrine's Nine Hells mural depicting a noble family kneeling before Asmodeus. Three independent lines of evidence point at Cassalanter Villa.

*Three Entry Methods.*

**Entry 1 — The Social Invitation.** If the party accepted Ammalia's dinner invitation in **Fireball!** Scene 5 and maintained a cooperative relationship, they have ongoing social access. The Cassalanters invite them to Waukeentide events as guests. Advantage: legitimate presence in the villa's public areas, ability to observe servant patterns and architecture during visits, social trust from the hosts. Disadvantage: the Cassalanters are watching. Willifort uses detect thoughts on every visitor. The party's cover depends on controlling their surface thoughts — a constraint that intensifies with every visit.

**Entry 2 — Guild Blueprints and Service Access.** Construction records from the Cellarers', Stonecutters', Carpenters', and Surveyors' guilds provide partial villa floor plans. The Surveyors' Guild has an old site map showing underground construction predating the villa — the temple's original footprint. Advantage: advance knowledge of layout, including the false vault trap at C27 and the hatch between C22 and C23. A Doom Raider contact can arrange guild contractor uniforms — delivery crew, maintenance staff, or chandlers — that grant access to the villa's service areas without a social invitation. Disadvantage: blueprints do not show C29 (later construction) or the temple interior. Contractor cover expires when the real crew arrives — typically a 2-hour window.

**Entry 3 — The Stream Entrance (A9).** The temple has a water exit: a stream and pool at A9 that connects to the Mud Flats northwest of Waterdeep. The approach is a 400-foot swim through an underground waterway, emerging in the temple's lowest level. Advantage: bypasses the entire villa, entering the temple directly. No social cover needed. Disadvantage: the party arrives at the temple's deepest point with no intelligence about what lies between A9 and the Eye. The approach is wet, cold, and loud — echoes carry. The temple's occupants hear anyone emerging from the pool unless the party succeeds on a DC 15 Group Dexterity (Stealth) check.

At least one of these three paths is available to any party regardless of prior quest results. The guild blueprint path does not require **Fireball!** dinner attendance or Doom Raider contacts — any party can approach the guilds directly, though the information takes 2–3 days to acquire without contacts to expedite it.

*Social Event Schedule.*

Waukeentide (Tarsakh 1–10) provides the primary casing and operation windows:

**Caravance (Tarsakh 1).** Gift-giving holiday. The Cassalanters give most staff the night off to celebrate privately with their children. A skeleton crew remains: perimeter guards, Willifort, imp watchers on Osvaldo's door. The primary heist window: minimal staff, the Cassalanters emotionally focused on the twins. After the children sleep, cultists arrive for a midnight gathering in the temple — the Cult Gathering adversary roster applies from midnight to dawn.

**Goldenight (Tarsakh 5).** Businesses open all night, gold-themed celebrations across the city. The Cassalanters host a gala at the villa — 40–60 guests circulating through the entrance hall, ballroom, dining rooms, and butterfly garden. Surveillance opportunity: the crowd provides cover for movement and observation. The Social Event adversary roster applies.

**The Waukeentide Charity Ball (Tarsakh 7).** The Cassalanters host a fundraiser for Field Ward reconstruction. This is the primary social-infiltration entry point. The party can attend as guests (if invited through **Fireball!**), infiltrate as servants (guild contractor uniforms from Doom Raider contacts, DC 14 Charisma (Deception) to maintain cover under staff questioning), or arrive as uninvited guests (DC 16 Charisma (Deception) at the door, advantage if the party has a plausible noble cover story). The ball fills the villa's public spaces and provides legitimate cover for being inside the house. The Social Event adversary roster applies.

**Founders' Day (Flamerule 1).** Not a heist window. This is the deadline. The poisoning feast occupies the villa's lawn pavilions. One hundred guests — the city's poorest, invited through Ammalia's Field Ward charity. Jandar prepares the food outside. Ninety-nine cups of wine contain midnight tears. The feast begins at noon. Midnight tears takes effect 24 hours after ingestion — the guests die the following day, scattered across the city, with no visible connection to the Cassalanters. If the party has not acted by this date, the sacrifice occurs.

*Willifort's Surveillance.* The villa's defining social challenge. Willifort Crowelle is a doppelganger who has served the Cassalanter household for eight years. He greets every visitor at C1, offers drinks, positions himself outside doors, and uses detect thoughts continuously on anyone inside the villa. He reads surface thoughts automatically; deeper probing requires his action and a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw from the target.

The party must address Willifort or accept that their surface thoughts are being monitored. Options: (a) consciously control surface thoughts — each party member declares what they are thinking about when Willifort is nearby; a DC 13 Charisma (Deception) check maintains the facade per interaction; (b) identify Willifort as a threat and neutralize him — risky, because his absence triggers an immediate household response; Ammalia checks on him within 15 minutes; (c) feed him false information deliberately — a DC 16 Charisma (Deception) check plants a specific misleading thought; Willifort reports what he reads without questioning it.

Characters who are immune to charm or have the ability to shield their thoughts (such as through a ring of mind shielding) bypass Willifort's surveillance entirely. He notices the blank spot but cannot force a reading.

*The Imp Network.* Six imps in raven form roost in the butterfly garden (C25). They observe all external approaches, track movement around the villa's perimeter, and follow visitors who linger near restricted areas. Detect magic reveals their fiendish nature (DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) to notice the eyes tracking with unnatural precision). The imps report to Ammalia through a one-way telepathic bond — she receives impressions, not words. Killing an imp alerts Ammalia immediately (the bond severs with a psychic snap). The party must account for aerial surveillance when planning any external approach or garden-adjacent movement.

*Questioning Staff.* The Cassalanter household employs sixteen named staff. Ten know nothing about the temple. Six are cult members with varying degrees of commitment.

**Cult Staff:**

- **Willifort Crowelle** (doppelganger, butler) — serves Asmodeus directly. True believer. Will not break under any social pressure; he would die before revealing the temple. If the Cassalanters are exposed as cultists, Willifort drops his tiefling disguise and fights.
- **Tissina Khyret** (cult fanatic, Ammalia's attendant) — devoted to Ammalia personally. Joined because Ammalia asked her to. Does not fully understand the contract's terms — she knows about the temple, attends gatherings, believes the worship is a family spiritual practice. If she learns the children's souls are forfeit, she breaks. DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) with evidence of the contract turns her.
- **Laiba "Nana" Rosse** (cult fanatic, the twins' caretaker) — loves Terenzio and Elzerina fiercely. Joined the cult to stay close to the children when the Cassalanters began restricting household access. She knows the gatherings happen, has seen the robes. She does not know about Osvaldo or the Founders' Day plan. If she learns the children are in danger — from any source — she turns immediately. No check required. She provides: the twins' daily routine, which rooms they frequent, which doors are always locked, and that "something terrible" lives in the attic.
- **Jandar Chergoba** (cult fanatic, head chef) — bound by debt. Victoro holds a 3,000 gp obligation over him from a failed restaurant venture. The debt converted into cult service three years ago. Jandar attends gatherings, prepares ritual meals, and commands the kitchen's ten animated knives (flying sword statistics). He hates what he does. DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) with an offer of protection — or DC 10 if the party can resolve the debt — turns him. He provides: kitchen schedules, which deliveries access the cellar, and the location of the wine cellar's false wall.
- **Seffia Naelryke** (cult operative, external operations) — commands the Cassalanter response team. Present in the villa only during cult gatherings or when summoned. True believer in Asmodean hierarchy. Will not turn.
- **Arn Xalrondar** (cult operative, field security) — handles outpost logistics. Has never entered the temple. Follows Seffia's orders. Pragmatic, not devout — if the operation is clearly failing, he surrenders and cooperates for clemency.

**Non-Cult Staff:**

- **Corvo** (footman, human male, 40s) — manages the entrance hall and guest coats. Knows the first three floors well. Has noticed that guests are sometimes escorted to the wine cellar and do not return the same night. Attributes this to a private wine-tasting club. Loyal to the family for the wage. If told the truth: horrified, cooperates fully, provides household schedules.
- **Pela** (laundress, halfling female, 50s) — works in the service wing. Has found crimson-stained fabric in the household laundry — she assumed it was wine. Knows that Ammalia's study (C22) is always locked and that the key never leaves Ammalia's person. If told the truth: terrified, will not actively help but will not impede the party.
- **Marchello** (gardener, human male, 30s) — maintains the butterfly garden. Knows the ravens (imps) are unnaturally attentive — "They watch you. Regular birds don't watch you." Has not connected this to anything supernatural. Tends the garden at dawn and dusk. If told the truth: angry, specifically at Ammalia ("She told me those were rescue birds from the docks").
- **Fiorella** (cook's assistant, human female, 20s) — works under Jandar. Has noticed Jandar's anxiety before specific nights and that he prepares food for "private events" she is never invited to attend. Knows where the cellar keys are kept (C10 pantry hook). If told the truth: frightened but resolute — wants the children safe.
- **Doro** (stable hand, dwarf male, 60s) — manages the carriage house at the villa's east wall. Has heard screaming from the attic on two occasions. Told Corvo, who told him to forget it. Knows the villa's exterior layout, including a service gate at the garden's south wall that is propped open during deliveries. If told the truth: grim acceptance — "I knew something was wrong in that house."
- **Lissette** (upstairs maid, human female, 30s) — cleans the family's private rooms on the second and third floors. Knows that Ammalia cries after visiting the top floor — she has heard her through the door. Has never been to the attic. Knows that C6 is sealed by more than a lock — she has seen the helmed horror through a crack when Victoro left the door ajar. If told the truth: conflicted loyalty to the children; provides the second-floor key rotation.
- **Rennet** (scullion, halfling male, teens) — kitchen cleanup, lowest-ranking household member. Knows nothing of substance. Willing to let strangers through the kitchen door for 5 GP and a story about a girl.
- **Maesta** (housekeeper, human female, 50s) — manages the non-cult staff. Professional, discreet, loyal to the household's reputation rather than to the Cassalanters personally. Knows the staff schedules, which rooms are restricted, and that Willifort is "more than he appears" — she has seen him change the shape of his hands when he thought no one was looking. If told the truth: her first concern is the staff's safety, her second is the children, her third is whether the party can keep this quiet.
- **Tomas** (night watchman, human male, 40s) — patrols the villa's exterior from sunset to dawn. Professional, ex-Watch, hired for his competence. Knows the patrol routes and blind spots. Has noticed that on certain nights, people in dark robes arrive through the garden gate between midnight and one — he was told these are private religious observances and instructed not to interfere. If told the truth: furious. Provides patrol routes, blind spots, and the garden gate schedule.
- **Bressa** (nursemaid, human female, 20s) — assists Nana Rosse with the twins during daytime hours. Knows the children's schedule, favorite hiding places in the villa, and that Terenzio has nightmares about "the thing upstairs." If told the truth: panics, then steadies — the children come first.

A 10% chance applies to any individual non-cult servant being a secret cult member who reports the questioning to the Cassalanters. This has been resolved: none of the named non-cult staff are secret cult members. The 10% risk manifests differently — if the party questions three or more non-cult staff in the same day, word reaches Maesta, who mentions it to Willifort as a professional concern ("Guests are asking unusual questions about the household"), advancing the Social Exposure System by one stage.

**Tone.** Social tension beneath a gilded surface. The party is casing a home — the walls are painted, the food is excellent, and the hosts are charming. What the Cassalanters have built sits thirty feet below. The staff are people with lives and loyalties, and each one knows a piece of the picture without seeing the whole.

## Scene 3: Preparation

**Purpose.** Step 4 of the heist framework. The party finalizes their plan, acquires tools and allies, and confronts the moral dimension of what they are about to do. The decisions made here shape Scene 4 and determine which of the four resolution paths the party is heading toward.

**Content.**

*Potential Allies Inside the Villa.*

**The Twins (Terenzio and Elzerina).** Eight years old, bright, restless, and oblivious to their parents' secret. They love exploring the villa and know every room that is not locked — and several that are. Terenzio has found a way to peek into C6 through a gap in the door's lower hinge ("Father gets very cross"). Elzerina knows the wine cellar has "a funny door behind a sun" — the Siamorphe symbol concealing the passage to C29. Neither child has been to the attic or the temple.

Using the children as guides is mechanically powerful. They know the layout. Servants do not question their movements. A child leading a "new friend" through the house bypasses most social exposure checks. The cost is moral: the party is exploiting children whose souls are forfeit. The children do not know this. They think they are showing their new friends a secret. The DM should not soften this — the discomfort is the design.

**"Vessa" (Lymeria Lhaurilstar).** During social events, a doppelganger posing as the noblewoman "Vessa" circulates in C15 (the banquet hall). She is a Bregan D'aerthe operative planted to monitor the Cassalanters. She knows the villa's public layout, the approximate location of the cult's gathering space ("below the wine cellar"), and that the Cassalanters have something the Grand Game factions want. If the party has BD renown 1+ (Initiate rank — any BD member), she reveals herself on approach — a brief flash of her true features, a knowing look. Otherwise, she is a mysterious ally who seems to know too much and asks pointed questions about why the party is really here. She provides: distraction (a fainting spell, a spilled drink, a loud argument with another guest), cover stories ("They're with me — the Lhauri family's representatives"), and passage through the banquet areas without social exposure checks. She will not enter the temple.

**Caladorn Cassalanter's Ghost (A4/A4a).** In the family crypt beneath the temple, the ghost of Victoro's father — a former Masked Lord, a hero of the North, and a devoted servant of Siamorphe — is bound with three specters (former Cassalanter ancestors). Caladorn's ghost attacks any creature that bears the taint of fiendish worship — the Cassalanters cannot reach him. They reshaped his family crypt's goddess-statue into Asmodeus using stone shape. His ghost has been raging in silence for three years, unable to leave A4a, unable to stop what his family has done to his legacy.

If a character presents a Siamorphe holy symbol (available from Savra in Scene 1), the specters in A4 stand down. Caladorn's ghost manifests in A4a — an armored figure with the Cassalanter features, silver-haired, carrying the family sword. He is lucid — steady voice, measured words, until he mentions his family. Then the voice cracks.

Caladorn provides: a +1 plate armor (his own, stored in his sarcophagus) and a mace of disruption (consecrated to Siamorphe, the weapon he carried as a Masked Lord). He asks for a vow: "Use the mace to destroy the effigy of evil incarnate. End the corruption of my house. Restore my family's honor." The mace is the key to destroying the Asmodeus statue in A7.

Caladorn does not know the contract's specific terms. He knows his family has turned to Asmodeus. He knows the statue in A7 was his wife's image of Siamorphe before they defaced it. He does not know about the children's souls or the Founders' Day plan. If told, his anguish deepens — but he does not waver. The mace is the answer. The statue must fall.

*The Moral Dimension — Four Resolution Paths.*

The party's preparation is shaped by what they intend to do when they find the truth. Four paths exist, each with real costs. The text presents them without advocacy.

**Path 1 — Help the Cassalanters Find the Gold (Cooperative).** The buy-out clause requires 999,999 gold pieces and ninety-nine souls. The Cassalanters have approximately 500,000 gold from their own fortune. They need the Vault of Dragons' 500,000 GP to complete the financial component. If the party discovers the full truth — the contract, the children, the deadline — and approaches the Cassalanters with this knowledge, the Cassalanters are open to cooperation. They are desperate parents, not ideological devil-worshippers. Victoro drops the social mask. Ammalia weeps. They explain the terms and insist the sacrifice is the only way — but a DC 12 Wisdom (Insight) check reveals they would accept any alternative that saves their children. The party can propose: help get the Vault gold in exchange for the Eye and a cessation of cult activities. The Cassalanters agree without hesitation. Cost: the entire Vault goes to Asmodeus. The party receives no gold from Neverember's hoard. And the ninety-nine — the Cassalanters will still attempt the feast unless the party explicitly addresses this. The sacrifice clause is separate from the gold clause.

**Path 2 — Destroy the Infernal Contract (Mace of Disruption).** The Cassalanters want this — they have searched for years and found nothing. The answer is in their own temple: the contract is bound to the Asmodeus statue in A7. Caladorn Cassalanter's ghost in A4a has the weapon that can do it — the mace of disruption. Striking the statue with the mace cracks it, shatters the sacrificial fire bowl (2d10 psychic damage to witnessing cultists), severs the contract, and releases Eye #2 from behind the statue's base. The children's souls are freed without the gold payment or the ninety-nine sacrifices. Osvaldo's chain devil form dissolves — he dies, but his soul is freed.

The Cassalanters cannot access this path. Caladorn's ghost attacks devil-worshippers on sight — anyone who has participated in Asmodean rituals triggers his hostility. The Cassalanters have tried to reach the crypt twice and retreated both times. Only outsiders bearing the Siamorphe holy symbol can reach Caladorn and learn the method.

Cost: Asmodeus considers the contract's destruction a breach. Infernal retribution follows — a bearded devil dispatched within days, escalating to a chain devil within weeks. These are seeds for Undermountain Level 18 (Vanrakdoom). The Cassalanters lose their infernal patron — they consider this a liberation, not a loss.

**Path 3 — Let the Sacrifice Happen.** The party does not intervene before Founders' Day. Ninety-nine die. The children's souls are saved by the completed ritual. This is a valid dark ending. The Cassalanters succeed. The party lives with what they allowed. Cost: moral weight. Political fallout if the party's knowledge of the plan becomes known. The memory of a choice that was within their power to prevent.

**Path 4 — Expose the Cassalanters.** Deliver evidence of the Asmodean cult to the Masked Lords through Mirt, Vajra, or Jalester. The Cassalanters are arrested. The cult is disbanded. The children's souls are forfeit — Asmodeus claims them on their birthday. They become lemures. Cost: the children die. The legally correct action produces the worst outcome for the only true innocents. The Cassalanters, from prison or exile, vow to destroy those who thwarted them.

*The Cooperative Discovery Path.* The plan must ensure that parties who investigate thoroughly can discover the full truth and reach a peaceful negotiation. The clue trail requires any two of the following:

- **Osvaldo in C24** — seeing a chain devil imprisoned in a warded attic room, weeping and clawing walls, establishes that the Cassalanters have an infernal connection that cost them a child.
- **Ammalia's grimoire in C22** — contains the commune ritual, the contract's terms, and Ammalia's marginal notes ("There must be another way. There must be.").
- **Victoro's journal in C4** — entries spanning three years: "Neverember's dragons are an answer to our prayers." "The Cassalanter mausoleum. Inform Y.G." (Yalah Gralhund). "Golorr is an aboleth. Now it all makes sense."
- **Nana Rosse (if turned)** — confirms the children's predicament, the deadline, and that the Cassalanters are "doing something terrible because they believe it's the only way."

When the party approaches the Cassalanters with knowledge of the pact, the confrontation unfolds:

Victoro and Ammalia do not fight. They break. Ammalia weeps. Victoro drops the social mask entirely — the polished banker vanishes and a frightened father remains. They explain the full terms: 999,999 GP and 99 souls. They insist the sacrifice is the only way. If the party asks "What if we destroy the contract?" — the Cassalanters are stunned. They have searched for years and found nothing. If the party has already discovered the mace from Caladorn's ghost, the Cassalanters are overwhelmed — the solution was beneath their own villa, in a crypt they desecrated, guarded by a ghost who will not speak to them. If the party has not yet found the mace, the Cassalanters beg them to search the temple: "There must be something down there we missed. We can never speak to Caladorn's spirit — he attacks us on sight." If the party asks about the ninety-nine, the Cassalanters go quiet. Ammalia looks away. Victoro says: "We have tried everything else."

*Variable Difficulty.*

| Tier | State | Effect on Villa |
|------|-------|----------------|
| Unaware | No Cassalanter outposts hit | Standard roster. Staff relaxed. Willifort uses detect thoughts casually. Social entry straightforward. |
| Suspicious | Converted Windmill hit | Imp surveillance doubled (12 imps, not 6). Willifort scans every visitor aggressively. 1 additional guard patrol in the cellar. Social events proceed but Willifort is more visible. |
| Alert | Both outposts hit or cross-faction spillover | Guards doubled. No social events hosted at the villa — the charity ball is cancelled, the Goldenight gala moved to a rented hall. The party must create their own entry method. Cultists armed during temple gatherings. Imps patrol the perimeter in visible raven form. |
| Lockdown | Spillover + both outpost hits + adversarial relationship | Villa closed to visitors. Seffia's response team stationed inside the villa. A bearded devil patrols the temple. Entry only through A9 (stream) or violent breach. The social infiltration is no longer possible — this quest's genre shifts to dungeon assault. |

**Tone.** Planning that carries moral weight. The party is deciding what they will do when they find the truth — and whether they can live with what they find.

## Scene 4: The Operation

**Purpose.** The heist itself. Step 5 of the framework. Full keyed-area treatment for seventeen gameplay-significant areas of the villa's thirty-eight total (twenty-nine villa rooms, nine temple areas). Two-layer structure: the villa above is social infiltration; the temple below is dungeon. The genre shifts at the staircase.

**Content.**

*Villa Layer — Critical Path Areas.*

**C1 — Entrance Hall.** Two-story foyer with marble floors and a grand staircase. Portraits of Cassalanter ancestors line the walls — Caladorn is prominently placed, silver-haired and armored. Willifort greets every visitor here, offering to take coats and provide refreshments. During social events: a harpsichord plays in the corner, chatting guests fill the space, and Willifort circulates with a tray. The party's social exposure stage begins here — Willifort's detect thoughts is active from the moment they enter.

**C2 — Garden Mudroom.** Service entrance connecting the butterfly garden to the villa's interior. Garden tools, muddy boots, hanging cloaks. During social events: Esvele Rosznar stages here. She arrives as a guest of the Rosznar family, excuses herself from the party, and changes into the Black Viper's mask and leathers behind a rack of hanging coats. A party member who enters C2 during an event catches her mid-change — she freezes, one glove on. DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) to recruit her as a temporary ally; she is defensive but not hostile. This is the first encounter with the parallel heist.

**C4 — Reading Room.** Ground floor, east wing. A reading room with leather chairs and a black dragon hunting trophy mounted on the wall. Victoro's private journal is on a side table — he reads it here and sometimes forgets to take it with him. The journal contains three entries relevant to the investigation: "Neverember's dragons are an answer to our prayers" (confirming the gold is the buy-out). "The Cassalanter mausoleum. Inform Y.G." (Yalah Gralhund — cross-references the **Gralhund Villa** connection). "Golorr is an aboleth. Now it all makes sense" (confirming Victoro understands the Stone's nature).

**C6 — Victoro's Office.** Second floor, locked (DC 17 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) or Victoro's key). A helmed horror stands in the corner — motionless until a creature other than Victoro or Ammalia touches the desk, at which point it attacks without warning. The desk contains: thirteen loan contracts (Cassalanter banking leverage over Waterdeep's guilds), a hidden compartment (DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation)) holding a gold Asmodeus holy symbol worth 250 GP, a report from Seffia Naelryke on outpost operations (cross-reference to **Faction Outposts** Converted Windmill), and the Cassalanter Report on the Grand Game. The Report on the Grand Game is the Three Clue Rule path to **Kolat Towers** — it references Manshoon's operations, identifies Kolat Towers by name and location, describes Zhentarim splinter movements, and notes that "the wizard who calls himself Manshoon guards his fortress with a force field that admits only those he has keyed."

**C10 — Kitchen.** Ground floor, west wing. Jandar Chergoba commands the kitchen with six commoner assistants and a rack of knives he can animate on command ("For'zaal" — ten flying swords). The cellar key ring hangs on a hook in the pantry. During Founders' Day: Jandar is outside on the lawn, preparing the feast. The kitchen is empty except for Fiorella, who has been told to stay inside.

**C13 — Ballroom.** The villa's centerpiece. A Siamorphe mosaic floor — the goddess's sun symbol rendered in gold and white tile. A nimblewright-statistic dancing mannequin stands in an alcove (decorative during non-event times; during social events, it dances with guests who lack partners). The ballroom is the primary social-infiltration space during events: masked dancing, murmured conversations, wine service. Social exposure checks occur here at advantage — the crowd provides cover.

**C22 — Ammalia's Study.** Third floor, locked (DC 18 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) or Ammalia's key). A red leather grimoire sits on a lectern — the commune ritual to contact Asmodeus (who responds through a pit fiend proxy), the contract's terms transcribed in Ammalia's handwriting, and marginal notes in increasing desperation. The desk holds Ammalia's spellbook and ten vials of midnight tears poison. A trapdoor in the floor opens to C23 (a closet leading to C24, the attic). The Cassalanter Report on the Grand Game is also here per the Alexandrian overlay — a second placement of the **Kolat Towers** clue document, ensuring that parties who reach C22 but not C6 still find the path to Kolat Towers.

**C24 — Osvaldo's Prison.** The attic. Soundproofed. Accessible via C23 from C22, or via a locked exterior door on the attic level (DC 19 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools)). Inside: a burned pentagram on the floor, abjuration wards glowing faintly, and Osvaldo — a chain devil who was once a fifteen-year-old boy. He is chained to an iron ring in the floor. Two imps in spider form cling to the rafters.

Osvaldo is insane. He alternates between the chain devil's combat instincts and fragments of the child he was — calling for his mother, asking why it hurts, then lashing with his chains at anything that moves. He cannot be reasoned with. If the party dispels the abjuration ward (DC 15 Arcana to identify; dispel magic to release), Osvaldo attacks — then stops mid-swing, weeps, and dies. His form dissolves into black ichor. If the infernal contract is later destroyed (Path 2), this death is retroactively a mercy — his soul is freed. If the contract is not destroyed, his soul belongs to Asmodeus.

This is a dramatic encounter. The party gains no tactical advantage from this room. What they gain is understanding — the pact's cost made visible in a child's ruined body.

**C25 — Butterfly Garden.** Walled garden at the villa's rear, filled with tropical species sustained by magical heat. During social events: guests wander here, nobles duel for entertainment, wine is served. Six imps in raven form perch in the garden's trees and on the garden wall. They observe all movement. During combat: the imps attack in pairs, using invisibility and sting.

**C27 — False Vault.** Basement level. A triple-locked iron door (DC 20 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) × 3, or force: AC 19, 90 HP) opens onto an empty room. A glyph of warding on the ceiling (DC 16 Intelligence (Investigation) to detect, dispel magic to disarm) triggers when any creature enters without speaking the passphrase "Gold is my shield." Effect: 8d8 cold damage (DC 16 Dexterity saving throw for half). The room is a decoy — designed to look like the vault and to punish anyone who reaches it without the passphrase. The real path continues through C28.

Guild blueprints from Scene 2 show this room's dimensions, which are smaller than the vault-space implied by the basement's footprint — a clue that C27 is not the true vault.

**C28 — Wine Cellar.** Stone walls lined with racks. The Cassalanters maintain a genuine wine collection here — 600 bottles, several worth 50+ GP. The west wall bears a carved Siamorphe sun symbol. Pressing the central ray opens a secret door (DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation) to find without prior knowledge; automatic if Elzerina, Jandar, or the guild blueprints have indicated the door's existence). Behind the door: C29.

**C29 — Secret Vault.** A small stone chamber. During normal operations: twenty silver trade bars (25 GP each) on shelving. On Founders' Day: 500 sacks of gold coins — the Vault of Dragons' contents, if obtained — stacked floor to ceiling. A stone staircase descends thirty feet from the vault floor to Temple Area A1.

*Temple Layer — Critical Path Areas.*

The genre shifts here. The villa above is social — painted walls, conversation, the management of appearances. The temple below is stone corridors, infernal iconography, and the accumulated spiritual weight of three years of devil worship. The air smells of incense and old blood. The temperature drops ten degrees.

**A1 — Hall of the Damned.** The staircase from C29 opens into a vaulted passage. Three empty sacrifice cells with iron doors line the east wall; a key ring on a hook opens all three. The cells are clean — whatever happened here was recent and has been scrubbed. A spiral stair descends further to A3.

**A3 — Anterior Vestibule.** An eighteen-foot portrait of Asmodeus in ceremonial pose dominates the south wall — oil paint over what was once a portrait of Siamorphe (visible in the layered brushstrokes, DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)). During cult gatherings: one bearded devil stands before the doors to A7, arms crossed. During non-gathering times: the vestibule is empty. The doors to A7 are unlocked but heavy — require Strength 14 to push open.

**A4 / A4a — Family Crypt / Caladorn's Crypt.** Twelve sarcophagi in a vaulted chamber. Three specters rise from the floor when a living creature enters — they attack unless shown a Siamorphe holy symbol, at which point they retreat into their sarcophagi and become inert. Beyond A4, a smaller chamber (A4a) holds Caladorn Cassalanter's sarcophagus and his ghost.

Caladorn's ghost manifests when approached. If the approaching creature bears fiendish taint (participated in Asmodean worship, bears an infernal mark), Caladorn attacks — Multiattack, Withering Touch, CR 4. If the approaching creature presents the Siamorphe holy symbol, Caladorn is lucid. He provides the +1 plate armor and the mace of disruption. He asks for the vow. His voice is steady until he mentions his family — then it breaks. "They turned my wife's goddess into that thing. Use the mace. End it."

**A7 — Ceremonial Hall.** The temple's heart. A thirty-foot statue of Asmodeus carved from what was once a Siamorphe figure — the proportions are wrong in ways that suggest alteration rather than original design. A sacrificial fire bowl sits at the statue's base (contact: 4d10 fire damage). During cult gatherings: thirty cultists in crimson robes and golden masks, Victoro leading the rite from the dais, Ammalia pouring wine, six invisible imps perched on the statue's shoulders.

Eye #2 is secured behind the statue's base. The base contains a warded compartment accessible via a concealed panel (DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation) to locate, DC 18 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) or DC 18 Intelligence (Arcana) to bypass the warding without triggering the fire bowl). The party can extract the Eye through careful manipulation — or through force.

Striking the statue with the mace of disruption: the mace resonates with the original Siamorphe consecration trapped in the stone. The statue cracks from the point of impact. The crack spreads. The Asmodeus features shatter and fall away, revealing fragments of Siamorphe's original face beneath. The sacrificial fire bowl shatters, dealing 2d10 psychic damage to every cultist who witnesses the destruction. The infernal contract — bound to the statue since the day it was signed — severs. Eye #2 is released from the statue's base as the compartment cracks open. The warding dies. The children's souls are freed.

**A9 — Stream and Pool.** The temple's lowest point. A natural cavern with an underground stream that feeds a shallow pool. The stream continues through a narrow passage to the Mud Flats northwest of Waterdeep — the alternate entry/exit route. The passage is 400 feet, partially submerged, with two air pockets. DC 12 Constitution saving throw against exhaustion at the midpoint. The cavern is unguarded unless the villa is at Lockdown tier, in which case a bearded devil patrols here.

*Adversary Roster — Standard Configuration.*

Victoro (9th-level spellcaster, see the **Bestiary** (not yet drafted)) occupies C4 or C6 during daylight hours; during cult gatherings, he leads the rite in A7. His combat behavior: spiritual weapon and hold person to control and divide, shield of faith on Ammalia if she is present, banishment on Ammalia if reduced to 30 HP or fewer (sending her to the Ethereal Plane for safety). He does not fight to kill initially — he uses magic to subdue.

Ammalia (9th-level spellcaster, see the **Bestiary** (not yet drafted)) occupies C22 or the second-floor family rooms during daylight hours; during gatherings, she is in A7. Her combat behavior: charm person (5th-level slot, up to 5 targets) to defuse confrontation, dominate person on the most dangerous combatant, counterspell defensively. She arrives to aid Victoro within 2 rounds of hearing combat. She does not fight to kill unless the children are directly threatened.

Willifort Crowelle (doppelganger) occupies C1 or circulates through the villa's public rooms. In combat: drops the tiefling disguise, uses Multiattack, and attempts to flee through the service wing if reduced below 15 HP.

The helmed horror in C6 activates on unauthorized desk contact. AC 20, HP 60. It pursues the triggering creature through the villa but does not leave the building.

Jandar's animated knives (10 flying swords) activate on his command word "For'zaal." They fight until destroyed or until Jandar speaks "Basta" (the deactivation word, obtainable from Fiorella or a DC 14 Charisma (Intimidation) check on Jandar).

Temple layer: 3 specters + ghost (A4/A4a) — hostile to fiend-tainted creatures, pacified by Siamorphe symbol. 1 bearded devil (A3, cult gathering only). 30 cultists + 6 invisible imps (A7, cult gathering only). 2 imps in spider form (C24, always present).

Area-assigned guards: 4 guards at villa perimeter (2 front, 2 garden gate), 2 guards at C5 (second-floor landing), 1 guard at C28 (wine cellar, night shift only). During social events: guards doubled at C5 and perimeter.

*Adversary Roster — Social Event Configuration.*

40–60 civilian guests fill C1, C5 lower, C9, C13, C15, and C25. Ten servant commoners circulate through service areas. Guards at C5 reinforced to 4. Willifort circulates with a tray. Ammalia moves between C13 (ballroom), C15 (banquet hall), and C25 (garden) on a 20-minute rotation. Victoro holds position in C9 (dining room) or C4 (reading room), entertaining guests. "Vessa" the BD operative is in C15. Esvele Rosznar stages in C2. Jandar is in C10 with full kitchen staff. The helmed horror in C6 is active regardless of event status. Temple layer is sealed during social events — the C28 secret door is closed, and no cult activity occurs.

*The Social Exposure System.*

This quest's defining mechanic. The social-layer equivalent of **Xanathar's Lair**'s alarm cascade.

**Stage 1 — Accepted Guest.** The party is socially legitimate. Free movement in public areas (C1, C5 lower, C9, C13, C15, C25). Willifort monitors via detect thoughts but has not flagged anything. Guards defer to guest status. Restricted areas (C6, C22, C24, second-floor private rooms, cellar access) are locked and off-limits.

Trigger to Stage 2: a servant or guest observes something mildly suspicious — a failed DC 13 Stealth check near a restricted door, an odd question to staff that Maesta reports to Willifort, or Willifort's detect thoughts catching a concerning surface thought (failed DC 13 Charisma (Deception) check when Willifort is nearby).

**Stage 2 — Noticed.** Willifort begins actively scanning the party — he positions himself within 30 feet and uses his action to probe deeper thoughts (DC 15 Wisdom saving throw). Guards reposition casually near restricted areas — the party notices them if they are looking (Passive Perception 12). Servants who previously chatted freely become quieter and glance at Willifort before answering questions. No direct confrontation yet. The party can recover by leaving the restricted area and maintaining normal social behavior for 15 minutes (automatic de-escalation to Stage 1).

Trigger to Stage 3: multiple suspicious incidents, or a single significant breach — caught inside C6 or C22, a cult member reports aggressive questioning, Willifort's deep probe catches hostile intent.

**Stage 3 — Suspected.** Ammalia engages the party in pointed conversation — charm person (5th-level slot, up to 5 targets, DC 15 Wisdom saving throw). She frames it as hospitality: "You seem so tense. Let me get you something to drink. Tell me what's really brought you here tonight." Guards take positions at all stairwells and the cellar door. Private areas are locked and watched. Jandar is positioned at the kitchen door. The party can still recover — but only through a dramatic pivot: revealing what they know about the children forces a confrontation (see the Cooperative Discovery Path in Scene 3), or a DC 18 Charisma (Deception) Group Check convinces the Cassalanters that the suspicious behavior was a misunderstanding.

Trigger to Stage 4: the party is caught in the temple, a cult member is subdued, or Willifort confirms through detect thoughts that the party intends to steal from or expose the Cassalanters.

**Stage 4 — Exposed.** The party's true intentions are known. Guards mobilize. Willifort drops the tiefling disguise. Ammalia attempts dominate person (DC 15 Wisdom saving throw) on the party member she considers most dangerous. The C28/C29 passage is sealed — Victoro uses arcane lock. The party must fight, flee, or make a dramatic pivot: revealing knowledge of the children's souls and the contract terms forces the confrontation scene from the Cooperative Discovery Path. At Stage 4, this pivot is the party's last chance at a non-combat resolution. The Cassalanters will listen — even now — if the party can prove they know the truth and offer a way out.

**Tone.** The villa layer should feel like a thriller — smiling faces, murmured conversations, the constant awareness that detection is one wrong word away. The temple layer shifts to horror — stone corridors, infernal iconography, the weight of what the Cassalanters have built beneath their home. The genre transition happens at the staircase in C29. Above it, the danger is a wrong word. Below it, the danger has teeth.

## Scene 5: Complications and Escape

**Purpose.** What goes wrong, what goes sideways, and how the party gets out. The Esvele Rosznar parallel heist fires here. The Cassalanter confrontation plays out if it has not already occurred in Scene 4.

**Content.**

*The Esvele Rosznar Parallel Heist.* Conditional — fires if Esvele Rosznar is active in the campaign (introduced in **Fireball!** Scene 5 dinner or encountered at a Waukeentide social event). Esvele, age seventeen, is the Black Viper. She is at the same social event as a guest of the Rosznar family. She slips away mid-event to rob the Cassalanters — jewelry, silverware, anything portable and valuable from the upper floors.

| PC State | Esvele's Action | Result |
|----------|----------------|--------|
| PCs undetected, social layer | Robs upper floors while PCs work the social event | PCs may encounter her in C2 suiting up, or discover evidence of her theft (missing valuables, a guard investigating a noise upstairs) — a distraction that helps or complicates depending on timing |
| PCs detected (Stage 3+) | Uses the chaos to accelerate her theft | Guards split between PCs and reports of "another intruder upstairs" — reduces guard pressure on PCs but creates confusion |
| PCs in temple layer | Completes her theft and escapes cleanly | No direct interaction — but guards may be on higher alert from her activity when the PCs resurface |
| PCs encounter Esvele directly | Defensive but not hostile; prefers temporary alliance | DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion): she joins the party as a temporary ally, providing rooftop escape routes and knowledge of noble villa layouts. She is skilled but overconfident and seventeen. |
| PCs capture or expose Esvele | Bargains with information | Her Viper Den sits directly above the Brandath Crypts. She has found "old tunnels" beneath it. This is a **Vault of Dragons** clue path — the Viper Den becomes a backup Vault of Dragons entrance discovery. |

Esvele is this quest's pressure-release valve. If the social infiltration is going badly, her activity redirects guard attention. If the party needs a **Vault of Dragons** clue path that does not depend on **Faction Outposts**, Esvele provides it. Her age and overconfidence should make the party protective — she is in over her head in ways she does not understand. The Cassalanters are not just rich nobles.

*Facing the Cassalanters.* If confrontation occurs (through Stage 4 exposure, the cooperative discovery path, or the party reaching the temple and being discovered):

Both are spellcasters. Mutually devoted — each arrives to aid the other within 2 rounds of hearing combat. They do not fight to kill initially: Ammalia opens with charm person and dominate person to divide and subdue; Victoro uses hold person and spiritual weapon to control the field. If Victoro is reduced to 30 HP or fewer, he casts banishment on Ammalia (sending her to the Ethereal Plane for 1 minute of safety). Both negotiate if reduced — they do not fear arrest (they have wealth, connections, and legal resources). They fear the Founders' Day clock. They plead with the party to let them finish saving the children.

The confrontation should feel tragic. These are desperate parents who chose a monstrous path. Ammalia reading to her chain-devil son. Victoro unable to visit the attic. Elzerina stepping between Terenzio and anything that frightens him. These details humanize without excusing.

*Escape Routes.*

**Route 1 — Through the Villa.** Fastest if social cover is intact — walk out the front door. At Stage 2 or below, the party simply leaves. At Stage 3, they leave under scrutiny — Willifort watches. At Stage 4, the front exit is blocked by guards; the party fights through or finds another way.

**Route 2 — Through A9.** The stream exit to the Mud Flats. Bypasses the entire villa — the party descends from the temple, enters the water, and swims 400 feet to open air. DC 12 Constitution saving throw against exhaustion at the midpoint. Wet, cold, slow. Safe.

**Route 3 — Esvele's Rooftop Route.** Available if Esvele has been recruited. She knows the villa's roof access (through C24's exterior door or the third-floor landing window) and a route across the rooftops to the garden wall, then down into the Sea Ward streets. DC 13 Athletics or Acrobatics to cross the roof; DC 10 to drop to the garden wall. Bypasses all interior guards.

**Tone.** The complications carry moral weight. Confronting Osvaldo is horror. Confronting the Cassalanters is tragedy. Esvele adds a note of youthful recklessness that contrasts with the arc's heavy themes. The escape should feel like leaving something behind, not just getting away.

## Scene 6: Aftermath

**Purpose.** Resolve the heist. Insert Eye #2. Trigger the Stone of Golorr's knowledge expansion. Set the Cassalanter faction state. Deliver the all-seven-faction debriefs, branched by outcome. Update the Founders' Day clock.

**Content.**

*Eye #2 Recovery.* Eye #2 is a small orb-shaped gemstone extracted from behind the Asmodeus statue's base — either through careful bypass of the warding or through the statue's destruction via the mace of disruption. Inserting it into the Stone of Golorr follows the same physical simplicity as Eye #1: it seats into a socket with an exact fit, a resonant tone felt more than heard.

*Stone of Golorr Upgrade — Three Scenarios.*

**If this is the first Eye restored (neither Xanathar's Lair nor Sea Maidens Faire completed).** The Stone awakens for the first time. The attuned character hears a voice — old and precise, like water that has been underground for a long time. It does not say hello. "Two remain." No elaboration. The fragmentary images from **Faction Outposts**, Scene 1 clarify: "salt air and creaking wood, dark-skinned elf" resolves to a ship in Waterdeep harbor, a theater company's banner — a bald elf at the prow, patient, watching. "An eye, unblinking in darkness, carved stone wet from underground springs" resolves to the beholder's lair beneath Skullport. Unprompted: "I want to be whole." The Stone's cooperation with the party is overlap of interest, not loyalty.

**If this is the second Eye restored (one of Xanathar's Lair or Sea Maidens Faire completed).** The Stone's voice deepens. It becomes more communicative — almost conversational, still alien and cold. New disclosures:

- "One remains."
- The remaining Eye's location resolves fully. Which impression sharpens depends on which quest the party completed first. If **Xanathar's Lair** was first (Xanathar's lair already raided), the final impression is: "A ship in the harbor. The Scarlet Marpenoth. A bald elf who has been patient for eleven years." If **Sea Maidens Faire** was first (Jarlaxle's Eye already recovered), the final impression is: "Beneath Skullport. An eye, unblinking in carved stone, wet from underground springs. It has been waiting longer than anyone has been looking." The Stone identifies each Eye by proximity-sense, not by name.
- The Vault's existence acknowledged: the Stone confirms it knows where the Vault is and that three Eyes will unlock the full knowledge. It does not reveal the location or command phrase.
- The Stone's agenda deepens: "I am remembering what I was." The aboleth consciousness bleeds through more strongly — references to deep water, dreaming minds, geological patience. The Mad Mage seed intensifies.
- What it does NOT reveal: the vault location, the command phrase, and Aurinax's nature. These require all three Eyes.

**If this is the third Eye restored (both Xanathar's Lair and Sea Maidens Faire already completed).** The Stone goes still, then speaks with sudden, precise clarity. The fragmentary impressions are gone. The vault's location, the command phrase, and the nature of its guardian resolve in full. Run the 3-Eye disclosure scene from **Vault of Dragons** at this point. Quest order does not change the awakening, only its timing.

*Faction State Changes.*

**Cassalanters — set the "Cassalanter active?" flag.** Five states:

*Redeemed (contract destroyed).* The infernal contract is severed. Children's souls freed. Osvaldo's chain devil form dissolves — he dies, but his soul is freed. The Cassalanters are shattered but liberated. Their public reputation survives if the party kept the cult activities quiet; collapses if exposure accompanied the destruction. Either way, the Cassalanters are genuinely grateful and become reliable allies — they owe the party everything. Their wealth and political connections are available without infernal strings. Asmodean retribution seeded: a bearded devil within days, escalating to a chain devil within weeks (Vanrakdoom payoff, Undermountain Level 18). Seffia and surviving cult operatives scatter or surrender. Willifort: if he served Asmodeus directly (he did), he flees. He has no loyalty to a family that no longer serves his master.

*Cooperative / Gold Delivered.* The party helped the Cassalanters secure the Vault gold. The buy-out ritual proceeds. Children saved if both the gold (999,999 GP total) and the 99 souls are provided. If the party stopped the poisoning but delivered the gold — the ritual is incomplete. Asmodeus does not accept partial payment; the children's souls remain forfeit unless the contract is destroyed by other means. If the party delivered the gold and the sacrifice happened — children saved, 99 dead. The Cassalanters are powerful allies stained by mass murder. The party's complicity is a tracked state for **Vault of Dragons**.

*Active / Adversarial.* The party took the Eye but did not expose or destroy the cult and did not cooperate. The Cassalanters are desperate — Seffia's team hunts for a replacement plan. Willifort's network may attempt to recover Eye #2. The Founders' Day plan accelerates. The Cassalanters become antagonists in **Vault of Dragons**, contesting the Vault directly.

*Exposed.* Evidence delivered to the Masked Lords. Cassalanters arrested or fled. Cult disbanded. Children's souls forfeit on their birthday — Asmodeus claims them. They become lemures. Victoro and Ammalia vow to destroy those who thwarted them (from prison or exile). Banking operations disrupted, affecting guild debts across the city.

*Founders' Day Completed (too late).* The party did not reach this quest before Founders' Day. The sacrifice happened — 99 dead, children saved. The Cassalanters are operational, powerful, and have gotten away with mass murder. They are not hostile to the party (they have what they wanted), but they are dangerous and owe no one. If the party later confronts them: "Our children are alive. What would you have done?"

**Xanathar Guild.** If **Xanathar's Lair** already completed, no change. If not, Xanathar's awareness of the party's activities depends on imp surveillance from C25 — if the villa was assaulted rather than infiltrated, reports of unusual activity near the Sea Ward reach the Guild's informant network.

**Manshoon's Zhentarim.** Monitors through double agents. If the Cassalanters are weakened, Manshoon considers one fewer rival and may accelerate his own timeline.

**Bregan D'aerthe.** "Vessa" reports the outcome to Jarlaxle. If the party recovered the Eye, Jarlaxle adjusts his plan for Eye #3 — he knows they are two-thirds of the way to the Vault.

*All-Seven-Faction Debriefs — Outcome-Branched.*

Each contact reacts to what the party actually did. Four outcome tracks:

**If Contract Destroyed (mace + statue):**

- *Mirt (Harpers).* Impressed but wary. The cult is broken, which is good. But can the Cassalanters be trusted now? Mirt wants evidence that infernal operations are truly ended. If the Cassalanters cooperated: he considers them potential intelligence assets.
- *Jalester (Lords' Alliance).* Relieved. The cult is neutralized without a politically costly arrest. The Cassalanters' banking operations continue — good for the city's economy. But: will the Cassalanters remain influential? A redeemed family with political power is better than a disgraced one that destabilizes the nobility.
- *Melannor (Emerald Enclave).* Were the imps dealt with? The butterfly garden can recover naturally now.
- *Savra (Order of the Gauntlet).* Exultant. Caladorn Cassalanter was a hero of the North — his ghost's holy weapon destroyed an infernal altar. The Gauntlet's Siamorphe symbol was instrumental. If the Cassalanters survive and are redeemed: "They were damned, and you undamned them. That is the Order's highest calling." She wants to know if the family will publicly repent. If they do, the Gauntlet may become an unexpected Cassalanter ally — watching over the family's redemption.
- *Vajra (Force Grey).* Fascinated by the mace's interaction with the infernal ward. Detailed questions about the statue's destruction and the contract's severance mechanism. Wants to study the temple's residual magic. Warns that Asmodean retribution is likely.
- *Tashlyn (Doom Raiders).* Pragmatic. The Cassalanters' banking leverage is intact — and now they owe the party. Useful.
- *Jarlaxle (Bregan D'aerthe).* Genuinely moved — the closest thing to unguarded emotion the party has seen from him. The children are saved. He recovers quickly: "Well. One fewer rival faction. How convenient."

**If Cooperative / Gold Payment:**

- *Mirt.* Demands to know about the 99. If the poisoning was stopped: pragmatic acceptance. If it was not: horrified. The Harpers move against the Cassalanters regardless of the party's alliance.
- *Jalester.* If the poisoning was stopped: a political win — no mass murder, children saved, Cassalanters cooperative. If not: shaken. Asks the party to testify before the Masked Lords.
- *Savra.* Troubled. The children are saved, but Asmodeus received payment. The Gauntlet does not approve of bargaining with devils — even to save innocents. If the poisoning happened: devastated and furious.
- *Jarlaxle.* Amused. "You gave away five hundred thousand gold dragons to save two children. I have never met anyone so wealthy in compassion and so poor in business sense."

**If Sacrifice Happened (party did not intervene in time):**

- *Savra.* Grief-stricken and angry. Ninety-nine dead. The Gauntlet failed. Savra questions whether the party had the power to act and chose not to.
- *Mirt.* Cold fury. Demands evidence for the Masked Lords.
- All contacts: the tone shifts to recrimination or resolve. The party's relationship with each faction is colored by what they could have done.

**If Cassalanters Exposed:**

- *Savra.* Satisfied that justice was served — but grim about the children. Asmodeus claims their souls in ten days. She asks if there is anything left to try.
- *Mirt.* The Harpers have what they need. The Masked Lords can act. But he is quietly haunted: "The children were innocent. I want you to know that I know that."
- *Jalester.* The legal system works. The arrest proceeds. He privately admits the outcome feels hollow.
- *Jarlaxle.* Offers, obliquely, to "look into certain infernal matters" — hinting that Bregan D'aerthe might have resources to contest the soul-claim. He does not promise success. This is a genuine lifeline or a characterful dead end, depending on how the DM runs **Vault of Dragons**.

*Founders' Day Clock.* This quest consumes 1–2 tendays total: several days of intelligence gathering and casing (Scenes 1–3), 1–3 days for the operation and escape (Scenes 4–5), and 2–4 days of recovery and debrief (Scene 6). Update the campaign calendar. The DM communicates the advancing deadline through environmental detail: Founders' Day preparations visible across the city, Cassalanter-funded pavilions being erected in the Field of Triumph, festival decorations going up in every ward, and — if the party has Cassalanter contacts — increasingly strained behavior from Ammalia.

*Transition.* The party holds the Stone of Golorr and 1–2 Eyes. The children's fate is resolved or ticking. The remaining lair heists — whichever of **Xanathar's Lair**, **Sea Maidens Faire**, and **Kolat Towers** have not been completed — are available in any order. The Cassalanter Report on the Grand Game (C6/C22) points toward **Kolat Towers**. Esvele's Viper Den (if discovered) points toward the Brandath Crypts (**Vault of Dragons**). The Stone's impressions guide the party toward remaining targets.

**Tone.** Decompression weighted with consequence. Unlike **Xanathar's Lair**'s revelatory aftermath — the Stone speaks for the first time — this quest's aftermath is about what the party chose. The children, the Cassalanters, the evidence, the poison. This quest's resolution is moral, not tactical. What the party did matters more than what they found.

# Design Notes: Cassalanter Villa

## Genre Identity

This quest must feel like social infiltration followed by dungeon horror, and the distinction from **Xanathar's Lair** (dungeon infiltration), **Sea Maidens Faire** (caper heist), and **Kolat Towers** (fortress raid) is structural, not cosmetic. The social exposure system — Accepted, Noticed, Suspected, Exposed — is the defining mechanic above ground. It creates a different kind of tension than the panopticus: the panopticus watches through magic; the villa watches through people. A failed Stealth check in **Xanathar's Lair** triggers an alarm cascade. A failed Deception check in this quest triggers a social escalation that the party can still recover from — but each recovery narrows their options.

The two-phase structure matters for pacing. The villa layer is anxious — conversation, observation, the constant management of who you appear to be. Maintaining cover while accomplishing objectives creates a cognitive load that pure combat does not. The temple layer releases that load into a different kind of pressure: no more social management, but stone corridors, fiendish guards, and the truth of what the Cassalanters have done carved into every wall. The staircase at C29 is a genre boundary. Crossing it should feel irreversible.

## The Social Exposure System

The system uses staged escalation rather than binary pass/fail because a single failed Deception check should not end the heist. A party that fails one check at Stage 1 gets noticed — Willifort pays more attention, guards shift. A party that fails again gets suspected — Ammalia intervenes with charm magic, doors lock. A party that fails a third time is exposed — the social layer collapses and combat begins. But even at Stage 4, the party has one final out: revealing what they know about the children forces the confrontation scene from the Cooperative Discovery Path. This is deliberate. The social exposure system is designed to create compounding tension, not a cliff edge. Each stage gives the party a chance to recover, adapt, or pivot. The party that panics and fights at Stage 2 had other options. The party that reaches Stage 4 and plays the children card has earned a dramatic moment.

The 15-minute de-escalation from Stage 2 to Stage 1 is a calibration decision. Fifteen minutes is long enough that the party feels the cost of the recovery (15 minutes of maintaining cover, not accomplishing objectives) without making the penalty so severe that Stage 2 becomes Stage 4 in practice.

## The Moral Dilemma as Central Design

This quest's four resolution paths exist because the Cassalanter story is a trolley problem with no clean solution. The "right" legal action — expose the cult — kills the children. Helping the Cassalanters save the children requires either paying Asmodeus or ignoring ninety-nine murdered innocents. Destroying the contract frees the children but invites infernal retribution and eliminates the family's patron. Every path has a cost. No path is clean.

The design has intentionally ensured that the cooperative path — where the PCs learn the truth and negotiate peacefully with the Cassalanters — is discoverable through play. The clue trail (any two of: Osvaldo, grimoire, journal, Nana Rosse) converges on the full picture. The confrontation scene is written so that the Cassalanters break — they are parents. A party that investigates thoroughly and approaches diplomatically deserves a non-combat resolution. That resolution still carries hard choices (the ninety-nine, the gold, the contract), but it respects player agency by rewarding investigation with options.

The text presents all four paths without advocacy because the choice belongs to the players. The DM should pre-decide which paths they are prepared to support. The design does not favor any outcome — it ensures each is viable, consequential, and emotionally loaded.

## Eye #2 Behind the Statue

The Alexandrian places Eye #2 in Victoro's desk (C6). Moving it to the temple's ceremonial hall (A7) is a deliberate structural decision. If the Eye were in C6, the party could complete the heist entirely within the villa's social layer — pick the lock, defeat the helmed horror, take the Eye, leave. The temple would be optional. The temple is not optional. It is where the truth lives — the statue, the sacrificial bowl, the crypt of the family that was. The two-layer heist structure forces the party through both phases: social infiltration to reach the cellar, then dungeon descent to reach the Eye. This is the correct structure for a quest whose dramatic question is about what lies beneath the surface.

The desk still contains critical documents — Seffia's report, the Report on the Grand Game — ensuring that C6 remains a high-value target. But the Eye itself demands the descent.

## Esvele Rosznar as Parallel Heist

The parallel heist mirrors Jarlaxle's simultaneous operation in **Xanathar's Lair**, with three structural functions.

Dramatic tension: the party is not the only person robbing the Cassalanters. Esvele's presence creates awareness that the heist is not a controlled operation — variables exist that the party cannot predict. She may help, complicate, or create an entirely new problem.

Pressure-release design: if the social infiltration is collapsing at Stage 3 or 4, Esvele's activity splits guard attention. Reports of "another intruder upstairs" give the party a window to move or escape. The interference is a consequence of two simultaneous heists, not a rescue.

**Vault of Dragons** setup: Esvele's Viper Den sits above the Brandath Crypts. If the party captures or allies with her, this location becomes a Three Clue Rule path to the Vault's physical entrance, independent of anything the party discovered in **Faction Outposts**.

Esvele's age — seventeen — is a deliberate characterization choice. She is overconfident, skilled, and in over her head. The Cassalanters are not just rich nobles. A party that encounters Esvele should feel protective — she does not understand what is beneath this house. The contrast between her adolescent bravado and the arc's heavy themes is tonal counterpoint.

## The Cassalanters as Tragic Antagonists

The confrontation is written to feel tragic rather than triumphant because the Cassalanters are not cackling villains. They are parents who chose a monstrous path to save their children. The details that humanize them — Ammalia reading to a chain devil, Victoro unable to visit the attic, Elzerina stepping between Terenzio and anything that frightens him — exist to make the judgment hard.

A party that storms the villa, takes the Eye, and leaves without engaging the moral dimension has completed the heist but missed this quest's dramatic question. A party that discovers the truth and makes a decision — any decision — about the children has experienced what this quest is for. The mechanical content (the Eye, the Stone upgrade, the faction state) is the same in both cases.

## Caladorn's Ghost and the Mace of Disruption

The family crypt provides the tool to destroy the infernal contract because the irony is load-bearing. The Cassalanters reshaped their ancestor's crypt — carved the goddess Siamorphe into Asmodeus, turned a place of family reverence into a place of infernal worship. Caladorn's ghost has been trapped beneath the temple for three years, unable to leave A4a, attacking anyone who enters because every visitor has been a cultist. The Cassalanters have tried to reach him twice and fled both times. The solution to their contract has been beneath their own villa the entire time, accessible only to people who are not corrupted by it.

Only outsiders — the PCs, bearing a Siamorphe holy symbol from the Order of the Gauntlet — can speak to Caladorn and receive the mace. The Cassalanters' own ancestor provides the weapon to end their corruption, but only to people who are not party to the corruption themselves. Caladorn represents what the family was before the pact. Using his mace to shatter the statue is simultaneously justice, mercy, and family restoration.

The mace of disruption is a permanent reward that the party retains. It is relevant to Undermountain encounters with undead and fiends. This is a long-tail investment that pays off across the campaign's second act.

## The Three Clue Rule Path to Kolat Towers

The Cassalanter Report on the Grand Game is placed in both C6 (Victoro's desk) and C22 (Ammalia's study). This dual placement ensures that a party reaching either room — not necessarily both — discovers the path to Kolat Towers. The report references Manshoon by operational name, identifies the Towers' location, describes Zhentarim splinter activity, and notes the force-field entry restriction. It is the third of three independent paths to **Kolat Towers**, alongside Samara's testimony from **Xanathar's Lair** X7 and Nihiloor's intelligence files from **Xanathar's Lair** X23.

The Three Clue Rule is now satisfied across quests. A party that skipped **Xanathar's Lair** entirely can still reach **Kolat Towers** through this quest. A party that completed **Xanathar's Lair** but missed both the Samara and Nihiloor paths can reach **Kolat Towers** through the Cassalanter report. No single failed check, missed room, or skipped quest blocks all three paths. This is the correct application of the Three Clue Rule in a campaign where lair heists run in variable order.

## Variable Difficulty and Social Escalation

The four-tier difficulty table translates **Faction Outposts** outpost consequences into this quest's environment with a harsher progression than **Xanathar's Lair**. At Alert tier, the social entry window closes entirely — no charity ball, no Goldenight gala. The party must create their own access. At Lockdown, the social infiltration genre is no longer possible — this quest's top layer collapses into a different kind of operation.

This is more severe than **Xanathar's Lair**'s scaling (where higher tiers added guards and traps but did not change the fundamental approach) because social infiltration depends on the target being socially open. A villa that is closed to visitors cannot be socially infiltrated. The party that hit both Cassalanter outposts in **Faction Outposts** traded social access for intelligence — a real cost that the difficulty table makes legible.

## Founders' Day as Real Deadline

Founders' Day matters more in this quest than in any other because it is not just a calendar marker — it is the sacrifice deadline. If the party pursues this quest late in the campaign, Founders' Day may have already passed, and the sacrifice with it. The aftermath handles both the "prevented" and "it already happened" states, but the DM should make the approaching deadline felt. Festival preparations in the streets, Cassalanter pavilions going up, staff discussing catering — these are environmental clock signals that the party can read.

If Founders' Day passes before the party reaches this quest, the "Founders' Day Completed" state applies: ninety-nine dead, children saved, Cassalanters operational. This quest can still be run — the Eye is still in the temple, the heist is still necessary — but the moral dimension shifts. The choice is no longer "can we save them?" but "what do we do about what already happened?" This is a designed consequence of player pacing decisions.

## Named Staff as Design Principle

Every servant in the household is named, assigned a cult or non-cult status, given a defined motivation, and specified in what they would reveal if questioned and how they would react to learning the truth. This follows the zero-prep design principle: the DM should not have to invent a servant's loyalty on the fly.

The cult members are individually motivated — true belief (Willifort), personal loyalty (Tissina), love for the children (Nana Rosse), debt-bound service (Jandar). They are not interchangeable. A party that turns Tissina gets different information than one that turns Jandar. A party that turns Nana Rosse gets the most emotionally charged cooperation: a woman who loves the children more than she fears the cult.

The non-cult staff are designed to be people, not obstacles. Each has one piece of information and a reaction. The DM can run any staff interaction from the text without preparation. The 10% secret-cult-member risk has been resolved: no named non-cult staff member is a secret cultist. The risk manifests through social mechanics — questioning too many staff in one day triggers Maesta's professional concern, which advances the social exposure system.

## The Stone's 2-Eye Upgrade

The 2-Eye disclosure is written as a progression from the 1-Eye awakening in **Xanathar's Lair**. The Stone becomes more communicative — almost conversational — because the restored Eye returns capacity. The final Eye's location resolves fully: the Scarlet Marpenoth, a bald elf, eleven years of patience. The Vault's existence is acknowledged without detail. The aboleth consciousness bleeds through more strongly.

The conditional handling (this is Eye #1 vs. Eye #2) ensures the scene works regardless of quest order. A party that runs **Cassalanter Villa** before **Xanathar's Lair** gets the 1-Eye awakening here; a party that runs **Xanathar's Lair** first gets the 2-Eye upgrade. Both are written in full. The DM does not need to interpolate.
