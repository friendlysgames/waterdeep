# Appendix D — Running the Player Factions

Each faction operates as an ongoing relationship, not a mission board. Contacts push for information, react to the PCs' choices in the Grand Game, and bring their own agendas to bear as events escalate. The missions below are structured as mini-arcs — each one has an investigation phase, a complication, and at least two resolution paths. No single skill check should determine success.

**A note on the Zhentarim:** Two Zhentarim cells operate in Waterdeep. Davil Starsong's **Doom Raiders** are the player-joinable faction covered in Appendix C. Manshoon's faction is the villain cell — called **the Splinter** throughout these notes. They are hostile to the Doom Raiders and to the PCs. When these missions say "the Splinter," they mean Manshoon's agents, not Davil's.

**Mission delivery:** Unless noted, missions arrive via the faction's preferred method — paper birds for Harpers, flying snakes for Zhentarim, animal messengers for the Enclave, Sending spells for Force Grey.

**Failure:** If a mission ends in failure, the situation changes — it doesn't simply reset. Targets escape, informants are compromised, and rival factions benefit. Each mission's Aftermath notes what specifically changes when things go wrong; use that, not a general reset.

**Renown:** Each mission lists a base renown award calibrated to its tier (Level 2–3 missions pay 2, Level 4–5 pay 3, Level 6–7 pay 4). Each mission also lists specific bonus conditions worth +1 each — award only those, not a general judgment call. A clean failure forfeits the award but costs nothing; a botched mission that causes collateral damage costs −1. Supplementary renown from upholding faction values (+1) and achieving major faction goals (+2) stacks on top — a dedicated faction member should reach Renown 30+ by the late heist arcs.

## Harpers

*Contact: Mirt (primary). Remallia Haventree: senior Harper; her faction identity is hidden from PCs until Mission 4.*

*Mirt rarely delivers missions in person — paper birds arrive at the tavern, or at wherever the PCs were last seen. He's always somewhere between hard to find and deliberately unavailable.*

### The Grand Game — Harper Stance

The Harpers know the Cassalanters are infernalists. They don't know the full scope of the vault situation until the PCs tell them. They want no single faction — including the Lords' Alliance, Force Grey, or the PCs — to walk away from the Grand Game with unchecked power. Their ideal outcome: the vault money is dispersed in ways that prevent political consolidation, the Stone of Golorr is handed to them for study, and whatever is dreaming below the city stays asleep.

**What the Harpers share proactively:**
- The Cassalanters' infernal history (after Mission 3, if PCs seem likely to encounter them)
- That the Waterdeep Harper network has been infiltrated by Zhentarim double-agents (after Mission 4, if the PCs have met Davil)
- Advance warning of faction response team deployments (at Renown 15+)

**What the Harpers ask for:**
- Regular intelligence updates on what the villain factions are doing
- The Stone of Golorr, or at minimum three days' access to study it (Mission 6)
- That the vault gold not be given to any one faction or individual

### Mission 1 — The Talking Mare
**Level 2 | 2 renown | No tangible reward**

**Hook**
Tickets to a matinée at the Lightsinger Theater arrive at Trollskull Manor — one per character, no note. When the PCs arrive, Mirt is waiting in a private box, watching the stage with the expression of a man who has no real interest in the play. He greets them without looking away from the stage, pours wine he's already had delivered, and gets to the point. "There's a draft mare named Maxeene working a dray route in the Dock Ward. She listens to everything and she remembers it. I need to know what she's heard before someone less friendly to us finds her first." He hands over a folded paper with Maxeene's route and the word *apple* underlined. "She responds well to courtesy."

He doesn't explain who he is or what the Harpers are. He assumes the PCs already know enough, or will figure it out. He's gone before the first act ends.

**Background** *(DM only)*
A Harper-sympathetic druid cast a permanent Speak with Animals on Maxeene years ago, a favor to Mirt. She gossips freely to anyone she finds interesting. She's been carrying a Manshoon Splinter operative on her route this week without knowing it, and the Splinter has sent a junior agent to locate her — waiting for someone else to identify which horse out of hundreds can actually speak.

**Act 1 — Finding Maxeene**
The Dock Ward has dozens of dray horses working daily routes. Asking around:
- A dray driver at the Wagonworks stable mentions a mare with a star marking who "seems to understand things" — never acts spooked, always waits patiently at the right corners. He finds it convenient, not strange.
- A Dock Ward urchin named Orvel will trade Maxeene's current location for a silver piece and one of the apples. He's found her useful in the past.
- A DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) check while talking to stablehands earns more specific route information — she's pulling a merchant's wagon on Fillet Lane this afternoon.

**Act 2 — The Watcher**
Maxeene has the star marking and turns her head when she hears her name. A hire-coach has been parked across the lane for forty minutes. Inside is Vell (Spy stat block), a Splinter operative who has been waiting for someone else to identify the right horse. Once the PCs approach, she moves in — intercepting the conversation, or following them when they leave.

A DC 14 Passive Perception notices the coach hasn't moved despite its driver appearing restless. Approaching Maxeene openly lets Vell watch from a distance.

**Act 3 — What She Remembers**
Maxeene is a grey roan with a white blaze, large and well-groomed, wearing dray harness of good quality. She speaks if given the apple and addressed politely. Her voice is unhurried, slightly dry, with the accent of someone who has spent a lot of time listening to the Castle Ward upper classes. Treat her as an animal or speak in the slow, loud cadence some people use with horses and foreigners — she goes silent and stays a horse. Treat her as a professional contact and she answers directly. She's chatty. Key information:
- A sun elf and a large half-orc took her dray two weeks ago, Dock Ward to the Yawning Portal. They argued quietly. The elf wanted to keep looking for someone; the half-orc thought it was too exposed. The elf said, "The war doesn't help anyone until we know who's running it."
- A pale man with a carved wooden wand hired her four days ago. No name, no conversation. Dropped at an unmarked building on Shesstra Street, Trades Ward — she describes it: three floors, no signage, a red lantern in the upper window.
- She'd like to be moved to the stables on Candle Lane. The Dock Ward has felt strange lately.

**Resolution**
*Relocate Maxeene:* Mirt is pleased. She continues as a Harper asset, now in safer stabling. The Splinter loses track of her.
*Leave her in place:* Vell confirms the identification and reports to her handler. Maxeene is no longer a viable intelligence asset.

If Vell is following the PCs when they leave: she can be spotted, confronted, or followed back to a Splinter safehouse in the Trades Ward. The Shesstra Street address Maxeene gave is the same building.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: Maxeene is relocated before the Splinter can confirm her identity. The Splinter's trail goes cold.
- +1: Vell is neutralized or followed back to the Shesstra Street address and the location reported to Mirt. He says nothing, but the address goes somewhere.

**Aftermath**
The Shesstra Street building is a Manshoon Splinter safehouse — one of the Arc E outpost seeds. Mirt thanks them and says nothing more about it immediately, but the address goes somewhere.

### Mission 2 — The Dead Drop
**Level 3 | 2 renown | Used spellbook (if Tessalar is caught)**

**Hook**
The message is longer than usual, the handwriting slightly less neat: *"An old friend named Uza Solizeph runs a bookshop on Sorn Street in the Trades Ward. She's done quiet favors for twenty years. Something's gotten into her shop and she's frightened. Go see what needs doing."* A postscript, crowded into the margin: *"She doesn't know what she's been helping us with. Keep it that way."*

**Background** *(DM only)*
Uza's bookshop has hosted a Harper dead drop for a decade — a loose brick behind a specific shelf section, used by Mattrim "Threestrings" Mereg for his network contacts. A Manshoon Splinter informant among Uza's regular customers identified the drop location and reported it. The Splinter sent a gazer to retrieve the cipher-message inside and read it through its eyebeam before the Harpers could recover it. The gazer is still searching for a secondary document it couldn't find.

**Act 1 — The Shop**
Uza is at Felzoun's Folly, two streets over, clutching a cup of tea with both hands. She is not given to dramatic exaggeration — she is given to detailed, precise, and comprehensive description of dramatic events, which other people experience as exaggeration. She describes the creature accurately: "Spherical, about the size of my head, one large central eye, smaller eyes on stalks. It knocked over my philosophy section and then just hovered there staring at the wall." She has a cat, Fillipa — a black-and-white cat of eleven years who has been demonstrating tactical intelligence for three days, knocking books into the gazer's path and retreating to the attic rafters whenever it got close. She lends the PCs her keys. Her only stated requirement is that Fillipa come out alive.

The bookshop is three stories on Sorn Street. Each floor has been through the situation differently.

*Ground Floor (Shop):* The philosophy section, shelf seven, near the back wall, is the primary damage zone — books knocked to the floor, display cases of illuminated manuscripts overturned. The smell is faintly ozone-sharp, like lightning held in a room for days. The staircase to the second floor is intact.

*Second Floor (Living Quarters):* Uza's apartments — bedroom, sitting room, kitchen, more books from her personal collection. Minor incidental damage. The gazer passed through here on the way up.

*Third Floor (Attic Workshop):* Low ceiling, 8 feet. Half storage — rolled maps, uncatalogued acquisitions in paper bundles — half workspace, with a long table of bookbinding tools. The gazer (use Gazer stat block) is here, hunting Fillipa through the rafters. Fillipa is 10 feet up in the roof beams, uninjured, highly agitated, and has been providing sustained feline commentary on the situation. She will not accept comfort from anyone until the gazer is dead.

The gazer is hostile and will not retreat — it's been instructed to stay until it finds the secondary document. Any stray eye ray that misses a character: roll d6; on 1–2 it strikes inventory. First hit: books worth 15 gp destroyed. Second hit: a shelf collapses into the stairwell (difficult terrain on the stairs). Third hit: a bundle of rare manuscripts catches arcane flame, ruined. Uza will notice all three categories of damage; she will note them without placing blame.

**Act 2 — What It Was Looking For**
After the gazer is dealt with (or driven off), examining the targeted shelf: a DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) check finds a loose brick. Behind it: a folded cipher note and a small space sized for a second document that isn't there. A DC 14 Arcana check on the cipher note detects fading scry-residue — the gazer read this note through its eyebeam, and someone on the other end was watching. The cipher note contains partial contact identifiers for Harper assets in two noble houses.

Mirt, when reached for comment, goes quiet for a moment. The secondary document he mentions carefully doesn't exist anymore — Mattrim misplaced it three months ago. "That's going to be a problem." He gives them one name: Tessalar, a clerk who started visiting the shop three months ago.

**Act 3 — The Informant**
Tessalar Maeridge is a human clerk, early twenties, works for a Trades Ward counting house. He started visiting Uza's shop after a Splinter handler recruited him with 50 gold pieces and instructions to report anything unusual about the philosophy section.

He had no idea it would escalate to a gazer attack. He's scared, guilty, and not committed to the Splinter. A DC 13 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion) check flips him. He knows his handler's drop location — a ledger at the Trades Ward customs house, second drawer, a specific mark inside the cover.

*Turn him:* He becomes a double agent, filing false reports. The Splinter's view of the Harper dead drop goes dark. The handler investigates eventually.
*Report him to the Watch:* He's arrested. The Splinter investigates the breach; the customs house ledger is burned.
*Warn Mirt and relocate the drop:* No secondary benefit, but clean. Mirt relocates the drop and thanks them for the discretion.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: Tessalar is turned as a double agent rather than arrested or warned off. The Splinter's view of the dead drop goes dark, and the Harpers get a live feed from inside the cell.
- +1: The handler's ledger at the customs house is recovered or destroyed before the Splinter can burn it. Mirt gets two additional agent identifiers he didn't have.

The mission reward regardless of Tessalar's fate: Uza gives the party a used spellbook from her back collection — a wizard who owed her more than he could pay. Four first-level spells and three second-level; the binding is coming loose but the text is sound. Specific spells are the DM's choice from the wizard list.

**Aftermath**
If the Splinter received the cipher contents: two Harper assets in noble houses are compromised. The Harpers lose access to one of their Cassalanter social contacts (relevant to Arc G) unless the PCs resolve this cleanly. Mirt never says this directly but will be notably less forthcoming about Cassalanter intelligence until it's fixed.

### Mission 3 — The Doppelganger Auditions
**Level 4 | 3 renown | 50 gp per participating character (from Harpers)**

**Hook**
No paper bird this time. Mirt finds the PCs at the Yawning Portal personally — or sends Remallia Haventree in his place if he's unavailable, which is his way of saying this one matters. He's uncomfortable: "A member of our network — Mattrim Mereg, you may have met him at the bar — has gotten it into his head that we should recruit a gang of doppelgangers. I've been resisting the idea, but he's persistent and I need a second opinion I can trust. Talk to them. All five. Tell me what you actually think."

He specifically does not tell them which ones he suspects.

**Background** *(DM only)*
Bonnie runs a crew of five doppelgangers out of the Yawning Portal. Four are effectively neutral — they find their current arrangement convenient and would not actively harm the Harpers. One, currently going by "Edric Tanner," is a Manshoon Splinter asset. Edric has been embedded in Bonnie's crew for two months and has already filed one report about Mattrim Mereg's Harper affiliation. He'll use the interviews to gather more intelligence on the PCs.

Bonnie has known about Edric for three weeks. She ran quiet background checks on each crew member after a Guild operation went wrong — a job someone tipped off before it happened. She identified Edric's behavioral drift, confirmed her suspicion, and said nothing. She didn't expose him because she wanted to see whether the Harpers were competent enough to catch him themselves. If they are, she's interested in a real arrangement. If they aren't, a neutrality pact is the best she can offer them.

During the interviews, she's running her own assessment of the PCs. She's the employer in this arrangement, deciding whether these particular clients are worth trusting.

**Act 1 — The Interviews**
Bonnie arranges meetings across two evenings at the Yawning Portal, one per day. Each arrives in human form; Mattrim is present at each interview, nursing a drink, playing quiet songs that are more perceptive than they sound.

*The five doppelgangers:*

**Edric Tanner** — presents as a cheerful, open-hearted Dock Ward teamster, the most disarming of the five. Open-faced, quick laugh, full of anecdotes about routes and cargo. Has been in Bonnie's crew two months. The Splinter asset.

**Kael** — presents as a tall, angular retired sailor. Makes clear within the first five minutes that he considers this meeting pointless. Leaves after twenty minutes regardless of how the conversation goes. His Insight reveal: he is not untrustworthy so much as uninterested — he does not want Harper membership and is only here because Bonnie asked. Not the mole.

**Syla** — presents as a Tethyrian merchant's widow, middle-aged. Answers every question with another question — not evasively, but genuinely, using the interview as much as the PCs are. Her Insight reveal: she is genuinely undecided about who she wants to be. Not lying; not the mole.

**The Scholar** — presents as a young academic, charming and forthcoming about everything except the things that actually matter. His Insight reveal: he is already considering how Harper membership could be monetized. Not actively dangerous, but his loyalty is negotiable.

**The Merchant** — presents as a Dock Ward import dealer, neutral and cooperative. Effectively trustworthy in the limited sense of finding the current arrangement convenient. No hidden agenda.

Each will answer questions freely. Skill checks aren't automatic gates — the PCs should ask smart questions. Useful approaches:
- Asking about specific Harper knowledge they shouldn't have (all five profess ignorance)
- Asking them to describe the Waterdeep they know (locals catch specific details; Edric's knowledge of the Dock Ward is too precise for a teamster, more like an observer's knowledge)
- DC 16 Wisdom (Insight) during conversation catches micro-tells: Edric blinks too infrequently and answers questions a half-second faster than natural
- DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation) examining Edric's hands after the meeting notices no callus pattern consistent with teamster work

**Bonnie herself** is available before or after the formal interviews. She answers questions about the crew with apparent openness. A DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check catches what she's actually doing: her interest in the PCs' questions is too precise, her attention too directed toward their judgment rather than their credentials. She's evaluating clients, not presenting herself for hire.

If a PC asks her directly whether she suspects anyone in the crew, she deflects — "That's what you're here for, isn't it?" But if the same PC presses with specific observations, or names Edric with any supporting evidence, she goes quiet for a moment. Then: "Tell me what you noticed. In order. Starting from the first evening." She's not confirming anything. She wants to see if their work matches hers.

**Act 2 — The Tail**
If the PCs don't flag Edric after the interviews, he begins following one of them the next morning. He's skilled — DC 15 Passive Perception to notice, or a deliberate Perception check during any active movement. If he's caught following and confronted, he drops his current guise and adopts a new face immediately. He won't fight unless cornered — he'll try to disengage and report back. Following him leads to a safehouse in the Trades Ward, different from Shesstra Street but run by the same Splinter cell.

**Mattrim's Revelation**
After the interviews conclude, Mattrim pulls the PCs aside before they report to Mirt. He has been thinking. He sets his lute down carefully and looks at the table. "I think one of them told someone about you. About your affiliation." He says it quietly, without drama. "I don't know which one. I've been going through every conversation and I keep coming up wrong."

Then: "I need to tell you something else. Bonnie knows. She's known for about a week. She hasn't told me because she thinks she can fix it herself. She can't." He looks up. "Whatever you recommend to Mirt — I'll abide by it. I just wanted you to know that she tried."

This reframes the whole assessment. Bonnie is trustworthy, and she has been trying to protect her crew while knowing it is already compromised. What the party recommends to Mirt — and whether they tell him about Bonnie's prior knowledge — becomes a character-defining moment before the formal report begins.

**Act 3 — The Report**
PCs report back to Mirt. He listens without interrupting.

*If Edric is correctly identified:* Mirt is visibly relieved. He recruits the other four under a provisional arrangement — they report to Bonnie, Bonnie reports nothing to the Harpers but agrees to a neutrality pact. Mattrim is chastened. Edric is either neutralized (the Splinter loses their embedded asset) or left in place as a controlled asset feeding false information.

*If Bonnie confirmed she already knew:* The PCs have something Mirt doesn't — evidence that Bonnie's own operational security is better than his. If they bring this to him, he's quiet for longer than usual. "She ran her own check. Sat on it. Watched." He doesn't say anything else immediately. He's recalibrating. A PC can push him: with a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check, or simply by making the case directly — Bonnie found Edric three weeks before the Harpers even knew to look — Mirt agrees to offer her a full operative role rather than a neutrality pact. She wants to name her own terms: her crew's identities stay protected, she routes intelligence through the PCs rather than directly to Mirt, and she retains discretion over which jobs she takes. Mirt accepts. Bonnie becomes a Harper operative with the Yawning Portal as her base of operations. Her crew's foot traffic access is a standing intelligence asset.

*If Edric is not identified:* All five are offered provisional recruitment. Within a week, Edric files a report. Names, descriptions, Harper affiliation. The Splinter now knows who the party is.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: Edric is correctly identified and reported to Mirt before any damage is done. The other four doppelgangers are retained under the neutrality pact.
- +1: Bonnie is recruited as a full Harper operative rather than left at a neutrality pact. Requires correctly identifying Edric AND making the case to Mirt with Bonnie's prior knowledge as evidence.
- +1: The Splinter safehouse Edric leads to is investigated and its contents reported. Mirt can clean house on two additional compromised assets.

**Aftermath**
If Edric is caught and the safehouse is investigated: it contains notes about two other Harper assets in the city — enough for Mirt to clean house before the Splinter acts. If Edric is missed: Manshoon's awareness of the PCs escalates, and the Kolat Towers raid (Arc I) has reinforced defenses tuned specifically to the PCs' known capabilities.

If Bonnie joins: she becomes a recurring contact — a source of Yawning Portal intelligence (who's meeting whom, what names are being asked about, what strangers came through) and a fallback safe house for the party. Her crew can provide short-term cover identities on a mission-by-mission basis. Mattrim is both delighted and slightly competitive about it.

### Mission 4 — A Friend's House
**Level 5 | 3 renown | 200 gp per attending character**

> **Design note — Pacing:** This mission is built to fill most or all of a 3-hour session. There is no time pressure and no combat trigger — the PCs move through the evening at their own pace. The "mission" (identify the impostor) is only one thread. A well-run session will have PCs stumble into two or three unrelated conversations that matter, gather information they didn't expect, and leave with more threads than they arrived with. Jarlaxle being identified early does not end the evening. Plan for at least 6–8 named NPC interactions. The guest list below gives enough material to sustain those. Not every NPC will connect with every party — let the PCs' instincts guide who they gravitate toward.

**Hook**
A formal invitation arrives at Trollskull Manor — heavy paper, Sea Ward seal, Haventree crest. Lady Remallia Haventree requests the pleasure of the party's company at a salon dinner at her estate. No further explanation. A shorter note arrives the same afternoon, in Mirt's cramped handwriting: *"Go. Dress well. Someone in that room is not who they appear to be."*

**Background** *(DM only)*
Remallia Haventree is a senior Harper operative whose faction identity has been hidden from the PCs until now. This dinner is the natural moment for that to surface — she's hosting in her own home, on her own terms. Mirt will be there. He'll let the implication land without announcing it.

Also in attendance: Jarlaxle Baenre, arriving as Erystian Demarne (a Luskan poet of some minor reputation, charming, improbably knowledgeable about everything). He has accepted Remallia's invitation through a mutual acquaintance — she doesn't know who he is. Jarlaxle has already marked Mirt across the room and is curious about the new faces he brought.

**The Guests** *(DM reference — create journal entries for any NPCs the party looks likely to interact with)*

Twenty to thirty guests attend. The following are named and fully playable. Others are generic color.

---

**Remallia Haventree** — *Host. Senior Harper operative (hidden).*
Moves through the room with the ease of someone who has run salons for thirty years. She greets everyone personally, remembers details about their lives, and asks better questions than she answers. She doesn't mention the Harpers. She doesn't mention Mirt, except to note they've worked together. She is watching the PCs more carefully than she appears to be.
*Topics:* City arts patronage. A new aqueduct proposal the Lords are quietly fighting over. How Waterdeep has changed since the Time of Troubles. If a PC mentions any of the villain factions casually: she listens without reacting, then changes the subject — and finds them again later in the evening.

---

**Mirt** — *Harper primary contact. Currently three glasses in and enjoying himself.*
He's positioned near the wine and is perfectly happy to spend the evening there. He's watching the room from his post near the wine. If PCs check in with him, he nods toward whoever is interesting. He gives the mission briefing (find the impostor) privately when the moment allows, usually within the first thirty minutes.
*Topics:* He deflects most personal questions with a short story about someone else. He's unexpectedly knowledgeable about architecture and will genuinely engage on that. Ask about his past and he'll say something that sounds like a joke but isn't.

---

**Jarlaxle Baenre** *(as "Erystian Demarne, Luskan poet)* — *See Act 2 and the Bregan D'aerthe section.*

---

**Commander Saeth Cromley** — *Watch Commander, North Ward. Uncomfortable at parties.*
He's here because Remallia maintains careful relations with the Watch and always invites one or two commanders. Cromley is blunt, a little disheveled, and visibly relieved when anyone wants to talk about actual problems instead of art. He's been dealing with a series of warehouse break-ins in the Dock Ward — organized, professional, nothing taken that wasn't supposed to be moved. He thinks it's connected to a turf dispute but can't prove it.
*Topics:* The warehouse breaks-ins (Xanathar Guild moving product). Recent Dock Ward violence between two groups he can't identify ("one group dresses like merchants, fights like soldiers"). A Watch officer who went missing two months ago; body never found. He'll share operational details freely with anyone who seems like they might actually help.
*What he knows:* The missing officer was Corene Wyldath's contact at the Watch. He doesn't know that. He just knows the file is cold.

---

**Tessabrant Elamondra** — *Half-elf shipping merchant. Losing money and looking for quiet help.*
She runs a mid-tier trading company out of the Dock Ward. Three shipments in four months have been redirected — the goods arrive, but to the wrong buyers, who deny receiving them and whose paperwork is pristine. She suspects bribery somewhere in the chain but doesn't know where, and she can't go to the Watch without triggering an audit she can't afford. She's been quietly asking if anyone at the dinner knows anyone discreet.
*Topics:* Trade route disruptions (she'll describe the pattern without naming it as Guild activity, which it is). Dock Ward politics — which guild masters have gotten quietly wealthier. She'll mention she's heard the Cassalanters have been buying unusual quantities of a specific imported dye. (True. The dye is used in a ritual the PCs will encounter in Arc G.)
*What she wants:* Someone to track who is receiving her shipments. This is a potential side mission the DM can develop. It leads into the Xanathar Guild's Dock Ward operation.

---

**Lord Aldric Talmost** — *Minor noble, early thirties. Desperate to matter.*
He's from a family that was important two generations ago and isn't anymore. He knows it, and it makes him exhausting — he name-drops, implies connections he doesn't have, and hints at secrets he half-understands. He's heard fragments about "the vault situation" from someone at his club and has been working it into every conversation for a week.
*Topics:* He'll tell anyone who listens that "something big is happening" in the city's financial circles and that "certain families" are repositioning. He's vague because he doesn't actually know specifics. He does, however, mention that a Cassalanter cousin told him the family had a significant windfall coming — "not from trade." He thinks this makes him sound important. It's actually useful.
*What he knows:* The Cassalanters believe something is imminent. He doesn't know what. He can be pumped easily with flattery (DC 10 Charisma/Persuasion) or mild social pressure.

---

**Zalara Moonwhisper** — *Elven archivist. Attached to the Font of Knowledge.*
She's been researching historical Waterdhavian financial records for the past two years and is here because Remallia funds the archive. She has an archivist's mind — everything cross-referenced, nothing wasted. She's genuinely pleasant company if you can keep up with how fast she changes subjects.
*Topics:* The Font of Knowledge's collection. Historical Waterdhavian financial structures — how the Lords controlled liquidity in the city's early years. She will, if the conversation goes long enough, mention that she recently found records suggesting a significant sum was placed in trust under the Lords' authority over a century ago and never formally disbursed. She thinks it's a clerical error. It isn't.
*What she knows:* She has found a reference to the Vault of Dragons in an administrative record dated 1244 DR, filed under "extraordinary reserve, Lords' disposition." She doesn't know what it means. The PCs might. If they explain (or hint), she'll offer to pull the full document — which names the vault's ceremonial lock but not its location.

---

**Farrak Iltimer** — *Dwarf gemcutter, Trades Ward. No secrets. Genuinely enjoyable.*
He has the best stories of anyone in the room, none of them relevant to the campaign. He's at the dinner because Remallia bought a set of matching aquamarine pieces from him last year and they stayed friends. He sells to half the noble families in the city and treats them all the same — with cheerful disrespect that they find charming.
*Topics:* He'll tell a story about every family the PCs mention, usually unflattering and usually funny. He mentions in passing that the Cassalanters ordered a set of blood-red rubies last month — unusual cut, very specific weight. He doesn't know what they're for. "Twelve stones, all identical. Not for jewelry. Too heavy, wrong shape. Ritual pieces, if I had to guess. Not my business." He shrugs. It is the PCs' business.
*What he's useful for:* Comic relief, world texture, and that detail about the rubies (relevant to the Cassalanter infernal contract in Arc G). He'll be a friendly contact in the Trades Ward if treated well.

---

**Serithka Ondal** — *Tiefling composer. Zariel Fiendish Legacy. Nervous and perceptive.*
She composed a string quartet that premiered at Remallia's last salon and was invited back. She doesn't know anyone well and has spent most of the evening near the garden door, watching. She's observant in the way people are when they've spent a lot of time being watched themselves.
*Topics:* Music, obviously. The politics of cultural patronage in Waterdeep — who controls which venues, which composers are "approved." **If a Party Member Is Bregan D'aerthe** *(DM only)*

### Mission 5 — The Sleeping Asset

**Hook**
Mirt meets them at Trollskull Manor in person. No message beforehand. He comes in through the back door, which means he had a key copied at some point, and he sits down with a drink before explaining: a Harper informant named Corene Wyldath, a halfling woman embedded in Xanathar's Dock Ward operation for six months, stopped checking in three weeks ago. "I need to know if she's dead, captured, or something worse. And if it's something worse — I need you to handle it."

**Background** *(DM only)*
Corene was compromised by Nihiloor twelve days ago. An intellect devourer ate her brain. She's walking and talking and maintaining her cover, filing false reports that the Dock Ward operation is running normally. Nihiloor uses her as a surveillance asset; she's been attending Harper contact meetings and reporting everything back. She isn't a prisoner and won't appear distressed.

**Act 1 — The Search**
Three investigation beats to locate Corene:

- The Dock Ward harbormaster's assistant (a minor Harper sympathizer) saw her twice recently at Guild meetings she had no reason to attend as cover.
- Asking around the boarding houses where she used the assumed name "Halla Ironstave" finds her landlord: she paid three months in advance last week — not like her.

**Act 2 — The Encounter**
Corene is found at her regular meeting point — a specific bench in the Trades Ward plaza she uses for dead drops. She looks fine. She's alert, well-rested, and says all the right things about her absence: a Guild operation she had to stay close to, no opportunity to check in safely.

DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) catches the wrongness: her eye contact is too steady, her speech too considered. A DC 12 Intelligence (Arcana) or Wisdom (Medicine) check, made after sustained interaction, identifies signs of intellect devourer occupation — flat affect, absence of involuntary reactions, the faint smell of psychic residue.

If she realizes they're getting close, she tries to extract more information before tipping off her handler. She asks specific questions about the party's current plans and location.

Detect Thoughts, cast on her: the surface thoughts are disciplined, controlled — not Corene's fear and careful improvisation but a flat operational calm. Deeper: she is monitoring the PCs and reporting, and the thing thinking through her is aware of the spell.

**Act 3 — Resolution**
*Remove the devourer:* Requires locating it inside the skull (DC 15 Arcana check, assisted by Detect Thoughts or similar), then magically extracting it. Telekinesis is the most direct option — the devourer makes a DC 16 Strength saving throw to resist being pulled free; on a failure, it is expelled and immediately hostile. Banishment works if the caster succeeds on a DC 14 Arcana check to target the devourer rather than the host. Any spell that forcibly moves or displaces a Tiny creature within a confined space achieves the same result. If successful, the real Corene needs a Long Rest before she can be debriefed. What she knows: six months of Xanathar Guild Dock Ward operations, two guard captain names, and the layout of one distribution warehouse. The devourer's intelligence connection to Nihiloor is severed.

*Kill her:* Grim. Mirt accepts it without comment. The devourer-Corene reports a breach before dying — Nihiloor knows the Harpers are onto the deception. The Xanathar Guild goes to increased alert in the Dock Ward for two weeks.

*Leave her in place as a double agent:* The Harpers feed false information through her dead drop for the next tenday. Mirt can plant one false piece of intelligence into Nihiloor's awareness — guard patrol timing, a dummy meeting location, a name that doesn't exist. High risk: Nihiloor is intelligent and may detect the manipulation.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: Corene is extracted alive with the devourer removed. Her six months of Dock Ward intelligence goes to the Harpers intact; she can continue field work after recovery.
- +1: The double-agent option is chosen and Mirt confirms the planted intelligence reached Nihiloor. One false piece of tactical information is now inside Xanathar's lair before the PCs ever enter it.

**Aftermath**
Resolving this mission activates the Renown 30+ Harper benefit (informants in Xanathar's Guild). If Corene is saved or used as a double agent: the Harpers can provide one tactical intelligence package for Arc F — patrol schedule for one section of Xanathar's lair, or the location of Nihiloor's lab. If she's killed: the information is lost but the Splinter operation is revealed to Mirt, who passes it to Vajra Safahr. Force Grey takes a greater interest in Xanathar's lair.

### Mission 1 — The Long Watch
**Level 2 | 2 renown | No tangible reward**

**Hook**
Jalester meets the PCs at the Yawning Portal, picks a table far enough from the bar that the conversation won't carry, and gets to it: "A gang war is making the streets dangerous and the Guild workers nervous. We've offered protection to the Dungsweepers' Guild — specifically to their Dock Ward route coordinator, Mareva Coile. Three of her subordinates have been roughed up this week, and she's the next logical target. I need people willing to walk her route every day for a tenday. The Watch is stretched thin. This is real work." No recruitment speech. Six bells, Muleskull Tavern, Ship Street, Dock Ward.

**Background** *(DM only)*
The attacks on Dungsweepers' Guild workers aren't random gang violence. Xanathar Guild muscle is being fed Mareva's routing schedule by a guild insider: **Delvon Harsk**, a route supervisor who has been accepting small weekly payments from a Guild street agent named **Pirn** in exchange for timing information. Delvon isn't malicious — he's 300 gp in debt to a moneylender and desperate. He doesn't know what the Guild does with the information; he tells himself it's just scheduling data. The attacks have told him otherwise, but he's in too deep to stop without knowing who to confess to.

**Act 1 — The Muleskull Tavern**
Mareva Coile is 45, sharp-tempered, and skeptical of outside help. She manages the route as a coordinator — she's the supervisor who speaks to Jalester, holds the contracts, and keeps the paperwork straight. The crew the PCs actually escort is led by **Berna**, a dwarf woman of approximately sixty with a grey braid and the expression of someone who has lived entirely on her own terms. Twenty years with the guild, seventeen of them on the Dock and Trades Ward circuits. She is not rude; she is economical. She will not make small talk. She expects the party to stay close, look capable, and not get underfoot. She will soften over the tenday as she determines they are, in her assessment, not useless.

Mareva gives the PCs the supervisor rotation schedule and makes her concern clear: "If my workers stop showing up because they're scared, the city fills with filth in a week. That's worse than a black eye."

**The Tenday — Selected Days**

The first three days pass without incident. Use two or three of the following to texture the routine before the attack:

*Day 2:* Berna's crew is working near the Market when a City Watch officer stops them for a routine check. The officer treats them with undisguised contempt — the dungsweepers are beneath notice, their escort barely registers. Berna's jaw tightens but she says nothing. After the Watch officer leaves, she goes back to work without comment.

*Day 4:* While working through a Market alley, one of the sweepers — a young Calishite man named **Herath** — finds a coin purse containing 12 gp, apparently dropped by someone in a hurry. He turns it in to the nearest Watch post without discussion, then returns to work. He catches the party watching and shrugs. "It's not mine." That is the entirety of his moral framework on the subject.

*Day 6:* Over lunch at a corner stall, Berna talks unprompted about why the gang war is more dangerous for her workers than for most people. "We go into alleys nobody else goes into. We move slow. We carry equipment, not weapons. If something happens, the Watch isn't going to respond quickly for us." She is not complaining. She is briefing.

*Day 7:* A drunk man in the early morning tries to start an argument with one of the sweepers over whose guild has priority in a specific alley. This is an actual guild jurisdictional dispute unresolved for six years. Berna defuses it with four sentences, all of them technically accurate, and keeps moving.

On Day 4, a DC 13 Passive Perception during the morning route also notices a figure watching from a second-story window on Margaster Street — a young man who keeps appearing at different points along the route, always slightly ahead of them.

**Act 2 — Night Three**
On the third night, a Xanathar strike: three thugs (use Thug stat block) and a scout (Spy stat block) named **Pirn** attempt to waylay Mareva on her return from a late supervision check at the Dockside pumping station. The attack comes from a side alley on Shank Street, Dock Ward. Pirn stays back — he's observing, not fighting, and will flee if the thugs are dropped.

The thugs fight until two of their three number fall, then break and run south toward the harbor. Pirn, if not intercepted, slips into a service lane and disappears into the Dock Ward. He can be tracked with a DC 15 Survival check or pursued with a DC 14 Athletics check — he's fast.

If Pirn is captured, he gives up Delvon Harsk under pressure (DC 12 Charisma (Intimidation)) or for a promise of Watch leniency (DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) with that specific offer on the table).

**Act 3 — Finding the Source**
After the attack, the question is how the ambush was timed so precisely. Investigation options:

- **DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)** at the attack site: one of the thugs has a folded paper in his pocket — a rough schedule notated by ward and time, in handwriting that doesn't match any of the surviving Guild workers but does match one of the supervisor duty rosters.
- **DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) with Mareva**: she confirms only four route supervisors have access to the real-time schedule. She lists them: Delvon Harsk, Orryn Saltwick, Bremma Dunnet, and Cass Fildura.
- **DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) when questioning supervisors**: Delvon can't make eye contact when asked whether he's shared routing information with anyone outside the guild, and his answer is too long.

**Resolution**
*Delvon arrested and turned over to Watch:* Mareva is informed. She's quiet for a long moment, then says: "Make sure they know he's not a killer. Just an idiot." Delvon is charged with conspiracy and sentenced to three months' labor. His debt to the moneylender is restructured under Watch oversight. Pirn's arrest or escape determines whether the Xanathar Guild contact chain is severed.

*Delvon warned off but not arrested:* He quits the guild the next day and disappears into the Trades Ward. The attacks stop. Jalester accepts this outcome but notes it in his report.

*PCs never identify the insider:* The attacks continue through the tenday. Mareva's workers organize their own protection rotation by Day 7, and the detail becomes redundant. Jalester pays the renown but his assessment of the PCs drops one notch internally — he gives them harder assignments going forward.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: Mareva's workers complete the full tenday without another successful attack.
- +1: Delvon Harsk is identified and arrested, or Pirn is captured and his connection to Delvon exposed.

**Aftermath**
If Pirn is captured and questioned thoroughly, he can name the Xanathar Guild street agent who hired him: **Krentz** (appears later in Arc B's sewers sequence). If the PCs haven't yet encountered Krentz, this is a name they can bring to the Watch — which flags the Xanathar Guild's Dock Ward cell for increased Watch scrutiny during the first two arcs.

On the final day of the tenday, Herath presses a folded note into the party's hands as they part ways. It gives the location of a sewer access point in the Trades Ward that his crew uses — a back-route into the sewer system that bypasses the Watch checkpoint on Coin Alley. He offers it with a shrug. "In case you ever need to move fast." He is not a Harper asset or an Alliance contact. He is a person who was helped and is returning the favor in the only currency he has.

### Mission 2 — The Exile's Return
**Level 3 | 2 renown | If Harko is taken alive: a sealed Thayan intelligence file (see Aftermath)**

**Hook**
The message arrives with a Watch seal, not a paper bird. Jalester's handwriting is precise: *"An exiled adventurer named Harko Swornhold has returned to Waterdeep illegally. **Background** *(DM only)*
Harko Swornhold (use Bandit Captain stat block) is not simply a Xanathar thug. He's playing two sides. He's working as a low-level incitement organizer for the Xanathar Guild, but a Manshoon Splinter agent named **Veralax** has also approached him — offering to use him as a communications cutout between the Splinter and Xanathar Guild street soldiers in the Dock Ward. Harko accepted the second offer as insurance. He hasn't told the Xanathar Guild about Veralax. If cornered, he'll offer both facts as a trade: Veralax's identity, meeting location, and a physical description.

Harko's two **kenku** companions (use the Kenku stat block) serve as his scouts. They're posted at the entrances to the warehouse he's using and communicate warnings through mimicked door-knock patterns.

**Act 1 — Three Days of Looking**
Locating Harko requires investigation. Each check phase represents eight hours of work. Three successes before three failures locates the warehouse; accumulating three failures means the kenku spot the PCs before they spot Harko and he moves.

- **Phase 1 — DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)** (Dock Ward contacts, taverns, Guild whisper networks): a ship chandler on Sail Street mentions that someone has been buying "extra portions" at the Dock Ward food stalls for the past tenday — enough for a group of four or five, always picked up by the same beak-faced buyer. Kenku are not common in the Dock Ward. The stalls are near Mudwagon Row.
- **Phase 2 — DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)** (Mudwagon Row): the block has a derelict rope warehouse, shuttered for two years. A neighbor has heard scuffling and low conversation through the wall at night.
- **Phase 3 — DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)** (surveillance): Watching the warehouse from the building across the lane confirms two kenku sentry positions — one at the street-facing alley mouth, one at the back service door. Harko moves between them at irregular intervals. A DC 12 Dexterity (Stealth) check avoids being spotted during this observation.

*If three failures accumulate:* The kenku spot the tail and mimic a coded warning knock at both doors. Harko moves to a backup location: the cellar of a closed cooperage on Wharf Mast Lane, three blocks east. The same investigation structure applies to finding him there (DCs each drop by 2, since he moved in haste).

**Act 2 — The Warehouse**
The rope warehouse has two entry points: the main street door (barred from inside; DC 15 Strength to force or DC 13 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) to unlock the side hatch) and the back service door (unlocked but squeaky; DC 12 Dexterity (Stealth) to open silently).

Inside: Harko, his two kenku, and two Bandit followers (use Bandit stat block). Harko fights if cornered but calls for surrender the moment he's bloodied (at or below half HP) — he doesn't want to die for either faction paying him.

If combat breaks out, the kenku immediately retreat to the street and begin mimicking a Watch whistle pattern they've memorized — an alarm that will bring a Watch patrol in four rounds. The PCs have that long to resolve the encounter before outside attention arrives.

**The Witness**
Whether Harko is dead or captured, the kenku flee the moment the fight breaks. The alley behind the rope warehouse goes quiet. Then: rapid footsteps from the alley entrance. A young Calishite woman — early twenties, wearing a server's apron, carrying a tray she clearly just set down somewhere — rounds the corner and stops dead.

**Lira** works evenings at the Muleskull Tavern. She came out the back door. She looks at Harko, then at the party, then at Harko again. She is not a Xanathar Guild plant or a Splinter watcher. She is a person who has just processed a great deal of information and is still working on what to do with it.

*If the party is immediately honest:* She is frightened, but responds to directness. She knew Harko — he had been drinking at the Muleskull for two weeks, always with the kenku, always quiet, never trouble inside. She is not going to go to the Watch tonight. "He wasn't a good person," she says. "But that's not the same as this being right." She leaves. She decides the following morning. Whether she files a Watch report depends on what she concluded in that time, and on how the party handled the sixty seconds after she appeared.

*If the party lies or threatens:* She says nothing and leaves. The Watch receives a report the next morning noting a body in the alley and a description of the party. Jalester receives it, sighs, and says: "I said quietly." He does not formally discipline the party — there is no official mission record — but he notes the competence gap. He will give them harder assignments going forward.

Jalester's order to do this "quietly" was not just about avoiding Watch attention. It was a test of whether the party can operate at the level the Alliance needs. Lira's presence is not a trap; it is the reality of working in a city full of people. The party that handles her with honesty and care is the party the Alliance wants. The party that doesn't will still complete the mission — but Jalester will notice.

**Act 3 — The Deal**
If Harko is captured or surrenders, he offers information in exchange for Watch custody over execution:

"I've been running messages between two handlers who don't know about each other. One works for Xanathar. The other one — the one who found me two weeks after I got back into the city — goes by Veralax. Dresses like a gem merchant. Meets at the Bent Nail alehouse, Dock Ward, every Highsun. Middle booth. He's Zhentarim — the bad kind, not the ones who do business. He's trying to use me to pass information between his people and the Xanathar street crews. I've been taking his coin but I haven't set up anything yet."

This information is: accurate, verifiable, and actionable. Veralax is a Manshoon Splinter fixer; the Bent Nail meeting is real. If the PCs act on it and report to Jalester, it seeds a Splinter thread the LA can pursue in Arc E.

**Resolution**
*Harko killed:* Mission fulfilled. The Splinter connection is lost unless the PCs found written evidence in his belongings (DC 14 Investigation of the warehouse: a scrap with "Veralax, Bent Nail, Highsun, middle booth" in Harko's handwriting).

*Harko taken to Watch:* He gives his full statement. Jalester receives the sealed intelligence file on the Splinter/Xanathar communication attempt. The Bent Nail address goes to Alliance Watch contacts for surveillance.

*Harko released for information:* Harko leaves Waterdeep within 24 hours. The Veralax information is good, but the LA's mission objective (Harko's removal) is partially fulfilled. Jalester accepts it and files the assessment.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: Harko is eliminated or handed to Watch custody. He is no longer an active incitement vector in the Dock Ward.
- +1: Veralax is identified (from Harko's statement or the warehouse evidence) and the Bent Nail address is reported to Jalester before the end of the arc.

**Aftermath**
If Veralax's address reaches Jalester: Watch surveillance identifies Veralax as a Manshoon Splinter operative. He's picked up two weeks later. His arrest removes one Splinter communication channel in the Dock Ward — the Splinter response team in Arc E is missing one of its usual liaisons. If the PCs encounter the Arc E response team, one position in their ranks is noticeably understaffed.

If the Thayan intelligence file is recovered: it contains documentation of Esloon Bezant's history — his failed coup in Thay, his arrival in Waterdeep, and a list of associates. This file arrives in Jalester's hands and reduces the DC to locate Esloon in Mission 3 by 2 (the file has a last-known associate in the Dock Ward who can be approached directly).

### Mission 3 — The Red Wizard's Price
**Level 4 | 3 renown | No tangible reward**

**Hook**
Jalester finds the PCs at Trollskull Manor this time — arrives at noon, declines to sit. He's moving faster than usual. "The Zhentarim — not Davil's people, the other ones — are courting a Red Wizard named Esloon Bezant. Former Thayan, probably exiled, has a crew of eight he calls contractors. They're negotiating right now. The deal is muscle for an operation we don't fully understand yet, but the Zhentarim faction running this isn't the kind you negotiate with. I need the deal killed tonight. I don't care how."

**Background** *(DM only)*
Esloon Bezant (use Mage stat block, Chaotic Neutral, with the following modification: his Arcane Recovery is replaced by a once-per-day ability to cast *Counterspell* as a Reaction without using a spell slot) left Thay four years ago after a failed coup against his chapter lord. He has a warrant outstanding with the Thayan embassy in Baldur's Gate, but no active warrant in Waterdeep. He built a crew of six thugs and two veterans in the Dock Ward who know better than to ask about his past.

The Splinter fixer meeting him is **Sarvos Miln** (use Spy stat block), a Manshoon operative who handles contractor recruitment in the Dock Ward. The operation Sarvos is recruiting for: a coordinated strike on a Watch checkpoint in the Dock Ward that would create a window for a large cargo movement — the cargo being a Splinter intelligence package bound for a Manshoon associate in Baldur's Gate. The meeting is at the Skewered Dragon Tavern, a private dining room booked under the name "Holvar." The meeting started an hour before Jalester briefed the PCs.

**Act 1 — Finding the Meet**
Jalester has partial intelligence: the Skewered Dragon, tonight. He doesn't know which room or when it ends.

- **DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) with the Skewered Dragon's taproom host:** The "Holvar" booking is in the back, the corner room. The host mentions two people arrived separately — "a foreign-looking gentleman in a merchant's coat, and a broad man with a signet ring." The meeting is still ongoing.
- **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) at the taproom entrance:** A Splinter watcher (Bandit stat block) is nursing a drink near the door — positioned to observe who comes in looking for the Holvar table.
- **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation) of the building exterior:** A second exit from the private dining room opens onto Dock Lane — a separate street-facing door, currently bolted from inside.

**Act 2 — Options for Scuttling the Deal**
The PCs must choose an approach before the meeting ends (they have roughly 30 minutes from when they arrive):

*Disrupt the meeting directly:* Entering the private room creates an immediate confrontation. Esloon's two veterans are seated outside the dining room door as guards. Sarvos will flee through the Dock Lane exit if the confrontation turns violent; Esloon will fight.

*Expose Esloon to Watch:* Presenting Jalester's intelligence file (from Mission 2's Aftermath, if obtained) to a Watch patrol gives them cause to detain Esloon — the Thayan warrant isn't active in Waterdeep, but the file's contents justify a holding inquiry. A Watch patrol responds in three rounds. If Esloon is detained, Sarvos leaves without a deal and the Splinter loses this avenue.

*Turn Esloon against the deal:* Reaching Esloon before the meeting concludes (DC 15 Dexterity (Stealth) through the taproom kitchen to approach before Sarvos finishes his pitch) opens a negotiation. Esloon is a pragmatist who has survived by correctly reading which side will win. Offering him something the Splinter can't — specifically, the Alliance's ability to quash any future Thayan embassy inquiries — requires a DC 16 Charisma (Persuasion) check. On a success, Esloon declines the deal and departs. He doesn't become an Alliance asset, but he removes himself from the equation.

*Neutralize Sarvos:* Capturing or following Sarvos instead of Esloon collapses the deal from the other direction. Sarvos can be intercepted on the way in or out. If captured, he will not give up Manshoon's identity but will confirm the cargo operation — giving Jalester actionable intelligence about the Watch checkpoint strike. If Sarvos is taken to Watch custody, the operation is exposed and the Splinter loses its Dock Ward cargo window.

**What Esloon Actually Wants**
Any approach that involves directly engaging Esloon — turning him against the deal, confronting him outright, or using the session to negotiate — may open this scene. After the initial negotiation, whatever its form, Esloon leans back.

"I want to ask you something. Not about the deal." He looks at the table for a moment. "I fled Thay because they were going to kill me. Not because I had done something wrong by their standards — I had done something right, which amounted to the same thing." He meets the party's eyes. "I have been in this city for five years. I have built something here. Not legitimate. But real."

"The Zhentarim offered me protection. Real protection — not the Alliance's kind, where you exist at their convenience. I am considering it because it is the only serious offer I have received."

He looks at them directly. "Is there another offer? Not from you personally. From whoever decides these things."

This is not a rhetorical challenge. He is asking whether there is a path in Waterdeep that does not end with him being used and discarded by whoever is currently convenient. The PCs can choose to bring this question back to Jalester. Jalester's response: a long pause, then: "He's not wrong that we're asking him for a favor without offering much. I'll look into whether there's something we can formalize." This is not a commitment. But it is Jalester acknowledging the point — and if the PCs followed through, Esloon's attitude toward the Alliance shifts from neutral to cautiously observant. He does not become an asset. But he stops being a liability.

**Act 3 — Resolution**
The deal is scuttled if any of the following is true: Esloon is detained or withdraws from negotiations; Sarvos is captured or neutralized; the meeting is disrupted before it concludes; or the Watch checkpoint operation is exposed.

If none of these occur before the meeting ends: Esloon and Sarvos shake hands in the private room and part through different exits. The Splinter gains eight additional pieces of muscle for the checkpoint strike. In Arc E, the Dock Ward Splinter response team has +2 members when the PCs encounter it.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: The deal is scuttled by any method before it concludes.
- +1: Sarvos Miln is captured and identifies the Watch checkpoint as the Splinter's target — Jalester can pre-position Watch reinforcements, and the checkpoint strike fails when it occurs in Arc E.

**Aftermath**
If Esloon Bezant turned away: he remains in the Dock Ward with his crew, nominally independent. He resurfaces in Arc E as a possible hire for any faction with coin — the PCs may encounter him working for Xanathar Guild or as a freelance complication.

If Esloon detained: he's held for ten days and released without charge (the Thayan warrant doesn't survive the embassy review process). He leaves Waterdeep. His crew disperses.

If the checkpoint strike is exposed: it occurs anyway in Arc E, but fails — the Splinter loses the cargo window and delays their Baldur's Gate communication by three weeks. One Splinter operation in Arc E is noticeably disrupted.

### Mission 4 — The Archer Above
**Level 5 | 3 renown | Cassalanter intelligence file (see Aftermath)**

**Hook**
Jalester finds the PCs with the short version: "We have a sniper problem. Three dead in the North Ward and Sea Ward transition — all killed with arrows from above, none of the bodies connected to any active faction operation. The Watch is overwhelmed. My contact says he was spotted going to ground near Trollskull Alley. I need him found and arrested. Not killed — killing him makes this worse. Alive, he tells us who hired him."

**Background** *(DM only)*
The assassin is **Vhaspar Holmbridge**, 57, a retired City Watch sharpshooter who served in the Dock Ward for twenty years. He was hired through a Cassalanter household intermediary named **Halek Farran** — a steward who handles sensitive errands for the Cassalanter family. The three victims are not faction operatives. They are former witnesses to a money transfer six weeks ago in which a Cassalanter-hired fence paid a Xanathar Guild broker for a quantity of ritual components (the components connect to Arc G's infernal timeline). The victims saw the transfer by accident; Halek was tasked with closing that loose end.

Vhaspar doesn't know who hired Halek, and Halek doesn't know the full scope of the Cassalanter operation. Vhaspar took the contract for 400 gp. He's not ideologically motivated and will cooperate with Watch custody if it means he doesn't die. He has one more name on his list that he hasn't acted on yet.

Vhaspar is living in a rented room above **Yander's Feed Store**, Pell Street, North Ward — one block from Trollskull Alley.

**Act 1 — Investigation**
Finding Vhaspar:

- **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** (canvassing the North Ward, asking about a man watching rooftops or departing buildings at unusual hours): A linen merchant on Tarnath Street saw "a grey-haired man who walks like a soldier" checking in at Yander's Feed Store a week ago with a long rolled canvas under his arm. He came back at odd hours.
- **DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) with the feed store's proprietor, Yander Coirn:** He confirms the man — gave the name "Belvor." Paid a tenday up front. Room is the top floor, north-facing window.
- **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception)** while watching the building from across Pell Street: the north-facing window is slightly open, the shutter angled to provide a line of sight while leaving the interior in shadow.

**Act 2 — The Room**
Confronting Vhaspar requires getting into the feed store and up to the top floor without triggering his exit protocol. He has a length of rope tied to the window latch — if the door to his room is forced or opened without warning, he exits through the window and onto the roof.

*Approach quietly (DC 13 Dexterity (Stealth) on the stairs):* Vhaspar is caught at his table, reviewing a sketch of his remaining target's walking route. He's alone, no weapon drawn. He raises his hands.

*Approach loudly or force the door:* He goes out the window. Catching him on the rooftops requires a DC 15 Athletics chase sequence (three checks, one success ends the chase; two failures means he reaches a pre-arranged rope drop to a back lane and escapes to a secondary location: the Skewered Dragon's cellar storeroom, Dock Ward).

Once caught, Vhaspar surrenders without further resistance. He says, quietly: "The name I was given for the last one — I haven't used it. You want to keep it that way, get me in front of someone who can guarantee my safety."

**Act 3 — The Interrogation**
In Watch custody or while the PCs hold him:

Vhaspar gives up Halek Farran — the Cassalanter steward who hired him — without needing a Persuasion check, provided he's promised Watch protection (Jalester can make this promise credibly). He confirms the hiring was through three layers of intermediary: a hire agency, a note, a drop box. He met Halek once, briefly, in a coffeehouse on Chandler Street, Sea Ward. He describes him: "Fifties. Neat. Smelled like cedar. Had a signet ring, kept turning it inward — the crest was facing his palm."

In Vhaspar's belongings: a sketch of his fourth target (a Dock Ward longshoreman), a partial address on a scrap of paper reading *"...ilanter Villa, Sea Ward, side entrance gate"*, and 320 gp remaining from his payment. A **DC 15 Intelligence (Arcana or Investigation)** check connecting the partial address to the known Cassalanter family location in the Sea Ward identifies it as Cassalanter Villa.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: Vhaspar is arrested alive and provides testimony identifying Halek Farran.
- +1: The partial address is identified as Cassalanter Villa and reported to Jalester before Arc G begins.

**Aftermath**
Jalester passes the Halek Farran lead and the Cassalanter Villa address to the Watch's Criminal Intelligence division. Within a week, they compile a basic dossier: Halek Farran's name, the Watch guard rotation at Cassalanter Villa's service entrance, and a sketch of the villa's walled exterior. This dossier becomes part of the LA's Arc G intelligence package (see Arc Hooks above). If the PCs are planning the Cassalanter heist, it gives them advance knowledge of the service gate timing before they conduct their own reconnaissance.

If Vhaspar was killed: the testimony is lost. Halek Farran cleans house — he disappears from Waterdeep within 48 hours. The dossier is never compiled, and the Cassalanter Villa service entrance information is not available through Alliance channels.

### Mission 5 — Fentree's Ledger
**Level 6 | 4 renown | Masked Lord's writ access (see Aftermath)**

**Hook**
Jalester sends a message to Trollskull Manor by regular courier — not a Watch seal, not a paper bird, just a folded letter with his name at the bottom. It's more formal than his usual style: *"An old trade investigator named Orvane Fentree was found dead in his home in the Trades Ward on Greengrass morning. The Watch has ruled it natural causes. His personal records — twenty years of guild and contract documentation — are missing from his study. Laeral Silverhand has a particular interest in those records. Find them before anyone else reads them."*

**Background** *(DM only)*
Orvane Fentree, 71, spent two decades tracking guild financial irregularities for the City of Waterdeep. He retired seven years ago but continued maintaining his private ledger out of habit. That ledger contains entries from 1244 DR showing a Neverember-linked shell company — Dunberton Holdings — still paying maintenance access fees to a vault custodian in the Waterdeep Vault district. If correctly interpreted, the entries prove the Vault of Dragons is active and located within Waterdeep — not Neverwinter. Laeral's intelligence has been wrong for years.

Fentree died of a genuine heart attack. His ledger was stolen by **Rezka** (use Spy stat block), a Xanathar Guild operative who had been assigned to shadow him after a guild analyst flagged Fentree as a potential threat to their interest in the Grand Game. Rezka searched the home while the Watch was still on scene, pocketed the ledger, and left before anyone noted her.

Rezka is now in a bidding situation of her own creation. She approached two buyers: **Noska Ur'gray** (a Xanathar Guild enforcer — use the Thug stat block, veteran training) representing the guild's senior intelligence cell, and a Cassalanter household representative named **Emira Volstren** (use the Noble stat block). Noska can pay 600 gp. The Cassalanters have offered 400 gp and a favor. Rezka is meeting with both tonight at the **Green Dragon Inn**, Bridge Street, Trades Ward — she's running them in adjacent rooms, planning to sell to the highest offer.

**Act 1 — Finding Rezka**
The investigation begins at Fentree's home, Copper Street, Trades Ward.

- **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** of the study: the dust on the shelves is disturbed in a consistent pattern — someone knew where to look. Three ledger bindings are present on the upper shelf but the fourth spine slot is empty and clean, meaning the missing volume was pulled within the last two days. A single copper button on the floor near the window doesn't match any of Fentree's clothing.
- **DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) with Fentree's neighbor, Orlass Hemmet:** She saw "a red-haired woman, thirties, practical clothes" leaving Fentree's building the morning he died. She was carrying a satchel when she arrived; it looked heavier when she left.
- **DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)** (Trades Ward contacts, asking about Rezka by physical description): a Caravan Court money-changer confirms she's a freelance information broker, seen regularly in the counting house quarter. She booked a private room at the Green Dragon Inn for tonight under the name "Torval."

**Act 2 — The Green Dragon Inn**
Rezka's plan: meet Noska at second bell in Room 4, Cassalanter representative Emira at third bell in Room 6. She's in Room 3 herself, reviewing the ledger, until the first meeting.

The Green Dragon's layout: ground floor taproom, single staircase, four rooms on the upper floor accessible from one corridor. The innkeeper, **Pravin Cloth**, is incurious about guests and their business.

Arriving before second bell: Noska is downstairs with two bodyguards (Bandit stat block each) at a corner table. Emira Volstren arrives five minutes later, alone but with a Cassalanter house guard waiting outside.

Options:
- *Take Rezka before either meeting begins (DC 14 Dexterity (Stealth) approach to Room 3):* She surrenders without fight if caught unprepared with the ledger in her hands. She'll trade the ledger for safe exit from Waterdeep — the Xanathar Guild will come for her when the sale falls through, and she knows it. The PCs can choose to take the ledger outright or honor the trade.
- *Disrupt the meetings:* Starting a confrontation in the taproom draws Noska and Emira's attention to each other — a DC 14 Charisma (Deception) check can be used to convince each that the other has already made a deal, collapsing the bidding competition. This delays but does not prevent Rezka from rescheduling.
- *Wait and intercept after:* If the PCs let the bidding conclude, Noska wins at 600 gp. Rezka hands over the ledger in Room 4 at second bell and thirty minutes. The PCs must intercept Noska and his bodyguards in the street or on the staircase — three against three, plus Noska himself.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: The ledger reaches Jalester within 24 hours of recovery. Laeral's intelligence is updated.
- +1: Noska Ur'gray is captured or the Cassalanter representative Emira Volstren is identified by name and reported to Jalester — either confirms external interest in the vault's location.

**Aftermath**
If the ledger reaches Jalester: he passes it directly to Laeral. Her intelligence corrects — she now knows the Vault of Dragons is in Waterdeep, active, and that Dunberton Holdings was the shell structure used to maintain it. She accelerates her political preparation. Mission 6 becomes available.

If the ledger goes to Noska: the Xanathar Guild has confirmation of the vault's Waterdeep location before the PCs have the Stone of Golorr deciphered. In Arc F-G, the Xanathar Guild's intelligence about the vault's ward structure is one step ahead of the PCs. Xanathar's faction response team in Arc F has advance knowledge that a vault access attempt is coming.

If Emira Volstren gets it: the Cassalanters share it with their infernal advisors. In Arc G-H, the Cassalanters arrive at the vault approach with a partial map of the ceremonial lock's location.

### Mission 6 — An Audience with the Open Lord
**Level 7 | 4 renown | Masked Lord's Writ (see Aftermath)**

> **Design note — Trigger:** This mission activates only if Mission 5 succeeded and the ledger reached Laeral. If Mission 5 failed, replace the Hook with: *Jalester arrives at Trollskull Manor and tells the PCs that Laeral Silverhand wants to speak with them directly. She has heard about their work. She has questions only firsthand witnesses can answer.* The audience scene (Act 2 onward) runs the same from that point.

**Hook**
Two City Watch officers in full dress uniform arrive at Trollskull Manor in the morning with a sealed summons on Waterdeep's formal vellum. The Open Lord Laeral Silverhand requests the presence of the adventuring company at Piergeiron's Palace at the close of the afternoon bell. Attendance is characterized as voluntary. The summons is signed.

**Background** *(DM only)*
Laeral has been building toward this moment since the ledger arrived. She now knows the vault is in Waterdeep, that the Stone of Golorr has resurfaced, and that at least four factions are competing for access to it. She does not know the current location of the Stone. She does not know the PCs' exact position in the Grand Game, though she suspects they're closer to it than anyone officially sanctioned.

She intends to offer them a Masked Lord's Writ — a legal instrument that authorizes the vault operation and gives the PCs formal standing as city agents during Arc J. Without the writ, opening the vault is technically trespassing on a sealed Lords' estate. With it, the Watch stands down, the Vault district gate is opened, and any legal complication from Arc J is handled through official channels.

She will not give this writ freely. She wants three things: the current holder of the Stone (whatever they can tell her), confirmation that the gold will reach the city rather than a faction, and the PCs' judgment on a specific political question she is weighing.

**The Political Question:** One of the villain factions has approached Laeral with a deal. The specific offer: Manshoon's Splinter has offered Laeral a complete accounting of the Masked Lords — every sitting identity he has been able to confirm — in exchange for permission to take 20% of the vault's contents before it reaches the treasury. She is genuinely considering it. The list would give her political leverage over opponents within the Lords' structure who have been obstructing her recovery efforts — and she doesn't intend to let distaste for Manshoon get in the way of that.

She discloses this to the PCs openly. She does not present it as a test. It is, in fact, a test.

**Act 1 — Piergeiron's Palace**
The palace is everything it should be — clean marble, Watch officers at every door, a functionary who leads the PCs through three corridors and a waiting room to a private audience chamber with a long table and a fire that doesn't need tending. Laeral is already there.

Read aloud:
> The Open Lord of Waterdeep doesn't look like a statue come to life. She looks like a woman who has been working since before you woke up. She's sitting at the far end of the table with a cup of tea she isn't drinking, a small stack of letters she isn't reading, and the expression of someone who has already thought about this meeting from your side of it. When you enter, she sets the cup down. "Sit. I don't have a long speech for you. I have questions."

Laeral asks three questions:
1. "Who has the Stone of Golorr right now?"
2. "What do the other factions intend to do with the gold when the vault is opened?"
3. "I've been offered something. I'd like to know what you think before I decide."

She listens. She does not interrupt. She takes notes in a small book.

**Act 2 — The Manshoon Offer**
Laeral presents the Splinter offer plainly: "Manshoon's faction has offered me a complete accounting of the Masked Lords — every sitting identity he has confirmed — in exchange for permission to remove twenty percent of the vault's contents before it reaches the city. I want to know if you think I should accept."

She adds: "Don't tell me what you think I want to hear. I have enough of that."

*If the PCs argue against the offer with a specific reason (DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) or a DC 13 Intelligence check to construct a convincing strategic argument):* Laeral considers for a long moment. "You're right that accepting it creates a precedent I'll regret." She declines the offer.

*If the PCs have no opinion, or argue for accepting it:* Laeral makes the deal with the Splinter. In Arc J, Manshoon holds a piece of political leverage — he can threaten to expose one Masked Lord identity publicly unless the PCs give him a concession during the vault endgame. Laeral will not be embarrassed about her choice, but she won't help them manage its consequences.

*If the PCs lie about the Stone's location or the factions' intentions (DC 16 Wisdom (Insight) on Laeral's part):* She doesn't call them on it at the table. After the meeting, the writ is still issued, but Jalester receives a private note from her that reads: "Keep an eye on them."

**Act 3 — The Writ**
Laeral rises and retrieves a sealed document from a locked drawer. The Masked Lord's Writ authorizes the party by name to enter the Vault of Dragons district and access the ceremonial lock under the authority of the Open Lord of Waterdeep. It includes a provision for Watch non-interference during the operation.

"The gold goes to the treasury. All of it. If you come out with it and take it somewhere else, the writ is void and what happens next is between you and the Lords." She looks at each of them once. "Good luck."

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: The PCs provide accurate information about the Stone's location and at least two factions' intentions during the audience.
- +1: The PCs successfully argue against the Manshoon offer, and Laeral declines it.

**Aftermath**
The Masked Lord's Writ gives the PCs a legal instrument for Arc J. City Watch officers who challenge them at the Vault district gate defer to it; the gate is opened within four rounds of presentation. One legal intervention is available during Arc J: Laeral will authorize Watch action on the PCs' behalf once — used to deal with a rival faction complication, a legal challenge, or an NPC arrest.

If the Manshoon offer was rejected: Manshoon's political leverage play in Arc J does not occur. The Splinter does not have a card to play at the vault.

If the Manshoon offer was accepted: In Arc J, Manshoon holds specific leverage — he can delay Watch cooperation with the PCs for up to two hours by activating a political contact, or threaten to expose one Masked Lord to the public press. The PCs may be forced to negotiate with him rather than fight him straight through Arc I.

If Jalester Silvermane was identified as Illuun-compromised (Harper Mission 6): any intelligence the PCs shared with him during this mission — including the Stone's location — has potentially reached Illuun. Laeral does not know about the compromise. The PCs face a choice: tell her now (she removes Jalester from active duty immediately, which disrupts LA operations in Arc J) or handle it themselves.

## Emerald Enclave

*Contact: Melannor Fellbranch (primary). Jeryth Phaulkon is the estate's patron spirit — present, aware, and occasionally intervening directly, but not a mission dispatcher. She communicates through the garden itself.*

*Melannor delivers missions by animal messenger — typically a pigeon that lands on a windowsill, speaks the briefing in his calm baritone, and departs. Urgent messages arrive through whichever creature happens to be nearby. He appears in person only when the situation requires it, which happens more often as the campaign progresses.*

### The Grand Game — Emerald Enclave Stance

The Emerald Enclave has no interest in the vault gold. Jeryth Phaulkon is explicit about this when it comes up: the gold belonged to Waterdeep before Lord Neverember took it, and will belong to Waterdeep again — that question is for the Lords to settle. What the Enclave cares about is what is moving underneath all of it.

Jeryth has been aware of a psychic disturbance in the earth beneath Waterdeep's Castle Ward since before the campaign begins. She calls it a dreaming presence — old, patient, and hungry. When the Stone of Golorr is activated, the resonance she has been tracking strengthens. She knows this is connected. She does not know what it is or where it is anchored, but the Brandath crypts feel wrong to her in the specific way that only very old, very wrong things feel wrong.

The Enclave's Grand Game goal is containment: whatever the Vault of Dragons wakes up, Jeryth wants it handled.

**What the Enclave shares proactively:**
- Phaulkonmere as a safe haven from day 1 — neutral ground no faction will openly violate, no questions asked
- Animal messenger communications anywhere in Waterdeep at Renown 3+
- Jeryth's sense of the dreamer's activity — she can tell the PCs when the underground disturbance intensifies (corresponding to Illuun's influence reaching upward), relevant from Arc C onward

**What the Enclave asks for:**
- Reports on aberrant creature activity — where intellect devourers are operating, what new creatures Xanathar has released, whether any mind-touched citizens have been encountered
- That the Stone of Golorr not remain in Waterdeep longer than necessary — its activation is pulling at something below the city
- One thing from Arc J: 24 hours' notice before the vault is opened. Jeryth wants time to prepare wards.

### Mission 1 — The Undercliff Scarecrows
**Level 2 | 2 renown | No tangible reward**

**Hook**
A pigeon lands on a windowsill at Trollskull Manor one morning, clears its throat with the deliberate clarity of a trained bird, and speaks in Melannor's calm baritone: "Animated scarecrows are terrorizing farms east of the city walls. Three of them. Livestock have been attacked and a farmer was struck unconscious. The City Guard is occupied with the gang war and considers animated agricultural fixtures beneath their threshold. Something must be done." The pigeon departs.

**Background** *(DM only)*
A Manshoon Splinter arcanist used the Undercliff as a testing ground for an animation ritual last tenday. The ritual succeeded beyond his intentions — all three scarecrows became fully animated and hostile — and he left before dealing with the results. Each scarecrow is independently mobile and has no fixed location; they move through the Undercliff's terraced farmland at irregular intervals, responding to movement, light, and the smell of animals.

**Act 1 — The Undercliff**
The farming terraces east of Waterdeep's walls, accessible through Trollgate. Spring: green, actively worked, smelling of earth and cold harbor air. When the PCs arrive, the birds are thin on the ground. Farms show abandoned work — tools dropped mid-task, a garden gate left open.

The three scarecrows, each with its own territory and behavioral pattern:
- *The Sackcloth One* (northern fields): moves mostly at dusk, attacks livestock rather than people.
- *The Pumpkin Head* (central orchards): operates at night, pursues anything that runs — horses, dogs, people moving quickly.
- *The Blanket* (southern terraces near Trollgate road): the most recently animated; establishes territorial positions near the road and attacks anyone who approaches its station.

**Act 2 — The Farmer**
**Gerrick Goodbarrel** — a halfling of fifty with a pitchfork and an expression of pure, settled impatience — is standing alone in his north field. He has been watching the scarecrows' patterns for three days and has decided he has waited long enough. He does not ask for help so much as update the PCs on his tactical assessment.

He knows the streambed running east-west through the middle terraces creates a natural funnel — anything moving north to south has to cross it. "If you could set something in that streambed," he says, not quite asking. A DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) check, or Gerrick's own input, allows the party to predict the next appearance point for each scarecrow.

Gerrick also knows, because he has farmed this land for thirty years, that the Undercliff has an old drainage tunnel sealed below the southern terraces — brick-lined, leads east toward the cisterns under the Castle Ward. He mentions it matter-of-factly, as one piece of local geography among others, with the detail that it was sealed six years ago after "something came up through it." The PCs may or may not register this now.

**Act 3 — The Hunt**
Each scarecrow encounter takes place in terrain suited to its habits. The Pumpkin Head pursues fleeing characters and should trigger at least one foot chase through orchard rows. The Sackcloth One targets horses or mounts first; characters with animals need to account for this. The Blanket cannot be snuck up on (supernatural senses) but can be drawn into the open with a light source.

Fire works — scarecrows are combustible — but risks spreading to dry fields in open terrain. A contained fire tactic is valid; an uncontrolled fire damages at least one farm and Gerrick notices.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: All three scarecrows are destroyed before any additional livestock or people are hurt.
- +1: The Splinter arcanist's testing site is found (a chalk animation circle and a discarded journal page describing the ritual) and reported to Melannor — confirming these were intentional, not natural manifestations.

**Aftermath**
Gerrick owes the party a favor. He gives them the exact location of the drainage tunnel — bricked entry near the southern terraces, leads east, connects to the Castle Ward cistern access — and notes it was sealed when "something came up through it." He never learned what. The tunnel is not immediately useful. It becomes the access point for Missions 5 and 6.

### Mission 2 — Ten Nights in the City of the Dead
**Level 3 | 2 renown | 100 gp per character who completed all ten nights (from the Enclave)**

**Hook**
A crow this time, which Melannor later explains is Jeryth's choice of messenger, meaning she considers this urgent. The briefing: "Bones are going missing from the City of the Dead. Someone is raising skeletons. Sir Ambrose Everdawn, a champion of Kelemvor, has been asked to patrol the southern half for ten nights. He needs someone to cover the northern section. Jeryth says go." A pause in the crow's delivery, as if a second thought was added: "She said it three times. I mention this because she doesn't usually repeat herself."

**Background** *(DM only)*
The skeletons are being animated by a rogue necromancer — never identified, never caught, not the point of this mission. They consistently move toward and return from one location: the **Brandath family crypt** in the northern section. The Vault of Dragons is beneath the Brandath crypts. Sir Ambrose has been aware of a psychic wrongness in that section of the cemetery for months — something old, contained, and restless. He does not know what it is. Jeryth has been tracking the same disturbance through Waterdeep's underground water table and ley lines. Neither has named it yet. The skeletons are the disturbance reaching upward for the first time.

**Act 1 — Sir Ambrose Everdawn**
Waiting at the southern gate of the City of the Dead at sunset. A Tethyrian man of approximately sixty, broad and heavy, wearing plain travelling clothes and a sword with the look of long use rather than ceremony. His only symbol of office is a small silver gauntlet pin — Kelemvor's, not Tyr's. He has the patient quality of someone who has spent a lot of nights in graveyards.

He explains plainly: bones have been going missing from three mausoleums in the northern section. He patrols the south half after dark. He needs the PCs to cover the north. He will meet them at the central gate path at dawn to compare notes.

A DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check — or simply demonstrating competence and sincerity — converts his initial skepticism to collegial partnership. Without it, he assigns the patrol with specific instructions and expects results, not conversation. Over the ten nights he becomes willing to share what he knows: he has patrolled this cemetery for fifteen years, has felt the Brandath section is wrong for months, and has encountered this specific quality of wrongness twice before in his career — both times associated with something very old and technically contained.

**Act 2 — The Ten Nights**
The City of the Dead at night fills with driftglobes drifting among the headstones in slow, purposeless paths. Each night, there is a 10% cumulative chance of encountering six **Skeletons** in the northern section. They are not clever. Each time they appear, they are moving in the same direction: toward the Brandath crypt, then returning. By the fifth encounter, the pattern is undeniable.

The Brandath crypt itself is sealed and guarded (two City Watch officers on rotation, assigned after the third skeleton incident). The PCs can look at it from outside. Entering without authorization is possible and will be reported. Looking at it from the exterior: the crypt has the quality of a place that is very carefully not letting anything out.

**Act 3 — Dawn of the Tenth Night**
The skeleton activity simply stops. The disturbance in the northern section doesn't stop — it becomes quieter, like something that was probing a wall and has decided to wait instead.

At the final dawn meeting, Sir Ambrose says: "The Brandath crypts. Whatever is happening there — the bones weren't the point. They were a symptom." He looks north across the cemetery. "Be careful near that mausoleum."

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: All ten nights are completed without any bones being stolen from the northern section on the PCs' watch.
- +1: The pattern of skeleton movement toward the Brandath crypt is documented and reported to both Melannor and Sir Ambrose before the tenth night. Melannor passes it to Jeryth; Jeryth is silent for three hours, then sends a single message: "Tell me the moment they open it."

**Aftermath**
Sir Ambrose becomes a reliable minor contact in the City of the Dead. Jeryth's message — "tell me the moment they open it" — is her first direct reference to the vault. She does not explain what she knows; it threads forward to Mission 6 and Arc J.

### Mission 3 — The Doppelganger Problem
**Level 4 | 3 renown | Name of the Manshoon Splinter contact (see Aftermath)**

**Hook**
A falcon — Jeryth's choice of messenger for urgent work. The briefing: "Doppelgangers are operating out of the Yawning Portal. Shapeshifters of no fixed loyalty, moving freely through the city while four factions compete for the same ground — the Enclave cannot permit it. They need to leave Waterdeep. Peacefully, if possible."

A postscript, in Melannor's own voice rather than the bird's flat delivery: "Jeryth said 'peacefully if possible' three times. I'm including that in case it's useful."

**Background** *(DM only)*
Melannor and Jeryth know about the doppelgangers through the Enclave's animal network — birds see things people don't. What neither knows: the Harpers may already have an existing relationship with Bonnie's crew (Harper Mission 3). One of the five doppelgangers is a Manshoon Splinter informant. Bonnie has known for some time and has been waiting for the right moment.

If Harper M3 has not been run: this mission serves as its discovery. The traitor is whichever doppelganger the DM has designated. Bonnie knows and has been sitting on it, waiting for something to break the situation open.

If Harper M3 has been run: Bonnie has already identified the traitor, or the traitor has already been neutralized. The Enclave mission becomes a follow-up — Bonnie must still leave, even if she handled the internal problem herself.

**Act 1 — Bonnie**
Bonnie is at her usual station at the Yawning Portal, working the evening shift. She sees the party the moment they enter. There is, in her expression, a flicker of something — not surprise, but the calibrated acknowledgment of a new complication.

She signals the party to a quiet table. "I know what you're here for. And I know why the Enclave wants it. It's reasonable." She looks at the table. "Waterdeep is the most interesting city I have lived in. I've lived in eleven cities. I've been here eight months and I have not had to be anyone I didn't choose to be." She looks up. "How long do I have?"

A DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation) check convinces her to agree to departure. The DC drops to 12 if the PCs acknowledge the genuine cost of what they are asking — engaging honestly with what they are taking from her rather than presenting it as a straightforward compliance issue.

**Act 2 — The Complication**
One of the five doppelgangers is in the tavern and moves actively to undermine the negotiation. He doesn't want to leave Waterdeep. His objections are smooth but self-interested rather than principled — a DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check reveals the distinction. If the party calls this out directly, Bonnie goes still, looks at him for a long moment, then looks at the party. "He's the one who sold your names." She has been sitting on this. The timing is right now. He leaves when she tells him to.

**Act 3 — The Agreement**
If Bonnie agrees to leave: she asks for two weeks to arrange departure for her crew. She will honor this. As a parting courtesy, she gives the party one piece of information — the name of the Manshoon Splinter operative who bought the intelligence her traitor sold. Melannor passes this to both the Harpers and the Lords' Alliance independently; it becomes a Splinter investigation thread in Arc E.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: Bonnie agrees to a departure timeline and honors it. No violence.
- +1: The traitor is identified and the Splinter contact named — whether through the party's own work, Bonnie's reveal, or Harper M3's resolution carried forward.
- +1: The party allows Bonnie to handle the traitor herself rather than imposing a resolution. She does so cleanly. It costs them nothing and earns her unspoken respect, which has a longer half-life than most currencies in this city.

**Aftermath**
The Splinter contact's name — extracted from Bonnie's intelligence — is the cleanest lead on Manshoon's information network the party has before Arc E. If reported to both Mirt and Jalester, they begin pulling the thread independently.

Three days after Bonnie's departure, a flying dove arrives at Trollskull Manor carrying a folded note: three dots in a triangle. Melannor, when shown it, identifies it as a doppelganger notation mark — her name, essentially. "It's a receipt," he says. "She's confirming she left." He seems to find this slightly touching.

### Mission 4 — The Grells in the Dock Ward
**Level 5 | 3 renown | Charm of heroism (supernatural gift) for each character who enters Phaulkonmere on their return (see Aftermath)**

**Hook**
No bird this time. Melannor appears at Trollskull Manor in person, already moving when the door opens. "Flying aberrations in the Dock Ward, snatching citizens from the waterfront. Third person in as many nights. I've told the Watch; they're stretched thin. This one is ours." He has a sketch of the affected pier. He is already on his way there and clearly expects the PCs to keep up.

**Background** *(DM only)*
Two **Grells** established a nest in an abandoned waterfront warehouse, disturbed from Undermountain Level 1 by Xanathar Guild excavation activity in the tunnels beneath the harbor district. Three citizens have been taken from Pier 17 on the south quay; one — **Mirsa**, an elderly Tethyrian seamstress — is still alive in the nest. A Xanathar Guild operative paid two dockhands to vacate Pier 17 the night Mirsa was taken; the Guild knows the grells are operating and has been using the disruption as cover for a cargo movement on the south docks.

**Act 1 — The Search**
Two investigation checks in sequence to locate the warehouse:

- **DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)** — Dock Ward contacts, the Harbor Master's assistant, pier records: multiple reports of sounds from a closed waterfront warehouse; a Harbor Master's aide describes a persistent ozone smell from one specific building, has been there for days.
- **DC 14 Wisdom (Survival)** — reading the physical environment: absence of the cats that normally prowl the dock warehouses; overturned crates near the south door. The nest is in the upper story; the loading doors high above are left open.

Melannor accompanies the PCs to the building's exterior and stops there. He is not a combat asset inside an enclosed space. He waits, holding very still in the way of someone who is scared and has decided not to show it.

**Act 2 — The Warehouse**
Cavernous, dark, lit only through gaps in the boarding and moonlight through the open loading doors 30 feet above. The smell is ozone and something organic. Two grells hang from the rafters. One has noticed the party; the other is focused on the far corner of the floor — **Mirsa**, cocooned loosely in grell secretion, unconscious but breathing.

Both grells fight. The first drops toward the party immediately; the second turns from Mirsa on round two.

Freeing Mirsa requires an Action and a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check. She cannot be freed safely with a grell within 5 feet of her. The party must decide whether to split attention.

A grell below half HP flees toward the open loading doors and over the harbor — it dives. A fled grell is not destroyed. If one escapes, Melannor asks the PCs two days later whether they know where it went; harbor workers will report a large flying creature over the south quay before it disappears into the harbor.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: Mirsa is rescued alive before either grell escapes.
- +1: Pier 17 and the dockhand payoff are investigated and reported to both Melannor and the Watch. The Xanathar Guild's cover operation on the south docks is flagged for Watch scrutiny — one Guild cargo movement in Arc E is disrupted by an unexpected Watch presence.

**Aftermath**
The party returns to Phaulkonmere to report. Melannor meets them at the gate and goes quiet for a moment when he hears Mirsa is alive, then nods once.

Jeryth's voice settles over the garden: "You did well. Come in."

Every party member who enters Phaulkonmere at this moment feels a warmth settle over them with no natural source. Jeryth bestows a *charm of heroism* (supernatural gift, 2024 DMG) on each of them. She says nothing about it. It simply happens.

Mirsa, when she recovers, is coherent and specific: she was grabbed near Pier 17; a Xanathar Guild operative paid two dockhands to vacate the pier that night and she heard them clearly before she lost consciousness. The pier number and the payoff detail are actionable intelligence pointing toward the Guild's south dock operation — a thread into Arc E.

### Mission 5 — The Fouled Channel
**Level 6 | 4 renown | Jeryth's healing on demand (see Aftermath)**

**Hook**
Three mornings in a row, Melannor finds herbs dying in the Phaulkonmere beds closest to the garden's eastern wall. Not neglected — dead overnight, overnight again, overnight a third time, each dead patch slightly larger than the last. Jeryth is silent on the subject, which Melannor has learned to read as concern rather than indifference.

He summons the PCs in person. "Something is wrong underground. I can feel it in the garden; Jeryth can feel it in the water table. We need to find the source before it reaches the deeper root systems." He has one lead: Gerrick Goodbarrel's drainage tunnel below the Undercliff southern terraces, which connects east to the Castle Ward cistern system. If something is moving through the underground water, that tunnel is the likely path.

**Background** *(DM only)*
A Manshoon Splinter arcanist — **Raeve Solnath**, the same arcanist responsible for the Mission 1 scarecrows — has been using a sealed section of the Castle Ward cisterns as a disposal site for alchemical waste from failed intellect devourer grafting experiments. The waste is psychically resonant: it creates a mild but spreading cognitive suppression in anyone who drinks from affected Trades Ward water sources. The Splinter has been introducing new batches twice a week for six weeks, intending this as a long-term softening agent in a district with heavy Watch presence. The contamination has now reached Phaulkonmere's groundwater via an underground stream.

Raeve is not currently at the cistern. He sends two **Cultist** assistants on a regular delivery schedule.

**Act 1 — The Tunnel**
Gerrick's drainage tunnel entry is exactly where he described: a bricked archway in the south Undercliff terraces, sealed with a fitted iron grate. The grate has been opened from the other side recently — scratches on the seal, evidence of regular passage.

Inside: a long brick-lined tunnel heading east, wet and cold, connecting after 200 feet to the Castle Ward cistern access — a larger vaulted space with channels running in three directions.

In the cisterns, a DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) or Intelligence (Investigation) check identifies the contamination: a section of the eastern channel has an oily iridescent film on the water surface. The smell is wrong — faintly chemical, with a psychic quality that a character proficient in Arcana identifies as "thought-residue." Extended exposure in the contaminated section (more than 10 minutes) imposes the Dazed condition until a Short Rest.

**Act 2 — The Cache**
Following the film upstream leads to a sealed side-chamber — a fitted stone in the cistern wall, recently removed and replaced. Inside: a Splinter storage cache. Six alchemical vessels containing waste product, ready for introduction. A coded logbook (Splinter cipher; readable with the codebook from Harper Mission 2 if the PCs obtained it, otherwise DC 14 Intelligence to partially decode). Six weeks of delivery dates, the frequency of visits, and a name — Raeve Solnath — as the operation's coordinator.

Roll 1d6 when the PCs begin examining the cache: on 1-3, Raeve's two **Cultist** assistants arrive on their scheduled delivery visit during this scene; on 4-6, they arrive as the PCs are leaving the chamber. The cultists will not fight — they flee immediately and report the breach to Raeve. If captured before they can flee (DC 14 Dexterity to interpose, or held by a spell), they know only that they were paid to add the vessels to the eastern channel every three days. They do not know Raeve's location.

**Act 3 — Purification**
With the cache destroyed and the source sealed: Jeryth can purify the contaminated channels through the PCs if they carry a cutting from Phaulkonmere's herb beds into the cistern and place it in the affected water. Melannor provides the cutting solemnly, as if handing over something precious. The purification takes 10 minutes of Jeryth's concentration through the cutting and fills the cistern with the smell of rain and turned earth for an hour afterward.

If the cultists escaped and Raeve relocates the cache before the PCs can return: the contamination continues. The Trades Ward Watch operates at disadvantage on Wisdom checks for the duration of Arc E as the cognitive suppression spreads through the district water supply.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: The Splinter cache is found and destroyed before any cultist can report the breach to Raeve. The delivery schedule ends.
- +1: The logbook is recovered and decoded, providing the six-week contamination schedule and Raeve Solnath's name. If reported to the Lords' Alliance, the LA sets an ambush at the cistern entrance timed to the next scheduled delivery. Raeve is arrested in Arc E.

**Aftermath**
Jeryth's purification restores the Phaulkonmere garden that same evening. She speaks once, as the PCs leave: "The water remembers what was put into it. So will I."

She will remember this in Arc J — when her wards are needed against Illuun's resonance, this act of restoration has deepened her connection to the underground water system in a way that makes those wards stronger.

Melannor tells the PCs the next morning that Jeryth has offered her healing to each of them, at no cost, for injuries sustained on any mission from this point forward. She will not say this herself. That is how she marks a debt repaid, he explains.

### Mission 6 — The Dreamer's Reach
**Level 7 | 4 renown | Jeryth's ward (see Aftermath)**

**Hook**
Jeryth Phaulkon has fallen silent. For three days, her voice has not been heard in the garden. Plants that normally turn toward her attention grow normally but without direction. The birds in the estate's oaks have begun roosting facing outward, which Melannor describes as "wrong in a way I cannot articulate but have not seen before."

Melannor sends for the PCs. He is standing in the garden when they arrive, looking at the eastern wall. "She's still there," he says. "I can feel her presence in the estate. She's not gone. But she's fighting something." He turns. "The disturbance in the ground beneath the Castle Ward — it's stronger than it's been in months. Mission 5 cleared the Splinter's contamination. That should have quieted it. Instead it got louder." He meets the PCs' eyes. "I think whatever you disturbed down there noticed you. And I think you need to go back and finish it."

**Background** *(DM only)*
**Illuun** — the abolethic dreamer anchored to Undermountain Level 4 — has been extending psychic reach upward through Waterdeep's underground water table for decades, barely perceptible. The Stone of Golorr's activation created a resonance that Illuun responded to, pushing more actively toward the surface. Jeryth, as a nature spirit deeply connected to Phaulkonmere's underground root systems and the water table, has been quietly absorbing and deflecting this influence for months. Mission 5's disturbance of the cistern system gave Illuun a path directly into the channel Jeryth uses, and it has been pressing through that path for three days.

Jeryth is resisting, and she is winning, but the effort is consuming her ability to communicate. She has 48 hours before she either breaks the connection or withdraws so deep into Phaulkonmere's earth that she will not surface again for months — meaning she will not be available to ward the vault opening in Arc J.

The PCs need to find and destroy Illuun's surface anchor point — a small nodule of abolethic matter, grown over centuries, embedded in the floor of the deepest accessible section of the Castle Ward cisterns, below the level reached in Mission 5. Physically destroying it (any weapon or spell dealing fire or radiant damage, minimum 15 damage in a single hit) severs the immediate connection. It does not harm Illuun — it is too far below — but it cuts the reach.

**Act 1 — Descent**
Back through Gerrick's tunnel, deeper than Mission 5. A passage downward, sealed with fitted stones the PCs can remove, leads to a flooded lower section. Melannor provides a scroll of *Water Breathing* if the party needs it, and a piece of Phaulkonmere bark that glows faintly in the presence of the abolethic anchor. "Jeryth managed that much," he says.

The deeper cisterns are flooded to knee-depth in most sections and chest-depth in others. The abolethic influence is palpable here — not combat, but wrongness. Characters must make a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw on entering the deepest section; on a failure, they experience the Dazed condition for one round as Illuun's passive awareness registers their presence.

Two **Chuul** servants of Illuun — crustacean aberrations drawn to its anchor point over years — are in the deepest chamber. They attack on sight.

**Act 2 — The Anchor**
The anchor is embedded in the floor of the deepest chamber, in an area underwater for decades. It does not look like much — a dark irregular nodule the size of a fist, slightly warm, faintly iridescent when the Phaulkonmere bark's glow touches it. It is surrounded by the specific stillness of something that has been waiting in one place for a very long time.

If a PC attempts to examine the anchor before destroying it: a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw must succeed or they receive a flash of psychic contact — not communication, not control, but awareness. Something immense and patient and very old, noticing them. They perceive what Illuun is in a moment of direct contact, without knowing its name. Mechanically: the Frightened condition (DC 15 Wisdom save to end at the start of each turn) for up to one minute. When it ends, the character knows with certainty that what they felt is alive, is below, and has now registered them personally.

Destroying the anchor: 15+ damage in a single hit from any fire or radiant source severs it. The nodule dissolves. The water clears.

**Act 3 — Jeryth Wakes**
When the PCs return to Phaulkonmere, Jeryth is speaking. Her voice has the quality of something restored — not weaker, but quieter, the way a person sounds after a sustained effort finally completed.

"You went below," she says. "I felt it when it broke." A pause. "Thank you."

She does not ask what they saw. She says, once, without elaboration: "It is still there. Further below. It will not reach me again through that path." Another pause. "But the vault — when the Stone opens it — make sure no one is watching from the water."

She falls silent again, but the normal silence of someone present and attentive, not three days of strained effort.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: The anchor is destroyed and both Chuul neutralized before the PCs leave the cisterns.
- +1: The psychic contact experience — if any PC experienced it — is reported to Melannor in full detail. He records it and sends the account to Jeryth. She will reference it in Arc J. A PC who received the direct perception is also now personally registered to Illuun: if they descend to Undermountain Level 4, Illuun is aware of their presence before they reach it.

**Aftermath**
Jeryth creates a ward — a small woven ring of Phaulkonmere root — and bestows it to the party as a group artifact. Any character holding it has advantage on saving throws against the Charmed and Frightened conditions caused by aberrations while the ward is intact (10 HP, AC 10; repaired by contact with any Phaulkonmere plant overnight).

She also fulfills the Arc J request she has been building toward since Mission 2: she will be listening when the vault opens, and she has prepared what wards she can. The PCs do not need to give her 24 hours' notice anymore. She is already ready.

If the PCs report what they perceived to Mirt (Harpers): the connection between the Stone of Golorr and Illuun's reach becomes concrete intelligence, resolving the mystery Mirt raised in Harper Mission 6. Mirt goes quiet for a long moment. "Undermountain Level 4," he says finally. "Well." He says nothing else immediately, but three days later a paper bird arrives at Trollskull Manor with a single line: *"That name is Illuun. Don't say it below the cisterns."*

## Order of the Gauntlet

*Contact: Savra Belabranta — arrives in person. No paper birds, no messengers, no flying snakes unless the situation is urgent. She knocks on the door.*

*Savra is in her late twenties and looks older in the way that people who have been through something and come out the other side look older. Dark hair worn back. A strong jaw. The patient, measured manner of someone who has learned to think before speaking and found the habit worth keeping. She was a member of the Howling Hatred elemental cult before she was an Order knight. She will tell you this if asked. She will not tell you what she did while she was in it. She is making amends. This is the work she found that made that meaningful.*

*She delivers mission briefings directly, speaks without drama, and expects results. She fights with disciplined efficiency when she says she will accompany the party — protects the people who need protecting, kills what needs to be killed, nothing more. Her goodness is hard-won in a way that makes it more convincing, not less.*

### The Grand Game — Order of the Gauntlet Stance

The Order has a hierarchy: diabolism first, aberrant evil second, everything else after that. The vault gold concerns Savra only as a function of who it might empower — 500,000 gold in the hands of an active Asmodeus cult would be catastrophic. In Manshoon's or Xanathar's hands, nearly as bad. In the city treasury, she has no objection.

At campaign start, Savra has believed for months that the Cassalanters are active infernalists. She has no legal evidence. She has been building toward a confrontation she knows will come, and she has been doing it without investigators capable of generating that evidence. The PCs change this.

The Order has no interest in playing the Grand Game on its own terms — not positioning for the vault, not competing for faction influence. They are hunting specific evil. If the PCs help them find it, the Order throws its full weight behind them when it matters.

**What the Order shares proactively:**
- Tactical intelligence on devil types, their combat behavior, and their contractual obligations (Savra's combat experience is extensive and specific)
- The Halls of Justice's file on Cassalanter public conduct — party appearances, charity donations to causes that don't survive scrutiny, Watch inquiries that were dismissed before completion
- Savra's read on Lord Victoro Cassalanter: "He's charming in the specific way that people are charming when they have something to hide."

**What the Order asks for:**
- Physical evidence of Cassalanter diabolism — a ritual record, a signed instruction from a named family member, a witness who will speak on the record — rather than a confession extracted under magical compulsion
- Support when the Cassalanter confrontation comes (Arc G)
- Honesty. Savra cannot work with people she is managing around. She will know when she is being managed.

### Mission 2 — The Black Viper Investigation
**Level 3 | 2 renown | No tangible reward**

**Hook**
A paper bird — unusual for Savra, who is at the Halls of Justice this morning and wants the party to start without her. The message: "The Waterdeep Wazoo is reporting that the masked thief called the Black Viper has returned. Interview the publisher and find out what he actually knows. I will meet you when I'm done."

**Background** *(DM only)*
The story is a planted trap. A genuine masked thief is active in the North Ward, but the publisher's claim that she's a secret Cassalanter twin is fabricated — invented by Seffia Naelryke, a Cassalanter household operative, and fed to the Wazoo as bait. The Cassalanters want to identify who in Waterdeep is investigating them seriously enough to track a lead about their family. The party's inquiry will be flagged through their imp network within hours of the first conversation with Gaxly.

Seffia is still in the city. She visited Gaxly a second time two days after publication to "check the story's reception." She left a message drop — a specific candle shop in the Trades Ward — where interested parties can arrange contact. Gaxly will not volunteer this. He can be made to.

The Hall of Records contains a sealed entry for "Petra Cassalanter, born 1470 DR, deceased 1471 DR" — a forged legal document installed by a Cassalanter lawyer to backstop the story if anyone checked. This changes the stakes: the Cassalanters did not just plant a rumor; they built infrastructure to support it.

**Act 1 — The Waterdeep Wazoo**
A narrow second-floor room at the corner of Immar and Stallion Streets in the North Ward. Fresh ink, cold coffee, broadsheet pages drying on strings overhead.

**Gaxly Rudderbust** — professionally untidy, forty, with the specific dishevelment of someone who has decided this makes them look like a journalist. He has two layers.

*Layer one:* Source confidentiality. He published the story. He stands by it. He promised not to name his source and is protected by that promise in the way that people use small obligations to avoid large ones. A DC 12 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion) check or 50 gp moves him: his source told him the Black Viper is the Cassalanters' secret twin sister, hidden since birth due to a disfigurement. The mask is concealment, not vanity. He delivers this with the relish of someone who knows it's the best story he's run in years. He published before he could verify it. He's not sure he regrets it.

*Layer two:* Seffia's return visit. Gaxly is warier about this. A DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion) check — phrased as genuine concern for his safety rather than extraction — or an honest disclosure that the story may be a planted trap convinces him to describe the contact arrangement: a red candle in the window of Barrow's Wick on Lamp Street in the Trades Ward means someone is willing to meet. He has not used it. He assumed it was for him to signal continued interest.

**Act 2 — Two Tracks**

*Track A — The Hall of Records:*
A day's work among clerks and registry shelves turns up the sealed "Petra Cassalanter" entry. **Ilmeth Waevlur**, a retired registry clerk who worked the Sea Ward records for thirty years and now volunteers three mornings a week out of habit, is the key: a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check or a mention of the Order of the Gauntlet prompts him to pull the entry and look at it critically. His verdict, after a long pause: the document has correct formatting and genuine official seals, but the clerk's signature is from a registrar who died in 1468 DR, two years before the alleged birth. Someone good at forgery had access to stolen seals. A real person did not exist. A real crime did.

*Track B — The Seffia Meeting:*
Placing the red candle at Barrow's Wick triggers a reply the following morning: a meeting time and a neutral location — a tea house called the Steaming Anchor in the Trades Ward, midday.

**Seffia Naelryke** is Tethyrian, mid-thirties, dressed to pass as a minor noblewoman. Gracious, composed, conducting a social audit from the moment the party enters. Her goal is to find out what they know and who they work for. She will not confirm the planted story. She will try to determine whether the party is a threat or an opportunity.

A DC 16 Wisdom (Insight) check catches her tells: questions about the party's other investigations are too specific, her interest in Savra Belabranta by name arrives unprompted.

The party has several options:
- **Play along:** Let Seffia believe they have no Order connection and are independent operators. She offers 100 gp for everything they learn about the Gauntlet's investigation into the Cassalanters. If they accept and report to Savra, Savra is cold but not surprised: "Now we know what they want to track." The Cassalanters receive limited misinformation.
- **Expose the trap:** Confront Seffia with the forged birth record. She goes very still. She will make a counter-offer: withdraw the investigation and the Cassalanters take no action against Trollskull Manor. She has two guards outside (use **Thug** stat block). If the party refuses and tries to detain her, she signals them.
- **Detain Seffia:** Forces the guards into the open (combat). Seffia will not break under ordinary pressure; *Charm Person* (DC 14 Wisdom) or equivalent extracts one significant intelligence: "The Cassalanters know who the real Black Viper is. They are not trying to stop her. They need the Watch looking at Trollskull and the North Ward, not the Sea Ward. Something is being moved."
- **Walk away:** The party leaves. Seffia files a report. The imp network escalates from observation to active tracking.

**Act 3 — The Imp at Dawn**
Regardless of how the Seffia meeting resolves, surveillance of Trollskull Manor begins within 48 hours. A hire-coach passes Trollskull Alley twice in one day at a slow roll. Before dawn on the second night, a character with Passive Perception 14 notices a small winged shape — too deliberate for a bat — hovering near the upper windows.

The party can attempt to catch it. The imp is invisible during the approach; a DC 16 Wisdom (Perception) check locates it. A DC 16 Dexterity (Athletics or Acrobatics) check, or a spell that restrains or banishes without killing, captures it before it can vanish. Bound and stripped of invisibility, it is a Cassalanter servant: its material form alone — with its infernal binding marks — constitutes evidence of the family's access to devil-bound servitors. Savra takes it seriously.

If not caught: it departs and the surveillance continues at a distance. The Cassalanters know the party is working for the Gauntlet. They will plan accordingly.

Savra meets the party at day's end. She hears everything, weighs it, and asks one question: "What are they moving?" She has no answer yet. Neither does anyone else. But the question is now open.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: The planted story confirmed fabricated and reported to Savra.
- +1: The forged Hall of Records entry recovered, or Seffia Naelryke identified and her name delivered to Savra with the contact method as evidence.
- +1 (bonus): The imp captured and delivered to the Halls of Justice. Physical evidence of Cassalanter devil-binding, filed formally.

**Aftermath**
The Cassalanter file opens. What the party chose to do with Seffia determines the immediate shape of the opposition: a deal accepted means quieter surveillance and a deliberate Cassalanter patience; an open confrontation means faster escalation and, by Mission 4, a more alert response from Gysheer's handlers. Savra's question — what are they moving — remains unanswered, and it stays with her through Mission 3.

Savra at the door, urgency without display — she has been moving for an hour: "Spined devils terrorizing a Field Ward courtyard. I am going there now. Come." Already turning as she says it, coat on, sword at her side. The "please" is in the sentence structure even if the word is absent.

The last spined devil is still twitching when an alley door opens. She releases Gysheer and watches her disappear into the Field Ward.

**Aftermath**

### Mission 5 — The Shrine on Aveen Street
**Level 6 | 4 renown | +2 renown from Earning Renown trigger (see Aftermath)**

**Hook**
Savra meets the party at the Halls of Justice, not at Trollskull Manor. She has been there most of the morning. The Order's Cassalanter file now occupies three shelves. She is calm with the specific calm of someone who has been waiting a long time for an investigation to reach the point where it can actually do something.

"The shrine on Aveen Street in the Field Ward — Gysheer named it. She looks at them. "If you find physical evidence — a ritual record, a signed instruction from a named Cassalanter, anything that implicates a specific person in a specific act — I can take it to the Lords. **Background** *(DM only)*
The Aveen Street shrine is a converted basement under a chandler's shop, leased two years ago through an intermediary and used intermittently as a ritual preparation site. The current configuration: a consecration circle for one of the twelve infernal rites Lord Victoro Cassalanter is conducting under his devil contract. Inside: ritual equipment, a locked strongbox containing an operational ledger in Victoro's own handwriting, and a standing guardian — a **Bearded Devil** assigned to protect the site.

The chandler upstairs — Orros, nervous, innocent — leases the basement to "a civic association" and has not been downstairs in fourteen months.

**Act 1 — Aveen Street**
The shrine entrance is a locked cellar door in an alley behind the chandler's shop. DC 13 Dexterity (Thieves' Tools) or DC 15 Strength to force it.

The basement is lit by infernal candles producing red-tinged light. A seven-pointed consecration circle dominates the floor in powdered iron filings, blood-stained at each vertex. The smell is sulfur and something older. Against the far wall: a locked strongbox (DC 16 Thieves' Tools; DC 18 Strength to force), ritual implements, and the ledger loose on a stone table.

The **Bearded Devil** guardian is bound to the ritual space's perimeter — it cannot leave, but within those bounds it fights without restraint. Its *Beard* attack grapples and causes ongoing bleeding; a PC reduced to 0 HP by it is Cursed (unable to regain HP) until a Short Rest with appropriate healing or a *Remove Curse*.

**Act 2 — The Evidence**
The ledger is in Lord Victoro Cassalanter's own handwriting: twelve ritual entries, each naming a specific step, a specific location, and a date. Gysheer's name appears twice. Three entries reference a Founders' Day deadline for a final "completion rite" called the Reckoning. The document does not explain what the Reckoning is. Savra will understand the infernal contractual structure.

A DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana or Religion) check confirms the ledger describes a classical Asmodean soul-bargain ritual fulfillment schedule — the type documented in Tyr's theological libraries.

If the strongbox is forced rather than picked, the lock is destroyed but the ledger is intact. Savra takes it regardless.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +1: The Bearded Devil is destroyed or driven off and the shrine cleared.
- +1: The ledger is recovered intact and delivered to Savra within 24 hours.
- +2 (Earning Renown trigger): The ledger constitutes solid evidence of Cassalanter diabolism delivered to Savra — triggering the +2 earning renown action from the faction table, regardless of how the other conditions resolved.

**Aftermath**

The petition does not result in immediate arrest — the Code Legal's religious protections are extensive and the Cassalanters have excellent lawyers. But it places the family under formal legal scrutiny.

The Cassalanters are informed of the petition within 48 hours through their Masked Lord contact. Lord Victoro accelerates the Founders' Day timeline by four days. Arc G's deadline tightens: the Reckoning is now four days earlier than the PCs would otherwise expect. Savra passes this calendar intelligence to the party as soon as she calculates it from the ledger's dated entries.

Lord Victoro also triggers an infernal rider in his contract — a clause that names and marks anyone who has physically handled his ritual documentation. The consequences of that clause arrive in Mission 6.

### Mission 6 — The Writ and the Weight
**Level 7 | 4 renown | Formal Order recognition (see Aftermath)**

**Hook**
Savra appears at Trollskull Manor in the late afternoon, earlier than expected. She is controlled, in the way that is distinct from calm. She closes the door behind her.

"The Cassalanters have filed three procedural objections to the Lords' review. A Masked Lord sympathetic to their cause has called the evidence technically inadmissible — the shrine's religious property status is being used to argue the evidence was obtained illegally. The review is proceeding, but it is slow." She looks at the party. "This morning the Halls of Justice received a formal notification through their infernal protocols. A petition filed by something identifying itself as Lord Victoro's contractual counterpart. It names each of you by physical description." She meets each of them. "That notification is not a legal document in Waterdeep. It is a flag. Something is coming to enforce it. I want you at the Halls of Justice tonight."

**Background** *(DM only)*
Lord Victoro's infernal patron — a contract-bound devil well above the tier of everything the PCs have faced so far — dispatched a **Horned Devil** with a specific mandate: recover or destroy the ledger, and kill everyone who touched it. The Horned Devil has been in Waterdeep for six hours. It is locating its targets methodically. Savra has summoned the party to the Halls of Justice for exactly this reason: it is the most defensible position in Waterdeep for a confrontation with an infernal enforcement agent, and the only location with the relevant theological protections.

**Act 1 — The Halls of Justice**
The high priest of Tyr has opened a consecrated hall on Savra's request. The consecration imposes disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws made by devils and advantage on saving throws made by characters against infernal fear effects while they remain within the hall. Two priests of significant ability (use **Priest** stat block with 5th level spell access) are present and can cast *Banishment* (DC 16 Charisma) if the party needs the assist, but they will not act first. This is the party's fight.

The hall has two exits — a main corridor door and a vestry door into the prayer space — and the Horned Devil will attempt to use both if given the opportunity.

Savra fights at full capacity and without hesitation. She has faced infernal enforcement before. She is angry.

**Act 2 — The Horned Devil**
A **Horned Devil** (2024 *Monster Manual*) arrives at the Halls of Justice at midnight, entering through a window in the vestibule. It does not negotiate. Its mandate is specific: destroy the ledger and kill the people who touched it. It will not be distracted from either goal. The ledger is in a locked cabinet in an adjacent room — not in the fight hall. The Horned Devil knows where it is. Part of its combat behavior is maneuvering to reach that cabinet; the party must prevent it from disengaging.

The consecrated space imposes the disadvantage described above and makes its fear abilities marginally less effective. This is a hard fight.

**Act 3 — The Morning After**
When the Horned Devil is destroyed or banished, the halls go quiet except for the smell of sulfur and scorch marks on the stone. The ledger is intact. Savra sits down on a pew and stays there for a moment.

"That's the first time they've sent something that heavy," she says. "Which means the petition is working." She looks at the party. "They're scared."

Two days later, the Lords' formal review returns a preliminary finding: sufficient grounds to proceed. The Cassalanters are officially under investigation. Lord Victoro Cassalanter attends a charity function the following week and smiles at everyone who approaches him.

**Renown Opportunities**
- +2: The Horned Devil is destroyed or banished before it reaches the ledger.
- +1: Every PC named in the infernal notice survives the encounter. Savra notes this in her report.
- +1 (supplementary): The kill or banishment came primarily from the party rather than Savra's intervention. She will not say she is impressed. She will file it accurately.

**Aftermath**
The Halls of Justice formally recognize the party as Order associates — not knights, not sworn members, but recognized affiliates with legal standing in Waterdeep. Two practical effects: Watch officers who see the party in conflict with a Cassalanter operative will not automatically intervene against them; and in Arc G, the formal recognition can be presented as grounds for Watch non-interference during the heist.

Savra gives each party member a copy of the formal recognition before they leave. Then: "The Founders' Day timeline is tighter than we thought. Whatever you're doing with the vault — do it fast."

She shakes each person's hand, and she doesn't let go right away.
