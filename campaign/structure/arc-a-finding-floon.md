# Arc A: Finding Floon

---

## Chapter Purpose

Arc A is the campaign's inciting incident. It introduces Waterdeep as an active faction battlefield, teaches the Three Clue Rule investigation structure that recurs throughout the campaign, and delivers the party's home base as the arc's reward. The arc exists to establish the scope of the Grand Game without overwhelming first-level characters: three villain factions are visible in Scene 1, but the party's only job is to find one man.

---

## 1. Core Concept

A street-level rescue mission drops the characters into the middle of a three-faction crossfire. Two people were kidnapped from a Dock Ward alley. One is in a warehouse, held by the wrong gang. One is in the sewers, held by the right one. The investigation teaches players exactly how the Three Clue Rule works — multiple independent paths to every answer — and ends with the party holding a deed to a derelict tavern that makes them homeowners, neighbors, and targets.

---

## 2. Sections

### Arc Opener

*An adventure for 1st-level characters.*

Three villain factions are visible inside the Yawning Portal before Volo Geddarm sits down to make his offer. Yagra Stonefist, a Doom Raiders operative and Davil Starsong's bodyguard, picks a fight with a Xanathar Guild thug named Krentz over a killing two nights ago in the Dock Ward, the same killing that lit the fuse on the ward's gang war. Four of Krentz's companions pile in. Before Durnan of the Yawning Portal can handle it himself, the Undermountain bell rings. A troll hauls itself out of the well with six stirges attached. Durnan vaults the bar, greatsword in hand, tells the party to deal with the stirges, and takes the troll. At the back of the settling taproom, seated near the observation balcony with a full glass of wine she has not touched, a drow woman watches the well. She has been at that table for two weeks. Volo arrives when the monster is dead, steered to the party's table by the contact who has been waiting for him, and describes a missing companion: Floon Blagmaar, wavy red-blond hair, early thirties, gone for two nights.

The trail begins in the Dock Ward. Three independent paths converge on a single warehouse on Candle Lane: a deep gnome shopkeeper who witnessed the kidnapping and will talk if given something purple or persuaded; dockworker regulars at the Skewered Dragon who know the warehouse address without prompting; and a trail of Floon's dropped blue pearl beads running from Old Xoblob Shop's doorstep to the mouth of Candle Lane. En route, the party crosses a City Watch crime scene: three arrested Zhentarim thugs kneeling in the street, black winged snake tattoos on their necks, while wagons arrive for the bodies.

Manshoon's Zhentarim hold the warehouse. Five of Urstul Floxin's thugs grabbed Renaer Neverember and Floon outside the Old Xoblob Shop two nights ago, brought them here, and questioned Renaer about his father's embezzled hoard. They took his mourning locket. Renaer believed it held a keepsake from his mother; it hid an Eye of the Stone of Golorr in a sealed compartment. The Eye was delivered to Kolat Towers before the Xanathar Guild raid killed most of the cell. Four kenku now search the wreckage. Renaer hid when the raid hit and is still hiding in a back room. Floon was mistaken for Renaer and dragged through a trapdoor into the sewer. Renaer, once found, tells the party what he saw: Xanathar agents, circle-eye tattoos, Floon unconscious over one man's shoulder, the trapdoor in the cellar floor. Three more clues in the warehouse confirm the route. They follow.

In the sewers, Xanathar's yellow-chalk eye markers guide the way to a converted brick vault, nine rooms of a decommissioned filtration chamber, now a Xanathar Guild cell. Floon is alive in a holding room, bruised and grateful, and his first question is about Renaer. The cell's leader carries keys to every room, including the sealed iron door at the back of the vault. Behind that door, in a chamber they were not meant to reach, something withdraws before they get a clear look: a rounded silhouette trailing three or four thin tendrils, retreating into a floor grate. Any character who approached felt it first — a single pulse of cold, alien patience pressed against their mind. The grate descends into Undermountain. They do not follow. Floon is rescued. The party surfaces. Volo, embarrassed and grateful, signs over the deed to a derelict tavern in Trollskull Alley. It is called Trollskull Manor. It has not served a drink in months. It is now theirs.

### Scene 1: The Yawning Portal

**Purpose:** Establish the Yawning Portal as a stage for faction conflict, introduce Durnan as formidable authority, deliver Volo's quest, and plant the BD observer seed. Three villain factions are visible before the party accepts any job.

**Content:**

*The Tavern in Full Swing.* The party arrives at the Yawning Portal to meet their contact, who has been there for at least an hour. The tavern is crowded and loud — adventurers, merchants, off-duty Watch guards, dock workers who have no business being this far north. The contact is **Threestrings** (Mattrim Mereg, see Appendix A): a lanky human bard who performs near the back wall playing a lute strung with three strings instead of six, which is all he says he needs. He catches the party's eye, gives a nod toward a free table, and makes his way over during a break between songs. He is waiting for a writer named Volo Geddarm who needs help finding a friend. "Shouldn't be long. Have a drink. Something interesting happens here every night."

NPCs available during this opening window: *Durnan* behind the bar — minimal words, one question answered per exchange, never initiates conversation; a DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check reveals he is watching every table in the room simultaneously even while appearing focused on his work. *Bonnie*, one of the barmaids, warm and efficient; she knows everyone in the room and will share tavern gossip freely; nothing she says is untrue, but she controls what she reveals. *Two veteran adventurers at the table nearest the well* — a dwarf woman and a half-elf man taking bets on whether tonight's "Dipper" makes it back; they explain the custom cheerfully to anyone who asks. The well itself is visible from anywhere in the taproom: a ten-foot-wide shaft in the center of the floor, surrounded by a low stone lip, a wooden winch and bell rope descending into darkness. A handwritten sign reads: UNDERMOUNTAIN ENTRY — 1 SILVER PER DESCENT — NO REFUNDS. One figure in the crowd does not fit the general energy: a drow woman posing as a Calimshan merchant, seated at the observation balcony table with an untouched glass of wine. She has been at that table every evening for two weeks. She is a Bregan D'Aerthe field observer cataloguing who descends into Undermountain and who comes back out.

*The Bar Brawl.* Within fifteen minutes of the party's arrival, the brawl starts. Yagra Stonefist (see Appendix A), a Doom Raiders operative and Davil Starsong's bodyguard, has been nursing a drink at the bar while watching a table of five Xanathar Guild thugs led by Krentz — a man with eye-shaped tattoos shaved into his scalp. Two nights ago, Krentz's crew killed a Doom Raiders operative in the Dock Ward. One of the Xanathar thugs says something audible across the room about snake men getting what they deserved. Yagra crosses the room. Before she throws the first punch, she says: "You had no quarrel with us. Those weren't your turf. That was my friend." Krentz: "Your friend." Pause. "He's dead." First punch.

Yagra's line is the party's earliest signal that the Dock Ward killing was personal — and that the faction at that bar is not the same faction that Durnan will eventually introduce in the form of Davil Starsong. Two Zhentarim cells operate under the same symbol; Yagra's Doom Raiders and Urstul Floxin's Splinter are enemies in everything but name. This distinction is introduced through lived conflict, before any NPC explains it.

**Party reaction branches — the brawl:**
- *Party helps Yagra:* The fight ends faster. Durnan still ejects Krentz's companions, but with less damage to the furniture. After: Yagra is briefly annoyed ("I had it") but acknowledges the party — "Good punch. Better timing." She buys them a drink and, if they mention looking for work, names Davil Starsong at the Doom Raiders. She is not warm until the troll fight; the drink is professional courtesy.
- *Party helps Krentz or the Xanathar thugs:* Yagra turns on the party briefly before Durnan ends it. She is not hostile after — but she will not work with this party without a significant reconciliation effort in Arc B or later.
- *Party stays out:* Yagra knocks Krentz unconscious; the four companions beat her unconscious in return; Durnan ejects all five Xanathar thugs. Yagra wakes at the bar with a bruised jaw and assesses the party once: "Watchers." Not hostile. Not warm.
- *Party attempts a peaceful resolution:* Possible — DC 16 Charisma (Persuasion) interrupts the fight before the first punch. Krentz and his companions leave muttering. Yagra looks at whoever stopped it: "Nice words." She goes back to drinking. No grudge, no warmth. The DC is high because she wanted the fight; talking her down is a genuine achievement.

Durnan watches until Krentz's companions pile in, at which point: one tankard flung, the word "Out," three Xanatharians carrying Krentz's unconscious body through the door. He says nothing else about the brawl.

*The Dip and the Going Down Song.* With the brawl settled and the room returning to noise, an announcement: a young man named Brandoch is going into Undermountain tonight. He approaches the well with a theatrical bow, checks the rope, and rings the bell once. The crowd reacts immediately.

The Going Down Song is a Yawning Portal tradition older than Durnan's tenure. Threestrings picks up his lute and leads it from the back of the room; the crowd joins on the chorus; the two veterans at the well table sing loudest. The song is upbeat, bawdy, performed in the key of C major and bad decisions. Every verse ends with the crowd counting down together. Durnan tolerates it. During the song, Brandoch descends into the well with the bell rope. [Note for final prose: adapt the Going Down Song lyrics from the original WDH campaign and Alexandrian Remix source. Do not reproduce licensed text verbatim — the structuring draft notes the tradition and its character. Confirm that Threestrings is named as the song leader and that the party can hear it clearly from their table.]

Three minutes pass. The bell rings three times in quick succession. The crowd cheers. Bets paid. "He made it!" Threestrings plays a brief victory riff.

Then the rope goes taut again — not a bell-ring rhythm. Something is climbing. Brandoch appears over the well's lip, moving fast, saying nothing. Six stirges are attached to him. Behind him, hauling itself up: the troll.

*The Troll Fight.* Durnan vaults the bar when the troll's head clears the rim — not one moment before. Greatsword in hand, already moving: "You focus on the stirges. The troll is mine." He does not look at the party when he says it.

Yagra (if conscious) joins the troll without being asked. She positions on its flank and pulls its attention. She does not take orders.

**Party reaction branches — the troll fight:**
- *Party focuses on the stirges (their stated assignment):* Durnan drives the troll back into the well when it drops. He says "You fought well" to any character who killed a stirge, then returns to the bar. Yagra nods once at the party.
- *Party also engages the troll:* Durnan notices. He keeps the troll's primary attention himself, preventing it from turning fully on party members who wade in. After the fight: "Useful. But that was mine." Said without heat — this is the closest Durnan gets to a compliment with strangers. The party has his respect, not just his acknowledgment.
- *Party uses fire or acid on the troll (preventing regeneration):* Durnan responds with one word: "Good." This is his highest register of praise. Yagra, if conscious, adds: "Experienced. Nice." The correct use of fire against a troll is not default knowledge; rewarding it signals to the party that the people in this room know exactly what they're doing.
- *Party does nothing:* Durnan and Yagra handle everything. Durnan does not address the party after the fight. He goes back behind the bar. The party is not acknowledged.

Brandoch survives. He is at the bar in shock. He turned back immediately after reaching the bottom — something large was in the dark. He accepts a drink from Durnan, which regulars at nearby tables note as extremely unusual.

*Volo's Quest.* Volo (see Appendix A) pushes through the settling crowd, steered to the party's table by Threestrings. He has already decided to hire the party based on Threestrings's recommendation; the troll fight confirmed his judgment. He offers 10 gp per character upfront, 100 gp per character when Floon is returned alive. DC 10 Wisdom (Insight): Volo is honest but stretching the truth about the 100 gp (he is awaiting royalties from *Volo's Guide to Monsters*; the book he is currently writing — *Volo's Guide to Spirits and Specters* — is not going well). He describes Floon: wavy red-blond hair, early thirties, more charming than sensible, wearing princely garb the night he disappeared. He last saw Floon at the Skewered Dragon last night. He does not know that Renaer Neverember met Floon after Volo left.

*Faction Visibility.* Doom Raiders: Yagra, flying snake tattoo on her neck, Zhentarim pendant worn openly. Xanathar Guild: Krentz and four thugs with eye-shaped tattoos on shaved heads. Bregan D'Aerthe: the silent drow observer at the balcony table. Manshoon's Zhentarim: not present at the Yawning Portal; the Dock Ward killing Yagra referenced is their work.

**Tone:** Loud, dangerous, and darkly funny. This is a city where a troll climbs out of a hole in the floor and the innkeeper handles it with four words. The comedy is real; the threat is real. Volo's earnestness cuts through the chaos cleanly.

### Scene 2: The Dock Ward Investigation

**Purpose:** Teach the Three Clue Rule through practice. Provide three independent routes to the warehouse so no single failed roll can stall the arc. Establish the Dock Ward as Xanathar territory and show the gang war as a lived condition, not a backdrop.

**Content:**

*Dock Ward Environment.* Streets are dark — most streetlamps smashed, candles stolen. Salt air and something worse. Tall tenements press together until midday light barely reaches the cobblestones. Yellow chalk eye-symbols on a few alley walls for those who know them (DC 15 Perception; finding them does not advance the investigation but tells a character familiar with the Xanathar Guild that this is their territory).

*Three Independent Clue Paths to Revelation #1 (Who took Floon and Renaer):*

- **Blood in the Streets.** En route from the Yawning Portal into the Dock Ward — before the party reaches the Xoblob Shop or the Skewered Dragon — they cross a City Watch crime scene. A dozen guards have arrested three surviving Zhentarim thugs from a street brawl: black leather armor, hands on heads, flying snake tattoos visible on necks and forearms. The Watch waves the party off, but the tattoos are in plain view. These are **Manshoon's Splinter Zhentarim**, not the Doom Raiders; they report to Kolat Towers, not to Davil Starsong. A DC 13 Intelligence (History) check with any criminal, faction, or guild background identifies the tattoo as Zhentarim; distinguishing which cell is not possible from a tattoo alone.
- **Old Xoblob Shop.** A deep gnome spy for the Xanathar Guild, Xoblob witnessed the attack outside his shop. He is reluctant to talk; a bribe of any purple item or a DC 13 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion) check loosens him. He describes five men in black leather armor grabbing Floon and a well-dressed companion outside his door; one had a winged snake tattoo on his neck. He also notes that one of the attackers grabbed the companion's silver necklace — pendant shaped like a horse, or possibly a unicorn. The stuffed beholder in Xoblob's window is a magical sensor through which Xanathar occasionally views the street; this is not discoverable at Arc A level.
- **Skewered Dragon.** A dim Dock Ward dive owned and operated by **Manshoon's Splinter Zhentarim** as an informal ward headquarters. Regulars here remember Volo and Floon from last night. After Volo left, Floon stayed and met Renaer Neverember. They drank, played Three-Dragon Ante, and left together around midnight. Five men followed them out. "Those men haven't come back," says one regular. "Flying snake tattoos. They keep a warehouse on Candle Lane." This clue delivers Revelation #1. The regulars will also name Candle Lane if pressed, giving the party both revelations in a single stop; this is not a formal third path to Revelation #2 per the Three Clue Rule, but it is the fastest route for parties who follow Volo's lead. The current regulars are Splinter men on their own ground; the party is in unfriendly territory if they push too hard without social skill.

*Three Independent Clue Paths to Revelation #2 (Warehouse location on Candle Lane):*

- **Neighborhood Canvass.** Asking around the blocks surrounding the Xoblob Shop, the crime scene, and the Skewered Dragon takes roughly ten minutes of active questioning. Not everyone in the Dock Ward talks to strangers about gangs:
  — *A shellfish vendor on Sail Street* (dwarf woman, taciturn): pulls her cart aside when strangers approach; DC 15 Persuasion to engage; if persuaded, she has seen the snake-tattoo men going in and out of a warehouse on Candle Lane for months, bringing crates at odd hours.
  — *A boy selling fish wrappers near the crime scene* (eleven years old, opportunistic): claims the men are sailors from the *Scarlet Gull* at the East Dock — a red herring (he overheard a Watch guard say something about sailors and filled in the rest). Will demand 2 cp for any further "information," all of it vague or wrong.
  — *Two dockworkers eating lunch in a doorway on Dock Street* (both human male, nervous): they have seen the snake-tattoo men; DC 13 Persuasion (DC 11 if the party shows any faction credential or Watch badge) confirms: a warehouse at the end of Candle Lane. "Don't say we told you."
  — *An old woman at an upstairs window on a cross alley*: noticed only if the party looks up — she has been watching the crime scene all morning. Calls down unprompted that she has seen those men going in and out of the Candle Lane place for a long time, asks if the Watch has finally done something. No check required.
- **Blood in the Streets prisoners.** The three arrested Splinter thugs are held at the crime scene while wagons are arranged to transport them. **The party has no faction contacts at Arc A level.** The Watch guards will not release the prisoners or allow unsupervised access. Options: (a) A character who names Floon Blagmaar or Renaer Neverember as the subject of a kidnapping can request a brief supervised conversation — the Watch sergeant (DC 14 Persuasion) is skeptical but allows it with a cooperative prisoner; (b) a bribe of 5 gp to a specific guard (DC 12 Persuasion) buys a five-minute supervised exchange; (c) a character with a City Watch background can invoke prior service without a check, limited to two questions per prisoner. Any cooperative prisoner names Candle Lane. Any prisoner who refuses requires DC 15 Intimidation — possible but difficult with four Watch guards present and watching.
- **Floon's bead trail.** Floon's necklace — a holy symbol of Lurue, a unicorn bas-relief on a blue pearl chain — shed beads during the struggle outside Xoblob's. A DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check spots the trail on the cobblestones; the beads are pale blue against dark stone and easy to miss in shadow. Following the trail leads directly to the mouth of Candle Lane and ends at the warehouse gate. If the party already knows about Candle Lane from another path, the trail still confirms which building.

*Proactive Failsafes.* If the party fails to find any path to the warehouse (unlikely given four independent routes): (a) A pair of Manshoon's Splinter thugs approaches the party at the Skewered Dragon and warns them to stop asking questions about the Renaer kidnapping — naming the subject they should be investigating. (b) A Splinter squad arrives on Candle Lane while the party is nearby and fights a group of kenku in the street directly in front of the warehouse, effectively delivering the address by combat.

*Faction Visibility.* Xanathar Guild: yellow chalk territory marks (passive). Manshoon's Splinter Zhentarim: under arrest at the crime scene, or at the Skewered Dragon, or approaching to intimidate. Doom Raiders: not present in the Dock Ward; the bar brawl at the Yawning Portal established the faction fracture.

**Tone:** Oppressive, lived-in, and slightly dangerous. Players should feel the gang war as a real condition — not backdrop, but the reason everyone on this street is moving fast and looking at their shoes. Investigation scenes should feel like opening a puzzle box, not grinding through skill checks.

### Scene 3: The Zhentarim Warehouse (Candle Lane)

**Purpose:** Deliver the arc's first major location — a twelve-area keyed warehouse occupied by Xanathar's kenku — teach the stealth-or-force choice that recurs in every heist arc, introduce Renaer Neverember as an ally, and establish three clues pointing to the sewer hideout. The warehouse is the arc's emotional pivot: the party finds the wrong person, learns what actually happened, and gets new leads pointing down.

**Content:**

*Location Overview.* The warehouse sits at the back of a yard behind a high fence; the gate is unlocked. Three entry points: front door, loading bay, painted-over window — all locked (DC 12 Dexterity with thieves' tools, DC 10 Strength [Athletics] to force). A black winged snake is painted above the front door handle; DC 10 Intelligence identifies it as the Zhentarim symbol. Four kenku occupy the building — left by Xanathar to kill any Zhentarim who return. Renaer Neverember is in area Z2 (storage closet), having slipped his bonds and hidden before the kenku began their search. The building is Manshoon's Zhentarim property. The Doom Raiders have no presence here.

*Keyed Areas (five areas; all receive full keyed entries in the final prose draft):*

- **Z1 — Main Room.** Tables and chairs overturned. Twelve corpses along the walls: five Zhentarim thugs (flying snake tattoos on neck or forearm) and seven Xanathar Guild thugs (one has a circle with ten spokes on their right palm — Xanathar's mark). Rapiers and daggers lying nearby. Crates of weapons, rations, boots, and black uniforms. Four kenku are here unless they heard the party enter, in which case they hide behind overturned furniture. Kenku speak only in mimicked voices; DC 10 Charisma (Intimidation) to force useful repetition — captured kenku say, in three different voices: "Xanathar sends its regards" (deep, orcish-accented); "Tie up the pretty boy in the back room!" and "Follow the yellow signs in the sewers" (thin, nasally); "No time to loot the place. Just get him to the boss" (scratchy). Characters attempting to enter without announcing themselves make a group DC 13 Dexterity (Stealth) check against the kenku's passive Perception of 13. If the kenku heard noise before entry (forced door, combat in the yard), the DC rises to 16.
- **Z2 — Storage Closet.** Renaer Neverember is here: unarmed, bruised, cooperative, and waiting for the kenku to be dealt with before he attempts escape. The room smells of rancid pickled herring.
- **Z3 — Secret Room.** Hidden behind a secret door (DC 15 Wisdom [Perception] to find; opening it rings a faint bell in Z5). Four wood-framed paintings wrapped in leather, depicting Luskan, Neverwinter, Silverymoon, and Baldur's Gate — 75 gp each. Fifteen 10-pound silver trade bars, black from corrosion but worth 50 gp each.
- **Z4 — Balcony.** Open second level overlooking the main warehouse. Crates of junk: moth-eaten cloth, bottles of spoiled olive oil, hundreds of pairs of wooden-soled sandals out of fashion last summer. Nothing of value.
- **Z5 — Offices.** A suite of offices used for Zhentarim cell planning. Desks, chairs, bare shelves. Steel alarm bells above each office door — wired to the Z3 secret door, ringing loudly when it is opened. Harmless rats. One paper bird (magic item, WDH Appendix A) on a desk. This is the room where Zhentarim agents discovered the Eye of the Stone of Golorr hidden inside Renaer's mourning locket. The locket still lies here — an elaborate ivory case on a broken chain, its secret compartment pried open and empty. DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) to spot it under a desk.

*Finding Renaer.* Renaer (see Appendix A) speaks freely once the kenku are dealt with, asking only for confirmation that the building is clear before emerging from Z2. He explains: five Zhentarim grabbed him and Floon outside Old Xoblob Shop last night. They brought him here and questioned him about his father's embezzled hoard — money Renaer refuses to help his father hide. They took his mourning locket, which he believed held a keepsake from his mother; he did not know there was a compartment inside. The Xanathar Guild attacked before the interrogation went further. He slipped his bonds in the confusion and hid. From a gap in the Z2 door he watched Xanathar agents drag an unconscious Floon — the man they mistook for Renaer — through the main room, out through the yard, and down the alley toward a sewer grate.

What Renaer freely shares: the Zhentarim wanted information about his father's gold; they took his locket; he watched Xanathar's agents drag Floon through the main room, out through the yard, and down the alley toward a sewer grate.
What Renaer does not know: what the locket contained; where the Vault is or how to access it; the Stone of Golorr's role in any of this. He believed the Eye was a piece of his mother's jewelry.
What Renaer mentions if asked about his father's enemies: the Cassalanters have been quietly acquiring properties near the Brandath family tombs — his mother's family plots. He does not know why. He raises it as an oddity, not an accusation.

*Three Independent Clue Paths to Revelation #3 (Floon is in the Xanathar Guild sewer hideout):*

- **Renaer's account.** He watched Xanathar agents drag Floon through the main room, out through the yard, and down the alley to a sewer grate. He describes them — circle-eye tattoos matching the dead in Z1. No check required.
- **Yellow chalk guildsign.** Xanathar's agents left guildsign on the alley wall outside the warehouse yard, pointing toward the sewer entrance half a block east. DC 11 Wisdom (Survival) to track the agents' route through the yard to the sewer grate; guildsign is visible at the grate itself with no check once the party reaches it.
- **Kenku interrogation.** Any captured kenku repeats the mimicked voices from Z1 under DC 10 Charisma (Intimidation): "Follow the yellow signs in the sewers" and "Just get him to the boss." A kenku that agrees to guide the party to the sewer entrance will attempt to betray them at the first opportunity, alerting the Q2 goblins before it can be stopped.

**Tone:** Grimly revelatory. The party arrived looking for Floon and found the wrong person — who turns out to matter more to the campaign's back story. The warehouse should read as a crime scene being picked through: cold, quiet except for kenku, and full of evidence that something worse happened here than a simple kidnapping.

### Scene 4: The Xanathar Guild Sewer Hideout

**Purpose:** Deliver the arc's climax location, rescue Floon, plant Nihiloor as the campaign's deepest foreshadowing thread, and establish the physical connection between Waterdeep's streets and Undermountain. The sewer hideout is where surface-city politics meet what lies beneath — literally.

**Content:**

*Navigation.* The Xanathar agents exited the warehouse yard and entered the sewers via a grate in the alley half a block east. Revelation #3 guides the party here. Once inside, three DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) checks are required to navigate to the hideout using guildsign at each intersection; a party already following guildsign from the alley entrance has advantage on all three checks. A party that fails all three checks arrives at Q7 too late — the interrogation is over and Floon's body is in Q7. A party that fails two of three checks makes enough noise in wrong tunnels to be heard at Q1: the Q2 goblins wake.

Midway through the route, at a branching tunnel intersection, a **gazer guard** — a small beholder-kin under the Guild cell's command — watches the junction. It is hostile and cannot be negotiated with. A party moving carefully (group DC 13 Dexterity [Stealth] check) may neutralize or bypass it without alerting the hideout. A party that defeats it in open combat should assume the noise carries to Q1 — the kenku sentries are now listening. The gazer's presence is one of the WDH source's two signals that Xanathar's surveillance network extends into the sewers; defeating or bypassing it opens the correct passage, which leads to the hideout in five minutes following the guildsign.

*Location Overview.* An eleven-room Xanathar Guild cell built into a decommissioned sewer vault beneath the Dock Ward. The complex runs from Q1 (entry, eastern approach) through Q11 (stone portal, western end). No natural light; the goblins in Q2 rely on darkvision. All interior doors are unlocked — Grum'shar assumes only those with guild business will reach this place. The Q7 boss chamber holds Floon and is where Nihiloor makes its appearance; Q11 is the escape portal Nihiloor uses to vanish into Xanathar's lair.

*Keyed Areas (all eleven areas receive full keyed entries in the final prose draft; this draft establishes their content and dramatic function):*

- **Q1 — Central Hub.** The party approaches from the east, wading through one foot of sewage. A secret door in the south tunnel wall (DC 15 Wisdom [Perception] to find) connects to Q9. The main passage continues west into the complex. If failed navigation or gazer combat has alerted the complex, the Q2 goblins are no longer asleep.
- **Q2 — Watch Posts.** Split into Q2a and Q2b, one goblin in each alcove flanking Q1. Both asleep, relying on darkvision. DC 9 Dexterity (Stealth) to sneak past. If alerted by failed navigation noise or the gazer fight, both goblins are awake and watching Q1.
- **Q3 — Messy Room.** Rusty weapons and threadbare clothing hang on hooks. Nothing of value. A coat rack has fallen across the passage.
- **Q4 — Empty Sleeping Area.** Six tattered straw-stuffed mattresses on the floor. Characters can hear activity from Q5 through the thin wooden door.
- **Q5 — Sleeping Area.** Six more mattresses. **Zemk** (duergar, hostile) is here, using furniture to barricade the door to Q6 — trying to contain the gray ooze that escaped Q6's cesspit. If Krentz survived the Yawning Portal brawl in Scene 1, he is here too, arguing that the blanket approach won't work. Neither notices intruders unless they make noise. Zemk fights to death; Krentz reacts based on how the party treated him at the Yawning Portal.
- **Q6 — Lavatory.** A cesspit hole in the floor opens onto a chamber below. A **gray ooze** emerged from the pit and killed two goblins sent to dispose of it; goblin bones float in the ooze. The goblins' weapons lie on the floor — pitted and unsalvageable.
- **Q7 — Boss Chamber.** When the party arrives (if not too late): Grum'shar (CE half-orc wizard) stands with his foot on Floon's chest, fire burning around his clenched fist, interrogating him. Nihiloor (mind flayer) is seated on a raised platform to the south, cradling an **intellect devourer**. The moment Nihiloor sees the party, it rises, sets the intellect devourer down (it is now loose and hostile), and glides toward the double door in the western wall — heading for Q11 and the escape portal. Nihiloor uses dominate monster on any creature that blocks its path. It does not stay to fight. Grum'shar and the intellect devourer cover its escape. Floon is alive but has 1 hit point — Grum'shar's interrogation has nearly killed him. If healed, Floon hugs everyone. If the party arrives too late (all three navigation checks failed): Grum'shar is gone, Nihiloor is gone, and Floon's body is on the floor. Grum'shar's satchel (whether he fled or is defeated here) contains his spellbook: burning hands, disguise self, false life, shield, unseen servant, witch bolt. Chest (unlocked, behind the stone chair): two potions of healing, 16 gp, 82 sp, 250 cp.
- **Q8 — Getaway Passage.** A rough-hewn tunnel hidden beneath a loose flagstone (DC 10 Wisdom [Perception] to find). The low tunnel leads north to Q9.
- **Q9 — Private Cellar.** A halfling family — the Peabodys — brews beer here in a cellar beneath their home on Fishgut Alley. Brewing supplies and clutter. The halflings have not noticed the secret tunnel beneath their flagstone. If the party surfaces through the tunnel, the Peabodys are initially alarmed but easily calmed. A potential Arc B resource: their cellar provides a second sewer entry and exit point for future Dock Ward operations.
- **Q10 — Sleeping Quarters.** Straw pallets on the floor. Rusty manacles bolted to the walls. Empty when the party arrives.
- **Q11 — Escape Portal.** In the center of this otherwise empty room stands a stone pillar carved with Xanathar's guildsign — a circle with ten equidistant spokes — with a small circular indentation shaped like a lidless eye at its center. If Nihiloor has not yet escaped: it presses its 3-inch stone orb into the indentation; an opaque black doorway (8 feet tall, 4 feet wide) opens in the south wall, and Nihiloor steps through into area X22 of Xanathar's lair. Any creature that follows appears in X22. The portal closes 1 minute after the orb is removed. Without the orb, the portal cannot be activated. If Nihiloor has already escaped before the party reaches Q11: the room is empty; the pillar is inert. DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana) identifies the indentation as a key socket; DC 16 Intelligence (History) identifies the guildsign as Xanathar's mark. This room is a seed for Arc F — the party will encounter X22 and the portal's other side when they infiltrate Xanathar's lair.

*Nihiloor.* Nihiloor (see Appendix E) arrives at Q7 before the party, using Grum'shar's interrogation session as cover to observe Floon. The party's encounter with it is designed to be a sight-line, not a fight: they see it rise, see it release the intellect devourer, and watch it glide purposefully out of the room. It does not attack the party directly — it uses dominate monster only to clear its escape path if blocked. The goal of this moment is not horror; it is scale. The party glimpses what is actually running the Xanathar Guild cell and understands immediately that Grum'shar was never the top of this structure. Nihiloor's full scope surfaces across Arcs B through F.

*Mad Mage Seeds.* The Q11 stone portal is the deepest Undermountain seed in Arc A. When Nihiloor steps through and the black doorway closes behind it, the party has witnessed a mind flayer access a route they cannot yet follow. The portal terminus in X22 of Xanathar's lair connects the sewer hideout to the dungeon's faction economy — a thread that becomes load-bearing in Arc F. Parties that examine the inert pillar in Q11 after Nihiloor has fled collect one piece of the puzzle; the rest arrives in Arc F when they reach the other side.

*Rescuing Floon.* Floon is alive and unharmed beyond bruising. He does not know why he was taken. He is grateful, disoriented, and stays close to whoever freed him for the rest of the session. The party's reunion of Floon and Renaer — whether at the warehouse on the way out, at the Yawning Portal, or at Trollskull Manor — is the emotional close of the arc.

**Tone:** Claustrophobic, escalating, and quietly terrifying. The sewer hideout is a descent — not just into the earth, but into the campaign's deeper layers. The kenku and thugs are a conventional dungeon fight; Q9 is not. Players should leave with the sense that Waterdeep has something much older and stranger beneath it than they expected.

### Milestone and Transition

**Purpose:** Mark the arc's two narrative achievements with XP, transition the party to their new home base, and close the investigation loop with Volo's reward.

**Content:**

*Milestone XP.*

| Level | Arc | Milestone | XP |
|:-----:|-----|-----------|---:|
| 1 → 2 | A | The party clears the Zhentarim warehouse and finds Renaer Neverember alive | 100 |
| 1 → 2 | A | The party rescues Floon Blagmaar from the Xanathar Guild sewer hideout | 200 |

Both milestones are awarded regardless of order — some parties may follow the bead trail past the warehouse and encounter Renaer only on the way out. Level 2 is reached when both milestones are awarded.

*Volo's Payment.* Volo does not have the 100 gp per character. He produces the deed to a derelict tavern in Trollskull Alley, North Ward, describing it as "worth considerably more than the gold owed" and "an investment in your futures." DC 12 Wisdom (Insight) confirms he is genuinely embarrassed. He offers to cover the gold difference "once the royalties arrive from Mordenkainen." He departs before anyone can press him. The deed is real. The tavern — Trollskull Manor — is real, non-operational, and now theirs.

*Aftermath.* Renaer (see Appendix A) stays in contact after the arc. He offers his North Ward townhouse as an occasional resource in Arc B and takes a quiet interest in the party's next moves; he knows his father's enemies are not done. Floon thanks everyone, invites the party to drinks he cannot afford, and reappears in Arc B as a Trollskull Alley regular with a gambling problem the party may eventually need to address.

*Transition to Arc B.* The party holds a deed to a derelict tavern. They are residents of Trollskull Alley. The city faction recruitment, Manor renovation, and the first rumors of something called "Neverember's Enigma" begin in Arc B.

**Tone:** Warm, slightly chaotic, and quietly triumphant. The party solved a crime in a city full of crimes. They did not stop anything — the Eye Manshoon's cell extracted from Renaer's locket is already in Kolat Towers. But they saved two people and earned a home. That should feel like something.

### Design Notes: Finding Floon

***Investigation Restructuring.*** The original Chapter 1 presents the Dock Ward investigation as a sequential chain: Skewered Dragon → warehouse name → Renaer → sewer. A single failed Charisma check at the Skewered Dragon can stall the party indefinitely, because the original text makes that tavern the sole source for the warehouse's location. The Three Clue Rule redesign distributes the warehouse address across four independent paths for Revelation #2 — the Skewered Dragon itself (simultaneously serving Revelation #1 and #2), neighborhood canvass, prisoner questioning, and the dropped bead trail — so that no single failure blocks progress. Revelation #1 has three independent paths; Revelation #3 has three. The four-path Revelation #2 reflects the warehouse address being the bottleneck in the original — extra redundancy where the original was most fragile. Players who follow all paths simultaneously discover more about the world; players who follow one path successfully still reach the warehouse.

***The Two Zhentarims in Arc A.*** The original adventure presents the Zhentarim as a single faction. This remix uses Arc A to establish the faction fracture that defines the Manshoon subplot. Yagra Stonefist — a Doom Raiders operative, Davil Starsong's people — is at the Yawning Portal bar, openly wearing the Zhentarim pendant, fighting Xanathar Guild thugs. The Candle Lane warehouse belongs to Manshoon's cell, Urstul Floxin's people, operating separately from Davil. Players who later join the Doom Raiders in Arc B and then raid Kolat Towers in Arc I will have seen both Zhentarim factions from Scene 1 — one as a potential ally, one as an adversary, both operating under the same symbol. The distinction should be felt before it is explained.

***Bregan D'Aerthe Observer.*** The drow woman at the Yawning Portal balcony table is the campaign's earliest Bregan D'Aerthe presence. She is never named in Scene 1 and the party is not meant to notice her unless they ask who is watching the well. She reappears in Arc B as a background presence — same table, different evenings — and is eventually identified as part of Jarlaxle's Waterdeep intelligence network monitoring the Undermountain entrance. Her placement in Scene 1 is a retroactive payoff: by the time the party is dealing with the Sea Maidens Faire in Arc H, they can look back at the Yawning Portal and understand that Jarlaxle has had eyes on them since their first night in the city.

***Cassalanter Name-Drop Through Renaer.*** Renaer's aside about the Cassalanters' property acquisitions near the Brandath tombs is designed to sit unexamined in player notes until Arc G. He does not frame it as a threat or a suspicion — he raises it as an oddity he cannot explain. Players who track it will revisit the detail when the Cassalanter Villa becomes a heist target. Players who do not track it will have it recapitulated by Renaer in Arc G when the connection becomes relevant. The name-drop is passive: it seeds without pressing. A first-level party has no use for it yet, and none is offered.

***Nihiloor as Foreshadowing.*** Q9 in the sewer hideout is the campaign's first contact with Nihiloor, who is eventually revealed in Arc F to be the true intelligence driving the Xanathar Guild — running three parallel projects, exercising psionic control over Xanathar, and infiltrating Waterdeep's civil institutions (see Appendix E). In Arc A, Nihiloor does not engage with the party. A telepathic pulse and a silhouette in the dark are the full extent of the encounter. This restraint is intentional. Players who investigate Q9 should feel that they glimpsed something vast and wrong that they could not reach. Nihiloor's full scope surfaces across Arcs B through F. By the time the party reaches Undermountain Level 17 in the Dungeon of the Mad Mage, the creature's presence in Arc A should read as a thread that began in the sewers under Trollskull Alley.

***Renaer's Introduction.*** In the original, finding Renaer in the warehouse is a pleasant surprise in an otherwise thin scene — a friendly NPC who points the party downward. This redesign makes Renaer's introduction structural: the warehouse is the arc's pivot, not its anticlimax. The party arrives expecting Floon and finds someone who matters more to the campaign's back story. Renaer's knowledge state is asymmetric by design — he knows what Manshoon's cell wanted and why the locket mattered to his father, without knowing what the locket contained. His ignorance is genuine. This positions him as a trustworthy ally (he does not conceal things he knows) while preserving the campaign's central mystery (the nature of the Eyes, the Vault's location). The gap between what Renaer knows and what the party will eventually learn gives the warehouse scene a retrospective weight it lacks in the original.

***Stealth-or-Force Preview.*** The warehouse presents the arc's first real structural choice: enter quietly and catch the kenku off-guard, or force entry and fight a prepared defense. Neither approach is obviously correct. This binary — infiltration versus direct assault — is the same decision the party will face at every heist target in Arcs F through J. Arc A is the tutorial, with first-level stakes and kenku rather than guild lieutenants. Each subsequent heist arc's design notes should acknowledge this parallel explicitly, so the DM can frame escalating heist complexity as the campaign's through-line skill development rather than a separate genre convention dropped into the story.

***Durnan's Secret.*** Durnan's Masked Lord status and Red Sashes leadership (see Appendix A, DM-layer context) are not revealed in Arc A and are not hinted at in any player-facing text. His Scene 1 portrayal — minimal words, the troll handled in seconds, "You fought well" as the full debrief — is designed to read as "formidable retired adventurer" to players and as "Masked Lord making deliberate choices about how much power to display" to the DM. The gap between those two readings is Durnan's dramatic potential. If the party gains enough standing to learn his secrets in Arc B or beyond, Scene 1 retroactively becomes a performance — a man who could have stopped that brawl before the first punch deciding it was more useful to let it play out.

## 3. Key Decisions Before Writing

All nine decisions are settled. No open items.

1. **Bar brawl trigger and scope.** The Alexandrian's six-step enhanced opening: contact (Threestrings) greets party → pre-brawl tavern atmosphere and NPC interactions → Yagra (Doom Raiders) vs. Krentz (Xanathar) → The Dip ritual and Going Down Song → troll and stirges → Durnan's and Yagra's reactions → Volo introduced through Threestrings.
2. **Party contact at the Yawning Portal.** **Threestrings** (Mattrim Mereg) — the bard performing at the YP. He greets the party, leads the Going Down Song, and introduces Volo. He is already in Appendix A; no new profile needed.
3. **BD watcher identity.** **Ryvarra** — a drow woman posing as a Calimshan cloth merchant. Full profile written and added to Appendix A (Section 11, Bregan D'Aerthe). Named field observer in Jarlaxle's Waterdeep intelligence network.
4. **Which Zhentarim cell at the warehouse.** Manshoon's cell — Urstul Floxin's thugs. Not the Doom Raiders. The Eye extracted from Renaer's locket was delivered to Kolat Towers before the Xanathar Guild raid.
5. **Skewered Dragon ownership.** Manshoon's Splinter Zhentarim. The Splinter operates the Skewered Dragon as an informal Dock Ward headquarters. The five men who followed Floon and Renaer out were Splinter thugs who have not returned.
6. **Cassalanter name-drop delivery.** Renaer dialogue in Scene 3. He mentions the Cassalanters' property acquisitions near the Brandath tombs as an unexplained oddity, not a suspicion.
7. **Nihiloor visibility level.** Q7: Nihiloor is seen briefly — rising, releasing its intellect devourer, gliding toward Q11. It uses dominate monster only to clear its escape path. No direct engagement. It vanishes through the Q11 portal into X22 of Xanathar's lair.
8. **Renaer's knowledge state.** Freely shares: Zhentarim interrogation about his father's money; locket seizure; Floon dragged through the main room, out through the yard, and down the alley to a sewer grate. Does not know: locket contents; Vault location; Stone of Golorr's role. His ignorance is genuine.
9. **Clue path counts.** Revelation #1: three paths (Blood in the Streets, Xoblob's testimony, Skewered Dragon regulars). Revelation #2: three formal paths (neighborhood canvass, prisoner interrogation, Floon's bead trail) — the Skewered Dragon also names Candle Lane as a bonus for parties who visit it, but is not a separate listed path per the Alexandrian. Revelation #3: three paths (Renaer's eyewitness account, yellow guildsign at the alley sewer grate, kenku interrogation).
