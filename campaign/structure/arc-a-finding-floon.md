# Arc A: Finding Floon

---

## Chapter Purpose

Arc A is the campaign's inciting incident. It introduces Waterdeep as an active faction battlefield, teaches the Three Clue Rule investigation structure that recurs throughout the campaign, and delivers the party's home base as the arc's reward. The arc exists to establish the scope of the Grand Game without overwhelming first-level characters: three villain factions are visible in Scene 1, but the party's only job is to find one man.

---

## 1. Core Concept

A street-level rescue mission drops the characters into the middle of a three-faction crossfire. Two people were kidnapped from a Dock Ward alley. One is in a warehouse, held by the wrong gang. One is in the sewers, held by the right one. The investigation teaches players exactly how the Three Clue Rule works — multiple independent paths to every answer — and ends with the party holding a deed to a derelict tavern that makes them homeowners, neighbors, and targets.

---

## 2. Sections

### Arc Opener

*An adventure for 1st-level characters.*

Three villain factions are visible inside the Yawning Portal before Volo Geddarm sits down to make his offer. Yagra Stonefist, a Doom Raiders operative and Davil Starsong's bodyguard, picks a fight with a Xanathar Guild thug named Krentz over a killing two nights ago in the Dock Ward, the same killing that lit the fuse on the ward's gang war. Four of Krentz's companions pile in. Before Durnan of the Yawning Portal can handle it himself, the Undermountain bell rings. A troll hauls itself out of the well with six stirges attached. Durnan vaults the bar, greatsword in hand, tells the party to deal with the stirges, and takes the troll. At the back of the settling taproom, seated near the observation balcony with a full glass of wine she has not touched, a drow woman watches the well. She has been at that table for two weeks. Volo arrives when the monster is dead, steered to the party's table by the contact who has been waiting for him, and describes a missing companion: Floon Blagmaar, wavy red-blond hair, early thirties, gone for two nights.

The trail begins in the Dock Ward. Three independent paths converge on a single warehouse on Candle Lane: a deep gnome shopkeeper who witnessed the kidnapping and will talk if given something purple or persuaded; dockworker regulars at the Skewered Dragon who know the warehouse address without prompting; and a trail of Floon's dropped blue pearl beads running from Old Xoblob Shop's doorstep to the mouth of Candle Lane. En route, the party crosses a City Watch crime scene: three arrested Zhentarim thugs kneeling in the street, black winged snake tattoos on their necks, while wagons arrive for the bodies.

Manshoon's Zhentarim hold the warehouse. Five of Urstul Floxin's thugs grabbed Renaer Neverember and Floon outside the Old Xoblob Shop two nights ago, brought them here, and questioned Renaer about his father's embezzled hoard. They took his mourning locket. Renaer believed it held a keepsake from his mother; it hid an Eye of the Stone of Golorr in a sealed compartment. The Eye was delivered to Kolat Towers before the Xanathar Guild raid killed most of the cell. Four kenku now search the wreckage. Renaer hid when the raid hit and is still hiding in a back room. Floon was mistaken for Renaer and dragged through a trapdoor into the sewer. Renaer, once found, tells the party what he saw: Xanathar agents, circle-eye tattoos, Floon unconscious over one man's shoulder, the trapdoor in the cellar floor. Three more clues in the warehouse confirm the route. They follow.

In the sewers, Xanathar's yellow-chalk eye markers guide the way to a converted brick vault, nine rooms of a decommissioned filtration chamber, now a Xanathar Guild cell. Floon is alive in a holding room, bruised and grateful, and his first question is about Renaer. The cell's leader carries keys to every room, including the sealed iron door at the back of the vault. Behind that door, in a chamber they were not meant to reach, something withdraws before they get a clear look: a rounded silhouette trailing three or four thin tendrils, retreating into a floor grate. Any character who approached felt it first — a single pulse of cold, alien patience pressed against their mind. The grate descends into Undermountain. They do not follow. Floon is rescued. The party surfaces. Volo, embarrassed and grateful, signs over the deed to a derelict tavern in Trollskull Alley. It is called Trollskull Manor. It has not served a drink in months. It is now theirs.

### Scene 1: The Yawning Portal

**Purpose:** Establish the Yawning Portal as a stage for faction conflict, introduce Durnan as formidable authority, deliver Volo's quest, and plant the BD observer seed. Three villain factions are visible before the party accepts any job.

**Content:**

*The Cold Open.* The party arrives to meet a contact at the Yawning Portal. The contact is already seated and waiting. One figure in the crowd — a drow woman posing as a Calimshan merchant, seated near the observation balcony with an untouched glass of wine — is a Bregan D'Aerthe field observer. She catalogues who descends into Undermountain and who comes back out. She has occupied that table every evening for two weeks. [Note: This observer requires a full profile in Appendix A before the final prose pass. Designate her as a named drow member of Jarlaxle's Waterdeep intelligence network.]

*The Bar Brawl and Troll Fight.* Yagra Stonefist (see Appendix A) challenges Krentz and four Xanathar Guild thugs over a killing in the Dock Ward two nights ago. The brawl is fast and loud and one-sided in Yagra's favor until Krentz's companions pile in. Characters who help Yagra end the fight before Durnan arrives; characters who wait watch the proprietor fling a tankard, say "Out," and watch three Xanatharians carry Krentz's unconscious body through the door. Yagra wears her Zhentarim pendant openly; the Doom Raiders / Xanathar tension is introduced without being named. In the brawl's third round, the Undermountain bell rings. A troll climbs out of the well with six stirges attached. Durnan vaults the bar, greatsword drawn, and says: "You focus on the stirges. The troll is mine." Yagra, if conscious, flanks the troll without being asked. Durnan drives the troll back into the well when it drops. He says "You fought well" to any character who handled a stirge, then returns to the bar. No further comment.

*Volo's Quest.* Volo (see Appendix A) pushes through the settling crowd to greet the party's contact and is introduced as the colleague the contact was waiting for. He has already decided to hire the party based on the contact's recommendation; the troll fight confirmed his judgment. He offers 10 gp per character upfront, 100 gp per character when Floon is returned alive. DC 10 Wisdom (Insight): Volo is honest but stretching the truth about the 100 gp (he is awaiting royalties from *Volo's Guide to Monsters*; the book he is currently writing — *Volo's Guide to Spirits and Specters* — is not going well). He describes Floon: wavy red-blond hair, early thirties, more charming than sensible, wearing princely garb the night he disappeared. He last saw Floon at the Skewered Dragon, two nights ago. He does not know that Renaer Neverember met Floon after Volo left.

*Faction Visibility.* Doom Raiders: Yagra, flying snake tattoo on her neck, openly wearing the Zhentarim pendant. Xanathar Guild: Krentz and four thugs with eye-shaped tattoos on shaved heads. Bregan D'Aerthe: the silent drow observer at the balcony table. Manshoon's Zhentarim: not present at the Yawning Portal, but the Dock Ward killing Yagra referenced is their work.

**Tone:** Loud, dangerous, and darkly funny. This is a city where a troll climbs out of a hole in the floor and the innkeeper handles it with four words. The comedy is real; the threat is real. Volo's earnestness cuts through the chaos cleanly.

### Scene 2: The Dock Ward Investigation

**Purpose:** Teach the Three Clue Rule through practice. Provide three independent routes to the warehouse so no single failed roll can stall the arc. Establish the Dock Ward as Xanathar territory and show the gang war as a lived condition, not a backdrop.

**Content:**

*Dock Ward Environment.* Streets are dark — most streetlamps smashed, candles stolen. Salt air and something worse. Tall tenements press together until midday light barely reaches the cobblestones. Yellow chalk eye-symbols on a few alley walls for those who know them (DC 15 Perception; finding them does not advance the investigation but tells a character familiar with the Xanathar Guild that this is their territory).

*Three Independent Clue Paths to Revelation #1 (Who took Floon and Renaer):*

- **Old Xoblob Shop.** A deep gnome spy for the Xanathar Guild, Xoblob witnessed the attack outside his shop. He is reluctant to talk; a bribe of any purple item or a DC 13 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion) check loosens him. He describes five men in black leather armor grabbing Floon and a well-dressed companion; one had a winged snake tattoo on his neck. He also notes that one of the attackers grabbed the companion's silver necklace — pendant like a horse, or possibly a unicorn.
- **Skewered Dragon.** Regulars at this Zhentarim-owned dive remember Volo and Floon from two nights ago. After Volo left, Floon stayed and met Renaer Neverember. They drank, played Three-Dragon Ante, and left around midnight. Five men followed them out. "Those men haven't come back," says one regular. "Flying snake tattoos. They keep a warehouse on Candle Lane. Snake on the door." This clue delivers both Revelation #1 and Revelation #2 simultaneously — the Skewered Dragon is the fastest single path.
- **Blood in the Streets.** En route to the Dock Ward, the party encounters a crime scene: a dozen City Watch guards have arrested three surviving Zhentarim thugs from a street brawl. The survivors kneel with hands on heads, black leather armor, flying snake tattoos visible on their necks and forearms. The Watch waves the party off — nothing to see here — but the tattoos are in plain view.

*Three Independent Clue Paths to Revelation #2 (Warehouse location on Candle Lane):*

- **Neighborhood canvass.** Any inquiry about the flying snake tattoo men in the surrounding blocks leads, within ten minutes, to locals who point to Candle Lane without prompting. The snake men keep a warehouse there.
- **Blood in the Streets prisoners.** Any prisoner who cooperates under DC 15 Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion) or Watch favor names Candle Lane. The Watch will consider releasing a cooperative prisoner to lead the party directly, in exchange for a positive report from a Watch-aligned faction contact.
- **Floon's bead trail.** Floon's necklace — a holy symbol of Lurue, six-inch unicorn bas-relief on a blue pearl chain — shed beads during the struggle outside Xoblob's. DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) spots the trail on the cobblestones. Following it leads directly to the mouth of Candle Lane and ends at the warehouse gate.

*Proactive Failsafes.* If the party fails to find any path to the warehouse (unlikely by design): (a) A pair of Manshoon's Zhentarim thugs approaches the party at the Skewered Dragon and warns them to stop asking questions about the Renaer kidnapping — which names the subject they should be investigating. (b) A Zhentarim squad arrives at the warehouse while the party is in the neighborhood and fights a group of kenku in the street directly in front of the building, at the address on Candle Lane.

*Faction Visibility.* Xanathar Guild: yellow chalk territory marks (passive). Manshoon's Zhentarim: under arrest at the crime scene, or approaching to intimidate. Doom Raiders: not present in the Dock Ward, but Yagra's bar fight established the faction fracture.

**Tone:** Oppressive, lived-in, and slightly dangerous. Players should feel the gang war as a real condition — not backdrop, but the reason everyone on this street is moving fast and looking at their shoes. Investigation scenes should feel like opening a puzzle box, not grinding through skill checks.

### Scene 3: The Zhentarim Warehouse (Candle Lane)

**Purpose:** Deliver the arc's first major location — a twelve-area keyed warehouse occupied by Xanathar's kenku — teach the stealth-or-force choice that recurs in every heist arc, introduce Renaer Neverember as an ally, and establish three clues pointing to the sewer hideout. The warehouse is the arc's emotional pivot: the party finds the wrong person, learns what actually happened, and gets new leads pointing down.

**Content:**

*Location Overview.* The warehouse sits at the back of a yard behind a high fence; the gate is unlocked. Three entry points: front door, loading bay, painted-over window — all locked (DC 12 Dexterity with thieves' tools, DC 10 Strength [Athletics] to force). A black winged snake is painted above the front door handle; DC 10 Intelligence identifies it as the Zhentarim symbol. Four kenku occupy the building — left by Xanathar to kill any Zhentarim who return. Renaer Neverember is in area Z2 (upper-level back room), having slipped his bonds and hidden before the kenku began their search. The building is Manshoon's Zhentarim property. The Doom Raiders have no presence here.

*Keyed Areas (all twelve areas receive full keyed entries in the final prose draft; this draft establishes their content and dramatic function):*

- **Z1 — Main Room (ground floor).** Tables and chairs overturned. Twelve corpses along the walls: five Zhentarim thugs (flying snake tattoos on neck or forearm) and seven Xanathar Guild members (one has the circle-with-ten-spokes palm tattoo — Xanathar's mark). Crates of weapons, rations, black uniforms. The four kenku are here unless they heard the party enter, in which case they hide behind overturned furniture; DC 16 passive Perception to hear the scramble.
- **Z2 — Back Room (upper level).** Renaer Neverember is here: unarmed, bruised, cooperative, and waiting for the kenku to be dealt with before he attempts escape.
- **Z3 — Office.** Manshoon's cell used this room to plan the kidnapping. Contents: a ledger of Dock Ward safe houses (all compromised, useful as Arc B intelligence resource), a note in cipher referencing "Renaer's locket — delivery confirmed" (DC 18 Intelligence to break without a cryptographer; a Harpers contact in Arc B can read it), and a hand-drawn map of the warehouse showing a concealed trapdoor in Z1.
- **Z4 — Guard Post.** Murder-holes overlooking the loading bay. Currently empty; the guards are dead in Z1.
- **Z5 — Sleeping Quarters.** Six cots, personal effects from the dead Zhentarim. One cot has a loose floorboard hiding 60 gp and a folded letter signed "U.F." (Urstul Floxin) ordering the cell to acquire Renaer Neverember "by any means" and retrieve what he carries.
- **Z6 — Storage (upper level).** Surplus gear: crowbars, rope, lamp oil. A crate holds four doses of a sleep draught (Wisdom save DC 12 or Unconscious for 1 hour) — brought for the kidnapping.
- **Z7 — Kenku Roost.** Two kenku sleep here in off-hours. Currently empty; both are in Z1.
- **Z8 — Loading Bay.** The large warehouse door opens here. A cart and two draft horses (still alive, frightened) stand against the north wall.
- **Z9 — Loft.** Accessible by ladder. Stores crated crossbows and bolts. One kenku is up here as a lookout, watching through a shuttered window.
- **Z10 — Privy.** Nothing of note.
- **Z11 — Cellar Stairs.** Stone stairs descending from Z1 to Z12.
- **Z12 — Cellar.** Damp, low-ceilinged. A rack of wine bottles (mundane). Against the east wall: a heavy iron trapdoor in the floor, currently ajar. Iron rungs descend into the sewer system below. The mud on the rungs is wet. Yellow chalk guildsign marks the sewer grate at the bottom — DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) to notice it from the cellar floor.

*Finding Renaer.* Renaer (see Appendix A) speaks freely once the kenku are dealt with, asking only for confirmation that the building is clear before emerging from Z2. He explains: five Zhentarim grabbed him and Floon outside Old Xoblob Shop two nights ago. They brought him here and questioned him about his father's embezzled hoard — money Renaer refuses to help his father hide. They took his mourning locket, which he believed held a keepsake from his mother; he did not know there was a compartment inside. The Xanathar Guild attacked before the interrogation went further. He slipped his bonds in the confusion and hid. He watched through a gap in the floor as Xanathar agents dragged an unconscious Floon — the man they mistook for Renaer — through the cellar trapdoor.

What Renaer freely shares: the Zhentarim wanted information about his father's gold; they took his locket; he watched Xanathar's agents take Floon through Z12's trapdoor.
What Renaer does not know: what the locket contained; where the Vault is or how to access it; the Stone of Golorr's role in any of this. He believed the Eye was a piece of his mother's jewelry.
What Renaer mentions if asked about his father's enemies: the Cassalanters have been quietly acquiring properties near the Brandath family tombs — his mother's family plots. He does not know why. He raises it as an oddity, not an accusation.

*Three Independent Clue Paths to Revelation #3 (Floon is in the Xanathar Guild sewer hideout):*

- **Renaer's account.** He watched Xanathar agents drag Floon through the Z12 trapdoor. He describes them — circle-eye tattoos matching the dead in Z1 — and confirms they went down. No check required.
- **Yellow chalk guildsign.** A DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check near Z12's trapdoor, or no check once the party physically descends the ladder and examines the sewer grate at the bottom, reveals yellow chalk eye-symbols — Xanathar's guildsign, marking a Guild-controlled sewer route.
- **Kenku interrogation.** Any captured kenku that can be questioned — through Intimidation (DC 13) or a language-capable party member — confirms that the Xanathar agents took a man through the cellar to the Guild's sewer hideout.

**Tone:** Grimly revelatory. The party arrived looking for Floon and found the wrong person — who turns out to matter more to the campaign's back story. The warehouse should read as a crime scene being picked through: cold, quiet except for kenku, and full of evidence that something worse happened here than a simple kidnapping.

### Scene 4: The Xanathar Guild Sewer Hideout

**Purpose:** Deliver the arc's climax location, rescue Floon, plant Nihiloor as the campaign's deepest foreshadowing thread, and establish the physical connection between Waterdeep's streets and Undermountain. The sewer hideout is where surface-city politics meet what lies beneath — literally.

**Content:**

*Navigation.* From the warehouse cellar, the party follows yellow chalk guildsign through the Dock Ward sewer system. DC 11 Wisdom (Survival) to find the correct sewer branch from Z12. Three DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) checks to navigate to the hideout using the guildsign as a guide; a party that found the guildsign in Z12 has advantage on all three checks. A party that fails all three checks arrives at Q7 — where Floon is dead. A party that fails two of three finds the hideout but triggers a wandering patrol that gets one additional round of attacks.

*Location Overview.* A converted brick vault: nine rooms of a decommissioned water-filtration chamber, built during an earlier expansion of Waterdeep's sewer system. Now a Xanathar Guild cell. Sits approximately thirty feet above Undermountain Level 1. A collapsed passage in Q8 drops into Level 1 if cleared. A sealed iron door in Q9 is warm to the touch. No light comes from under it.

*Keyed Areas (all nine areas receive full keyed entries in the final prose draft; this draft establishes their content and dramatic function):*

- **Q1 — Entry Sluiceway.** Iron grate (DC 12 Dexterity to open without noise; DC 10 Strength to force). Two kenku sentries on the far side; they roll Perception with advantage on any party making more than minimal noise.
- **Q2 — Guard Room.** Four Xanathar thugs playing cards. If sentries raised an alarm: all four stand ready. If the party arrives undetected: the thugs have disadvantage on their first initiative rolls.
- **Q3 — Cistern Crossing.** A twenty-foot-wide cistern crossed by a three-foot-wide stone bridge. Falling in: 1d6 cold damage, DC 12 Athletics to swim (mild current). A rope is knotted to a ring on the near bank.
- **Q4 — Storage Chamber.** Alchemist's fire, standard supplies, a locked chest (DC 15 thieves' tools, DC 16 Strength). Chest contents: 150 gp, two potions of healing, and folded operational notes for this cell — referencing a "project in Q9" and a "current subject," a halfling named Delwa Narrant who has been held eleven days.
- **Q5 — Kenku Quarters.** Three off-duty kenku. If Krentz survived Scene 1, he is here recovering; his presence gives the kenku advantage on their first round (he warns them in time).
- **Q6 — Leader's Room.** Rolph, the cell's human lieutenant, and one personal guard. Rolph carries a ring of keys — all interior doors, including Q9's iron door. He also carries a written report on Q9 awaiting delivery to Xanathar's lair; it describes experimental relay organisms without naming Nihiloor.
- **Q7 — Floon's Cell.** Floon Blagmaar (see Appendix A) is here: alive, very bruised, relieved. He has his Lurue necklace (missing several beads from the pursuit). He does not know why he was taken; he was mistaken for Renaer. His first question is about Renaer. He has no information that advances the Grand Game.
- **Q8 — Collapsed Passage.** The eastern wall has partially collapsed into darkness. DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) detects a draft from below and the sound of water — not sewer water, something larger. DC 12 Intelligence (Nature) identifies the ventilation pattern as indicating a large excavated space, not a sewer. The passage can be cleared with one hour of work; it opens into Undermountain Level 1. A secondary collapse seals it again within 24 hours (structural instability).
- **Q9 — Nihiloor's Chamber.** Sealed with an arcane lock (DC 25 thieves' tools, DC 22 Strength, or Rolph's key). The room is lit by faint bioluminescence from three specimen tanks along the north wall — living creatures in various states of experimental modification. A stone table holds vivisection tools. Specimen notes are written in Deep Speech (DC 16 Intelligence [Arcana] to recognize the language; cannot be read at this level without a cipher or translation). Nothing moves when the party first looks in.

If the party enters Q9: all characters make a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, the character experiences a telepathic pulse — a vast, cold patience pressed briefly against their mind — and is Frightened for 1 round (frightened of an unseen presence; no source is visible). The pulse originates from a grate in the floor at the back of the room. If the grate is examined: it opens onto a shaft descending into Undermountain. A silhouette — large, rounded, trailing three or four thin tendrils — is visible in the darkness below for one moment, then withdraws. No further engagement occurs in Arc A. Nihiloor does not fight the party. A character who descends the shaft finds only empty Undermountain tunnels.

*Nihiloor.* Nihiloor (see Appendix E) is running three parallel projects in the sewer hideout under Xanathar Guild cover. The party is not meant to fight it in Arc A. The Q9 encounter is designed to register as deeply wrong and then end — one moment of alien contact, then silence. The specimen tanks are disturbing. There is no combat unless the party pursues down the shaft, in which case they find nothing. Nihiloor has withdrawn into Undermountain Level 1 through passages it knows and the party does not.

*Mad Mage Seeds.* The collapsed passage in Q8 connects to Undermountain Level 1. A character who descends briefly and makes a DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check hears something at the edge of perception — music, or a thing that sounds like music without being one. The sealed grate at the bottom of Q9's shaft is carved with glyphs; DC 18 Intelligence (History) identifies them as Melairkyn dwarven script — the clan that dug these tunnels long before Waterdeep was built. Neither thread resolves in Arc A.

*Rescuing Floon.* Floon is alive and unharmed beyond bruising. He does not know why he was taken. He is grateful, disoriented, and stays close to whoever freed him for the rest of the session. The party's reunion of Floon and Renaer — whether at the warehouse on the way out, at the Yawning Portal, or at Trollskull Manor — is the emotional close of the arc.

**Tone:** Claustrophobic, escalating, and quietly terrifying. The sewer hideout is a descent — not just into the earth, but into the campaign's deeper layers. The kenku and thugs are a conventional dungeon fight; Q9 is not. Players should leave with the sense that Waterdeep has something much older and stranger beneath it than they expected.

### Milestone and Transition

**Purpose:** Mark the arc's two narrative achievements with XP, transition the party to their new home base, and close the investigation loop with Volo's reward.

**Content:**

*Milestone XP.*

| Level | Arc | Milestone | XP |
|:-----:|-----|-----------|---:|
| 1 → 2 | A | The party clears the Zhentarim warehouse and finds Renaer Neverember alive | 100 |
| 1 → 2 | A | The party rescues Floon Blagmaar from the Xanathar Guild sewer hideout | 200 |

Both milestones are awarded regardless of order — some parties may follow the bead trail past the warehouse and encounter Renaer only on the way out. Level 2 is reached when both milestones are awarded.

*Volo's Payment.* Volo does not have the 100 gp per character. He produces the deed to a derelict tavern in Trollskull Alley, North Ward, describing it as "worth considerably more than the gold owed" and "an investment in your futures." DC 12 Wisdom (Insight) confirms he is genuinely embarrassed. He offers to cover the gold difference "once the royalties arrive from Mordenkainen." He departs before anyone can press him. The deed is real. The tavern — Trollskull Manor — is real, non-operational, and now theirs.

*Aftermath.* Renaer (see Appendix A) stays in contact after the arc. He offers his North Ward townhouse as an occasional resource in Arc B and takes a quiet interest in the party's next moves; he knows his father's enemies are not done. Floon thanks everyone, invites the party to drinks he cannot afford, and reappears in Arc B as a Trollskull Alley regular with a gambling problem the party may eventually need to address.

*Transition to Arc B.* The party holds a deed to a derelict tavern. They are residents of Trollskull Alley. The city faction recruitment, Manor renovation, and the first rumors of something called "Neverember's Enigma" begin in Arc B.

**Tone:** Warm, slightly chaotic, and quietly triumphant. The party solved a crime in a city full of crimes. They did not stop anything — the Eye Manshoon's cell extracted from Renaer's locket is already in Kolat Towers. But they saved two people and earned a home. That should feel like something.

### Design Notes: Finding Floon

***Investigation Restructuring.*** The original Chapter 1 presents the Dock Ward investigation as a sequential chain: Skewered Dragon → warehouse name → Renaer → sewer. A single failed Charisma check at the Skewered Dragon can stall the party indefinitely, because the original text makes that tavern the sole source for the warehouse's location. The Three Clue Rule redesign distributes the warehouse address across three independent paths — neighborhood canvass, prisoner questioning, and the dropped bead trail — so that no single failure blocks progress. The same three-path structure is applied to both revelations in Scene 2, ensuring redundancy at every step. Players who follow all three paths simultaneously discover more about the world; players who follow one path successfully still reach the warehouse.

***The Two Zhentarims in Arc A.*** The original adventure presents the Zhentarim as a single faction. This remix uses Arc A to establish the faction fracture that defines the Manshoon subplot. Yagra Stonefist — a Doom Raiders operative, Davil Starsong's people — is at the Yawning Portal bar, openly wearing the Zhentarim pendant, fighting Xanathar Guild thugs. The Candle Lane warehouse belongs to Manshoon's cell, Urstul Floxin's people, operating separately from Davil. Players who later join the Doom Raiders in Arc B and then raid Kolat Towers in Arc I will have seen both Zhentarim factions from Scene 1 — one as a potential ally, one as an adversary, both operating under the same symbol. The distinction should be felt before it is explained.

***Bregan D'Aerthe Observer.*** The drow woman at the Yawning Portal balcony table is the campaign's earliest Bregan D'Aerthe presence. She is never named in Scene 1 and the party is not meant to notice her unless they ask who is watching the well. She reappears in Arc B as a background presence — same table, different evenings — and is eventually identified as part of Jarlaxle's Waterdeep intelligence network monitoring the Undermountain entrance. Her placement in Scene 1 is a retroactive payoff: by the time the party is dealing with the Sea Maidens Faire in Arc H, they can look back at the Yawning Portal and understand that Jarlaxle has had eyes on them since their first night in the city.

***Cassalanter Name-Drop Through Renaer.*** Renaer's aside about the Cassalanters' property acquisitions near the Brandath tombs is designed to sit unexamined in player notes until Arc G. He does not frame it as a threat or a suspicion — he raises it as an oddity he cannot explain. Players who track it will revisit the detail when the Cassalanter Villa becomes a heist target. Players who do not track it will have it recapitulated by Renaer in Arc G when the connection becomes relevant. The name-drop is passive: it seeds without pressing. A first-level party has no use for it yet, and none is offered.

***Nihiloor as Foreshadowing.*** Q9 in the sewer hideout is the campaign's first contact with Nihiloor, who is eventually revealed in Arc F to be the true intelligence driving the Xanathar Guild — running three parallel projects, exercising psionic control over Xanathar, and infiltrating Waterdeep's civil institutions (see Appendix E). In Arc A, Nihiloor does not engage with the party. A telepathic pulse and a silhouette in the dark are the full extent of the encounter. This restraint is intentional. Players who investigate Q9 should feel that they glimpsed something vast and wrong that they could not reach. Nihiloor's full scope surfaces across Arcs B through F. By the time the party reaches Undermountain Level 17 in the Dungeon of the Mad Mage, the creature's presence in Arc A should read as a thread that began in the sewers under Trollskull Alley.

***Renaer's Introduction.*** In the original, finding Renaer in the warehouse is a pleasant surprise in an otherwise thin scene — a friendly NPC who points the party downward. This redesign makes Renaer's introduction structural: the warehouse is the arc's pivot, not its anticlimax. The party arrives expecting Floon and finds someone who matters more to the campaign's back story. Renaer's knowledge state is asymmetric by design — he knows what Manshoon's cell wanted and why the locket mattered to his father, without knowing what the locket contained. His ignorance is genuine. This positions him as a trustworthy ally (he does not conceal things he knows) while preserving the campaign's central mystery (the nature of the Eyes, the Vault's location). The gap between what Renaer knows and what the party will eventually learn gives the warehouse scene a retrospective weight it lacks in the original.

***Stealth-or-Force Preview.*** The warehouse presents the arc's first real structural choice: enter quietly and catch the kenku off-guard, or force entry and fight a prepared defense. Neither approach is obviously correct. This binary — infiltration versus direct assault — is the same decision the party will face at every heist target in Arcs F through J. Arc A is the tutorial, with first-level stakes and kenku rather than guild lieutenants. Each subsequent heist arc's design notes should acknowledge this parallel explicitly, so the DM can frame escalating heist complexity as the campaign's through-line skill development rather than a separate genre convention dropped into the story.

***Durnan's Secret.*** Durnan's Masked Lord status and Red Sashes leadership (see Appendix A, DM-layer context) are not revealed in Arc A and are not hinted at in any player-facing text. His Scene 1 portrayal — minimal words, the troll handled in seconds, "You fought well" as the full debrief — is designed to read as "formidable retired adventurer" to players and as "Masked Lord making deliberate choices about how much power to display" to the DM. The gap between those two readings is Durnan's dramatic potential. If the party gains enough standing to learn his secrets in Arc B or beyond, Scene 1 retroactively becomes a performance — a man who could have stopped that brawl before the first punch deciding it was more useful to let it play out.
