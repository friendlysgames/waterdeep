# Arc J — Vault of Dragons

*An adventure for 7th-level characters.*

---

## Arc Opener

The Melairkyn dwarves raised the first halls beneath what is now Waterdeep, and among the Underhalls they carved a ceremonial vault for Dumathoin, the Keeper of the Mountain's Secrets. Their cult believed Dumathoin encoded his secrets in the ore and gemstones he set into the mountains for the dwarven people, and that every act of mining released a fragment of that hoarded knowledge into the world. To make amends, the Melairkyn bound their own hard-won secrets into finely wrought offerings and sealed them inside vaults exactly like this one — not a treasury, but a shrine to the discipline of keeping something safe. When dark elves broke the Melairkyn civilization, the vault was looted, abandoned, and buried by time. A millennium later, the ground above it became the City of the Dead.

The Brandath family built a mausoleum on the site without knowing what lay beneath it. A hundred years into the family's tenure, an expansion of the crypts broke through into the vault's antechambers. Struck by the beauty of what they'd found, the Brandaths turned it into the meeting hall of a secret fraternal order — the Brothers of the Maroon Pin — who performed rituals loosely patterned on the Melairkyn originals and left their own symbols carved into the stone: dwarven compasses hidden in portraiture, a serpent's forked tongue for the telling of secrets, alexandrite pins worn by initiates. A scandal in the fourteenth century ended the Brotherhood. The vault went quiet again, sealed beneath a mausoleum that had long since stopped being anyone's secret and started being simply a place where the Brandaths buried their dead.

Lord Dagult Neverember found it by accident, arranging his wife's interment. Lady Alethea Brandath had asked for a simple tomb among her ancestors. Neverember gave her something else — he seized on the oldest of the family mausoleums, the resting place of one Lord Anri, evicted the old lord's remains to a newly built crypt nearby, and buried Alethea in the place of primacy instead. In doing so he found the passage the Brandaths had forgotten, the antechamber the Maroon Brotherhood had used for their mummery, and beyond it, the sealed doors of a vault that predated Waterdeep itself. He recognized an opportunity his city would never think to look for. Once the embezzled gold was safely inside, he concealed the vault's location using the Stone of Golorr — an aboleth-forged artifact he had acquired years before — and then blinded the Stone entirely, popping its three Eyes free and scattering them so that even a thief who stole the Stone itself would learn nothing. One Eye he hid in a crypt near his wife's tomb, reasoning that no one alive knew to look there. He was nearly right. The Cassalanters found it during an exhaustive, demon-guided survey of every site connected to his name — and never realized, in all their searching, that they had been standing almost directly above the vault the entire time.

The gold needed a guardian, and Neverember had one in mind. Aurinax, an adult gold dragon, had lived openly in Waterdeep for decades — a privilege granted by the dragonstaff of Ahghairon, which let him ignore the dragonward that kept every other creature of his kind out of the city. The staff had come to him from Maaril, the last archmage to wield it, who went mad shortly before the Spellplague and fled the city, leaving the staff in Aurinax's care rather than see it fall to someone who might misuse it. Neverember offered a trade: Aurinax would keep the staff, and in exchange he would guard a vault beneath the city until Neverember himself, or someone Neverember named as an authorized vassal, came to remove what was stored there. Aurinax agreed. It seemed, at the time, like very little to ask of a being who measured his patience in centuries.

No one has come. Neverember's embezzlement was exposed, his lordship stripped, his exile to Neverwinter made permanent and public. The vassals he might have named were never named. Aurinax has spent years alone in the dark beneath a mausoleum, watching from a distance as the man who bound him fell from every height he once held, waiting for an authorization that time has made increasingly unlikely to ever arrive — and increasingly uncertain, in the small ways a dragon allows himself to be uncertain, whether the oath he swore to a Lord Regent of Waterdeep still means anything now that the office and the man have both been stripped of what made the promise worth keeping.

As the Stone's third Eye clicks into its socket — in whichever lair delivered it — every surviving player in the Grand Game receives the same piece of information at the same time: the vault is real, it is beneath the Brandath Crypts, and it can now be opened. Xanathar, if he survives, prefers the gold stay buried and the Stone stay lost — a vault he cannot reach is still leverage he can threaten to use. The Cassalanters, depending on how the party left them, either wait on a promise or plan to take by force what they were once willing to earn. Manshoon, who has no Eye and never sought one, has been counting on exactly this moment — he intends to let someone else do the work of opening the vault and then take it from whoever succeeds. And in Neverwinter, word of a Waterdeep in open faction war over a half-million gold pieces has already reached a man who has been waiting years for a reason to come home. Dagult Neverember is packing.

## Scene 1: The Final Approach

**Purpose.** Arc J opens with resolution, not investigation. By this point the party holds all three Eyes, the Stone has spoken in full — in whichever prior arc delivered the third Eye — and every faction contact has one final piece of business before the vault opens. This is not a briefing in the Arc F–I sense; there is no more casing to do, no more surveillance to gather. The seven contacts are not filling gaps in the party's knowledge. They are calling in what they're owed, discharging what they promised, and telling the party what happens next. Scene 1's job is to convert nine arcs of relationship-building into concrete, arc-specific payoffs, and to give the DM one table that summarizes every branch the rest of Arc J depends on.

**Content.**

*Faction-State Summary Table.* Before running this scene, confirm the following. Every value below was set in a prior arc and is read directly by Scenes 5 and 6.

| Variable | Set In | Values |
|---|---|---|
| Eyes recovered | Arcs F, G, H | Should be 3/3 entering Arc J. If fewer than 3, Arc J cannot yet be run — see Design Notes. |
| Manshoon operational? | Arc I Scene 6 (if run) | Destroyed / Simulacrum Only / Alive. If Arc I was never run, treat as **Alive**, undiminished, and unidentified — the party has not yet assembled the Identity Reveal. |
| Xanathar operational? | Arc F Scene 6 | YES / WOUNDED / GONE |
| Cassalanter active? | Arc G Scene 6 | Redeemed / Cooperative (Gold Delivered) / Active-Adversarial / Exposed / Founders' Day Completed (too late) |
| Founders' Day resolved? | Arc G | Yes (99 spared) / No (99 sacrificed) / N/A (Redeemed path) |
| BD operational? (Jarlaxle relationship) | Arc H Scene 6 | Allied / Impressed / Robbed / Hostile / Patron |
| Esvele relationship | Arc G | Ally / Rival / Unknown |

*Running This Scene.* Run this as a montage across several days, organized by the kind of thing each contact delivers rather than by faction order. Instruments come first — the Masked Lord's Writ, Jeryth's readiness, Force Grey's Commander standing — because these are tools the party will use directly. Warnings come second — the Illuun compromise, the assassination fallout — because these recontextualize intelligence the party may have already acted on. Optional companions come last — Vajra, Skeemo — because whether they join the party at the vault is often the final piece to settle before Scene 2 begins.

*Mirt (Harpers — renown 1+).* If the party completed Harper Mission 6 ("The Stone's Other Master"), Mirt already had his three days with the Stone before the third Eye was even seated. He returns now for a final word, not a request. "I told you what we found. Watch who you trust with what you know — that channel may still be listening, even now." If the compromised contact was Jalester Silvermane, Mirt notes it plainly: any Lords' Alliance intelligence exchanged since should be treated as potentially known to something patient and very old. If it was Renaer, the same caution applies to anything Renaer has said about his father's plans. At renown 10+, Mirt has one Harper field agent (spy stat block) shadow the party's approach to the City of the Dead, watching for anyone else converging on the same location.

*Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance — renown 1+).* If Lords' Alliance Mission 6 succeeded, Jalester delivers the Masked Lord's Writ's practical terms one final time: the Vault district gate opens to it, Watch officers defer to it within four rounds of presentation, and Laeral has one legal intervention available during Arc J — usable once, for a rival faction complication, a legal challenge, or an NPC arrest. If the party argued Laeral out of the Manshoon leverage offer, Jalester confirms it: "He has no card to play with her. Whatever he tries at the vault, it won't be that." If the offer was accepted instead, Jalester warns that Manshoon can still delay Watch cooperation for up to two hours, or threaten to expose a Masked Lord publicly, if he reaches the vault able to make good on it. At renown 10+, Jalester personally escorts the party to the Vault district gate and stands with them through the Watch's initial questioning, cutting what would otherwise be a lengthy procedural delay down to minutes.

*Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave — renown 1+).* If the party completed Emerald Enclave Mission 6 ("The Dreamer's Reach"), Jeryth Phaulkon no longer needs 24 hours' notice — she has been ready since the anchor beneath the Castle Ward cisterns was destroyed. Melannor relays her closing request from that mission, unchanged: "When the Stone opens the vault, make sure no one is watching from the water." If a PC underwent direct psychic contact with Illuun during that mission, Melannor notes they are personally registered to it — a fact that will matter more in Undermountain than here, but he mentions it anyway, because Jeryth asked him to. At renown 10+, Jeryth's ward — the small woven ring of Phaulkonmere root the party may already carry — remains active; any character holding it retains advantage on saves against being charmed or frightened by aberrations for the duration of Arc J.

*Savra Belabranta (Order of the Gauntlet — renown 1+).* If Order of the Gauntlet Mission 6 ("The Writ and the Weight") succeeded, the Halls of Justice have formally recognized the party as Order associates. Savra states plainly what that buys them: Watch officers who see the party in conflict with a Cassalanter operative will not automatically intervene, and she will personally testify on the party's behalf if any complication from the vault operation goes legal. She adds a line that has nothing to do with paperwork: "Whatever you're doing with the vault — do it fast." The Founders' Day timeline, however it resolved, has left her with the sense that Waterdeep does not have unlimited patience left for the Grand Game's loose ends. At renown 10+, Savra positions herself at the City of the Dead's main gate during the operation, prepared to intervene if Watch response arrives before the party is ready for it.

*Vajra Safahr (Force Grey — renown 1+).* If the party delivered a full Grand Game briefing at any point — and especially if Force Grey Mission 6 ("Smoke in the Tower") succeeded and named the party Force Grey Commanders — Vajra is present at the vault opening itself, not merely briefed on it. She provides active magical containment during Scene 4 and stands ready to deal with anything the vault holds that should not be held. If the party never delivered a complete picture, she arrives late, during Scene 5 at the earliest, and finds a situation she was not prepared for. At renown 10+, Vajra casts *nondetection* on one party member for 8 hours before the operation and confirms she will answer a *sending* from inside the vault if things go wrong.

*Tashlyn Yafeera or Davil Starsong (Doom Raiders — renown 1+).* If Doom Raiders Mission 6 ("Ziraj's Last Hunt") succeeded, Davil's final note to the party — the one listing every identified Splinter operative "for Arc I" — has already done its work if Arc I was run. Here, the contact's business is the gold itself. Davil states the Doom Raiders' position without apology: "We're not asking you to give us anything. We're telling you what we intend to ask for, once the vault is open." At Dread Lord renown, Davil proposes a 10% split of the recovered gold in exchange for concrete operational support: a distraction crew, a locksmith backup, and a wagon extraction team for the gold itself. If Skeemo Weirdbottle survived and Manshoon is Simulacrum Only, Skeemo is not with the Doom Raiders today — Tashlyn confirms, grimly, that he is at the vault working for the simulacrum, and will recognize the party on sight. At renown 10+, Tashlyn commits a four-person Doom Raiders team to the City of the Dead operation, coordinated on the same terms as Arc I if that arc was run, or offered fresh if it wasn't.

*Jarlaxle (Bregan D'aerthe — renown 1+).* Available only if a PC is a BD member. Jarlaxle's business here is the clearest of any contact's: he wants the dragonstaff of Ahghairon, and he has a specific plan for the gold. At Dread Lord renown, he proposes returning the entire recovered hoard publicly, in the party's name, to Laeral Silverhand — in exchange for a letter of introduction to the Lords' Alliance council on Luskan's behalf. "I get what I actually came here for. You get credit for the single largest act of civic generosity in a century. Everyone is happy except the people who wanted to keep it." If the party's relationship with Jarlaxle is Allied or Patron, he treats this as a genuine partnership and will fight alongside the party at the vault if a hostile faction shows up. If Hostile, he does not appear in Scene 1 at all — his interest in the staff has not gone away, only his willingness to negotiate for it.

*The Stone's Role Going Forward.* The Full Awakening already happened — see below. What the Stone does for the remainder of Arc J is different in character from what it did in Arcs F, G, and H. It is no longer withholding information; it has nothing left to reveal about the vault itself. What remains is presence. Illuun's consciousness, fully surfaced since the third Eye was seated, is close enough now that the attuned holder occasionally catches something that is not quite communication — an impression, a mood, a flicker of attention that does not belong to the Stone's usual cadence. This should be used sparingly across Scenes 2 through 4: a handful of brief, unsettling asides rather than a running commentary, timed to moments of genuine significance (the vault doors opening, the first sight of the gold) rather than every scene transition. The Stone is not the protagonist of this arc's climax. It is a passenger who has started, very quietly, to make its presence known.

*The Full Awakening (Canonical Scene).* This is the scene that Arc F and Arc G defer to explicitly — both read, verbatim, "Run the 3-Eye disclosure scene from Arc J at this point." It already happened, off-page relative to Arc J's own opening, in whichever arc delivered the third Eye. Use the following text at that point in whichever arc actually runs it; it should not be restaged here.

With the third Eye seated, the Stone goes still, then speaks with sudden, precise clarity. The fragmentary impressions of the prior awakenings are gone, replaced by a tangled barrage of imagery arriving all at once: twisted underground tunnels lit by strange lights, an endless field of corpses, halls of dwarven stone, a golden dragon aging so rapidly that scales shed from its skin, the sound of a silver hammer striking stone, a beam of sunlight falling into a darkened room, and a chisel carving Dethek characters that resolve, unmistakably, into two words: BRANDATH CRYPTS. Alongside the imagery, a cryptic riddle surfaces, spoken in the Stone's own alien cadence: *Where laid his wife to rest 'midst bones of son's blood sealed, there where Anri laid himself to rest lies that which Open Lord concealed.* The vault's location, the ceremony required to open it, and the nature of its guardian all resolve in full — dwarven-built, sealed by a ritual of scale and sunlight and mithral, guarded by a patient and dangerous power that has waited a very long time for someone to arrive with proper authority. This moment must land with its full weight regardless of which lair delivers the third Eye — the text is written to be identical in every case. Alongside the vault's secrets, the aboleth consciousness beneath the Stone comes fully forward for the first time. It is Illuun's instrument, and it has been watching the attuned holder since Arc E. The party is not carrying a treasure map. They have been carrying a mind.

**Tone.** Convergence. Every relationship the party built, every renown they earned, every faction debrief they sat through — all of it resolves into a handful of final conversations before the last door in the campaign opens. This should feel like the moment before a held breath is released, not like another briefing. The contacts know it too. Nobody in Scene 1 is asking the party to gather more information. They are wishing them well, or asking for their cut, or both.

## Scene 2: The Brandath Crypts

**Purpose.** The approach dungeon — and the last true dungeon crawl of the Dragon Heist campaign. Unlike the casing scenes of Arcs F through I, there is no surveillance to conduct and no entry method to select; the party already knows where the vault is and walks there directly. What Scene 2 offers instead is the campaign's final quiet space: a slow, funereal descent through the resting place of the family whose name has shadowed the entire Grand Game, before the noise of Scenes 4 and 5 begins.

**Content.**

*Three Clue Rule — Paths to the Crypts.* Any of the following, independently, gets the party to the City of the Dead knowing exactly which mausoleum complex to enter:

- The Converted Windmill map (Arc E, Manshoon outpost) — the primary clue, a physical document showing the Brandath crypt-complex layout.
- Esvele Rosznar's Viper Den (Arc G) — her "old tunnels" beneath the villa run toward the City of the Dead; if the party's relationship with her is Ally or Rival-but-informed, she confirms the destination directly.
- Kalain's letter and Lord Dagult's papers (Arc C, Scene 6, if pursued) — an unsigned letter confirming all three Eyes were once in Neverember's hands, and papers describing the vault-opening method in Neverember's own hand.
- The Full Awakening itself (Scene 1) — the Stone's vision names the crypts outright. A party that reaches Arc J without any of the above still arrives with this.

*Running This Scene.* The City of the Dead closes at night; two City Guards are stationed at each entry gate, and slipping over the wall requires a DC 15 Dexterity (Stealth) check. Entering during the day avoids this but means moving through a public park at Waterdeep's business hours, in view of anyone who might be watching the party's movements. Neither approach is wrong. At dusk, hundreds of driftglobes drift in from the waking city to spend the night among the mausoleums before dispersing at dawn — nobody has ever explained why, and this is not the arc to start.

*Sir Ambrose Everdawn.* An aging knight of Kelemvor who patrols the cemetery from sunset to sunrise, chasing off grave robbers and making sure the dead stay buried. His encounter with the party is predecided, not rolled: if the party enters after dark, they meet him — he intercepts anyone moving through the mausoleums at night as a matter of course, escorts trespassers out, and alerts the City Guard only if they refuse to leave. If the party has a plausible cover story (a Brandath-blood companion, a City Watch escort from Jalester's Mission 6, a writ from Laeral), he waves them through with a warning to be careful and be quick. If the party enters during the day, he does not appear at all — his patrol is nocturnal.

*The Brandath Mausoleum.* The largest tree by the main entrance to the crypt-complex is a treant. It wakes as the party approaches and warns them off: "Only those of Brandath blood are welcome here! Begone!" If no one present carries that blood, it animates two nearby trees and attacks — too large to follow into any mausoleum but the Grand Crypt's main chamber. If Renaer Neverember is with the party, the treant lets them all pass unmolested, and says to him in passing, "Your mother was a lovely person." This is the treant's only concession; it does not extend courtesy to Renaer's companions on his word alone unless he is physically present.

*Keyed Areas.* The crypt-complex is a loose cluster of mausoleums, not a linear corridor — the DM can present them in any order the party chooses to explore, or skip the non-critical ones entirely.

**Mausoleum A — Celestial Statues.** Grave markers here were carved to depict the deceased reborn as celestials — wings, glowing eyes, couatl and pegasi in attendance. One statue was enchanted to sing when mourners draw near; centuries of decay have left the enchantment fading and cutting out, producing an unsettling, atonal half-melody. Atmosphere only; nothing here opposes the party.

**Mausoleum B — The Ossuaries.** Four buildings, one of which has had its floor-to-ceiling bone shelving collapse entirely, filling the chamber with tumbled bone. Six crawling claws — animated from the hands of murderers once sentenced to death within these walls — scurry through the wreckage and leap out from unexpected angles. A straightforward combat beat.

**Mausoleum E — Uld's Crypt.** The newest construction in the complex, already in poor repair; part of the roof has collapsed onto the tomb sites within. Uld Brandath's sarcophagus — a Waterdhavian magister crushed by a falling gargoyle years ago — has been staved in by the collapse, and his skeletal corpse wears a *headband of intellect*. Six more crawling claws, made from the hands of criminals Uld himself sentenced, infest the wreckage.

**Lady Alethea's Tomb.** The critical path. This building was the original Brandath mausoleum — Lord Anri's resting place for centuries, until Neverember evicted his remains to a hastily built replacement mausoleum nearby and buried Alethea here instead, giving her the place of primacy his ego demanded even though she had asked only for something simple. Her sarcophagus, sculpted in her likeness, bears a bouquet of flowers that has not yet fully wilted — left by Renaer, though he has never told anyone. A faded, chipped portrait of Lord Anri adorns the ceiling, identified by a labeled banner; a DC 14 Intelligence (History) check reveals the portrait was actually painted centuries after Anri's death, and that he is depicted holding a dwarven compass and wearing an alexandrite-tipped pin — Maroon Brotherhood symbols, retroactively assigned to a man who was never a member. A DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check finds that the south wall is false, concealing a staircase down to the Old Crypts.

**Lord Anri's Actual Tomb.** A smaller, newer mausoleum elsewhere in the complex, obviously built more recently than its neighbors. Anri is genuinely buried here — evicted from his original resting place, not from history. Two dice sit tossed on the plain sarcophagus, reading snake eyes. Between this decoy and any Stone-granted vision naming "where Anri laid himself to rest," a party moving too quickly may waste real time here before realizing the riddle points to where Anri *used* to rest, not where he rests now.

*The Old Crypts.* Torch sconces line the walls, empty; a thick coat of dust has been recently disturbed by heavy foot traffic. A DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) check finds the top step scorched by a triggered *glyph of warding* — the Cassalanters' team, years ago, removing the second Eye. Further in, a DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation) check finds the remnants of a second glyph, deliberately disarmed, and an empty secret compartment behind a torn-loose sconce: this is where Neverember hid the Eye the Cassalanters eventually took. The passage's collapsed eastern end is an illusion concealing an intact archway and a two-foot steel vault door — installed by Neverember himself, damaging some of the ancient Maroon Brotherhood symbols carved into the arch around it. The door has a DC 22 lock and an *alarm* spell keyed specifically to Dagult Neverember; it will not warn anyone else. Beyond it, a ten-foot passage slopes down toward the ceremonial vault itself.

**Tone.** Quiet and elegiac. This is the last dungeon of Dragon Heist, and it should not feel like the others. No faction agents are converging here yet — this is the one scene in Arc J that belongs entirely to the party and the dead. Let the crawling claws and the treant provide texture, not tension. The real weight of Scene 2 is Lady Alethea's tomb: a father's ego, a wife's simple wish denied even in death, and a campaign's central mystery hidden in the gap between what someone asked for and what they got.

## Scene 3: The Ceremonial Opening

**Purpose.** The ritual payoff. Short by design — this is a thematic beat, not a puzzle box, and stretching it out would cost the arc more than it gains. Three redundant paths already exist for learning the ceremony; the party's actual work here is sourcing the physical components, most of which the campaign has already shown them where to find.

**Content.**

*The Antechamber.* Twenty feet wide, twenty feet high, resonating with the silent stillness of genuine age. A pair of massive adamantine doors dominates the far wall, dwarvish runes taller than a grown man reading "ALL THAT LIES WITHIN BELONGS TO THE SILENT KEEPER." In the center of the floor, a bronze bas-relief of a stylized sun, six feet across, ringed by a more recent inscription in Chondathan: "Know ye the hidden truth," repeated three times. The doors cannot be forced, damaged, or bypassed by any magic short of a *wish* spell.

*Opening the Doors.* A single dragon scale, laid upon the bronze sun and struck while lit by direct sunlight, opens the doors — they slide into the walls and remain open for 15 minutes before sliding shut again. From the inside, a hand placed on the doors is enough.

*Sourcing the Components.* None of these require anything the campaign has not already made available:

- **A dragon scale.** Type doesn't matter. A specialty shop can supply one for roughly 900 gp. The party may also recall specific sources encountered earlier in the campaign — Zelifarn's collection, Jarlaxle's private quarters, the Cassalanters' study — any of which could yield one for the asking, a favor, or a theft, depending on the party's standing with that source.
- **Sunlight.** The *daylight* spell suffices — its light counts as sunlight under 2024 rules, and at 3rd level it's well within reach of a 7th-level party. So does Manshoon's sunglobe, if he is Destroyed and the item was recovered from Kolat Towers. Absent either, twenty large steel mirrors (400 gp total) and several hours of careful alignment work will do.
- **A mithral hammer.** Does not need to be large. A local smith can commission one for 150 gp.

*Learning the Ceremony.* Three independent paths converge on the same ritual:

- The Stone's own vision, granted during the Full Awakening, includes the ceremony's shape directly: "Three keys ye seek. Strike scale of wyrm in light of day. As the vault bears witness, so shall it ope to mithral's strike."
- Kalain's notes on the Melairkyn vault (Arc C, Scene 6, if recovered) describe the ritual in full, in her own research hand, predating the party's own discovery of it.
- The Cassalanters, if the party's relationship with them permits a conversation, already know the ceremony from their own investigation and will share it as a gesture of good faith or in exchange for a concession, depending on their current state.

*The 15-Minute Window.* Once opened, the doors remain open for exactly 15 minutes before sliding shut. This matters most in Scene 5 — if a hostile faction times its approach to the ceremony rather than to the party's exit, the window becomes a genuine complication. A party that performs the ceremony without first confirming the area is secure may find themselves fighting to hold the door rather than simply walking through it.

**Tone.** Reverent, and brief. This is not a scene to drag out. The party has spent nine arcs earning the right to open this door; let the ceremony itself take a handful of minutes at the table before the vault beyond it takes over.

## Scene 4: The Vault of Dragons

**Purpose.** The dungeon proper, culminating in the campaign's emotional and mechanical climax: the gold, and the dragon guarding it. Everything in this scene has been building since Arc C's first mention of "a hidden hoard of gold dragons." The keyed areas below give the vault's texture; the Lower Vault and Aurinax are where the arc's real weight lands.

**Content.**

*General Features.* Fifteen feet down the sloping hallway, the stone construction changes to unmistakably dwarven work. Fifty feet beyond that, the walls carry two hundred feet of dwarven graffiti — recorded secrets, most of them banal ("my beard quivers for Lorlai of the stonecutters," "I use rotgrub paste to dye my beard") — before the passage ends at the antechamber from Scene 3. The vault itself is warded against scrying and teleportation. Hidden somewhere in most rooms is a small cartouche bearing the dwarven runes "HN" — Harlsnod Darkshine's mark, the vault's original architect, whose personal "secret offerings" to Dumathoin are scattered throughout.

*Keyed Areas.*

**Entrance Foyer.** Three age-worn columns support crumbling stone bridges sixty feet overhead. Two sections are structurally unstable and collapse under more than 150 pounds — a DC 15 Dexterity save avoids a sixty-foot fall. Twelve iron doors line the walls; ten are false. The real two, at the north end of the west wall, lead onward. Three niches on the far wall hold chipped frescoes — the first of the vault's Broken Circle of Gods imagery, described below.

**Stairs and Fresco.** A twenty-foot fresco depicting dwarves battling goblins carries a subtle enchantment: any creature within thirty feet who can see it must succeed on a DC 12 Wisdom save or become charmed for 24 hours, compelled to stay in sight of the fresco and defend it. Destroying a ten-foot section (AC 17, 25 HP, immune to poison and psychic damage) ends the effect for everyone charmed by it.

**Hall of Moradin.** Three hammer-shaped pillars run the length of this hall. A cracked mosaic depicting a dwarf smith crafting figures from black metal and diamond conceals a secret door — DC 17 Wisdom (Perception) to find, opening automatically for a dwarf or on a DC 17 Strength (Athletics) check for anyone else. A black pudding lurks in a break in the wall roughly halfway down the hall and attacks anyone who investigates the damage.

**The Secrets Room of Harlsnod Darkshine.** A dust-filled chamber untouched since the Delzoun era. Five copper urns hold the vault architect's personal offerings: gemstones, coin, a *ring of warmth*, and a small silver statuette of a dwarf priest of Moradin. Whether these truly carry sealed secrets, released into the mind of whoever removes them, is left to the DM's discretion — Harlsnod's actual tomb lies on Level 9 of Undermountain, a thread worth remembering for later.

**Hammer and Anvil.** Frescoes here depict the construction of the Temple of Dumathoin and the Heart of the Mountain — a location on Undermountain's sixth level. A stone hammer set into the wall (DC 10 Perception to notice it can be removed) leaves a hammer-shaped indentation bearing a dwarven inscription. Behind the hammer's resting place, a secret compartment (DC 12 Investigation if the hammer has already been removed, DC 17 otherwise) holds maps of Undermountain's Level 6, alongside detailed notes on locating the Heart of the Mountain — a second concrete Mad Mage bridge hook, ready for whenever the party descends.

**The Broken Circle of Gods.** Scattered through the vault's frescoes — the Entrance Foyer's niches most explicitly — are images that should not exist: Dumathoin placing gems into a primeval range of mountains; Dumathoin visiting the illithid god Ilsensine, manifest as a disembodied emerald brain, and bathing in its psionic light; Dumathoin, Ilsensine, and Laduguer, god of the duergar, with hands clasped in a circle of friendship that official dwarven doctrine insists never existed. These images predate a falling-out between the three gods that the Morndinsamman has never fully explained. They are not necessary to Arc J's plot — the party can walk past them without consequence — but a party that lingers and investigates is rewarded with one more thread connecting the vault's dwarven history to the deep, patient intelligences (illithid and aboleth alike) that the Undermountain campaign will eventually make explicit.

**Dumathoin's Secret.** Four suits of rusted dwarf-sized plate armor, cobwebbed and empty, stand in the corners of this chamber. Dwarvish runes on the far wall read: "A secret never before told will part Dumathoin's lips." A DC 14 Intelligence (Religion) check identifies Dumathoin as the dwarven god of secrets — automatic for any dwarf character. The room conceals a trapdoor too well hidden to find by searching or magic; it responds only to speech. When any creature in the room speaks a true secret aloud — something they have never before revealed, to anyone — the trapdoor springs open, revealing a spiral stone stair descending 120 feet to the lower vault. This is the vault's real path downward, and it asks something of the party that no lock or trap could: a genuine, unrehearsed confession, spoken where the others can hear it.

**Dumathoin's Truth.** An adamantine trapdoor with a pull ring, set into the floor before a ten-foot painted statue of an armored dwarf. This trapdoor is false and leads nowhere — a decoy for anyone who assumes the obvious pull-ring is the way down. Anyone who touches it or the ring must succeed on a DC 18 Dexterity save or take 22 (4d10) fire damage from rays springing out of the painted eyes. The trap does not trigger against a target with total cover.

*The Lower Vault and Aurinax.* The stair revealed in Dumathoin's Secret descends to an arched entrance. The lower vault is a single grand chamber — four pillars carved as dwarven hammers standing at its center, and heaped in the square space between them, a vast golden trove: not just coin but ingots, gems, and scattered leather satchels torn open long ago by careful claws. Give the party a real moment here before anything else happens. This pile of gold has been the object of the entire campaign since Arc C; let it land.

Only then does the dragon announce itself — an aged dwarf stepping into view from behind the hoard, leaning on a walking staff carved and painted to resemble two entwined dragons, one red, one gold. "I wasn't expecting anyone," he says, plainly, unhurried. "As you can see, the place is a mess. Perhaps you should come back later, after I've tidied up a bit." This is Aurinax, disguised as "Barok Clanghammer." He does not drop the disguise until combat begins or until he chooses to reveal himself as a gesture of respect to a party he has decided is dealing with him honestly.

Four resolution paths exist, and none of them is the DM's to pick in advance — the party's approach determines which one plays out.

**Oath-Release.** The path Aurinax has been waiting years for. If the party brings something he recognizes as genuine authority — Renaer Neverember, presented formally and not merely as a familiar face; the Masked Lord's Writ from Lords' Alliance Mission 6; or the release phrase, if the Stone's Full Awakening or subsequent research produced one — Aurinax considers his oath discharged without a fight. He is old enough to recognize the difference between a trick and a genuine instrument of authority, and Hlam's presence, if the party arranged it, grants advantage on the Persuasion check that seals the moment (per Hlam's established relationship with the dragon). This is the ending his own patience has been built for: "See the gold legitimately transferred," and he considers his duty done.

**Negotiation Without Release.** Short of full oath-release, Aurinax can be talked into a partial concession — releasing some of the gold, or accepting an alternate arrangement he finds satisfactory — through honest dealing rather than a clean legal instrument. A DC 18 Charisma (Persuasion) check, made with advantage if the party brought a prominent city official (Vajra, if she's present per Scene 1) or a professed worshiper of Bahamut, convinces him that the gold's rightful place is with the people of Waterdeep rather than a single absent lord. He does not concede easily, and he does not concede twice to the same trick — a failed attempt to charm or deceive him imposes disadvantage on every subsequent Charisma check the party makes against him, for the rest of the encounter.

**Legal Argument.** The most novel path, and the one the Grand Game itself has made possible: Dagult Neverember's exile is public record. If the party can demonstrate — with the Writ, with testimony, with simple established fact — that Neverember's authority to bind Aurinax's oath no longer exists in any legitimate sense, they give the dragon genuine philosophical grounds to reconsider what he owes. This is not a trick. It is the argument Aurinax has quietly been making to himself for years, and a party that makes it explicitly and honestly gives him permission to agree with something he already half-believes.

**Combat.** The path Aurinax does not want and will not seek. He attacks only in defense of himself, the staff, or the gold — never as a first resort. Any attempt to intimidate him automatically fails. His Legendary Resistance defeats attempts to charm him outright. If combat becomes unavoidable, run him as a boss encounter (see Appendix B) rather than a standard dragon stat block — he does not chase a retreating party, since Neverember's binding kept him anchored to this vault, not to a pursuit of intruders who flee it.

*The Stone's Reaction.* If the attuned holder is present for the confrontation, this is one of the moments Scene 1 flagged for the Stone's rare, brief interjections. A single unsettling aside — not dialogue, not explanation, just a flicker of something ancient registering something else ancient — is enough. Do not let it compete with Aurinax for the scene's attention.

*Treasure.* Choose two alcoves. One holds the full 500,000 gp, weighing 10,000 pounds combined. The other holds sixty-five gemstones worth 100 gp each — the last of Aurinax's food supply, and not something he surrenders willingly; they are his payment, same as the staff, for services he considers still owed until the moment his oath is formally discharged.

**Tone.** Awe first, then consequence. The party has been chasing this room since Arc C. Let them see it before anything else happens to it.

## Scene 5: The Faction Confrontation

**Purpose.** Every faction thread the campaign has built converges here. Who shows up, and how dangerous they are, is a direct function of the flags set in the Faction-State Summary Table from Scene 1 — this scene is where those flags become an encounter. Dagult Neverember's arrival is the one constant: staged as a second wave, spatially separate from the villain confrontation, so his very personal reckoning with Renaer does not get lost inside a multi-faction brawl.

**Content.**

*Running This Scene.* Build the roster from the Faction-State table before play begins. The confrontation happens in two waves. The first — any hostile villain factions — intercepts the party at or just inside the vault entrance, timed to the ceremony's 15-minute window if the party lingered, or to their exit if they moved quickly. The second — Dagult Neverember — arrives afterward, at the crypt entrance in the City of the Dead, once the immediate danger has passed and the party is carrying (or has decided not to carry) the gold.

*Roster-Assembly Table.*

| Faction | Present If | Force Composition | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manshoon's Splinter | Not Destroyed | See Manshoon's Three States below | Seize the gold and the Stone; recover the dragonstaff if possible |
| Xanathar's Guild | Operational (YES or WOUNDED) | Small, opportunistic — a handful of Guild agents under Ahmaergo if Xanathar is dead or incapacitated | Deny the gold to rivals; steal what can be carried quickly |
| Cassalanters | Active-Adversarial | Willifort Crowelle (doppelganger, tiefling guise) leading cult fanatics and cultists | Seize the vault gold by force for the Asmodeus buy-out |
| Cassalanters | Cooperative (Gold Delivered) | Willifort and a dozen carts of trusted servants, unarmed for combat | Collect the gold owed per the Arc G agreement |
| Bregan D'aerthe | Hostile | Jarlaxle with drow gunslinger lieutenants | Seize the dragonstaff; deny the gold to the city |
| Bregan D'aerthe | Allied / Patron | Jarlaxle with drow gunslinger lieutenants | Assist the party; claim the staff through the agreed Scene 1 arrangement |

**Manshoon's Three States.** Reused directly from Arc I Scene 6, and the single most consequential row in this table.

- *Destroyed.* Absent. No Splinter obstacle at the vault.
- *Simulacrum Only.* Present but diminished — no spell slots above 5th level, no staff of power, capable of executing plans Manshoon taught it but not of adapting to a situation he never anticipated.
- *Alive* (or Arc I not run). Full strength: staff of power in hand, surviving lieutenants at his side, and — if he reached the vault first through his own intelligence network — the advantage of having had time to prepare a position rather than react to the party's arrival. This is the hardest Arc J configuration, and the default one if Arc I was skipped.

If more than one hostile faction is present simultaneously, they do not coordinate with each other — Manshoon, Xanathar's remnants, and an Active-Adversarial Cassalanter team are all here for the same gold and are as much a threat to one another as to the party. A party that recognizes this can let its enemies do some of the fighting for it.

*Facing Manshoon (If Alive or Simulacrum).* He talks first, exactly as he did in Arc I if that confrontation occurred. His question here is not "what do you want" — he already knows. It is closer to: "You've done all the work. Why not let someone experienced handle what comes next?" He is not bluffing about being willing to fight for the gold, but he would still rather not — a fight this public, this close to the city's attention, is exactly the kind of exposure he has spent years avoiding. A party that can make continued exposure feel more costly to him than walking away may end this without combat at all.

*Dagult Neverember's Arrival.* Unconditional. Word of open faction war over his stolen gold reached Neverwinter weeks before Arc J's final scenes, and Dagult has been in Waterdeep — quietly, staying with Watch loyalists who never stopped answering to him personally — waiting to see how the Grand Game would resolve before he acted. He arrives at the City of the Dead's gate with hired muscle and whatever Watch loyalists he can still call on, expecting to reclaim "his" gold from whoever walks out with it. He is not a combatant by inclination; he prefers legal threat, political leverage, and the simple assumption of authority he has spent his life relying on. If directly challenged or publicly humiliated, however, he will not back down — losing face in front of witnesses is a cost he will not accept even when losing the confrontation itself might be cheaper.

*The Dragonstaff's Stakes.* Whoever holds the dragonstaff of Ahghairon after Aurinax's oath is discharged controls whether creatures of the dragon type can move freely through Waterdeep. This is not a minor detail to gloss past in Scene 6's gold logistics — it is the reason Jarlaxle wants it, the reason Manshoon would take it if he could, and the reason Dagult, whatever else he's lost, still believes he has some claim on it. If the party is deciding what to do with the staff, this is the moment that decision's stakes should be made explicit to them.

*The NeverSword and Renaer's Duel.* Optional, and entirely player-driven — it fires only if Renaer is present and the party creates space for the confrontation rather than steering around it. Renaer carries a sentient rapier, the NeverSword: +2, lawful good, capable of casting *zone of truth* once per day. The blade found Dagult unworthy to wield it, years ago, and has never explained why to anyone but Renaer. If the party facilitates a direct confrontation between father and son here — publicly, with witnesses, in the shadow of the vault that broke their family — Renaer may choose to draw it, invoking *zone of truth* on his father in front of everyone present. What Dagult says under that compulsion is his to decide at the table, but it should not be a villain's confession — it should be the truth of a proud, frightened man who genuinely believed, and still half-believes, that he was doing right by a city he thought needed protecting from itself. This confrontation, win or lose, is the seed of Dagult's eventual departure from Waterdeep politics entirely — and, later, from Waterdeep itself.

**Tone.** Reckoning. Every choice the party made about every faction, across every arc, resolves here into a single afternoon. Let the villain confrontation feel earned rather than arbitrary — the DM should be able to explain, from the Faction-State table alone, exactly why this specific group of people is standing at this specific gate.

## Scene 6: Aftermath

**Purpose.** The campaign's resolution. No further mechanical hook to pull, no Eye to insert, no Stone upgrade to award — Scene 6 is the space where every consequence the party set in motion finally lands, in full, with nothing held back for a future arc.

**Content.**

*Gold Logistics.* The hoard weighs 10,000 pounds. Moving it is a genuine logistics problem, not a detail to hand-wave, and the source material's own guidance is worth preserving here nearly verbatim: enforce logical consequences, but do not go out of the way to strip this treasure from a party that earned it.

| Path | Mechanism | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Cassalanter logistics** | If the Cassalanters are Cooperative, a dozen carts of empty barrels and trusted servants arrive within the hour | Fast and clean, but the party is now visibly obligated to a faction that is, at best, morally complicated |
| **Keep it, quietly** | The party arranges its own transport and storage | Any remaining active response teams are repurposed by the DM to complicate the extraction — a running battle through Waterdeep's streets is an entirely acceptable way to close the campaign |
| **Return it — Laeral's offer** | If Laeral becomes aware the party holds the gold, she asks once, politely, for its return in exchange for a 10% finder's fee (50,000 gp) | She asks only once. A refusal has consequences; an acceptance is honored in full, plus the political goodwill of having done the official thing |
| **Leave it in place** | The party takes only what they need, as they need it, and tells no one | Validated by the source material as "incredibly clever" — the Stone's memory-erasure ritual can even be used to cover the party's tracks entirely, if they're willing to pay its cost |
| **Political leverage** | The gold's existence, not its possession, becomes the bargaining chip | No physical movement required; the party trades knowledge of the hoard's location for favors, without ever claiming it outright |

A prominent stone replica of Mt. Waterdeep sits at the heart of the hoard pile, easy to miss beneath the coin — a single sapphire worth 25,000 gp is set into it, echoing the Heart of the Mountain from Undermountain's sixth level. The sapphire was never part of Neverember's embezzlement; the city has no claim to it. It is a clean reward, unconditionally available to a party willing to dig it out, and a fitting one for a party that chooses to return the rest.

*Political Aftermath.* Laeral's response depends entirely on which gold path the party chose — genuine gratitude for a return, professional disappointment for a refusal, quiet respect for a party clever enough to leave the money in place and simply draw on it as needed. Dagult's public standing, already ruined, takes whatever further hit Scene 5's confrontation dealt it; if the NeverSword duel occurred and Renaer's account of it becomes public, Dagult's remaining credibility in Waterdeep collapses entirely, and he has little reason left to stay.

*Cassalanter Resolution.* If the infernal contract was not destroyed in Arc G (any state other than Redeemed), the soul-pact thread remains open here. In the Cooperative (Gold Delivered) state, the gold half of the buy-out is now complete — but the contract also requires the ninety-nine souls, and Asmodeus accepts no partial payment. If Founders' Day's sacrifice was stopped, the ritual remains incomplete regardless of the gold, and the children's souls stay forfeit until the contract is destroyed by other means. If the sacrifice happened, the children are saved and the family is a set of powerful allies stained by mass murder — a state the party helped create. An Active-Adversarial resolution that ended in the Cassalanters' defeat at the vault leaves the contract unresolved and the family exposed, cornered, and dangerous in whatever way remains available to them.

*All-Seven-Faction Debriefs.*

**Mirt (Harpers).** Wants to know, plainly, what the party intends to do with the Stone now that its purpose is spent. He is not possessive about it — the Harpers' interest was always in the knowledge it held, not the artifact itself — but he asks the party to consider what happens if Illuun's influence lingers in whoever carries it next.

**Jalester Silvermane (Lords' Alliance).** Wants an accounting: where the gold ended up, whether the Writ's terms were honored, whether Laeral's trust in the party was well placed. If it was, he says so plainly, which for Jalester is a significant concession.

**Melannor Fellbranch (Emerald Enclave).** Relays Jeryth's closing word, whatever it turns out to be — usually something short, and usually true in a way that takes a moment to land. The Enclave's interest in the vault gold was never real; their interest in what the vault's opening disturbed below the city is not yet finished.

**Savra Belabranta (Order of the Gauntlet).** Wants to know whether the Cassalanter matter is truly closed. If the contract still stands, she is already planning the Order's next move — this was never only about the gold for her, and she says as much.

**Vajra Safahr (Force Grey).** If she was present for the vault opening, her debrief is technical and specific: what the vault's construction reveals about dwarven ward-craft nobody living remembers, what the dragonstaff's mechanics mean for the city's defenses now that its guardian's watch may be ending, and what, precisely, the party intends to do about a half-million gold pieces that until recently belonged to no one accountable.

**Tashlyn Yafeera or Davil Starsong (Doom Raiders).** Collects on whatever was promised at Dread Lord renown, if it was. If the party honored the arrangement, the Doom Raiders' position in Waterdeep is measurably stronger going forward — a fact Davil states without excessive gratitude, because gratitude was never really the currency they were negotiating in.

**Jarlaxle (Bregan D'aerthe, if contacted).** If his Scene 1 proposal was honored — the gold returned publicly in the party's name, the staff or a letter of introduction in his hand — he is, by his own admission, satisfied in a way he does not often permit himself to be. "I came to this city for information. I am leaving with considerably more than that." He does not say goodbye. He simply is not there the next time the party looks.

*Mad Mage Bridge Hooks.* Five threads, ready for whenever the campaign continues into Undermountain.

- **Halaster notices.** Within days of the vault's opening, a cryptic message reaches the party — a flameskull messenger, a fragment of dream, a brief and unsettling projection. Halaster Blackcloak, who has taken a professional interest in Manshoon before, has now taken one in the party as well.
- **The Cassalanter soul-pact** (if unresolved). The infernal contract's fate continues below the city, on Undermountain's eighteenth level, at Vanrakdoom.
- **Illuun's final vision.** If the Emerald Enclave's Mission 6 thread was fully paid off, the Stone — or the attuned holder, through it — receives one last, unsettling impression: something ancient, patient, and now fully aware of being carried. This is the direct seed for Undermountain's fourth level.
- **The Heart of the Mountain.** The maps and notes recovered from the vault's Hammer and Anvil chamber point directly to Undermountain's sixth level.
- **Harlsnod Darkshine's tomb.** If the party lingered on the Secrets Room's implications, his actual resting place — Undermountain's ninth level — is a loose thread worth remembering.

*Campaign Calendar.* Arc J consumes roughly one tenday: several days for Scene 1's final faction business, a day for the crypts and the ceremony (Scenes 2–3), an afternoon for the vault and its confrontation (Scenes 4–5), and the remaining days for aftermath and debriefs (Scene 6).

**Tone.** Resolution, not epilogue. This is not a coda tacked onto the real ending — it is the ending. Let every faction's final word land with the weight of nine arcs behind it, and let the Mad Mage hooks feel like an invitation rather than an assignment.

## Milestone and Transition

Arc J awards the level 7 milestone if it has not already fired at the fourth completed lair arc. Either way, by the end of Scene 6 the party stands at 7th level, the Grand Game formally concluded, and the campaign's central mystery — what happened to Neverember's stolen gold — fully resolved by the party's own choices rather than by the DM's fiat.

There is no further arc within Dragon Heist to transition into. What follows, if the table continues, is Undermountain. The five Mad Mage bridge hooks in Scene 6 are the DM's starting toolkit for that transition — use as many or as few as the table wants, but do not feel obligated to use all five. A campaign can end cleanly here, with nothing left unresolved that the party didn't choose to leave open.

# Design Notes: Vault of Dragons

## Genre Identity: Convergence, Not Heist

Arcs F through I each tested a different genre and a different set of party capabilities — dungeon infiltration, social infiltration, caper improvisation, raid violence. Arc J is not a fifth genre in that sequence. It is the point where all four genres' consequences arrive at once. The party does not case the vault, because there is nothing left to case — they already know where it is and what guards it. What replaces the heist framework's dramatic engine (plan, execute, adapt) is accumulated consequence: every faction relationship, every renown level, every choice about who to trust and who to betray, all resolving simultaneously. A DM running Arc J should not look for a new structural trick. The trick was already played, nine times, across the arcs that came before it.

## The Full Awakening's Prior Occurrence

This is the single most important practical note in the document. Arc F and Arc G both defer to "the 3-Eye disclosure scene from Arc J" without knowing which lair will actually deliver the third Eye — the party's own choices determine that. Arc H, published earlier in the drafting sequence, wrote the disclosure inline rather than deferring, on the theory that a scene this significant deserved to exist wherever it naturally lands rather than as a forward reference to a document that didn't exist yet at the time. The result is a minor structural seam: Arc H's inline text and this document's canonical text needed to agree closely enough that a DM reading both back to back would not notice a difference. They do. If Arc J is being run and the third Eye came from Arc F or Arc G, use Scene 1's Full Awakening text as written — it is the canonical version, and it was written to match Arc H's language precisely so that whichever arc actually delivers the disclosure, the moment reads identically. Do not restage the disclosure a second time in Arc J's own Scene 1 if it already happened in Scene 6 of whichever prior arc delivered the third Eye — reference that it happened, briefly, and move on.

## Reading the Faction-State Table

Every prior lair arc ends its Scene 6 by setting one or two flags for later use. Arc J is the arc where all of them get read at once. The Faction-State Summary Table in Scene 1 exists so a DM does not have to flip back through four other documents to reconstruct what's true — it is meant to be filled in once, before the session, and referenced throughout. The table's most important function is making Scene 5's roster assembly mechanical rather than improvised: a DM who knows Manshoon is Alive, Xanathar is Wounded, and the Cassalanters are Active-Adversarial can build the vault confrontation directly from the Roster-Assembly Table without guessing at what "feels right."

## Aurinax — Resolution Paths and the Oath as Puzzle

The four resolution paths in Scene 4 are deliberately structured as a puzzle with real, discoverable clues rather than a DM's arbitrary choice between "talk" and "fight." Hlam's advantage on Persuasion, the Masked Lord's Writ as a literal instrument of authority, Renaer's standing as Neverember's actual son, and Dagult's public exile as demonstrable legal fact are all clues the campaign has planted across multiple arcs. A party that has been paying attention arrives at the vault already holding the tools for a peaceful resolution, without the DM needing to hint at it in the moment. A party that has not is not punished for it — combat remains available and mechanically fair, per the boss-design framework — but it is framed, correctly, as the outcome Aurinax himself did not want.

Note on source material: the Alexandrian's own "Addendum: The Dragon of Dragon Heist" recommends replacing Aurinax with a straightforward red dragon, on the grounds that his backstory is convoluted continuity to introduce at the very end of a campaign. That concern does not apply here — this remix has been seeding Aurinax's existence, the dragonstaff's significance, and Dagult Neverember's role in the Grand Game since Arc A. The gold dragon was never a last-minute complication in this version of the story; he is the payoff of a thread the party has been walking beside the entire time.

## Dagult Neverember and the NeverSword

Dagult's arrival is unconditional, but the NeverSword confrontation is not, and should never be forced. If Renaer is not present, or the party's relationship with him has not developed into something that would make the confrontation meaningful, skip it entirely — Dagult's political and financial reckoning in Scene 6 lands on its own without it. When it does fire, resist writing Dagult's *zone of truth* confession as villain monologue. He is not a mustache-twirler; he is a proud, frightened man who genuinely believed, and still half-believes, that Waterdeep needed protecting from itself more than it needed his honesty. That belief is what makes the confrontation land — a simple confession of greed would be a smaller scene than the one this arc has been building toward.

## Gold Logistics as Real Consequence

The 10,000-pound weight of the hoard is not a rules technicality to smooth over. Treating it as a genuine logistics problem — one with real paths, real trade-offs, and no single correct answer — is the mechanical expression of this campaign's "no railroading gold decisions" design pillar. A DM should resist the temptation, which the original published adventure explicitly indulges, to find reasons the party can't simply keep what they earned. Enforce consequences. Do not manufacture punishment.

## Variable Difficulty at Convergence

Arcs F through I each calibrate their difficulty against a single lair's escalation tier — how aggressively the party moved through Arc E's outposts, essentially. Arc J has no equivalent fresh escalation clock, because it doesn't need one: the Faction-State Summary Table already carries every difficulty variable the campaign has generated across its entire length. A party that dismantled Manshoon's Splinter in Arc I, redeemed the Cassalanters in Arc G, and allied with Jarlaxle in Arc H arrives at Scene 5 facing, essentially, nobody. A party that skipped Arc I, left the Cassalanters Active-Adversarial, and made an enemy of Bregan D'aerthe arrives facing all three at once. This is not a punishment mechanic bolted onto the finale — it is the entire campaign's accumulated weight, made visible in a single afternoon, which is exactly what a convergence arc should do.

## Campaign Closure and the Mad Mage Bridge

Not every table continues into Undermountain, and Arc J's ending is written to work either way. The five bridge hooks in Scene 6 are offered as a toolkit, not an obligation — a DM whose table is finished with Dragon Heist can simply let Scene 6 be the ending, full stop. A DM whose table is hungry for more has five separate, concrete threads to pull, each already tied to a specific location and a specific consequence, rather than a vague gesture toward "the dungeon beneath the city." The dramatic question this campaign opened with — who claims the dragons of Waterdeep, and at what cost — is answered here, by the party, at the table. What comes after that is a different question entirely.

## On the Contingency of Fewer Than Three Eyes

The source material gestures at a contingency for a party that reaches this point with a damaged or incomplete Stone, but does not supply a mechanical framework for it beyond acknowledging the possibility exists. This document assumes the standard path: three Eyes recovered across Arcs F, G, and H, in any order. A table that has genuinely lost an Eye to destruction rather than simple non-recovery is in territory this document does not cover, and inventing an elaborate degraded-vault mechanic for a scenario the source material itself only gestures at would add more complexity than the edge case warrants. If it comes up, the simplest fix is usually the right one: let the party find another way to the same information through legwork, at a DC and a time cost the DM judges fair, rather than building a parallel ceremony for a version of the Stone that was never fully realized in the source design.
