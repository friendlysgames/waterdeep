# Sea Ward

> Structuring draft.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - The Sea Ward is Waterdeep's wealthiest ward, the home of the noble villas, the Field of Triumph arena and six of the city's major temples. It is also the Cassalanters' home ward.
> - **Fireball!** sends the party to the House of Inspired Hands in **The House of Inspired Hands**. In **The Nimblewright Hunt** the detector clicks at two more Sea Ward owners, Lord Adarbrent's bodyguard and the House of Wonder's temple guardian. **The Cassalanter Dinner** takes place at the villa, and Victoro Cassalanter's cancelled order for a nimblewright (NW-03) turns up in the ledger in **The Sea Maidens Faire**.
> - **Trollskull Alley** runs a gladiator tournament at the Field of Triumph in **The Field of Triumph**. Renaer offers his Sea Ward townhouse in **The Neighbors**, the tavern's financing passes through the Cassalanter Counting House in **The Grand Opening**, and one procession in **The Twin Parades** comes from this ward.
> - **Cassalanter Villa** is set at the villa. At the end of **Xanathar's Lair** the Stone's sharpened impressions point to a Sea Ward villa.
> - Faction events set here: Harpers (Mirt's manor, the Sea Ward dinner), Lords' Alliance (the Cassalanter side gate, a Sea Ward townhouse, a coffeehouse on Chandler Street), Doom Raiders (the Rosznar estate, a Sea Ward merchant's death), Bregan D'aerthe (a false Sea Ward address, an exposé about a Sea Ward family, the Commander audiences), Order of the Gauntlet (a Sea Ward family under suspicion, a Cassalanter agent, a sealed bond), Emerald Enclave (a butterfly garden, a drainage channel, a supply barge).
> - Location journals: **Cassalanter Villa** and **House of Inspired Hands** apply. No other mapped site sits in this ward.

**Purpose:** Gives the GM the ward as a place to run: where the nobility live, which temples and public grounds a party can walk into, and which front doors in the ward lead to the campaign's secrets.

The Sea Ward is Waterdeep's wealthiest ward, a district of walled villas, grand temples and the Field of Triumph on the high ground in the city's northwest, where the nobility live behind their garden walls.

## At a Glance

- **District Type:** Wealthy residential, with the city's arena and six major temples.
- **Boundaries:** North and west, the Trollwall. South, Julthoon Street. East, Shield Street, extending to the High Road north of Vondil Street (wiki, *City of Splendors* 2e). The wiki's North Ward entry describes the same line from the other side, and neither says which ward owns the strip between Shield Street and the High Road. See Draft Notes.
- **Founded:** 1276 DR, when the Sea Ward and the North Ward were officially added as wards (wiki, *The Grand History of the Realms* 3e and *City of Splendors* 2e). The four original wards date from 1035 DR.
- **Population:** No source gives a ward figure, and the wiki leaves the field blank. For scale, the city figures are 1,347,680 across Waterdeep and the farmland it commands (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting* 3e, 1372 DR), 132,661 counted as the metropolis, and about 200,000 in the city proper by the wiki's own estimate.
- **Lifestyle:** The wiki leaves this blank. Dragon Heist describes the residents as the rich and the powerful, "or those who wish you to think such of them, and can afford the rent", so the page treats the ward as wealthy (derived).
- **Services:** The Temple of Beauty's free public baths and mirrored salons, open from before dawn to after dusk. The House of Inspired Hands, where the city's inventors work. Heroes' Garden, a public park. The Street of the Singing Dolphin, one of the city's major arteries, where hire-coaches and drays gather. The wiki's 1357 to 1372 DR lists add inns such as the Golden Harp Inn, whose magical harp sings ballads, and shops selling gems, glassware, bread and chapbooks, and the Eltorchul Academy. Which of them survive in 1492 DR is [research].
- **Influences:** Blue and gold in ward competitions, and a sea lion mascot, a fanciful mix of fish and cat. The Lion Gate at the Field of Triumph, and a false legend that it is a sea lion's maw.
- **Primary Peoples:** No ward figure exists. The city figures are humans 64%, dwarves 10%, elves 10% (mostly moon elves), halflings 5%, half-elves 5%, gnomes 3%, half-orcs 2% and others 1% (wiki, *City of Splendors* 2e).
- **Languages:** The wiki leaves this blank. [research]
- **Religion:** House of Inspired Hands (Gond), Shrines of Nature (Mielikki and Silvanus), House of Wonder (Mystra), Tower of the Moon (Selûne), Temple of Beauty (Sune) and House of Heroes (Tempus), the six Sea Ward rows of the Dragon Heist Major Temples table. The table puts Tymora's Tower of Luck in the Castle Ward, and the Sea Ward text describes it anyway. See Culture under Life in the Ward. No dominant faith is sourced for the ward. [research]
- **Governance:** No ward officer is sourced. [research] The City Watch keeps single-person watch boxes on nearly every street corner, and the noble houses guard their own walled estates. Two Masked Lords, Corylus Thann and Jelenn Urmbrusk, belong to Sea Ward families (Dragon Heist).
- **Architecture:** Palatial homes behind garden walls, many built around the remains of old pirate and warlord fortresses. The Cassalanter Villa's white walls and crimson roof and turrets. The House of Wonder's ornate towers and mosaics. The Tower of the Moon, rising seventy-five feet above the street. The Field of Triumph, over five stories tall.
- **Economy:** Rents that only the rich can pay. Banking through the Cassalanter Counting House, trading houses, vintners and gem merchants, and the arena's crowds. Guild law applies as everywhere in the city.
- **Organizations:** The Cassalanters, House Rosznar, the Thann and Urmbrusk families, the Watchful Order (Barnibus Blastwind), the Harpers (Mirt's dinner house, Renaer Neverember), the Order of the Gauntlet (a family under suspicion), the Emerald Enclave (Jeryth's sense of infernal influence), the House of Inspired Hands.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Secret
> - Cassalanter Villa hides an illegal temple to Asmodeus below its sub-basement. Victoro and Ammalia traded their three children's souls to Asmodeus about 1489 DR, and the contract falls due on Flamerule 11. They mean to poison ninety-nine guests at the Founders' Day feast on Flamerule 1 to buy out the twins' souls. The villa is mapped in **Cassalanter Villa**.
> - Nobody in the ward knows. Every faction suspects the family because it is too nice to be this rich, and none knows about the pact. Savra Belabranta of the Order of the Gauntlet hunts for proof, and Jeryth Phaulkon feels an infernal influence in the Sea Ward that the Emerald Enclave cannot trace to a house. The one exception is Jarlaxle, who knows through **Vessa**, his doppelganger spy in the Cassalanter social circle, and tells Bregan D'aerthe members nothing unless they work it out.

## Landmarks

**Field of Triumph** — Arena
- A stadium over five stories tall at the southern end of the Street of the Singing Dolphin, across the street from the House of Heroes. Its Lion Gate is carved as a lion's maw, and the persistent legend that it is a sea lion's maw is false. The gate's plans are kept in the Map House in the Castle Ward.
- It holds horse races, shows of magic and illusion, martial displays, gladiatorial combats and exhibitions of strange creatures from late spring to late autumn, with crowds in the thousands (wiki, 2e). The wiki names a director only for 1360 DR, so the present director is [research].
- Hook: in **Trollskull Alley**, Zardoz Zord of the **Sea Maidens Faire** sponsors the party's tournament entry. Nobles send commissions between rounds, Meloon Wardragon leads the team in the final, and a Xanathar's Guild scout hands over a token.

**House of Heroes** — Temple of Tempus
- The largest temple in the city, across the street from the Field of Triumph. Its grand halls celebrate the city's champions of battle and sport.
- The winners of ward competitions are paraded here after their victories, carried on shoulders or passed hand over hand above the crowd. The sources name no priest. [research]
- Hook: the Plowing and Running finals on Mirtul 9 send a ward champion here. No campaign event uses the temple.

**House of Wonder** — Temple of Mystra
- Dragon Heist calls it the most splendid temple to the gods of magic in the world, with ornate towers, brilliant mosaics and magical displays. Ahghairon's favorite violets grow around it, and on Ahghairon's Day (Eleasis 1) commoners leave violets on its altars.
- It stands near where Diamond Street and Delzorin Street cross, the spot Dragon Heist recommends for a view of the villas. The sources name no high priest. [research]
- Hook: in **The Nimblewright Hunt** the detector gives a quiet click at the temple guardian, and the high priest confirms its presence all morning with several witnesses.

**Tower of the Moon** — Temple of Selûne
- Also called the House of the Moon. It has the tallest tower of any temple in the city, seventy-five feet above the street, and its priests bask in the moonlight at the top in every season.
- A high priestess keeps the Wand of the Four Moons, a relic said to be the mace Selûne carried against Shar. Visitors can see it from a well-guarded distance. No source names her.
- Hook: no campaign event uses the temple. On the fullest night of Uktar, after the campaign ends, a parade leaves it at moonrise for the harbor.

**House of Inspired Hands** — Temple of Gond
- On the corner of Seawatch Street and Shark Street. Dragon Heist says that "worship is work" here and that the city's inventors build everything from flying machines to stronger door hinges. The toothed cog of Gond appears on every surface.
- Valetta (Neutral, Dragonborn, she/her), a bronze dragonborn priest of Gond, is the senior cleric. Nim, a nimblewright that cannot speak and signs in Common Sign Language, lives in the attic. The temple is mapped in **House of Inspired Hands**.
- Hook: in **The House of Inspired Hands** the party meets Valetta and Nim after the fireball and leaves with a nimblewright detector.

**Cassalanter Villa** — Noble estate
- Stark white walls and a gleaming crimson roof with turrets stand out even among the Sea Ward's other opulent estates. A tall white brick wall surrounds green gardens of hedges and water features, and one wrought-iron gate carries the family crest, a green Y overlapping a stylized goose being fed. Armored guards flank the gate. The campaign journal gives the mansion four stories above ground and a topiary garden kept for two generations, and Ammalia's butterfly garden lies at the back.
- Victoro and Ammalia Cassalanter live here, and the family hosts public galas during Waukeentide (Tarsakh 1 to 10).
- Hook: the family invites the party to dinner in **The Cassalanter Dinner**. A Watch box stands at the street corner outside the gate.

> - In **Cassalanter Villa** the villa layer is a social infiltration with a four-stage exposure track (Accepted, Noticed, Suspected, Exposed), and the temple below the sub-basement stair is a dungeon.

**Rosznar Villa** — Noble estate
- On Thunderstaff Way between Copper Street and Shield Street, west of the High Road. House Rosznar is a Tethyrian house that was once banished from Waterdeep for smuggling, slavery and other crimes. It has returned and trades in wine-making and gem trading, under the motto "We fly high and stoop swift." No source names the head of the house. [research]
- Hook: Cevin Rallygar, a Sea Ward merchant, is suing a trading house in which House Rosznar holds shares, as the party can learn in **The Poisoned Delivery**.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Lady Esvele Rosznar is the Black Viper, and the coach in **The Poisoned Delivery** takes her to her estate in the Sea Ward. The page treats that estate as this villa (derived). The published adventure says she keeps the double life secret from her house.

**Neverember House** — Residence
- A four-story house owned by Renaer Neverember. He spends as little time here as he can, because spies loyal to his father, Dagult Neverember, watch it constantly. His friends have an open invitation to use it as they please.
- Hook: in **The Neighbors**, Renaer offers the party his Sea Ward townhouse as a workspace for anything sensitive, and means it.

**Heroes' Garden** — Public garden
- The only green space in the city open to the public besides the City of the Dead, in the far north of the ward between Heroes' Walk and Trollkill Street. Grass, trees and ponds surround fine statues of figures important to the city's history, among them marble figures of the Open Lords Baeron and Lhestyn (wiki, 3e).
- Few visitors come, and local citizens and the Watch keep it free of debris (wiki). The Lords of Waterdeep made the garden from the cleared site of the Tower of Yintros.
- Hook: the wiki places a manhole into the sewers in the central stand of trees at the southern end, and no campaign event uses it. Whether it survives in 1492 DR is [research].

**Blue Alley** — Hidden alley
- Dragon Heist's narrator will not say where it is. Blue tiles on the streets and walls lead into an alley that passes under the surrounding buildings, and at night the tiles glimmer with the blue light of foxfire. More than one route leads in and few lead out. Most who enter do not come back.
- The alley serves a house in the ward that has no windows or doors, and the neighbors will not speak of it. What lives there is [research]. No campaign event sends the party here.

## Characters of Interest

**Victoro Cassalanter (Lawful Evil, Half-Elf, he/him)**
- A devilishly handsome half-elf banker and philanthropist whose profits are said to fund charities across Waterdeep. He is publicly a worshipper of Lathander, and the family is known for its devotion to Siamorphe. His unreadable smile warms when he speaks of his younger children.
- Hook: he hosts the dinner in **The Cassalanter Dinner**, and in **The Wazoo Affair** he sends two household agents, posing as solicitors, to find who gave the exposé to the publisher.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Victoro is a secret priest of Asmodeus. Ammalia makes the family's final calls, and he has stopped questioning that.

**Ammalia Cassalanter (Lawful Evil, Human, she/her)**
- A wizard known for driving a hard bargain, and keeper of the most beautiful butterfly garden in Waterdeep, where she can often be found supervising her youngest children at play. She runs a charity booth for the Cassalanter Foundation's orphan fund during the festival in **The Twin Parades**.
- Hook: in **Summerstrider** the Emerald Enclave's Sea Ward report describes a butterfly garden at the back of one villa that keeps tropical species alive in Waterdeep's climate. The report names no owner.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Ammalia plans the Founders' Day feast and reads to her eldest son, Osvaldo, each week in the villa attic, from a chair outside his shielding circle. Osvaldo is a chain devil now, and the twins are told he is at school.

**The Black Viper (Chaotic Neutral, Human, she/her)**
- A masked cat burglar who robs the estates of Waterdeep's nobility, slipping past guards and wards with a skill that has made the name a byword in the Sea Ward. No one has caught her, and no one agrees on who she is.
- Hook: in **The Poisoned Delivery** she collects a coffer of vials meant to loosen the tongue of a Sea Ward merchant she means to rob. The vials are poison.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - She is Lady Esvele Rosznar, the dutiful younger daughter of House Rosznar. A party can recruit her for **Cassalanter Villa** with a DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation) check. At the Cassalanter event she attends as a guest and slips away mid-dinner to rob her hosts.

**Renaer Neverember (Chaotic Good, Illuskan Human, he/him)**
- The estranged son of Dagult Neverember, a well-read collector of rare tomes who knows the city's factions, geography and politics. He owns considerable property in Waterdeep, all of it inherited from his mother.
- Hook: he appears as an ally in **Finding Floon** and **Trollskull Alley**, and the party can ask him about any faction in the city.

**Barnibus Blastwind (Lawful Good, Illuskan Human, he/him)**
- An investigator of the Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors who handles unusual crimes. He is prickly and secretive, and he confides in Saeth Cromley and almost no one else.
- A lifelong bachelor, he lives on a small, tidy estate in the Sea Ward that he inherited from his grandmother, and he spends his free days reading and writing in her library.
- Hook: **Fireball!** puts him in charge of the fireball scene.

**Jelenn Urmbrusk (Neutral, Tethyrian Human, she/her)**
- A proud noblewoman in her fifties who never travels without sixteen guards and has no time for strangers. The Urmbrusk family keeps a walled estate in the Sea Ward.
- Hook: a Bregan D'aerthe Commander in **Commander** can ask for an audience with her, and she receives the member in a closed carriage for a fifteen-minute drive through the Sea Ward.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Jelenn is a Masked Lord. Bad investments ruined her money-lending business, and she borrowed from Manshoon's Zhentarim to avoid gossip. They now blackmail her, and her name is in the Kolat Towers ledger. If the party frees her from them, she grants a special favor.

## Life in the Ward

**Governing metaphor:** The Sea Ward is a ward of garden walls, with gilt and gates facing the street and the households keeping their business behind them.

### Culture
- Residents are the rich and powerful, or those who wish to look it, and the ward wears that on its streets. For the best all-around view of the glittering homes behind their garden walls, stand where Diamond Street and Delzorin Street cross, near the House of Wonder, and turn in a circle.
- Ward pride centers on blue and gold and the sea lion mascot. Homes and businesses fly the colors during the city's races and parades, and ward champions are paraded to the House of Heroes.
- Temples of the ward: the House of Inspired Hands for Gond, the Shrines of Nature for Mielikki and Silvanus, the House of Wonder for Mystra, the Tower of the Moon for Selûne, the Temple of Beauty for Sune and the House of Heroes for Tempus (Dragon Heist Major Temples table). The first, third, fourth and sixth have Landmarks entries.
- The Temple of Beauty offers marbled public baths and mirrored salons, open from before dawn to after dusk, for travelers who want to refresh themselves or primp before a meeting or a night out. There is no fee for the baths or the advice of the many attendants, though donations are encouraged. No campaign file uses the temple. [research]
- The Shrines of Nature are resplendent gardens of shrines, statues and fountains behind an iron fence, and only residents of the Sea Ward may enter. Passers-by can glimpse the grounds from the street. No campaign file uses the shrines. [research]
- Tymora's Tower of Luck is a Castle Ward temple by the Major Temples table, so this page does not count it among the ward's temples. Dragon Heist's Sea Ward text describes it as a many-pillared atrium roofed in glass, with a bronze statue of Tymora as a laughing girl leaping from the top of a fountain. Visitors toss a coin into her outstretched hand, and a coin that lands there is a sure sign of her favor. The same text puts the Shrines of Nature a block from it. See Draft Notes.
- Gondwatch Lane, at the southern entrance to the House of Inspired Hands, is the testing ground for inventions too dangerous to run inside the temple. Locals stand and watch while food vendors work the crowd.
- Siamorphe's holy day, the Divine Pageantry, is kept by her noble followers in the Sea Ward and North Ward, and the nobles take over Heroes' Garden and Heroes' Walk (wiki, 3e). Whether it is still kept in 1492 DR is [research].

### Defense
- The City Watch keeps small single-person watch boxes on nearly every street corner, including the one outside the gate of Cassalanter Villa. If a guard sounds the alarm, the Watch member on duty fetches forty City Watch veterans to cordon off the property.
- The Watch has closed every complaint against one Sea Ward family without findings, according to the Order of the Gauntlet's files. In **Stingblade** the Lords' Alliance's Watch household inquiries file holds three closed inquiries against one unnamed Sea Ward house, for a missing servant, a debt dispute and a noise complaint. Every sergeant in the ward knows the Watch posts a guard at the Cassalanter service gate.
- The Watchful Order has an investigator in the ward, Barnibus Blastwind, whose estate lies here.
- The noble houses keep their own armed guards. Jelenn Urmbrusk travels with sixteen, and Corylus Thann with six, and Corylus has eight crooked Watch veterans to call on.

### Politics
- The ward's power sits in its noble houses and their Masked Lords. Dragon Heist places walled estates for the Thann and Urmbrusk families here, and both Corylus Thann and Jelenn Urmbrusk are Masked Lords whom Manshoon's Zhentarim squeeze.
- Mirt, a Masked Lord and senior Harper, receives Harper members at a manor in the Sea Ward in the campaign's Harper events. Dragon Heist also sends visitors to his manor in the Sea Ward, where he is home one time in ten. The Castle Ward page keeps his mansion near the Naval Harbor in that ward.
- Dagult Neverember, the former Open Lord now Lord of Neverwinter, still has agents watching his son's house.
- Who speaks for the ward as a whole is [research].

### Economy
- Only those who can afford the rent live or run businesses here, and the sources give no rents or prices. [research]
- The Cassalanter Counting House banks across the ward and holds accounts throughout the North Ward, and the party's renovation loans pass through it in **The Grand Opening**.
- House Rosznar trades in wine and gems, and the Thanns are Waterdeep's most prominent vintners. Trading houses and merchants such as Cevin Rallygar do their business here. In **Winterstalker** a covered barge leaves a private pier on the Sea Ward shore at midnight, and the harbor master's clerk takes a coin each tenday to leave its trips out of the book.

### Lore
- When the warlords and pirates of early Waters Deep gained enough gold, they built fortresses on fields of sea-tousled grass. Their remains are built into the palatial homes of today's noble families.
- The Lords of Waterdeep made Heroes' Garden from the cleared site of the Tower of Yintros after the tower's destruction (wiki, 3e).
- Pharra's Alley is named for the first leader of the House of Wonder and is better known for its Circle of Skulls. Seven floating skulls hover in a circle and argue in whispers about events in the city. If they are interrupted, they may help the intruder or attack with murderous spells.
- The Field of Triumph hosts the finals of Plowing and Running on Mirtul 9, a gala dance on Guildsmeet (Tarsakh 7), and the illusory history displays and martial exhibitions of Founders' Day on Flamerule 1. The Cassalanters' feast falls on Flamerule 1 as well (see Secret).
- The wiki (2e) says that every fourth year, on Shieldmeet, the Lords of Waterdeep gather on the Field in covered helms and robes, seated on sixteen thrones, to be reaffirmed. Shieldmeet in 1492 DR falls after Flamerule 30, past the campaign's Flamerule 11 deadline, and no campaign file uses it.
- The festival calendar is in **Timekeeping and the Festival Calendar**, and Blue Alley is described in Landmarks.

## Overview

The Sea Ward is the wealthiest ward in Waterdeep, a district of walled villas in the northwest of the city. Noble families live in palatial homes that incorporate the remains of old fortresses built by early warlords and pirates. Its temples are among the largest in the city, and the Field of Triumph hosts public races, shows and gladiatorial bouts. Residents fly blue and gold in ward contests and keep a sea lion, part fish and part cat, as their mascot.

## Read Aloud

Sensory anchors for the GM to build the passage from (not written out):
- Standing where Diamond Street and Delzorin Street cross: garden walls, glittering villas, wrought-iron gates and the towers of the House of Wonder.
- The Field of Triumph: a crowd filling Gulzindar Street, the carved Lion Gate and the noise of a stadium over five stories tall.
- The House of Wonder: ornate towers, bright mosaics, magical displays and violets planted among the ornament.
- The Tower of the Moon rising above the street, with figures at its top in the moonlight.
- The House of Inspired Hands: a toothed cog on every doorknob, gutter spout and collar clasp, the wrought iron gate and the main hall open to the street.
- Gondwatch Lane: a test going wrong at a safe distance, with a crowd watching and food vendors working through it.
- Cassalanter Villa: stark white walls, a crimson roof with turrets, green gardens, a white brick wall and armored guards at a wrought-iron gate.
- The Temple of Beauty: marbled baths and mirrored salons, open before dawn.
- Heroes' Garden: a lush park of grass, trees and ponds with marble statues, and almost no visitors.
- Single-person watch boxes at the street corners.
- Blue Alley: blue tiles on the walls, glimmering with foxfire at night.
- The ward's overall look: rich, walled, green and a little guarded.

**Tone:** A rich, well-kept ward that is polite to strangers and protective of its walls, played straight and a little dry. Keep the temples welcoming, and keep the Cassalanters' secrets in the Secret and GM Notes callouts.
