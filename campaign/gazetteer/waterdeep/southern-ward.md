> - The Southern Ward is Waterdeep's caravan ward, a district of stables, inn yards and guildhalls on the city's southeast side. It holds Phaulkonmere, the Emerald Enclave's estate, and Kolat Towers, Manshoon's fortress, which stand one block apart.
> - **Trollskull Alley** sends Emerald Enclave candidates to Phaulkonmere on a cat's invitation. **Gralhund Villa** has an aftermath branch in which Istrid Horn leaves a note naming a Zhentarim courier cache in the "South Ward", which this page reads as the Southern Ward.
> - **Faction Outposts** (a structure doc, not yet a quest journal) sends the party to the Converted Windmill on Coachlamp Lane (Cassalanters) and to Orb Confectioners on Rising Ride (Xanathar's Guild). It also has Jeryth report a wound in the ley lines near Coachlamp Lane. The papers at Orb Confectioners lead toward **Xanathar's Lair**, and the windmill's papers point to the Asmodean Shrine on Aveen Street and the Brandath Crypts. **Kolat Towers** is the quest set at the towers.
> - Faction events set here: Emerald Enclave (**Emerald Enclave First Meeting**, **The Undercliff Scarecrows**, **Ten Nights in the City of the Dead**, **The Doppelganger Problem**, **The Grells in the Dock Ward**, **The Water Table Stirs**, **Summerstrider**, **Autumnreaver**, **Winterstalker** and **Master of the Wild**, nearly all at Phaulkonmere), Doom Raiders (**The Missing Snobeedle** among the ward's halflings, **Viper** at the Dusty Ladle, **Dread Lord** at a boarding house), Bregan D'aerthe (**The Wazoo Affair** and **The Theater's Back Room**, both about the windmill) and Lords' Alliance (**Stingblade** and **Fentree's Ledger** at Caravan Court counting houses, and the design notes for **Redknife**, which place Kolat Towers here).
> - Location journals: none applies. No mapped site sits in this ward. The windmill, Orb Confectioners and Kolat Towers have structure docs and faction guides only, and **Xanathar Sewer Hideout** names no Southern Ward entrance.

**Purpose:** Gives the GM the ward as a place to run: where travelers and halflings gather, where the Emerald Enclave keeps its gate, and which doors in the ward open onto the Cassalanters and Manshoon.

The Southern Ward is the caravan district of Waterdeep, a ward of stables, inn yards and guildhalls where wagon trains arrive and the people who serve them live above the animals.

## At a Glance

- **District Type:** Caravan district, laborers' quarter, inns and guildhalls. The wiki lists the aliases Temple Ward and Caravan City.
- **Boundaries:** North, the Trades Ward across Telshambra's Street and Caravan Court. West and south, the Dock Ward across the Way of the Dragon. East, the Trollwall, with the southern inner gate to the Caravan Grounds and the South Gate beyond (wiki, circa 1372 DR). Dragon Heist's orientation rule for the Honorable Knight, a Trades Ward statue between Snail Street and the Way of the Dragon, puts a visitor in the Southern Ward when the statue stands to the north and west.
- **Founded:** 1035 DR by Ahghairon, as one of the four original wards, where the wiki calls it the Temple Ward (wiki, 2e). The wiki's own Southern Ward page gives no date.
- **Population:** No source gives a ward figure, and the wiki leaves the field blank. Its Inhabitants text says caravan merchants, teamsters and guards swell the population for most of the year. For scale, the city figures are 1,347,680 across Waterdeep and the farmland it commands (*Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting* 3e, 1372 DR), 132,661 counted as the metropolis, and about 200,000 in the city proper by the wiki's own estimate.
- **Lifestyle:** The wiki infobox leaves this blank. Its Inhabitants text calls the ward the "poor" ward, and Dragon Heist calls it a district of laborers who cater to travelers, so the page treats the ward as modest to poor (derived).
- **Services:** Six guildhalls (wiki, 2e): Builders' Hall for stonecutters, masons, potters and tile-makers, the House of Good Spirits for vintners, distillers and brewers, Metalmasters' Hall for smiths and metalforgers, the Road House for carters and coachmen, Saddlers' and Harness-Makers' Hall, and the Stone House for carpenters, roofers and plaisterers. Taverns such as the Full Cup, the Red Gauntlet and the Swords' Rest, and shops such as Nueth's Fine Nets and the Old Monster Shop. The wiki's lists date from 1357 to 1372 DR, and which of them survive in 1492 DR is [research].
- **Influences:** A mule, pugnacious and rampant, on a red and white flag. Dragon Heist says the colors stand for the blood and tears the ward's people have shed in their labors.
- **Primary Peoples:** No ward figure exists. Dragon Heist says the ward is made up of enclaves, blocks and streets occupied by citizens who trace their ancestry to other realms, with halfling cooks, Calishite singers and dwarven craftsmen among them. The city figures are humans 64%, dwarves 10%, elves 10% (mostly moon elves), halflings 5%, half-elves 5%, gnomes 3%, half-orcs 2% and others 1% (wiki, *City of Splendors* 2e).
- **Languages:** The wiki leaves this blank. [research]
- **Religion:** Helm's Hall (Helm), the Southern Ward row of the Dragon Heist Major Temples table, and a Yondalla shrine on Larkhaven Street that only the campaign places here. The wiki's religion field is blank. Phaulkonmere belongs to Mielikki's Chosen, but no file calls it a temple or a shrine. Tymora's Tower of Luck is a Castle Ward row in the table and is not listed here. See Culture under Life in the Ward. No dominant faith is sourced for the ward. [research]
- **Governance:** No ward officer, Watch post or garrison is sourced. [research] The wiki says a City Guard patrol and a Watchful Order member kept order at the Moon Sphere in the 14th century.
- **Architecture:** Homes and businesses stand above stables or around inn yards, near where wagon trains are housed (Dragon Heist). Most buildings are old multi-story tenements with shops on the ground floor, and dust and mud are common in the streets (wiki). The Jade Dancer has three stories with balconies, and Kolat Towers is a dilapidated pair of towers.
- **Economy:** The caravan trade, with its merchants, teamsters, guards, stables and counting houses, and the guildhalls' crafts. Guild law applies as everywhere in the city.
- **Organizations:** The Emerald Enclave (Phaulkonmere), Manshoon's Zhentarim (Kolat Towers), the Cassalanters (the Converted Windmill), Xanathar's Guild (Orb Confectioners), the Doom Raiders (a boarding house and the ward's halfling informants), the Shard Shunners (a halfling wererat gang) and the guilds above.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Secret
> - The Converted Windmill on Coachlamp Lane is an Asmodean shrine and a fallback site for the Cassalanters' cult. Seffia Naelryke and Arn Xalrondar keep it, the family pays the rent on the upper floor under a false name, and a ritual room holds a map with the Brandath Crypts circled. It is a separate site from the Asmodean Shrine on Aveen Street and from the temple beneath **Cassalanter Villa**.
> - Nobody outside the household and the cult knows the family worships Asmodeus. The one exception is Jarlaxle, whose spy **Vessa** moves in the family's social circle and who tells Bregan D'aerthe members nothing unless they work it out. The footmen's rumor in **The Wazoo Affair** names the windmill only as a place where lanterns burn on moonless nights, and the Enclave has felt a wound in the ley lines near Coachlamp Lane without finding a cause.

## Landmarks

**Phaulkonmere** — Emerald Enclave estate
- A walled noble villa and garden whose buildings are covered with moss and ivy (Dragon Heist). The garden is older than the street around it, and every path bends back to its oldest oak.
- The Tarm and Phaulkon families own it and spend most of the year abroad. **Melannor Fellbranch** keeps the grounds, and **Jeryth Phaulkon** speaks from the leaves as a disembodied voice. The gate stands open before an invited visitor arrives, and response teams may watch the street but never enter.
- Hook: a cat's invitation brings candidates here in **Emerald Enclave First Meeting**, and nearly every later Enclave event starts or ends at the gate. The walk from Trollskull Alley takes about two hours.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Phaulkonmere stands one block south of Kolat Towers (Dragon Heist). Until **Manshoon Named** is marked for a member, Enclave speakers say "the Splinter" and never point toward the towers.

**Kolat Towers** — Wizards' towers
- A dilapidated, two-towered edifice that stands tall over its neighbors. Light from *continual flame* spells spills from the windows, and most folk believe the place is abandoned. Others say the ghosts of the Kolat brothers haunt it (Dragon Heist).
- The campaign names the builders as the brothers Duhlark and Alcedor Kolat, who raised the towers in the late 14th century. Alcedor died there in 1385 DR, and Duhlark was found dead in his study nine years later.
- Hook: **Kolat Towers** is the quest set here. The ward is the one the published adventure gives in its character appendix, and the Trades Ward reading is in Draft Notes.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Manshoon's clone lives here and runs the Splinter cell from the towers. He posts no visible guards, because he wants the site believed abandoned, and he is never seen entering or leaving, because he moves by teleportation circle.
> - A magical barrier with the properties of a wall of force surrounds the towers. Splinter agents carry pass-amulets that bypass it. Teleportation circles link the towers to Yellowspire in the Castle Ward and to the Interrogation House in the North Ward.
> - Lady Gondafrey, a City Watch captain taken in 1379 DR, is a prisoner inside. The rooms, the sanctum and the escalation rules are in **Kolat Towers**.

**Converted Windmill, Coachlamp Lane** — Decrepit windmill
- A decrepit building that used to be a windmill (Dragon Heist). **Seffia Naelryke** and **Arn Xalrondar** live on the upper floor as ordinary tenants, and she does not receive visitors.
- A Cassalanter steward, Brimel Crestfall, has carried baskets of candles, salt, black wax and wine to the second landing of its stair for years. The family keeps the windmill off its calendar and its house books.
- Hook: **The Wazoo Affair** hides a rumor of it among the footmen's gossip, **The Theater's Back Room** lets the party buy a floor plan from Brimel, and **Faction Outposts** treats it as a mini-heist.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Ammalia Cassalanter visits twice a month, alone, in a hired cab with the curtains drawn. The family pays the upper floor's rent in the name of Marra Selby.
> - A note under the bed in the secure apartment (Area W7) reads "Meeting Ches 17th, the shrine on Aveen Street". The ritual room (Area W8) holds a blood-painted pentagram and a desk with a map of Waterdeep, and a charcoal circle on the map marks the Brandath Crypts. The roster is Seffia, Arn and one invisible Imp on watch.

**Orb Confectioners** — Sweets shop and Xanathar's Guild outpost, Rising Ride
- A sweets shop that sells eye-shaped treats: smallcakes, sweetmeats, sugar plates and candied nuts. The Rising Ride is one of the streets that meet Caravan Court (wiki).
- **Korgstrod Uxgulm**, a duergar, runs the cellar operation beneath it for Xanathar's Guild and leads its second response team. A customer who asks for "eight small eyes" and answers "Around an ocular cake" earns an escort to the basement stairs.
- Hook: **Faction Outposts** treats it as an encounter-plus outpost. A letter there threatens the mechanical beholder's delivery to Terasse Estate, and correspondence with Nar'l Xibrindas gives guildsigns that lead toward **Xanathar's Lair**.

**Helm's Hall** — Temple of Helm
- The Dragon Heist Major Temples table lists Helm's Hall as the temple of Helm in the Southern Ward. No source describes the building, its size or its keepers. [research]
- The campaign's Temple of Helm treats the Watch's poor for free and runs a hospice wing for Watch veterans and their families. This page treats the Temple of Helm and Helm's Hall as one temple (derived).
- Hook: Order of the Gauntlet members who name Savra Belabranta to the door-warden receive healing, a bed and briefings here. The events place the temple in the Castle Ward, and the conflict is in Draft Notes.

**The Jade Dancer** — Festhall and tavern, Dancing Court
- A three-story tavern, dance hall and inn at the end of the Dancing Court, a cul-de-sac off the southern end of Slop Street. Patrons sometimes leap from its balconies into the Moon Sphere.
- The festhall takes its name from an eight-foot jade statue of a woman that animates, dances for patrons and sometimes serves as a bouncer. Dragon Heist says it is as strong as a stone golem. The sources name no owner. [research]
- Hook: on every full moon a glowing blue sphere appears in the Dancing Court, and anyone inside it can fly by willing it. Amateurs are unwelcome except at certain daylight appearances. No campaign event uses the festhall or the sphere.

**Caravan Court** — Courtyard
- A large courtyard in the northeast of the ward where caravans muster and disband. Its north side borders the Trades Ward, and old men gather on stools and crates to watch others labor (wiki, 2e).
- Ruid's Stroll runs from the court to the Trollwall. The hooded ghost of the mage Ruid haunts it, and his touch brings deathly chills on foggy nights. Anyone who lets him pass through them learns a secret truth about someone or something in their life, if they survive, and attempts to banish him have failed.
- Hook: **Stingblade** sends a Lords' Alliance member to the counting house of the Caravan Court factor Tessaly Brightwater, and **Fentree's Ledger** uses the money-changer Dessa Voll and the Green Dragon Inn, which stands just off the court. Neither event names a ward.

**Yondalla Shrine, Larkhaven Street** — Halfling shrine
- A whitewashed cottage with a round green door and a hearth burning inside. A few dozen halflings fill the pews for the evening blessing, and a gray-haired halfling in a plain robe sets fresh candles along the wall.
- A stair beside the cooperage on the same street leads to rented rooms, and the halfling community gathers in those rooms, at this shrine and at the market by the Waymoot.
- Hook: in **The Missing Snobeedle**, the Snobeedle family's notices hang here, and the party spends three days among the ward's halflings. Pippa Underbough, Tolliver Greenbottle and Marda Goodbarrel decide whether the party reaches **Kelso Fiddlewick**.

**The Waymoot** — Crossroads and signpost
- Where the High Road and the Way of the Dragon meet in the south of the city. A high signpost with hanging arrows points toward the harbor and each gate, and a destination spoken into a crystal on the post appears on the right arrow with its distance in miles.
- The Watchful Order made the signpost and local merchants paid for it. It cannot find places inside the city, and hawkers nearby offer to guide visitors for a fee.
- Hook: a halfling market fills the paved road south of the post, and the meeting with Dasher Snobeedle and Kelso takes place at noon in a small square with a dry fountain. The ward is unsettled (see Draft Notes).

**The Dusty Ladle** — Taproom
- A low, smoky taproom with sawdust on the floor and a sea-coal fire in the hearth. Most patrons hunch over their bowls and do not look up.
- Hook: in **Viper**, while **Davil Arrested** is marked and **Davil Released** is not, **Tashlyn Yafeera** receives each Doom Raiders member who reaches Renown 10 at the back table at 20:00.

## Characters of Interest

**Melannor Fellbranch (Neutral Good, Half-Elf, he/him)**
- The friendly but humorless groundskeeper of Phaulkonmere, who sends the Enclave's missions by *Animal Messenger* in messages of no more than twenty-five words. He never uses contractions, never jokes and never swears, and he answers a joke literally.
- He is the brother of **Tally Fellbranch**, whom he calls Talisolvanar, and he asks after him at the First Meeting.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Melannor senses something wrong in the earth beneath the Castle Ward and cannot say what it is. The Castle Ward Secret holds the answer.

**Jeryth Phaulkon (Neutral Good, Disembodied Presence, she/her)**
- A noblewoman who became a Chosen of Mielikki and now lives in the gardens of Phaulkonmere as a voice. She speaks one or two short sentences at a time, and the branches move when she speaks.
- She sends no missions, and Melannor's animals carry her requests. Asked about the vault gold, she answers once, "That question is for the Lords to settle."

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Jeryth's Southern Ward report in **Summerstrider** is that she has felt a wound in the ley lines near Coachlamp Lane for a tenday and a half. It is not healing, and the Enclave has no lead on the cause. The windmill in the Secret is the cause, and Jeryth does not know it.

**Seffia Naelryke (Lawful Evil, Tethyrian Human, she/her)**
- A cult fanatic of Asmodeus who leads the Cassalanter response team from the Converted Windmill. The team is Seffia, two Spies, one Veteran and one invisible Imp, and it gathers intelligence, watches targets and supports combat.
- She talks in operational terms such as "loose ends" and "clean", in a low, even, clipped voice. Her report on the Cassalanters' outposts sits in a hidden compartment of Victoro's desk in **Cassalanter Villa**.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Victoro Cassalanter gave her two missions, to retrieve an Eye of the Stone of Golorr from the Brandath Crypts and to silence three cult members who knew too much. She and Arn killed two, and Vaelle Lurval survived. Victoro does not know, and Seffia means to repair the failure without telling him.

**Arn Xalrondar (Lawful Evil, Human, he/him)**
- A cult fanatic who shares the windmill with Seffia, defers to her on all planning and does not draw attention to himself. She speaks to him in half-sentences that he completes.
- Hook: his Book of Worship, found in the Asmodean Shrine on Aveen Street, names him and gives the windmill as the place of assembly. The handprints in the apartment match those at **Cassalanter Villa**.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Arn acts on his own when Seffia is out of the room. He sent the Eye away with a spined devil rather than let the party take it, and he believes the decision was correct.

**Manshoon (Lawful Evil, Human, he/him)**
- A clone of the Zhentarim's founder, a wizard who lost his left arm to Halaster Blackcloak and wears a crafted replacement. The Zhentarim operatives on the city's streets answer to him, and none of them will say so. He leads the Splinter cell, which is **Manshoon's Zhentarim** and not the Doom Raiders.
- Hook: the party has no name for him until **Manshoon Named** is marked for a member.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Manshoon lives in Kolat Towers and lets his simulacrum take the opening volley in a fight. He prefers to capture rather than kill. His location in the towers depends on the escalation tier, and **Kolat Towers** sets it.

## Life in the Ward

**Governing metaphor:** The Southern Ward is a long inn yard, where the wagons roll in, the people who feed and mend them live in the rooms around the yard, and everyone is on the way to somewhere else.

### Culture
- Waterdavians are particular about the name. Dragon Heist says that "it is called the Southern Ward, not the South Ward," and that anyone who insists on the second will be corrected or thought a fool. The wiki adds that by 1491 DR only outsiders used the short form.
- The ward hosts most of the traveling merchants who visit the city. Its enclaves, blocks and streets hold families who trace their ancestry to other realms, and the name comes from the southerners who settled here as the city grew.
- The finest halfling food, the best Calishite singers and the most striking dwarven crafting are all here, and the first challenge is finding where they are housed (Dragon Heist).
- Ward pride rests on overland travel and hard work. The mule on the red and white flag stands for both, and locals call the stretch of streets between the Way of the Dragon and the High Road the Tween Run (wiki, 2e).
- The Moon Sphere draws visitors from across the city. Couples come to dance in it, and sailors extend a hand into the field and ask Selûne to guide them to their next port. The wiki says a priestess from the House of the Moon came to dance and pray there whenever it appeared (14th-century DR sources). The Sea Ward's Tower of the Moon is the table's name for Selûne's temple.
- The ward's halflings gather in three places, the market by the Waymoot, the Yondalla shrine and the rooms above the cooperage, and they keep Dasher Snobeedle's secret from strangers.
- Temples of the ward: Helm's Hall for Helm, from the Dragon Heist Major Temples table, and the Yondalla shrine, which only the campaign places here. Tymora's Tower of Luck is a Castle Ward row in the table, and the Sea Ward text describes it as a Sea Ward temple, so it is not a Southern Ward temple here. Phaulkonmere is an estate and not a temple. No campaign hook for Helm's Hall beyond the Order of the Gauntlet's healing is sourced. [research]

### Defense
- No Watch post, garrison or patrol size is sourced for the ward. [research] The wiki puts a City Guard patrol and a Watchful Order supervisor at the Moon Sphere, and Dragon Heist credits the Watchful Order with the Waymoot signpost.
- The Emerald Enclave's sanctuary is the ward's one defended place. No faction has openly violated Phaulkonmere, and response teams may watch the street and never enter.
- The Doom Raiders keep a safe house here, a boarding house near the ward's market. A stay of up to five consecutive nights is allowed, and the Watch does not know the address (**Dread Lord**).
- In the **Kolat Towers** briefing, Mirt tells a Harper party that Watch coverage in the streets around the towers has thinned over the past eighteen months, and that someone with civic influence arranged it (`arc-i-kolat-towers.md:33`, which writes the Trades Ward).

### Politics
- No ward government or ward leader is sourced. [research]
- Guild halls hold local weight, and the House of Good Spirits and the Road House serve the caravan trade directly. See **The Guilds**.
- The ward's halflings decide how much the Doom Raiders learn. If the party respects Dasher's wishes in **The Missing Snobeedle**, Kelso's people take its word. If it gives up Dasher or fights at the Waymoot, he tells the ward's halflings to close their doors.

### Economy
- The wiki calls this the "poor" ward and says caravan merchants, teamsters and guards fill it for most of the year. Stables, inn yards, counting houses and guildhalls carry the trade.
- Caravan Court counting houses handle payments and manifests, as **Stingblade** and **Fentree's Ledger** show. Nueth's Fine Nets, Pelauvir's Counter and an outlet of Aurora's Emporium are among the shops the wiki lists, and the Old Monster Shop buys and sells unusual creatures.
- Bellister's House, a warehouse, is rumored to take stolen goods (wiki). Rents and prices in the ward are [research].

### Lore
- The wiki says the ward began in 1035 DR as the Temple Ward, one of the four original wards Ahghairon established.
- The Moon Sphere comes from an ancient spell of Selûne. A group of good-aligned lycanthropes, the Cult of the Howling Moon, gathered at the Dancing Court from 390 DR and later became Waterdeep's church of Selûne. Nimoar the Reaver drove them out, and they returned (wiki).
- The Enclave's map of three sewer routes to Undermountain Level 1, from **Autumnreaver**, includes a Southern Ward route. Its entrance is a sealed iron door at the end of a service ledge in the deep cisterns, and the walk takes three hours and ends in an empty room with burned torch stubs and discarded potion bottles.
- In **The Fouled Channel** the party plants Melannor's cutting in a sluice whose current runs down to the water table beneath the Southern Ward.

## Overview

The Southern Ward is Waterdeep's caravan district, a ward of stables, inn yards and guildhalls in the southeast of the city. Most of the traveling merchants who visit the city stay here, among enclaves of families from other realms. Waterdavians insist on the full name, and the ward's mascot is a mule on a red and white flag. Each full moon a field of blue light appears in the Dancing Court, and anyone inside it can fly.

## Read Aloud

Sensory anchors for the GM to build the passage from (not written out):
- A wagon yard: drovers unhitching teams, hay and dust, stables on the ground floor with rooms above them, and merchants haggling over crates.
- The Caravan Court at morning muster, with old men on stools and crates watching the work.
- The Jade Dancer: a jade statue dancing for a crowd, balconies above the Dancing Court, and a ball of blue light on a full-moon night.
- Ruid's Stroll in fog: a hooded shape, a sudden chill and the sense of being passed through.
- Phaulkonmere: an open iron gate between weathered stone posts, a garden thick with old trees, gravel paths bending toward a great oak, and moss and ivy on the walls.
- The Yondalla shrine: a whitewashed cottage, a round green door, a hearth and halflings at the evening blessing.
- The Waymoot: a tall signpost with hanging arrows, hawkers in bright scarves and a halfling market on the paved road.
- Kolat Towers: two dark towers behind an empty street, with light spilling from the windows.
- The decrepit windmill on Coachlamp Lane at dusk.
- The ward's overall look: dusty, loud, crowded and used to strangers.

**Tone:** A busy caravan yard, dusty and a little rough, played straight and warm toward travelers. Keep the Cassalanter windmill, Kolat Towers and Manshoon in the Secret and GM Notes callouts.
