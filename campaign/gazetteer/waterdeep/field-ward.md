> #### Gamemaster's Summary
> - The Field Ward is the slum between Waterdeep's two northernmost walls. Dragon Heist files it under "Outside the City Proper", and it has no Watch patrols, no sewers, no lamplighters and no tax.
> - **Trollskull Alley** lists the Order of the Gauntlet's **Breaking Up the Brawl** in **The Factions Come Calling**, set in a Field Ward market square. Kelso Fiddlewick, who leads the Shard Shunners from this ward, is Emmek Frewn's contractor against the party's tavern, and his crew shows in the alley in **The Neighbors** and **The Twin Parades**.
> - The Trollskull Manor guide adds Handler Sabeen, who runs child pickpockets from the ward, and a staff applicant whose Field Ward reference proves to be a Xanathar's Guild front.

> - Other faction events: Doom Raiders (**The Missing Snobeedle**, with Kelso as the Shunners' leader, and **Dread Lord**, with a hay and feed yard safe house), Emerald Enclave (**The Doppelganger Problem**, which names Kelso, **Summerstrider**, which gives a Field Ward and Undercliff report, and **Winterstalker**, which sketches a crocodile in a shuttered tannery), Harpers (**The Sleeping Asset**, at Three Lanterns House) and Bregan D'aerthe (**Officer**, whose fourth spy is a grain factor's tallyman here).
> - The Cassalanters' Field Ward charity feeds hundreds. The **Cassalanter Villa** structure doc has the Waukeentide Charity Ball raise money for Field Ward reconstruction and draws the Founders' Day feast guests from the ward. The **Sea Maidens Faire** structure doc has the Doom Raiders name a private animal-feed supplier in the ward. The **Faction Outposts** structure doc names the ward once, for the chandler's cellar on Aveen Street.
> - Location journals: none applies, and no mapped site sits in this ward.

**Purpose:** Gives the GM the ward as a place to run: where the Order of the Gauntlet's missions go, who keeps order when the Watch stays out, and which cellar doors open onto the campaign's secrets.

The Field Ward is Waterdeep's slum, an unofficial ward of mud, tanneries and slaughterhouses between the two northernmost walls, outside the city proper and beyond the reach of the Watch.

## At a Glance

- **District Type:** Slum and tannery district. Dragon Heist calls it "not an official ward of the city" that "is commonly referred to as one", and the wiki's own text calls it an unofficial ward (its infobox says City Ward). Dragon Heist files it under "Outside the City Proper", with Undercliff and the Waymoot.
- **Boundaries:** Between the inner and outer walls on the north side of town (wiki). Dragon Heist puts it "between Waterdeep's two northernmost walls". Aveen Street runs along the line between this ward and the **North Ward** (campaign), and Endshift Street meets the Breezeway at the Endshift Tavern (wiki). No source gives street boundaries beyond these. [research]
- **Founded:** No year is given. The ground was a caravan yard "kept free of settlement to serve as a killing field in times of war", and refugees "from various calamities" settled it after being refused entry to the city's wealthy northern neighborhoods (Dragon Heist). The wiki ties the settlement to the Spellplague, which began in 1385 DR.
- **Population:** No source gives a ward figure, and the wiki leaves the field blank. The city figures on the **Castle Ward** page are from 1372 DR, before the refugees the wiki describes, so they say little about this ward (derived).
- **Lifestyle:** Poverty. Dragon Heist: "The area is a muddy mess, populated by the poorest people and those who take advantage of those folks' desperation." The wiki calls it the city's slums, with the poorest living conditions in the city.
- **Services:** None from the city. The ward "has no sewer system and isn't served by the Dungsweepers' Guild" (Dragon Heist), and the Guild of Chandlers and Lamplighters lights the city's streets "excepting the Field Ward and the most dangerous areas of the Dock Ward". Three independent soup houses feed about four hundred people a day, and the Endshift Tavern is the one place Dragon Heist recommends for help. Ammalia Cassalanter's charity feeds hundreds, and the Halls of Justice fund a legal aid office for residents who cannot afford a hearing.
- **Influences:** No source gives colors, a mascot or a race champion for the ward. [research]
- **Primary Peoples:** The wiki lists poor elves, people of mixed blood, servants, homeless squatters and other outcasts. The campaign adds halfling day laborers at the dye works and the halfling wererats of the Shard Shunners.
- **Languages:** The wiki leaves this blank. [research]
- **Religion:** No temple or shrine in the Field Ward appears in any source, and the Dragon Heist Major Temples table has no row for it. No dominant faith is sourced. [research] See **The Deities** for the table.
- **Governance:** No ward government exists. Dragon Heist: "The Watch doesn't patrol this area, however, and many crimes go uninvestigated. The City Guard oversees the Field Ward from the walls around it, but its members get involved only when folk moving into or out of the city are threatened." The wiki repeats that the Guard watched the road between the inner and outer gates. The city taxes and registers visitors at the gates, and the ward sits outside both rules (see Politics under Life in the Ward). The campaign's Order of the Gauntlet events add a watch house that answers reports and does not patrol.
- **Architecture:** Muddy, unpaved lanes and crooked buildings that lean on each other for support. The wiki adds a ward that stinks of slaughterhouses, tanneries and waste. Aveen Street is a narrow cobbled lane between tanneries and tenements. A fire-scorched windmill has been turned into a butcher's shop (Dragon Heist).
- **Economy:** The Guild of Butchers' slaughterhouses, smokehouses and leather works, which the city pushed out of its proper wards. Fences and black-market middlemen such as Kelso Fiddlewick. Day labor, and a few small yards and kitchens.
- **Organizations:** The Guild of Butchers, the Shard Shunners, the City Guard (on the walls and gates), a Watch house, the Order of the Gauntlet (missions and a gate knight), the Cassalanters (a charity and a cellar), Manshoon's Zhentarim (the Splinter, paying gang leaders), Xanathar's Guild (dealers and a front), the Harpers (Three Lanterns House), the Emerald Enclave (watchers), Doom Raiders (a feed yard) and Bregan D'aerthe (a spy).

> #### Secret
> - The Cassalanters use the Field Ward as a source of victims and a cover for their cult. Ammalia's charity picks the one hundred poor guests for the Founders' Day feast on Flamerule 1, where ninety-nine cups of wine carry midnight tears, and the guests die within 24 hours, scattered across the city.
> - Victoro Cassalanter has told a hired agent, Gysheer Omfreys, to keep the Field Ward frightened and the Watch busy elsewhere. ## Landmarks

**Mudlark Square** — Market square
- A market square with grain stalls and a water trough, where locals gather in a ring to watch a quarrel rather than walk away. The Watch does not go in for a street fight.
- Hook: in **Breaking Up the Brawl** four hired men corner a Xanathar's Guild dealer named Pell here at noon. About fifteen locals watch, and the party scatters the mob with Intimidation or by defeating four Thugs.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - A lean, limping man in a brown coat paid the four 5 gp each two days earlier. He works for the Splinter, Manshoon's cell, which has also paid three Field Ward gang leaders to look away when Guild men are hurt. Pell has noticed and knows which leaders took the money. This Pell is a Guild dealer who drinks at the Rusted Anchor in the **Dock Ward**, not the Harper clerk Pell Tormar.

**The Endshift Tavern** — Tavern
- A tavern on the corner of Endshift Street and the Breezeway that never closes, because the people who drink there work in shifts. Tanners in stained aprons, dockhands, hired guards and off-duty Guards share its long tables, and it stands beside the dye works.
- Trevar (Neutral, Illuskan Human, he/him), a heavyset man who is behind the bar at any hour, runs it. He has no Notable Figures page.
- Hook: Dragon Heist sends visitors here for help, since the guards technically owe a visitor safe passage to the city proper. In **The Shard Shunners** a halfling gang robs the night guards, and the Watch plans to put a dozen Guards in the taproom on the sixth night.

**Kelso's Yard** — Dye works yard
- A yard behind the dye works on Endshift Street, hung with sheets of red, blue and mustard wool over steaming vats. Halfling laborers sit on upturned buckets along the wall, and they gather there in the late afternoon.
- Kelso Fiddlewick keeps court here as the leader of the Shard Shunners. His fourteen-year-old sister Danika watches from the drying shed.
- Hook: the party reaches him through Trevar, the cook Wynne Cobb, a tip from Pell, or by asking at the corner stalls. If the party earns **Kelso's Debt**, any Shunner in the ward spends one favor in the Field Ward.

**Twelvedog Court** — Court
- A small cobbled court two streets east of the soup house, with six buildings facing inward and every shutter barred. A baker's shop, a cooper's yard, two empty houses and the old kennel-master's house fill its sides, and a narrow alley runs off the northeast corner.
- Hook: in **Spined Devils in Twelvedog Court** Savra fights beside the party against spined devils on the rooflines, while three residents hide behind an overturned market cart. When the last devil falls, the summoner comes out of the alley with a knife.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - The summoner is Gysheer Omfreys, a Tethyrian woman in her forties and a hired agent of Victoro Cassalanter. A chalk sigil at the alley mouth holds the devils to the court, and wiping it sends them back. Her note carries the initials "V.C.", and her day-book names the Aveen Street cellar. She has no Notable Figures page.

**Orros's Chandlery** — Shop, Aveen Street
- A chandlery on the Field Ward side of Aveen Street, open from the second bell to dusk, with bunches of candles in the window and a bell over the door. Orros (Neutral, Human, he/him), a stooped, tallow-stained man of about sixty, owns the building and has never asked a tenant a question.
- Hook: in **The Shrine on Aveen Street** the party reaches the cellar by Orros's key, by joining a Halls of Justice fire inspection, or by knocking three times at the yard door.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - The cellar is a back way into the Cassalanters' Asmodean shrine on the North Ward side. A charity with no members, the Aveen Street Almsgivers' Society, has leased the cellar and a service passage under the street for six years, and a courier brings the rent each quarter. Orros charges it 3 gp a month and knows nothing about its people.

**Three Lanterns House** — Lodging house, 7 Windborne Way
- A house kept by Sella Brant (Lawful Neutral, Waterdhavian Human, she/her), a practical landlady who dislikes surprises and is home all day.
- Hook: in **The Sleeping Asset** Sella says a halfling lodger called Halla prepaid three months' rent last week, has slept at the house most nights since and asked to be found at the plaza well at four o'clock.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Halla is Corene Wyldath, the Harper Spy whom a devourer from Nihiloor's brood is riding. See **Trades Ward** for the bench and the well.

**Field Ward Soup Houses** — Charity kitchens
- Three independent kitchens feed about four hundred people a day, and all three are underfunded. No source names their keepers or their streets. [research]
- Hook: a cook at one of them overheard the plot against Pell and told Savra, and **The Tithe** lists the kitchens as a cause an Order of the Gauntlet member can fund.

**Field Ward Watch House** — Watch post
- A post staffed by Guards and Watchmen that answers reports and does not patrol. In **Spined Devils in Twelvedog Court** it sends two Guards an hour after the fight to rope off the court, and its sergeant asks no questions of anyone.
- No source gives its street or its commander. [research] See the Defense notes under Life in the Ward for how the campaign's post squares with Dragon Heist.
- Hook: in **The Shard Shunners** it threatens a dozen Guards in the Endshift, and in **Marcheon** a Temple of Helm list records two watch-houses in the ward shuttered after dark for a tenday.

**Tanneries and Slaughterhouses** — Guild of Butchers works
- Dragon Heist says the Guild of Butchers "operates several slaughterhouses, smokehouses, and leather-making facilities" here, noisome trades that the city pushed out of its proper wards. No source places the guildhall itself in the ward.
- Justyn Rassk, the Guild's deliveryman and meat supply inspector in **Trollskull Alley**, is a Field Ward native in Dragon Heist, where he "grew up in the toughest neighborhood in the Field Ward". The campaign files give him no home ward.
- Hook: in **Winterstalker** an Enclave watcher sketches a giant crocodile in the cellar of a shuttered tannery here, resting at the deep end in the cold hours before dawn and feeding at dusk.

**Thrakkus's Butchery** — Converted windmill and butcher's shop (published only)
- Dragon Heist describes an old, fire-scorched windmill that the dragonborn Thrakkus has converted into a butcher's shop. He belongs to the Guild of Butchers, and squatters live in his house. Those who do menial chores are spared and fed scraps, and the rest "end up on the chopping block".
- No campaign file uses Thrakkus or this windmill, and the campaign's Converted Windmill is a different building in the Southern Ward (see Draft Notes).

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Dragon Heist has Zhents loyal to Manshoon pay Thrakkus to chop up the people they kill, and he sells the meat on the sly. The published text has Sidra Romeir deliver the bodies at night in a covered cart. This is the published Winter chain, and no campaign file adopts it.

## Characters of Interest

**Kelso Fiddlewick (Neutral, Lightfoot Halfling wererat, he/him)**
- A compact halfling with a scar along his jaw and the manners of a cheerful undertaker, who leads the Shard Shunners, a small wererat gang. He is the most reliable black-market middleman in the ward and keeps every bargain.
- Emmek Frewn hired him to sabotage Trollskull Manor. In **The Twin Parades** a cracked cask at Frewn's Brews shows the Shunners hit the wrong building.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Kelso is not a Splinter agent. He sells what people pay for, and the Splinter is one of his customers. A Shunners runner carries Edric Tanner's paper to Beldan Rusk's house on Nethpranter Street in **The Doppelganger Problem**, and no player learns who bought the names.
> - He will not discuss his customers or the Shunners' other business, and eight wererats of his gang stay out of sight when he meets strangers.

**Filthy Meg (Neutral, Merfolk, she/her)**
- A heavily tattooed merfolk information broker who works out of Mistshore in the **Dock Ward**. She is independent of every faction, which is what makes her valuable.
- Her homeless network in the Field Ward is her main way of collecting news. They notice who passes through, where people sleep and what disappears, and she keeps them fed and overlooked.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### GM Notes
> - Jarlaxle gave Meg a ring of *true sight* as a retainer and gets harbor intelligence in return, and the Cassalanters have paid her for Field Ward surveillance twice. If her Field Ward people are threatened she does not fight, and she sells the name of whoever is responsible to someone who will act.

**Savra Belabranta (Lawful Good, Human, she/her)**
- Head of the Order of the Gauntlet's Waterdeep chapter, who sends the party into the Field Ward because a knight in the Order's coat turns a quarrel into a message. She fights beside the party in Twelvedog Court, and she cannot walk through the Aveen Street door herself. See the **Castle Ward** page for her office and her history.

## Life in the Ward

**Governing metaphor:** The Field Ward is the city's back lot, where Waterdeep keeps the work and the people it would rather not smell, and where the city's rules stop at the gate.

### Culture
- Residents are the poorest Waterdhavians and the people who profit from their need. Dragon Heist's advice to newcomers is that "being friendly with a burly fellow who is good with a knife is one of your best defenses in the Field Ward."
- Trouble draws a ring of onlookers who watch rather than leave. The Field Ward talks about the Mudlark Square standoff for a tenday afterward, and what it says depends on whether anyone bled.
- The streets smell of cookfire smoke, wet earth, tallow and wet leather, and of the tanneries and slaughterhouses. After dark no lamplighters tend them.
- Dragon Heist notes that you will see less reading material here and in the Dock Ward than elsewhere in the city.
- Dragon Heist calls the ward the city's most dangerous district, a title it took from the Dock Ward.
- No temple or shrine is sourced. The Major Temples table has no Field Ward row, and Tymora's Tower of Luck and Helm's Hall are not Field Ward temples. Draft Notes holds the conflicts.

### Defense
- Dragon Heist: "The Watch doesn't patrol this area, however, and many crimes go uninvestigated. The City Guard oversees the Field Ward from the walls around it, but its members get involved only when folk moving into or out of the city are threatened." Of the Guard in general, the book says it patrols the roads, watches the walls, guards civic structures and protects magisters, while "Waterdeep's streets, however, are policed by an altogether different force: the City Watch."
- A visitor who needs help can try the Endshift Tavern. Dragon Heist warns that "the guards might not be inclined to assist you" but that "your status as a visitor to Waterdeep technically obliges them to help you reach the city proper in safety."
- The wiki says the Guard watched the road between the inner and outer gates to protect those passing through. An Order of the Gauntlet knight, Sir Merrick Tollwood, is on duty at the Field Ward gate in **Vindicator**.
- The campaign also has a Watch house in the ward. This page keeps Dragon Heist's rule as the base: the Watch does not patrol, and the house sends Guards only after a report or for a planned operation. Savra says the Watch will not go into Mudlark Square for a street fight.
- The Shard Shunners and the other gangs keep the peace in practice. Any Shunner spends **Kelso's Debt** for a party that holds it.
- Dragon Heist describes ordinary Watch posts as having a green and gold lantern outside, lit by *continual flame*. No source says whether the Field Ward post has one.

### Politics
- The ward has no government and no officer. Dragon Heist says both it and Undercliff "have yet to be formally accepted as wards of the city". Who would accept it, and when, is [research]. See **The Open Lord and the Masked Lords**.
- Because they are not official wards, they "aren't subject to taxation", but they also "don't benefit from the securities of Guild Law or the protection of the Watch."
- The city reaches travelers at the gates, where a visitor in a large caravan registers with a magister, a black-robed official with a City Guard escort who "can pass a sentence without a trial". Registration brings monthly taxation. See **The Code Legal and the Magisters**.
- The Order of the Gauntlet fills part of the gap. Tyr's clergy argue for Field Ward residents who cannot afford a hearing before the Lords, through the Halls of Justice's Legal Aid Fund, and Savra wants the ward to see that someone is paying attention.
- Which guilds hold weight with the ward's gangs is [research]. See **The Guilds**.

### Economy
- The Guild of Butchers' works and the tanneries are the ward's large employers. Rents and prices are [research].
- Fences and middlemen run the black market. In **The Doppelganger Problem** three payments of 5 gp trace from a Field Ward fence's till to Edric Tanner.
- The ward sends its servants across the city. Dragon Heist says the Cassalanter Villa's household staff live "in dingy homes in the Dock Ward or the Field Ward" and arrive before the family wakes. The same text has the Founders' Day pavilions at the villa fill with "one hundred of Waterdeep's poorest residents", most of them brought from the Field Ward.
- Ammalia's charity feeds hundreds, and the Waukeentide Charity Ball on Tarsakh 7 raises money for Field Ward reconstruction. See **Waukeen**.
- Smaller trades include a hay and feed yard at the ward's edge, a grain factor whose tallyman is a Bregan D'aerthe spy, and a private animal-feed supplier to the Faire.

### Lore
- Dragon Heist: "This district was once a caravan yard between Waterdeep's two northernmost walls, kept free of settlement to serve as a killing field in times of war." Refugees settled it, and "the area has grown up into a lawless town of its own."
- The wiki places the settlement after the Spellplague of 1385 DR and calls the ward the last destination for the city's refugees.
- Dragon Heist calls the neighboring Undercliff, east of the city, a rural community of farms and inns that caters to travelers. It holds a large Guard training camp and a Watch prison farm called Amendsfarm, where those convicted of minor offenses work off their debt to the city. The Field Ward has no equivalent.
- Aveen Street marks the line between the Field Ward and the North Ward. The tanneries and tenements flank it, and the Cassalanters' shrine lies on the far side.

## Overview

The Field Ward is the poorest district of Waterdeep, a crowded slum between the city's two northernmost walls. It is not an official ward, so the Watch does not patrol it and its residents pay no city tax. Slaughterhouses and tanneries pushed out of the city fill its muddy, unpaved streets, and it has no sewers. Visitors are advised to pass through from one gate to the next.

## Read Aloud

Sensory anchors for the GM to build the passage from (not written out):
- The smell first: cookfire smoke, wet earth, tallow, wet leather and the sour reek of tanneries and slaughterhouses.
- Unpaved lanes of crooked buildings that lean on each other for support, with mud underfoot.
- Mudlark Square at noon: a ring of locals around a grain stall, a large brown dog barking beside the trough, and the voices of a crowd that has decided to watch.
- The Endshift Tavern: loud and warm, wet wool and fried onions, tanners in stained aprons beside dockhands, two men with Watch cudgels on their belts, and Trevar wiping the same mug twice.
- The dye works yard: sheets of red, blue and mustard wool hung up, steaming vats and halfling laborers on upturned buckets.
- Twelvedog Court: six buildings facing inward with barred shutters, an overturned cart with spines in its boards, and small winged shapes on the rooflines.
- Aveen Street: a narrow cobbled lane between tanneries and tenements, bunches of candles in a chandler's window and a bell over the door.
- Streets without lamplighters at night, and no Watch lantern in sight.
- The ward's overall look: muddy, crowded, foul-smelling and watchful.

**Tone:** A muddy, lawless slum played straight and with sympathy for the people in it, where the Watch stays out and neighbors settle things among themselves. Keep the Cassalanter and Splinter threads in the Secret and GM Notes callouts.
