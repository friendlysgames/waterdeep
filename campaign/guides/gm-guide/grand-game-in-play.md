### The Grand Game in Play

**Purpose:** Gives the DM a practical faction reference — what each faction knows and is doing at campaign start, how they escalate, and how they interact with each other independent of the PCs.

**Content:**

*Starting Faction Knowledge (campaign day one)*

What each villain faction knows before the PCs do anything:

- **Xanathar's Guild:** Knows Dalakhar was moving the Stone; knows the Stone is now in circulation; does not know about the Eyes or the vault's location; is reacting to the loss of expected income rather than pursuing the gold directly; Nar'l Xibrindas's intelligence has been selectively filtered by Jarlaxle
- **Manshoon's Splinter Cell:** Knows the Stone exists and what it unlocks; Urstul Floxin's team has been tracking Dalakhar; does not yet know the Stone reached the Gralhunds; Kolat Towers is established and operational
- **The Cassalanters:** Know exactly what they need and why (the vault gold pays the debt); have been planning the Founders' Day timeline for eight years; do not yet know the Stone is in play; their outpost network is focused on soul coin acquisition, not the Stone
- **Bregan D'aerthe:** Nar'l Xibrindas has fed Jarlaxle accurate intelligence about the Stone and the Eyes since before the campaign begins; Jarlaxle has the most complete picture of any faction; the Sea Maidens Faire is in port and operational; he is waiting to see which party proves most useful

*Escalation Tiers*

How each villain faction's posture toward the PCs shifts across four tiers.

| Tier | Faction posture | Trigger | What the DM does |
|------|-----------------|---------|------------------|
| 1 — Unaware | Faction doesn't know the PCs exist as a threat; normal operations only | Campaign start | Background activity only |
| 2 — Suspicious | Faction has noticed unexplained losses; observation and investigation under way | PCs appear at a faction-adjacent scene | Intelligence assets begin tracking; no action taken |
| 3 — Alert | Faction knows the PCs are active; response teams deployed | PCs hit a faction asset or acquire something faction wants | Response team deployed; clue-seeding props in play |
| 4 — Lockdown | Full security posture, lair defenses hardened; direct confrontation and full mobilization | PCs have cost the faction significantly, hold an Eye, broken an agreement, or blocked a core goal | Lieutenant takes personal interest; job offer OR assassination attempt depending on faction; full mobilization; faction leader attention; Arc-level consequences |

Each faction reaches these tiers at different paces. Xanathar escalates faster and more irrationally. Jarlaxle almost never reaches Lockdown without choosing to. The Cassalanters prefer Alert indefinitely — the less visible their pursuit, the better.

*Inter-Faction Conflict (independent of PCs)*

The Grand Game does not pause for the PCs. Between sessions, the factions act on their own interests:

- Manshoon and Xanathar's Guild: active street-level conflict in the Dock Ward; Manshoon's operatives are probing Guild territory; Xanathar has issued a kill order on Manshoon (failed twice so far)
- Jarlaxle and Xanathar: managed tension; Nar'l keeps Xanathar occupied with curated intelligence; Jarlaxle's goal is a predictable beholder, not a dead one
- Jarlaxle and Manshoon: fragile non-interference; each considers the other the only genuine peer in the game; neither wants direct conflict because both would rather the other faction absorb the other factions' attention
- The Cassalanters and everyone else: largely parallel track; they are not fighting the other factions for the Stone; they intend to wait until the Stone is activated and the vault location revealed, then move on the vault directly; their outpost activity is soul-coin focused, not Stone-focused

When a PC action weakens one faction significantly, the other factions respond — not because they know what the PCs did, but because they notice the suddenly quieter territory or the absence of a known operative. The DM should play this organically: faction activity fills vacuums.

*When Factions Back Down*

Not every escalation resolves through combat or confrontation. Guidance on:
- When a faction withdraws from a conflict it is losing (Doom Raiders will almost always negotiate; Cassalanters retreat to legal/social pressure; Xanathar rarely backs down once engaged)
- What a faction wants in exchange for standing down (Doom Raiders: information; Manshoon: an asset or a demonstration of usefulness; Cassalanters: discretion; Bregan D'aerthe: the political favor they've always wanted)
- What a faction remembers (all of them; Xanathar especially; grudges held by Xanathar are permanent unless expunged through an act that satisfies the beholder's ego)

**Tone:** Operational. This is how a DM runs a living city between sessions without needing to improvise faction behavior from scratch every week.
