# The Grand Game in Play

The Grand Game doesn't wait. While the party rests, the factions are moving: issuing orders, reading intelligence, replenishing losses, and adjusting to whatever the PCs did last session. Between sessions, use this routine to keep four active villain factions coherent without rebuilding them from scratch each week. For the reference tables (faction knowledge at campaign start, the full escalation tier table, and the inter-faction operations matrix), see **Running the Villains**.

## The Weekly Faction Check

Running four simultaneous factions is less complicated than it sounds, because only one or two are visible in any given session. The others are working in the background. Between sessions, spend five minutes asking three questions about each faction:

1. What do they know right now?
2. What are they doing about it?
3. When does that action become visible to the PCs?

Write a concrete, timestamped list. "Gorath's duergar will arrive at Trollskull Manor on Tuesday at noon. Soluun Xibrindas will attempt to ambush whoever leaves the manor after midnight on Wednesday." Keep the list on your GM screen. When the PCs act, check it and adjust.

The factions know things the PCs don't. Response teams, surveillance operations, and intelligence networks run in the background throughout the campaign. When PCs find documents in a faction outpost, they're reading real intelligence that has already shaped the faction's next move. The faction is playing an active game.

Play factions like thinking opponents, not obstacles. Each one has goals, pursues them methodically, and reacts to setbacks. A faction loses interest in the PCs the moment the PCs stop mattering to its plans. Some factions will try to kill the party, but only because the PCs represent a specific threat to a specific goal.

> [!abstract]**The Weekly Check at a Glance**
> **Three questions per faction:**
> 1. What do they know right now?
> 2. What are they doing about it?
> 3. When does that become visible to the PCs?
>
> **Four things to track per faction:**
> - Current knowledge of PC identities and capabilities
> - Response status: team deployed? leadership notified? which escalation tier?
> - What the faction is pursuing this week, independent of the PCs
> - Whether the faction's Grand Game position has shifted (Eye acquired or lost, operative compromised, alliance changed)

## Faction State Tracking

After each session, update a short faction record for each villain faction. Four items per faction.

**Current knowledge of PC identities and capabilities.** Write down what each faction knows about the party: names, descriptions, and demonstrated ability. If the PCs used a disguise during a heist, note whether the disguise held. If a faction captured someone, record what they extracted.

**Response status.** Record which escalation tier each faction currently occupies and what would push it to the next one. Note whether a response team has been deployed and whether the PC threat has reached faction leadership.

**Independent weekly activity.** Write one sentence describing what the faction is doing this week regardless of PC involvement. This keeps factions present in the narrative even when the party is focused elsewhere.

**Grand Game position.** Record any shift in the faction's standing: an Eye acquired or lost, a key operative killed or compromised, an alliance formed or broken, a safe house burned.

Inter-faction activity continues off-screen. If the party spends three sessions pursuing Cassalanter outposts, Xanathar and Manshoon haven't been idle. Keep a one-line answer ready for what each faction did during that time. When PCs canvass contacts, read Watch reports, or press informants, they may turn up traces of that off-screen activity. The world feels inhabited rather than scripted when the factions are visibly busy with their own agendas.

## Escalation Pace

Each faction moves through four tiers: Unaware, Suspicious, Alert, and Lockdown. The tier table and faction-specific trigger lists live in **Running the Villains**. What matters here is how each faction moves through those tiers in practice, because the pace and texture differ significantly.

**Xanathar** escalates faster than any other faction and less rationally. His paranoia means a single unexplained loss can push him from Suspicious to Alert without any intermediate step. Anything touching Sylgar, his goldfish, bypasses the tier sequence and produces Lockdown behavior immediately. When Xanathar reaches Alert or Lockdown, plan for overreaction: more force than the situation warrants, a readiness to burn assets to make a point.

**Manshoon** underestimates threats that aren't magical. A party that avoids Kolat Towers and doesn't advertise itself through arcane channels can operate at Suspicious tier for multiple sessions before he escalates. His preference for capturing over killing creates tactical openings, since a confrontation often produces a job offer before a fight.

**The Cassalanters** are largely binary. In passive mode they barely react; their outpost network is focused on soul coin acquisition, not the PCs. The switch to hostile mode happens when the PCs are identified as a threat to the soul-pact plan, and they move directly to Willifort Crowelle's full operation. They prefer Alert to Lockdown because the less visible their pursuit, the better their political position. When they do escalate to Lockdown, they exhaust city leverage first: Watch complaints, noble pressure, guild sanctions. Infernal force is the last resort.

**Jarlaxle** almost never reaches Lockdown without choosing to. His default response is leverage and observation. Direct confrontation comes only when the PCs have killed his lieutenants without first offering terms, and even then he opens with an offer.

## When the Factions Aren't Watching the PCs

For most of the campaign, the factions are primarily watching each other. Their competition over Neverember's Enigma predates the PCs' arrival and would continue without them.

Manshoon's Zhentarim and Xanathar's Guild are in active conflict at street level in the Dock Ward. Manshoon's operatives probe Guild territory regularly; Xanathar has issued a kill order on Manshoon that has failed twice. This is a grinding low-level engagement that produces casualties on both sides and keeps both factions consuming resources on a front entirely separate from the PCs.

Jarlaxle's relationship with Xanathar is more managed. Nar'l Xibrindas, Xanathar's own senior advisor, reports to Jarlaxle by *Sending* spell. The intelligence Nar'l provides has been selectively curated to keep the beholder occupied and predictable; Jarlaxle's goal is a manageable Xanathar, not a dead one. Xanathar suspects a mole in his organization and has been hunting in the wrong direction. He has not identified Nar'l.

Jarlaxle and Manshoon maintain a fragile non-interference arrangement. Each considers the other the only genuine peer in the Grand Game, and neither wants direct conflict while other factions are more immediate problems. The arrangement is under strain: Manshoon has identified Fenerus Stormcastle as a Bregan D'aerthe cut-out and ordered his abduction. If that abduction proceeds, Jarlaxle will act unilaterally, and the non-interference collapses.

The Cassalanters run a parallel track, largely independent of the others. They aren't competing with the other factions for the Stone; they intend to wait until someone activates it and reveals the vault's location, then move on the vault directly. Their outpost network is focused on soul coin acquisition. Cassalanter imps conduct ongoing surveillance of the Sea Maidens Faire, but Jarlaxle's counter-intelligence hasn't detected it yet.

Factions fill vacuums. When the PCs weaken one faction significantly, killing a response team, burning an outpost, or compromising an operative, the others notice the suddenly quieter territory or the absence of a known contact. They don't know what the PCs did. They know that a piece of the board has gone quiet, and thinking factions move on opportunity. For the specific active operations and the documents that cross faction lines, see the inter-faction operations matrix in **Running the Villains**.

> [!design]**Why Factions Fill Vacuums**
> This is the Grand Game's self-reinforcing pressure. If the PCs dismantle Xanathar's Guild outposts, Manshoon's Zhentarim moves into the vacated Dock Ward territory. If the PCs weaken Manshoon, Xanathar's paranoia briefly lifts and he turns outward. The PCs' choices produce real chain reactions because thinking factions respond to changing conditions. The inter-faction operations matrix in **Running the Villains** shows where each faction's active operations overlap and which documents cross faction lines; consult it whenever the party clears territory a rival faction would want.

## When Factions Back Down

Escalation rarely ends in a stand-up fight. Most factions have exits from direct conflict, and knowing when each one takes those exits is as important as knowing how they escalate.

**The Doom Raiders** are a player-joinable faction, not a villain faction. If the party has turned them hostile, Davil Starsong will negotiate the moment he sees a path to de-escalation. What he wants is information: something useful about the party's other activities or contacts. A working relationship can be bought back with the right currency. Turning the Doom Raiders hostile closes the Zhentarim renown track and the cell's contact network; restoring goodwill reopens them.

**Manshoon** can be satisfied in the short term by a demonstration of usefulness or the delivery of an asset he values. His preference for capturing over killing means a direct confrontation often produces a job offer before violence, at least until the party has cost him too much. Manshoon holds debts. A party that delivers something useful will find him willing to deal again.

**The Cassalanters** retreat to social and legal pressure before infernal force. Their first response to a party that has become a problem is Watch complaints, noble pressure, guild sanctions, and quiet damage to the PCs' reputation in the city. What they want for standing down is discretion: a party that will stay out of their affairs and never speak of the soul pact. Ammalia Cassalanter is more comfortable wielding Waterdeep's social machinery than facing direct confrontation. What they remember is anyone who has learned too much.

**Bregan D'aerthe** reaches for leverage before force in almost every circumstance. What Jarlaxle wants is the political favor he has pursued for years: Lords' Alliance membership for Luskan. Direct confrontation comes only when the PCs have killed his lieutenants without offering terms. Jarlaxle holds debts and opportunities. A party that has crossed him simply moves to a different column in his ledger.

**Xanathar** rarely backs down once engaged. He takes opposition personally, his grudges are permanent, and he has no diplomatic instincts. Standing down is possible only if the PCs perform an act that satisfies his ego: a gesture of submission, a gift of intelligence about one of his enemies, a public humiliation of someone he hates. The act must be specific and visible to him. Quiet de-escalation doesn't register.
