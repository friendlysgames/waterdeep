### Structural Rules

**Purpose:** Establishes the recurring mechanical systems that operate throughout the campaign — invoked constantly, not explained in individual arcs.

**Content:**

*The Two Zhentarims*

The campaign contains two distinct Zhentarim factions that must never be conflated:

- **The Doom Raiders** (Davil Starsong's cell): a player-joinable faction; presents a public face of legitimate business and moderate criminal enterprise; will not cooperate with Manshoon and does not acknowledge his cell as legitimate Zhentarim; found in Trollskull Alley and across the city as apparent professionals
- **Manshoon's Splinter Cell**: the villain faction; operates from Kolat Towers; the "Zhentarim" that conducts assassinations and uses mind-controlled servants; Manshoon views the Doom Raiders as deserters who stole his name

When PCs encounter "the Zhentarim" in any given scene, the DM must know which cell is present. These factions will not cooperate. They will actively undermine each other if their operations overlap. The distinction should become apparent to attentive PCs during **Trollskull Alley**.

*Faction Response Teams*

Each villain faction maintains mobile squads — small groups (2–4 members) deployed reactively when the PCs hit faction assets. Cover:
- Composition: what a typical response team looks like for each faction (personality, tactics, escalation threshold)
- Deployment triggers: what activates a response team (PC incursion into a faction asset, faction surveillance flagging PC activity, orders from a faction lieutenant)
- Clue-seeding props: every response team carries intelligence items (documents, coded messages, maps) that reward PCs who search or interrogate rather than simply defeat; each item connects to a different investigation thread
- Escalation: hitting one faction's assets does not automatically alert others; factions do not share intelligence freely; but PC actions that become visible citywide (Watch involvement, public confrontation) may reach multiple factions

Faction-specific team personalities:
- **Xanathar Guild**: brute force and gazer scouts; paranoid overreaction; will call for reinforcements faster than necessary
- **Manshoon's Zhentarim**: professional mercenary tactics; more willing to negotiate mid-fight; always more members waiting nearby than visible
- **Doom Raiders** (if turned hostile): streetwise ambushes; leverage over PC contacts; prefer cutting deals to prolonged conflict
- **Bregan D'aerthe**: disguises and misdirection; non-lethal containment preferred; will not commit to direct conflict in the open city
- **Cassalanters**: hired muscle with devil backup on the lower temple level; social and legal leverage as a first response (threats of Watch involvement, reputation damage)

*Festival Calendar*

The Waterdeep festival calendar is a structural device, not flavor. Festivals provide social cover for heist preparation, create timing pressure, and anchor the Grand Game to specific dates. The DM should establish the campaign's starting date and track the calendar actively.

Key dates:

| Festival / Date | Date | Structural function |
|----------|------|---------------------|
| Fleetswake | Ches 21–30 | Maritime festival; Sea Maidens Faire arrives in harbor; cover for **Sea Maidens Faire** reconnaissance |
| Twin Parades | Ches 21 | Opening day of Fleetswake; nimblewright sighting in the procession (**Trollskull Alley** ev-07); fireball strikes the following morning |
| The Fireball | Ches 22 | Campaign inciting incident; opens **Fireball!**; the morning after the Twin Parades |
| Waukeentide | Tarsakh 1–10 | Trade festival; Cassalanter social events peak; cover for **Cassalanter Villa** surveillance phase |
| Founders' Day | Flamerule 1 | HARD DEADLINE: Cassalanter sacrifice window closes; if PCs have not resolved the children's situation, the sacrifice occurs |

The DM should decide the campaign start date (Alturiak or early Ches is typical, leaving two months before Fleetswake) and maintain a session-by-session calendar. Players who are paying attention should be able to track the approach of Founders' Day without being told what it means until they understand the Cassalanters.

*Three Clue Rule in Practice*

Every investigation beat in this campaign follows the Three Clue Rule: at least three independent clue paths lead to every conclusion the PCs need to reach. No single failed roll, missed NPC, or skipped location should derail the investigation.

How to apply it:
- **Active clues**: require a roll, a decision, or a successful interaction to discover
- **Passive clues**: arrive without PC effort (NPCs volunteer information, something is visible on entry, an ally passes word)
- **Deduction clues**: available to PCs who combine two pieces of existing information without needing a new discovery

Every critical investigation beat should have at least one of each type. If all three paths require successful rolls, the rule is technically met but practically fragile.

Critical beats to audit (one table per beat, verification in **Running the Villains** (GM Guide) revelation lists):
- Finding Floon
- Identifying the nimblewright and its owner (**Fireball!**)
- Connecting the fireball to the Gralhunds (**Fireball!**)
- Locating each Eye of the Stone (**Faction Outposts**)
- Finding each villain faction's lair (**Faction Outposts**)
- Opening the Vault of Dragons

*The Fireball Victim*

The fireball that opens **Fireball!** must kill or destroy a named NPC the players bonded with during **Trollskull Alley**. This is the DM's active observation task throughout **Trollskull Alley** — not a decision made at session zero, but a read made at the table.

How to run it:
- During **Trollskull Alley**, watch for which NPC the group gravitates toward. Not necessarily the one with the most page time — the one the players ask about, reference between sessions, return to.
- Identify the victim before **Fireball!** begins. Write them into the fireball scene. Do not leave it vague.
- The scene should not be survivable-with-a-heal. The victim is dead or, in Lif's case, gone — the campaign's first irreversible consequence.

Suggested candidates (not prescriptive — the actual victim is whoever the players chose):

| Candidate | Why it works |
|-----------|-------------|
| Fala Lefaliir (herbalist) | The most likely bond for groups who frequent their shop; gentle and memorable; death feels like losing a neighborhood |
| Lif the poltergeist (Trollskull Manor ghost) | Non-lethal in the conventional sense but the manor's personality disappears; works best if the group anthropomorphized Lif heavily |
| A PC's backstory NPC | Maximum personal stakes; requires session zero coordination to establish the NPC exists in Waterdeep; must be a named character with at least one scene |

One rule: the victim cannot be a PC. One firm suggestion: the victim should not be someone the DM found narratively convenient to remove. The players need to feel the loss. A DM who picked the victim for their own reasons made a different scene with a different result.

*Faction State Tracking*

Between sessions, the DM should update a simple faction state record for each villain faction. A brief checklist is enough. For each faction, track:
- Current knowledge of PC identities and capabilities
- Whether PC actions have triggered a response (response team deployed? direct leadership notified?)
- What the faction is actively pursuing this week independent of PCs
- Whether the faction's position in the Grand Game has shifted (Eye acquired or lost, ally gained or turned, key operative compromised)

Inter-faction activity continues off-screen. If the PCs spend three sessions on Cassalanter outposts, Xanathar and Manshoon have not been idle. The DM should have a rough answer for what each faction did during that time if the PCs investigate.

**Tone:** Technical and precise. Consult these during session prep; they're not table-talk. Write for quick reference, not extended reading.
