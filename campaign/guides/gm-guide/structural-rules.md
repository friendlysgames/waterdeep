# Structural Rules

## The Two Zhentarims

The campaign contains two distinct Zhentarim factions. Conflating them is a preparation error with real consequences.

**The Doom Raiders** are Davil Starsong's cell — a player-joinable faction that presents a public face of legitimate business and moderate criminal enterprise. They operate openly, hold meetings in taverns, and recruit adventurers. They don't acknowledge Manshoon's cell as legitimate Zhentarim, and they surface during **Trollskull Alley** as the first Zhentarim contact the party encounters.

**Manshoon's Zhentarim** are the villain faction. They operate from Kolat Towers, conduct assassinations, and maintain mind-controlled servants. Manshoon regards the Doom Raiders as deserters who stole his name.

These two cells won't cooperate. If their operations overlap, they actively undermine each other. When a scene involves "the Zhentarim," know which cell is present. The distinction should become apparent to attentive players during **Trollskull Alley** — earlier encounters with the Doom Raiders' professionalism make Manshoon's operatives land harder when they appear.

> [!warning]**Two Zhentarims, Never One**
> Every scene involving a "Zhentarim" NPC requires knowing which cell is present. The Doom Raiders and Manshoon's Zhentarim won't coordinate or share intelligence. If their operations run parallel, they'll try to disrupt each other. Davil Starsong's renown mechanic is entirely separate from Manshoon's faction state. Track them separately and never conflate the two when reading attunement flags.

## Faction Response Teams

Each villain faction maintains mobile squads — groups of 2–4 members deployed when the party hits faction assets. Response teams are reactive, not proactive: they investigate intrusions, follow up on flagged activity, and act on orders from faction lieutenants.

**Deployment triggers.** A response team activates when the party intrudes on a faction asset, when faction surveillance flags their activity, or when a lieutenant issues direct orders after receiving a report. These triggers are independent. One faction's response doesn't automatically alert others; factions don't share intelligence freely. Party actions that become visible citywide — Watch involvement, a public confrontation on a main thoroughfare — may reach several factions simultaneously, but covert intrusions rarely do.

**Clue-seeding.** Every response team carries intelligence props: a coded letter, a partial map, a faction token, a list of names. These items reward parties who search bodies or question captives rather than simply winning the fight. Each item connects to a different investigation thread. When preparing a response team encounter, assign at least two props and know which thread each one feeds.

Faction state tracking runs between sessions; the full procedure is in [The Grand Game in Play](grand-game-in-play.md).

**Team personalities.** Full rosters are on the organization pages ([Bregan D'aerthe](../../setting/organizations/07-bregan-daerthe.md), [Xanathar's Guild](../../setting/organizations/08-xanathars-guild.md), [Manshoon's Zhentarim](../../setting/organizations/09-manshoons-zhentarim.md), [Cassalanters](../../setting/organizations/10-cassalanters.md)). Their tactical characters at the table:

- **Xanathar's Guild** — brute force with gazer scouts on point; paranoid overreaction to any threat; calls for reinforcements faster than necessary.
- **Manshoon's Zhentarim** — professional mercenary tactics; more willing to negotiate mid-fight than any other faction; there are always more members nearby than visible.
- **Doom Raiders** (if turned hostile) — streetwise ambushes; leverage over PC contacts; prefer cutting deals to prolonged conflict.
- **Bregan D'aerthe** — disguises and misdirection; non-lethal containment preferred; won't commit to direct conflict in open city streets.
- **Cassalanters** — hired muscle with devil backup available on the lower temple level; social and legal leverage as a first response, including threats of Watch involvement and reputation damage.

## The Campaign Calendar

The festival calendar is structural, not flavor. Festivals provide social cover for heist preparation, create timing pressure, and anchor the Grand Game to specific dates. Track it actively.

The campaign begins on **Ches 1**, when the party arrives at the Yawning Portal and **Finding Floon** opens. Floon was kidnapped the night before.

| Date | Event | Structural Function |
|---|---|---|
| Ches 1 | Campaign opens; **Finding Floon** begins | Party arrives; Floon was taken the night before |
| Ches 20 | Grand Opening of Trollskull Manor (**Trollskull Alley** ev-06) | Renovation complete; the tavern is open for business |
| Ches 21–30 | Fleetswake | Maritime festival; Sea Maidens Faire fleet in harbor; cover for Sea Maidens Faire reconnaissance |
| Ches 21 | Twin Parades (Fleetswake day 1) | Nimblewright sighting; **Trollskull Alley** ends |
| Ches 22 | Fireball, first light | **Fireball!** opens; the morning after the Twin Parades |
| Ches 24 | Urstul Floxin strikes Gralhund Villa | **Gralhund Villa** opens |
| ~Ches 25 | Stone attunement | **Faction Outposts** begins |
| Tarsakh 1–10 | Waukeentide | Trade festival; Cassalanter social events peak; cover for Cassalanter Villa surveillance |
| Tarsakh 20 | Sea Maidens Faire departs at dawn | Eye #3 leaves Waterdeep; alliance path with Jarlaxle closes; **Sea Maidens Faire** must be completed before this date |
| Flamerule 1 | Founders' Day | **Hard deadline:** Cassalanter charity feast; 99 cups of midnight tears served; guests die within 24 hours; stop the feast to stop the sacrifice |
| Flamerule 11 | Cassalanter twins' ninth birthday | Contract deadline: 999,999 gp and 99 souls due to Asmodeus; the twins are forfeit if the terms go unmet or unbroken |

Three dates demand close attention.

**Tarsakh 20** closes the Sea Maidens Faire heist and alliance paths entirely. Jarlaxle's ships leave on schedule; no delay is possible.

**Flamerule 1** is the sacrifice window. The Cassalanters host a charity dinner at Cassalanter Villa; 99 guests receive cups laced with midnight tears and die within 24 hours. The party must stop the feast to prevent the sacrifice. Arriving after the dinner ends changes nothing.

**Flamerule 11** follows ten days later — the contract's due date. Parties who destroyed the contract during **Cassalanter Villa** don't face this deadline. Those who didn't have ten days after Founders' Day to find another resolution.

Attentive players can track Founders' Day approaching without being told what it means. Calendar awareness is a player resource; the connection to the Cassalanters is a discovery they earn.

> [!info]**Founders' Day vs. the Twins' Birthday**
> Two deadlines, ten days apart. Founders' Day (Flamerule 1) is the feast, where ninety-nine guests are poisoned; it is the party's real deadline. The twins' ninth birthday (Flamerule 11) is when the contract comes due. A party that stopped the feast still has those ten days to settle the twins' fate through one of the **Cassalanter Villa** resolutions.

## The Three Clue Rule in Practice

Every investigation beat follows the Three Clue Rule: at least three independent paths lead to every conclusion the party needs to reach. No single failed roll, missed NPC, or skipped location should derail the investigation.

Three clue types address different failure modes:

- **Active clues** require a roll, a decision, or a successful interaction. They're the most common and the most fragile; a single bad roll cuts the path.
- **Passive clues** arrive without party effort: an NPC volunteers information, something is visible on entry, an ally sends word unsolicited. These never fail.
- **Deduction clues** are available to parties who combine two pieces of existing information without a new discovery. No roll required — only the two prior pieces.

Every critical beat needs at least one of each type. Three active clues technically satisfies the rule but fails it in practice: a run of bad rolls leaves the party with nothing. The goal is three independent paths, not three rolls.

Critical beats to audit:

| Beat | Quest |
|---|---|
| Finding Floon | **Finding Floon** |
| Identifying the nimblewright and its owner | **Fireball!** |
| Connecting the fireball to the Gralhunds | **Fireball!** |
| Locating each Eye of the Stone | **Faction Outposts** |
| Finding each villain faction's lair | **Faction Outposts** |
| Opening the Vault of Dragons | **Vault of Dragons** |

Verification lives in the Revelation Lists on the villain organization pages ([Bregan D'aerthe](../../setting/organizations/07-bregan-daerthe.md), [Xanathar's Guild](../../setting/organizations/08-xanathars-guild.md), [Cassalanters](../../setting/organizations/10-cassalanters.md)).

## The Fireball Victim

The fireball that opens **Fireball!** kills a named NPC the party bonded with during **Trollskull Alley**. Observation begins in ev-03, when the party first meets their neighbors, and runs through the rest of the quest. The victim must be designated before **The Twin Parades** (ev-07) ends.

The players tell you who it is. They're the ones asking follow-up questions about a particular NPC, returning to their shop, referencing them between sessions, mentioning their name when it doesn't come up organically. Page time doesn't determine the victim. Attention does. Your job through Trollskull Alley is to watch for it.

Before ev-07 ends, write the chosen NPC's name in your session notes and record the attunement **Fireball Victim Designated: [NPC Name]**. **Fireball!** reads this attunement in its opening beat.

| Candidate | Introduced | Loss Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Fala Lefaliir (they/them) | ev-03 | Ziraj's shelter gone; the Doom Raiders thread cut. If Fala dies, Tally replaces them as the eyewitness in **Fireball!** ev-01. |
| Tally Fellbranch (he/him) | ev-03 | Connected to Renaer and Melannor; his death damages two faction relationships |
| Floon Blagmaar | ev-03, ev-05 | The party rescued him in **Finding Floon**; losing him a second time carries weight it didn't before |
| Lif | ev-01 (if appeased) | His second death is the manor's spirit going with the neighborhood |
| A hired staff member | ev-06 | Only works if the party visibly bonded with a specific candidate during hiring |
| A PC backstory NPC | Setup-dependent | Requires a Waterdeep backstory connection established and activated during Trollskull Alley |

Three rules hold. The victim can't be a PC. The victim isn't chosen for your narrative convenience — a fireball that removes someone you wanted gone is a plot move, not a consequence. And the scene isn't survivable with a healing spell. A death that can be reversed is a near-miss, not a loss. The campaign's first irreversible consequence has to feel irreversible.
