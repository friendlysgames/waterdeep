# Adventure Summary

Four acts, 29 to 51 sessions, 1st through 7th or 8th level. The campaign is built as a genuine heist story: a city mystery in Acts I and II, planned operations in Act III, and a convergence in Act IV that collects on everything the party set in motion. The acts shift meaningfully in tone and structure, from neighborhood life through investigation to high-stakes planning to reckoning. Read the whole shape before pitching this to players.

## The Four Acts

| Act | Title | Levels | Quests | Est. Sessions | Dramatic Question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | The Setup | 1–3 | Finding Floon, Trollskull Alley | 6–10 | Who are these people, and what have they stumbled into? |
| II | The Investigation | 3–4 | Fireball!, Gralhund Villa | 4–8 | Who killed Dalakhar, and what was he carrying? |
| III | The Heists | 4–7 | Faction Outposts, Xanathar's Lair, Cassalanter Villa, Sea Maidens Faire, Kolat Towers | 16–28 | Can the PCs recover all three Eyes before any faction assembles them? |
| IV | The Vault | 6–8 | Vault of Dragons | 3–5 | Who claims the Dragons of Waterdeep — and at what cost? |

Total estimated sessions: 29–51. Roughly 7–13 months of weekly play.

> [!info]**Pacing at a Glance**
> Act I runs three weeks of in-world time, from Ches 1 through the Grand Opening on Ches 20 and the Twin Parades on Ches 21. Act II compresses into two to three days. Act III unfolds over months as the party plans and executes its heists, with two hard deadlines: the Sea Maidens Faire departs on Tarsakh 20 and the Cassalanters' Founders' Day feast falls on Flamerule 1. Act IV is a single convergence sequence of three to five sessions.

### Act I: The Setup

Finding Floon opens on Ches 1 with a missing-persons job that pays in real estate: a deed to a dilapidated tavern in the North Ward. Trollskull Alley expands that into three weeks of renovation work, neighborhood politics, and faction recruiters appearing at the door with offers the party isn't yet equipped to fully refuse or trust. The act has no single villain and no grand stakes, by design. Waterdeep opens district by district as the players earn their place in it: the guilds, the Watch, the neighbors, and the city's wider machinery. The tavern opens to the North Ward on Ches 20; the Twin Parades fill Waterdeep's streets on Ches 21. By then the players know their neighborhood, their neighbors, and at least one faction whose name will matter later. Act I works because the city feels like somewhere worth protecting before anything threatens it.

### Act II: The Investigation

The fireball goes off the morning after the parades. Someone the party knows is among the twelve dead. What starts as street-level forensics (witnesses, a nimblewright trace, one strange lead after another) accelerates north toward a noble villa where every major faction has converged on a single object. Gralhund Villa is the act's climax: a quinpartite confrontation inside a building where everyone has a competing claim on the same night. When it ends, the party holds the Stone of Golorr. Act II is short by design; its job is to collapse the mystery into a complication bigger than the premise and hand the party something they don't yet know how to use.

### Act III: The Heists

Five quests, four heist targets, no fixed order. The party holds the Stone and needs three Eyes to open the Vault. Faction Outposts provides the intelligence to plan. Three of the four lair heists hold an Eye; Kolat Towers holds Manshoon. Each target is built as a genuine operation: scoutable, plannable, and responsive to how thoroughly the party prepares. Xanathar's Lair is a dungeon infiltration into the criminal undercity. Cassalanter Villa is social penetration into a noble household with a cult temple beneath it. The Sea Maidens Faire is a nautical caper aboard three ships. Kolat Towers is a fortress assault against Manshoon's extradimensional sanctum. Two clocks run underneath all of it: the Faire departs Tarsakh 20 and Founders' Day falls Flamerule 1. Act III is the bulk of the campaign. Thorough planning pays off; the structure supports improvisation.

### Act IV: The Vault

The Vault of Dragons is a convergence, not a heist. The Faction-State Summary reads every flag the party set across Act III and populates the final confrontation accordingly: which villains survived, which factions arrived to stake their claim, what the Stone's Full Awakening revealed. Aurinax, the adult gold dragon guarding the vault, has four paths to resolution and no obligation to take the simplest one. After the two-wave faction confrontation, 500,000 gold pieces sit on the vault floor. What the party does with them is the campaign's last decision, and the campaign doesn't make it for them.

## Milestone Progression

| Quest | Points | Cumulative | Level |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Finding Floon | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Trollskull Alley | 3 | 5 | 3 |
| Fireball! | 2 | 7 | 3 |
| Gralhund Villa | 2 | 9 | 4 |
| Faction Outposts | 1 | 10 | 4 |
| Lair Heist (1st, any order) | 4 | 14 | 5 |
| Lair Heist (2nd) | 4 | 18 | 6 |
| Lair Heist (3rd) | 4 | 22 | 6 |
| Lair Heist (4th) | 4 | 26 | 7 |
| Vault of Dragons | 2 | 24 or 28 | 7 (3 heists) or 8 (4 heists) |

Milestone Points go to the whole party, including players who missed the session. Level-up is instant and party-wide, with no Long Rest required. Faction missions award no Milestone Points; their rewards are gold, renown, and cross-quest intelligence. For which specific events award points, see each quest's overview.

> [!design]**Why Level 7 Needs Kolat Towers**
> Three lair heists bring the party to 22 cumulative points, which is Level 6. The Level 7 threshold is 23 points. Completing all four heists, including Kolat Towers, pushes the cumulative total to 26 and crosses that threshold before the Vault of Dragons begins. A party that skips Kolat Towers enters the Vault at Level 6 and reaches Level 7 inside the Vault sequence. A party that completes all four heists enters at Level 7 and exits at Level 8, the Undermountain handoff.
