# Session Zero

Five beats before the first session can determine whether the table weathers Act III or fractures under it. Run them in order. Budget ninety minutes.

---

## The Pitch

Lead with this before anything else lands:

> "This is a campaign about crime and consequence in a city where everyone has an agenda. Your characters will get tangled up in something much bigger than a job."

Then establish two things before character sheets come out.

Every useful ally in this campaign has done something questionable. That's not a warning about moral compromise — it's a description of Waterdeep. Characters whose ethics prevent all morally grey employment will find large stretches of this campaign frustrating. Be honest about that at the table now, not after the third faction recruiter appears.

This campaign doesn't have clean resolutions. Political, moral, and personal situations end in compromises, tradeoffs, or open wounds. That's intentional, and the players should know it going in.

---

## Character Creation

Point the players to four sections of the Players' Guide: [Character Creation](../players-guide/character-creation.md), [Debts of the City](../players-guide/debts-of-the-city.md), [Bonds and Flaws](../players-guide/bonds-and-flaws.md), and [Faction Affiliations](../players-guide/faction-affiliations.md).

Each player chooses a Debt, a Bond, and a Flaw, then reads all three aloud at the table. The conversation that follows — what these mean, why they were chosen, what the rest of the table hears — is often more useful than the entries themselves.

While the players build their characters, note two things for yourself:

- Each Flaw maps to a specific quest where the campaign presses on it. That table is in [Bonds and Flaws](bonds-and-flaws.md), in the "Where Each Flaw Lands" sidebar. Note which quest each player's Flaw maps to and give that character the spotlight when the campaign reaches it.
- Each Debt has a Holder. The Holder sections in [Debts of the City](debts-of-the-city.md) tell you who holds the marker, how they make first contact, and what they want. Note which Holders are in play at your table.

## The Gold Question

Before the group finalizes their characters, pose this to the whole table:

**"What would your character do with half a million Dragons?"**

> [!info]**The Gold Question**
> There's no right answer. The point is to surface player values before they matter in play. Record what each character wants from the money — comfort, power, justice, absolution, something else entirely. If players disagree vigorously, better still. How each player answers here defines how they experience the Vault of Dragons, the campaign's culminating choice. Return to what they said when you get there.

## The Campaign Contract

Three things to name before play begins.

**PvP is off by default.** A player who wants their character to act against the party brings it to the table as a conversation, not a unilateral decision.

**Joining a villain faction is a table conversation, not a solo decision.** Four rival factions are actively dangerous and will attempt to recruit player characters over the course of the campaign. Whether a player accepts those overtures requires the same discussion.

**The two Zhentarims.** In Waterdeep, "the Zhentarim" describes two separate organizations with nothing in common except the name. Davil Starsong's Doom Raiders are criminals who present as legitimate businesspeople and will recruit the party during Trollskull Alley; they're a joinable player faction. A rival Zhentarim cell operates elsewhere in the city, answers to a different and far more dangerous leader, and is openly hostile to the Doom Raiders. Establish this at session zero — it's the distinction most likely to confuse players once the city's faction dynamics become visible. Joining the Doom Raiders doesn't mean working for the campaign's primary villain faction.

The [Faction Affiliations](../players-guide/faction-affiliations.md) page uses the same player-safe framing. The rival cell's leader is not named there, and you don't name him here either. The Bregan D'aerthe appear on the faction preview table without their contact. He introduces himself in play.

**Pacing.** The campaign rewards initiative. You're presenting a living world, not a guided tour. Players who wait for events to come to them will see less of the story than players who go looking.

## Safety Tools

Disclose these content categories before play begins. Phrasing should describe the territory without spoiling the specific scenes.

- Infernal bargaining and soul forfeiture
- The death of a child, which the players cannot prevent
- Child endangerment with a time-limited resolution window
- Sudden, unforeseeable loss — characters may lose people they care about
- Body horror involving transformation
- Organized crime as a systemic texture: extortion, coercion, blackmail, and targeted violence as standard villain tools

> [!warning]**Sarlo**
> The child death in the disclosure is **Sarlo**: a twelve-year-old halfling boy who dies in the opening scene of **Fireball!** (see [The Fireball](../../quests/act-ii/fireball/ev-01-the-fireball.md)). His death is written into the event with no intervention mechanic. Players cannot prevent it. Confirm this is something the table can handle before the campaign reaches Act II.

Use the **X-Card** for mid-session intervention, especially once the campaign reaches Act III. Set **Lines and Veils** at session zero — hard stops and fade-to-black preferences, established now rather than negotiated under pressure. If a content moment lands badly mid-session, slow down and check in. The campaign's moral weight is a feature the table chose together; it's not a mandate to push through.

## Before the First Session

The campaign opens on Ches 1, 1492 DR, when the characters arrive at the Yawning Portal and **Finding Floon** begins. The Grand Opening of Trollskull Manor falls on Ches 20 and the fireball on Ches 22. From the neighbors' first appearance in **Trollskull Alley**, watch which one the players care about most: that neighbor dies in the fireball. [Structural Rules](structural-rules.md) covers how to choose and record the victim.
