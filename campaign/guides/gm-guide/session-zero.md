### Session Zero Guide

**Purpose:** Gives the DM a script for the pre-campaign conversation. Should feel like advice from someone who has run this before.

**Content:**

*The Conversation Before Character Creation*
- Script for the campaign pitch: "This is a campaign about crime and consequence in a city where everyone has an agenda. Your characters will get tangled up in something much bigger than a job."
- Two things to establish before character sheets come out:
  1. Every useful ally in this campaign has done something questionable. Characters whose ethics prevent all morally grey employment will find large stretches of this campaign frustrating. Be honest about that at the table.
  2. This campaign does not have clean resolutions. Political, moral, and personal situations will end in compromises, tradeoffs, or open wounds. This is intentional.

*The Gold Question*
- Pose this to the table during character creation: **"What would your character do with half a million Dragons?"**
- No right answer. The point is to surface player values before they matter in play. If players disagree vigorously, even better.
- The DM should note what each character wants from the money — this will define how each player experiences the campaign's final choice.

*The Campaign Contract*
- PvP: default is off. If a player wants to join a villain faction, this is a table conversation, not a solo decision.
- The two Zhentarims: establish that in Waterdeep, "the Zhentarim" refers to two unrelated organizations. The Doom Raiders (Davil Starsong's cell) are criminals who present as legitimate businesspeople; they are a joinable player faction. Manshoon's splinter cell is an enemy faction. Characters who join the Doom Raiders are NOT working for the campaign's main antagonist. This distinction causes confusion if not established at session zero.
- Pacing: the campaign rewards players who take initiative. The DM will present a living world, not a guided tour.

*Safety Tools*
- Content categories to disclose (phrased to not spoil):
  - Infernal bargaining and soul forfeiture
  - Child death — a named child dies in the opening event of Fireball; no player intervention is possible
  - Child endangerment with a time-limited resolution window
  - Sudden, unforeseeable loss — characters may lose people they care about
  - Body horror involving transformation
  - Organized crime: extortion, coercion, blackmail, and targeted violence as standard villain tools
- Recommended tools: X-Card for mid-session intervention (especially once the campaign reaches Act III); Lines and Veils at session zero for hard stops and fade-to-black preferences
- DM guidance: if a content moment lands badly mid-session, slow down and check in. The campaign's moral weight is a feature, not a mandate.
