# Player Factions Overview

You don't choose a faction during character creation. You can tell your GM which factions interest you, but formal membership happens in play. Every major faction in Waterdeep initiates contact with your characters during **Trollskull Alley**, and you decide how to answer when they do. Your character can belong to more than one faction at once — memberships in rival factions can pull your party in different directions. That tension is by design. Talk it through when it comes up.

Use this table to plan your character. Each faction's contact introduces themselves in play.

| Faction | What they offer | What they ask |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| Harpers | Intelligence, political cover, archive access | Surveillance, dead drops, attending specific events |
| Lords' Alliance | Official sanction, noble-tier social access, legal protection | Reporting on crime, protecting civic stability |
| Order of the Gauntlet | Reliable combat backup in righteous causes | Actively exposing evil, especially infernal dealings |
| Emerald Enclave | Safe passage through parks, ranger contacts | Protecting natural spaces; opposing undead activity |
| Force Grey | Powerful backup in genuine city-scale threats | Exclusive; earning the leadership's trust first |
| Doom Raiders (Zhentarim) | Criminal network access, fences, safe houses, street intelligence | Jobs that keep the network profitable |
| Bregan D'aerthe | Exceptional intelligence; rare resources | Loyalty, and eventually a favor that reaches beyond Waterdeep |

> [!warning]**The Two Zhentarims**
> If you want your character to join "the Zhentarim," you're joining the **Doom Raiders**. A separate Zhentarim cell operates elsewhere in the city, answering to a different and far more dangerous leader, with different contacts, different methods, and a moral code that has nothing in common with the Doom Raiders'. The two organizations do not get along, and your GM will always make clear which one you're dealing with.

## Renown

- **Starting Renown.** Your Renown with a faction starts at 0 the moment you join.
- **Rank thresholds.** Named ranks unlock at Renown 1, 3, 10, 25, and 50. Benefits are cumulative. A character at Renown 10 keeps everything earned at 1 and 3.
- **Earning Renown.** You earn Renown by completing faction missions and by taking the specific Grand Game actions each faction lists under its own Earning Renown section. No other source awards Renown; general good roleplay earns none on its own.
- **Losing Renown.** Serious offenses cost 2 Renown each: betraying a mission, harming faction members, or working against the faction's interests. Renown never falls below 0.
- **Lords' Alliance discipline.** The Lords' Alliance is the only faction that enforces consequences for refusing or failing an assigned mission without compelling reason: formal suspension until reinstated, or outright dismissal.

> [!info]**Renown at a Glance**
> Starts at 0. Ranks unlock at 1, 3, 10, 25, and 50. Serious offenses cost 2 Renown each; Renown never drops below 0. Only the Lords' Alliance suspends or dismisses members over a refused or failed mission.

## Running the Player Factions

Each of the six standard factions assigns a named contact who handles recruitment and mission delivery. Full profiles, mission tables, and First Meeting scenes are in the **Organizations** pages. The table below adds what the player version withholds: contacts and the faction's actual agenda.

| Faction | Contact | What they want from PCs |
|---------|---------|------------------------|
| Harpers | **Mirt** (primary); **Remallia "Remi" Haventree** (secondary — identity withheld until Mission 4) | Intelligence on faction activity; protection of vulnerable NPCs; exposure of corruption |
| Lords' Alliance | **Jalester Silvermane** | The gold returned to the city treasury; factions contained; no public embarrassment of the Lords |
| Emerald Enclave | **Melannor Fellbranch** | Anything tied to unnatural or fiendish corruption — Cassalanter diabolism, undead in Undermountain |
| Order of the Gauntlet | **Savra Belabranta** | Combat support against known villain lairs; the Cassalanter sacrifice stopped before Founders' Day |
| Force Grey | **Vajra Safahr** | The same outcomes the Open Lord wants, handled quietly and without the Lords' Alliance's politics |
| Doom Raiders | **Davil Starsong** (Missions 1–2); **Tashlyn Yafeera** (Missions 3–6) | Intelligence on Manshoon's cell; containment of Xanathar Guild activity; anything that weakens their rivals |

## Bregan D'aerthe

Bregan D'aerthe is the only villain faction available to player characters, and joining it changes the shape of a major quest. Full faction profile is on the **Bregan D'aerthe** organization page.

Recruitment follows the same schedule as the other six factions: during **Trollskull Alley**, three drow lieutenants shadow the party for several days. Any PC is a candidate. Drow party members draw closer surveillance, but the invitation reaches every member of the group regardless of species or background.

The surveillance has three branches:

- **Watch reported:** The spies vanish within hours. All BD contact ends for the campaign. Set **BD Contact Severed**.
- **Surveillance confronted:** The spies melt away and leave a single black eye patch on the cobblestones. J.B. Nevercott knocks the next morning.
- **Party does nothing:** Surveillance continues through the tenday. J.B. Nevercott knocks regardless of whether the party noticed the watchers.

**J.B. Nevercott** (haberdasher, burgundy coat, *hat of disguise*) arrives at Trollskull Manor and maintains the fiction until he chooses to drop it. The full First Meeting scene is in the **Bregan D'aerthe** organization page. Set **BD Contact Established** when it concludes.

> [!warning]**Bregan D'aerthe Contact Severed**
> If the party reports the BD surveillance to the Watch, set **BD Contact Severed** and do not run the J.B. Nevercott scene. This flag closes BD membership for the entire campaign. Jarlaxle becomes aware of the party regardless; his intelligence network is thorough enough that the report changes nothing except the relationship. He operates as a neutral third party in **Sea Maidens Faire** rather than a potential ally. There is no second recruitment opportunity. The decision is permanent.

Jarlaxle's true identity surfaces in **Fireball!** and **Sea Maidens Faire**, when the nimblewright thread leads the party to the carnival and its captain. Membership opens several quests before that disclosure.

For parties who join, **Sea Maidens Faire** inverts: the Faire becomes a home base and Jarlaxle sends the party after another faction's Eye rather than holding the Faire as a heist target. The quest's inversion is the point — BD members experience the Grand Game from Jarlaxle's side of the table, where the resources and the objectives both belong to him.

Members take on Jarlaxle's long-term goal: Lords' Alliance recognition of Luskan's reformed governance. That obligation comes due in **Vault of Dragons**, where Jarlaxle proposes returning the gold publicly in the party's name in exchange for a letter of introduction to the Alliance council. It is what he has been working toward since he arrived in the city.

## Conflicting Loyalties

The factions' interests are incompatible by design. The Lords' Alliance wants the gold returned to the city treasury intact. The Doom Raiders want Manshoon destroyed; his cell threatens their entire operation. Force Grey wants the Grand Game resolved before it spills into public view and embarrasses the city. Bregan D'aerthe wants a political favor that extends well beyond Waterdeep's walls.

A party with members in multiple factions will face these tensions as individual character obligations. When a Harper PC and a Doom Raider PC disagree about whether to hand the Stone to Mirt or protect Manshoon's rival operation, that's a character moment. Let it play as one. Treat each faction position as a real constraint on that character, give the players space to negotiate it between themselves, and resist resolving it by erasing one character's obligation. The disagreement is the texture of the campaign's second act.

The practical limit: faction pressure should complicate a decision the party cares about, not force someone to act against the group's survival. Almost every faction obligation has a tenday of slack built in. Surface the competing positions as information; let the players find the path.

Renown rules and mission-running procedures are in the **Factions** guide: see [Factions: Overview](../factions/01-overview.md).

## Non-Player Factions

The seven factions above are the organizations available for player characters to join. The other major powers active in Waterdeep during the Grand Game — **Xanathar's Guild**, **Manshoon's Zhentarim splinter**, and the **Cassalanters** — are adversary factions. Players may interact with, negotiate with, or broker deals with these organizations, but formal membership is not available through normal play. See **Notable Figures** for details on their leaders and agents, and **Running the Villains** (GM Guide) for how to run them at the table.
