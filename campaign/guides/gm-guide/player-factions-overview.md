### Player Factions

**Purpose:** Brief operational overview of the factions PCs can join, so the DM understands each faction's role and character before play begins. Full profiles and mission tables live on the Organizations pages; renown rules are below.

**Content:**

Seven factions are available to PCs. Six are standard player factions; one is unique.

*The six standard factions*

| Faction | Character | What they want from PCs |
|---------|-----------|------------------------|
| Harpers | City's shadow conscience; intelligence-first, non-lethal where possible | Information on faction activity; protection of vulnerable NPCs; exposure of corruption |
| Lords' Alliance | Political stability and Waterdeep's institutional authority | The gold returned to the city; factions contained; no public embarrassment of the Lords |
| Emerald Enclave | Nature and balance; thin presence in a city, but present | Anything connected to unnatural or fiendish corruption (Cassalanters; undead in Undermountain) |
| Order of the Gauntlet | Active suppression of evil; direct action over subtlety | Combat support; assault on known villain lairs; the Cassalanter sacrifice prevented |
| Force Grey (Gray Hands) | Vajra Safahr's personal agent corps; high-powered, high-discretion | The same things the Open Lord wants, but handled quietly and without the Lords' Alliance's politics |
| Zhentarim (Doom Raiders) | Davil Starsong's cell; criminal enterprise with a palatable face | Intelligence on Manshoon's operation; containment of Xanathar Guild activity; anything that weakens their rivals |

*The unique faction: Bregan D'aerthe*

Bregan D'aerthe is the only villain faction PCs can join. This changes **Sea Maidens Faire**'s structure entirely — the Sea Maidens Faire becomes a home base, and Jarlaxle sends the party after another faction's Eye rather than being the target of a heist. The arc's inversion is the point: PCs working for Jarlaxle experience the same heist framework from the other side.

Bregan D'aerthe is available only if the nimblewright investigation (**Fireball!**) leads to the Sea Maidens Faire and the PCs respond to Jarlaxle's approach by negotiating rather than stealing. PCs who join take on Jarlaxle's goal (Lords' Alliance membership for Luskan) as their own faction obligation, which comes due in **Vault of Dragons**.

*Conflicting loyalties*

PCs in different factions will have conflicting faction interests by design. The Lords' Alliance wants the gold returned; the Doom Raiders want Manshoon destroyed; Force Grey wants everything kept quiet; Bregan D'aerthe wants a political favor. This is the campaign's social texture. The DM should let faction loyalties create real pressure on individual PCs without forcing party fragmentation.

**Full faction detail:** the **Organizations** pages (faction overviews, key members, Quest Hooks, missions) and **Notable Figures**.

**Tone:** Overview-level. Read for orientation; run the factions from the Organizations pages.

### How Renown Works

Renown is tracked as a numerical score for each faction a character belongs to, starting at 0 when they join. The five named ranks per faction unlock at **Renown 1, 3, 10, 25, and 50**. Benefits are cumulative — a character at Renown 10 retains the benefits of ranks 1 and 3.

**Gaining Renown:** Completing faction missions earns the renown listed in each mission table. Specific Grand Game actions earn the renown listed in each faction's Earning Renown section. No other renown is awarded.

**Losing Renown:** Ordinary disagreements with faction members do not reduce renown. Serious offenses — betraying a mission, harming faction members, working against the faction's interests — cost 2 renown per offense. Renown cannot fall below 0. For the **Lords' Alliance specifically**: refusing or failing to complete an assigned mission without compelling justification results in formal suspension (no further missions until reinstated) or dismissal (loss of membership and all renown). The Alliance is the only faction that enforces this.

**Tracking:** Renown is tracked as a number for every faction a character belongs to. Ranks unlock only at the listed thresholds.

### Running Faction Missions

Each faction operates as an ongoing relationship. Contacts push for information, react to the PCs' choices in the Grand Game, and bring their own agendas to bear as events escalate. Faction missions are structured as mini-arcs: each has an investigation phase, a complication, and at least two resolution paths. No single skill check should determine success. Full write-ups are in the **Faction Missions** quest journals.

**A note on the Zhentarim:** Two Zhentarim cells operate in Waterdeep. Davil Starsong's **Doom Raiders** are the player-joinable faction covered in the **Organizations** pages. Manshoon's faction is the villain cell, called **the Splinter** throughout these notes. They are hostile to the Doom Raiders and to the PCs. When these missions say "the Splinter," they mean Manshoon's agents, not Davil's.

**Mission delivery:** Unless noted, missions arrive via the faction's preferred method: paper birds for Harpers, flying snakes for Zhentarim, animal messengers for the Enclave, Sending spells for Force Grey.

**Failure:** If a mission ends in failure, the situation changes; it doesn't simply reset. Targets escape, informants are compromised, and rival factions benefit. Each mission's Aftermath notes what specifically changes when things go wrong; use that, not a general reset.

**Renown:** Each mission lists a base renown award calibrated to its tier (Level 2–3 missions pay 2, Level 4–5 pay 3, Level 6–7 pay 4). Each mission also lists specific bonus conditions worth +1 each — award only those, not a general judgment call. A clean failure forfeits the award but costs nothing; a botched mission that causes collateral damage costs −1. Supplementary renown from upholding faction values (+1) and achieving major faction goals (+2) stacks on top; a dedicated faction member should reach Renown 30+ by the late heist arcs.

### Non-Player Factions

The seven factions above are the organizations available for player characters to join. The other major powers active in Waterdeep during the Grand Game — **Xanathar's Guild**, **Manshoon's Zhentarim splinter**, and the **Cassalanters** — are adversary factions. Players may interact with, negotiate with, or broker deals with these organizations, but formal membership is not available through normal play. See **Notable Figures** for details on their leaders and agents, and **Running the Villains** (GM Guide) for how to run them at the table.
