# About This Campaign

Welcome to Waterdeep. Before you build a character, we want you to know what kind of game you're building one for. This campaign asks a lot of its players: patience, curiosity, and a willingness to let the city's factions pull your characters in directions you didn't plan for. Read this page first.

> [!info]**How to Read This Guide**
> This guide contains only what your character could plausibly know, or what you need to build them: backgrounds, factions as your character would encounter them, the city as any resident understands it. The rest belongs to your GM. Don't go looking in the GM Guide for what's coming next. Let the campaign unfold at the table.

## The Three Pillars

**Make It a Heist.** Every major score in this campaign, whether it's a faction's stronghold, a rival's stash house, or the vault at the center of it all, is something you can case, plan against, and hit on your own terms. You'll scout entrances, map guard patterns, and choose an approach before you commit. Charging in the front door works. So does bluffing your way past a guard captain, bribing a servant for a floor plan, or slipping in through the sewers. The city rewards a plan.

**Feature All Villains.** Waterdeep is not waiting for one archvillain to be defeated. Several rival powers are chasing the same prize at the same time, each with a different method and a different temperament. They aren't standing still while you work. They're moving pieces of their own, and your actions shift which of them gains ground. You won't be handed a single antagonist to topple. You'll be navigating a city where the balance of power is never settled.

**Your choices drive the story.** Every mystery in this campaign has more than one way in. If a lead goes cold, another will surface, so you're never one missed roll away from a dead end. What you decide to chase, who you decide to trust, and how loudly you announce yourselves all change what the city looks like the next morning. The people and factions around you notice what you do, and they respond. We build situations, not scripts.

## What Makes This Campaign Different

This campaign rewards planning over raw power. A well-cased approach beats a well-rolled Attack Roll more often than not, and the smartest table victories in this campaign rarely involve a fight at all.

Several rival powers are active in the city at once, all pursuing the same goal from different angles. None of them are waiting their turn. That means the city keeps moving even when you're not looking at it, and a choice made in one faction's favor is a choice made against another's.

At the heart of this campaign is a hidden fortune somewhere in Waterdeep, and everyone, factions, nobles, your own characters included, has an opinion about who deserves it. We're not going to tell you the right answer, because there isn't one. What your character decides to do with that fortune, if they ever get the chance, says more about them than any stat block could.

> [!design]**Why Four Villains at Once**
> We built this campaign around several rival powers acting at the same time rather than a single mastermind, because a city with one villain is a city that stops moving the moment you deal with them. Here, the other powers keep working, keep reacting to what you do, and stay ready to fill any vacuum you create. Your choices shift the balance between them, sometimes toward you, sometimes not. We won't tell you here which of them wants what. You'll find that out in play.

## Who This Campaign Suits

This campaign is built for tables that enjoy exploring a living city: walking its wards, following a lead down an unexpected street, and treating a rumor as seriously as a written clue. It rewards lateral thinking. If the front door is locked, we expect you to look for a window, not wait for the GM to open the door for you. Your home base matters too. The people who depend on it, and the stakes that come with keeping it standing, are as much a part of this campaign as any dungeon. And because several factions are working independently and reacting to you, the story you get is one nobody at the table, including your GM, has fully scripted in advance.

This campaign is not built for tables that want a primary combat focus. Fights happen, and some of them are dangerous, but they are rarely the fastest or smartest way through a problem. It's also not built for tables that want a clear, guided throughline from scene to scene. We won't always tell you what to do next; figuring that out is part of the game. If your table wants a tightly scripted story with a single villain and a predictable rhythm of encounters, this isn't that campaign, and it's better to know that now than three sessions in.
