# Tavern Time

> Structuring draft.

Use this four-step procedure for each significant night the characters spend at the tavern.

**Step 1 — Check for an Event.** Roll 1d6. On a 1, an Event occurs tonight. At Fame 81+ (Fashionable), Events occur on a 1–2.

**Step 2 — Determine Significant Patrons.** Roll 1d6 for the number of significant patrons present. Roll on the Patron Table (below) for each. Duplicate results mean that patron is more central to the evening than usual.

**Step 3 — Establish Topics and Agendas.** Look up Topics/Agendas for each patron. Choose one topic per patron, or select a single topic to drive the evening. Default to the first unplayed topic.

**Step 4 — Let It Run.** The party decides how to engage. Two strong interactions are worth more than five rushed ones. Look for collisions between patrons — that's where the interesting moments live.

## Tenday Schedule

Tavern Time mixes set nights with rolled ones. Each tenday listed below has one **scheduled night**: the first significant night the party spends at the tavern during that tenday. On that night, skip Steps 1 and 2 and use the listed patrons and Event. Every other night, and every tenday not listed, runs on the procedure and the tables below.

The rolls in this guide (the Patron, Renaer's Friends, and Event tables, the Revenue Roll, and the dice inside individual staff and Event entries) are a deliberate exception to the campaign's no-dice rule. The tavern is the one place where the evening should surprise the GM as well as the players. The scheduled nights make sure the moments the campaign depends on still happen.

Some rows fire on a calendar tenday; others fire in the first tenday after a quest ends. If two rows land in the same tenday, run the calendar row first and push the quest row to the next tenday.
