# Design Notes: Force Grey Mission 4 — Destroy the Intellect Factory

## Nihiloor's Quest-Spanning Role

***The same enemy, twice.*** Nihiloor is the same mind flayer who appears in Xanathar's personal retinue in **Xanathar's Lair**. This mission intentionally precedes **Xanathar's Lair** in the expected encounter sequence — the Force Grey arc builds toward **Xanathar's Lair** throughout, and Mission 4 is where the two tracks converge. A party that ran this mission and drove Nihiloor out will encounter a weakened version of it in **Xanathar's Lair**: still dangerous, but without the production infrastructure that made it a threat at scale.

***If Xanathar's Lair runs first.*** It is possible for the party to reach **Xanathar's Lair** before completing Mission 4, particularly if their Force Grey renown path is slower than their main-quest advancement. In this case, the mission encounter with Nihiloor carries different weight: the party recognizes it from the lair, knows what it can do, and has prior evidence of its capabilities. Nihiloor, in turn, has encountered them before and has a prepared escape route from X24, so it moves toward that exit faster. Note the sequence when planning this mission.

***Destruction versus escape.*** Whether Nihiloor is destroyed here matters for **Xanathar's Lair**. This is documented in the Next Steps block of ev-02. Track the outcome and carry it forward — it is one of the few places in the faction mission arc where a specific binary outcome has named downstream effects.

## Soluun Xibrindas

***Why include a rescue here.*** Soluun appears in Doom Raiders Mission 1 as the antagonist and reappears here as a prisoner. A party that dealt with him in Mission 1 will recognize him in X24 and face a concrete decision: the man they may have arrested, fought, or let escape is now in need of rescue from the same organization they are currently infiltrating. Whether they help him says something about the party's moral accounting.

For parties who never encountered Soluun before: he is a drow claiming BD affiliation to stay alive, which has kept him alive for six tendays, and the debt he owes if rescued functions as a future asset without requiring prior acquaintance.

***The escape cost.*** The decision to rescue Soluun is made in ev-01; the cost is paid in ev-02. Carrying an unconscious person through an alerted dungeon is a concrete mechanical burden. This is deliberate — the design ensures that the rescue has weight at the moment it matters, not just at the moment of decision. Players who chose to help him and then feel the cost of that choice have engaged with the mission more fully than players who never had to pay for their intentions.

## The Pool as the Real Objective

***Nihiloor is secondary.*** The briefing makes this explicit, and the design reinforces it: the pool can be destroyed without ever engaging Nihiloor, and the +2 renown goes to destroying the pool rather than defeating the mind flayer. This is structurally unusual in a dungeon-crawl mission — the combat encounter is secondary to the objective. This reflects how Force Grey missions are designed. Vajra cares about outcomes. The party that slips past Nihiloor, destroys the pool with holy water, and exits clean has completed this mission better than the party that spent six rounds in a hard fight and dragged themselves out with three exhausted characters.

***The three destruction methods.*** Each has a different profile: holy water is fast but loud, physical destruction is loud, elemental damage is quiet but slow. In a potentially occupied room with a three-to-five minute enforcer clock, these are meaningful tactical choices. Present all three as viable, and let the party's preparation — did they bring holy water, do they have sustained fire damage — shape which path they take.
