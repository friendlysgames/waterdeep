# Design Notes: Force Grey Mission 2 — The Dragon in the Harbor

## Zelifarn as a Long-Form Contact

***Why a dragon, not a contact.*** The original appendix positions Zelifarn as an intelligence asset and a recurring presence. The design decision here is that his first appearance must earn that recurrence — the party needs a reason to care about a bronze dragon who lives in a harbor. The solution is making him a genuine transaction partner rather than an information vendor. He wants things; he notices things; he will tell you what he notices if you give him what he wants. This is more interesting than "roll Persuasion to extract information," and it means the relationship has texture when he resurfaces in Arc H.

***The gift mechanic.*** A character who gives Zelifarn something they actually use earns warmth that persists beyond this conversation. This is a signal to DMs: let the players decide what they value, and let that decision matter. A party that gives him a holy symbol or a favored weapon is investing more than a party that fishes a spare coin from their pocket. The mechanic does not require a roll — the DM's judgment about what constitutes genuine value is sufficient. The point is not to make giving expensive but to make it meaningful.

## The Submarine as the Mission's Spine

***The reveal structure.*** The mission sends the party to assess a dragon. The dragon, incidentally, has been watching a submarine. This order matters — the party goes underwater to talk to Zelifarn, not to find the submarine. If they went underwater to find the submarine, this would be a reconnaissance mission with a dragon obstacle. Because they went to talk to Zelifarn, the submarine is something Zelifarn noticed, and its significance is established entirely through Vajra's reaction rather than through exposition. Her quiet, her question about timing, her writing while they are still talking — these communicate what the submarine is without naming it.

***Forward connection.*** The submarine is a Bregan D'aerthe vessel. It connects directly to the Sea Maidens Faire, Arc H, and Jarlaxle's underwater operation. A party who understood what Zelifarn described here and told Vajra everything is better positioned in Arc H — they know there is infrastructure below the *Eyecatcher* before they arrive. Vajra's Watch observation on the *Eyecatcher*'s harbor position (the second renown condition) is a quiet way of saying: what you found here changed how the city watches that ship.

## The Fleetswake Variant

***When to use it.*** The Fleetswake variant exists because the festival calendar in this campaign is a real element of the city's texture, and a mission happening during a maritime festival should feel different from the same mission in a quiet month. Meritide Blackfin provides a different emotional register for the hook — the Church of Umberlee has a legitimate grievance, and the party is being recruited as problem-solvers by a genuinely intimidating religious authority rather than receiving a bureaucratic Sending. The underlying assignment is identical.

***The DC adjustment.*** The Fleetswake variant raises the Insight check to assess Zelifarn from DC 13 to DC 15. This is not a penalty but a condition: choppy harbor water, a dragon in rougher conditions, and more chaotic surroundings make a clean read on his intentions harder to achieve. It does not change who he is — only how legible he is in a more difficult environment.
