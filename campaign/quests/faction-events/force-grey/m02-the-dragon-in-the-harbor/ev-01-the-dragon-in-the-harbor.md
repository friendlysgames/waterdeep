# The Dragon in the Harbor

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> This Social/Exploration Event begins when Vajra's *Sending* arrives and the party descends into Deepwater Harbor. In this Event, the party can:
>
> - Receive Vajra's twenty-five-word *Sending* and stop at Blackstaff Tower to collect potions of water breathing.
> - Descend to the harbor floor at depth and find Zelifarn before he finds them.
> - Assess his intentions as genuinely non-hostile and give Vajra a clean report.
> - Give Zelifarn something they actually value and earn his warmest regard.
> - Learn about the submarine attachment on the *Eyecatcher* and deliver that intelligence to Vajra.
>
> #### Hook Variant: Fleetswake
>
> If the campaign is running during the Fleetswake festival (Ches 21–30) and the party has had at least one scene in the Dock Ward or harbor, use this variant in place of Vajra's *Sending*. Umberlee's tithes are disappearing from the deepest harbor moorings. **Meritide Blackfin**, the Dread High Priest of the Queenspire — a Darfellan priest of distinctive presence — contacts the party through a dockside priest, a message in a sailor's tankard, or a locathah messenger. The priest gives them 48 hours to find the thief before the goddess calls a wave. Meritide provides gillweed or potions of water breathing. The party brings the problem to Vajra through Renaer or through Renown 1 with Force Grey, and she folds it into Mission 2 as the same underwater assignment. During the Fleetswake variant, the harbor is rougher and the DC 13 Wisdom (Insight) check to read Zelifarn's intentions is DC 15. Use the standard *Sending* hook at all other times.
>
> #### Background
>
> Zelifarn has been at the harbor for eleven days. He surfaced twice — once to examine a fishing boat, once to speak briefly with a sailor who fainted — and has not harmed anyone. He has also been watching the *Eyecatcher* closely enough to notice the secondary hull structure below its waterline. He would have approached it, but the crew discouraged him. He is correct that they would have. The structure is a Bregan D'aerthe operational submarine. Vajra does not know about the submarine before this mission.

### The Brief

The *Sending* arrives in one party member's head.

> [!readaloud]
> The voice drops into your thoughts without any signal — Vajra's quick, clipped cadence, the same words chosen with the same care as before.
>
> > *"A young bronze dragon has taken up residence in Deepwater Harbor. I need its intentions assessed. Come to Blackstaff Tower at once. Bring your people."*

The party stops at Blackstaff Tower before descending. Four potions of water breathing are on Vajra's desk when they arrive, laid out in a row.

> [!readaloud]
> Four glass vials are on Vajra's desk in a row. She does not look up from her map.
>
> > "Thirty minutes each. Move with purpose."

> [!social]**Before the Descent**
>
> Vajra Safahr (Lawful Neutral, Calishite human, she/her) :: the Blackstaff of Waterdeep; brief and operational at this meeting.
>
> Vajra is not conducting a briefing. She has placed the potions on the desk, she has said what she needs to say, and she is waiting to see if the party has a question that is actually worth answering. Her manner is the same as always: present, exact, not inviting small talk.
>
> Conversation topics Vajra will engage on:
> - What the party is looking for and what she wants to know.
> - What the dragon has been doing and whether it has harmed anyone.
> - Whether the potions are enough for the depth required.
>
> Specific dialogue for Vajra on these topics is presented below.

> [!qna]**What are we looking for?**
>
> > "Its intentions. Whether it's there by accident or by design, and whether it plans to stay. Report everything it tells you. Report everything it doesn't tell you."

> [!qna]**Has it hurt anyone?**
>
> > "Not yet. There have been two sightings, one sailor's report, and no injuries. I want to keep it that way."

> [!qna]**Are four potions enough?**
>
> She glances up from her map, briefly.
>
> > "Forty feet. Thirty minutes each. Yes."

Once the party is ready, proceed to **The Descent**.

### The Descent

The harbor bottom at depth is cold and dark at the edges, lit in the center by diffracted light filtering down from the surface. The old elvish wreck rises from the sediment — two centuries of growth on its frame, its masts reduced to bare poles.

> [!readaloud]
> The harbor floor at depth is cold, the water dark at the edges and dim at the center where light comes down from above in slanted columns. An old wreck rises from the sediment ahead of you, its hull encrusted with two hundred years of growth, its masts bare poles with nothing left to catch. Something is circling it — a creature approximately forty feet long, trailing a faint cold bioluminescence in the water behind it. It turns before you are within speaking range.

### Zelifarn

He closes the distance at a speed that leaves no time to formulate an approach. He is young and intensely curious and not remotely concerned about the gap in size between himself and the party.

> [!readaloud]
> The creature covers the distance between you in the time it takes to notice it has started moving. Forty feet long, broad through the chest, bronze scales catching the diffracted light from above, and the moment it is close enough for you to see its face, it is clearly delighted to have visitors.
>
> > "Hello! Hello, boat people! You're new! Tell me something I don't know!"

> [!social]**The Bronze Dragon**
>
> Zelifarn (Neutral Good, Young Bronze Dragon, he/him) :: a bright, bouncy young sea dragon who trades facts the way sailors trade rumors, with complete sincerity on both sides.
>
> Zelifarn wants things, and he pursues them through relentless enthusiasm and fair trading rather than threats or leverage. He is proud of his harbor — "my harbour" is how he refers to it — and he has been watching everything in it with the focused attention of someone who genuinely enjoys watching. He calls ships by their names and people by what they are wearing. He picks up rude sailor words and uses them wrong and innocently.
>
> His cooperation is best earned through a genuine exchange. A character who gives Zelifarn something they actually use — a holy symbol they carry, a tool of their trade, a weapon they favour — earns his warmest regard for the rest of the conversation and well beyond it. A character who offers a spare coin or an object of no value gets polite attention and nothing more. There is no check for this: the GM's judgment about what constitutes genuine value is sufficient, and what matters is that the choice is meaningful.
>
> Conversation topics Zelifarn will engage on:
> - What the party is carrying, wearing, or has been doing.
> - What he has seen in the harbor over the last eleven days.
> - The large ship with the flags — but only once he has warmed to the party.
>
> Specific dialogue is presented below.

> [!qna]**What have you seen here?**
>
> His head tilts one way, then the other, taking in the party's equipment.
>
> > "So many ships! This harbour is wonderful. I've been here eleven days and I've seen twelve different sizes of boats, seven kinds of cargo, one whale — only briefly, she was passing through — and four sailors who fell off their ships and had to be fished out, which is good because fish, but also worrying because humans." He waves one flipper. "Trade? I tell you one thing, you tell me one thing! Fair trade!"

> [!qna]**About the big ship with the flags?**
>
> This topic is available once Zelifarn has had a few minutes with the party and warmed to them. He brings it up himself if the party gives him something of genuine value; otherwise they may raise it directly.
>
> > "The Eyecatcher! Yes! The big ship with the funny name and all the flags. There is something on its bottom that doesn't belong there." He tilts his head. "Not an anchor. Not a keel extension. A shape I don't know. Like a second hull that faces down. I've been watching it for a tenday — it comes out sometimes at night, goes out into the harbour, comes back again."
>
> He pauses to think about this with obvious enjoyment.
>
> > "I considered offering to help them with their security. I decided they would say no. They seem shy."

> [!exploration]**Assessing His Intentions**
>
> Any character who watches Zelifarn's behavior during the encounter and makes a successful **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight) check** (DC 15 during the Fleetswake variant) confirms that he is genuinely non-hostile and curious about the harbor, with no agenda beyond collecting interesting objects and information.
>
> - **On a success:** The party can report to Vajra that Zelifarn is harmless and genuinely curious. Vajra finds this satisfying.
> - **On a failure:** His motives remain uncertain. The party can report that he did not attack anyone but cannot confirm he will not. Vajra, given this ambiguous report, does not love it and asks them to return if he surfaces again. A partial report is a real consequence.

### The Report

The party returns to Blackstaff Tower. Vajra hears the Zelifarn assessment first — she takes it in and makes a brief note. Then the submarine.

> [!readaloud]
> When you describe the hull structure, Vajra sets down her pen and looks at you for a moment without speaking.
>
> > "A carnival fleet with an underwater attachment. In Waterdeep's harbour." A pause. "For how long?"
>
> She picks up her pen before the answer is finished and begins writing.
>
> > "Thank you. This is useful."

She asks follow-up questions about the submarine's dimensions and attachment method if the party can answer them. Each additional detail earns her focused attention for another moment. When they have given everything they have, she closes the notebook and says nothing more about it. She is revising her understanding of how far Jarlaxle's reach extends in Waterdeep.

### Concluding the Event

The submarine is a Bregan D'aerthe operational vessel, and Vajra's Watch observation on the *Eyecatcher*'s harbor position (awarded with the second renown condition below) is a quiet consequence of what the party found here.

> [!gamemaster]**Event Outcomes**
>
> Mark each outcome that occurs. Later events read them.
>
> - **Zelifarn Contacted** — mark when the party made contact with Zelifarn and established a relationship. Read by **Sea Maidens Faire**: parties who met him here have prior standing with a young bronze dragon who has been watching the harbor. He recognizes them, has been following their activities with interest, and cooperates more readily because of what was exchanged here. Parties who gave him something of genuine value have an even warmer reception.

> [!gamemaster]**Next Steps**
>
> Award **+1 Renown** when Zelifarn's intentions are confirmed as non-hostile and reported accurately to Vajra.
>
> Award **+1 Renown** when the submarine observation is delivered in enough detail that Vajra can brief Laeral Silverhand's naval intelligence contact. The *Eyecatcher*'s harbor position is now under discreet Watch observation.
>
> Zelifarn is now a loose contact — unconventional, interested in a fair exchange, and watching everything that moves below the harbor surface. He shares observations for something interesting added to his collection.
>
> He resurfaces in **Sea Maidens Faire**. Parties who gave him something genuine here have established prior standing. Parties who gave him nothing worth keeping will need to earn it fresh.
>
> **Force Grey Mission 3 — The Trouble with Meloon** becomes available when the party reaches Renown 4 and 4th level.

## Overview

Vajra sends the party into Deepwater Harbor to assess a young bronze dragon who has taken up residence below the surface — and to find out what he has noticed. Zelifarn proves non-hostile, enthusiastically curious, and very good at watching ships.

## Summary

Vajra sent the party to assess a young bronze dragon named Zelifarn living near an elvish wreck in Deepwater Harbor. He proved non-hostile and intensely curious, trading information for objects of genuine value. He also mentioned, conversationally, that a second hull structure had been attached to the bottom of the *Eyecatcher* — one of the Sea Maidens Faire vessels — that he had been watching for a tenday. Vajra received both reports and immediately began writing.
