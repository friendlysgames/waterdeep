# Design Notes: Force Grey Mission 3 — The Trouble with Meloon

## The Split and Why

***Why two events, not one.*** The tenday and the confrontation are different kinds of work. The tenday is accumulation — evidence building across days, each beat adding to a picture that only completes at the end. The confrontation is decision — what the party does with the picture they built. Keeping them in one file would require the party to navigate both tracks simultaneously, which buries the different skills each demands. Splitting at Day 7 preserves the building-tension structure of the surveillance and gives the confrontation its own dramatic weight as a distinct scene.

## Azuredge as the Mission's Heart

***The sentient weapon mechanic.*** Azuredge is the most important element of this mission, not Meloon. Meloon is the problem. Azuredge is the moral architecture: a weapon that has been fighting its user's possession for tendays, that has prevented worse outcomes, that cannot do this alone and knows it. When it communicates — three emotional impressions, not words — it is not asking the party to complete a task. It is asking them to finish something it started.

The DC 14 plus a direct question (versus DC 18 without) rewards players who think to address the axe rather than just roll to understand it. Addressing an object that might be alive is the sort of gesture many players make automatically — reaching toward something rather than just rolling at it. The lower DC is the mechanical reward for that gesture, because it changes what the roll is.

***Playing Azuredge.*** Azuredge communicates entirely through emotional impression. It has no voice. DMs playing this beat should think of the way a dog communicates urgency — by fixing on whoever is addressing it and refusing to let go, not through words. The three impressions are ordered carefully: concern first, frustration second, hope last. This sequence contains an argument. Let there be a pause between each impression, long enough for the player to sit with one before the next arrives.

## The Consequence of Failure

***The inconclusive path is fully designed, not punitive.*** A party that reports without specifics does not fail the mission — they fail to end the devourer's operation. The consequence is calibrated: Mission 4 is harder, not impossible. The devourer having identified the party increases an existing threat rather than creating a new one. Some parties will genuinely be unable to confirm the possession clearly in ten days, and the mission should remain completable at increased cost rather than closed off.

***Why this connects to Mission 4.*** Nihiloor's network is the thematic spine of Force Grey's mission arc. Mission 3 removes an observation asset; Mission 4 destroys the production facility. If the party failed Mission 3's confirmation step, Nihiloor has time to prepare. This is one of the few places in the faction mission arc where the sequence creates mechanical stakes for performance — a direct consequence of how well the party handled the surveillance.
