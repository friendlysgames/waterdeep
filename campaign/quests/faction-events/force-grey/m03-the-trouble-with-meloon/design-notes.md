# Design Notes: Force Grey Mission 3 — The Trouble with Meloon

## The Split and Why

***Why two events, not one.*** The tenday and the confrontation are different kinds of work. The tenday is accumulation — evidence building over days, each beat adding to a picture that only becomes complete at the end. The confrontation is decision — what the party does with the picture they built. Keeping them in one file would require the party to navigate both tracks simultaneously, which obscures the different skills each demands. Splitting at Day 7 (or whenever Azuredge speaks) preserves the building-tension structure of the surveillance and gives the confrontation its own dramatic weight as a distinct scene.

## Azuredge as the Mission's Heart

***The sentient weapon mechanic.*** Azuredge is the most important element of this mission, not Meloon. Meloon is the problem. Azuredge is the moral architecture: a weapon that has been fighting its user's possession for tendays, that has prevented worse things from happening, that cannot do this alone and knows it. When it communicates — three emotional impressions, not words — it is not asking the party to complete a task. It is asking them to finish something it started.

The DC 14 + question variant (vs. DC 18 without) is the mission rewarding players who think to ask the axe a question rather than just rolling to understand it. Addressing an inanimate object that might be alive is the kind of thing players either do instinctively or never think to do. The lower DC rewards the instinct.

***Inspirations for playing Azuredge.*** Azuredge communicates entirely through emotional impression. It has no voice. DMs playing this beat should think of the way a dog communicates urgency without words — through body language, persistence, and the quality of its attention. The three impressions are carefully ordered: concern first (drawing attention to the problem), frustration second (establishing that the axe has already tried and failed), hope last (naming why the party is different). This sequence has an argument embedded in it.

## The Consequence of Failure

***The inconclusive path is fully designed, not punitive.*** The party that reports without specifics does not fail the mission — they fail to end the devourer's operation. The consequence is calibrated: Mission 4 is harder, but not impossible. The devourer having identified the party is an increase in existing threat, not a new threat. This matters because some parties will genuinely not be able to confirm the possession clearly in ten days, and the mission should remain completable at increased cost rather than bricked.

***Why this connects to Mission 4.*** Nihiloor's network is the thematic spine of Force Grey's mission arc. Mission 3 removes an observation asset; Mission 4 destroys the production facility. If the party failed Mission 3's confirmation step, Nihiloor has time to prepare for Mission 4. This is one of the few places in the faction mission arc where the sequence creates mechanical stakes for performance — a direct consequence of how well the party handled the surveillance.
