# The Tenday Watch

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> This Exploration Event begins when Vajra's *Sending* assigns the surveillance mission. In this Event, the party can:
>
> - Receive Vajra's twenty-five-word *Sending* and establish their observation post at the Yawning Portal.
> - Watch Meloon's table over a tenday, with three named beats surfacing the possession across Days 1, 3, 5, and 7.
> - Interpret Azuredge's direct communication on any morning the party addresses the axe by name during the Day 3 ritual.
> - Approach Meloon in conversation on Day 5 and notice the tells that confirm something is steering him.
> - Confirm the possession by Day 7 through magic, behavioral tracking, or both.
>
> #### The Observation
>
> Meloon Wardragon is a Force Grey veteran and a Yawning Portal regular. Official Force Grey surveillance changes his behavior immediately. The party is Vajra's only option for unmarked observation because they are new Gray Hands rather than established agents, and the devourer occupying Meloon does not yet know their faces.
>
> Day 3 (the morning ritual) requires a character to position themselves in the gallery above Meloon's room before dawn. The party can learn of this routine by watching him leave on Day 1 and Day 2, or by asking Durnan, who will confirm the ritual with a single nod without elaborating.
>
> Day 5 (conversation) requires the party to approach Meloon. This is permitted by Vajra's brief — she said not to approach unless necessary, and the phrase "unless necessary" is doing work here. A direct conversation surfaces the cataloguing tells more clearly than passive observation alone.
>
> Azuredge can communicate with the party on any morning of the tenday when a character addresses the axe by name during the morning ritual. The axe does not speak. It delivers emotional impressions: three of them, in sequence, with complete certainty.
>
> Durnan knows what the party is here for. Vajra told him. He will not discuss it, acknowledge it in company, or give the party anything they haven't already earned. He watches.

### The Brief

Vajra does not visit. The *Sending* arrives in one party member's head, mid-morning, without announcement.

> [!readaloud]
> The voice arrives without preamble, landing in one party member's head mid-morning — quick, clipped, each word counted as if the sender had done the arithmetic and left herself no room for softening.
>
> > *"Force Grey operative Meloon Wardragon is behaving strangely. Observe him for a tenday. Do not approach him unless necessary. Report what you see to me."*
>
> The connection closes before there is any opportunity to respond, and no follow-up is coming.

### Day 1 — Arrival

The Yawning Portal at evening is what it always is: loud, warm, smelling of old wood and bad ale, with the well at its center and the darkness below it. Meloon's table is near the fire. Two chairs beside him sit empty.

> [!readaloud]
> Meloon Wardragon is at his usual table near the fire, a full cup in front of him and Azuredge propped against the bench at his side, facing the portal's mouth. Two chairs beside him are empty in the way that chairs are empty when people used to fill them automatically and then stopped, and no one remarked on it. His attention follows the door each time it opens, tracks who came in with whom, and settles only when nothing new has happened in the last several minutes. Durnan is behind the bar and glances toward you once when you come in, then looks back at his work.

> [!gamemaster]**Durnan**
>
> Durnan (Lawful Neutral, Illuskan human, he/him) knows what the party is here for. He will not acknowledge them in company. He is not cold toward them; he simply knows the value of maintaining the appearance that he has no idea who they are. If a party member approaches the bar and asks quietly whether Meloon has been acting strangely, Durnan pours a drink, sets it down, and says:
>
> > "New customers."
>
> He will not elaborate.

### Day 3 — The Morning Ritual

A character who positions themselves in the gallery above Meloon's room before dawn watches the ritual. He sits on the edge of the bed with Azuredge across his knees and stares at it. His lips move. No sound. This continues for several minutes, with the quality of a conversation that is not going the way either side wants. Then he straps the axe on and leaves.

> [!readaloud]
> In the grey light before dawn, Meloon is seated on the edge of his bed with Azuredge laid across his knees, his lips moving without sound as he looks at it. The axe holds its stillness with intent — oriented toward him, steady in a way that objects are not steady, present in a way that suggests it is attending to him just as much as he is attending to it — and there is a conversation happening here, and Meloon is the one losing it. After several minutes he stops, picks up the axe, straps it on, and leaves without speaking.

> [!exploration]**Observing the Ritual**
>
> Any character watching the morning ritual and making a successful **DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check** perceives the following:
>
> > The axe and the man are in genuine conflict. The axe is pressing for something. Meloon's refusal has the quality not of willpower but of something suppressing the articulation of that willpower — as if Meloon himself cannot choose to hear the axe, because something else is making that choice for him.

#### Azuredge Speaks

Azuredge reaches toward a lawful or divine-adjacent character. This can occur on any morning of the tenday when a character addresses the axe directly by name, during the ritual, while Meloon is still seated — either from the gallery above or after the party has earned a conversation with Meloon and directly asked to see the axe.

> [!exploration]**Azuredge**
>
> Addressing the axe by name — not Meloon, the axe — while it rests across his knees during the morning ritual opens a connection. The character must either make a successful **DC 18 Wisdom (Insight) check**, or make a **DC 14 check** while actively asking the axe a question ("Are you trying to tell us something?", "Can you hear me?", or anything that acknowledges the axe as a living thing).
>
> On a success, the character feels three things in sequence, with complete certainty, as pure emotional impression:

If the check succeeds, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
> The impression arrives in three parts, no voice and no words — only the certainty of each one, as if you have carried this knowledge for a long time without knowing it. The first is concern: something has been wrong with him for longer than you have been watching, and what is delivering this message has known it since the beginning and could not reach anyone until now. The second is frustration, the kind that comes from trying and failing for tendays without stopping, which would explain itself more precisely if it had words available to use. The third is hope, and it is aimed directly at you.

> [!gamemaster]**Azuredge: Playing the Beat**
>
> The axe has no voice. It communicates the way a dog communicates urgency — by fixing on whoever is addressing it, holding that orientation, and making clear it will not stop. The three impressions arrive in that exact sequence: concern first (drawing attention to the problem), frustration second (establishing that the axe has already tried and failed), and hope last (naming why the party is different). The sequence contains an argument. When the DM plays this beat, let there be a pause between each impression, long enough for the player to sit with one before the next arrives.
>
> This beat transforms the surveillance mission into something the party was not assigned. Can they do something about what they found?

### Day 5 — Conversation

If the party approaches Meloon in conversation, he warms slightly toward adventurers and asks questions with apparent genuine interest. Something is wrong with the questions. They go in the wrong direction.

> [!readaloud]
> Meloon brightens when you sit down with him and gestures to the empty chairs as if he expected you — the handshake is bone-crushing, at least, and that part has not changed. He orders a round without being asked, pays without looking at the coin, and leans back with the ease of a man who is glad to have company. He asks what you have been working on, listens carefully to the answer, and then asks two or three follow-up questions for every one you put to him. The questions keep coming, each more specific than the last — which wards you spend time in, who else is working nearby, whether the Watch has been paying attention to your movements.

> [!social]**The Observation Post**
>
> Meloon Wardragon (Neutral Evil [possessed], Human, he/him) :: Force Grey veteran; warm and familiar on the surface, with an intellect devourer watching from behind his eyes.
>
> The devourer has been doing this for three tendays. It has Meloon's surface warmth and his easy manner with strangers. What it lacks is the sense of what conversation is *for*. Meloon used to ask questions because he was interested in people. Now he asks questions because he needs information, and the questions are cataloguing ones: who else is working this area, what are their names, have they had any trouble with the Watch, which wards have they been spending time in.
>
> His responses to the party's own questions lag — a beat too long between their words and his, as if something else is deciding what Meloon would say.
>
> He refers to himself in the third person when he is not paying attention. "Meloon would usually know a place like that." "Meloon used to drink here more often." He does not seem to notice he has done it.
>
> He orders ale and does not touch it. He used to drink everything he ordered.
>
> The party may notice any of this with the check below.
>
> Conversation topics Meloon is willing to engage on:
> - The party's recent work, with more interest than the warmth alone explains.
> - Waterdeep's wards and which ones the party frequents.
> - Force Grey and its current operations (he steers toward this).
> - Undermountain, about which he asks directions that are oddly precise.
>
> Specific dialogue for Meloon on these topics is presented below.

> [!exploration]**The Tell**
>
> Any character who makes a successful **DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check** during the conversation detects the fundamental wrongness: the lag before responses, the cataloguing direction of the questions, the third-person slip. A character who has already observed the Day 3 ritual and made the Insight check there has Advantage on this check.
>
> > The interest is real. But its direction is wrong. He is not asking about your work because he wants to know about you — he is filing the answers somewhere, sorting them, and moving to the next question before the last one is fully out of your mouth. Whatever is inside Meloon is building a picture, and you are part of it.

> [!qna]**Your recent work?**
>
> He leans forward, forearms on the table, and gives the question his full attention.
>
> > "What have you been working on lately? Anything down in the Dock Ward?" A pause — a beat longer than it should be. "Meloon has heard there are interesting things happening down there. Which streets, mostly?"

> [!qna]**About Azuredge?**
>
> He reaches back and pats the axe at his shoulder with the performance of easy familiarity.
>
> > "Oh, she's fine. She's right here." He does not draw it. His hand comes away quickly. "Meloon keeps her close. Best to keep her sheathed in a place like this, yes?"

> [!qna]**About Undermountain?**
>
> His interest sharpens — not quite Meloon's usual open enthusiasm, but something more focused underneath.
>
> > "Going down? Interesting. Which entrance are you planning to use? The well here, or the sewers? Meloon knows the sewer routes — there's a junction off Dock Ward, three tunnels in, that gets you down faster. The Watch doesn't patrol it. Which way were you thinking?"

> [!gamemaster]**What Meloon Does Not Do**
>
> He does not swear. The real Meloon drops "fuck" and "shit" into conversation the way other people use punctuation. Three tendays of possession and not one. If a party member who knows Meloon from Act I or Trollskull Alley notices this and asks about it (either then or in their report to Vajra), it is the clearest single tell available and earns Advantage on the Day 7 confirmation checks.

### Day 7 — Confirmation

By Day 7, the behavioral record is complete and magical confirmation is available. Any one of the following confirms the possession.

> [!exploration]**Confirming the Possession**
>
> Three methods are available:
>
> - *Detect evil and good* reveals an aberrant presence in Meloon.
> - *Detect magic* reveals Meloon himself as a magical source — the devourer's presence registers as a faint, persistent aura of transmutation.
> - A character who has been tracking the behavioral tells since Day 1 and Day 3 gains Advantage on any subsequent **DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana or Medicine) check** to identify the specific signature of intellect devourer occupation.
>
> Any one of these, combined with the behavioral documentation from earlier days, gives Vajra what she needs to act.

> [!gamemaster]**What the Party Now Knows**
>
> By Day 7, the party has: Meloon's behavioral changes documented across multiple days, Azuredge's direct communication if they pursued it, and one or more magical confirmations available. They have everything Vajra needs. Proceed to the **Azuredge Confrontation** Event.

### Concluding the Event

The tenday ends. The party's record of what they observed is their primary asset. Proceed to the **Azuredge Confrontation** Event to decide what happens with it.

> [!gamemaster]**Event Outcomes**
> Mark each outcome that occurs. Later events read them.
>
> - **Possession Confirmed** — mark when the party has at least one behavioral beat (from any Day) and at least one magical confirmation from Day 7; read by **Azuredge Confrontation** and Mission 4 ev-01.
> - **Azuredge Contact Made** — mark when a party member successfully receives Azuredge's emotional impressions; read by **Azuredge Confrontation**.
> - **Swearing Absence Noted** — mark when the party records Meloon's missing profanity as evidence; read by **Azuredge Confrontation** (grants Advantage on the Vajra specificity check).
> - **Mission 4 Alert** — mark when the tenday ends without **Possession Confirmed** being marked; read by Mission 4 ev-01 (the lair's alert state is higher).

> [!gamemaster]**Next Steps**
>
> The tenday ends. Proceed to the **Azuredge Confrontation** Event. This mission awards no Milestone Point.

## Overview

Vajra assigns the party a tenday of surveillance on Force Grey veteran Meloon Wardragon at the Yawning Portal. Something is wrong with him, and she cannot watch him herself.

## Summary

We watched Meloon Wardragon at the Yawning Portal for a tenday. He sat with the focused attention of something keeping a record, asked cataloguing questions about the wards and our work, and referred to himself in the third person when he wasn't paying attention. He never swore once. On Day 3, his axe Azuredge reached out to one of us during the morning ritual — three impressions, no words: something is wrong with him, I cannot reach him, you can. By Day 7, a magical check confirmed it: an aberrant presence, occupying the space behind his eyes.
