# Consulting Hlam

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> This Social/Exploration Event begins when the party receives Vajra's *Sending* and decides to climb Mount Waterdeep. In this Event, the party can:
>
> - Receive Vajra's twenty-five-word *Sending* and make the ascent.
> - Arrive at Hlam's cave after a genuine climb that costs something if they rush it.
> - Earn Hlam's first answer through directness or a successful DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) check.
> - Earn his second answer — naming Manshoon — through patience or a DC 15 check.
> - Receive an unprompted third message about something buried beneath the city that is waking.
> - Return to Blackstaff Tower and deliver both pieces to Vajra.
>
> #### Social Mechanics
>
> Hlam's cooperation is not automatic. What he responds to: directness, honesty, and the acknowledgment that the party does not actually know what they are asking for and is here anyway. What imposes Disadvantage on any check with him: appeals to authority, urgency framing ("the Blackstaff needs this immediately"), or flattery. A character who states the situation plainly — "We work for someone who needs to know what threats are moving against the city" — rolls straight. A character who specifies Force Grey by name, having been told not to, earns a long silence and starts over.
>
> The unprompted message fires regardless of how the checks go. Hlam delivers it because he has decided to, not because the party earned it. The mission's most important content cannot be missed, only under-contextualized.

### The Brief

The *Sending* arrives in one party member's head mid-morning, without announcement.

> [!readaloud]
> The voice arrives without preamble — the same woman's voice from the *Sending* that brought you into Force Grey, quick and clipped, each word chosen carefully.
>
> > *"Hlam is a monk on Mount Waterdeep's western slope. Seek him out and ask what threats he has perceived. Don't mention my name. Don't overstay."*

There is no follow-up and no opportunity to ask questions. The *Sending* is the whole brief.

### The Climb

The path up Mount Waterdeep's western slope begins in a maintained city park and becomes unmarked above the treeline. The harbor falls away below, then the Sea Ward roofline, then the horizon. The path narrows twice and disappears entirely once, requiring a short scramble across bare stone.

> [!exploration]**The Ascent**
>
> Any character who climbs without taking extra care must make a **DC 12 Constitution saving throw** or arrive at the cave with one level of Exhaustion. Taking extra time to pace the ascent grants Advantage on the saving throw. A character with a climbing speed or Expertise in Athletics who actively assists others grants them automatic success.
>
> The Exhaustion is not punitive — it marks the effort as real.

The cave is visible only from above it, a narrow fissure in the western face with a view that makes everything else small.

### Hlam

He is seated in meditation, facing the harbor. He heard the party much earlier than his stillness suggests. He opens his eyes when they arrive.

> [!readaloud]
> An old man is seated at the mouth of a narrow cave in the rock face, cross-legged and facing the harbor far below. He does not turn when you arrive. He opens his eyes. Old in a way that resists any particular estimate — his clothes match the stone, and he looks at each of you once, briefly and completely, and returns his gaze to the harbor.

> [!social]**The Mountain Monk**
>
> Hlam (Lawful Good, Calishite human, he/him) :: a hermit monk of indeterminate age who perceives threats through sustained attention that most people never achieve.
>
> Hlam says almost nothing by way of greeting. He answers questions with questions when he thinks the party is asking the wrong thing, and he waits out silences with more patience than the party is likely to have. His dry humour surfaces in a short, soft observation and then goes, without any signal that it was meant to be funny. He does not explain his riddles, and he will not repeat an answer.
>
> He speaks softly, unhurriedly, in complete sentences, with long pauses between them. The pauses are not hesitation. They are Hlam deciding whether to continue.
>
> Conversation topics Hlam is willing to engage on:
> - What threats he has perceived moving against the city — one piece at a time.
> - Whether the party is prepared to sit with an answer before pressing for another.
>
> Specific dialogue for Hlam on these topics is presented below.

> [!qna]**What threats have you perceived?**
>
> He looks at whoever asks for a long moment before answering. A **DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) check** is required unless the character states the situation with plain honesty ("We work for someone who needs to know what is moving against this city"), in which case no check is required.
>
> On a success or plain honesty:
>
> > "Evil's twin hides its face for now. Expect that to change before winter's end."
>
> He will not elaborate if asked directly. On a failure, he asks a question back: "Why did you come up here?" A character who answers honestly on that second attempt may roll again with no penalty.

> [!qna]**What do you mean? / What is coming?**
>
> Pressing for the second answer requires either a **DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion) check** or genuine patience — a character who stops asking and simply sits with the first answer earns the same result as a character who passes the check.
>
> If the check succeeds or the party waits in silence:
>
> > "An archmage thought dead has returned. He is rebuilding something. The shape of it is familiar to anyone who remembers what this city was before it had to be rebuilt."
>
> He means Manshoon. He is choosing what to say with characteristic precision, and he will not confirm the name if pressed.

### The Unprompted Message

As the party prepares to go — Hlam returns his gaze to the harbor when the meeting is over, which is the indication — he says one more thing, not in response to any question.

> [!readaloud]
> Hlam has turned back to face the harbor when he speaks, his voice carrying back without any effort.
>
> > "The Blackstaff carries more than she knows. Not the staff — she knows what the staff is. The city. She does not know what is buried beneath it, and the buried thing is waking."
>
> He turns his head slightly, though not enough to look back at the party.
>
> > "Tell her that from me. She will understand the half of it that matters."

The Stone of Golorr's abolethic resonance has been faint but consistent for tendays. Hlam's sustained attention has caught it. This is the party's first thread toward the Stone and the Vault of Dragons.

### The Report

The party returns to Blackstaff Tower with two pieces of intelligence. Vajra is at her standing desk. She hears the Manshoon warning first — "straightforward," she says, and makes a note. Then the unprompted message.

> [!readaloud]
> When you finish the second message, Vajra sets her pen down and goes still for a moment.
>
> > "Repeat it."
>
> When you do, she picks up a fresh page and writes it down, word for word.
>
> > "Thank you." A pause. "This doesn't leave the Tower."

She does not explain what she thinks it means. She will work that out on her own.

### Concluding the Event

> [!gamemaster]**Next Steps**
>
> Award **+1 Renown** when Hlam's two-part answer about Manshoon is delivered to Vajra accurately.
>
> Award **+1 Renown** when the unprompted message about the buried thing is delivered verbatim. Vajra asks the party to repeat it once and writes it down.
>
> Vajra now knows two things she did not know before: Manshoon's Splinter is actively rebuilding its presence in Waterdeep, and something old below the city is stirring. The second piece she files separately from the first. She does not yet know they are connected. The party can prompt the connection, or let her work it out.
>
> **Force Grey Mission 2 — The Dragon in the Harbor** becomes available when the party reaches Renown 2 and 3rd level.

## Overview

Vajra sends the party up Mount Waterdeep to consult a reclusive monk named Hlam about threats he has perceived. The climb is real. Hlam is not impressed by urgency or authority, and his cooperation is earned through patience and honesty rather than rank. With the right approach, he gives the party a warning about Manshoon's return and, unprompted, something more: a description of the Stone of Golorr's resonance that neither he nor the party can yet name.

## Summary

Vajra sent the party to consult Hlam, a monk living in a cave on Mount Waterdeep's western slope. He confirmed that a dead archmage has returned and is rebuilding in Waterdeep. Before they left, he added something he had not been asked: something buried beneath the city is waking. Vajra heard both pieces, asked the party to repeat the second one, and wrote it down.
