# The Theater's Back Room

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> This Social and Exploration Event begins when Krebbyg Masq'il'yr briefs an individual Bregan D'aerthe member in the booking office of the Seven Masks Theater, and it ends the next morning when the member receives the key to the dressing room. It runs while the Faire is in Deepwater Harbor, before the Faire sails on Tarsakh 20, and only when **Windmill Raided** is not marked. If **Windmill Raided** is marked, run **The Debrief** and skip this Event. Only Bregan D'aerthe members receive the brief and meet Brimel in the doorway. Their companions can sit in the house and help with every other part of the mission. In this Event, the party can:
>
> - Take Krebbyg's brief and learn that a frightened Cassalanter steward is selling a floor plan of a windmill, and that the family has set a watcher on him.
> - Pick Florette Cressyn out of the lobby crowd, or meet her late.
> - Keep Florette away from Brimel by one of four approaches, each with its own steps and its own way to go wrong.
> - Make the handoff in section C during the first number, with the grey coat on peg seven as a fallback.
> - Lose Florette in the lobby crush or on Net Street if she saw the pass.
> - Meet Brimel in a doorway, pay him, and hear what he knows about the windmill.
> - Read the plan with Krebbyg, and receive 200 gp and the key to the dressing room the next morning.

> [!gamemaster]**Who Knows What**
>
> Brimel Crestfall is a household steward in the Cassalanter villa. For years he has carried baskets of candles, salt, black wax and wine to a converted windmill on Coachlamp Lane in the Southern Ward, as far as the second landing of its stair, and the door at the top opens only to the two people who keep the rooms. The family pays the rent on that floor in the name of a Marra Selby and leaves the windmill out of the house books. Lord Victoro once told him it would be needed "for the resolution of the family's obligation," and Madam Ammalia goes out there alone, twice a month, in a hired cab with the curtains drawn. Brimel does not know what the family does there. He does not know that the family is anything but rich, pious and generous, and he takes the "obligation" for a debt. Nobody in this Event can learn more than suspicion.
>
> Brimel is not a man of conscience. After *The Waterdeep Wazoo* printed its piece and two "solicitors" called on the publisher, Lord Victoro's butler, Mr. Crowelle, began asking every servant who they speak to. Brimel believes the family will dismiss whoever it can blame, and he took 300 gp from Bregan D'aerthe, through the household's wine merchant, to draw a floor plan of the windmill from memory. He has been paid 150 gp. The other 150 gp is in the envelope Krebbyg gives the member, and Brimel wants the money to leave Waterdeep.
>
> Florette Cressyn is a household investigator on Ammalia Cassalanter's retainer and not a servant, and she reports to Ammalia directly. She has followed Brimel for ten days. She has no evidence and no idea what the windmill is for, and she wants a reason to pull him in before he hands anything over. She will report any pass she sees.
>
> The plan is a single folded sheet in Brimel's neat hand. It shows the ground-floor sail room, the shared stair, the middle floors let to ordinary tenants, the apartment at the top of the stair and a back room off that apartment marked with a small cross and the word "locked." On the back he has pencilled that he once saw a map of the City of the Dead on the desk in that room with one mausoleum ringed in red ink and "Brandath" written beside it. That is the Brandath Crypt, and it is the approach to the vault. Brimel does not know what it means.
>
> The windmill is occupied by Seffia Naelryke and Arn Xalrondar, who are cult operatives. The party does not meet them in this Event, and nobody here knows what they are.
>
> No Bregan D'aerthe speaker says "Jarlaxle" in this Event. Krebbyg says "the captain" or "J." If **Jarlaxle Unmasked** is marked for a member, Krebbyg may say the name once, in a low voice, when he reads the plan, and Fel'rekt would tell him to wait until they are out of the lobby. Zardoz Zord is Jarlaxle Baenre, and he does not speak in this Event. He is in the box because this is the first mission that runs under his name, and he keeps silent because a Cassalanter watcher in the house could hear him greet the party and learn that the Faire's captain is involved.

> [!gamemaster]**Before You Run This**
>
> If **Zardoz Introduced** is marked for the member, Zardoz sits in the owner's box above stage right for the whole showcase. If it is not marked, the box is empty with a plumed hat on its rail, and Krebbyg says only that the captain sends regards. If **Kreb Unmasked** is not marked for the member, Krebbyg stays in his Kreb Sorrush cover through the brief, behind a closed door, and the member treats him as human.
>

### The Brief

A playbill with a red 6 circled on it lies under the door of Trollskull Manor that morning, and it means the stage door at six bells. The matinee crowd is long gone. Krebbyg takes the member through the passage behind the stage to the booking office and locks the door. Companions are not on the playbill and wait outside the stage door, and Krebbyg gives each member a ticket for every companion the member names.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The stage door opens before you knock, and a slim young drow in a velvet waistcoat waves you through a passage hung with painted flats and coils of rope. In the booking office a brown wig hangs on a hook beside a schedule with tonight's showcase circled twice in red pencil, and a tray of tea has gone cold on the desk. He locks the door behind you and drops into the chair with a playbill in one hand and a sealed envelope in the other.
>
> > "Right, darling, listen, because I'll say this once and then Fel will say it again slower, and I'll have to hear it twice. There's a man called Brimel Crestfall, a steward in the Cassalanter house, and he has a floor plan of a windmill on Coachlamp Lane in the Southern Ward that the family keeps off every list and calendar it owns. He's selling it because he's shitting himself since your newspaper piece, and the captain wants it before he changes his mind."

If the member marked **Cassalanter Reaction Observed**, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
>
> Krebbyg flips the playbill over and taps the back of it with the envelope.
>
> > "You saw those two so-called solicitors yourselves, the ones who leaned on the printer, and they've been leaning on the Cassalanter staff ever since, one footman at a time. Our wine merchant says nobody in that house will say good morning to anybody anymore."

If the member did not mark **Cassalanter Reaction Observed**, read or paraphrase the following instead:

> [!readaloud]
>
> Krebbyg flips the playbill over and taps the back of it with the envelope.
>
> > "After your newspaper piece came out, two men calling themselves solicitors went round to lean on the printer, and nobody knows whose they were, though I've got a guess. The house has been leaning on its own staff ever since, one footman at a time, and our wine merchant says nobody in there will say good morning to anybody anymore."

Then read the following aloud:

> [!readaloud]
>
> He tears a ticket stub in half and lays the two pieces side by side on the desk, and he starts talking before he has finished tearing.
>
> > "He'll be in section C, row four, on the aisle, at nine bells tonight, which is the Faire showcase, and the first number's a shanty with forty tumblers stamping on the boards, so nobody hears a word of anything. One of you sits in the seat next to his and says the soprano's better in the second act. He says only if she remembers the words, and then he gives you a folded paper and doesn't look at you again. Anyone else who wants that paper is a problem, and there is one, because the family's put a watcher on him."

> [!social]**The Stage Manager with the Envelope**
>
> Krebbyg Masq'il'yr (Chaotic Neutral, Drow, he/him) :: a young drow gunslinger who runs the Seven Masks' bookings as the human Kreb Sorrush, and who has the wig off for the first time in a working day.
>
> Krebbyg talks in rapid run-ons and checks the schedule in the middle of a sentence. He calls the member "darling," swears constantly and cheerfully, and answers anything he cannot settle with "Ask Fel." Fel'rekt is out on the captain's business and not at the theater tonight, so Krebbyg has to remember everything himself and says so. He is proud of the plan and nervous about the watcher, and he will complain about the ghost at least once.
>
> Conversation topics Krebbyg is willing to discuss include:
>
> - Brimel Crestfall, whom he has never met and whom the household's wine merchant describes.
> - The watcher, whose face he does not know.
> - The phrase, the seat and the grey coat on peg seven.
> - What the captain gets out of the plan, which he answers with as little as he can manage.
> - The pay.
>
> Specific dialogue for Krebbyg on these topics is presented below.

> [!qna]**Who is Brimel?**
>
> Krebbyg checks the schedule before he answers.
>
> > "He's about forty-five, bald on top, in a steward's black coat, with a gold watch chain he keeps touching when he's scared, which is always. Our wine merchant says he's competent and quiet and wants to leave town, and honestly I can't blame him, because I've seen that house from the street and it gives me a feeling."

> [!qna]**What about the watcher?**
>
> > "A woman, about thirty, Fel says, with a long brown braid under a grey hat and a book she never turns a page of. But half the women in this city own a grey hat, darling, so don't go dragging the first one you see out of the lobby. She'll be watching Brimel and not the stage, and that's the only thing I'm sure of."

> [!qna]**Why is he selling this?**
>
> > "Money, mostly, and fear. He's got three hundred gold coming from us, a hundred and fifty in his pocket since last tenday, and the rest is in this envelope, which you hand him after the handoff and not before, because Fel says men who get paid first don't turn up. I wouldn't ask him about his conscience if I were you. It's not what he came for."

> [!qna]**What if the handoff goes wrong?**
>
> > "If nobody speaks to him by the end of the first number, he'll leave the plan in the left pocket of the grey coat on peg seven in the cloakroom at the interval, the same coat you've used for our messages. Somebody can walk in and collect it, and then you'll want to be somewhere else when the watcher goes looking."

> [!qna]**Anything else?**
>
> > "The captain's in the box tonight, above stage right, and he won't say a single word to you, darling, so don't wave at him in the lobby and don't be hurt when he doesn't wave back. I swear on the ghost it's nothing personal. There's a rack of usher's coats in the wardrobe if you want to walk about without being looked at, and take whatever you like."

> [!qna]**What do we get?**
>
> > "Two hundred gold between you, whenever it's done, and a very pleased captain, which is better than gold, I've found, though it doesn't pay for the soprano's wine. Come back and tell me when you've got the paper."

The wardrobe holds five usher's coats in the Seven Masks' maroon and gold. A member who wears one has Advantage on the check in **The Double-Booked Seat**. Krebbyg gives the member the envelope with 150 gp inside, a ticket for the member and each companion in rows C and D, and no other instruction. If the party has not found the watcher by the end of the hour, he says the soprano has taken his best wig and goes to look for it.

### The Lobby

The showcase opens its doors at eight bells. The lobby of the Seven Masks fills with Faire fans in good coats, tumblers in half-painted costumes and children carrying paper ships on sticks. Florette Cressyn arrives at a quarter past eight and waits in the lobby for Brimel. Brimel arrives at half past eight and goes straight to his seat in row four of section C, and Florette follows him into the house and takes a seat in row six at twenty-five to nine. The party has from eight bells until half past eight to find her in the lobby and use **The Double-Booked Seat** or **The Lifted Stub**. After that it has until five to nine to use **The Fire Aisle** or **The Service Corridor**.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The lobby of the Seven Masks smells of hot wax, greasepaint and fried dough from a stall by the door, and a crowd in their good coats presses toward the doors of the house while two ushers in maroon and gold stamp tickets as fast as they can. A tumbler in half a fish costume juggles three lanterns by the cloakroom, and at the foot of the gallery stair a translucent figure in a long director's coat is shifting a pile of programs from one step to the next. Beyond him a slim young man in a velvet waistcoat shouts at a stagehand about a painted castle.

> [!exploration]**Finding the Watcher**
>
> Any character who scans the lobby and the doors of the house before half past eight and makes a successful DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check picks out Florette Cressyn, a woman of about thirty in a grey hat, with a long brown braid, who holds a closed book and watches the front doors instead of the stage. Each character can try once before Brimel arrives.
>
> - **Advantage:** the character watches the front doors until Brimel comes in and sees who turns to follow him.
> - **Auto:** the character has greeted Malcolm Brizzenbright by name, politely, and listens to what he says (see **The Ghost at the Stair**).
> - **Late:** if nobody has found her, a member seated next to Brimel notices at a quarter to nine, without a check, that he has glanced back twice at the same woman in row six. **The Double-Booked Seat** and **The Lifted Stub** are closed by then. **The Fire Aisle** and **The Service Corridor** are open until five to nine.

> [!exploration]**The Ghost at the Stair**
>
> Malcolm Brizzenbright is moving programs at the foot of the gallery stair, and he is visible to anyone who speaks to him. He treats the party as the rest of the theater's staff do, with a complaint, and he warms to anyone who greets him by name and does not talk down to him. A character who does so hears the following:
>
> > "Finally, someone who greets the house like a person! You'll want to know about the woman in the grey hat, my darling, because she bought a ticket and hasn't looked at the stage once, which is the rudest thing a patron can do. She stood by the doors for ten minutes counting the exits, and she's got a book she hasn't opened, and nobody reads that way. I've seen her type before, and they're never here for the music."
>
> Malcolm does not know who she works for or what she wants. He says "my stage" at least once and complains about the new management by the second sentence, and he tells anyone who asks that "Rongquan Mystere" is not who he claims. A character who asks about the Faire hears that the new management talks about nothing else, and that he knows nothing more.

### Four Ways to Keep Her Off Brimel

Florette is a professional with a plain, courteous manner, and she does not leave her post for a stranger's convenience. The party needs only one clean approach to leave her with nothing to report, and each one has a failure that costs the party something without ending the mission.

> [!gamemaster]**Florette's States**
>
> Florette is always in one of three states, and the approaches below move her between them.
>
> - **Watching:** she sits in row six with a clear line to Brimel. This is her state from five to nine unless an approach changes it.
> - **Occupied:** an approach has moved her out of the house or out of her sightline until the interval, and she cannot see the pass.
> - **Noticed:** she saw a member's face during a failed approach or a failed pass. Track it by the member's name. It lasts for the evening and can be combined with Watching or Occupied.
>
> She follows Brimel at the interval if she saw the pass or has Noticed any member. She reports only if she follows him and the party fails both **The Interval Crush** and **The Street Intercept**, or after **The Service Corridor**.

> [!social]**The Watcher in the Grey Hat**
>
> Florette Cressyn (Lawful Neutral, Waterdhavian Human, she/her) :: a household investigator on Ammalia Cassalanter's retainer, thirty and patient, who has watched Brimel for ten days and has nothing yet.
>
> Florette is courteous, precise and cold in a way she keeps polite. She speaks in complete sentences, asks one probing question where another person would ask three, and does not swear. She never raises her voice and never shows impatience. She does not know why the family wants Brimel watched, only that Madam Ammalia has asked for a reason to act, and she would sooner have a solid reason than a hunch. She will not discuss the Cassalanters with a stranger.
>
> Any character who speaks to her for a minute and makes a successful DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check determines that she is not afraid of anything in the lobby and that she is waiting for something specific, and that she would leave her post for any clear and plausible reason that does not look like an attempt to move her.
>
> Conversation topics Florette is willing to discuss include:
>
> - The showcase, which she says she came to see.
> - Her seat, which she has chosen carefully.
> - Whoever is asking, about whom she asks one question before she answers anything.
>
> Specific dialogue for Florette on these topics is presented below.

> [!qna]**Are you enjoying the show?**
>
> Florette looks at you for a moment before she answers.
>
> > "I haven't seen it yet, and I'd like to, so I'd be grateful if you'd let me find my seat. May I ask whether you work for the theater, or whether you've come to see the Faire as well?"

> [!qna]**Who are you here with?**
>
> > "A friend, who will be along shortly. Forgive me, but I don't believe we've met, and I find in a crowd that the people who ask where you've come from are never the people who intend to say where they've come from themselves."

> [!qna]**Your ticket is wrong.**
>
> She holds the stub between two fingers and examines it from both sides.
>
> > "It was issued this afternoon at the box office, with the date stamped on it, so I don't think there's any error. If there is, I'll ask the manager myself. Who did you say you were?"

#### A: The Double-Booked Seat

The party can persuade Florette that her seat has been sold twice and walk her to the box-office window, where Kreb Sorrush, in his human cover, finds her another. The upper gallery is two flights up at the far end of the house, from which section C is a row of small heads under the lamps. She can see the stage and she cannot see anybody's hands.

> [!exploration]**The Double-Booked Seat**
>
> Any character in an usher's coat who tells Florette in the lobby between a quarter past eight and half past eight that her seat has been sold twice, and makes a successful DC 14 Charisma (Deception) check, persuades her to follow to the box-office window. The character has Advantage if they wear a coat from the wardrobe and Disadvantage if she has Noticed them before.
>
> - **On a success,** Kreb Sorrush, flustered and apologetic, writes her a new stub for the upper gallery and sends her up with a bow. She is **Occupied** until the interval and sees nothing at the first number.
> - **On a failure by 4 or less,** Florette asks to see the manager, and Kreb loses her stub and the argument eats ten minutes. She is **Noticed**. She takes her seat in row six at five to nine, and **The Lifted Stub** is closed.
> - **On a failure by 5 or more,** she says "I'll keep my seat, thank you," holds her ground and asks the character's name. She is **Noticed** and watches the usher for the rest of the evening, and both **The Double-Booked Seat** and **The Lifted Stub** are closed.

#### B: The Lifted Stub

An usher at the door of section C will not admit anyone without a stub, and the box office reissues one only after Kreb has checked the book, which takes ten minutes. A party that takes Florette's stub from her coat in the lobby crush keeps her out of the house until the first number is over.

> [!exploration]**The Lifted Stub**
>
> Any character who passes close to Florette in the lobby crush between a quarter past eight and half past eight and makes a successful DC 14 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check lifts her stub from her coat pocket. The character has Advantage if the crush is thick, which it is from half past eight until the house doors open, and Disadvantage if she has Noticed them.
>
> - **On a success,** the usher at section C's door will not admit her, and Kreb Sorrush takes ten minutes over the book. She is **Occupied** and does not reach the house until after the first number.
> - **On a failure by 4 or less,** she feels the tug and turns. A character who makes a successful DC 12 Charisma (Deception) check says that she dropped it and hands it back, and she thanks them with a long look. She is **Noticed**.
> - **On a failure by 5 or more, or a failed DC 12 Charisma (Deception) check,** she grips the character's wrist and calls for the Watch. Constable Pimm arrives at once, Captain Dalloway a minute later, and Kreb Sorrush vouches for the character as a member of his staff within the quarter hour. She is **Noticed**, and the party loses the bonus for keeping the Watch out of it.

#### C: The Fire Aisle

Captain Ilsa Dalloway has the stage-left exit, and Constable Pimm has the aisle. A showcase of this size draws pickpockets, so they patrol the house until the first number. The captain will move anyone who blocks a fire lane, and Florette will stand in a lane of her own choosing if she thinks it gives her a line of sight.

> [!social]**The Captain on Crowd Duty**
>
> Ilsa Dalloway (Lawful Neutral, Waterdhavian Human, she/her) :: the Dock Ward Watch captain on crowd duty at the Faire's showcase, practical, tired and polite.
>
> Captain Dalloway checks the house twice before each performance and cares more about exits than about anything on the stage. She knows Kreb Sorrush and likes his free tea. She does not like being told how to do her job, and she answers a member of the public who has a reason in plain words and a member who has a theory in none. Constable Harl Pimm stands at her shoulder with sore feet.
>
> Conversation topics Dalloway is willing to discuss include:
>
> - The exits and the fire lanes, which she keeps clear.
> - The crowds the Faire draws and the pickpockets they bring.
> - Kreb Sorrush, who she thinks is nervous and kind.

> [!qna]**Is the aisle clear?**
>
> Dalloway glances toward section C and then back at you.
>
> > "It's clear until somebody stands in it, and somebody always stands in it. If you've got a specific complaint, tell me which seat and which person, and I'll go and have a word, but I don't move people on a feeling."

> [!qna]**About Kreb Sorrush?**
>
> > "He's a nervous little man who gives out free tea and counts his tickets twice, and I've never had a moment's trouble in this house. Pimm likes him as well, and Pimm doesn't like anyone."

> [!exploration]**The Fire Aisle**
>
> Any character who finds Captain Dalloway between half past eight and five to nine, tells her that a woman in a grey hat is standing in the aisle of section C with a closed book and blocking the route to the stage-left exit, and makes a successful DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) check persuades her to move Florette to the standing rail at the back of the house. The character has Advantage if they first read the house plan Kreb has pinned beside the box office and make a successful DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) check, which shows that the aisle beside row six is the narrowest in the house.
>
> - **On a success,** Dalloway moves Florette to the standing rail, from which she can see the backs of heads and nothing else. She is **Occupied** until the interval.
> - **On a failure,** Dalloway walks over and asks Florette to move, and Florette declines politely and asks who sent the captain. The captain says "a young person in an usher's coat," and Florette marks that member as **Noticed**. **The Fire Aisle** is closed.

#### D: The Service Corridor

The party can take Florette by force, and it is the only approach that always ends in a report. She will wake and tell Ammalia she was jumped in the theater by an usher and two others. A party that does this has chosen to be seen, and Captain Dalloway is twenty feet from the corridor door.

> [!exploration]**The Service Corridor**
>
> A member in an usher's coat who tells Florette at any time before five to nine that there is a problem with her ticket and that the manager is in the stage-left corridor, and who makes a successful DC 12 Charisma (Deception) check, leads her through the service door at the side of the house. The characters who wait beside the door make a DC 13 Group Check using Dexterity (Stealth), so that Captain Dalloway at the stage-left exit does not see them move.
>
> - **On a success,** Florette is in the corridor with the door closed behind her, and the party can take her by surprise.
> - **On a failure of the first check,** Florette stops at the door and asks to see the manager in the lobby. The approach is closed, and she is **Noticed**.
> - **On a failed Group Check,** the captain sees movement at the service door, and she arrives at the start of the third round of any fight in the corridor.

> [!hazard]**The Corridor Scuffle**
>
> Florette uses the 2024 **Spy** statistics, with a shortsword and no poison, because she would not use the hand crossbow's poison in a crowded theater. She wants to reach the stage-left exit, where Captain Dalloway stands, and to stay alive until she does. The **Bregan D'aerthe Mechanics Reference** shows her alone as below Mild for three, four and five combatants.
>
> #### Florette Cressyn Tactics
> At the start of combat, Florette takes the Dodge action if she is surrounded, and Disengages with Cunning Action if she is not.
>
> During combat, Florette:
> - Runs for the stage-left exit and shouts for the Watch if she reaches the door.
> - Hits only to break a grapple or free a path.
> - Surrenders at 13 Hit Points or fewer and asks to be let go.
>
> A character who reduces her to 0 Hit Points with a melee attack can knock her out, and she wakes with 1 Hit Point after one hour. If the Group Check failed, Captain Dalloway (2024 **Guard Captain**) and Constable Pimm (2024 **Guard**) arrive from the stage-left exit at the start of the third round. The captain arrests any member holding a weapon and takes names. Kreb Sorrush vouches for the members within the hour, and they are released. If the Group Check succeeded, nobody comes and the fight runs its course.
>
> If Florette is knocked out or restrained, Kreb leaves her in the locked booking office with her purse and her book untouched, and she wakes at ten bells and walks out the stage door. She reports to Ammalia the next morning. Mark **Florette Reported** for the member she describes. If the captain arrives, Brimel sees the arrests, panics and leaves the plan in the grey coat on peg seven at the interval, and the handoff goes through the fallback.

### The First Number

The first number begins at nine bells. Forty tumblers in sailor's costume stamp across the boards of the Seven Masks while a chorus sings a shanty about the *Eyecatcher*, and the owner's box above stage right holds a figure in scarlet.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The house lights drop to a low amber, and forty tumblers in striped sailors' shirts stamp onto the boards in a line, rattling the lanterns hung from the ceiling beams. A chorus of voices from the pit takes up a shanty about a ship that never touches the shore, and the whole house begins to clap on the third beat. In the box above stage right, a man in scarlet sits alone with a plumed hat on the rail beside him and a glass in his hand, and he watches the stage without a word.

> [!social]**The Captain in the Box**
>
> Zardoz Zord (Chaotic Neutral, Drow in the guise of an Illuskan human, he/him) :: the flamboyant captain of the Sea Maidens Faire, who is the man at the top of Bregan D'aerthe and does not say a word tonight.
>
> Zardoz sits in the box with his hat on the rail and a glass in his hand, and his normal habit of filling every silence is gone. He claps along with the house. He does not wave, he does not call out and he does not offer a ticket. A character who looks at him sees a man enjoying himself, whose eyes keep returning to the aisle of section C between the verses. If **Zardoz Introduced** is not marked for the member, the box is empty and the hat sits on the rail alone.
>
> Any character who calls up to the box or tries to speak to him gets no sound back.

> [!qna]**Wave to him?**
>
> > Zardoz tips the plumed hat to the character, smiles, and goes back to the stage.

> [!qna]**Speak to him at the interval?**
>
> > He raises one finger to his lips, and the smile does not change.

> [!exploration]**Making the Pass**
>
> The member who sits in the seat next to Brimel's says the phrase during the chorus. Brimel answers "Only if she remembers the words," hands over a folded sheet under his program without looking, and does not look again. If the member does not say the phrase, or says it before the first number begins, he sits rigid and gives nothing, and the fallback below applies.
>
> - **Florette is Occupied:** the pass needs no check. She sees nothing.
> - **Florette is Watching from row six:** the member makes a DC 15 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check to take the sheet under cover of the dark and the singing. On a success, she sees two people lean toward each other and nothing more. On a failure, she sees the paper change hands and marks the member as **Noticed**.
> - **The fallback:** if the pass is missed, Brimel leaves the plan in the left pocket of the grey coat on peg seven at the interval. A member who walks into the cloakroom collects it without a check unless Florette is Watching, in which case it takes a DC 14 Dexterity (Stealth) check, and on a failure she sees the member take it and the party marks **Florette Reported**. Brimel walks straight home, the doorway meeting does not happen, and **The Street Intercept** does not apply.

### The Interval and the Street

The first half ends at ten bells. Brimel leaves by the side door in the first rush of the interval and walks to the sailmaker's doorway on Net Street. He is expected home by eleven bells.

Florette follows him if she saw the pass or has **Noticed** any member. If she is **Occupied** and has noticed nobody, she finds his seat empty and files only that he left early, which tells Ammalia nothing. The party can lose her in two places.

> [!exploration]**The Interval Crush**
>
> Any character who steps into the lobby crush between Florette and the side door and makes a successful DC 13 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check keeps her in the crowd for the minute Brimel needs. The character has Advantage if a second character uses the Help action or if Kreb Sorrush at the lobby bar is asked to start the interval act early.
>
> - **On a success,** Brimel is through the side door and gone before she reaches the street. She has seen nothing that can be reported.
> - **On a failure,** she slips through the crowd and follows him out. The party faces **The Street Intercept**.

> [!exploration]**The Street Intercept**
>
> Florette stays thirty feet behind Brimel on Net Street. A character who tries either of the following keeps her from seeing the sailmaker's doorway, and one success is enough.
>
> - **Talk.** Any character who stops her at the corner of Net Street and Dock Street and makes a successful DC 15 Charisma (Deception or Persuasion) check holds her in conversation or sends her down the wrong lane, and Brimel turns into the doorway unseen.
> - **Block.** Any character who tips a handcart of net floats across the lane and makes a successful DC 13 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check causes a noisy delay that costs her a minute, and she loses him in the crowd of the Faire's departing audience.
>
> On a failure of both, Florette watches Brimel enter the doorway and meet the member. She files her report the next morning and gives the description of the member who made the pass. Mark **Florette Reported**.

If Florette was kept off Brimel by the Interval Crush, the Street Intercept or one of the first three approaches, the party meets Brimel unobserved.

### The Doorway on Net Street

Brimel waits in the doorway of a shuttered sailmaker's on Net Street, two blocks from the theater, with his coat buttoned to the chin. He speaks quickly and asks the member to be fast.

> [!readaloud]
>
> A shuttered sailmaker's shop stands on Net Street with a deep arched doorway full of shadow and the smell of tar and canvas. A bald man in a steward's black coat stands inside it with one hand on a gold watch chain, and he steps back a pace when he sees you and then forward again with an embarrassed cough.
>
> > "Lathander keep me, is it done? I mean, you have it, don't you, and nobody followed? I've got to be back at the villa by eleven bells, and Mr. Crowelle counts the household at the door, and I don't think I could explain myself if I were late."

> [!social]**The Steward in the Doorway**
>
> Brimel Crestfall (Neutral, Waterdhavian Human, he/him) :: a household steward in his forties, competent, orderly and frightened, who sold a floor plan for coin and now wants to leave the city.
>
> Brimel talks in careful, over-explained sentences, hedges every answer and keeps one hand on his watch chain. He says "Lathander keep me" when he is afraid, which is often. He does not swear, and he does not pretend to be brave, because he knows he is not and says so readily. Any character who watches him and makes a successful DC 12 Wisdom (Insight) check determines that he is frightened for himself and for nobody else, and that he has decided to take the money and leave Waterdeep.
>
> Conversation topics Brimel is willing to discuss include:
>
> - The windmill, its stair and the baskets he carries there.
> - The family's private gatherings and Madam Ammalia's visits, which he reports and does not interpret.
> - Why he sold the plan and what he wants in return.
> - The woman who follows him, if he is asked.
>
> Specific dialogue for Brimel on these topics is presented below.

> [!qna]**About the windmill?**
>
> > "It's an old mill on Coachlamp Lane that someone made into rooms, sir, and the family rents the top floor and the sail room at the bottom, under the name of a Mistress Selby, who doesn't exist. I carry baskets of candles, black wax, salt and wine as far as the second landing, and the door at the top only opens to the lady and the gentleman who keep the rooms. I've never been through it, but I drew the stair and the apartment from memory, and I put in the back room."

> [!qna]**What does the family do there?**
>
> > "I couldn't say, and I've tried not to ask. There are gatherings after dark, with carriages the family doesn't enter in the stable ledger, and Madam Ammalia goes out alone twice a month in a hired cab with the curtains drawn. I did hear Lord Victoro say once that the property would be needed for the resolution of the family's obligation, and I took it to mean a debt, because what else could it be?"

> [!qna]**Why sell it?**
>
> > "Because I'm frightened, sir, and I'd rather be frightened somewhere else. Since the piece in the newspaper, Mr. Crowelle has asked every servant who they speak to, and the family dismisses whoever it can blame, and I carry the baskets. If I'm to be blamed anyway, I'd like the three hundred gold, and a ship."

> [!qna]**Who is the woman following you?**
>
> > "I don't know her name, sir, but she's been outside the villa gate for ten days, and at the market, and in the chandler's. I think the family hired her. I think if she sees me with you she'll tell Madam Ammalia within the day, and I'd be dismissed before nightfall, and I don't like to think what else."

> [!qna]**Can you leave Waterdeep?**
>
> Brimel takes the envelope from you and counts it twice with his thumb, then looks at the sheet of paper again.
>
> > "I'd like to, sir, I'd very much like to, but if I run it looks like guilt, and I've served that house for nineteen years. I don't know where I'd go, or whether I could do any work that isn't carrying baskets."

Krebbyg put a printed pass in the envelope for a cook's mate's berth aboard the *Heartbreaker*, which sails on Tarsakh 20. Brimel finds it when he counts the coins.

> [!exploration]**The Berth**
>
> Any character who tells Brimel about the berth and makes a successful DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) check persuades him to board the *Heartbreaker* the night before it sails, or at once if **Florette Reported** is marked. The character has Advantage if they point out that a steward who is gone before the family's questions start cannot be blamed for anything said afterward.
>
> - **On a success,** Brimel agrees, and the party earns a bonus under **Mission Renown**.
> - **On a failure,** Brimel keeps the pass and says he will decide, and he stays in the villa.

> [!gamemaster]**What Brimel Adds**
>
> If the member asks about the upper floor, Brimel says that the back room off the apartment has a desk with a map of the City of the Dead on it, with one mausoleum ringed in red ink and "Brandath" written beside it. He saw it once through an open door and drew a small cross on the plan. He tells nobody that unless asked, and the pencilled note on the back of the plan carries it either way.

Any member who marked **Exposé Read** in **The Wazoo Affair** recognizes the windmill without a check. The exposé mentioned "a windmill in the Southern Ward where lanterns burn on moonless nights."

### The Plan in Krebbyg's Hands

The member returns to the booking office at eleven bells, after the showcase lets out. Krebbyg unlocks the door, takes the plan and unfolds it under the desk lamp.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The booking office smells of stage paint and tea, and a lamp on the desk throws a circle of yellow light over the folded sheet Krebbyg has smoothed flat with both hands. He reads it slowly, from the sail room at the bottom of the sheet to the small cross at the top, and then he turns it over and reads the pencilled lines on the back, and his whole face lights up.
>
> > "Fucking hell, darling, look at this. A stair, an apartment, a locked room with a map in it, and one tomb ringed in red with a name next to it, which is the Brandath crypt, isn't it? This is what the captain was waiting for. He's wanted a look inside that house since the Wazoo business, and here it is on one sheet of paper, drawn by a man who's scared of his own coat."

If **Jarlaxle Unmasked** is marked for the member, Krebbyg adds in a low voice:

> [!readaloud]
>
> Krebbyg lowers his voice and glances at the door.
>
> > "Jarlaxle's going to love this, and for fuck's sake don't say that name out in the lobby, because the soprano repeats everything."

> [!exploration]**Reading the Plan**
>
> Any character who studies the plan and makes a successful DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) check reads Brimel's steward's notation, which lists the sail room, the shared stair, the apartment and the back room marked with a cross. The plan is not hidden, and the pencilled note on the back is visible to anyone who turns it over without a check.
>
> - **Auto:** the pencilled note on the back shows a map of the City of the Dead on a desk in the back room, with the Brandath Crypt ringed in red.
> - **Result of 17 or higher:** the character notes that the sail room is the only way into the apartment, and that the stair has three landings, with the second landing as far as a servant goes.
>
> Mark **Brandath Lead from Brimel** for each member who sees the plan or hears Brimel's account of the back room.

Krebbyg keeps the plan in the dressing room and gives the member a copy in his own hand. The party now knows where the Brandath Crypt is and has an independent source for it. The windmill itself is a Cassalanter outpost that **Faction Outposts** treats as a mini-heist.

### The Key

The next morning a boy from the Faire brings an envelope to Trollskull Manor. It holds 200 gp for the party, a brass key stamped with a seven, and a note on a gilt ticket in a looping hand.

> [!readaloud]
>
> An envelope sealed with a little painted ship lies on the table by the door, delivered by a boy who has run off down the alley. It holds two hundred gold in a leather purse, a brass key stamped with a single seven, and a gilt ticket with a few lines written across the back in a looping hand.
>
> > Marvelous work. The last dressing room on the stage-left corridor is yours from tonight, and Krebbyg will tell you the knock. Come and go as you please, and bring friends. Free tickets enclosed. Z.Z.

If **Zardoz Introduced** is not marked, the note is in Krebbyg's quick sloping hand and signed K.S.

Krebbyg meets the member at the stage door at four bells the same afternoon and walks them down the stage-left corridor to the dressing room.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The last dressing room at the end of the stage-left corridor has a door painted the same maroon as the walls and no name on it. Krebbyg knocks twice, waits, and knocks three times, and a drow in a stagehand's apron opens it a hand's width and then steps back. Inside, a long mirror ringed with candle stubs hangs above a washstand, six cots stand folded against the wall, and a second guard sits on a costume trunk with a deck of cards.
>
> > "This is it, darling. Two knocks, a gap, three knocks. The guards sleep in shifts, the cots are clean, and if the ghost comes through the wall, tell him it's an emergency and he'll go away."

> [!gamemaster]**The Back Room**
>
> The dressing room at the end of the stage-left corridor is Bregan D'aerthe's emergency safe house in the Seven Masks, and it is now open to the member by right.
>
> - **Entry:** two knocks, a gap and three knocks at the door, at any hour. Two drow guards in stagehand's aprons, who use the 2024 **Scout** statistics, are always inside while the room is in use.
> - **What it holds:** six cots, a washstand, a locked costume trunk that the member can use to store gear, and a stock of drow-made crossbows and bolts that Fel'rekt sells at cost to Bregan D'aerthe members.
> - **Rest:** the member and up to five companions the member names to the guards can take a Long Rest there, for up to three nights in a row. The guards do not ask questions.
> - **Messages:** a message left with the guards at any hour reaches Krebbyg by the next dusk. This does not replace the grey coat on peg seven, which Krebbyg still clears at closing.
> - **Meetings:** **The Dive** briefs here, and the rank events **Officer**, **Commander** and **Houseless Noble** meet here when **Seven Masks Back Room** is marked. If it is not marked, they meet at the stage door.
> - **Loss rule:** the member loses access by marking **BD Contact Severed**, by bringing a Watch officer into the corridor, or by telling anyone outside the company that the room exists.
>
> **What changes if the member already has access.** At Renown 3 and above, the door staff admit the member to the theater and the booking office, and this does not include the dressing room. If the member entered the dressing room during **Faction Outposts** (the **Seven Masks Raided** outcome, which the **Faction Outposts** conversion must write), the entry was unsanctioned and Bregan D'aerthe's guards treated it as an intrusion. After this Event, the note forgives the raid by name, the guards stop treating the member's knock as an intrusion, and the Alert tier that the raid set toward Bregan D'aerthe drops to Suspicious for that member.

## Overview

A nervous Cassalanter steward has a floor plan of a windmill his employers keep off their calendar. The party helps Bregan D'aerthe collect it at a Faire showcase while the family's watcher tries to stop the handoff.

## Summary

We met Krebbyg at the Seven Masks and took a seat beside a frightened Cassalanter steward named Brimel Crestfall during the Faire's showcase. While Zardoz Zord watched from his box without saying a word, we kept the family's watcher off Brimel and took a folded plan of a windmill in the Southern Ward. It showed a map of the City of the Dead with the Brandath Crypt ringed in red, and we now have the key to the dressing room at the end of the stage-left corridor. What does the family keep in that windmill?
