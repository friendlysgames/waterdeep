# Design Notes: The Theater's Back Room

## Jarlaxle's Long Intelligence Play

***Three missions building to one document.*** The Wazoo Affair (Mission 2) planted a story that pressured the Cassalanters publicly and put their household staff under stress. That stress is why Brimel Crestfall started paying attention to family conversations he was not supposed to hear. This mission is where that investment pays off — the intelligence chain from Mission 2 runs directly to the windmill floor plan that Brimel is carrying tonight.

The party does not need to understand this chain to complete Mission 5. They need to get the document. But the design notes should flag it because a DM who knows the chain can let Jarlaxle demonstrate it when the moment comes — in **Sea Maidens Faire**, if Jarlaxle ever explains himself, this is one of the things he was actually doing.

## Florette Cressyn as a Character

***The watcher who is just doing her job.*** Florette is not a villain. She is a professional investigator protecting her employer from exactly the kind of activity that Brimel is engaging in. She is correct about what she suspects and wrong only about whether she can stop it tonight. The mission does not ask the party to harm her — it asks them to neutralize her for the duration of one handoff. How they do that is up to them. She files a report tomorrow or she does not, and the renown consequence follows.

Making Florette a real professional rather than a generic obstacle raises the social challenge register. She is not fooled by clumsy cover stories; she is a **DC 14** task, not a DC 11. A party that uses a creative misdirection — something specific to the theater environment, the performance, the crowd — is rewarded with a cleaner success than a party that tries to walk her out of the lobby with a thin story. Both approaches work. The distinction is craft.

## The Windmill and What It Contains

***Brimel's word "resolution" is load-bearing.*** Victoro Cassalanter used the word "resolution" to describe the locked room's contents. This is the same word the Cassalanters use for the moment when their infernal contract is fulfilled — when the children's souls are delivered, the debt is cleared, and the family survives. The locked room contains documents pointing toward the Brandath Crypts approach, which is the vault's back-door entrance. Brimel does not know any of this. He heard the word once at a family dinner and it was specific enough to remember.

A party that reaches **Vault of Dragons** with this intelligence has two pieces of context that shape the confrontation: they know the Cassalanters used the vault approach, and they know the vault's ceremonial significance to a family trying to buy their children's survival. Neither piece makes the confrontation easier. Both make it more real.
