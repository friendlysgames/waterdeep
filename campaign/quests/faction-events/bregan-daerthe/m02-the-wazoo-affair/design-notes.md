# Design Notes: The Wazoo Affair

## Pressure From a Man Who Already Knows

The exposé comes from the source: a lurid piece on devil worship and orgies among unnamed Waterdhavian noble families, written by Jarlaxle and delivered by break-in. The restored draft made it accurate. It described a ritual space matching the Cassalanter villa's lower temple and credited a witness account to Nar'l Xibrindas, so Xanathar's Guild would have known the Cassalanters were infernalists before the party did. That contradicts the standing rule that no other faction knows.

The user ruled that Jarlaxle knows about the Cassalanters through a spy and won't tell members who haven't worked it out. The spy is Vessa, the Bregan D'aerthe doppelganger embedded in Cassalanter society, who reads thoughts and learned of the Asmodean pact and the children's contract that way. Here the piece stays rumor-shaped. It names no family and no witness. Its only particulars are an Aveen Street cellar and a Southern Ward windmill, given as footmen would give them, and it scatters hooks that fit several great houses: charity, a pleasant manner, a large fortune. Its purpose is pressure. Jarlaxle wants the Cassalanters to feel watched, and wants to see how they react and whom they send, without anyone tracing the piece to what he knows.

The reaction is the clue. Two "solicitors" from a firm that doesn't exist lean on Gaxly the morning after publication, and the Wazoo suffers a tenday of small troubles it can't explain. Nothing the party sees proves who sent the men, and the carriage with a covered crest is as far as the evidence goes. The Cassalanters come out as the family that overreacted, which is how suspicion works and why no one has to know anything.

## Why the Break-In Has Three Steps and an Alternative

The source resolves the break-in in three rolls: two DC 15 Stealth checks and a DC 10 thieves' tools check or *knock*. That made the mission borderline single-check. Each roll now has a failure that costs something: a witness, ten minutes, a scratched lock or a Watch patrol. The two easy ways, a direct request to Hovan Dree and a courier's disguise, skip the rolls but cost the party anonymity, because **Wazoo Delivery Witnessed** is marked and the +1 for an unseen delivery is lost. A party can always finish the mission, and the clean version is the harder one.

The two windows from the source, lunch and after hours, change who is watching and what a failure looks like. Pennet and Hovan Dree fill the source's "other tenants" line. The earlier draft's dinner party on the ground floor had no source and is gone. The two-hour clock keeps the desk from being a free search, since every ten minutes of looking eats into the margin.

## The Desk and the Black Viper Note

The source's desk note puts Gaxly's Black Viper source at "anonymous, Tethyrian, well-dressed". That matches Seffia Naelryke, the Cassalanter household operative who planted the story that **The Black Viper Investigation** later has the Order of the Gauntlet unpick. Nevercott has no reason to know this, and the note is a loose thread here and a clue elsewhere. **Black Viper Source Noted** is set so that the Gauntlet mission, once converted, can read it.

## Nevercott

J.B. Nevercott is Jarlaxle in a haberdasher's coat. He briefs at the Yawning Portal because the source and the organization page both place him there, and he says "Do not read it" because the source says he expects them to. The closing question, "Did you find it informative?", is the source's too, and it is where a member's honest guess about the piece earns a bonus. He never confirms a guess, because that would show what he knows.

Nevercott is Jarlaxle in disguise, so his speech follows the Jarlaxle profile in `character-voices` and the haberdasher persona in the First Meeting. He is courteous and unhurried, he answers with longer sentences than the question needs, and he doesn't swear. No BD speaker says "Jarlaxle" here, in line with the **Jarlaxle Unmasked** gate.

## Gaxly Rudderbust Across Factions

Gaxly appears in two faction chains: this one and **The Black Viper Investigation** for the Order of the Gauntlet. The older draft also listed Doom Raiders and Xanathar's Guild. The Doom Raiders notes retire him, and no Xanathar mission uses him. A party that has run both missions meets a man who has had two people leave things on his desk and a third ask him about it. He isn't corrupt. He prints what sells and what he can half verify, which is why every faction can use him.

## Out-of-Scope Notes

Invented names, all for minor NPCs: Pennet (the candlemaker's apprentice), Hovan Dree (the third-floor scrivener) and the false firm Harrow & Pell. Pennet's and Hovan's ages and trades are invented to fill the source's "other tenants". Contradictions outside this folder, listed for the Bregan D'aerthe section of the out-of-scope notes:

- `campaign/setting/notable-figures/xanathars-guild/03-narl-xibrindas.md` line 8 lists **The Wazoo Affair** under Nar'l's "Featured in". Nar'l no longer appears here.
- `campaign/setting/villains/jarlaxle.md` line 51 says Jarlaxle "knows about their infernal bargain through intelligence". Under the user's ruling this is now correct, and the page could name Vessa as the source.
- `campaign/guides/factions/08-bregan-daerthe.md` line 67 describes the exposé as being about "an unnamed Sea Ward family's hidden gold and missing servants", not devil worship.
- **The Theater's Back Room** now puts the windmill on Coachlamp Lane, as the source and Seffia's Notable Figures page do.
- **Kreb Drops the Cover** and **Contact Severed** no longer read the cut Betrayal Pitch, and **Kreb Drops the Cover** reads **Wazoo Exposé Published**.
- `campaign/guides/gm-guide/design-notes-running-the-campaign.md` line 126 calls Gaxly a "single-mission NPC", but he also appears in **The Black Viper Investigation**.
