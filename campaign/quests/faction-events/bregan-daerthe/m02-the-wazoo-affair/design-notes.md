# Design Notes: The Wazoo Affair

## J.B. Nevercott's Identity

***Two disguises, one man.*** J.B. Nevercott is Jarlaxle in a different costume from Zardoz Zord. He does not acknowledge this — not when the party has met both, not when a perceptive player asks, not in **Sea Maidens Faire** when Jarlaxle drops all his personas. The two disguises operate in different social registers: Zardoz Zord is a flamboyant sea captain for public-facing work; J.B. Nevercott is a quiet businessman for private arrangements. A party that has met both and pays close attention will notice, eventually, that they have the same manner — the same deliberate curiosity, the same unhurried quality in conversation.

The mission brief does not tell the DM to telegraph this. It is a reward for player attention, not a puzzle to be solved. When Jarlaxle reveals himself in **Sea Maidens Faire**, a party that already suspects will have a different reaction than one learning it fresh. Both reactions are worth having.

## The Cassalanter Disruption Function

***Jarlaxle writing intelligence warfare.*** The document is not a generic mission prop — it is Jarlaxle using Nar'l Xibrindas's Guild intelligence to pressure the Cassalanters directly. This is worth making explicit because it plants a question the party should be asking by Mission 4: how much does Jarlaxle actually know about the other factions? The answer is: considerably more than he has indicated. The document is one piece of evidence. The party sees another piece in Mission 4 when Nar'l's placement inside the Guild becomes relevant.

The Cassalanter follow-up (solicitors at Rudderbust's door) is deliberately available to witness because it shows the party how the Cassalanters respond to pressure — fast, organized, using legal cover rather than direct violence. This detail matters in **Cassalanter Villa**, where the party has to decide how much leverage they have over the Cassalanters and how the Cassalanters will respond to being confronted.

## Gaxly Rudderbust and Cross-Faction Presence

***The editor who keeps showing up.*** Gaxly Rudderbust appears in three faction mission chains: Doom Raiders M2, Xanathar's Guild M2 (as the target of a misinformation plant), and here. A party that has already encountered him — whether they planted the Doom Raiders' document or the Xanathar Guild's competing story — will find him in a different frame of reference. He may have started to notice that people keep leaving things on his desk. He may be more cautious about unexplained documents if prior missions involved him in something uncomfortable. None of this prevents mission success, but a party that has met Gaxly before approaches him as a person rather than a location, which is a better session.

The design intent is that Gaxly is genuinely doing journalism — he publishes what he can verify, protects sources he does not have, and does not know he is being used by multiple factions simultaneously. He is not corrupt; he is manipulable precisely because he is honest. That is a more interesting situation than if he were simply compromised.
