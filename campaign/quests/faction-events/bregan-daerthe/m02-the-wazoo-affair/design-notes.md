# Design Notes: The Wazoo Affair

## J.B. Nevercott's Identity

***Two disguises, one man.*** J.B. Nevercott is Jarlaxle in a different costume from Zardoz Zord. He does not acknowledge this at any point — not when the party has met both personas, not in **Sea Maidens Faire** when Jarlaxle finally drops the pretense. The two operate in different registers. Zardoz Zord is for public-facing work, all salt and showmanship; J.B. Nevercott is for private arrangements, quiet and businesslike. A party that has met both and pays close attention will notice, eventually, that they share the same measured curiosity and the same unhurried way with conversation.

The mission brief does not instruct the GM to telegraph this. It is a reward for player attention, not a puzzle to be solved. When Jarlaxle reveals himself in **Sea Maidens Faire**, a party that suspects will have a different reaction than one learning it fresh. Both are good scenes, and neither requires the other.

## The Cassalanter Disruption Function

***Jarlaxle writing intelligence warfare.*** The document is not a generic mission prop — it is Jarlaxle using Nar'l Xibrindas's Guild intelligence to pressure the Cassalanters directly, on nothing more than suspicion. Jarlaxle does not know the family are infernalists (nobody does until the party finds out); he knows about the gold and the vanished servants, and he wants to see what the family does when pushed. This is worth making explicit in play because it plants a question the party should be asking by Mission 4: how much does Jarlaxle actually know about the other factions? The answer is considerably more than he has indicated. The document is one piece of evidence; Mission 4 provides another when Nar'l's placement inside the Guild becomes relevant.

The Cassalanter follow-up (solicitors at Rudderbust's door) is available to witness because it shows the party how the Cassalanters respond to pressure — fast, organized, using legal cover rather than direct violence. That detail matters in **Cassalanter Villa**, where the party has to assess how much leverage they have over the Cassalanters and how the family will respond to being confronted.

## Gaxly Rudderbust and Cross-Faction Presence

***The editor who keeps showing up.*** Gaxly Rudderbust appears in three faction mission chains: Doom Raiders Mission 2, Xanathar's Guild Mission 2, and here. A party that has already encountered him will find him in a different frame of reference. He may have started to notice that people keep leaving things on his desk. He may be more careful about anonymous documents if prior missions put him in an uncomfortable position.

None of this prevents mission success, but a party that has met Gaxly before approaches him as a person rather than a location. The design intent is that Gaxly is genuinely doing journalism: he publishes what he can verify, protects sources he does not have, and does not know he is being used by multiple factions simultaneously. He is not corrupt; he is manipulable precisely because he is honest, and that is the more interesting situation.
