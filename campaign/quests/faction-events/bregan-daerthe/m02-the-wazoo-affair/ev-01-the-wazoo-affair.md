# The Wazoo Affair

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> This Exploration/Social Event takes place at the Wazoo building in the North Ward during an ongoing dinner party. In this Event, the party can:
>
> - Receive the mission brief from J.B. Nevercott at the Yawning Portal and ask him what he knows
> - Choose among three entry routes into the Wazoo building
> - Navigate past the wandering guest on the second floor
> - Place the document in Rudderbust's editorial stack
> - Observe or hear about the Cassalanter follow-up visit three days later and report it to Krebbyg
>
> #### Background
>
> The document is an exposé: an unnamed Sea Ward noble family, it says, uses its famous charities to hide gold purchases on an enormous scale, and servants who asked where the money went have left service and not been seen again. The language is carefully vague, and it is aimed at the Cassalanters. Jarlaxle wrote it himself from what Nar'l Xibrindas picked up inside Xanathar's Guild: the Guild has noticed the family buying gold through a dozen intermediaries, and a few Cassalanter servants have vanished. Jarlaxle does not know what the family is doing, and nobody in the Guild does either. He suspects something rotten behind the charity and wants to see how the family reacts when someone kicks it. He cannot publish under any name that traces to him. Once published, the story will force the Cassalanters to redirect resources toward damage control during **Faction Outposts**.
>
> The document contains three pages in an unfamiliar hand — careful, specific, and written to read as investigative journalism rather than a tip. It accuses an unnamed Sea Ward noble family of buying gold on a vast scale behind its charities, cites a single anonymous witness (a former servant's cousin), and lists three household staff who left service and have not been seen since. Jarlaxle lifted the witness account and the names from Nar'l's intelligence summaries and wrote the rest himself. Every claim is true as far as it goes. None of it says why.
>
> Gaxly Rudderbust is a freelance journalist, fifties, deliberately disheveled, grey braid, ink stains on every item of clothing. His dinner parties run long and loud. He will publish the document without knowing its source because the story is good and he can verify the core facts himself.
>
> J.B. Nevercott will not explain who he works for. He will not explain why the document cannot be delivered by normal means. He is cheerful about this.

### The Brief

J.B. Nevercott finds the party at the Yawning Portal. He does not explain how he knew to look there. He orders a round before sitting down.

> [!readaloud]
> A tall man in an overcoat that costs more than most merchants earn in a month is at the bar when you arrive, and he has already ordered. He has one eye covered by a silk patch, and he shakes your hand with the unhurried warmth of someone who planned to be here first.
>
> > "J.B. Nevercott. It's been a little while. Sit. I've taken the liberty."
>
> He settles into his chair and folds his hands on the table.
>
> > "I have a publishing matter. There's a broadsheet in the North Ward called *The Waterdeep Wazoo* — perhaps you've read it. The editor is a man named Gaxly Rudderbust. I need a document delivered to his editorial desk before tomorrow's print run. The document is sensitive. It should appear to arrive without a sender."
>
> He produces a sealed folder from his coat and sets it on the table.
>
> > "His office is on the second floor of the Wazoo building, corner of Immar Street and Stallion Street. Tonight, Rudderbust is hosting a small dinner party on the ground floor."
>
> A pause.
>
> > "None of his guests are expecting you."

> [!social]**The Publishing Contact**
>
> J.B. Nevercott (Neutral, Human (apparent), he/him) :: a Bregan D'aerthe contact with a talent for appearing before you in places you didn't mention you'd be.
>
> Nevercott is unhurried, precise, and genuinely curious about the party — he asks about their impressions of the city, what they have seen, whether they have met interesting people, and he listens to the answers with more attention than a hat merchant's schedule would seem to require. He does not explain what the document contains or who it concerns. He is cheerful about this.
>
> He names a price of 80 gp, payable after the document has been published. He will not pay before. He does not discuss Jarlaxle, Bregan D'aerthe, or the document's purpose.
>
> Conversation topics Nevercott is willing to discuss:
> - The delivery logistics: the address, the building layout as he knows it, tonight's dinner party
> - Why the document needs a sealed folder and a missing sender rather than a post rider
> - The party's recent activities and impressions of Waterdeep, which he asks about with apparent warmth

> [!qna]**What does the document say?**
>
> > "I'd prefer you not know. I find that people who know what a document says tend to have opinions about it, and opinions about it lead to conversations about it, and conversations about it are rather the opposite of what I need. The sealed folder is for everyone's convenience."

> [!qna]**Why can't you deliver it yourself?**
>
> He turns his cup slightly on the table.
>
> > "The document should have no connection to anyone who might reasonably be expected to have written it. I am a recognizable man in certain circles, and those circles have some overlap with the circles who will find this document interesting. You don't have that complication. Not yet, at least."

> [!qna]**Why no sender?**
>
> > "Because a document that arrives without a sender is a story Rudderbust found. A document that arrives with a sender is a story someone told him. The first kind he publishes; the second kind he investigates. I need the first kind."

> [!qna]**What if we get caught?**
>
> A small pause.
>
> > "Then you were there for the dinner party and took a wrong turn. Rudderbust runs loose gatherings. I wouldn't worry excessively — the risk is modest if you're sensible about it."

> [!qna]**The pay?**
>
> > "Eighty gold pieces, paid in person after the document has appeared in print. I'll find you. I tend to."

Once the party accepts, Nevercott refills the nearest glass and rises.

> [!readaloud]
> He sets the sealed folder on the table, slides it toward the nearest hand, and stands.
>
> > "Rudderbust's parties run until the small hours. You have plenty of time. I'd suggest getting there before the second round, when people start to wander."
>
> He tips well and leaves the tavern without looking back.

Proceed to **The Wazoo Building**.

### The Wazoo Building

Corner of Immar Street and Stallion Street in the North Ward. A four-story building with *The Waterdeep Wazoo* painted above the ground-floor windows; lights and noise on the lower two floors. Three entry options.

> [!readaloud]
> The Wazoo building is easy to find — the name is painted above the windows in hand-lettered text that doesn't quite line up at the corners, and the ground floor is lit and loud. A couple is arguing in the back garden, near the exterior staircase on the north side of the building. Through the front windows, a party of about a dozen people is visible, a mix of ages, animated in conversation, and nobody appears to be keeping a close eye on the door.

> [!exploration]**Entering the Building**
>
> **Front door:** A DC 13 Charisma (Deception or Persuasion) check to arrive as late guests who know someone inside. On a success, the door servant waves them through — Rudderbust runs loose parties. On a failure, the servant goes to consult Rudderbust, which puts him between the party and the stairs for the next ten minutes.
>
> **Back stairs:** An exterior staircase on the north side of the building goes directly to the second floor. A DC 12 Dexterity (Athletics) check to reach the landing, then a DC 14 Dexterity (Stealth) check to pass unnoticed by the couple in the garden, who are arguing and mostly looking at each other.
>
> **Kitchen entrance:** The kitchen door is propped open for air. A server passes every few minutes. A DC 13 Dexterity (Stealth) check timed against the server's route clears the entrance and gives unobserved access to the inner stairs.

### The Editorial Office

The second floor is unlit. Rudderbust's editorial office is at the far end of the corridor: a desk the size of a small boat, covered in correspondence and proof pages, with tomorrow's draft content stacked at the center. The document needs to go into that stack, near the top, where Rudderbust will find it when he sits down in the morning.

> [!readaloud]
> The second floor is dark and smells of ink and paper and something that might have been coffee three days ago. The editorial office is at the far end: the door is open, and through it you can see a desk that occupies most of the room, covered in neat stacks of correspondence, proof pages, and what looks like tomorrow's front page in working draft. A lamp on the desk has been left burning low.
>
> In the outer room, a dinner guest has drifted up from the party downstairs and is pulling back issues from a shelf with the loose attention of someone looking for something specific. He is facing away from the corridor.

> [!exploration]**The Wandering Guest**
>
> Any character who passes through the outer room must make a DC 11 Dexterity (Stealth) check to pass the open doorway without drawing the guest's attention. A character who instead approaches him and makes a successful DC 12 Charisma (Deception) check with a plausible story about looking for the necessary gets waved past with a nod — Rudderbust's guests are used to the building being large and difficult to navigate.

> [!exploration]**Placing the Document**
>
> Any character who examines Rudderbust's filing system and makes a successful DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) check places the document where it will be found during the morning print run. On a failure, the document lands somewhere plausible but not prominent — Rudderbust finds it two days later, slightly delaying publication but not preventing it.

> [!gamemaster]**The Document's Contents**
>
> If the party opens the sealed folder before placing it, they find three pages in an unfamiliar hand: a carefully written accusation that an unnamed Sea Ward noble family hides enormous gold purchases behind its charities, a single anonymous witness account, and the first names of three servants who left that household and were never seen again. The writing is good — specific enough to be interesting, vague enough to be legally unprovable. Nothing in the document names the Cassalanters or connects it to Jarlaxle.

### The Follow-Up

*The Waterdeep Wazoo*, three days after the delivery: a front-page story, anonymously sourced, about a generous Sea Ward family, a mountain of hidden gold, and servants who disappeared. It is specific enough to be alarming and legally unprovable.

Within a tenday, two associates arrive at the Wazoo building dressed as solicitors, carrying threatening paperwork. Gaxly tells the truth about his source: he found the document on his desk and has no idea who left it. He says this under any pressure because it is true. The associates leave without satisfaction.

If the party is watching the Wazoo building when the associates arrive, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
> Two men in solicitors' coats are at the Wazoo building's front door when you arrive. They have the easy confidence of people who expect the door to open promptly, and it does. They are inside for about twenty minutes before they come back out, and neither of them looks satisfied. They walk away quickly and do not speak to each other.

If the party hears about it from Gaxly afterward, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
> Gaxly Rudderbust is at his desk when you find him, his grey braid over one shoulder and a correction he is crossing out with the focused energy of someone who regards bad writing as a personal insult. He sets down the pen.
>
> > "The solicitors? Yes. Two of them, very polished, very unhappy. Wanted to know my source." He spreads his hands. "I told them the truth — I found it on my desk and I have no idea who left it. They didn't believe me." A pause. "They'll have to keep not believing me. It's still true."

> [!social]**The Editor**
>
> Gaxly Rudderbust (Neutral Good, Human, he/him) :: the sole full-time journalist at *The Waterdeep Wazoo*, who publishes what he can verify and protects sources he doesn't have.
>
> Gaxly is deliberately disheveled, ink-stained, and deeply serious about the work. He is not naive — he knows that anonymous documents arrive with agendas — but his standard is whether the facts check out, not whether the sender is trustworthy. He is not corrupt; he is manipulable precisely because he is honest. If the party has appeared in connection with a previous document on his desk, he is beginning to notice a pattern. He has not yet connected the dots.
>
> Conversation topics Gaxly is willing to discuss:
> - The solicitors' visit and what they asked, which he will describe with exasperated candor
> - The story itself, which he will defend professionally
> - What it takes for an anonymous source to get a front page, which he explains with genuine intellectual pleasure

> [!qna]**About the solicitors?**
>
> He leans back in his chair with the expression of someone who has had this conversation already and is prepared to have it again.
>
> > "Two men in good coats with paperwork that implied consequences. I told them the truth, they called me a liar, and then they left. I've had worse meetings." He picks up the pen. "If they come back with a court order, we'll have a different conversation. Until then, I have a paper to put out."

> [!qna]**Who were they working for?**
>
> > "They didn't say, and the paperwork was drawn up to imply a consortium of aggrieved parties rather than naming anyone specific. Good lawyers. Whoever they work for has money and doesn't want to be visible." He writes something, crosses it out. "That's interesting, isn't it."

> [!gamemaster]**The Follow-Up as Intelligence**
>
> A party that observes or hears about the solicitors' visit and reports it to Krebbyg can extract a useful piece of intelligence: the Cassalanters moved fast and used legal cover rather than direct confrontation, which means their response apparatus is organized and already running. Krebbyg, told about it: "Good. They're burning resources." He does not explain why that is good. The intelligence has direct relevance in **Cassalanter Villa**.

### Concluding the Event

J.B. Nevercott pays 80 gp in person at the Yawning Portal two days after publication. He is cheerful and mentions nothing about the front page. He asks about the party's time in the city, whether they have met interesting people, and what they have been doing. He takes his leave after twenty minutes and tips well.

If the **Follow-Up Observed** outcome is marked, Nevercott mentions while rising from his chair:

> [!readaloud]
> > "By the way — if you ever find yourselves near the harbor at the wrong hour, there's a boat I occasionally use. The booking manager at Seven Masks knows the arrangements."
>
> He does not elaborate. He settles his coat and goes.

## Overview

A publishing matter: plant a sensitive document on a North Ward editor's desk during a dinner party, without leaving a sender.

## Summary

The party infiltrated the Wazoo building during Gaxly Rudderbust's dinner party and placed J.B. Nevercott's sealed document in the editorial stack. Three days later it ran as the front page: an anonymously sourced exposé about an unnamed Sea Ward family's hidden gold and its missing servants. Within a tenday, two associates dressed as solicitors arrived at the Wazoo asking Gaxly about his source. J.B. Nevercott paid 80 gp at the Yawning Portal and asked nothing about any of it.
