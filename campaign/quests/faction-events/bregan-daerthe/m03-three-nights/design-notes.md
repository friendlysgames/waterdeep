# Design Notes: Three Nights

## Ott Steeltoes as a Character

***Genuine pathos in an unlikely hostage.*** Ott is the keeper of a goldfish. This is played entirely straight. His worry about Sylgar during a situation in which he is a factional intelligence asset, held by a drow crime lord while three waves of monsters try to retrieve him, creates an emotional register that no other mission in the BD chain reaches. The party is asked to protect a frightened halfling in a fish-stained apron. The stakes are real — Xanathar's attachment to Sylgar is what makes holding Ott valuable — but the mission never asks the party to engage with that strategic logic. They just need to keep him alive and not too rattled.

Krebbyg visiting Ott every evening and having quiet conversations with him is not described as an event because it is not a scene the party witnesses. It is background. Ott's decision to cooperate — to tell Krebbyg about the panopticus blind spots and the beholder's routines — is his own. He made a calculation. The party does not have to engage with this unless they ask Ott directly about his conversations with Krebbyg.

## The Escalation Sequence

***Why these three specific threats.*** Night One (muscle and noise) establishes that the Guild knows Ott is in the building and is willing to make a public scene. Night Two (intellect devourer scouts) establishes that Xanathar is willing to sacrifice commoner hosts and that the threat has escalated from brute-force retrieval to intelligence-gathering. Night Three (beholder zombie) establishes that Xanathar has stopped reasoning about this operationally — he is sending an apex creature to retrieve a goldfish keeper because the absence of Sylgar's caretaker has overwhelmed his capacity for measured response.

The progression tells a story about Xanathar even though Xanathar never appears. A party running the Xanathar's Guild faction chain (if any) will eventually see the beholder from the inside — and this mission will have already shown them what Xanathar looks like when he is frightened.

## The Cellar Constraint

***Why the beholder zombie enters from below.*** The cellar limits the antimagic cone's field of coverage, which makes the Night Three encounter survivable for a 4th-level party without requiring significant numerical reduction. The "one round of warning" from the disintegration ray is enough for the party to position themselves; the low ceiling is enough to prevent the cone from covering the full room on the first round. These are not favorable conditions for the beholder zombie, which is the point — Xanathar sent it in a state of desperation, not a state of tactical planning, and the party should be able to feel that.

## Ott's Return

***The intelligence payload.*** Ott returns to Xanathar's lair and says nothing that could trace back to Bregan D'aerthe. This matters for the party's ongoing safety: if Ott had been debriefed and broke, the Guild would have a connection between Trollskull Manor and Jarlaxle's operation. Ott chose not to provide that connection. This is not heroism on his part — it is a calculation about which relationship he is safer in. He trusts Krebbyg more than he trusts Xanathar's mercy. That is a reasonable position.

The intelligence Ott provided feeds **Xanathar's Lair**. The party will not see how it was used until Krebbyg or Jarlaxle mentions it, if they ever do.
