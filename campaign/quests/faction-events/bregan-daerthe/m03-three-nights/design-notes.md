# Design Notes: Three Nights

## A Prisoner Nobody Placed

The restored draft made Ott a halfling in a fish-stained apron and had Krebbyg collect him on the fourth morning, which solved a mystery the source leaves open. In Dragon Heist, Ott appears in the cellar in iron bands nobody can explain and vanishes after the third attack, and the Bregan D'aerthe appendix adds that nothing arrives to say why. This version keeps both. Ott is a dwarf in an eyestalk skullcap, the bands go when the third night ends, and nobody in Bregan D'aerthe, Ott included, knows who put him there.

Leaving the cause open means the mission needs another reason for the party to care, and the captain's note supplies it. Jarlaxle did not set the trap, but he saw its use. Xanathar cannot do without the one man who feeds Sylgar, so the three nights show what the Guild will spend to get him back, and Fel'rekt counts it from the roof of the Bent Nail. The party never learns Fel'rekt is there unless it looks, and he never comes down. Krebbyg is a poor source and says so, which keeps the captain's real interest out of reach until later events.

## Six Bugbears and a Level 4 Party

The source sends six bugbears. Six 2024 Bugbear Warriors at once are a Devastating fight for four 4th-level characters and an Impossible one for three, so the audit scales the roster to the number of characters who fight. Three combatants face a Bugbear Warrior and a Tough, four face two Bugbear Warriors and a Bandit, and five face three Bugbear Warriors. The rest of the gang waits at the alley mouth with the cart and runs when the first group falls, which keeps both the Guild's six and the party's survival true.

The roster also follows the geography. The cellar door is in the kitchen corridor and the courtyard gate opens into the kitchen, so the courtyard group threatens Ott and the front door group is a taproom brawl. Splitting the gang gives the party a choice instead of one fight to win.

## The Dungsweepers Were Already Dead

Night Two had two problems. A 2024 Intellect Devourer that takes a body consumes the brain, so the draft's promise to save hosts by extraction was impossible. The devourer's Detect Intelligence also senses minds through walls to 300 feet, so the draft's idea that the hosts would leave if Ott was merely out of sight could not work. This version states the first as a fact and builds the second into the route. The hosts are dead when they sit down, and nothing the party does changes that. They know where Ott is the moment they sit, because exactly one mind is below ground, and the only way to avoid them is to take him more than 300 feet away.

The plain Dungsweepers keep the table at four as the source describes, and the roster uses them to scale the fight. Keeping every plain worker alive earns a Renown bonus, which gives the party a reason to look at the table before it acts. Three independent clues reach the same conclusion. They are Ott's own account of the Guild's tools, a DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) or Wisdom (Perception) check at the table, and the fixed 23:00 move for a party that misses both. The devourers never use Steal Body on a fallen character, because the stat block would otherwise let one failed save end a character's story. The text says so plainly.

## The Zombie's Four Rays

The draft gave the zombie an antimagic cone that belongs to a full beholder and a scent-tracking trait it does not have, and Appendix C gave it a Central Eye. The 2024 Beholder Zombie has four Eye Rays and nothing else, and one of them turns a creature to dust. At level 4 it is a High-Power encounter, so the text makes four named changes.

- It surfaces with fewer hit points at three and four combatants.
- It has one ray on its first turn.
- Its rays fire in a fixed order that holds the Disintegration Ray until its third turn.
- Its goal is Ott, so it aims at characters and never at him.

The sewer grate replaces the draft's cellar window, because the cellar has no windows and T7 is the way in. Lif does not manifest in the cellar, so this is the one fight where the party cannot lean on him, and the text gives them a way to carry Ott upstairs. The six-round clock is invented. It lets the party win without killing the zombie and makes the bands' departure the end of the fight.

## What Lif Does

Lif is an ally on the ground floor and above, because Appendix C makes him one and the Trollskull Manor pages mark the cellar as out of his reach. His four effects are ordered instead of rolled, per the zero-prep rule, and only the party that marked **Lif Appeased** gets them. The Grand Opening decided whether Lif likes the party, and this is where that decision pays back.

## Where Ott Goes

Ott vanishes with the bands and reappears in Xanathar's Lair by morning with no explanation, because the Faction Outposts draft has him strapped to a chair in the Interrogation House after being captured in the Guild war. That entry calls him a gnome, which the source and his Notable Figures page contradict, but it needs him to be a Guild man the Zhentarim could take, and this mission gives it one. **Ott Kept** and **Ott Lost** both lead to the same chair, and the only difference is whether the Guild knows Trollskull Manor held him.

## Out-of-Scope Notes

- The Ott entry in **Faction Outposts** (`arc-e-faction-outposts.md`, around line 209) calls him a gnome.
- The Notable Figures page for Ott names his stat block "Cult Fanatic", the 2014 name for the 2024 Cultist Fanatic.
- The Trollskull Manor area overview (`area-overview.md`, around line 41) says Lif was a dwarf, and his Notable Figures page says half-elf.
- The First Meeting says nothing about the pouches. The Factions Guide and **Coin Pouches** give the second one a note, and this mission follows **Coin Pouches**.
- **Faction Outposts** is unconverted and is named as the reader of **Ott Kept** and **Ott Lost**.
- Invented details are Fel'rekt on the roof, the Guild's cart at the alley mouth, the flying snake's blue-and-orange scales, the 30-foot distance from the grate to Ott's corner, the six-round clock and Lif's four-effect list.
- The oldest of the plain Dungsweepers is unnamed.
