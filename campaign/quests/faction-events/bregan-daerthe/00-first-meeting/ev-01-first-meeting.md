# Bregan D'aerthe — First Meeting

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> This Social Event occurs during the renovation period in **Trollskull Alley**, running parallel to the other faction recruitments. In this Event, the party can:
>
> - Notice Ryvarra surveilling the alley and attempt to identify her as BD-affiliated
> - Report the surveillance to the Watch, cutting off BD contact permanently
> - Confront the watchers directly and trigger Nevercott's visit the next morning
> - Take no action and receive Nevercott's visit at the tenday's end regardless
> - Meet J.B. Nevercott at Trollskull Manor's front door, and accept or decline a first assignment
>
> #### Preparation
>
> Ryvarra has been filing weekly intelligence reports to Jarlaxle from the Yawning Portal since before the party descended the well. She now follows them to Trollskull Alley, moving through each morning under her cloth merchant cover. The passive Perception threshold for glimpsing the broader surveillance network around the alley is 18.
>
> If the party noticed Ryvarra during **The Neighbors** Event and the **Ryvarra Identified** outcome was marked, they recognize her here on sight. That outcome affects Nevercott's opening when he arrives — see **The Haberdasher at the Door** for the variation.
>
> This event is entered from **The Factions Come Calling** Event.

### The Calimshan Merchant

Over the course of several days, the same woman passes through Trollskull Alley each morning, taking a table near one of the stalls with the easy manner of a merchant letting trade negotiations run long. She buys nothing. She finishes nothing she orders to drink.

> [!readaloud]
> A woman in a dun coat has settled at the stall table near the corner of Trollskull Alley for the third morning running. She has a full glass in front of her and a small cloth sample open on the table, and she is watching the street while appearing to study the fabric. Her accent, when she speaks to the stall-keeper, carries a Calimshan cadence that is not quite consistent from sentence to sentence.

> [!exploration]**Identifying the Watcher**
>
> Any character who observes the woman for more than a few minutes and makes a successful DC 14 Wisdom (Perception) check recognizes her from the Yawning Portal — she had the same reserved table there during the party's early days.
>
> Any character who passes the Perception check and then makes a successful DC 17 Intelligence (Investigation) check identifies her as Bregan D'aerthe-affiliated, based on the deliberate method of her observation and the tells in her accent.
>
> - **Critical (Perception):** The character also notices that she never touches her drink — the glass is always full when she leaves — and that the cloth sample has not moved since she sat down.

If the party approaches and questions Ryvarra directly:

> [!readaloud]
> She folds the cloth sample without hurry and tucks it into her bag, then looks up at you with composed courtesy.
>
> > "Good morning, honoured friends. I'm afraid I have another appointment — negotiations have been going long enough this tenday. Please, do enjoy the alley."
>
> She excuses herself and walks away without looking back. The stall-keeper, asked about her a minute later, cannot describe her face with any particular precision.

> [!social]**The Cloth Merchant**
>
> Ryvarra (Neutral, Drow, she/her) :: a Bregan D'aerthe field observer maintaining a Calimshan cloth merchant cover that is better than most.
>
> Ryvarra's cover is smooth and practiced. She is unhurried, polite, and precise. She answers questions with questions, and she volunteers nothing. Her accent slips, occasionally, into something colder than a Calishite merchant's voice should carry. Any character who watches her face closely during a conversation and makes a successful DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check notices that she is reading the party's positions and reactions rather than listening to what they are actually saying.
>
> She will not admit to working for anyone. She will not be drawn into a longer conversation. If pushed, she excuses herself and is gone.
>
> Conversation topics Ryvarra is willing to discuss while maintaining cover:
> - The cloth trade, which she handles fluently.
> - Waterdeep's merchant districts, which she discusses with a traveller's practiced neutrality.
> - The alley's renovation, which she mentions with genuine-sounding interest.

> [!qna]**Who are you?**
>
> > "Ryvarra. Cloth merchant, mostly silks from Calimport. And you? You're the ones renovating the tavern on the corner, aren't you? I've been meaning to say, the stonework on that front wall is coming along nicely. Who did you hire for it?"

> [!qna]**Why are you watching us?**
>
> A brief pause before the answer, unhurried.
>
> > "I'm watching the street, honoured friend. Merchants do that when they're waiting for someone who is running late. You know how it goes."

> [!qna]**Who do you work for?**
>
> She tilts her head the smallest fraction, then smiles.
>
> > "A trading house in Calimport, principally. If you're looking for a fabric supplier, I can leave a card. Otherwise, I really do need to be going."

### The Party's Response

Once the party has noticed the surveillance network — or at the tenday's end regardless — one of three outcomes follows.

If the party reports the surveillance to the Watch, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
> A Watch officer takes the report, makes a note, and says he will look into it. By that evening, the alley is quiet. The next morning, a formal letter arrives at Trollskull Manor — a Watch sergeant's seal, a few lines thanking the party for their civic contribution, and a note that no criminal activity was found.

> [!gamemaster]**If the Watch Is Notified**
>
> Jarlaxle has the Watch report from his own contacts before the ink on it dries. He closes the party's file. In **Sea Maidens Faire**, he treats the party as a neutral variable to manage rather than a potential asset. Mark **BD Contact Severed** and cross-reference the **Contact Severed** Standalone Event for downstream BD interactions. Do not proceed to **The Haberdasher at the Door**.

If the party confronts the watchers directly, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
> The watchers step back and move into the foot traffic without urgency. One figure stays a half-beat too long, and when you reach the spot where she was standing you find a small black leather eye patch on the cobblestones. None of the people nearby can say which direction she went.

The next morning, **J.B. Nevercott** knocks at the front door of Trollskull Manor. Proceed to **The Haberdasher at the Door**.

If the party takes no action, surveillance continues for the rest of the tenday. At its end, **J.B. Nevercott** knocks at the front door of Trollskull Manor. Proceed to **The Haberdasher at the Door**.

### The Haberdasher at the Door

A man in a burgundy coat is waiting at Trollskull Manor's front door when someone answers it. He is holding a hat box he is carrying the way people carry things they are not particularly interested in.

> [!readaloud]
> The man at the door has a burgundy coat of exceptional cut and a hat box he holds with the casual disregard of someone running a small errand. He looks at you across the threshold with the measured warmth of someone who has formed an opinion and is giving you the opportunity to change it.
>
> > "J.B. Nevercott, haberdasher. I believe I'm expected."
>
> He is not expected. He steps inside when invited, sets the hat box on the nearest surface, and maintains the cover until the door is closed and the room has settled. He is precise and conversationally fluent about hat-making — brim widths, felt grades, the relative merits of a Scornubel block — and entirely uninterested in selling any of it.

If the **Ryvarra Identified** outcome was marked, Nevercott knows before the door opens that the party spotted the surveillance. His opening shifts when it closes:

> [!readaloud]
> > "I see you've already done some of the work. That does save time."
>
> His act compresses. Once the room is private, he drops it faster than usual, acknowledging the watcher's presence without giving her name — a small concession that signals his information on the party is current.

When Nevercott drops the haberdasher cover, he does it without ceremony. He sets a plain black card with a silver ship embossed on one face on the nearest flat surface.

> [!readaloud]
> He sets down the hat box and turns to address the room, his manner shifting from a milliner's easy patience to something quieter and more precise.
>
> > "I represent an organization with interests in this city. Discreet interests. The kind that benefit from capable people who know when to be quiet."
>
> He places a plain black card on the table. One face bears a small silver ship in relief.
>
> > "We have a small matter that wants handling. The kind that doesn't get written down. If it goes well, we'll have more to discuss."

> [!social]**The Haberdasher**
>
> J.B. Nevercott (Neutral, Human (apparent), he/him) :: a Bregan D'aerthe contact maintaining a haberdasher cover well past its useful life, out of what appears to be professional habit.
>
> Nevercott is unhurried and exact. He is genuinely curious about the party, and his questions — about their work, their impression of the city, the interesting people they have met — are rather more specific than a haberdasher's should be. He is warm and precise in about equal measure, and does not drift toward familiarity after the cover drops. He does not name Jarlaxle, and he says "Bregan D'aerthe" once, only if pressed directly on the card.
>
> He does not confirm his own identity beyond the name at the door, and his face is somewhat harder to describe after the conversation than it was during it. He takes the hat box when he leaves.
>
> Conversation topics Nevercott is willing to discuss:
> - What the organization wants from the party and what it can offer.
> - The hat trade, which he handles with practiced fluency.
>
> He will not discuss who he works for beyond the card, who the watcher in the alley was, or what the first assignment involves before the party signals interest.

> [!qna]**Who do you represent?**
>
> He turns the card once on the table.
>
> > "An organization with interests in this city that keeps capable people in interesting work. I find that tends to be enough for most conversations."

> [!qna]**The card — that ship?**
>
> > "Bregan D'aerthe." He says it once, without elaboration. "If the name means something to you, you know more than most. If it doesn't, it will mean something in time."

> [!qna]**What is the small matter?**
>
> > "Something the organization would like handled with discretion. The specifics are better discussed when we are closer to the time. The first step is establishing whether you are interested in the conversation at all."

> [!qna]**Why us?**
>
> He considers the room for a moment.
>
> > "You made a reasonable impression on someone whose impressions I trust. In my experience, that's enough to start."

> [!qna]**What does the organization do?**
>
> > "A great deal. Some of it is interesting. People who want a full account before they've seen any of it tend to be asking the wrong question — the question worth asking is whether the work is good and whether the people doing it are good at it, and I can tell you with some confidence that the answer to both is yes."

If the party declines, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
> Nevercott picks up the card from the table, tucks it into his coat, and nods once.
>
> > "Fair enough. If that changes, the booking manager at Seven Masks Theater knows how to reach me."
>
> He takes the hat box and goes. He leaves the hat box on the table for a moment before noticing and coming back for it.

If the party accepts, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
> He leaves a time and a district — not a street — and goes. He mentions once, without elaboration, that the organization is mostly drow. He does not appear to regard this as a problem.

> [!gamemaster]**About the Joining**
>
> Any party member may accept Nevercott's assignment. The hat box contains nothing. The *hat of disguise* Nevercott wears is why his face is harder to describe afterward than it was when he was standing in the room.

## Overview

Bregan D'aerthe has had eyes on the party since the Yawning Portal. During the Trollskull Manor renovation, those eyes follow them home, and a man in a burgundy coat eventually knocks on the door.

## Summary

A Bregan D'aerthe contact named J.B. Nevercott arrived at Trollskull Manor during the renovation period, maintained the haberdasher cover for as long as it served him, named the organization once when pressed, and offered a first assignment on terms the party could accept or decline. He left a black card with a silver ship on the table when he went.
