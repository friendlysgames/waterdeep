# Design Notes: The Dive

## Eye #3 as a Mission Payload

***The Eye that determines who has leverage.*** This mission is the BD chain's most consequential moment for **Sea Maidens Faire**. If **Eye 3 Recovered by BD** is marked, Jarlaxle enters **Sea Maidens Faire** holding all three Eyes — two from the other lair heists and one his people just secured. That posture gives the alliance path real weight: the party can negotiate from Jarlaxle's strength rather than arguing him into cooperation. If the flag is not marked, Eye #3 is in Guild hands, and **Sea Maidens Faire** confronts Jarlaxle with a deficiency rather than a position. Both versions of **Sea Maidens Faire** are playable; the mission determines which one the party walks into.

The flag is intentionally named from Bregan D'aerthe's perspective rather than the party's — "Eye 3 Recovered by BD" rather than "Eye 3 Recovered." This is because **Sea Maidens Faire**'s consequence is about institutional leverage, not personal accomplishment. The party did the work; what **Sea Maidens Faire** cares about is who holds the stone.

## Krebbyg's Exposure

***Why this mission features Krebbyg directly.*** The BD mission chain has used Krebbyg primarily as a relay: he delivers briefs, receives debriefs, and operates at a remove from the field. Here he is in a rowboat at midnight, thirty yards from a Guild dive staging area, personally holding the party's lifeline. He cannot hide if the Guild spots the operation. This is the most vulnerable he has been across all six missions.

The party has now worked with Krebbyg for six missions but has never had reason to look at him directly. This is the moment he becomes more than a booking manager. His "I don't dive" is not an explanation — it is a boundary he delivers without justification, in the same voice he uses for everything else. A party that has been paying attention to him since Mission 1 will read something in that line. A party that hasn't will learn it here.

## The Rope Signal System

***Concrete coordination in an environment that prevents communication.*** The rope signals — one slow tug, two quick, three rapid — are provided as concrete, usable mechanics rather than a narrative gesture toward coordination. Underwater operations that depend on surface coordination fail interestingly when the communication breaks: the party misses a signal, the Guild boat cuts the rope, or Krebbyg gives the emergency signal and the party has to decide how quickly they can actually surface with the Eye in hand. The signals are not decoration; they are a resource the DM can threaten.

## The Sea Maidens Faire Bridge

***What the chain delivers.*** The BD mission chain ends here and hands its flags directly to **Sea Maidens Faire**. The four flags produced — **Eye 3 Recovered by BD** and the three Nar'l flags from Mission 4 — collectively describe what Jarlaxle is holding and who he has lost when **Sea Maidens Faire** begins. A party that completed every BD mission with maximum success approaches Jarlaxle in **Sea Maidens Faire** from a position of demonstrated competence, mutual familiarity, and operational trust built across six missions. Jarlaxle notices. He has noticed everything about them from the start.

A party that failed M6 approaches the same table with Jarlaxle knowing the Guild is aware of his harbor operations, Eye #3 is in Xanathar's hands, and the alliance he was building with the party did not succeed on its most important test. That is a different conversation, and it is worth running.
