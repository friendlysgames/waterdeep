# The Compromised Eye

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> This Social and Exploration Event occurs when an individual Bregan D'aerthe member reaches Renown 8 and 5th level after **Three Nights**, and it ends when the party stands in Nar'l Xibrindas's office with the door shut and a decision to make. J.B. Nevercott briefs by letter, and in this Event the party can:
>
> - Read Nevercott's three-tier order and the map he sends, and learn what he believes about Nar'l's brother.
> - Meet the four drow who wait at the head of the secret staircase in the Castle Ward sewer, and notice that they carry an order they do not mention.
> - Follow a trail of faded red-eye guildsigns to the secret door at area X1 of Xanathar's lair.
> - Reach Nar'l in area X35 while his grell bodyguard is on its rounds, hear his trade and judge whether he is telling the truth.
> - Search his desk for the captain's bag of holding and for the report that links Bregan D'aerthe to the Seven Masks Theater.
>
> Only Bregan D'aerthe members receive the letter. Companions come down the stair and into the lair, and they can help with every part of the mission that follows.

> [!gamemaster]**What Is Actually True**
>
> Nar'l Xibrindas has served as Xanathar's drow advisor for a year, and he reports to Bregan D'aerthe by *Sending* once every third day. Xanathar has grown suspicious without being able to say why, and he has given Nar'l a grell bodyguard with one standing order, to kill the drow at the first sign of disloyalty. The grell also keeps Nar'l from sending or receiving a word unobserved, so the company has heard nothing from him for a tenday. Nevercott has decided that Nar'l is compromised and has ordered three ways of dealing with it, in order of preference.
>
> The beholder's suspicion now has a file behind it. Xanathar told Ahmaergo to find out what is wrong with his advisor, and Ahmaergo's clerks have built a case from two things. The first is three Dock Ward incident reports that Nar'l never forwarded. The second is the word of a fence named Tarn Hobble, who told the clerks that a drow in a Guild coat was seen near the quay on two of the nights the elves died. Nar'l held the reports back to protect his elder brother, because the Dock Ward killings were Soluun's work, and he has told nobody in the company.
>
> J.B. Nevercott is Jarlaxle Baenre in a *hat of disguise*, and "the captain" is the same man aboard his ships as Zardoz Zord. Until **Jarlaxle Unmasked** is marked for a member, every Bregan D'aerthe speaker says "the captain" or "J.", and nobody in this Event says the other name aloud. Nar'l never says it either, and calls the captain "your principal" or "the man who pays you". The captain's letter also hides one thing from the party. He has given the four drow at the stair a separate order, which is to kill Nar'l if he comes up the stair alive and to bring back the bag of holding in any case.
>
> Nar'l has not betrayed the company, and he would if the choice came down to his own life. He keeps the *Report on the Seven Masks Theater* in a false-bottomed drawer as insurance against exactly that choice, and he has not mentioned it to anyone.

> [!gamemaster]**Soluun and the Case**
>
> Read the state that applies. Nar'l, Krebbyg and the letter all follow it, and each reading is repeated where the speaker uses it.
>
> - **Soluun Expelled** (marked by **The Killer's Fate**): Soluun has been put ashore by the captain, and the disownment that began as a cover story is now true. Nobody in this Event calls it a cover story. Nar'l is relieved and ashamed about the relief, and he knows the Guild's case exists because of what his brother did.
> - **Soluun Killed** (marked by **The Dockside Killer**, a Doom Raiders mission): Soluun is dead, and Nar'l knows it. He grieves and is angry in a controlled way, and when his brother's name comes up he does not wait to be asked. If a character tells him who killed Soluun, he says nothing for a long moment and answers every later question in a single sentence, and his one swear word in this Event is the one he says then.
> - **Neither is marked:** Soluun is still alive and still carries forged company papers, and the disownment is a cover story that only members of the company know. Nar'l treats it as a fact he does not want to discuss, and Krebbyg calls it a story at the debrief.
>
> The killings affect the discredit route in **Twelve Minutes**. If Soluun was killed, the Guild's watchers know the killings have stopped, and the replacement reports are easier to believe. If neither is marked, the killer is still loose in the Dock Ward, and they are harder.
>
> Two further outcomes from **The Killer's Fate** change how this Event reads for an individual member:
>
> - **Soluun Pressed (Brother):** The member heard Soluun say that his brother has spent two months burying his trail with the Guild's own paper, and so the member arrives suspecting what the reports hold. Every Insight DC in **Is He Telling the Truth?** is 4 lower for that member.
> - **Soluun Witness Penalized:** Nevercott's letter to that member opens with a cool allusion to the conversation in the corridor aboard the *Scarlet Marpenoth*, and the member reads it in **The Brief**. Nothing else in this mission changes for the member, and the lost Renown is not given back here.

> [!gamemaster]**If Xanathar's Lair Has Already Run**
>
> A party at 5th level has usually finished one lair heist, and **Xanathar's Lair** (unconverted) may be the one. Read that quest's outcomes in prose, using whichever case fits.
>
> - **The lair was entered and left standing.** The Eye is gone from the bowl, Xanathar is in a rage, the Guild is at Alert or Lockdown and Nar'l's position is at its worst. Nevercott's letter is unchanged. Nar'l's trade offers the X18 bypass and Xanathar's present location in place of the Eye, and every Guild response in **Twelve Minutes** uses the Hard row.
> - **The lair was brought down with the smokepowder, and the party took Nar'l out.** There is no mission. Nevercott's letter does not go out, and the member reports to Krebbyg as the debrief only. Mark **Nar'l Extracted** and use the debrief scene in **Twelve Minutes**.
> - **The lair was brought down with the smokepowder, and Nar'l was left inside.** He did not survive the collapse. Nevercott's letter does not go out, and the member reports to Krebbyg as the debrief only. Mark **Nar'l Eliminated** and use the debrief scene in **Twelve Minutes**.
>
> If the grell died in the first heist, remove it from every roster below. Everything else in this Event runs unchanged.

### The Brief

At 08:00 a flying snake finds each eligible member wherever they are. Companions receive no letter, and a member who brings them shows them the map. If several members are eligible, each receives a letter, and only one packet is sealed inside the first.

> [!readaloud]
>
> A flying snake with wings the colour of wet slate drops out of the morning haze and settles on your shoulder with a flick of its tail. It holds a folded sheet of black paper in its jaws and waits until you take it, and the silver ink catches the light as you open it. The hand is careful and a little old-fashioned.

If **Soluun Witness Penalized** is marked for the member, read or paraphrase the following before the rest of the letter:

> [!readaloud]
>
> The first line sits alone above the rest, and the pen has not hurried over it.
>
> > After our conversation in the corridor, I hope you will forgive a short letter and a long favour.

Then read the letter to every member:

> [!readaloud]
>
> > I have valued a man in Xanathar's court for a year, and I am afraid the beholder has begun to suspect him. A grell now follows him everywhere with orders to kill him at the first sign of disloyalty, and I have not had a word from him in a tenday. It grieves me to be writing this, but I must ask you to help, and the captain has asked me to put three requests to you in order of preference.
> >
> > If you can keep him where he is, do so, because he is worth more to us in that chair than anywhere else in Waterdeep. If you cannot keep him there, bring him out alive. If neither is possible, you must see to it that he can never speak to anyone, and I would ask that it be done swiftly and without pain.
> >
> > The enclosed map will take you to him. The grell leaves his office for twelve minutes when the watch changes, so the hour matters, and I have written it on the map. Please bring back the bag I lent him, which is black leather with a silver ship stitched inside the flap, and give it to the four friends who will meet you at the head of the stair. The sealed packet is for him alone.
> >
> > J.B. Nevercott

If **Soluun Expelled** is marked, read or paraphrase the following as a postscript below the signature:

> [!readaloud]
>
> A few lines in a smaller hand follow the signature, and the pen has pressed harder on this part of the page.
>
> > You will know that his brother Soluun has been put out of the company for good. I believe the business of his brother's killings in the Dock Ward is what first drew the Guild's eye to Nar'l.

If **Soluun Killed** is marked, read or paraphrase the following as a postscript below the signature:

> [!readaloud]
>
> A few lines in a smaller hand follow the signature, and the pen has pressed harder on this part of the page.
>
> > You will know that his brother Soluun is dead. I believe the business of his brother's killings in the Dock Ward is what first drew the Guild's eye to Nar'l, and I do not know how he has taken the news.

If neither is marked, read or paraphrase the following as a postscript below the signature:

> [!readaloud]
>
> A few lines in a smaller hand follow the signature, and the pen has pressed harder on this part of the page.
>
> > You will know that his brother Soluun was put out of the company in public for his killings in the Dock Ward. I believe the attention those killings drew is what first fixed the Guild's eye on Nar'l.

> [!exploration]**Reading the Letter**
>
> Any character who studies the letter and makes a successful DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check notices that the pen paused before the third request. The ink pools at the start of "If neither is possible," and the line is set closer to the signature than the two above it, which suggests that it was added in a second sitting after the captain read the first draft. The character concludes that the order to silence Nar'l was an afterthought, and that Nevercott may have given other instructions to other people.
>
> - **Result of 19 or higher:** The character also notices that the letter says "the four friends who will meet you" and does not say what those four have been told to do.

> [!exploration]**The Map and the Packet**
>
> The map shows a grate in the floor of the Castle Ward main sewer, a spiral stair beneath it, and a passage that runs from the foot of the stair to the lair. Red eyes are drawn at three junctions in the passage, and a time is written beside the last one in the same hand as the letter: *13:00, the change of the watch*. The packet is a flat bundle of paper sealed with silver wax, marked *For him, and for no one else*.
>
> A character who breaks the seal finds four sheets that describe a Black Network enforcer named Dorrim Ketch, who works the quay at night, together with the name of the Dock Ward drinking house he uses and a description of the long grey coat he wears. The packet is real intelligence and costs the company a man it would sooner see dead. A character who opens it and reseals it badly loses nothing, but Nar'l notices the seal and says so, and the +1 Renown for protecting his cover is not lost by it.

### The Head of the Stair

The party reaches the grate at 11:15. Four drow stand on the ledge above the channel, waiting for the party with their hoods back and a shuttered lantern at their feet.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The map ends at an iron grate set into the floor of the Castle Ward sewer, where the main channel narrows and a stone ledge runs along the wall above the water. Four tall figures in dark cloaks wait on that ledge with their hoods thrown back, and the lantern at their feet shows grey skin and white hair on every one of them. The nearest steps forward and lifts the grate with one hand, so that you can see a stair of black stone curling away into the dark.
>
> > You'll be the ones with the captain's letter. I'm Arannis, and these are Beldar, Rylvar and Draknafein, and we'll stay up here with the lantern, because four more drow on that trail would be smelled out inside an hour. If you need something carried or someone covered, say so now, and if you come back up with trouble behind you, we'll deal with it.

> [!social]**The Man Who Holds the Grate**
>
> Arannis Nur'zekk (Neutral Evil, Drow, he/him) :: the spokesman of four drow soldiers who wait at the head of the secret stair, polite and watchful and carrying an order he has not mentioned.
>
> Arannis talks in plain, practical sentences and does not waste breath on opinions. He is courteous to a company member and a little stiff with any companion, and he checks the party's gear while he talks, as a man does who is counting. Beldar and Rylvar Tlabbath are brothers who finish each other's remarks, and Draknafein Uriss says nothing at all unless he is asked a direct question. All four carry loaded hand crossbows and will help with anything the party names that does not involve going below.
>
> Conversation topics Arannis is willing to discuss include:
>
> - The trail below and how long it takes.
> - What the four of them are here to do.
> - The bag of holding, which he asks for twice.
> - Nar'l Xibrindas, whom he has never met and has heard described as a clever man who talks too much.

> [!qna]**What are your orders?**
>
> Arannis answers without looking at the other three.
>
> > We're to see you to the stair, wait at the top and help you with whatever you need, whatever it costs us. The captain was very clear on that part, and he's not a man who says "whatever it costs" about things he doesn't mean.

> [!qna]**What's down there?**
>
> > A long stair, then a passage that was dug for smugglers a century back, and Nar'l's old guildsign painted on the walls at every junction, a red eye, mostly worn away. It's an hour and a half to the door at the far end if you don't get lost, and the map has the time written on it for a reason.

> [!qna]**Why only four?**
>
> > Because the Guild doesn't know this route exists, and the captain would like it to stay that way. Four of us on the ledge is a patrol. Four of us in the passage is a message to everyone who lives down there.

> [!qna]**Do you know Nar'l?**
>
> > I've never laid eyes on him, but I know what he's worth. He's been sitting in the beholder's lap for a year and sending us what he hears, and I'd rather he stayed there, if you want my opinion. Will you be bringing him back up the stair?

> [!exploration]**What the Four Carry**
>
> Any character who watches Arannis and makes a successful DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check notices that all four have their crossbows loaded and cocked, and that Arannis has asked twice whether the party means to bring Nar'l back up the stair. He asks with the flat attention of a man who has been told what to do in each case. The four are not hostile to the party, but they carry an order that depends on the answer.
>
> If the character presses Arannis, he says only that the captain was thorough, and he does not volunteer more. The truth is in **What Is Actually True**, and the party meets it at the head of the stair in **Twelve Minutes** if Nar'l is brought out alive.

### The Guildsign Trail

The party reaches the foot of the stair at about 11:30. The trail is ninety minutes of old smugglers' tunnel, and a party that follows it without losing time reaches the secret door at X1 at 13:00, in time for the change of the watch.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The stair ends in a low brick passage where the air smells of old fish and wet stone, and a thin trickle of water runs along the middle of the floor. A red eye the size of a dinner plate has been painted on the wall at about the height of a man's shoulder, so faded that you have to hold the lantern against it to see the pupil. Farther on, the passage splits around a pillar, and a second red eye on the left-hand wall has been scraped halfway off by something that left long grooves behind it.

> [!exploration]**Following the Red Eyes**
>
> The passage has three junctions: the foot of the stair, the pillar and the flooded bend. At each one, the character leading makes a DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check to find the painted eye, and another character who gives the Help action grants the leader Advantage. A party that carries the map's key (written in the corner of the map) can use DC 10 instead.
>
> - **Success:** The party follows the eye without losing time.
> - **Failure:** The party backtracks and loses 30 minutes.
> - **Result of 18 or higher:** The leader also notices a fresh scrape on the floor at the third junction, left by something that dragged its tentacles through the water. The grell has walked this passage within the last tenday, probably looking for the way Nar'l's visitors would come.
>
> The party reaches the X1 door at 13:00 plus 30 minutes for each failed check. If the arrival time falls between watch changes, the party waits behind the door until the next change, and a failed junction costs nothing worse than waiting. The wait has one effect. Nar'l expected the party at 13:00, and a party that arrives at 14:00 finds him with his hands pressed flat on the desk and his first words are "You're late." His calm is cracking, and a DC 10 Wisdom (Insight) check reads it more easily.

> [!hazard]**Two Rats at the Second Junction**
>
> Two **Giant Rats** live in the pillar's base, and the Guild keeps the passage monitored by feeding them. They notice the party when it passes and attack.
>
> #### Giant Rat Tactics
> At the start of combat, both rats attack the character at the front of the party.
>
> During combat, the rats:
> - Bite the nearest creature and flee into the pillar's gaps when one of them is hurt.
> - Squeal when struck, which carries down the passage but does not carry as far as the lair.
>
> The encounter ends when both rats are dead or have fled. A party that makes a DC 12 Dexterity (Stealth) group check passes the pillar without waking them. A party that kills them in two rounds loses no time, and a party that chases them loses 30 minutes.

### The Door at X1

At 13:00, or at the next change of the watch if the party is late, the passage ends at a stone slab with a red eye cut into it.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The passage ends at a slab of dressed stone with a red eye cut into its face, and a ring of iron is set into the wall beside it. When you lean on the ring, the slab swings inward on a counterweight, and a corridor opens ahead with lamps burning at long intervals along its ceiling. Somewhere far off a brass bell rings the hour, and booted feet pass in a cross-corridor and fade, and the passage falls so still that you can hear the lamps burn.
>
> A short way ahead, a purple thing the size of a large barrel drifts out of a doorway and along the corridor away from you, trailing a skirt of tentacles that brush the stones. It has a hooked beak and no eyes at all, and it moves with a smooth patience along a route that it plainly follows every hour. When it has gone around the bend at the corridor's far end, the door it left stands open on a lamp turned low.

### Nar'l's Office

The corridor is empty because Nar'l cleared it for the watch change. The door ahead is X35, and the party has about a minute to get inside before the grell's route comes round to a place where it might see them.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The office is small and tidy, with a desk, a stool, a wall of ledgers and a single lamp turned low. A thin-faced drow sits behind the desk with a stylus in one hand, and he does not look surprised when you come in. He sets the stylus in its tray, folds his hands on the blotter and looks at each of you in turn.
>
> > I thought it might be today. Please close the door behind you, because the grell keeps to a regular round and I'd estimate that we have about twelve minutes before it comes back this way.

When the door is shut, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
>
> The latch clicks, and the drow lets his breath out slowly and does not move his hands. He speaks more softly now, and he keeps his eyes on your faces.
>
> > I know who sent you, and I suspect I know what you were told to do with me. Before you decide, I'd like to put something on the desk. The Master keeps what you would call the Eye in the goldfish bowl in his sanctum, area nineteen, and I can tell you how to reach it without the panopticus noticing, by way of the audience chamber, where I'm able to log the sensors as under calibration. In return I'd ask only to leave this room alive. What would you offer?

> [!gamemaster]**The Twelve Minutes**
>
> The clock starts when the door latches, and the party spends minutes from a total of twelve. Track it aloud at the table so that the players feel it.
>
> - The opening exchange, from the latch to the end of Nar'l's trade, costs 2 minutes.
> - Each question the party puts to Nar'l costs 1 minute, whether or not a **qna** block below covers it.
> - Reading him with Insight, noticing the desk and glancing at the shelves cost nothing.
> - Searching the desk, doctoring reports, packing and every action in **Twelve Minutes** cost the minutes listed there.
> - At the twelfth minute the grell returns, and **Twelve Minutes** describes what follows.
>
> Nar'l hands over the trade as soon as the party says it will hear him out. If the party refuses to listen, the trade still stands, but the exchange costs 1 minute more while he asks again. Mark **Nar'l Bypass Learned** when the party has heard it.

> [!social]**The Advisor Who Spoke Carefully**
>
> Nar'l Xibrindas (Neutral Evil, Drow, he/him) :: Xanathar's drow advisor and the company's man in the Guild, a thin and precise mage who has run out of ways to be careful.
>
> Nar'l speaks softly and in qualified sentences, saying less than he knows and phrasing everything so that it can be denied later. He glances at the door before he answers a hard question, out of a habit built by a year of the grell at his shoulder. He never says the captain's name and calls him "your principal" or "the man who pays you", and he calls Xanathar "the Master" with exactly the weight a man gives a word that is overheard. He swears once, only when a question about his brother breaks him, and it is a tight, frightened "fuck".
>
> Conversation topics Nar'l is willing to discuss include:
>
> - His trade, which he explains in full and repeats if asked.
> - The grell, and the standing order it carries.
> - Why the Guild suspects him, which he answers if pressed.
> - His brother Soluun, which he avoids until someone says the name.
> - What he would offer in return for his life, which is the question he returns to most often.
>
> Nar'l is nervous and far from weak, and a party that threatens him gets nothing from him that he has not offered freely.

> [!qna]**The grell?**
>
> Nar'l glances at the door before he answers.
>
> > It was assigned to me four months ago, and I'm fairly certain it was given a single instruction, which is to kill me when it sees a reason. It doesn't speak, and I can't tell you what it thinks it has seen. It goes on its round at the change of the watch and it comes back after twelve minutes, and I would prefer that we were not both in this room when it does.

> [!qna]**Why does the Guild suspect you?**
>
> > Because of a gap in my reports, which is a small thing that a clerk noticed and a larger man chose to take seriously. Three reports on the Dock Ward never reached the Master's desk over the last two months. I held them back for reasons of my own, and when Ahmaergo's clerks asked why, I didn't have an answer I could give them.

> [!qna]**What do you want?**
>
> > To leave this lair alive, preferably by a route that doesn't involve the grell. I'd like to keep my position if that can be arranged, since it's worth a great deal to your principal, but I wouldn't insist on it. I'd prefer a life with fewer risks, and I have no strong feelings about where it's spent.

> [!qna]**About the Eye?**
>
> > Area nineteen, the bowl in the Master's sanctum, where the little gemstone sits among the decorative rocks at the bottom. He keeps it with the fish, and I wouldn't touch either without a good reason. The audience chamber beyond area eighteen is the only approach with any cover, and I can log the sensors as under calibration for ten minutes at a time, so if you're coming in, come that way.

If **Soluun Expelled** is marked, read or paraphrase the following when a character asks about his brother:

> [!qna]**About Soluun?**
>
> Nar'l's hands, which have been flat on the desk, do not move.
>
> > He was put ashore by your principal, and I'm told it was done properly and that it's permanent. I'd say I'm relieved, and I'm ashamed of being relieved, because he's my brother. The reports I held back were about what he did on the docks, and I think you know what that was.

If **Soluun Killed** is marked, read or paraphrase the following when a character asks about his brother:

> [!qna]**About Soluun?**
>
> Nar'l's face stays still while his voice drops until you have to lean in to hear it.
>
> > He's dead, and I heard it from a Guild watcher at the quay before anyone in your company bothered to tell me. I held back the reports about what he did on the docks, because I thought I could keep him alive by keeping them quiet, and I was wrong. I'd be grateful if we didn't talk about it for the next ten minutes.

If neither is marked, read or paraphrase the following when a character asks about his brother:

> [!qna]**About Soluun?**
>
> Nar'l's eyes go to the door, and then back to your faces.
>
> > My brother does things that I wouldn't have chosen for him, and I've been covering them for as long as I've been in this chair. The three reports I held back were about the Dock Ward. I'd like to leave it at that, if you don't mind, since I'm not at all certain the grell can't hear through the wall.

> [!exploration]**Is He Telling the Truth?**
>
> Any character who watches Nar'l through his opening speech and makes a successful Wisdom (Insight) check reads him according to the result. A member marked **Soluun Pressed (Brother)** reads each DC below 4 lower, so that the DCs are 6, 10 and 14.
>
> - **DC 10:** He is not panicking. He is unnaturally calm for a man who expects to be killed, and the composure looks rehearsed.
> - **DC 14:** He is telling mostly the truth. He is not building a story as he talks, and the isolation he describes is real.
> - **DC 18:** His account is substantially true, and he really has not worked against the company. The only thing he skates over is the Dock Ward, and when he says the word "reports" his eyes drop to the lowest ledger on the shelf.
>
> He would trade the company for his own life if the choice came down to it, and no check reveals that. A party that reads him as honest has read him correctly, and a party that reads him as a liar has read his self-interest.

> [!exploration]**The Desk and the Shelf**
>
> The desk has two drawers, and the shelf behind it holds the ledgers he keeps for the Guild. Nar'l hands over anything the party asks about for free once it has agreed to hear him out. If he is dead or unwilling, the same things can be found by search, and each search costs the minutes shown.
>
> - **The bag (1 minute).** A small compartment under the left armrest of the chair holds a black key, and a character who searches the chair and makes a successful DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check finds it. The key opens both drawers, which can also be picked with a DC 17 Dexterity check using Thieves' Tools or broken with a DC 15 Strength (Athletics) check. The first drawer holds Nar'l's spellbook, a black leather tome wrapped in webbing that contains the prepared spells and *Sending*. The second drawer holds the captain's *bag of holding*, which is black leather with a silver ship stitched inside the flap and is empty. Nar'l borrowed it to smuggle kegs of smokepowder into the lair.
> - **The false drawer (1 minute).** A character who searches the desk and makes a successful DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation) check finds a false bottom under the second drawer, and beneath it the *Report on the Seven Masks Theater*, written in Nar'l's hand. It traces the theater's ownership to Rongquan Mystere, ties the theater to the Sea Maidens Faire and has been kept from Xanathar for eleven months. Nar'l keeps it as insurance and does not mention it.
> - **The cipher log (1 minute).** A bound log of company correspondence in the captain's cipher sits among the ledgers, and a character who reads it with a successful DC 16 Intelligence check learns the dates of Nar'l's reports and the names of the cell contacts in Waterdeep. It is dangerous to leave behind and tiring to carry.
> - **The lowest ledger (1 minute).** A character who searches the shelf and makes a successful DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation) check finds the three unsent Dock Ward reports tucked into the back of the lowest ledger, each in Nar'l's neat hand. A character who made the DC 18 Insight reading finds them without a check.

### Concluding the Event
