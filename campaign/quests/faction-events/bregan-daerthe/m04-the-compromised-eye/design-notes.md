# Design Notes: The Compromised Eye

## The Three-Flag Architecture

***Why three outcomes instead of one multi-state.*** The three resolution options produce results that matter differently in different downstream arcs. Nar'l Active and Nar'l Extracted both represent success, but they create different capabilities: Active means the party has an embedded source they can query before **Xanathar's Lair**; Extracted means they have a living guide who can walk them through the lair in person. Nar'l Eliminated produces a different kind of result — **Xanathar's Lair** loses specific entry intelligence, and Jarlaxle's posture in **Sea Maidens Faire** shifts. Three separate outcomes let each downstream quest query exactly the relevant condition rather than unpacking a single "what happened to Nar'l" variable.

The Eliminated outcome exists because removing it would require the document to pretend the party cannot make that choice. It is presented without a mechanical penalty beyond what the fiction produces — the mission still technically completes — but the narrative cost is clear. Jarlaxle is not vindictive; he is evaluative. A party that killed Nar'l against the mission brief has told him something about how they operate under pressure.

## Nar'l's Hostility as a Real Obstacle

***The DC 14 check is not a formality.*** Nar'l has operational reasons to treat strangers at his contact point as a threat. A party that rolls poorly on the initial persuasion is not locked out — the additional approaches (different party member, different angle, specific intel disclosure) are listed to give the table tools rather than a single-attempt gate. But the hostility is not performative; it is the natural response of a three-year deep-cover operative who has just learned that his cover may already be blown.

The DC 14 threshold is about the mission's dramatic register. He is afraid. He is also professional. Winning his cooperation should cost the party something — time, good rolls, a reveal of what they know — rather than being automatic.

## Soluun's Cross-Faction Thread

***The brother who costs him everything.*** Soluun Xibrindas appears in Doom Raiders M1 as the Dock Ward drow operating under a forged token — a low-level encounter whose significance is withheld at the time. DR-M1's design notes name the connection explicitly: Soluun's unauthorized presence is the root cause of Nar'l's exposure here. A party running both faction chains will eventually connect these events. A party running only the BD chain learns the backstory in Krebbyg's brief and understands it as the story of a professional who chose family over operational security. Neither judgment is offered. The facts are sufficient.

## Jarlaxle's Private Dinner

***The relationship change.*** The in-person appearance by Zardoz Zord at Seven Masks is the mission's emotional payoff. The party has been working for an organization that has never explained itself, been paid anonymously, and protected a hostage without being told its significance. The private dinner does not explain any of that — but it acknowledges that Jarlaxle has been watching the whole time and has decided the party is worth meeting as people rather than just as operatives. This is the moment the BD mission chain shifts from professional arrangement to something more complex, and **Sea Maidens Faire** builds on it directly.
