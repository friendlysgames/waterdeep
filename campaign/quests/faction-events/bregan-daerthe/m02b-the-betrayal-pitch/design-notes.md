# Design Notes: The Betrayal Pitch

## Jarlaxle Evaluates the Party

***The test within the test.*** The Betrayal Pitch is where Jarlaxle determines whether the party is worth investing in. He has established through M1 and M2 that they can handle basic delivery work and infiltration under social cover. Now he needs to know whether they can plan and execute a shipboard approach, handle unexpected complications without panicking, and make good decisions when the job turns out to be different from what they were told.

Taking the jade compass — obsidian was not specified, and jade is what is there — marks the party as competent. Taking the Ledger, alone or alongside the compass, and asking Nevercott what it means is what earns their place in Mission 3. Bregan D'aerthe's later missions, protecting Nar'l, running the theater handoff, the harbor dive, all require a party that exercises judgment under incomplete information, and the Betrayal Pitch is where Jarlaxle finds out whether this one does.

## The Scarlet Marpenoth Access Path

***Planting the submarine before Sea Maidens Faire.*** The from-below approach introduces the *Scarlet Marpenoth* as a location before **Sea Maidens Faire** names it directly. A party that used this route has been inside the submarine; they know the hatch location, the lower deck layout, and that it can be accessed from below without alerting the topside crew. In **Sea Maidens Faire**, this prior knowledge can serve as an entry advantage or a negotiation point with Jarlaxle directly.

The *Scarlet Marpenoth*'s presence is not announced in the approach description. It is described as a submarine shape attached to the *Eyecatcher*'s keel. The party can investigate further or not. The hatch is unlocked from below because Jarlaxle chose not to lock it.

## The Jade Compass

***Small lies about minor details.*** J.B. Nevercott said "obsidian." The compass on the shelf is jade. A party that looks for the obsidian compass and finds only jade has a real decision in front of them: bring the wrong compass, bring the Ledger instead, bring both, or bring nothing and report the compass wasn't there. All four answers tell Jarlaxle something useful about how the party operates under ambiguity. The operational token he pays with is a flat piece of carved obsidian shaped like a compass, which is his quiet acknowledgment of the joke.
