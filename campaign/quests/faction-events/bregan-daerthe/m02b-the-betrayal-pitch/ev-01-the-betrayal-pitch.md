# The Betrayal Pitch

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
>
> This Exploration Event is a rigged test — Jarlaxle is evaluating the party on shipboard infiltration, their response to unexpected complications, and whether they will take a job without sufficient information. In this Event:
>
> - Three approach routes to the *Eyecatcher* each have different risk profiles.
> - The crew is deliberately placed to make the approach feel earned without actually stopping the party.
> - The stateroom contains the thing the party was not asked to take. Whether they take it is the test.
> - Zardoz Zord returns early. He is not a threat. He is watching.
> - J.B. Nevercott's reaction the next day is calibrated entirely to what the party brought back.

**Background (DM only)**

There is no obsidian compass. Jarlaxle invented it. The *Eyecatcher* is his ship. Zardoz Zord is his persona. The crew are his people, running at half complement and under orders not to physically intercept anyone who reaches the stateroom corridor — they will make themselves visible and move toward sounds, but they will not block the corridor or use force.

What is in the stateroom: the *Ledger of Commissioned Automata*, open on the chart table to the most recent entries. This is Jarlaxle's complete record of every Nimblewright sold, with purchaser names, delivery addresses, and programming notes. The voided Cassalanter entry is visible on the open page. Jarlaxle placed it. He wants to see if the party is smart enough to take the thing they were not asked to take.

Zardoz Zord boards early — by design — so the party sees his face at the moment they are most committed to a clean exit. This is not a threat. It is information he wants them to have.

### The Brief

J.B. Nevercott finds the party again. Same Yawning Portal. Same coat. He sits down with the air of someone who has thought about this conversation and found it interesting.

> > "There's a vessel in the harbor, the *Eyecatcher* — the Sea Maidens Faire flagship. Captain Zardoz Zord's personal ship. I need a specific item retrieved from his stateroom. A carved obsidian compass, about so."

He holds his thumb and index finger two inches apart.

> > "The captain is going to be ashore this evening for a performance. His crew is reliable but not alert. I can provide a skiff and a suggested route to the hull."

He names a price. He does not explain why he wants the compass, or why it is aboard a carnival ship.

### Planning the Approach

The *Eyecatcher* is anchored in the deep harbor, accessible by skiff. The party has one hour before Zardoz's shore performance ends. Three approach options:

**Swimming:** Cold harbor water, 60-foot swim to the hull. **DC 12 Strength (Athletics)** checks; characters with a swim speed succeed automatically. Arrival on the hull's shadow side is unobserved.

**Skiff:** J.B. Nevercott's provided skiff, with a shuttered lantern at the stern. Docking at the service ladder is quiet at a walking pace; rushing it takes a **DC 12 Dexterity (Stealth)** check to keep the dinghy from knocking.

**From below:** Characters with water breathing access can approach the *Scarlet Marpenoth* — a submarine shape attached to the *Eyecatcher*'s keel. The hatch is not locked from below. It leads into the hull's lower deck, thirty feet from the stateroom stairs. The route is the quietest of the three.

### The Ship

Four crewmembers are visible topside, three below. All are drow in disguise. A **DC 14 Wisdom (Insight)** check on sustained observation reveals that they move like trained soldiers rather than sailors — precise headings, no idle conversation, each one watching a quadrant.

Group **DC 12 Dexterity (Stealth)** checks to move between decks. On a failure, the nearest crewmember pivots toward the sound and moves to investigate — they do not call out, and they do not block the corridor, but they will be standing in it. A second failure on the same deck means two crewmembers converge; they position themselves where the party can see them, then hold. They are watching, not stopping.

**The stateroom:** Unlocked. A desk, a weapons rack, an elaborate wardrobe, and a chart table. On the chart table: the *Ledger of Commissioned Automata*, open to the most recent entries.

> **[GM]**
>
> #### The Ledger
> A **DC 11 Intelligence (Investigation)** check identifies it as a customer registry — names, delivery addresses, Nimblewright model specifications, and pricing. A **DC 13** check reads the most recent entry: a voided Cassalanter order, cancelled with a forfeited deposit, no stated reason. The cancellation is dated nine months ago. A character who has seen Nimblewright construction knows that a custom order of this size takes months of advance planning — the Cassalanters were acquiring a spy device and then stopped. A **DC 15** check extrapolates: they did not cancel because they lost interest. They cancelled because they realized the device would be used to watch them as much as to watch others.
>
> The obsidian compass is not here. There is a carved **jade** compass on the wardrobe shelf. It was never specified as obsidian.

### The Exit

Zardoz Zord boards early from a skiff that was already in position below the rail. He is at the top of the boarding ladder before the party is clear of the stateroom corridor. He sees them. He smiles.

He does not stop them. He does not reach for a weapon. He is dressed for a performance — the full Faire captain costume, hat and all — and he watches the party get back to their skiff with the expression of someone who expected this outcome and is not disappointed by it.

> > "I do hope J.B.'s commission was specific enough. He can be vague."

He lets them go.

### Concluding the Event

> **[GM]**
>
> Two days later, J.B. Nevercott meets the party to collect the commission. He is not surprised by whatever they brought — the jade compass, the Ledger, nothing, or some combination. He pays what he promised, plus a bonus, without explanation.
>
> **+1 Renown** if the party returns something to J.B. Nevercott — the compass (jade counts), the Ledger, or both.
> **+1 Renown (supplementary)** if the party takes the Ledger regardless of the commission, recognizing it as more valuable than what they were nominally sent for. Krebbyg, when told: "They make good decisions under pressure." Jarlaxle, when told: "Excellent. Send them Mission 3."
>
> The 100 gp and the operational token are on the table when J.B. Nevercott arrives. He counts the gp over to the party and slides the token across without comment. It is a flat piece of carved obsidian — the compass he asked for, give or take.

## Overview

J.B. Nevercott wants a compass from a ship's stateroom. The ship belongs to a man named Zardoz Zord. The compass does not exist. The ship belongs to J.B. Nevercott.

## Summary

The party infiltrated the *Eyecatcher* and reached the stateroom, which contained no obsidian compass but did hold the *Ledger of Commissioned Automata* — a complete record of every Nimblewright Jarlaxle had sold, including a voided Cassalanter commission. Zardoz Zord boarded early and watched them leave without intervening. J.B. Nevercott paid the following day and seemed unsurprised by whatever the party brought back.
