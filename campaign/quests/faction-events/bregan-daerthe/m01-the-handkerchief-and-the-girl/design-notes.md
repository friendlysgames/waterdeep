# Design Notes: The Handkerchief and the Girl

## The Parade and the Pickpocket

The restored draft moved the job to a private reception hosted by an invented noblewoman, which discarded the one thing the source did well. The Twin Parades gives a first mission a crowd to hide in, a fixed target, a clock and a walk across the city, and it lets the party plan a small heist before the large ones begin. The mission now runs on Ches 21 beside **The Twin Parades**, at the Great Drunkard on Bazaar Street, with Maester Roderick Bartlethorpe as the target. Lady Ashford is cut, because she existed nowhere else in the campaign and the Castle Ward reception had no source behind it.

The mission has more than a roll in it. The party picks a route to Bartlethorpe, takes the cloth by hand or by talk, and escapes a Watch constable if it was noticed. It then crosses the city to a deadline and decides whether to read what it carries. Each failure costs something specific, and the mission ends in a fight only if the party starts one, in which case Bartlethorpe is a **Noble** and the Watch arrives in the third round.

## One Message, One Mechanism

The draft used embroidered knots, a cipher key that Vessin held and a DC 17 check for a partial decode. The source has a scent-coded message that a Perception check or *detect magic* can find. The knots are cut, and the scent is the only mechanism. The party cannot read the language and learns only that a message exists, and at a high result that it names a waterfront place in the evening. That is enough to make the party suspect that a pickpocketing job is covering something larger, and it never gives them a plot.

## "Do Not Open the Handkerchief"

Nevercott's single instruction exists to be tested. Reading the scent breaks no rule that anyone can see, and the party has the cloth in its hands for an hour. The debrief asks the question directly, and the honest answer earns a bonus while a lie costs every bonus if Vessin's book contradicts it. Vessin writes down what the party says in front of her, so the party learns that the company keeps records even on children in crates. That fits the faction's long game, in which the company asks for competence and discretion and rewards honesty it did not ask for.

## Vessin

Vessin is eleven in the source and is kept at eleven. The draft made her sixteen and gave her eighteen months of fieldwork that nothing else in the campaign repeats. She keeps the source's carved sun, her book and her line "He said you'd come today". No later page reads her yet, so the earlier draft's promise that she returns in **Sea Maidens Faire** is dropped until that quest is converted, and a party that fed her a pie here has at least met a person rather than a name.

## The Little Hat

The nimblewright's hat is the carry-forward from the source. It is a detail that matters later and never announces itself: a figure that does not retrieve its hat is a figure that is not a performer. **Nimblewright Noticed** is the same outcome that **The Twin Parades** marks, so a party that saw the construct in either place carries the same knowledge. No converted quest reads it yet, and it is logged for **Fireball!**.

## Out-of-Scope Notes

Vessin's age, the Faire performer who left the cloth and the flower-stall detail are invented here. Bartlethorpe's alignment and species are not stated in the source, and this page treats him as a Lawful Neutral human. Contradictions with files outside this folder are left unedited and listed for the Bregan D'aerthe section of the out-of-scope notes: the organization page and the Factions Guide still describe the first mission as theater tickets that lead to Krebbyg; **Coin Pouches** now uses a three-lead trace and reads **Handkerchief Delivered** for its first pouch; **The Twin Parades** gives different processions than the Twin Parades addendum; the **First Meeting** says Nevercott leaves a time and a district rather than the manor as the brief's venue; and a later mission draft says Krebbyg has worked with Vessin for three years.
