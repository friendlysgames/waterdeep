# The Tenday Watch

> **[GM]**
>
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
>
> This Exploration/Social Event begins when the party meets Jalester at the Yawning Portal and runs through the first seven days of the detail. In this Event:
>
> - The party meets Mareva Coile and Berna and receives the supervisor rotation schedule.
> - Four optional day-beats texture the tenday: a Watch encounter (Day 2), Herath's coin purse (Day 4), Berna's unsolicited briefing (Day 6), and a guild jurisdictional argument (Day 7).
> - On Day 4, a DC 13 Passive Perception may reveal a figure watching from Margaster Street — the first sign the escort is itself being scouted.
> - No combat occurs in this Event. The pressure builds through detail and repetition.
>
> This Event ends when the party completes Day 7. The **Night Three** Event follows immediately.

**Background (DM only)**

The attacks on Dungsweepers' Guild workers are not random gang violence. **Delvon Harsk**, a route supervisor, has been accepting weekly payments from **Pirn** — a Xanathar Guild street agent — in exchange for timing information from the route schedule. Delvon is not malicious. He is 300 gp in debt to a moneylender and convinced himself the scheduling data was harmless. The injured workers have told him otherwise. He is in too deep to stop voluntarily and has no idea who to confess to even if he wanted to.

Pirn is not acting on formal Guild authority. This is a low-level operation at the margins of what his handler permits — destabilizing the Dungsweepers' Guild contract with the city to create openings for Guild muscle. Pirn's handler does not know Delvon's name. Only Pirn does.

### At the Yawning Portal

**Jalester Silvermane** picks a table far enough from the bar that the conversation won't carry and delivers the mission without preamble. The gang war is making the Dock Ward dangerous. The Alliance offered protection to the Dungsweepers' Guild route coordinator. Three of her subordinates were roughed up this tenday, and she is the next logical target. He needs people willing to walk the route every day for a tenday. The Watch is stretched thin. This is real work.

> > "Six bells, Muleskull Tavern, Ship Street, Dock Ward. Her name is Mareva Coile. She'll give you the schedule. Do the work."

He offers two renown on completion and no tangible reward. He expects professionalism and does not say so.

### The Muleskull Tavern

**Mareva Coile** is 45, sharp-tempered, and skeptical of outside help. She manages the route as a coordinator — holds the contracts, keeps the paperwork straight, speaks to Jalester. She does not apologize for being skeptical. She makes her concern explicit.

> [!npc-narrative]**Mareva Coile**
> A sharp-eyed woman in her mid-forties, already at the table before you arrive. Her expression is that of someone who has asked for outside help once and is not pleased to have needed to. She does not pretend otherwise.

> > "If my workers stop showing up because they're scared, the city fills with filth in a tenday. That's worse than a black eye."

She gives the party the supervisor rotation schedule and names the four supervisors who have access to the real-time route timing: Delvon Harsk, Orryn Saltwick, Bremma Dunnet, and Cass Fildura.

The field crew leader is **Berna**: a dwarf woman of approximately sixty with a grey braid and the expression of someone who has lived entirely on her own terms.

> [!npc-narrative]**Berna**
> A dwarf woman of approximately sixty with a grey braid, standing behind Mareva with the expression of someone who has had a great deal of time to develop her assessments of other people. She is reserving judgment — visibly, deliberately. Twenty years with the Guild have given her a particular quality of stillness. Twenty years with the Guild, seventeen of them on the Dock and Trades Ward circuits. She is economical. She will not make small talk. She expects the party to stay close, look capable, and not get underfoot. She will soften over the tenday as she determines they are, in her assessment, not useless — but this will manifest as acknowledgment, not warmth.

> [!profile]+ **Profile: Berna**
>
> **Roleplaying Information**
> ***Resonance.*** Berna should inspire respect through her absolute competence, warmth through her eventual willingness to acknowledge the party as useful, and gentle discomfort through her capacity to say difficult things with perfect plainness.
>
> ***Emotions.*** Berna most often feels watchful, impatient, dry, quietly proud of her crew, and tired in a way that does not diminish her.
>
> ***Motivations.*** Berna wants to finish the route on time, protect her workers, and be left alone by anyone who will make her job harder.
>
> ***Inspirations.*** Channel Sam Gamgee (*The Lord of the Rings*), Sergeant Johnson (*Halo*), and Nan (*Mary Poppins Returns*).
>
> **Character Information**
> ***Persona.*** To the world, Berna is a grey-braided crew leader with no interest in anything outside her route. To those she trusts, she is fiercely protective of every person on her crew. Deep down, she is still angry that the city doesn't see the people who keep it clean.
>
> ***Morale.*** In a fight, Berna puts herself between the crew and whatever is happening, then assesses. She does not panic. She does not freeze. She has been in three knife fights and won two of them.
>
> ***Relationships.*** Berna is Mareva Coile's field crew leader and the closest thing to a guardian that Herath has had since he joined the Guild.

### The Tenday — Selected Days

The first three days pass without incident. Use two or three of the following beats to texture the routine before Night Three arrives. These are not encounters — they are observations. The mission's emotional content lives here.

#### Day 2 — The Watch Check

While working near the Market, a City Watch officer stops the crew for a routine check. The officer treats the workers with undisguised contempt — the dungsweepers are beneath notice, their escort barely registers. Berna's jaw tightens but she says nothing. After the Watch officer leaves, she goes back to work without comment.

If a party member objects or steps in on the crew's behalf: Berna does not acknowledge it immediately. Two blocks later, without looking up from her work, she says:

> > "Don't make it worse."

She does not mean it as a rebuke. She means it as advice. The party that understands the distinction is the party she will begin to acknowledge.

#### Day 4 — Herath's Coin Purse

While working through a Market alley, one of the sweepers — a young Calishite man named **Herath** — finds a coin purse containing 12 gp, apparently dropped by someone in a hurry. He turns it in to the nearest Watch post without discussion, then returns to work. He catches the party watching and shrugs.

> > "It's not mine."

That is the entirety of his moral framework on the subject. He returns to his broom.

#### Day 4 — The Watcher

During the morning route, a **DC 13 Passive Perception** notices a figure watching from a second-story window on Margaster Street — a young man who keeps appearing at different points along the route, always slightly ahead of the crew. He vanishes when the route turns a corner and reappears elsewhere.

This is **Pirn**, scouting the route's rhythm and timing. If the party attempts to approach or investigate immediately, he withdraws. He knows the Dock Ward alleys better than the party does. He can be spotted again on Days 5 and 6 with a **DC 14 Passive Perception**, but only fleetingly — he is careful, not careless.

#### Day 6 — Berna Briefs

Over lunch at a corner stall, Berna talks unprompted about why the gang war is more dangerous for her workers than for most people. She is not complaining. She is briefing.

> > "We go into alleys nobody else goes into. We move slow. We carry equipment, not weapons. If something happens, the Watch isn't going to respond quickly for us."

She goes back to eating. She does not ask for reassurance. This is not a request — it is a statement of how the world works, offered to the party on the assumption they are capable of handling accurate information.

#### Day 7 — The Guild Dispute

A drunk man in the early morning tries to start an argument with one of the sweepers over whose guild has priority in a specific alley. This is a genuine jurisdictional dispute that has been unresolved for six years. Berna defuses it with four sentences, all of them technically accurate, and keeps moving.

If a party member tries to help: they need a **DC 14 Intelligence (History)** check to know the relevant guild ordinance. Without it, Berna handles it before they can contribute. Either way, she keeps moving.

## Overview

Jalester assigned the party to protect a Dungsweepers' Guild crew coordinator through a tenday escort detail in the Dock Ward. The job is real work: cold mornings, long circuits through the ward, and the weight of a city not noticing the people who keep it clean.

## Summary

Jalester assigned the party to protect Mareva Coile's Dungsweepers' Guild crew through a tenday detail in the Dock Ward. They met Mareva and her field crew leader Berna, received the route schedule, and began walking the circuit. Over the following days they noticed a young man appearing ahead of the crew at different points along the route.
