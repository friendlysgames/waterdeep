# Design Notes: The Doppelganger Problem

## Cross-Faction NPC — Bonnie

***Bonnie appears in both EE-M3 (The Doppelganger Problem) and Harper Mission 3 (The Doppelganger Auditions).*** These two missions approach the same five doppelgangers from different angles with different stakes: the Harpers want intelligence and an asset; the Enclave wants the city's information environment protected. They are not redundant. The party's relationship with Bonnie is shaped differently depending on which mission they encounter first.

If both missions run in sequence, EE-M3 represents the Enclave acting on intelligence the Harpers uncovered. Bonnie acknowledges this if the party raises it. The departure requirement stands. The Splinter contact name stands. The **Harper M3 Complete** outcome governs how the Complication phase runs in ev-01: when marked, the traitor has already been neutralized and the taproom meeting is cleaner and faster.

The three-renown ceiling is intentional. It rewards a party willing to trust Bonnie's judgment over their own. The easiest resolution — pressing for compliance without engaging her position — is not the most rewarding one.

## Cross-Campaign NPC — Kelso Fiddlewick

***Kelso Fiddlewick is the Manshoon Splinter contact Bonnie names in this mission's resolution.*** He also appears in Order of the Gauntlet Mission 3 (The Shard Shunners) as the leader of a Field Ward wererat gang who, after mediation, provides intelligence about which gang leaders have taken Splinter coin. He appears in Doom Raiders Mission 3 in a related capacity.

His presence as the named operative in EE-M3 serves two purposes. First, it rewards parties who ran OG-M3: the name Bonnie gives them is a name they already know, and knowing it in two contexts establishes Kelso as a figure with real operational depth in the Splinter's Field Ward network. Second, it gives the GM a character with an established history with the party when his name surfaces in **Faction Outposts**. That history changes how the **Faction Outposts** investigation plays out — a party that handled the Endshift Tavern situation in good faith will find the Kelso thread lands differently than one that resolved it through force.

The cross-faction utility of Field Ward gang leaders with Splinter connections is also a structural feature of the Splinter network's design: they use informal criminal infrastructure because it is deniable and harder for the Lords' Alliance to track. Kelso's role across three faction missions reflects that pattern.
