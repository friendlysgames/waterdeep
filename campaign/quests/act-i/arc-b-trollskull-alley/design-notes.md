# Design Notes: Trollskull Alley

## Sandbox Structuring

**Seven Scenes for an Episodic Arc.** Arc A has four scenes because Arc A is a linear investigation — it flows from clue to clue, from the surface to the sewers, and ends when Floon is found. Arc B is structurally different: the party has a home base, multiple concurrent obligations, and no investigation to follow. The seven-scene structure imposes narrative progression on open-ended content without prescribing player choices. Scenes 1–3 run sequentially because the party cannot receive faction invitations before they have somewhere to receive them. Scene 4 overlaps the renovation period with faction recruitment running parallel to neighbor introductions. Scene 5 (Field of Triumph) slots into the renovation period — the party is recruited, fighting in the city's arena, and hiring staff all in the same stretch of time. Scene 6 (Grand Opening) is the renovation's endpoint and the arc's emotional culmination. Scene 7 (Twin Parades) is the city's calendar, not the party's — it falls when it falls.

Run Arc B as weeks of parallel activity, with these scenes as waypoints rather than a script.

**The Arc's Dramatic Question.** "Can the PCs establish themselves in Waterdeep's web of factions and obligations?" The answer is given at the Grand Opening: the party has become residents, not just occupants. Scenes 1–5 build toward that answer. Scene 7 (Twin Parades) is the city continuing to move around them, which is its own kind of acknowledgment.

## The Fireball Victim as Structural Goal

**Arc B's Most Important Output Is Invisible.** The party spends Arc B getting to know the Trollskull Alley community. The DM spends Arc B observing which NPC the players have bonded with. These are the same activity with opposite stakes. The observation is not optional — the fireball victim must be identified before Arc C begins. A fireball that kills a stranger is a plot event. A fireball that kills someone the party tried to warn, or saved once, or hired is the campaign's first genuine loss.

**Why the DM Chooses, Not the Players.** Letting players vote on who dies converts a tragedy into a game mechanic. The victim is chosen based purely on observation: who did the players ask follow-up questions about? Who made them laugh? Who showed up in player speculation between sessions? The victim is whoever the DM watched become real to the table — and the fireball is when the campaign's stakes become real to the players.

## Arc B as the Conductor's Score

**Arc B Contains Less Original Mechanical Content Than Any Other Arc.** This is intentional. The tavern renovation system lives in Appendix F. The staff candidates live in Appendix F. The faction recruitment scenes live in Appendix C. The faction missions live in Appendix D. Arc B's job is to establish *when* each appendix system becomes relevant and *what the party is doing* when those systems activate — not to duplicate the content. A DM who reads Arc B and then Appendix F will have a complete picture of the renovation period. A DM who reads only Arc B will have enough to run the arc. The appendix depth is there when they want it.

## Emmek Frewn as Sustained Antagonist

**The Arc's Only Persistent Conflict Thread.** Every other conflict in Arc B is either friendly (factions) or reactive (guild inspections, neighbor eccentricities). Emmek Frewn is the arc's only genuine antagonist — a man actively working to destroy what the party is building. His escalation sequence runs from guild interference through the Shard Shunner hiring threat and into Stage 4's misfired sabotage at the Twin Parades. He starts in Scene 2 and remains active through the end of the arc.

The party can resolve him through four paths: *confront* (exposing his Istrid Horn loan creates pressure — Istrid doesn't want Watch attention, and her Doom Raiders affiliation complicates public enforcement); *outcompete* (become successful enough that he can no longer afford the Shard Shunners); *cover his debt* (Istrid is owed 150 gp; covering it removes his leverage); *Watch report* (the Shard Shunners are halfling wererats — a Watch report implicates him in something far more serious than a business dispute). No path should feel costless. Emmek is a Neverwinter refugee whose family was destroyed by volcanic eruption, bought out by his sisters, and staking everything on a pub he cannot afford to lose. He is not a monster. He is desperate. The party should feel the weight of that when they win.

## Ammalia Cassalanter's Introduction

**Her Warmth Must Be Genuine.** The temptation is to signal the Cassalanters as villains through dramatic irony — give Ammalia slightly too-bright smiles, odd conversational choices, a moment of coldness beneath the grace. Resist this entirely. Ammalia Cassalanter is a warm, generous, politically sophisticated woman who is also a cult leader preparing to sacrifice children to Asmodeus. These are not contradictory. The warmth is how she has always functioned in the world; the diabolism is a choice she made under duress and has not stepped back from. If players suspect her in Scene 6, it should be because they are suspicious people, not because the text gave them a signal.

The dramatic irony of Arc G — watching a woman they genuinely liked negotiate a devil's contract — requires that the liking be earned honestly in Arc B.

## The Field of Triumph and Meloon Wardragon

**Meeting Meloon Before He's Gone.** The Field of Triumph places Meloon Wardragon in front of the party as himself: direct, skilled, warm, genuinely likeable. He leads the opposing team in the final round, gives them a real fight, and congratulates them afterward regardless of outcome. This is the party's only opportunity to know Meloon before Force Grey Mission 3 reveals what happened to him — the intellect devourer takes hold after Arc C, not before. The design depends on the sequence: the encounter works as foreshadowing only because it comes before the corruption. Parties who fight Meloon at the Field of Triumph and then encounter him again in Mission 3 will remember him as the man who shook their hands and asked their names — and that memory is what makes Mission 3 matter.

**Why Through a Fight Rather Than a Conversation.** Meloon is not primarily a talker. Placing him in a gladiatorial bout characterizes him faster and more memorably than any social encounter could. A man who fights hard and congratulates his opponents honestly is fully established. There is nothing to add.

## The Twin Parades as Nimblewright Foreshadowing

**Pure Setup with No Arc B Payoff.** The nimblewright sighting in Scene 7 offers nothing the party can act on. They see a mechanical figure in a procession. They may or may not make the Arcana check. The procession moves on. There is no investigation thread to pull, no NPC to question, no immediate consequence. This is deliberate. Arc C's Nimblewright Investigation lands harder because the party has a prior sighting they may or may not have registered as significant. If they made the check and noted it, Arc C opens with an immediate corroborating clue. If they missed it, Renaer's Arc C account is the first data point. Either way, Arc B has done its work.

## Mad Mage Seeds

**Three Seeds, No Hooks.** Arc B plants three Undermountain threads — the Skullport Trader (ev-06), Durnan's Warning (ev-06), Hlam's message (Force Grey Mission 1) — but none of them resolve in Arc B or provide actionable investigation paths. They are designed to register and then recede, accumulating below the surface until the Dragon Heist resolution makes Halaster's awareness of Waterdeep's events feel retrospectively obvious rather than bolted on. The seeds must be planted lightly, without pulling focus from Arc B's actual dramatic work.

## Force Grey / Zelifarn

**The Most Arc-B-Specific Faction Content.** Force Grey Missions 1 and 2 are the only Level 2 faction missions designed primarily to feed information threads rather than build renown in isolation. Hlam's warning (Mission 1) gives the first specific reference to Manshoon by implication. Zelifarn's barnacle-covered shipwreck lair at the harbor bottom (Mission 2) contains physical evidence of unusual deep-harbor activity pointing toward Skullport. Force Grey is the only faction where the Level 2–3 missions function as intelligence operations. Parties who join Force Grey in Arc B arrive at Arc F with more operational context than any other faction affiliation provides.
