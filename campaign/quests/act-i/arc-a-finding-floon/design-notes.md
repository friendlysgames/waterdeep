# Design Notes: Finding Floon

## Investigation Architecture

***Investigation Restructuring.*** The original Chapter 1 presents the Dock Ward investigation as a sequential chain: Skewered Dragon → warehouse name → Renaer → sewer. A single failed Charisma check at the Skewered Dragon can stall the party indefinitely, because the original text makes that tavern the sole source for the warehouse's location. The Three Clue Rule redesign distributes the warehouse address across four independent paths for Revelation #2 — the Skewered Dragon itself (simultaneously serving Revelation #1 and #2), neighborhood canvass, prisoner questioning, and the dropped bead trail — so that no single failure blocks progress. Revelation #1 has three independent paths; Revelation #3 has three. The four-path Revelation #2 reflects the warehouse address being the bottleneck in the original — extra redundancy where the original was most fragile. Players who follow all paths simultaneously discover more about the world; players who follow one path successfully still reach the warehouse.

## Faction Architecture

***The Two Zhentarims in Arc A.*** The original adventure presents the Zhentarim as a single faction. This remix uses Arc A to establish the faction fracture that defines the Manshoon subplot. Yagra Stonefist — a Doom Raiders operative, Davil Starsong's people — is at the Yawning Portal bar, openly wearing the Zhentarim pendant, fighting Xanathar Guild thugs. The Candle Lane warehouse belongs to Manshoon's cell, Urstul Floxin's people, operating separately from Davil. Players who later join the Doom Raiders in Arc B and then raid Kolat Towers in Arc I will have seen both Zhentarim factions from Scene 1 — one as a potential ally, one as an adversary, both operating under the same symbol. The distinction should be felt before it is explained.

***Bregan D'Aerthe Observer.*** The drow woman at the Yawning Portal balcony table is the campaign's earliest Bregan D'Aerthe presence. She is never named in ev-01 and the party is not meant to notice her unless they ask who is watching the well. She reappears in Arc B as a background presence — same table, different evenings — and is eventually identified as part of Jarlaxle's Waterdeep intelligence network monitoring the Undermountain entrance. Her placement in ev-01 is a retroactive payoff: by the time the party is dealing with the Sea Maidens Faire in Arc H, they can look back at the Yawning Portal and understand that Jarlaxle has had eyes on them since their first night in the city.

## Long-Game Seeds

***Cassalanter Name-Drop Through Renaer.*** Renaer's aside about the Cassalanters' property acquisitions near the Brandath tombs is designed to sit unexamined in player notes until Arc G. He does not frame it as a threat or a suspicion — he raises it as an oddity he cannot explain. Players who track it will revisit the detail when the Cassalanter Villa becomes a heist target. Players who do not track it will have it recapitulated by Renaer in Arc G when the connection becomes relevant. The name-drop is passive: it seeds without pressing. A first-level party has no use for it yet, and none is offered.

***Nihiloor as Foreshadowing.*** Q07 in the sewer hideout is the campaign's first contact with Nihiloor, who is eventually revealed in Arc F to be the true intelligence driving the Xanathar Guild — running three parallel projects, exercising psionic control over Xanathar, and infiltrating Waterdeep's civil institutions (see Appendix E). In Arc A, Nihiloor does not fight the party. It is simply there — seated, watching Grum'shar work — and then it is gone, faster than a creature that large has any right to move, through a portal the party cannot follow. This restraint is intentional. The party does not fight Nihiloor in Arc A and cannot interact with it meaningfully; the encounter is designed to register the creature's existence and its agency over the Guild cell before any context is available to understand what it is. Nihiloor's full scope surfaces across Arcs B through F. By the time the party reaches Undermountain Level 17 in the Dungeon of the Mad Mage, its presence in Arc A should read as a thread that began in the sewers under Trollskull Alley.

## NPC Design

***Renaer's Introduction.*** In the original, finding Renaer in the warehouse is a pleasant surprise in an otherwise thin scene — a friendly NPC who points the party downward. This redesign makes Renaer's introduction structural: the warehouse is the arc's pivot, not its anticlimax. The party arrives expecting Floon and finds someone who matters more to the campaign's back story. Renaer's knowledge state is asymmetric by design — he knows what Manshoon's cell wanted and why the locket mattered to his father, without knowing what the locket contained. His ignorance is genuine. This positions him as a trustworthy ally (he does not conceal things he knows) while preserving the campaign's central mystery (the nature of the Eyes, the Vault's location). The gap between what Renaer knows and what the party will eventually learn gives the warehouse scene a retrospective weight it lacks in the original.

***Durnan's Secret.*** Durnan's Masked Lord status and Red Sashes leadership (see Appendix A, DM-layer context) are not revealed in Arc A and are not hinted at in any player-facing text. His ev-01 portrayal — minimal words, the troll handled in seconds, "You fought well" as the full debrief — is designed to read as "formidable retired adventurer" to players and as "Masked Lord making deliberate choices about how much power to display" to the DM. The gap between those two readings is Durnan's dramatic potential. If the party gains enough standing to learn his secrets in Arc B or beyond, ev-01 retroactively becomes a performance — a man who could have stopped that brawl before the first punch deciding it was more useful to let it play out.

## Structural Mechanics

***Stealth-or-Force Preview.*** The warehouse presents the arc's first real structural choice: enter quietly and catch the kenku off-guard, or force entry and fight a prepared defense. Neither approach is obviously correct. This binary — infiltration versus direct assault — is the same decision the party will face at every heist target in Arcs F through J. Arc A is the tutorial, with first-level stakes and kenku rather than guild lieutenants. Each subsequent heist arc's design notes should acknowledge this parallel explicitly, so the DM can frame escalating heist complexity as the campaign's through-line skill development rather than a separate genre convention dropped into the story.
