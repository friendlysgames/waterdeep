# Design Notes: The Betrayal Pitch

## Jarlaxle Evaluates the Party

***The test within the test.*** The Betrayal Pitch is not a mission where the party completes an objective. It is a mission where Jarlaxle decides whether the party is worth investing in. He has already established that they are competent at basic delivery work (M1) and infiltration under social cover (M2). Now he needs to know whether they can plan and execute a shipboard approach, handle unexpected complications without panicking, and — most importantly — whether they are clever enough to take the thing they were not sent for.

The Ledger is on the table because Jarlaxle put it there. A party that takes the compass equivalent (jade, since obsidian was not specified) is competent. A party that takes the Ledger is interesting. A party that takes both and asks J.B. Nevercott what the Ledger means is the kind of party Jarlaxle will trust with Mission 4.

This calibration is intentional. The faction's later missions — protecting Nar'l, running the theater handoff, the harbor dive — require a party that exercises judgment under incomplete information. The Betrayal Pitch is where Jarlaxle determines whether this party exercises that judgment.

## The Scarlet Marpenoth Access Path

***Planting the submarine before Sea Maidens Faire.*** The from-below approach path introduces the *Scarlet Marpenoth* as a location before **Sea Maidens Faire** names it directly. A party that used this route has already been inside — they know the hatch location, the lower deck layout, and that the submarine is accessible from below without alerting topside crew. In **Sea Maidens Faire**, this knowledge can serve as an entry advantage or a negotiation point.

The Scarlet Marpenoth's presence is not announced in the approach description. It is described as "a submarine shape attached to the *Eyecatcher*'s keel." The party can investigate further or not. The hatch not being locked from below is deliberate — Jarlaxle chose not to lock it.

## The Jade Compass

***Small lies about minor details.*** J.B. Nevercott said "obsidian." The compass on the shelf is jade. A party that looks for the obsidian compass and finds only jade faces a real decision: bring the wrong compass, bring the Ledger instead, bring both, or bring nothing and claim the compass wasn't there. All four outcomes tell Jarlaxle something useful about how the party operates under ambiguity. The operational token he pays with — a flat piece of carved obsidian — is his acknowledgment of the joke.
