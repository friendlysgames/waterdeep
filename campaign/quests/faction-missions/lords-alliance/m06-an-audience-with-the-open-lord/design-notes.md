# Design Notes: An Audience with the Open Lord

## Why Mission 6 Is Gated

***The payoff requires the arc.*** Mission 6 is the political culmination of everything the Lords' Alliance mission arc has been building toward. Laeral Silverhand does not summon adventurers to Piergeiron's Palace because she has heard they are capable — she summons them because they have spent six missions demonstrating that they are the kind of people who can be trusted with the Masked Lord's Writ.

Running Mission 6 before the Cassalanter thread has developed — before Mission 5 at minimum, and ideally before Arc G has been engaged — removes its function as a payoff and turns it into an early-access tool. The Writ is most meaningful when the party earned it. Laeral's three questions are most meaningful when the party has genuine intelligence to share based on direct engagement with the Grand Game, not early-arc speculation.

The alternate trigger (Mission 5 failed) exists to ensure Mission 6 is never permanently locked. If the ledger was lost, Laeral reaches out through a different path — Jalester's accumulated field reports and her own intelligence have placed the party in her range of awareness regardless. The audience is still valuable, even if she enters it with less corrected intelligence.

## Laeral's Three Questions as Design Structure

***Each question does structural work.*** The three questions are not flavor — they are mechanics.

**Question 1 (Stone's location)** creates an honesty checkpoint. Laeral has DC 16 Insight and she is using it. A party that lies to the Open Lord of Waterdeep about the thing she most needs to know is a party that has decided the Alliance cannot be fully trusted. That is a legitimate choice, but it has consequences — Jalester's note after the meeting is not a slap on the wrist, it is the beginning of a different kind of Alliance relationship.

**Question 2 (faction intentions)** tests what the party has actually learned about the Grand Game. A party that has been paying attention across Arcs C–H can give Laeral a genuine intelligence briefing. A party that has been narrowly focused on objectives without attending to faction dynamics gives her less. Neither outcome prevents the writ. But the quality of the briefing shapes how prepared she is when she arrives at Arc J.

**Question 3 (Manshoon offer)** is the mission's moral and strategic test. It is the only question where the party's answer has a direct mechanical consequence in Arc J. Laeral discloses the offer openly because she is testing whether the party can reason about it correctly — not morally, but strategically. The DC 13 is calibrated to be achievable by a party that has been paying attention to what Manshoon actually wants from his various operations across the mission arc. A party that has processed that intelligence correctly can construct the argument. A party that has not, cannot.

## The Manshoon Leverage Arc J Consequence

***Why it is specific, not abstract.*** If Laeral accepts the Manshoon offer, the Arc J consequence is not "things are harder" — it is two concrete mechanisms: Watch cooperation delay and the Masked Lord exposure threat. These were chosen deliberately to create negotiation pressure rather than direct combat difficulty. Manshoon's strength is not violence; it is political leverage. The consequence should feel like Manshoon, not like an additional encounter.

The party that argued against the offer and succeeded has earned a cleaner path through Arc J. The party that did not has earned a complication that reflects exactly the kind of political manipulation Manshoon has been demonstrating across the entire campaign. Both are valid outcomes. Only one of them required the party to think like the Alliance.

## Laeral's Relationship to the Party Going Forward

***Arc J implications.*** An audience that went well — accurate intelligence on both questions, successful argument against the Manshoon offer — produces a Laeral who arrives at Arc J as an active ally. She does not fight alongside the party. But she has Watch positioning done in advance, she gives the legal intervention without hesitation when asked, and she is ready for Neverember's arrival because the party told her he was coming.

An audience that went poorly — hedged on the Stone's location, no opinion on the Splinter — produces a Laeral who is present at Arc J as an uncertain variable: technically on the party's side, with the writ active, but not prepared and not invested in outcomes beyond the treasury receiving the gold.

This distinction is designed to reward the party that treated the mission arc as an investment rather than a series of isolated tasks. The Alliance missions are not just about renown. They are about building a relationship with the most powerful institutional actor in Waterdeep. A party that did that work well has an ally at the vault. A party that did not has a neutral authority figure with a writ.
