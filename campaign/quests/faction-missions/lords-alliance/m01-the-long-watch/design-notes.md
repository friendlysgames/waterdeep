# Design Notes: The Long Watch

## The Two-Event Structure

***Why the split.*** The Long Watch is divided into two events because the dramatic break between them is genuine. Seven days of close observation and accumulating tension constitute a distinct mode of play — slow, social, textural — and Night Three's combat and investigation are a different mode entirely. A single event file would force one of these two things to be subordinate: either the tenday beats become an extended preamble before the "real" content starts, or Night Three gets compressed into a paragraph after the main event body. Neither serves the mission.

The tenday beats are not filler. They establish Berna and Herath as specific people before Night Three arrives, so that what happens to Mareva's crew means something. The Watch contempt scene in Day 2, Herath's coin purse in Day 4, Berna's unsolicited briefing in Day 6 — these are the work the party is actually doing, and they build the investment that makes the ambush feel like a violation rather than a random encounter.

## Berna as Mission Anchor

***The party's actual relationship.*** Berna is designed to be more memorable than Mareva. Mareva is the client — she gives the schedule and makes clear what failure costs. Berna is the reason the party cares whether they succeed. She earns acknowledgment slowly and without ceremony. The DM should resist any impulse to accelerate this: Berna does not warm to the party because they are charming or because they make a good Persuasion roll. She warms because they show up every morning, do the work without complaint, and handle the Day 2 Watch officer correctly.

The "don't make it worse" line in Day 2 is the mission's thesis statement. The party is operating in a city where the institutions that should protect these workers do not. The correct response is not to challenge that directly but to be present, capable, and consistent. This mission rewards patience above nearly everything else.

## Herath's Note as Long-Range Seed

***The organic approach path.*** Herath's sewer access note is a planted element for Arc F. The party receives it without fanfare, from a character with no institutional weight, as a personal gesture of reciprocity. The note has no immediate use. Its value only becomes clear much later, when the Sewer Route into Xanathar's Lair is the cleanest available entry point.

This is by deliberate design. The Three Clue Rule applies to major arc beats, but it also applies to entry points. Herath's note is the most organic path to the sewer approach: earned through specific behavior, arriving before the party knows they will need it, from someone with no agenda beyond returning a favor. The contrast with receiving a mission briefing packet from Jalester is intentional — this is the kind of intelligence you get by being decent to people who work the city's margins.

## Delvon Harsk and the Question of Clean Resolutions

***The mission's moral weight.*** Delvon is in this mission to prevent the resolution from being clean. He is a person who made a series of bad choices under financial pressure, who knew before Night Three that he had caused harm, and who has been unable to stop. His guilt is complete and his culpability is genuine.

The resolution options are deliberately varied and non-prescriptive. Arresting him is correct by most moral frameworks. Warning him off is less accountable but achieves the stated objective. Failing to find him at all is a genuine miss with real downstream consequences. None of these is the canonical outcome — Jalester responds differently to each, and his internal assessment shapes the texture of future missions.

The party that arrests Delvon and makes sure Mareva knows he is "not a killer, just an idiot" is doing the work the Alliance wants. The party that warns him off is making a judgment call the Alliance would not have made. Jalester knows the difference. He will not say so, but he knows.
