# Harpers First Meeting

> [!gamemaster]**Gamemaster's Summary**
>
> This Social Event occurs during **The Factions Come Calling**, after Renaer recommends the party to Mirt and the party includes at least one Good-aligned character. In this Event, the characters can:
>
> - Follow a paper bird's invitation to an evening at Lightsinger Theater, with free formal attire arranged at a Delzorin Street tailor.
> - Meet Mirt in Private Box C and discuss the play before he explains his interest in recruiting them.
> - Ask about the Harpers and decide individually whether to accept membership, without receiving an assignment tonight.
> - Receive a silver pin and anonymous safe-house access if they join, while their companions remain free to choose other affiliations.
>
> #### Candidates and Companions
>
> The invitation names Renaer's recommended, unaffiliated recruitment candidates and encloses one ticket for each of them; Private Box C has a seat for every invited candidate. The Good-aligned trigger determines when the invitation arrives, and creates no additional alignment test at the theater door. Characters who have joined another faction receive no second membership offer.
>
> Companions can attend the public performance through ordinary admission, but only invited candidates enter the box for the private recruitment conversation. If no eligible candidate remains interested, conclude the Event without a private meeting or a membership outcome.
>
> #### Background
>
> Mirt has followed the party's activities since Renaer named them, and Renaer's recommendation persuaded him to arrange an evening together before offering work. He reserved Private Box C at Lightsinger Theater in the Castle Ward and sent instructions to the Delzorin Street tailor that morning.
>
> *The Fall of Tiamat* is an opera sung in Giant, presenting the dragon goddess's defeat as a failure of political will rather than a triumph of divine power. A printed Common libretto rests on every box seat, so following the performance requires no language or check. Mirt chose the play because it gives him something to discuss with the candidates before approaching the Harpers' purpose and methods.

### The Paper Bird

> [!gamemaster]**A Noon Invitation**
>
> The bird arrives at noon at a Trollskull Manor window and waits until someone opens it; the invitation has neither signature nor seal. Read or paraphrase the following when a character approaches the window, then give the players the invitation text.

> [!readaloud]
>
> A small bird folded from paper taps its beak against the window, then settles on the sill with its wings tucked beside it. When you open the window, it steps toward your hand and spreads its wings, showing writing along the inside of the folds.

> [!exploration]**The Invitation**
>
> Unfolding the bird reveals the candidates' names, their theater tickets and these directions, written in the same hand throughout:
>
> > "Renaer has recommended you to Mirt. Your tickets are enclosed for tonight's performance of The Fall of Tiamat at Lightsinger Theater in the Castle Ward. Meet Mirt in Private Box C at intermission, at 7:15 p.m. The foyer opens at 5 p.m., and the curtain rises at 6:30 p.m. You are welcome to take your seats in the box before the performance.
> >
> > Formal attire is required. Present this note to the Delzorin Street tailor if you need clothes fitted; there is no charge. Allow 45 minutes for the fitting and half an hour to walk from the tailor to the theater."
>
> Any character who examines the invitation carefully and makes a successful **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation)** check concludes that its sender expects the recipient to follow the directions without needing a signature. The examination supplies no name or concealed Harper message.

### The Delzorin Street Tailor

> [!gamemaster]**Clothes for the Evening**
>
> The tailor recognizes the note and outfits its named candidates without payment or a check, taking 45 minutes to fit everyone attending together. The walk between the tailor and Lightsinger Theater takes 30 minutes each way; the theater is also a half-hour walk from Trollskull Alley. Candidates who have suitable formal attire can go directly to the theater.

> [!readaloud]
>
> Inside the tailor's shop, lengths of wool hang beside a mirror while a measuring tape lies across the open pages of a ledger. The tailor reads your note, brings out clothes for you to try, and clears a space beside the mirror for the fitting.

> [!gamemaster]**Arriving Without Formal Attire**
>
> The foyer admits guests from 5 p.m., and the attendants check attire before directing candidates to Private Box C. They refuse admission to anyone without formal attire and point out the tailor's directions on the invitation.
>
> - Candidates who leave the theater by **5:30 p.m.** reach the tailor at 6 p.m., finish at 6:45 p.m. and return for the **7:15 p.m.** intermission. They miss Act 1 but keep their meeting.
> - Candidates already at the tailor can begin their fitting by **6 p.m.** and still reach the theater for intermission.
> - Candidates who miss either cutoff receive a paper bird at noon the following day with a replacement ticket for each rescheduled candidate. It appoints them to meet Mirt in **Private Box C at 7:15 p.m. that evening**, with foyer admission from 5 p.m. and formal attire required. Their free fitting remains available; the note repeats the 45-minute fitting and 30-minute walk, with a 6 p.m. fitting cutoff.
>
> Use the remaining scenes for the following evening's meeting, assessing intermission arrivals through their conversation instead of claiming Mirt watched them during Act 1. Candidates arriving from 7:15 p.m. until the 7:35 p.m. bell join the ongoing conversation; those arriving at or after the bell receive the same next-evening appointment.

If the attendants refuse admission, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
>
> An attendant stops beside the foyer entrance and looks from your clothes to the ticket you have brought for the evening's performance. They return the ticket and point to the tailor's directions on your note, leaving you space to step out of the arriving crowd.
>
> > "I'm sorry, but formal attire is required for admission. The tailor listed here can help you, and your ticket will still be valid when you return."

### Private Box C

> [!gamemaster]**Before the Curtain**
>
> **Mirt** (Chaotic Good, Illuskan human, he/him), the Old Wolf, waits in the box with wine for the invited candidates. He welcomes arrivals before the 6:30 p.m. curtain, then keeps conversation brief while watching the first act from the shadowed half of the box. Skip this scene for candidates who arrive at intermission.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The orchestra's instruments carry up from the stage as an attendant leads you past the galleries toward a curtain marked with the letter C. Inside the box, a large, gray-haired man in a wool coat sits back from the rail beside a decanter and several glasses.
>
> He turns toward you, lifts the decanter from its tray, and gestures to the empty seats arranged along the rail, each with a printed program beside its cushion.
>
> > "Come in, there's wine here if you'd like some, and a better view from those seats than from mine. Have you eaten? It's a fucking long evening on an empty stomach."

> He lifts a program from the nearest seat and opens it to the first page of the libretto before putting it beside your glass.
>
> > "They're singing in Giant, but the words are printed in Common here. Keep this open if you want to follow it; there's one for each of you."

> [!social]**Company Before the Curtain**
>
> Mirt refills glasses and offers the bread and cheese on the side tray while the candidates settle into their seats. Before intermission he keeps his purpose private, with the conversation below covering hospitality, the performance and Renaer's recommendation. His public Old Wolf manner is expansive and vulgar; pointed business questions receive a short deferral without confirming his affiliation.

> [!qna]**The wine?**
>
> Mirt turns the decanter toward the speaker and leaves their glass within reach.
>
> > "Help yourself if you like it. I wanted something worth drinking for the evening, and there's no sense leaving it on the tray while everyone pretends they don't want another glass. Water's there too, if you'd rather."

> [!qna]**Have you arranged supper?**
>
> Mirt draws the side tray closer, with sliced bread beside a wedge of cheese and a clean knife.
>
> > "Take some of that before the curtain goes up. Bread and cheese will keep you going until afterward, and you can tell me whether you've found somewhere in the city that serves a decent fucking supper."

> [!qna]**Why can't we understand the singers?**
>
> Mirt lays a finger beside the Common verses in the open program.
>
> > "The opera's in Giant. The Common libretto follows it scene by scene, so you can read what they're singing without having to guess from the costumes. I wouldn't ask you to sit through the whole bloody thing with nothing to follow."

> [!qna]**Why the theater?**
>
> Mirt points along the gallery toward the neighboring boxes before sitting back with his glass.
>
> > "You can get between these boxes without crossing in front of the audience, and the curtains keep a good deal of the conversation inside. Someone thought about the people using the building. I enjoy that, and I'd like to hear whether you enjoy the performance."

> [!qna]**What is the play about?**
>
> Mirt turns the program back to its opening scene and leaves it facing the questioner.
>
> > "Tiamat's defeat, but watch what the people supporting her do when they have to keep their promises. This production puts a good deal of the trouble there. We can argue about whether that works when the actors give us a pause."

> [!qna]**What did Renaer say?**
>
> Mirt tilts his head toward the speaker and sets the decanter back on its tray.
>
> > "He spoke well of you and thought we ought to meet. I trust him, but I'd rather hear you talk than spend the evening repeating his account of you. How have you found the city since then?"

> [!qna]**Why invite us?**
>
> > "Renaer recommended your company, and I had a box to fill. I'd like to meet you before discussing anything further. We have time at intermission, and for now you've got a seat, a drink and something to watch."

> [!qna]**Are you offering faction work?**
>
> Mirt sets his glass down and gives the questioner a moment of his attention.
>
> > "We'll discuss my reason for asking you here at intermission. I haven't given you a job tonight, and you needn't promise me anything before we've spoken about it."

> [!qna]**What are you assessing?**
>
> > "I'd like to get to know the people Renaer recommended. Sit where you're comfortable, ask what you want to ask, and let us hear the performance while we've got the seats."

> [!qna]**The Stone, the vault or a villain's plans?**
>
> Mirt puts a finger against the program and turns back toward the stage.
>
> > "I won't give you an account of that tonight. We can discuss why I've invited you when the interval comes, but don't expect this evening to become a briefing on other people's business."

> [!exploration]**Watching the Old Wolf**
>
> Mirt looks toward each candidate when they speak, occasionally tilting his head before returning his attention to the actors on the stage. Any character who watches him for an extended period and makes a successful **DC 14 Wisdom (Insight)** check senses that he is assessing the candidates against specific criteria. Success doesn't reveal those criteria, and he won't name them if asked.

> [!gamemaster]**Mirt's Assessment**
>
> Mirt watches who speaks first, who listens and who notices the exits, looking for composure and competence without conducting a formal interview. These observations guide his conversation and confer no additional check or membership requirement; Renaer's recommendation brought the candidates this far. Candidates arriving at intermission receive the same offer, with Mirt attending to their questions and responses instead of their conduct during the performance.
>
> Mirt is a Masked Lord and Laeral Silverhand's advisor as well as a senior Harper, but he reveals neither private office tonight. He names no other member of the Waterdeep cell and gives no Grand Game briefing or mission assignment at this meeting.

### The First Half of the Performance

> [!gamemaster]**Stage Cues and Conversation**
>
> Use each timed beat only for candidates present then. The quoted actor lines are the Common program's translation of the sung Giant libretto, available to every candidate without a check; the staging remains visible regardless of language. These are original scenes for this production, supplying no campaign clue, historical revelation or mechanical reward. Between beats, use the early Q&A if someone speaks to Mirt, keeping exchanges brief while the singers continue.
>
> Mirt's optional openers follow the pauses indicated below. He listens to the candidate's answer without treating agreement with his opinion as a recruitment condition. Candidates arriving at intermission skip these beats and receive the same offer.

At 6:30 p.m., the curtain rises:

> [!readaloud]
>
> The orchestra draws out a low chord as the curtain rises on a painted fortress and a council table surrounded by actors in embroidered coats. Above the battlements, a five-headed dragon puppet unfolds its cloth wings, with its bearers moving together behind the wall while the council turns toward it.
>
> An actor at the head of the table lifts a sealed document, and the Common libretto gives his sung words:
>
> > "Each house has promised its soldiers and wagons. Sign beside the others, and the Queen will see that we have kept the agreement."

At 6:42 p.m., the council's promises are tested:

> [!readaloud]
>
> A quartermaster rolls an empty wagon onto the stage and opens its lid beneath the council's outstretched papers, while two soldiers wait beside the fortress gate. One councillor folds his banner across his lap and turns away from the document being passed toward him.
>
> The quartermaster's verse appears in the program:
>
> > "Your names are on the order, but the stores have not arrived. Which company am I to feed when every house has kept its grain behind its own gate?"

During the brief change of scenery afterward, Mirt leans toward the candidates:

> [!readaloud]
>
> Mirt lowers his glass and points toward the empty wagon as the stagehands draw it behind a screen, keeping his voice within the box.
>
> > "Would you have signed that order? Tell me what you'd have wanted to see before putting your name beside theirs."

At 6:55 p.m., a messenger reaches the fortress:

> [!readaloud]
>
> A messenger enters through the painted gate with a torn road map, laying it across the council's orders while the soldiers turn their empty bowls upside down. Behind them, the dragon's heads move toward different banners as the bearers pull the wings across the battlements.
>
> The messenger sings, with the translation printed beneath the scene title:
>
> > "The road is open, but no escort came to meet us. I can bring the wagons through if your soldiers leave these gates together, rather than waiting for each house to move first."

If a candidate turns toward Mirt during the next orchestral pause, he offers another opening:

> [!readaloud]
>
> Mirt leaves his glass on the tray and turns toward you while the messenger holds the map before the council.
>
> > "Would that offer get them moving? I'd like to know what you'd ask the messenger before sending anyone out with him."

At 7:10 p.m., the council refuses a common order:

> [!readaloud]
>
> The councillors take their banners from the table one by one, leaving the messenger's map beneath an unsigned order as the soldiers turn toward separate doors. The dragon puppet bends over the deserted chairs, and its central head opens while a singer steps into the light below it.
>
> The Queen's line is printed at the end of the scene:
>
> > "You pledged the road and the fortress to me. Who will carry the order now, when each of you has sent your people home?"

The orchestra closes that scene at 7:15 p.m. and the curtain falls for intermission. Mirt's recruitment conversation begins only then.

### The Intermission

> [!gamemaster]**A Private Conversation**
>
> At 7:15 p.m., Mirt sets down his glass and begins the conversation below. Intermission arrivals are shown directly to the box, where he welcomes them before making the same introduction. An intermission arrival who observes him throughout the conversation can use the **DC 14 Wisdom (Insight)** check from **Watching the Old Wolf**, with the same limited result.
>
> The earlier wine, food, libretto, architecture and Renaer answers remain available to intermission arrivals; skip the missed performance beats without treating their absence as a failure.

> [!readaloud]
>
> The stage curtain hangs closed, and conversations carry up from the rows below as people pass between their seats and the foyer. Mirt sets his glass beside the decanter and turns his chair toward you, with one hand resting on its wooden arm.
>
> > "Renaer spoke to me about you, and I wanted a chance to meet you myself. I'm a Harper, and we oppose tyranny and help people who have very little protection of their own. Much of our work begins with finding out what's happening before someone gets hurt."
>
> He draws his chair nearer to the others, leaving the view of the stage behind him as he continues.
>
> > "We need people who can investigate and judge what they find. I'd like to offer each of you a place with us, if you're interested. What would you like to know?"

> [!social]**The Old Wolf**
>
> Mirt (Chaotic Good, Illuskan human, he/him) :: A moneylender and senior Harper who wants to get to know Renaer's recommended candidates.
>
> Mirt is Friendly toward the candidates because he trusts Renaer's judgment, and he makes room for their questions without pressing anyone for an answer. He refills glasses during small talk and speaks at length about food or the theater; when the conversation turns to membership, his voice drops and his swearing stops. Use his slight head-tilt when a question makes him reconsider how much explanation someone needs.
>
> His hospitality gives hesitant candidates an opening to talk, while questions about the architecture draw him into a longer conversation with visible enthusiasm. He asks what they thought of the play, then listens to their reasoning rather than correcting their conclusions. A candidate who missed Act 1 can say so, and Mirt moves on to the questions they brought for him.
>
> Conversation topics Mirt is willing to discuss include:
>
> - He welcomes questions about the wine and whether the candidates have eaten, keeping the early conversation comfortable while they settle into the box.
> - He enjoys the theater's architecture and wants to hear what the candidates think of the play's treatment of political power.
> - He trusts Renaer's recommendation and speaks of him with affection, without explaining their private dealings.
> - He describes the Harpers' opposition to tyranny and their reliance on investigation, explaining that the network answers to neither a guild nor a government.
> - He offers each unaffiliated candidate an individual choice, with recognition as a Watcher and safe-house access for those who join.
>
> If a candidate asks about the Stone of Golorr, the Cassalanters or the vault, or names a suspected leader they have already learned about, Mirt returns the discussion to membership without supplying intelligence. He won't disclose his assessment criteria, identify other Harper operatives or describe their first assignment tonight. Specific dialogue for his permitted topics follows.

> [!qna]**The play?**
>
> Mirt gestures toward the closed curtain with the hand resting on his chair.
>
> > "They've made the defeat a question of political judgment, which gives us something to argue about afterward. I'd like to hear what you made of it. Do you think the people in charge understood what they were risking?"

> [!qna]**The Harpers?**
>
> Mirt puts his glass aside and rests both hands on the arms of his chair.
>
> > "We work against people who use power to put everyone else at their mercy. Finding out what they're doing is often the first useful thing we can do. We share what we learn with people who can help, and we protect those who'd suffer for telling us."

> [!qna]**Who gives orders?**
>
> Mirt turns his chair toward the person asking and gives them his attention.
>
> > "We answer to no guild and no government, though you'll have people you work with and I'll be your contact here. You're expected to think about what you're asked to do and speak up when something troubles you, so I can hear those concerns."

> [!qna]**Must we all join?**
>
> Mirt looks from the speaker to the other candidates sitting in the box.
>
> > "Each of you can answer for yourself. Your companions have their own choices to make, and helping you doesn't make them Harpers. If you're committed to another faction, I won't offer you a second membership."

> [!qna]**What does joining provide?**
>
> Mirt reaches into his coat and brings out a pin, a key and a small card.
>
> > "You'll be recognized as a Watcher, and I'll give you a pin so other Harpers can recognize you. There's a lodging in the North Ward where you can rest when you need it, with your own key and directions. You can use it without finding me first."

> [!qna]**What work comes next?**
>
> Mirt settles back in his chair and leaves the pin resting in his hand.
>
> > "Tonight I'd like you to decide whether you want to join. I'll send word when there's work for you. You don't need to agree to an assignment before you've heard what it involves."

### A Pin and a Key

> [!gamemaster]**Each Candidate's Answer**
>
> Ask each invited candidate for their answer and record it separately; an accepting character who remains unaffiliated becomes a Harper, while everyone else retains their existing status. Joining requires no persuasion check, and mixed answers produce no argument from Mirt. Read or paraphrase the following for each accepting candidate, using their name when Mirt hands over the items.

> [!readaloud]
>
> Mirt closes your fingers around a silver pin shaped like a harp within a crescent moon, then places a key beside it. He hands you a small card with an address and directions to a side entrance, turning it so you can read the writing.
>
> > "Welcome, and keep the pin somewhere safe where you can find it when you need to show it to someone. Take the key with you when you go; the address is on the card."

> [!gamemaster]**Watcher Benefits**
>
> Each accepting candidate starts at **Renown 1**, holds the **Watcher** rank and receives their own silver harp-and-crescent pin, mundane key and address card. Other Harpers are Friendly toward the new member by default. These benefits belong to the accepting characters; declining candidates and companions receive neither membership nor Renown from attending.
>
> The card identifies the side entrance at **12 Delzorin Street, North Ward**, a rented Harper lodging with five bunks, drinking water and a hearth. The key opens that entrance, allowing the new member to enter and rest without contacting anyone. Remallia "Remi" Haventree maintains the lodging through intermediaries, but her name appears nowhere on the card and Mirt doesn't identify her. This safe house is separate from her villa; she neither appears nor signs a message before **A Friend's House**, Harper Mission 4.

If a candidate declines, read or paraphrase the following:

> [!readaloud]
>
> Mirt listens to your answer and returns the pin to his coat before reaching for the decanter to refill his own glass.
>
> > "Of course, and thank you for taking the trouble to come and speak with me about it. You're welcome to stay for the rest of the performance if you'd like."

> [!gamemaster]**Before the Second Act**
>
> At the 7:35 p.m. second-act bell, Mirt takes his leave whether the candidates have accepted, declined or given him different answers. Read or paraphrase the following once the conversation ends.

> [!readaloud]
>
> A bell sounds in the gallery, and Mirt rises from his chair while the audience below begins returning to its seats. He adjusts his coat and pauses beside the curtain, looking back toward you before stepping out into the passage.
>
> > "I am almost never home."

### The Rest of the Evening

> [!gamemaster]**Staying for the Second Half**
>
> The candidates and public-admission companions can remain for the performance after Mirt leaves at 7:35 p.m. Use these optional beats if they stay, with the final curtain and bows ending at 8:30 p.m. Leaving earlier changes no membership, Renown or outcome, and Mirt doesn't return for another conversation tonight.

At 7:50 p.m., the soldiers make another agreement:

> [!readaloud]
>
> The fortress set turns to reveal the road behind it, where actors from the separate companies stand beside the quartermaster's wagon and unfold their banners together. Their captain lays the council's abandoned order on the ground, using a stone to hold it while the companies take places along the road.
>
> The Common libretto gives the captain's verse:
>
> > "We can wait for their signatures until the road is lost, or bring the stores through while there are still people here to use them. I'll march with whoever keeps the agreement beside me."

At 8:20 p.m., the Dragon Queen loses the fortress:

> [!readaloud]
>
> The companies wheel the wagon through the gate together, while the councillors return to an empty table with their folded banners still under their arms. The dragon puppet reaches toward the road, but its bearers pull it back behind the fortress as the gate closes across its wings.
>
> The Queen's final verse appears on the program's last page:
>
> > "I called for the houses that promised me this place, and none has brought an order the others will obey. You have shut the gate together while my council still argues over whose name should lead."
>
> The curtain rises for the bows afterward, with the puppet bearers joining the singers along the stage's front edge before the final curtain falls at 8:30 p.m.

## Overview

A paper bird invites specific characters to an evening at Lightsinger Theater, where Mirt awaits them in Private Box C for a conversation at intermission.

## Summary

### After the Meeting

We received an invitation to Lightsinger Theater and met Mirt in Private Box C, where he explained the Harpers and answered our questions. He offered each candidate an individual choice about joining, then left us to the evening's performance when the second-act bell rang.

### Without a Private Meeting

We continued with the renovation at Trollskull Manor without attending a private meeting with Mirt or changing any of our faction affiliations.
