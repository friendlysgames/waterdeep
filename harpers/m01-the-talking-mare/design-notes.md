# Design Notes: The Talking Mare

## A Contact with Work of Her Own

***Maxeene's conversation.*** A search followed by an automatic information delivery gives the party little reason to remember how they treated the contact. Maxeene expects courtesy, remembers what passengers actually said and asks for safer stabling, giving the interview a practical decision alongside its intelligence payoff. Her permanent Harper enchantment is an explicit campaign feature, so the ordinary duration of *Speak with Animals* doesn't become an unexplained rules exception.

***The three inquiries.*** The Wagonworks drivers and Orvel independently provide the current route, while the stablehands preserve the existing DC 12 Persuasion check with a useful direction on failure. These paths keep the search moving without making one failed roll decide the mission, and they introduce the dray workers before the party needs their cooperation during relocation.

## Identification and Relocation

***Vell's observation.*** The watcher needs to see or hear an actual speaking contact, allowing the party to distinguish ordinary interest in a horse from evidence of a Harper asset. The wagon and coach create positions the characters can use without requiring a particular spell or build, and Vell's approach becomes visible when she tries to recover her view.

***The later move.*** The older resolution treated relocation as though it erased an identification that had already happened, leaving the two outcomes difficult to carry forward. Preserving **Maxeene Identified** after a successful move records what the Splinter knows, while **Maxeene Cover Preserved** records the narrower early success. The party can still protect Maxeene after exposure, so missing the bonus doesn't remove their reason to act.

***The paid stable order.*** Mirt's written order settles stabling and the merchant's lost work before the street operation begins, allowing the players to spend their attention on surveillance and Maxeene's account. Hessa Dorn is the mission's local stablekeeper, and her immediate acceptance keeps the move from depending on an improvised payment or an additional persuasion gate.

## Optional Opposition

***The resident roster.*** Two resident Spies replace the older three-Spy house, with Vell reporting and departing rather than becoming an automatic extra guard. Vell uses the ordinary 2024 Spy, following the user's decision that faction missions don't require boss phases for named operatives. The **Harpers Mechanics Reference** audits the separate and combined encounters for actual three-, four- or five-character groups, while the party can hand Mirt the address without entering the house.

***Observation before intrusion.*** The unlocked entrance and upper window provide visible places to examine, while the residents' pursuit and surrender limits make withdrawal a concrete option. This gives the party control over whether they follow the lead now, scout it for later or pass it to the Harpers.

## Campaign Connections

***Shesstra Street.*** Maxeene's destination and Vell's reporting route independently establish the Splinter safehouse, giving **Faction Outposts** an early lead that survives either character's absence. Hearing the address earns the main mission's information payoff, while the separate bonus rewards verifying the watcher or ending her surveillance.

***The unnamed passengers.*** Maxeene's account of the sun elf and half-orc remains limited to what she saw and heard, including the argument about learning who is running the war. Their identities have been intentionally left unresolved in this mission, preserving the established seed without importing names from a different source version.

***Individual participation.*** The private theater brief and debrief belong to Harper members, while the operation remains open to their companions. Base Renown and both listed bonuses apply to the participating members individually, and the five-character encounter baseline measures the fighters present rather than enrolling the party together.
